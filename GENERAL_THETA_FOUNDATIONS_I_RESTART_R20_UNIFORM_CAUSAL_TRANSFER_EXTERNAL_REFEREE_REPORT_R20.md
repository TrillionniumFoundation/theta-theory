## Recommendation

**Reject at the level of the Annals of Mathematics, Inventiones Mathematicae, the Journal of the American Mathematical Society, or Acta Mathematica in the manuscript's present form.**

This is **not a technical-correctness rejection**. Revision R20 is a substantial mathematical advance over R18 and is, in my assessment, the strongest manuscript in the restart sequence. It answers the most important structural request in the preceding report: the paper now treats one parameter-independent causal programme chosen before the worst-case model, proves an exact joint parameter--phase allocation criterion, establishes robust attainment and finite-model obstruction in a finite-dimensional positive-instrument category, and supplies effective upper and lower certificates at the unchanged persistent-state budget under explicit uniform presentation data.

The central new point is genuine. Separate feasibility at each parameter does not imply feasibility by one observer. The manuscript eliminates the common report-to-label kernel through a simultaneous affine support-function inequality whose maximum over next labels is taken only after the parameter and phase contributions are summed. This is precisely the quantifier required to rule out a hidden model-dependent or phase-dependent programme. The proof does not exchange
\[
\inf_{\gamma\ \mathrm{common}}\sup_{\theta}R_\theta(\gamma)
\quad\text{with}\quad
\sup_\theta\inf_\gamma R_\theta(\gamma).
\]

In the load-bearing arguments I checked, I did not find an elementary gap invalidating:

- the exact finite-target barycentric allocation theorem;

- the inherited exact allocation, regular acquired-geometry, blind-transport, and typed simulation statements; or
- the source-to-artifact provenance and independent-rebuild claims at the level at which they are stated.

The negative top-four judgment concerns the **concentration, intrinsic reach, and external mathematical impact of the advance**, not an identified fatal error. The exact theorem remains a finite-horizon, finite-action, fixed-cardinality characterization in a jointly dominated compact predictive category. Its invariant retains complete parameter-indexed and phase-indexed occupancy profiles together with all affine moment inequalities. The robust compactness theorem additionally uses a common finite-dimensional ordered physical presentation, and the effective theorem additionally requires common support-resolving report reconstruction, computable model nets, uniform raw-instrument moduli, and exhaustive programme enumeration. These are clear and legitimate hypotheses, but they are not yet an intrinsic numerical classification of general causal finite-memory predictability.

The Gaussian and quantum families verify the theorem in nontrivial continuous-report settings and do not rely on normalized-filter contraction. Nevertheless, they are still deliberately regular finite-dimensional known-family models. The exact calibration example is sharp and useful, but purpose-built. The manuscript does not yet resolve a recognized difficult natural problem in robust filtering, stochastic control, causal coding, information design, or quantum information; nor does it establish unknown-kernel learning rates, an infinite-horizon theory, a nondominated common-programme theorem, or a matched optimal region for programme length, workspace, precision, planning time, physical time, and simulator state.

For a strong specialist journal in probability, stochastic control, information theory, statistical decision theory, robust POMDPs, quantization, controller synthesis, or mathematical quantum information, my assessment is **favorable after a focused major revision**.

## 2. Executive assessment of Revision R20

R20 changes the scope of the exact allocation theorem from one prepared experiment to a family with a fixed but inaccessible model parameter. The observer knows the family offline but receives no parameter oracle at runtime. A single action row, report transformation, update rule, stopping subset, and readout map must work throughout the family.

The new theorem has three distinct layers.

### 2.1 Exact common-programme allocation

For each model and phase, the certificate records actual occupancies and conditional predictive barycenters. Joint domination places every common report row in a weak-star compact simplex. The unknown row is mapped to all model/phase mass and affine-moment coordinates. Membership of the proposed moments in that image is equivalent to the displayed joint support-function inequalities.

The placement of the maximum is the mathematical center:
\[
\max_j
\left\{
\sum_{(\theta,t)}
h_{\theta,t,z,a}(y)
\phi_{\theta,t,j}\bigl(F_{\theta,t}(\cdots,y)\bigr)
\right\}.
\]
The maximum is outside both sums. Moving it inside either sum would authorize a different hidden transition rule by phase or by model.

This yields an exact risk-valued invariant
\[
R_{\mathrm{aut}}^\Theta(n,M)=\Phi^\Theta(n,M)
\]
without subtracting a modelwise oracle baseline and without replacing common readouts by parameter-specific Bayes actions.

### 2.2 Robust attainment and finite-model obstruction

On a common finite-dimensional ordered physical space with positive instruments, finite actions, joint report domination, and compact continuous readouts, the programme space is compact after fixing the finite discrete choices. Integrated instrument coefficients are weak-star continuous; finite-horizon state/label propagation is a finite composition of continuous matrix operations. Legality, no premature stopping, and exact terminal stopping are closed constraints.

The worst-case risk is a supremum of continuous functions and is lower semicontinuous. It therefore attains its minimum on the nonempty compact common-programme class. The finite-intersection property then gives
\[
R_{\mathrm{aut}}^\Theta(n,M)
=
\sup_{\varnothing\ne F\subset\Theta,\ |F|<\infty}
R_{\mathrm{aut}}^F(n,M).
\]
A strict target below the robust value is excluded by finitely many fixed models. This is a global obstruction against all common programmes, not merely a separator against one proposed occupancy flow.

The result is exact but topological. It gives neither a witness-size bound nor an efficient way to find the finite subfamily.

### 2.3 Effective same-budget minimax completion

Under a stronger uniformly effective presentation, one common support-resolving report partition and reconstruction is applied across the entire family. Every Borel report row is averaged once, and every common finite row is rounded once on its original support. This preserves the same `M`-label budget and exact legality/deadline throughout the family.

A finite model net supplies the lower comparison by inclusion. A programme-uniform raw-instrument modulus supplies the upper comparison. Complete enumeration of the resulting finite programme list and certified risk enclosures give
\[
L-E_h-\beta_Q
\le
R_{\mathrm{aut}}^\Theta(n,M)
\le
U+\Omega_n(s),
\qquad
U-L\le\eta.
\]
The implementing upper programme works on the original uncountable family without runtime access to the true parameter. The lower endpoint controls every admissible Borel programme of size `M`, not only the best sampled controller.

This is substantially stronger than pointwise approximation of a fixed programme. It is also intentionally an exhaustive, potentially enormous procedure rather than an efficient synthesis theorem.

## 4. Audit of the exact common-programme theorem

### 4.1 Quantifier order

The manuscript fixes one programme before taking the parameter supremum. The physical parameter remains fixed during the entire path. It is not reselected adversarially at each call. The modelwise conditional states in the certificate are analyst variables under the respective deployed laws, not registers or parameter information available to the observer.

This quantifier order is correctly maintained in the theorem, proof, examples, effective approximation, and simulation corollary.

### 4.2 Joint domination and weak-star compactness

For each action, one finite reference measure dominates every legal report law across parameters, phases, and predictive states. The common report row lies in a finite product of simplex-valued `L^infty` unit balls. Positivity and the row-sum identity are weak-star closed, so the source set is convex and compact.

Each model/phase/label/affine-test coordinate is an integral of the row against an `L^1` coefficient. The moment map is therefore weak-star continuous into the product topology, even for an uncountable parameter set.

### 4.3 Finite-coordinate separation

If the target moment family is outside the compact convex image, a basic product neighborhood separates it from the image in a finite projection. Finite-dimensional separation then yields finitely many parameter--phase pairs and finitely many affine tests. The support function of the allocation simplex is the integral of the pointwise maximum after all selected coefficients are summed.

This proves both necessity and sufficiency. It also explains why modelwise or phasewise maximization is the wrong test.

### 4.4 Borel implementation and common null sets

A maximizing label can be chosen by the least-index rule, giving measurability of the support function. For a feasible moment family, weak-star rows have Borel representatives. Completing the row on one reference-measure null set preserves its meaning for every model because of joint domination. The proof does not choose a different version for an uncountable subfamily.

### 4.5 Calibration under each deployed law

Starting from the fixed preparation, the common action and report rows generate the proposed masses. The remaining affine moments identify the proposed conditional predictive barycenters under each model's actual deployed law. This induction uses the unnormalized instrument identities and does not compare with a full-history posterior generated by a different controller.

### 4.6 Operational status of the dual

The appendix/test-span lemma correctly distinguishes three statements:

1. each affine component can be uniformly approximated by finite signed combinations of executable test expectations;
2. a strict finite-coordinate violation survives sufficiently accurate approximation; and
3. a maximum of several affine expectations, or a finite family of hypothetical models, is not thereby one free executable continuation.

This prevents the mathematical dual certificate from being misdescribed as physical access to the true parameter.

I found this proof architecture coherent under the stated compact predictive and domination hypotheses.

## 5. Audit of robust compactness and the finite-model value identity

The additional compactness theorem does not make the invalid claim that arbitrary pointwise products of weak-star-convergent kernels converge. Instead, finite-dimensional positive instruments are integrated against the common row first. Every integrated matrix coefficient is weak-star continuous. A finite execution then uses only finite products and sums of those continuous coefficients.

After padding to `M` labels and fixing the finite discrete choices, the ambient programme space is compact. Phase legality, absence of premature stopping, and terminal stopping mass one are closed equalities in the continuous occupancy functions. Intersecting them over an arbitrary parameter set leaves a compact common class.

For each fixed parameter, terminal risk is continuous. The supremum over parameters is lower semicontinuous and hence attains its minimum. For a risk target `r`, the sets imposing legality, deadline, and `R_theta<=r` are closed subsets of one compact programme space. Their finite-intersection property gives the finite-model obstruction.

The proof of
\[
R^\Theta=\sup_{F\subset\Theta,\ |F|<\infty}R^F
\]
is therefore not an interchange of minimax operators. It is a compactness statement about one shared decision variable.

The exact boundary must remain prominent:

- `n`, `M`, and the action alphabet are finite;
- the physical ordered space is finite-dimensional;
- report coefficient measures are jointly dominated;
- readouts form a compact metric set with continuous loss dependence; and
- no bound or algorithm for the finite witnessing subfamily follows from compactness alone.

Within that scope I found no elementary defect in the argument.

## 6. Audit of the effective minimax theorem

### 6.1 Common report reconstruction

The effective theorem requires more than joint domination. On each active report cell, one reconstruction measure must be equivalent to the report measure for every normalized physical state, phase, and parameter; inactive cells must carry zero instrument. This common support signature is what preserves exact legality and stopping after report coarsening.

This is a strong hypothesis. It is not merely a numerical regularity assumption, and it should be treated as one of the theorem's principal interface conditions.

### 6.2 Report-reduction direction

Every finite cell programme executes on the original experiment, so the original Borel optimum is no larger than the cell optimum. Conversely, averaging an arbitrary common Borel row against the same reconstruction measure on each cell produces one common cell row. Positive direct-sum contraction bounds the risk change by `E_h`.

The directions
\[
R\le R_h\le R+E_h
\]
are correct.

### 6.3 Exact support preservation

The equivalence of active cell null sets implies that finite label/action paths are possible under the fine row exactly when their cell-averaged edges have positive reconstruction mass. This is stronger than small total variation and is what prevents an approximation from introducing a forbidden early stop or failing to halt.

### 6.4 Dyadic approximation

Each common probability row is rounded once, on its original support. Removing support edges cannot create a forbidden path. Coupling action and next-label samples gives the displayed `O(n(A+M)/Q)` risk allowance. The rounding does not enlarge the persistent alphabet.

### 6.5 Parameter-net comparison

For the finite programme list,
\[
S_G\le R_G^\Theta\le S_G+\Omega_n(s)
\]
uses two different facts: the parameter net is a subset of the family for the lower inequality, and the programme-uniform raw-instrument modulus controls the upper inequality. This correctly places the model modulus only on the upper endpoint.

### 6.6 Certified global lower

The lower endpoint does not come from a local optimizer or sampled-risk success. Completeness of the finite programme list, together with the comparison of every Borel programme to a listed one, yields a lower bound for the whole `M`-state Borel class.

The theorem is mathematically meaningful, but its algorithmic interpretation must remain modest. The candidate count is exponential or worse in the tabulated row dimensions, and the model net multiplies the number of certified evaluations. The result is effective convergence, not practical robust planning in general.

## 7. Audit of the raw family realizations

### 7.1 Unknown Gaussian families

The proof compares unnormalized instruments rather than normalized posterior maps. The transition perturbation contributes its `1->1` norm, and the Gaussian emission perturbation is controlled by integrating derivatives along mean and covariance paths. The mean derivative bound `m/sigma_-` and covariance derivative bound `v/(sqrt(2) sigma_-^2)` are consistent with the displayed calculation.

Every nonsingular Gaussian density is positive on the common report space, so one common cell support table is available even when transitions are reducible, deterministic, or permutations. The tail is retained as a genuine report cell; it is not removed by conditioning.

This verification genuinely avoids normalized-filter contraction.

### 7.2 Unknown noncommuting quantum families

The channel replacement is controlled by trace-norm contraction of a positive normalized instrument. Effect perturbation is controlled by the square-root/Sylvester estimate on the fixed spectral interval `[kappa,K]`. The resulting modulus
\[
c_t(s)+\sqrt{K/\kappa}\,e_t(s)
\]
is consistent with the two-factor congruence expansion.

The theorem remains finite-dimensional, and the observer memory is classical. Pure preparations and unitary channels do not create a denominator problem because the comparison is made before posterior normalization. The paper correctly avoids any claim about unrestricted quantum memory or unbounded operators.

### 7.3 Singular and changing-rank extensions

The Cantor and countable-support clauses retain actual cylinder or tail masses. The rank-changing example uses separately flagged positive branches and does not normalize a rare stratum to probability one. These are legitimate effective examples, though they still require a common reference/support presentation.

### 7.4 Calibration threshold

The two-call calibration experiment is an effective test of the common-programme quantifier. The lower at `p=p_+` uses a least-favorable prior only as a minimax lower bound. The five-state upper is one parameter-independent programme. The three/four-state converse uses the exact clock-support accounting and includes randomized rows.

The hidden offset adds `delta^2` because the matching forecast has zero mean error under each fixed model. The erasure extension computes an attained deficiency rather than treating an arbitrary error tolerance as a statistical lower bound. The manuscript correctly refrains from claiming that seven states are minimal after the report alphabet is enlarged.

These examples are sharp and useful. They remain sharpness witnesses rather than solutions of a major external open problem.

## 9. Literature and novelty assessment

The literature positioning is substantially improved. The manuscript now treats robust finite-state-controller synthesis, parametric controller synthesis, oracle-guided inductive synthesis, and hidden-model controller extraction as genuine neighboring theories rather than claiming robust finite memory as a new subject. It also distinguishes finite-window control, continuous-observation quantization, belief aggregation, causal transport, sequential information design, zero-delay coding, and nonanticipative rate distortion.

- continuous report spaces;
- a finite-model exact value identity for the whole common programme class;

That is not an independent priority certificate. For publication, the introduction should retain a concise theorem-by-theorem comparison, especially with robust FSC and finite-state-controller parameter-synthesis formulations, and should avoid presenting compactness, support-preserving rounding, or exhaustive finite controller enumeration as individually new.

## 11. Major issues before specialist-journal publication

### 11.1 Make the hierarchy of hypotheses visually unavoidable

The paper has three materially different theorems:

1. exact joint allocation under compact predictive realization and joint domination;
2. robust attainment/finite-model obstruction under a common finite-dimensional positive-instrument presentation; and
3. effective minimax completion under common support reconstruction and computable uniform moduli.

These layers are correctly separated in the proofs, but the introduction and main theorem package remain dense. A one-page hypothesis/conclusion diagram should be part of the article itself, not only a repository audit file.

### 11.2 Elevate common support reconstruction to a headline assumption

The effective theorem's exact interface preservation depends on equivalent active-cell null sets across every state, phase, and parameter. This is stronger than continuity, domination, or small approximation error. It excludes many natural observation models with parameter-dependent or state-dependent support.

The abstract currently emphasizes uniform support and continuity certificates, but the body should explain earlier that this assumption is the price of exact legality and deadline preservation.

### 11.3 Reserve “programme” terminology carefully

The exact theorem may produce a noncomputable Borel transition rule. Such an object is a mathematical controller kernel, but “programme” can suggest a finite syntactic description. The article does state the distinction. It should make it terminologically systematic, for example by using “Borel controller” in the exact layer and “finite programme/table” only in the effective layer.

### 11.4 Clarify the novelty of the finite-model obstruction

Once the whole common-programme space is compact and each model's risk/legality constraints are closed, the finite-subfamily identity follows from a finite-intersection argument. The result is useful, but the novelty is in establishing the correct compact common programme topology for positive instruments, not in the abstract finite-intersection principle.

This should be stated explicitly in the introduction and literature comparison.

### 11.5 Add a complete numerical certificate for one continuous family

The effective theorem is currently supported by exact formulas and finite regression examples, but a reader would benefit from one end-to-end continuous-model certificate displaying:

- a concrete report partition;
- `E_h`, `beta_Q`, `Omega_n(s)`, and `eta`;
- the programme-list and model-net sizes;
- the resulting lower and upper values; and
- the actual common programme selected.

This need not be computationally impressive. Its purpose would be to make the theorem's global lower and same-budget upper operationally transparent.

### 11.6 Develop one less purpose-built natural consequence

The unknown Gaussian and quantum theorems show applicability, but mainly establish effective computability of the robust finite-memory optimum. The calibration theorem is designed to be exactly solvable.

A specialist-paper case would be materially strengthened by a theorem that extracts a new qualitative or quantitative conclusion for a standard robust filtering or POMDP family beyond “the optimum is effectively approximable under supplied moduli.”

### 11.7 Condense the theorem-level comparison with robust FSC work

The manuscript's literature section is responsible and detailed. The introduction should nevertheless contain a compact table comparing:

- model uncertainty set;
- finite versus continuous reports;
- finite versus arbitrary model family;
- horizon and objective;
- controller timing convention;
- local versus global synthesis guarantee;
- exact value versus upper bound;
- comparison class;
- and preservation of the original state budget.

### 11.8 Separate the native article from the historical packet editorially

The 48-page native article is the submission object. The six companions should be presented as optional archival supplements rather than one 218-page paper package. Their preservation role is legitimate, but requiring an editor or referee to navigate the entire packet would obscure the new theorem.

### 11.9 Keep the fixed-parameter semantics near every robust statement

The true parameter is fixed throughout a path. A timewise adversary produces a different experiment. This distinction is currently correct and repeatedly stated; it should remain explicit in theorem summaries and examples.

### 11.10 Keep the quantum resource boundary explicit

The persistent `M` is a classical controller alphabet. The physical density matrix is part of the experiment, not free quantum memory. This is clear in the theorem and should remain equally clear in the abstract, introduction, and any publicity language.

### 11.11 Do not market the exhaustive search as a complexity result

The candidate bound is valuable because it makes effectivity explicit. It does not imply practical synthesis. No polynomial, singly exponential, or otherwise meaningful general complexity guarantee is proved.

### 11.12 Reconsider the breadth signaled by “Foundations”

## 13. What would materially change the top-four assessment

Further source auditing, larger regression counts, or another deliberately soluble finite example would not materially change the recommendation. A top-four case would require an advance of a different order, such as one of the following.

### 13.1 An intrinsic simplification of the exact frontier

Reduce the full parameter/phase law-valued feasibility object to a more canonical invariant or duality that can be interpreted directly from raw experiments and gives nontrivial structural consequences without retaining every conditional occupancy profile.

### 13.2 A recognized hard natural application

Use the theorem to settle a previously unresolved robust finite-memory law for a standard nonlinear filter, a nonuniformly observed POMDP, a regime-switching partially observed system, a causal coding problem, or a noncommuting quantum experiment.

### 13.3 A nondominated or support-changing common-programme theorem

Extend exact or effective completion beyond the common domination/support regime while preserving one Borel programme and exact causal interfaces.

### 13.4 A long-run theory

Establish an infinite-horizon, average-cost, discounted-uniform, or unbounded-stopping counterpart in which state reuse and robust common-programme constraints remain exact.

### 13.5 A nontrivial synthesis-complexity theorem

Give a meaningful complexity bound, approximation scheme, or structural reduction substantially stronger than exhaustive enumeration over all finite tables.

### 13.6 A genuinely matched multi-resource region

Prove lower as well as upper bounds for several of persistent states, programme length, scratch space, numerical precision, planning time, physical time, and simulator state in one natural experiment family.

Any one of these could concentrate the architecture into a result of broader general-mathematics significance.
