## 4. Detailed proof audit: the v73 upper modulus

### 4.1 One-use reference assistance

For the positive outcome, the effect difference is

```text
Delta E = (lambda/2)(n_theta-n_phi)·sigma.
```

Its eigenvalues are `±a` with

```text
a=lambda sin(h/2).
```

For a bipartite state `rho`, the two output blocks of the flagged measurement difference are `B` and `-B`, where

```text
B=Tr_A[(Delta E tensor I)rho].
```

Testing against a Hermitian contraction on the reference gives `||B||_1<=a`. An eigenstate of `Delta E` attains equality without an ancilla. Therefore the unhalved one-use distance is exactly `2a`.

This normalization is consistent with the paper's convention that classical total variation is half the displayed block trace norm.

### 4.2 Hybrid estimate

Replace the unknown measurement one call at a time inside an arbitrary common adaptive tester. Trace-norm contraction at each replacement gives

```text
d_N <= N d_1 = 2Na.
```

The argument is valid with an initial reference, quantum memory, classical feedback, and bounded public stopping. A stopped strategy can be padded to `N` irrelevant calls.

### 4.3 Secant programme geometry

Choose midpoint and perpendicular directions `u,v`. The target Bloch vectors are

```text
m+a v,  m-a v,
m=lambda cos(h/2)u.
```

Set

```text
eta=1-lambda^2,
b=sqrt(eta+a^2).
```

Since `||m||^2+b^2=1`, `m±bv` are valid pure Bloch vectors. The two target measurements are exact mixtures of the same endpoint measurements with weights

```text
p_±=((1±t)/2,(1∓t)/2),
t=a/b.
```

No positivity margin is assumed uniformly in `lambda`; the endpoints remain legal through the projective limit.

### 4.4 Programme fidelity

Conditioned on a programme string, the tester interacts with the same sequence of endpoint measurements under both hypotheses. Hence the entire final state is a common channel of the programme string. The root fidelity of the two one-bit distributions is `sqrt(1-t^2)`. After `N` bits, the trace distance is bounded by

```text
2 sqrt(1-(1-t^2)^N)
 = 2 sqrt(1-[eta/(eta+a^2)]^N).
```

The use of a pair-dependent endpoint pair is legitimate for the metric. It does not yield one target-independent processor or seizer for the full circle.

### 4.5 Reduction to `K_(N,lambda)`

The inequality

```text
1-(1-t^2)^N <= N t^2
```

and `a<=lambda h/2` give the programme branch

```text
lambda h sqrt(N/eta).
```

The hybrid branch gives `N lambda h`. Taking the smaller and capping at two gives

```text
d_N <= min{2,K_(N,lambda)h},
K_(N,lambda)=lambda sqrt(N min{N,eta^(-1)}).
```

At `eta=0`, the programme denominator is not used; the hybrid branch supplies the projective endpoint. At `lambda=0`, the instruments coincide.

## 5. Detailed proof audit: the lower modulus

### 5.1 Majority derivative

For an odd sample size `r=2j+1`, let `g_r(a)` be the difference between the two majority-event probabilities under Bernoulli biases `±a`. Exact differentiation and cancellation give

```text
g_r'(a)
 = r C(2j,j) 4^(-j) (1-a^2)^j.
```

The induction bound on the normalized central binomial coefficient and Bernoulli's inequality imply a derivative of order `sqrt(r)` for `a<=r^(-1/2)`. Integration yields the local bound; monotonicity yields a fixed saturated bound. Dropping at most one coordinate handles even `m`.

The final factor is consistent with unhalved `L1` distance. I found no missing endpoint or sign case.

### 5.2 GHZ parity identity

For the state

```text
(|0>^k + exp(i alpha)|1>^k)/sqrt(2),
```

the product of output signs has expectation

```text
lambda^k cos(k theta-alpha).
```

Choosing

```text
alpha=k(theta+phi)/2+pi/2
```

produces opposite biases of magnitude

```text
a_k=lambda^k |sin(kh/2)|.
```

This phase is common to the two hypotheses. It may depend on the known pair of alternatives, which is allowed in a pairwise lower bound.

### 5.3 Visibility scale

Let

```text
T=min{N,eta^(-1)},
k_0=floor(T).
```

Since `T>=1`, `k_0>=T/2`. For `1<=k<=k_0`,

```text
lambda^(k-1) >= exp(-1/2) > 1/2,
```

using

```text
log(1-eta)>=-eta/(1-eta).
```

Thus `lambda^k>=lambda/2`. The restriction `k<=k_0` is essential and is respected throughout the proof.

### 5.4 Small angular separation

When `h<=1/k_0`, take `k=k_0`. Then

```text
sin(kh/2)>=kh/pi,
floor(N/k)>=N/(2k).
```

The repeated-block signal obeys

```text
sqrt(m)a_k >= constant * lambda sqrt(N k_0) h
             >= constant * K_(N,lambda)h.
```

The majority lemma supplies the desired two-sided scale.

### 5.5 The `k_0=1` regime

When `k_0=1`, take single-copy blocks. Then `T<2` except at the exact endpoint, so the ordinary `sqrt(N)lambda sin(h/2)` signal dominates the target `K h` up to an absolute constant for all `h<=pi`.

This case prevents a gap at strong noise or short horizon.

### 5.6 Larger angular separation

For `k_0>=2` and `h>1/k_0`, take

```text
k=max{1,floor(1/h)}.
```

Then `k<=k_0` and `1/2<=kh<=pi`. Also `lambda>=1/sqrt(2)`. The parity bias is therefore bounded below by an absolute positive constant. One block already gives distance greater than `1/64`, matching the saturated side of the theorem.

### 5.7 Endpoint audit

- `lambda=0`: both metric and `K` vanish.
- `lambda=1`: `T=N`, GHZ block size can reach `N`, recovering linear coherent resolution.
- `N=1`: every floor and block estimate reduces to the exact one-use regime.
- `h=0`: both instruments coincide.
- `h=pi`: ordered labels distinguish the antipodal directions; no outcome relabelling quotient is taken.

## 6. Detailed proof audit: covering and coding

### 6.1 Lower packing

For `K>0`, select an equally spaced circle packing at angular separation at least

```text
256 delta/K.
```

On `delta<=2^(-10)`, the metric lower bound gives pairwise separation at least `4delta` before saturation. A radius-`delta` ball with any legal memoryless centre cannot contain two packing points. The resulting cardinality is `Omega(K/delta)` whenever the prescribed spacing is below the circle diameter.

When `K/delta` is small, one target supplies the lower bound after adding one inside the comparison expression. This also covers `K=0`.

### 6.2 Upper charts

The maps

```text
z_s(t)=s((1-t^2)/(1+t^2),2t/(1+t^2))
```

with `s=±1` and `t in [-1,1]` cover the circle. Their angular derivative has magnitude at most two. Nearest rounding at denominator `B` gives angular error at most `1/B`.

A grid of order `K/delta` therefore gives an adaptive cover. The use of `4K/delta` in the executable formula provides slack for certificates and does not change the order.

### 6.3 Exact grid computation

For rational `N,lambda,delta`, the implementation computes

```text
K^2=lambda^2 min{N^2,N/(1-lambda^2)}
```

as a rational number and chooses an integer ceiling square root. No floating-point branch determines the active noise regime.

### 6.4 Legal decoded effects

Every chart point lies exactly on the unit circle. The effects have eigenvalues

```text
(1+lambda)/2, (1-lambda)/2.
```

Thus they are positive and sum to the identity. The implementation assembles input-first Choi blocks using the transpose convention and validates the resulting instrument exactly.

### 6.5 Payload convention

The codebook has `4B+2` slots, except at `lambda=0`, where it has one. The mathematical fixed-length payload is the ceiling of the binary logarithm of this slot count. Canonical hexadecimal serialization may omit leading zeroes; JSON metadata, public parameters, and expanded rational matrices are separate resources.

## 7. Detailed proof audit: conditional states

### 7.1 Lower angular coordinate

The channel that discards the quantum output and retains only the binary outcome maps every conditional-state instrument to the underlying noisy readout. Therefore angular separation is unaffected by the chosen conditional states.

### 7.2 Lower state coordinates

On input `I/2`, both outcomes have probability one half for every angle and visibility because each binary qubit effect has trace one. The retained output is

```text
Omega_rho=(1/2)rho_+ direct_sum (1/2)rho_-.
```

Compact maximal-rank patches in the two state strata have dimension

```text
V=sum_y[r_y(2n-r_y)-1].
```

The inherited local binary/Choi test gives an `N^(-1/2)` packing scale.

### 7.3 Product packing

Take the Cartesian product of an angular packing and a state packing. If angles differ, the readout witness separates the pair regardless of state. If angles agree, the mixed-input programme-state witness separates differing states. Thus every distinct product point is separated, although the witnessing experiment may depend on which coordinate differs.

This is sufficient for a covering lower bound and does not assert one simultaneous decoder for the whole packing.

### 7.4 Product upper code

Encode the angle to error `delta/2`. Encode each state so that its `N`-copy programme error is at most `delta/4`. A common processor receives one copy of each row state at every slot, performs the encoded measurement, and outputs the selected row state. The product of the two programme collections differs by at most `delta/2`, and triangle inequality gives total error at most `delta`.

The state codes preserve their individual rank bounds. Assembly with the effects preserves complete positivity and trace preservation.

### 7.5 Scope limitation

This proof depends on:

- retained binary readout;
- trace-one conditional states;
- equal one-half outcome probabilities under `I/2`; and
- independent codebooks for the angle and row states.

It does not cover arbitrary effect/state couplings or unobservable labels.

## 8. Independent audit of v72

### 8.1 Ordered flag geometry

The ordered rank-one flag manifold has dimension `d^2-d`. Column phases are gauge; outcome permutations are not. The manuscript uses the correct dimension.

The one-use distance is bounded above and below by fixed-dimensional multiples of the Frobenius flag metric. The lower bound tests one outcome event; the upper bound sums reference-assisted block estimates.

### 8.2 Imported projective multiple-shot theorem

Revision 72 invokes the exact multiple-shot discrimination theorem for ordered von Neumann measurements. The cited primary result states that parallel querying is optimal in the relevant minimum-error setting and characterizes perfect finite-query discrimination. The manuscript uses this result only for the projective readout coordinate.

This imported theorem should remain visibly external. It does not apply directly to the noisy v73 family; the new noisy lower and upper arguments are written separately.

### 8.3 Pivoted triangular atlas

Given rational projectors, rational nonzero columns can be selected without square roots. Partial-pivot LU gives a permutation and a unit lower-triangular matrix with bounded multipliers. Right multiplication by the upper factor preserves nested column spans.

For an arbitrary valid chart word, rational projection formulas on the nested spans give a complete ordered flag. Successive projection differences are orthogonal rank-one projections summing to the identity.

The perturbation estimate follows from a uniform lower singular-value bound on the bounded triangular cube. Constants grow rapidly with dimension; no dimension-uniform claim is made.

### 8.4 Mixed-scale upper and lower covers

The flag grid has scale `delta/N`; the row-state factor grid has scale `delta/sqrt(N)`. The upper code is their product.

For the lower bound, a local flag chart and maximal-rank state patches are packed at the corresponding scales. Pairs with different flags are separated after discarding row outputs; pairs with equal flags are separated by a row-state witness. Arbitrary centres are excluded by triangle inequality.

The proof is coherent on the stated small-error range.

### 8.5 Readout recoverability

If the classical flag is omitted but encoded into fixed pairwise orthogonal output supports, one fixed output measurement recovers it without disturbing the conditional state. This gives an exact equivalence on the target family.

If all conditional rows coincide and the flag is forgotten, the readout coordinate disappears. The manuscript correctly uses this as an identifiability warning.

### 8.6 Uniform seizing

The state-to-instrument processor and instrument-to-state seizer are fixed over the whole ambient programme state space. Repeating them gives exact `N`-copy metric equality. Their composition retracts arbitrary legal memoryless centres and cannot increase target distance.

The proof correctly separates:

- target code rank preservation; and
- arbitrary-centre retraction, which may increase Choi rank.
