\section{A locally readable extension of the query menu}
\label{sec:v28-readout}
For a fixed graph the norms induced by spanning finite query menus are
equivalent. For growing graphs their equivalence constants are part of
the problem. We therefore specify the readout used in the size-uniform
result, while leaving every preceding theorem under its original menu.

Throughout this section all edges are independent copies of one fixed
scalar experiment. They have the same prior, detector, horizons $n,m$,
compact calibration chamber and known calibration $a$. Write
$P=|E|\ge1$ for the number of edges, to distinguish it from the remaining
scalar horizon $m$. The number of vertices and hence the total raw
horizon $P(n+m)$ may vary. Put
\[
 p=\min\{n(r-1),q_m\},\qquad
 D_\ell(a)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}d_i(a),\qquad
 V_\ell(a)=\mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a),\quad D_0=V_0=1.
\]
We assume $p\ge1$; a constant experiment requires no predictive memory.
The scalar results give $D_1=V_1=1$ and
$D_\ell\le V_\ell\le\ell!D_\ell$, including zero terms.

\subsection{An executable menu containing the original probes}
At a queried boundary, independently choose one of two types with equal
probabilities. The first is exactly the original tensor-product query
of Section~\ref{sec:v25-graph}, with its original product selection law
and its original all-remaining-failures target. For the second type,
select an edge uniformly from all $P$ labelled edges. On that edge use
its scalar future failure-product menu for the number of its remaining
trials. The binary target is that all those reports are failures.
A completed edge has target one. On all other edges execute their
remaining trials in the fixed canonical order with a fixed admitted
command and ignore their outcomes in this target. In both types all
remaining raw trials are executed, including trials following a
nonfailure. There is still one queried checkpoint and exactly
$P(n+m)$ raw trials in each physical run.

The descriptor reveals the query type, the selected probe and edge
where applicable, and the visited set. It reveals no discarded command
or report. This is an explicitly enlarged menu, not a reinterpretation
of the old tensor loss. Denote its loss by $\mathcal L_j^\diamond$,
and the two component losses by $\mathcal L_j^{\rm ten}$ and
$\mathcal L_j^{\rm loc}$. With seeds fixed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v28-mixture-loss}
 \mathcal L_j^\diamond
   =\tfrac12\mathcal L_j^{\rm ten}
                    +\tfrac12\mathcal L_j^{\rm loc},\qquad
 \mathcal L_j^{\rm loc}
   =\frac1P\sum_{e\in E}
                    \|q_{e,S}(h)-\widehat q_{e,S}\|_{e,S}^2.
\end{equation}
The scalar menu norm includes its actual selection probabilities.
Unopened-edge probabilities are known from the prior, and completed
ones are constant, so they can always be predicted exactly.

The added tests do not enlarge the exact operational quotient. They
are probabilities of future report events obtained by summing over all
reports on the other edges, and belong to the future test space.
Conversely, the enlarged menu still contains the original spanning
tensor probes. The distinction is quantitative: the new menu assigns a
specified mass to tests which read one edge without multiplying its
signal by the failure probabilities of all the other edges.

Write $\mathcal R^{\diamond,\rm av}_{G,M}$ and
$\mathcal R^{\diamond,\rm max}_{G,M}$ for
\eqref{eq:v25-risk-definitions} with this loss. The infimum still
ranges over one common charged controller for all boundaries. Define
$B^{\diamond,\rm av}_G(\varepsilon,a)$ and
$B^{\diamond,\rm max}_G(\varepsilon,a)$ as the least nonnegative
integer $B$ for which the respective infimum at $M=2^B$ is at most
$\varepsilon$. Since both summands in \eqref{eq:v28-mixture-loss}
are nonnegative, control of this new loss also controls the original
tensor loss within the fixed factor two.

\begin{lemma}[Recovery without a tensor norm cost]
\label{lem:v28-local-recovery}
For $1\le\ell\le p$ with $D_\ell>0$, choose a scalar affine
recovery map of norm at most $L_\ell$ from the scalar active query
vector to $(d_i z_i)_{i\le\ell}$. The constants $L_\ell$ are uniform
in calibration. For any $r_0$ selected active edges, their combined
recovery map from the enlarged query vector has norm at most
$\sqrt{2P}\,L_\ell$, independently of $r_0$, the graph, and the
visited set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The scalar maps are the bounded scaled-coordinate recovery maps used
in Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-checkpoint}; they are defined on arbitrary
centres and do not invert a zero scale. For differences of enlarged
query vectors, summing the squared scalar recovery bounds over the
selected edges gives at most
$L_\ell^2\sum_{e\ {\mathrm{selected}}}\|\Delta q_e\|_e^2$.
By \eqref{eq:v28-mixture-loss}, this is bounded by
$2P L_\ell^2\|\Delta q\|_\diamond^2$. Taking square roots proves
the assertion. The unused tensor coordinates need not be inverted.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A size-uniform deterministic construction}
Define the single-edge bit profile
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v28-single-bit-profile}
 b(\varepsilon,a)=
       \max_{\substack{0\le\ell\le p\\ V_\ell(a)>0}}
       \left\{\log_2 V_\ell(a)
                        +\frac\ell2\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\right\}.
\end{equation}
The zero index makes this nonnegative. This is the same capped profile
as in Corollary~\ref{cor:v25-graph-bits}, specialized to identical
edges. In particular $b(\varepsilon,a)\ge\tfrac12\log_2(1/\varepsilon)$
for $0<\varepsilon\le1$.
Let $\operatorname{cw}(G)=\min_\pi\max_j|\delta(S_j^\pi)|$ be
unweighted cutwidth, counting parallel edges with multiplicity.

\begin{proposition}[A product filter with no graph-length accumulation]
\label{prop:v28-uniform-upper}
There is a constant $C_+$ depending only on the scalar experiment and
its compact calibration chamber such that, for every finite graph,
calibration and $0<\varepsilon\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v28-uniform-upper}
 B^{\diamond,\rm max}_G(\varepsilon,a)
       \le \operatorname{cw}(G)
                                 \{b(\varepsilon,a)+C_+\}.
\end{equation}
An empty graph has zero risk. The upper bound is realized by a fixed
order and one causal joint-label filter. Its constants do not depend
on the number of vertices, edges or raw trials.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The scalar whole-image classification, with its integer-budget bound,
supplies a reachable active codebook with at most $2^{B_e}$ entries
and squared scalar menu error at most $u$ on every first block, where
\[
                  B_e\le b(u,a)+C_0.
\]
Increasing $C_0$ includes the integer ceiling. This follows directly
by inverting $\Psi(M,a)\le u/C$; the maximum over capped positive
indices gives \eqref{eq:v28-single-bit-profile}. Thus $C_0$ is uniform
in $u$ and calibration, including all additive collision strata.

Choose an order attaining cutwidth. At the first endpoint of an edge,
compute its acquired state from its fresh first block and its exact
prior, quantize once in this codebook, and retain its index. Keep that
index unchanged until the second endpoint, where it is discarded.
Distinct edges have independent priors, so no other block changes
this active posterior. At a boundary encode the tuple of active
indices as one joint label. The order and clock specify which edges
are active; no order history is stored. At most
$\operatorname{cw}(G)B_e$ bits persist. The fresh vertex batch is
processed under the same atomic-batch convention as before. In
particular, an unchanged active state is not requantized at each
intervening vertex. There is no recurrence with a number of error
terms proportional to the graph size.

It remains to bound the retained tensor part of the menu as well as
the local part. Every active edge's future all-failure probability,
for every probe and every reachable representative, is at most
$q_*=(1-\eta)^m<1$. If $f$ edges are active, telescoping the product
of their probabilities, using Cauchy--Schwarz, and then averaging the
product query law gives
\[
 \mathcal L_j^{\rm ten}
    \le f q_*^{2(f-1)}
                  \sum_{e\ {\mathrm{active}}}\|q_e-\widehat q_e\|_e^2
    \le f^2q_*^{2(f-1)}u.
\]
Unopened-edge factors are identical in the true and representative
products and are at most one; completed factors are one. For $f=0$
the error is zero. The positive series
\[
 C_{q_*}=\sum_{f=1}^\infty f^2q_*^{2(f-1)}
                        =\frac{1+q_*^2}{(1-q_*^2)^3}
\]
bounds the preceding coefficient uniformly in $f$. Also
$\mathcal L_j^{\rm loc}\le u$ at every boundary. Choose
$u=2\varepsilon/(1+C_{q_*})$. Then
\eqref{eq:v28-mixture-loss} is at most $\varepsilon$ on every tape.
Finally, replacing $\varepsilon$ by a fixed positive multiple changes
$b$ by at most $p/2$ times the absolute base-two logarithm of that
multiple. This proves \eqref{eq:v28-uniform-upper} after increasing
$C_+$, uniformly at all boundaries with the same filter.
\end{proof}
