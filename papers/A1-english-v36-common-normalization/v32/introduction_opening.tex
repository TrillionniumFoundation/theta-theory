\section{Introduction}
A finite future experiment tests only part of a posterior distribution.
The relevant directions must first be acquired, may become indistinguishable
as the experiment varies, and must then be preserved by one state through
subsequent observations. The dimension of an ambient test space and a
collection of separately optimal checkpoint encoders do not determine
this causal prediction problem.

Our principal result is an all-budget classification for positive sparse
monomial experiments, uniform through additive collisions of the exponents.
It identifies the acquired determinant scales and realizes them by one
persistent-label filter. We develop its operational consequence in the
same nonregenerative model: exact finite compatibility equations, uniform
approximation by finite command alphabets, and global certificates whose
relative widths remain controlled through changes of attained dimension.
The history--state equations keep the same transition rule at every
history reaching a given label. They do not replace the posterior by a
fresh current block.

The subsequent graph and occupation results address acquisition order and
separate information conventions. Their regenerative exactness is a
statistical realization theorem within its stated class, not a
characterization of arbitrary shared-posterior memory. A positive
nonregenerative example evaluates the latter distinction directly, using
two admissible schedules with identical label and seed resources.
