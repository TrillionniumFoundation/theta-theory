\section{Information beyond the leading hierarchy}
\label{sec:v4-nonlinear}
The nonlinear law of the preceding section is not determined by the
contact Hessian. We prove this by an analytic deformation with fixed
area, gap and contact curvatures. Its higher coefficient can be computed
from the half-line action and determinant, without extrapolating an
uncontrolled finite-length error.

\subsection{The quartic variation of a boundary law}
Assume first that the two facing graphs are identical and even, and
write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-even-jet}
 \psi(y)=\frac\kappa2 y^2+\frac q{24}y^4+O(y^6),\qquad
 c=1+g\kappa,\quad \gamma=\arcosh c,\quad
 a=\frac{\sinh\gamma}{g},\quad \lambda=e^{-\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Here $q$ is a fourth graph derivative, not a source variable. Fix $g$
and $\kappa$ and vary $q$ in a smooth family. Higher graph jets may
vary as well. The two boundary actions and amplitudes now agree;
write them as $S$ and $B$. Both limiting parities give one function
$\mathcal F$.

\begin{proposition}[A nonlinear coefficient and its quartic derivative]
\label{prop:v4-quartic}
Let $\mathcal R_\infty(0)=\partial_d\mathcal F(0)$. At fixed
$g,\kappa$, its derivative with respect to $q$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-quartic-derivative}
 \partial_q\mathcal R_\infty(0)
       =-\frac{\cosh(2\gamma)+2}{12a^2\sinh(2\gamma)}.
\end{equation}
This derivative does not depend on the graph derivatives of order six
or higher. More precisely, if $S(u)=au^2/2+tu^4/24+O(u^6)$ and
$B(u)=1+\beta u^2+O(u^4)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-quartic-parts}
 \partial_qt=\coth(2\gamma),\qquad
 \partial_q\beta=-\frac1{4a\sinh(2\gamma)},\qquad
 \mathcal R_\infty(0)=\frac{2\beta}{3a}-\frac{t}{12a^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The linear half-line segment is $x_i=\lambda^i u$. Because the
quadratic action is stationary with its first endpoint fixed, its
change upon substituting the cubic bridge correction begins at degree
six. The quartic part of the stationary action is therefore the
quartic part of the broken action evaluated on that linear segment.
In one flight, the $q$-dependent quartic term of the length is
$q(x_i^4+x_{i+1}^4)/24$. The endpoint is counted once and every
interior site twice. It follows that
\[
 \partial_qt=1+2\sum_{i\ge1}\lambda^{4i}
                  =\frac{1+\lambda^4}{1-\lambda^4}=\coth(2\gamma).
\]
This use of stationarity may equivalently be obtained by differentiating
finite stationary actions and passing to the convergent half-line sum.

To degree two in $u$, the $q$ variation of an edge's mixed derivative
vanishes: the quartic term just displayed is a sum of two pure fourth
powers. Thus the product term $U$ in \eqref{eq:v4-half-product} has
no $q$-dependent quadratic coefficient. The interior Hessian variation
at that degree is diagonal, with entries $q\lambda^{2i}u^2$.
Changes in the cubic bridge do not enter degree two, and products of
two Hessian perturbations enter only degree four. Consequently the
quadratic coefficient of the logarithmic determinant has derivative
$\sum_{i\ge1}G_{ii}\lambda^{2i}$. At equal curvatures,
\eqref{eq:v4-half-green} gives
$G_{ii}=(1-\lambda^{2i})/(2a)$. Hence
\[
 \partial_q\beta=-\frac1{2a}
 \left(\frac{\lambda^2}{1-\lambda^2}
              -\frac{\lambda^4}{1-\lambda^4}\right)
             =-\frac1{4a\sinh(2\gamma)}.
\]
The quadratic coefficient of $B$ equals that of $\log B$ because
both have vanishing linear coefficient by evenness.

Finally use \eqref{eq:v4-limit-integral}. The density correction is
$\beta(u^2+v^2)$, whose expectation under the quadratic residual-time
weight is $2\beta d/(3a)$. For the quartic action correction,
first variation of the positive-part integral gives the negative
integral of $t(u^4+v^4)/24$ over $a(u^2+v^2)/2<d$; there is no
boundary term because the residual weight vanishes there. The two
elementary integrals are
\[
 \int(d-ar^2/2)_+\,\dd u\dd v=\frac{\pi d^2}a,
 \qquad
 \int_{r^2<2d/a}(u^4+v^4)\,\dd u\dd v
                                  =\frac{2\pi d^3}{a^3}.
\]
Taylor's formula in the uniform Morse coordinates justifies the
remaining $O(d^2)$ relative error. This gives the last identity in
\eqref{eq:v4-quartic-parts}, and substitution proves
\eqref{eq:v4-quartic-derivative}. Terms of degree six or higher cannot
contribute to this first offset coefficient.
\end{proof}

For an additional finite-length identity, put
$L_i=(\sinh((j-i)\gamma),\sinh(i\gamma))/\sinh(j\gamma)$,
$M_j=\mathsf H_j^{-1}$ and $v_i=L_i M_jL_i^t$ in the equal-curvature
case. Thus $L_0=(1,0)$ and $L_j=(0,1)$. The same calculation before
taking the limit yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-finite-quartic}
 \partial_q\mathcal R_j(0)=
 -\frac13\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(G_j)_{ii}v_i
 -\frac1{24}\left(v_0^2+v_j^2+2\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}v_i^2\right).
\end{equation}
Indeed, the logarithmic amplitude variation is
$-\sum(G_j)_{ii}(L_iY)^2$, and its quadratic conditional expectation
is $-d\sum(G_j)_{ii}v_i/3$. The unweighted fourth moment in the
quadratic sublevel, divided by its residual-time integral, is
$d v_i^2$. These observations prove \eqref{eq:v4-finite-quartic},
including $j=1$ with an empty interior sum. Its limit is
\eqref{eq:v4-quartic-derivative}. This identity provides a finite
check of the normalization but is not needed to define the infinite
boundary determinant.

\subsection{An analytic family invisible to all leading data}
\begin{theorem}[Identical leading metrics and distinct nonlinear laws]
\label{thm:v4-jet-fiber}
On the lattice $3\mathbb Z\times4\mathbb Z$ there is a real-analytic
family of strictly convex scatterers $D_s$, $|s|<s_*$, with $D_0$ the
unit disk, for which
\[
 g=1,\qquad A=12-\pi,\qquad \kappa_0=\kappa_1=1
\]
at the two horizontal contacts. The horizontal orientations exhaust
the ground-onset event. All leading count amplitudes and all leading
endpoint covariance matrices, at every flight number, are independent
of $s$. Nevertheless their nonlinear boundary coefficient satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-detected-jet}
 \left.\frac{\dd}{\dd s}\mathcal R_\infty^{(s)}(0)\right|_{s=0}
                                  =\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\end{equation}
It therefore determines $s$ locally with a bounded inverse derivative.
For every sufficiently small fixed positive $d$, the limiting
normalized probability $\mathcal F^{(s)}(d)$ also varies with $s$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Consider the support function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-support-family}
 h_{s,z}(\theta)=1+s\sin^4\theta+z\sin^6\theta.
\end{equation}
Its obstacle area is
$\mathcal A(h)=\tfrac12\int_0^{2\pi}(h^2-(h')^2)\,\dd\theta$.
At $(s,z)=(0,0)$ its $z$ derivative is
$\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^6\theta\,\dd\theta=5\pi/8>0$.
The analytic implicit-function theorem gives $z=z(s)$, $z(0)=0$,
with $\mathcal A(h_{s,z(s)})=\pi$ exactly. Since the $s$ derivative
is $3\pi/4$, one also has $z'(0)=-6/5$.

At $\theta=0,\pi$, both perturbations and their first two derivatives
vanish. Hence $h=1$, $h'=0$ and the curvature radius $h+h''=1$.
The support points remain $(1,0)$ and $(-1,0)$, their curvatures
remain one, and the supporting lines of horizontal neighbors have
separation one. These points attain that separation. At the disk the
vertical gap is two; all other nonhorizontal gaps are larger. A small
analytic parameter interval preserves this strict gap, disjointness,
positive curvature, and clearance. Thus only the horizontal pair
contributes to the ground onset. Reflection and central symmetry of
\eqref{eq:v4-support-family} make the two facing graphs identical
and even. Area and the two contact Hessians are fixed exactly, not
merely to first order. Equations~\eqref{eq:g-ratio} and
\eqref{eq:v3-metric-law} give the assertion about every leading datum.

We compute the first higher graph jet. Let
$r(\theta)=h(\theta)+h''(\theta)$ be the curvature radius. Near the
right contact the boundary parametrization has derivatives
$x'(\theta)=-r(\theta)\sin\theta$ and
$y'(\theta)=r(\theta)\cos\theta$. Since $r'(0)=0$, integrating these
expansions and eliminating $\theta$ gives for the facing graph
\[
 \psi(y)=\frac{y^2}{2r(0)}+
          \frac{3r(0)-r''(0)}{24r(0)^4}y^4+O(y^6).
\]
In our family $r(0)=1$ and $r''(0)=h''(0)+h^{(4)}(0)=24s$;
$\sin^6\theta$ has zero fourth jet and does not affect this identity.
Thus $q_s=3-24s$. Here $\gamma=\arcosh2$ and $a=\sqrt3$, so
$\cosh(2\gamma)=7$, $\sinh(2\gamma)=4\sqrt3$.
Multiplying \eqref{eq:v4-quartic-derivative} by $q_s'=-24$ yields
\eqref{eq:v4-detected-jet}. The inverse-function theorem proves the
local scalar reconstruction. Finally the joint analytic expansion
$\mathcal F^{(s)}(d)=1+d\mathcal R_\infty^{(s)}(0)+O(d^2)$ has
uniform differentiated remainder. Its $s$ derivative at zero is
$d\sqrt3/2+O(d^2)$, nonzero for all sufficiently small positive $d$.
\end{proof}

The family in Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-fibers} varies the two contact
curvatures while preserving only their scalar product. The family in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-jet-fiber} instead keeps both contact curvatures
fixed, so even the entire leading conditional metric is unchanged.
It is separated by the nonlinear law, not by a relabelling of the
leading inverse. Neither construction claims equality of finite-offset
probabilities or of marked length spectra. Higher-jet information from
other billiard observations has its own inverse theory; the assertion
here is its explicit realization in a full-phase statistical threshold
law with a collision-order-uniform limiting construction.
