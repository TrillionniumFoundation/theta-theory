\section{Sparse centroids cannot be angularly uniform}\label{sec:sparse}
Write $m=d-1$. Fix a cap constant $A_d\ge1$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap}
 \sigma\{\theta:\norm{\theta-u}\le r\}\le A_dr^m
 \quad(0<r\le1,\ u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}).
\end{equation}
For example, with $|\mathbb S^j|$ denoting surface area, one can use
\[
 A_d=\max\left\{1,
 \frac{|\mathbb S^{m-1}|}{m|\mathbb S^m|}
          \left(\frac\pi2\right)^m\right\}.
\]
Indeed a chordal radius $r$ has angular radius
$2\arcsin(r/2)\le\pi r/2$, and polar integration with $\sin t\le t$
proves the estimate. In dimension three, $A_3=1$ is a convenient
nonoptimal choice, since the exact cap mass is $r^2/4$.

\begin{lemma}[A scale-uniform sparse-mixture obstruction]
\label{lem:sparse-root}
Suppose $\norm Z\le1$, $Z$ has at most $k$ values, and
$\E\norm Z\ge\kappa$ for $0<\kappa\le1$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothing-scale}
 r=\left(\frac{\kappa}{16A_dk}\right)^{1/m},\qquad
 \tau=\frac{\kappa r}{8\sqrt{2d}},\qquad
 f=\Law(Z)*\gamma_\tau.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-root}
 \norm{\sqrt f-\Pi\sqrt f}_2^2\ge\frac{\kappa}{16}.
\end{equation}
The locations and masses of the atoms are arbitrary.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $L=\{\norm Z\ge\kappa/2\}$. Since
$\E\norm Z\le\kappa/2+\Pp(L)$, its probability is at least $\kappa/2$.
Around each direction of a nonzero atom satisfying $L$ place a spherical
cap of chordal radius $r$. Their union $\Omega$ satisfies
$\sigma(\Omega)\le kA_dr^m=\kappa/16$.

Let $G$ be independent standard Gaussian. Markov's inequality gives
$\Pp(\norm G\le\sqrt{2d})\ge1/2$. On this event and $L$,
\[
 \norm{\tau G}\le\kappa r/8,
 \qquad
 \left\|\frac{Z+\tau G}{\norm{Z+\tau G}}-
              \frac{Z}{\norm Z}\right\|
 \le\frac{2\norm{\tau G}}{\norm Z}\le r/2.
\]
The denominator is nonzero here because $r\le1$ and
$\norm{\tau G}\le\norm Z/4$. Thus the radial cone
$C_\Omega=\{s\theta:s>0,\theta\in\Omega\}$ has
$\int_{C_\Omega}f\ge\kappa/4$.

Write $u=\Pi\sqrt f$. This is radial, and $\norm u_2\le1$.
Polar integration yields
$\int_{C_\Omega}u^2=\sigma(\Omega)\norm u_2^2\le\kappa/16$.
The triangle inequality in $L^2(C_\Omega)$ now gives
\[
 \norm{\sqrt f-u}_2
 \ge\sqrt{\kappa/4}-\sqrt{\kappa/16}=\frac{\sqrt\kappa}{4}.
\]
Squaring proves the assertion. Repeated atom directions and zero atoms
only reduce the number of caps. No regularity or separation assumption
on the centroid locations was used.
\end{proof}

The cap scale in this lemma is the classical $k^{-1/m}$ quantization
scale. To fix the comparison precisely, for a sphere probability
measure $\nu$ its $k$-center cosine distortion is
\[
 \inf_{u_1,\ldots,u_k\in\mathbb S^m}
 \int\min_i(1-\langle v,u_i\rangle)\,d\nu(v)
 =\frac12\inf_{u_1,\ldots,u_k\in\mathbb S^m}
 \int\min_i\norm{v-u_i}^2\,d\nu(v).
\]
Repeated centers are permitted. For surface measure its order is
$k^{-2/m}$; the cap lower estimate and a covering upper estimate already
prove that order. This is part of classical quadratic quantization,
not a new geometric exponent; see Graf--Luschgy \cite{GL} and the
manifold extension of Iacobelli \cite[Theorem 1.4]{Iac}.
The quantity in \eqref{eq:sparse-root} is different: it is an angular
Hellinger obstruction for a \emph{Gaussian-smoothed} finite register.
Combined with the one-step entropy increment, it need not wait for a
word product to approximate Haar measure at this scale.
