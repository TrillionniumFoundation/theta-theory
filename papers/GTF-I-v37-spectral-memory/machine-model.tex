\section{The experiment and its registers}\label{sec:model}
\begin{definition}[Clocked stochastic realization]\label{def:machine}
For a prescribed horizon $N$, a machine consists of finite alphabets
$[K_t]$, $0\le t\le N$, initial distributions $E_x$, row-stochastic
matrices $T_{t,a}$ of size $K_t\times K_{t+1}$, and columns
$d_j\in[-1,1]^{K_N}$. Its response mean is
$E_xT_{0,a_1}\cdots T_{N-1,a_N}d_j$. Its error is one half the maximum
absolute discrepancy from $\rho e_j^TU_wx$ over the finite set of seeds,
words and queries. Its peak is $\max_tK_t$, also including two labels
if the final binary answer is retained at a charged cut. The minimum
peak at error $\varepsilon$ is $W_{N,\varepsilon}$.
\end{definition}

The seed and query are each supplied once. The current command is consumed
in its update. Only the updated register survives; a retained seed,
input transcript, random seed, or data-dependent scheduling variable
would be part of that register. Available, not merely reachable, labels
are counted; padding therefore gives a larger admissible profile. Every
unused row is normalized. The epoch, horizon, row tables, their construction,
lookup and arithmetic, and exact atomic stochastic sampling are free.
A machine may be redesigned for every $N$ and need not use common rows.
This is a nonuniform probabilistic ordered read-once width, not total
program size, autonomous state count or uniform computational space.

All converse distributions below are imposed on the external word,
independently of private randomness. Wordwise correctness licenses this
choice; the test law is not additional runtime information. The commands
in this paper's new converse are independent, with a possibly different
law at each epoch. No independence of the hidden past given its current
state is assumed.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional moments and calibration]\label{lem:terminal}
Fix a unit seed $x$. Let the fresh command $a_{t+1}$ be independent of the
past and let private updates obey the stated Markov rule. Define
\[
 Z_t=\E[Y_t\mid S_t],\quad q_t=\E\norm{Z_t},\quad
 v_t=\E\norm{Z_t}^2,\qquad Y_t=U_{a_t}\cdots U_{a_1}x.
\]
Then $\norm{Z_t}\le1$, $q_t$ and $v_t$ are nonincreasing, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:martingale-update}
 Z_{t+1}=\E[U_{a_{t+1}}Z_t\mid S_{t+1}],\qquad
 \Delta_t:=v_t-v_{t+1}=\E\norm{U_{a_{t+1}}Z_t-Z_{t+1}}^2.
\end{equation}
In particular $\sum_{t<N}\Delta_t\le1$. If the machine has wordwise
error at most $\varepsilon$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:calibration}
 q_t\ge q_N\ge\kappa:=\rho/\sqrt d-2\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Conditioning on $S_t,a_{t+1}$ and its randomized successor does not change
the conditional mean of $Y_t$: the new command is independent of the
past and the update uses that past only through $S_t$. The tower property
gives \eqref{eq:martingale-update}. Conditional Jensen and the
orthogonality of $U_a$ give both monotonicities. The orthogonal projection
identity for conditional expectation gives the equality for $\Delta_t$.
Initially $Z_0=x$, even when initialization randomizes the register.

In the proof only, combine the separate decoder columns as
\[D(S_N)=(d_1(S_N),\ldots,d_d(S_N)),\qquad\norm{D(S_N)}\le\sqrt d.\]
For every word its conditional mean is within $2\sqrt d\varepsilon$
of $\rho Y_N$. Hence
\[
 \rho-2\sqrt d\varepsilon
 \le\E\langle Y_N,D(S_N)\rangle
 =\E\langle Z_N,D(S_N)\rangle\le\sqrt d q_N.
\]
This does not require jointly sampling multiple answers. The vector
combines different permitted final query columns only in the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The isolated-cut minimum]\label{prop:separate}
If $\Id\in\mathcal A$ and $d\rho<1$, the normalized residual rank and
separate positive minimum at every internal cut equal $d+1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The residuals are affine functions of $\rho U_wx$. Idle prefixes from
$\pm e_j$ span $\R^d$, and idle suffixes with coordinate queries separate
these coordinates. Normalization adds an independent constant direction,
so the rank is $d+1$. A regular simplex with inradius $\rho$ has
circumradius $d\rho<1$ and contains every reachable predictive vector.
Its vertices therefore define legal binary response channels after every
orthogonal suffix. Decompose the selected-cut vector into those vertices.
One may store the raw prefix before this cut and the raw suffix together
with the simplex label after it. Those other state sets are finite and
uncapped. Thus this is a factorization within a complete machine. Rank
gives the matching lower bound. This proposition, inherited from
\cite{GTF36}, does not assert simultaneous attainment.
\end{proof}
