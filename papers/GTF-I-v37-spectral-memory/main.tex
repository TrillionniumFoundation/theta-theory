\begin{abstract}
Let a fixed finite family of orthogonal commands have an absolute spectral
gap on the full mean-zero $L^2$ space of the sphere. We prove that its
wordwise numerical behavior over $N$ commands requires and admits
$\Theta(N^{(d-1)/2})$ persistent classical labels, at zero error and at
every fixed sufficiently small output error. The lower bound permits
arbitrary hidden states, horizon-dependent design and unrelated rows at
different epochs. It removes the logarithmic loss of a packet-mixing
argument. The proof compares entropy production under random rotations
with the entropy cost of conditional-mean compression after Gaussian
smoothing; the latter is charged to one telescoping quadratic loss.
A weighted occupation theorem also permits time-dependent command laws
and unbounded widths at the other cuts. For five explicit rational
three-dimensional commands, the classical width is consequently
$\Theta(N)$, or $\log_2N+O(1)$ persistent label bits, while a fixed qubit
realizes the same numerical probabilities. The external spectral-gap
input is qualitative, not a numerical gap certificate. Our resource is
nonuniform clocked atomic-row width. A separate padded fair-bit compiler
has no update-duration side channel and does not identify this width
with uniform computational space.
\end{abstract}
