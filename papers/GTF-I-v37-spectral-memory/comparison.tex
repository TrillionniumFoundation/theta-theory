\section{Comparisons and scope}\label{sec:comparison}
\subsection{Quantization, smoothing and the new accumulated estimate}
Classical quadratic quantization minimizes the expected squared distance
to a finite codebook. The cosine loss on a sphere is exactly one half of
the squared chordal distance. Graf--Luschgy \cite{GL} develops the general
quantization framework, and Iacobelli \cite[Theorem 1.4]{Iac} proves the
corresponding manifold asymptotics under explicit integrability
hypotheses. On the compact sphere these give the familiar exponent
$2/(d-1)$. Neither this geometric exponent nor the cap argument used
here is a priority claim. Our lower bound concerns a sequence of
\emph{hidden conditional centroids}, not independently selected nearest
neighbors in a static codebook.

Gaussian smoothing and entropy continuity are also established tools.
The bound in \cite[Corollary 4]{PW} applies to unrelated smoothed laws
and is linear in an appropriate Wasserstein distance. Lemma~\ref{lem:centroid-entropy}
uses the particular conditional-mean coupling and a semiconvex Gaussian
log density to remove the first-order term. We do not assert that this
elementary Gaussian identity is absent from the literature. Its role
here is to make the entire cost bounded by one quadratic variation
budget, rather than by the sum of square roots of individual costs.

The entropy increment in Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-gap} uses the standard
relative-entropy/Hellinger comparison and an $L^2$ spectral bound, not
a logarithmic Sobolev assumption. Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-root} gives a
uniform positive angular defect after smoothing at the register's
resolution. The conclusion that combines these ingredients is
Theorem~\ref{thm:occupation}: a finite horizon with arbitrarily varying
hidden implementations has a gap-weighted small-register occupation
of order at most $k^{2/(d-1)}$. That inequality, rather than a new
quantization exponent or expansion theorem, is the main assertion.

\subsection{Positive realization and branching programs}
After padding layer alphabets, the model is a probabilistic ordered
read-once layered program, with command-dependent row-stochastic
matrices and a bounded real output. The scalar decoder for one query
can be turned into randomized Boolean acceptance by one Bernoulli row.
The total number of layer nodes and transition-table size are not the
maximum width. In particular the result is not a lower bound in a
uniform algorithmic-space model.

Fourier-growth and pseudorandomness results for regular, permutation
and width-three branching programs \cite{RSV,SVW,LPV} use their stated
transition assumptions and control Boolean-input Fourier coefficients.
Their regularity is not implied by row stochasticity: a reset matrix
with every row $(1,0,\ldots,0)$ does not preserve the uniform state law.
Some bounds for unrestricted deterministic tables do extend to random
tables by convexity, so randomness alone is not an originality boundary.
Here the assumed gap is on the external sphere action, whereas the
arbitrary hidden matrices need have no gap or regularity. The converse
uses the entropy of a smoothed conditional orbit mean, not an estimate
on the Fourier mass of a Boolean acceptance function. We make no claim
of an exhaustive branching-program priority classification.

Earlier positive-realization mechanisms and the packet theorem remain
useful \cite{GTF35,GTF36}. In particular the full-word endpoint kernel
must be allowed to distinguish different spellings of the same product.
The present one-step proof uses actual independent command letters,
not a product-sufficiency reduction. No previously corrected packet
quantifier is relaxed.

\subsection{Which quantum memory is compared}
Quantum advantages for process simulation precede this work. Gu et al.
\cite{Gu} construct models with smaller stored entropy for classical
stochastic processes. Thompson et al.\ \cite{Thompson} also treat
\emph{input--output} processes, not only autonomous outputs: their
framework includes stationary input-dependent memory entropy and its
worst-input counterpart. Our claim must not be distinguished from that
work merely by the presence of input commands. The distinction here is
finite-horizon worst-word numerical error, arbitrary horizon-specific
classical tables, and maximum available label count; we do not minimize
stationary memory entropy.

Ghafari et al.\ \cite{Ghafari} demonstrate a memory-dimension advantage
for stochastic process simulation. Thus dimension, as well as entropy,
has established quantum-memory comparisons. Proposition~\ref{prop:qubit}
is an explicit driven family whose \emph{classical} cardinality has a
matched growth law in the horizon at fixed positive error. It is not a
first claim of quantum dimension advantage. The recent world-model
comparison \cite{LMTG} is also relevant; its process and update
requirements are not replaced by our nonuniform one-query convention.

For strict cutpoints, Chen--Wu \cite[Theorem 3.2]{CW26a} gives a
classical PFA with at most $n^2+1$ states for a general $n$-dimensional
one-way quantum automaton. The conversion preserves a threshold sign
while allowing a length-dependent shrinking numerical margin
\cite[Proposition 3.1]{CW26a}. It therefore does not preserve the fixed
terminal correlation used here. The result in \eqref{eq:rational-rate}
does not contradict or improve that theorem in its own language metric.

\subsection{Conclusions}
The full-sphere spectral-gap class has matched exact and fixed-error
width $\Theta(N^{(d-1)/2})$. The explicit three-dimensional gate family
has linear classical label count and logarithmic classical label bits.
The proof allows hidden directions, cut-dependent rows, and fixed
positive error, and it does not repeat a mixing-time charge.

The constants in the occupation theorem are not optimized. Neither an
exact leading asymptotic constant nor every finite integer optimum is
identified. A numerical lower constant for the displayed gates still
requires a certified spectral-gap value. Arbitrary compact actions
without the assumed sphere gap, all irrational cyclic arithmetic,
multiple jointly constrained queries and adaptive experimental design
are different problems. The retained monomial and cyclic theorems keep
their own hypotheses in the supporting article.

Finally, nonuniform atomic-row width and its padded finite-coin
implementation are different resources. The latter has the additional
control factor proved below. Both leave table construction and storage
outside the counted register, and neither is represented as a uniform
space bound. The complete predecessor development is preserved separately;
it is not needed to validate the new proof or part of the editorial
claim for this article.
