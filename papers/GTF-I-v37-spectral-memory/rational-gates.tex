\section{A sharp rational example and its quantum realization}
\label{sec:rational}
Use the fixed five-letter alphabet
$\mathcal A_* =\{\Id,R_x,R_x^{-1},R_z,R_z^{-1}\}$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gates}
 R_x=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&-7/25&-24/25\\0&24/25&-7/25\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 R_z=\begin{pmatrix}-7/25&-24/25&0\\24/25&-7/25&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
These are the rational commands from \cite{GTF36}. Take $\rho=1/10$,
six seeds $\{\pm e_j:1\le j\le3\}$ and three final coordinate queries.
No alphabet grows with the horizon.

\begin{theorem}[Linear classical label complexity]\label{thm:rational}
For every fixed $0\le\varepsilon<1/(20\sqrt3)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-rate}
 W_{N,\varepsilon}(\mathcal A_*)=\Theta_\varepsilon(N),\qquad
 \left\lceil\log_2 W_{N,\varepsilon}(\mathcal A_*)\right\rceil
                =\log_2N+O_\varepsilon(1).
\end{equation}
The exact upper machine may have rational common command rows.
The separate positive minimum at each cut is four. If $\lambda_*<1$
is the norm-gap constant for the walk with probabilities
$(1/2,1/8,1/8,1/8,1/8)$, every such machine has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-explicit}
 K\ge\frac{(1-\lambda_*^2)
                (1/(10\sqrt3)-2\varepsilon)^4}{98304}\,N.
\end{equation}
The existence of $\lambda_*<1$ is an external spectral-gap input; its
numerical value is not evaluated here.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z$ be the Pauli matrices and define
$A_x=(3I-4i\sigma_x)/5$, $A_z=(3I-4i\sigma_z)/5$ in $\SU(2)$.
Their adjoint actions are \eqref{eq:gates}. Their eigenphases include
$(3+4i)/5$, which is not a root of unity: its sum with its inverse is
$6/5$, not an integer, whereas a rational algebraic integer is an integer.
The closures of the respective cyclic subgroups therefore contain the
two axial circles. The Lie algebra generated by $i\sigma_x,i\sigma_z$
also contains their bracket, a nonzero multiple of $i\sigma_y$.
The closed generated subgroup is $\SU(2)$.

The generators have algebraic entries. Bourgain--Gamburd
\cite[Theorem 1]{BG12} supplies the upper spectral gap for their symmetric
averaging operator on $L^2(\SU(2))$. Adding weight $1/2$ at the identity
makes its spectrum nonnegative and its mean-zero norm strictly less than
one. Under the left action, lift a sphere function by
$f(v)\mapsto f(\operatorname{Ad}_g e_3)$. This is an isometry from the
homogeneous quotient $L^2(\mathbb S^2)$ into $L^2(\SU(2))$ and
intertwines the left pullbacks. Thus the sphere operator has the same
norm bound; inverse conventions are harmless for this symmetric walk.

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and use $A_3=1$ in
\eqref{eq:constants}, giving $a_3=4$ and $B_3=98304$.
This proves \eqref{eq:rational-explicit} and the matching upper order.
Rational sparse rows follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:net-upper}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:separate} gives four. Taking binary logarithms
proves the bit statement, including the integer ceiling.
\end{proof}

The rationality and alphabet are explicit, but a numerical finite-horizon
lower threshold requires an additional certified bound on $\lambda_*$.
We provide neither such a certificate nor a claim that finite-dimensional
matrix eigenvalues establish the infinite-dimensional gap. Formula
\eqref{eq:rational-explicit} gives the full dependence on any supplied
gap certificate. The new conclusion here is removal of the logarithmic
factor from the classical label count, not a new construction of expanding
generators.

\begin{proposition}[One qubit with reset, unitary commands and final query]
\label{prop:qubit}
One qubit realizes \eqref{eq:law} for \eqref{eq:gates} exactly, using a
fixed finite collection of operations independent of $N$. Thus its quantum
memory dimension is two, whereas its classical atomic label complexity
and label bits satisfy \eqref{eq:rational-rate} even at the stated fixed
positive error. This is a numerical-behavior comparison, not a
strict-cutpoint language-state separation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On seed $x$ reset the qubit to
$\varrho_x=(I+\rho\sum_jx_j\sigma_j)/2$. Its eigenvalues are positive
because $\rho<1$. Commands apply the unitaries
$I,A_x^{\pm1},A_z^{\pm1}$. The Bloch vector after the word is $\rho U_wx$.
Measurement of $\sigma_j$ has outcome $b$ with probability
$(1+b\rho e_j^TU_wx)/2$. Only one final query is made. A seed reset is a
completely positive operation; it is not being called a unitary
measure-once transition. The classical statement is
Theorem~\ref{thm:rational}.
\end{proof}

The same qubit operations appeared in the preceding revision. What is
strengthened is the matching classical cost. In particular this is one
qubit versus $\log_2N+O(1)$ classical \emph{label bits}, not one qubit
versus linear bit space or an entropy lower bound for a chosen stationary
input distribution.
