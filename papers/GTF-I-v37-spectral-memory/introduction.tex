\section{Introduction}
A positive factorization at one time does not specify a stochastic machine
that remains valid at all later times. This distinction becomes quantitative
when a finite register must preserve an orthogonal orbit under every
command word. At each isolated cut a fixed-dimensional simplex can encode
the observable vector, yet its successive positive realizations need not
be compatible. The problem is to determine the number of labels required
by one complete causal realization, with all hidden directions allowed.

Fix $U_a\in O(d)$ for $a$ in a finite alphabet $\mathcal A$,
seeds $x\in X=\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_d\}$ and signal
$0<\rho\le1/10$. The last command acts on the left:
$U_w=U_{a_N}\cdots U_{a_1}$. A query $j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}$ is supplied
only after the $N$ commands. The required binary response is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
 \Pp(B=b\mid x,w,j)=\frac{1+b\rho e_j^TU_wx}{2},\qquad b\in\{-1,1\}.
\end{equation}
All probabilities lie in $[9/20,11/20]$. Equality, or the stated uniform
error, is required on every word, not merely under a stationary input law.
Let $W_{N,\varepsilon}$ be the least maximum number of persistent labels
with binary row total-variation error at most $\varepsilon$; the complete
machine convention is Definition~\ref{def:machine}.

For a probability vector $p$ on $\mathcal A$, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-intro}
 Pf(v)=\sum_a p(a)f(U_a^{-1}v),\qquad
 \norm{P|_{L^2_0(\sigma)}}\le\lambda<1,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is normalized surface measure on $\mathbb S^{d-1}$.
The inverse convention makes $P$ also the density transfer operator.
The same norm condition holds for its adjoint. It is a hypothesis on
\emph{all} spherical harmonics, not the spectrum of the defining
$d$-dimensional representation.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp finite-alphabet width]\label{thm:main}
Suppose \eqref{eq:gap-intro} holds and
$0\le\varepsilon<\rho/(2\sqrt d)$. Then, for constants depending only
on $d,\lambda,\rho,\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
 cN^{(d-1)/2}\le W_{N,\varepsilon}
       \le W_{N,0}\le C_dN^{(d-1)/2}\qquad(N\ge1).
\end{equation}
The lower bound allows arbitrary hidden state continuations and
cut-dependent stochastic rows. The upper realization has common command
rows and an $N$-dependent terminal decoder. For rational commands,
seeds and signal, its rows can all be rational and each update has at
most $d+1$ successors. If the identity is available and $d\rho<1$,
every isolated cut nevertheless has positive minimum $d+1$.
\end{theorem}

The preceding finite-alphabet result \cite{GTF36} gave lower order
$(N/\log(N+1))^{(d-1)/2}$ and the same upper construction. Its converse
waited $O(\log k)$ command slots for the product distribution to become
uniform to the resolution of a $k$-center quantizer. The proof here does
not shorten that mixing time or assert one-step total-variation mixing
of an atomic walk. It retains an entropy potential across \emph{all}
updates, so that repeated mixing need not be purchased afresh.

Put $Y_t=U_{a_t}\cdots U_{a_1}x$ and
$Z_t=\E[Y_t\mid S_t]$. The auxiliary Gaussian density
$f_t=\Law(Z_t)*\gamma_\tau$ is used only in the proof.
Randomizing a command increases its entropy by an angular Hellinger
variance times the spectral gap. Replacing the resulting vector by a
conditional mean decreases entropy by at most
\[
 \frac{\E\norm{Z_t}^2-\E\norm{Z_{t+1}}^2}{2\tau^2}.
\]
This cost telescopes. If $Z_t$ has only $k$ possible values but has
nonvanishing mean norm, smoothing at scale $\tau\asymp k^{-1/(d-1)}$
still leaves a definite angular Hellinger variance. These statements
combine to give an $O(k^{2/(d-1)})$ occupation bound, with no logarithmic
factor. No noise is added to the actual command experiment.

The five rational gates of \cite{GTF36} consequently have exact and
fixed-error classical width $\Theta(N)$. Their one-qubit realization is
unchanged. This is a separation in worst-case numerical behavior under
externally supplied words: the classical label bits are
$\log_2N+O(1)$, not linear bit space. The gap for these gates is supplied
by Bourgain--Gamburd \cite{BG12}; we do not give a numerical gap value.

The new results are the one-step entropy comparison, the gap-weighted
occupation law and its sharp width consequences. Rational spherical
synthesis and the qubit operations are inherited and reproved. Spectral
gaps, Gaussian score identities, Hellinger comparison and spherical
quantization are classical inputs. Appendix~\ref{sec:coins} repairs the
implementation timing convention; it is not counted as a new sampling
algorithm. Section~\ref{sec:comparison} gives the corresponding theorem
comparisons. The prior packet, monomial and cyclic results remain intact
in the supporting article rather than being silently weakened or used
under altered hypotheses.
