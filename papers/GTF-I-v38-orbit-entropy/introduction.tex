\section{Introduction}
A finite probabilistic device can preserve a linear statistic through a
long sequence of transformations without retaining the underlying point.
Consequently the dimension of the statistic does not determine the size
of an exact positive realization. The distinction is particularly visible
when the transformation group expands functions on its orbits. Mixing
creates entropy, while replacement by finitely many conditional means
removes it. An occupation inequality can compare these operations without
assigning an observable vector to each hidden basis state.

The spherical form of this argument gives the order
$N^{(d-1)/2}$ under a full spherical spectral gap \cite{GTF37}. Two issues
arise beyond the sphere. First, invariant densities need not be radial.
Second, a conditional mean of orbit points need not be another orbit
point. The second issue is decisive for unitary conjugation: a conditional
mean of pure states usually has several nontrivial eigenvalues. Applying
a pure-state cap estimate directly to these means would be invalid.

We resolve both issues through orbital averaging on the whole ambient
representation and a spectral-projector estimate uniform over all
traceless Hermitian directions. The resulting exponent is intrinsic to
the smallest nontrivial conjugation orbit, rather than the dimension of
the ambient sphere. A construction on complex projective space matches
this lower exponent. Thus the theorem is not obtained by substituting
$q^2-1$ into a spherical formula.

\subsection{Principal statements}
Let $G$ act orthogonally on $V=\R^D$. Write $\Pi_G$ for Haar averaging
and suppose that a finitely supported command law has squared norm gap
$g>0$ away from $\Pi_G$ on $L^2(V)$. Assume, for $s>0$ and $A\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-smallball}
 \sup_{u,v\in\mathbb S(V)}
 m_G\{a:\norm{a u-v}\le r\}\le A r^s\qquad(0<r\le1).
\end{equation}
The input word is universally quantified; the command law is chosen only
for the lower proof. Theorem~\ref{thm:occupation} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-budget}
 \sum_{t:K_t\le k}g_t
 \le 2048D(16A)^{2/s}\kappa^{-3-2/s}k^{2/s},
\end{equation}
whenever a terminal correlation forces $\E\norm{\E[Y_N\mid S_N]}\ge\kappa$.
Widths at the other epochs are unrestricted. The gap may vary with time.

For $q\ge2$, put $D=q^2-1$ and consider the normalized pure-state orbit
in $\Herm(q)$, equipped with its Hilbert--Schmidt inner product.
A fixed finite unitary alphabet drives this orbit. One final query asks
for a coordinate of the resulting vector, with fixed amplitude
$0<\rho\le1/10$.
\begin{theorem}[Projective-orbit memory order]\label{thm:main}
Suppose the command alphabet supports a probability measure with a norm
gap on $L^2_0(\SU(q))$. For every fixed
$0\le\varepsilon<\rho/(2\sqrt{q^2-1})$, the least classical width satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:headline}
 W_{N,\varepsilon}=\Theta_{q,\rho,\varepsilon,\mu}(N^{q-1}).
\end{equation}
The lower bound permits arbitrary hidden states, time-dependent rows and
horizon-dependent design. The exact upper construction uses common
command rows and an $N$-dependent final decoder. There are explicit finite
algebraic alphabets satisfying the hypothesis for every $q$. The behavior
has a fixed quantum realization of dimension $q$; its classical label
bits are $(q-1)\log_2 N+O(1)$.
\end{theorem}
The proof, including the alphabet and the fixed quantum device, is in
Section~\ref{sec:projective}. For $q>2$ the exponent $q-1$ differs from
the ambient-sphere exponent $(q^2-2)/2$. The group-gap hypothesis here is
qualitative; its existence follows from an external expansion theorem.
It is not inferred merely from density of the generated subgroup.

A second application addresses effective constants. Section~\ref{sec:effective}
uses the classical six $V$-gates, whose adjoint rotations have rational
entries. The Lubotzky--Phillips--Sarnak bound gives a certified squared
gap $4/9$ on the sphere. For amplitude $1/10$ the explicit consequence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-effective}
 W_{N,\varepsilon}\ge
 \frac{(1/(10\sqrt3)-2\varepsilon)^4}{221184}\,N.
\end{equation}
This is a different alphabet from the earlier five-gate example, whose
numerical gap is not evaluated here. The arithmetic spectral estimate is
an external theorem, not a numerical truncation of spherical harmonics.

\subsection{Conventions and attribution}
All growth statements concern available persistent labels. The external
epoch, the horizon, read-only row tables, their construction, arithmetic
and exact atomic sampling are not charged. One query is selected after
the command word; no independent joint answers are required. A finite
random-bit implementation is a different resource problem.

\noindent\textbf{Notation.}
$N$ is the command horizon; $K_t$ is the available label count; $W$ is
its maximum. $D$ is the real ambient dimension and $s$ is the orbital
small-ball exponent. $q$ denotes quantum dimension, while $q_t$ below
denotes a conditional-centroid norm. Entropy and $\log$ use natural units;
$\log_2$ is reserved for label bits.
