\section{An effective six-command rational example}\label{sec:effective}
The preceding group-gap application is qualitative. We now choose a
separate arithmetic alphabet for which a classical theorem supplies a
numerical infinite-dimensional norm bound. This does not evaluate the
gap of the earlier five-command example.

Let $R_x,R_y,R_z$ be rotations about the coordinate axes with cosine
$-3/5$ and sine $4/5$; explicitly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:six-gates}
 R_x=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&-3/5&-4/5\\0&4/5&-3/5\end{pmatrix},\quad
 R_y=\begin{pmatrix}-3/5&0&4/5\\0&1&0\\-4/5&0&-3/5\end{pmatrix},\quad
 R_z=\begin{pmatrix}-3/5&-4/5&0\\4/5&-3/5&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Use the fixed six-letter alphabet
$\mathcal A_V=\{R_x^{\pm1},R_y^{\pm1},R_z^{\pm1}\}$ and its uniform
law. These are the adjoint rotations of the classical norm-five
quaternions $1\pm2\mathbf i$, $1\pm2\mathbf j$, $1\pm2\mathbf k$,
normalized by $\sqrt5$.

\begin{proposition}[External arithmetic gap certificate]\label{prop:LPS}
For $P_Vf=(1/6)\sum_{R\in\mathcal A_V}f(R^{-1}\cdot)$ on
$L^2_0(\mathbb S^2)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LPS-norm}
                 \norm{P_V}\le\frac{2\sqrt5}{6}=\frac{\sqrt5}{3}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $1-\norm{P_V}^2\ge4/9$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is the $p=5$ specialization of the Lubotzky--Phillips--Sarnak
spherical Hecke bound \cite{LPS86,LPS87}. In its unnormalized convention
the $p+1$ norm-$p$ quaternion representatives act by a sum of rotations
whose mean-zero norm is at most $2\sqrt p$. The representatives have
positive odd real coordinate and even other coordinates when
$p\equiv1\pmod4$; at $p=5$ they are exactly the six displayed ones.
The coauthor account \cite[Section 3.2, the $V$-gate example]{PS}
spells out these representatives and their Hecke interpretation.
The unnormalized spherical norm convention is also stated in
\cite[Theorem 3(ii)]{Pisier}. Dividing by six gives
\eqref{eq:LPS-norm}. Passing from quaternions to adjoint rotations
identifies $\mathbb S^2$ as the homogeneous quotient; inverse signs do
not affect this symmetric average. The arithmetic bound is invoked as
an external theorem, not reproved by finite harmonic tests.
\end{proof}

Use six seeds $\{\pm e_j:1\le j\le3\}$, amplitude $\rho=1/10$ and
three final coordinate queries in \eqref{eq:law}.
\begin{theorem}[Numerical finite-horizon width certificate]
\label{thm:effective}
For $0\le\varepsilon<1/(20\sqrt3)$ and every integer $N\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-both}
 \frac{(1/(10\sqrt3)-2\varepsilon)^4}{221184}\,N
          \le W_{N,\varepsilon}(\mathcal A_V)
          \le W_{N,0}(\mathcal A_V)<150N.
\end{equation}
The upper machine has exact rational command and decoder rows, common
command matrices, and an $N$-dependent decoder. At zero error every
internal selected cut has separate positive minimum four, while
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-zero}
 W_{N,0}\ge\frac{N}{19\,906\,560\,000}.
\end{equation}
The constants are proof constants, not sharp asymptotic constants.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the standard $\SO(3)$ action the orbit of every unit vector is
uniform on $\mathbb S^2$. A chordal cap of radius $r\le1$ has probability
$r^2/4\le r^2$, so take $D=3,s=2,A=1$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:occupation}. The sphere gap transfers to $L^2(\R^3)$
by polar coordinates. Thus $B_{3,1,2}=98304$, and the squared gap
$4/9$ gives the lower bound in \eqref{eq:effective-both}.
At $\varepsilon=0$ the fourth power of $1/(10\sqrt3)$ is $1/90000$,
which yields \eqref{eq:effective-zero}.

Here is an explicit cardinality estimate for the upper construction.
Take both rational stereographic charts
\[
 t\longmapsto\frac{(2t_1,2t_2,\pm(1-\norm t^2))}{1+\norm t^2},
 \qquad t\in[-1,1]^2,
\]
with a grid of step $1/M$ and $M=\lceil2\sqrt{2N}\rceil$.
Every hemisphere is covered by its chart on the unit disk. Each chart
is $2$-Lipschitz, so its grid images form a sphere net of chordal radius
at most $\sqrt2/M\le1/(2\sqrt N)$. Its convex hull contains
$\eta$ times the unit ball, with $\eta=1-1/(8N)$, by the support
function identity $u\cdot v=1-\norm{u-v}^2/2$.
There are at most $2(2M+1)^2\le(82+48\sqrt2)N<150N$ labels.
Use scales $a_t=\eta^{-t}/4$, initialization representing $\rho x$,
rows representing $\eta Ru$ in this hull, and decoder $a_N e_j\cdot u$.
The verification is the induction in Theorem~\ref{thm:projective-upper}.
All data are rational; each nonempty rational convex system has rational
weights, with at most four successors. The final decoder is bounded by
$2/7$.

At a selected internal cut, fix any common command prefix. The six seed
images affinely span $\R^3$, so the normalized continuation rank is
four; future coordinate queries still separate these dimensions after
any common suffix. For an upper factorization take a regular tetrahedron
containing the radius-$\rho$ ball and having circumradius $3\rho<1$.
Its four vectors give legal continuations under all future rotations.
One embeds this selected-cut factorization in a complete machine by
allowing uncapped raw histories elsewhere. Therefore the separate
minimum is exactly four. This does not give a four-state machine at
all cuts simultaneously.
\end{proof}
For example, \eqref{eq:effective-zero} exceeds four once
$N\ge79\,626\,240\,001$. The size of this number illustrates the
conservative constants; the benefit is that no unspecified spectral
constant is needed for this alphabet.

The associated qubit operations are
$(I-2i\sigma_j)/\sqrt5$ and their inverses, with seed Bloch vector
$x/10$ and final Pauli query. They realize the numerical experiment
exactly, independently of $N$. This quantum construction and the
arithmetic gates are classical; \eqref{eq:effective-both} is the
finite-register consequence. The earlier five-gate alphabet remains
qualitatively gapped with a non-evaluated constant, and is retained
unchanged in the supporting material.
