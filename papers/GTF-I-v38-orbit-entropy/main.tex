\begin{abstract}
We prove a finite-memory occupation theorem for orthogonal representations
of compact groups. A norm gap away from invariant functions and a uniform
orbital small-ball bound of exponent $s$ force the gap-weighted number of
$k$-state epochs to be $O(k^{2/s})$. The competing transducer may have
arbitrary hidden states and unrelated stochastic rows at every epoch.
The small-ball hypothesis is required on the directions of all possible
conditional means, not only on the original orbit. For conjugation on
traceless Hermitian $q$ by $q$ matrices we establish it uniformly over all
spectra with exponent $2(q-1)$. A projective-net construction then gives
matching $\Theta(N^{q-1})$ classical labels for finite algebraic unitary
alphabets with a full group spectral gap, at exact output and at every
fixed sufficiently small wordwise error. The same numerical behavior
has a fixed $q$-dimensional quantum realization. We also give a separate
six-command rational three-dimensional example with an analytic numerical
gap certificate from the classical Ramanujan bound, yielding an explicit
finite-horizon lower constant. The resource is nonuniform clocked
atomic-row width, not uniform computational space. Gaussian entropy
identities, classical expansion and coding antecedents are distinguished
from the orbital occupation and synthesis conclusions.
\end{abstract}
