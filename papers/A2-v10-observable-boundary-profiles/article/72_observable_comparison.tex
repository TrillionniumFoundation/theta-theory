
The whole-profile count theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-acquisition},
occupies a different place from these finite-dimensional benchmarks.
It uses the same labelled scalar events as the relative boundary law,
not their endpoint positions, and supplies no exact finite-offset
probability formula to the estimator. The known normalization and
uniform relative estimates allow its finite-bridge bias to be separated
from scalar sampling noise. The positive-node regularization makes
recovery possible on the full collar without an observed derivative
oracle. Its preparation bound records both the cost of differentiating
noisy scalar data and the exponentially decreasing event mass. This
is the operational interpretation of the boundary energy invariant;
it neither subsumes nor borrows the lower bounds of the other experiments.
