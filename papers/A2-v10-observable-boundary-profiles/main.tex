\begin{abstract}
We establish nonlinear relative boundary laws for near-onset collision
records in smooth periodic dispersing billiards, without symmetry or
finite horizon assumptions. Stationary actions and normalized mixed
derivatives separate into half-line actions and relative determinant
amplitudes. Physical residual-time integration preserves this
factorization on a common closed offset collar with all fixed-order
geometric and offset derivatives. The two same-type scalar boundary
laws determine the complete normalized boundary energy profiles;
the inverse is Lipschitz from a specified third-derivative data norm
to the uniform profile norm. The mixed-type law satisfies an exact
Volterra compatibility identity, whose transformed finite jets have
rank one and whose finite-record defect decays exponentially.
On bounded smoothness classes, a regularized procedure recovers both
full profiles from finitely many selected-event binary reports. Its
explicit preparation bound accounts for finite-bridge bias, scalar
noise and the exponentially small success probability; normalization
and patch labels are supplied.
The law also yields rare-event-preserving comparison of endpoint
experiments and a joint small-offset testing profile with unequal
facing curvatures. In an explicit analytic physical family, four fixed
windows invert an unknown gap and coalescing curvature coordinates.
For bounded-flight binary designs, joint curvature and gap accuracies
$\varepsilon,\delta$ have optimal preparation order
$(\varepsilon^{-6}+\delta^{-2})\log(1/\eta)$.
For an unknown positive $C^m$ remainder envelope with strict interior
slack, the corresponding order is
$(\varepsilon^{-(6+6/m)}+\delta^{-2})\log(1/\eta)$.
We also establish contact-jet inverses, geometric realizations,
acquisition bounds and collision-record response formulas.
\end{abstract}

The independent referee reports of September 9, 2026 supplied the
quadratic support-overlap benchmark, the cubic physical count-testing
comparison, and the fixed-offset four-window construction. We thank the
referees for these calculations and for requesting that exact-family
inversion, unknown smooth remainders, and joint timing loss be
distinguished. Source identities and the detailed responses are
provided separately from this article. The subsequent smooth-envelope
splice, its sharp minimax comparison and the positive-window equality
were supplied in the AI referee-style memorandum~\cite{A2ReviewV8};
