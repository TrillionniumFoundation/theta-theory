\begin{abstract}
We study acquisition and whole-table certification for periodic dispersing
billiards from laboratory launches and intrinsic endpoint laws. Short
independent launches in a bounded neighborhood of an observed flight
produce unlabelled first-impact point clouds. A uniform collision-flux
minorization gives simultaneous confidence enclosures for all nearby
solids and for the complete boundaries of the encountered bodies.
Consequently boundary-value and distance-to-solid oracles can be replaced
by localized collision measurements. A support-value fingerprint of size
polynomial in the numerical geometric and separation bounds recognizes
bounded bridge classes, without analytic continuation. On a fixed
$C^{6,\beta}$ class with positive shape, symmetry and physical margins,
these observations and two growing endpoint histograms per record recover
the full period group and certify that no obstacle type is missing.
The certificate has a Lipschitz geometric error budget rather than a
continuation exponent. Revalidation of raw records at every epoch prevents
an early statistical misidentification from obstructing later discovery.
We give simultaneous stopping-time validity and finite expected total
launch cost, including the auxiliary geometric launches and solid failures.
The experiment requires resettable spatial launches and localized impacts;
it is not a passive single trajectory or a count-only observation.
All earlier analytic, local-probe, moment and relative-law results are retained.
\end{abstract}
