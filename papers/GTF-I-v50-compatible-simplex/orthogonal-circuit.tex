\section{A three-epoch orthogonal circuit beyond separate flattenings}
\label{sec:orthogonal50}
The cubic table in Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic49} can be realized by a
noncommuting rational orthogonal alphabet with three actual command
epochs. This is a selected-query experiment: only the coordinate
specified below is available, not all coordinates of the ambient space.
Its dimension is nine. No fixed-dimensional universality claim is made.

Index eight orthonormal vectors by $z\in\{0,1\}^3$ and a ninth by $*$.
For $a\in\{0,1\}$ let the permutation matrix $U_a$ fix $e_*$ and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:permutation50}
 U_ae_{(z_1,z_2,z_3)}=e_{(z_2,z_3,z_1\mathbin\oplus a)}.
\end{equation}
These are rational orthogonal matrices. They do not commute:
$U_0U_1e_{000}=e_{010}$, whereas $U_1U_0e_{000}=e_{001}$.
Put $F_z=1/10,2/5,4/5,2/5$ according as $|z|=0,1,2,3$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:seed50}
 c=\frac{200}{357},\qquad x_z=cF_z,\qquad x_*=-\frac{157}{357}.
\end{equation}
The seed set is $\{x,-x\}$, the horizon is three, and the sole available
query is $e_{000}$. The target mean is
$\rho\langle e_{000},U_{a_3}U_{a_2}U_{a_1}(\pm x)\rangle$.

\begin{theorem}[An orthogonal cubic optimum]\label{thm:orthogonal50}
The vector $x$ is a rational unit vector. For every $0<\rho\le1$,
the optimum binary-TV error among machines with two labels at all
four command cuts is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orthogonal-opt50}
 \frac{100\rho}{357}\,t,
 \qquad 500t^3-375t^2+540t-124=0,
 \quad\frac{13}{50}<t<\frac{261}{1000}.
\end{equation}
Algebraic stochastic rows attain it. When $\rho$ is rational, no machine
whose initialization, transitions and decoder are all rational attains
it, although rational machines approach it arbitrarily closely.
Every separate matricization of its positive-seed three-mode response
tensor admits a bounded rank-one approximation with mean error at most
$c\rho/5$, strictly smaller than the true mean optimum $c\rho t$.
Thus separate flattening bounds cannot certify this optimum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
One has $\sum_zF_z^2=257/100$, and
\[
 \sum_z x_z^2+x_*^2
 =\frac{40000(257/100)+157^2}{357^2}=1.
\]
Three applications of \eqref{eq:permutation50} send $e_z$ to
$e_{z\oplus(a_1,a_2,a_3)}$. Consequently the positive-seed mean is
exactly $c\rho F_{a_1a_2a_3}$. The scalar odd normal form, proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar47}, makes every legal two-state odd response a
bounded decomposable three-mode tensor. The seed and decoder scalars
can be absorbed into one mode, so the lower bound applies to every
machine, not merely to centered transition rows.

For $\lambda=c\rho$ the five entries
$000,011,101,110,111$ give the identity
\[
 g_{011}g_{101}g_{110}=g_{000}g_{111}^{\,2}.
\]
After replacing factors by their absolute values, a mean tolerance
$\delta<4\lambda/5$ must therefore obey the scaled inequality
\[
 (4\lambda/5-\delta)^3
       \le(\lambda/10+\delta)(2\lambda/5+\delta)^2.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic49} implies $\delta\ge\lambda t$; larger
$\delta$ already satisfy this bound. For attainment take
$u_0=(1/10+t)^{1/3}$ and $u_1=(2/5+t)^{1/3}$, always the positive
real cube roots. Initialize with signed coordinate $\pm\lambda$,
use the slope row $T(u_a)$ at each of the three epochs, and use
terminal decoder $(1,-1)$. The resulting response is
$\pm\lambda\prod_{r=1}^3u_{a_r}$. Its maximum mean error is
$\lambda t$ by the cubic identity and the weight-one estimate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic49}. All factors lie in $[0,1]$.

The integer cubic is primitive and irreducible modulo seven, as checked
in the cited theorem. Rational $\rho>0$ therefore gives an irrational
optimum. Every all-rational finite machine has rational responses and
rational maximum error against this rational target, proving
nonattainment. Continuity under rational approximation of finitely
many stochastic rows proves approximation from above.

Finally, by symmetry each nontrivial matricization of $F$ is, up to
row/column permutations or transpose,
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1/10&2/5&2/5&4/5\\
                 2/5&4/5&4/5&2/5\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The matrix with both rows $(1/4,3/5,3/5,3/5)$ has rank one, factors
bounded by one, and maximum entry error $1/5$. Scaling by $\lambda$
preserves boundedness. Since $t>13/50>1/5$, the claimed strict gap
holds simultaneously for every separate flattening. The flattening
approximants need not arise from one common tensor; requiring that
compatibility is exactly the additional constraint.
\end{proof}

The displayed monomial relation is a Segre lattice circuit, not a new
algebraic identity. The conclusion is stronger than observing that a
particular determinant is nonzero: it computes the exact error of a
complete, noncommuting, multi-epoch orthogonal experiment while all its
individual matrix relaxations have smaller error. The stochastic
compiler is essential to its upper bound.

\begin{proposition}[Finite word-table embedding]\label{prop:embedding50}
For any rational table $F:\{0,1\}^n\to\Q$, $n\ge1$, there are rational
orthogonal commands on $\R^{2^n+1}$, rational unit seeds $\pm x$, one
coordinate query and $n$ epochs whose positive-seed response is
$\rho cF_w$, with $c=2/(1+\sum_wF_w^2)$. The construction is explicit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the cyclic shift-and-xor permutation on $n$ bits, fixing an extra
coordinate. Put $x_w=cF_w$ and
$x_*=(1-\sum_wF_w^2)/(1+\sum_wF_w^2)$. Direct expansion gives norm
one. After $n$ commands the permutation is xor by the command word,
so the zero coordinate reads $x_w$. In particular every target
coordinate is bounded by one, without any assumption on the size of
$F_w$. For $n=1$ no noncommutativity is asserted.
\end{proof}
The dimension is exponential in $n$ and is explicitly part of the
input. This embedding transfers finite-table examples; it is not an
efficient construction for a succinct long-horizon process.
