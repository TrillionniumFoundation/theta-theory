# Literature and novelty audit — v50

Date: 2026-09-27. This is a targeted primary-source comparison, not independent exhaustive priority certification.

**Kahle, Kubjas, Kummer, Rosen.** *The geometry of rank-one tensor completion*, SIAM Journal on Applied Algebra and Geometry 1 (2017), 200–221; arXiv:1605.01678v2; DOI 10.1137/16M1074102. The author manuscript, Section 2 (Proposition 2.2, Remarks 2.3–2.5 and Proposition 2.7), was consulted directly, including the PDF page containing the circuit/sign discussion. Its Segre exponent, lattice, real-sign and zero-support structures are antecedents, not discoveries of this paper. The added problem is the complete bounded moving absolute-error box together with stochastic compilation. The three-epoch example uses a classical circuit to establish a new controlled-interface conclusion; it does not claim a new circuit identity.

**Benvenuti and Farina.** *A tutorial on the positive realization problem*, IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 49 (2004), 651–664. The primary manuscript's invariant-cone and MIMO discussion was compared directly. Invariant positive cones and positive-realization order versus linear rank are classical. Revision 50's necessity argument uses equality of ranks at particular clocked controlled cuts to eliminate hidden suffix directions; it does not extend that elimination to arbitrary positive factorizations. The volume identity used afterward is classical simplex geometry, proved elementarily in the article.

**Gillis and Shitov.** The retained matrix infinity-norm support deletion, fixed-sign feasibility and NP-completeness are explicitly credited. Revision 50 does not recast imported hardness as a new succinct-word complexity theorem.

**Boyd, Kim, Vandenberghe, Hassibi; Boyd and Vandenberghe.** Monomial logarithms and Farkas alternatives remain classical tools. The new output-size proposition uses standard cofactors and root isolation to quantify this particular explicitly listed problem.

**Schrijver.** *Theory of Linear and Integer Programming*, Wiley, 1986, Chapter 10, is cited for the standard rational-polyhedron bit-size background. The needed determinant estimate and pointedness justification are now given in the text, so the NP witness argument does not depend on an unexplained citation.

No independent priority claim is assigned to simplex asymmetry D, the simplex difference-body identity, the Segre relation, standard compactness, or the general existence of positive-cone realizations. The claims submitted for independent assessment are their specific causal realization consequences, the separated-cut occupation budget, the rank-tight all-horizon classification, the exact profile comparison, and the sharp orthogonal interval example beyond separate flattenings. The article's theorem-by-theorem table distinguishes these additions from their ingredients.
