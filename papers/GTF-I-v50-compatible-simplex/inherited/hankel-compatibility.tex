\section{Controlled Hankel arrays and compatible positive realizations}\label{sec:hankel}
For a horizon $N$, define the probability-valued prefix--suffix matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hankel}
 H_t[(x,u),(v,j,b)]
 =\frac{1+b\rho\ip{e_j}{U_vU_ux}}2,
 \qquad |u|=t,\quad |v|=N-t.
\end{equation}
The two columns indexed by $b=\pm1$ sum to one for every fixed $(v,j)$.
Let $\mathcal C_t$ be the product of these binary probability simplices.
Thus a point of $\mathcal C_t$ is a legal collection of responses to all
remaining words and queries, not a joint distribution of those answers.
The restriction $L_{t,a}:\mathcal C_t\to\mathcal C_{t+1}$ reads the
coordinates whose first remaining command is $a$.

A normalized positive factorization is $H_t=E_tB_t$, where $E_t$ is
row stochastic and every row of $B_t$ belongs to $\mathcal C_t$.
Its least inner dimension is denoted $\rank_{\mathrm{st}}H_t$.
The usual nonnegative rank permits arbitrary nonnegative factors and
need not impose these simultaneous normalizations. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-chain}
 \rank H_t\le\rank_+H_t\le\rank_{\mathrm{st}}H_t\le K_t
\end{equation}
for every exact stochastic realization. The last inequality follows by
conditioning the remaining experiment on the actual label at cut $t$.
The factor $E_t$ contains its state probabilities; $B_t$ contains its
complete future responses, including those of states not reached by a
particular prefix.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-horizon positive Hankel normal form]\label{prop:hankel-form}
For any finite table of binary responses, a profile $K_0,\ldots,K_N$
is exactly realizable if and only if there are polytopes
$P_t\subset\mathcal C_t$, generated by at most $K_t$ points, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compatible-hankel}
 H_0[x,\cdot]\in P_0\quad(x\in X),\qquad
 L_{t,a}P_t\subset P_{t+1}\quad(0\le t<N,\ a\in\calA).
\end{equation}
The held answer additionally costs two labels. The optimum error for a
fixed profile is the least entrywise error from the specified table to
one having such a compatible family.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given a machine, let $b_{t,s}\in\mathcal C_t$ be the conditional response
to every remaining word when its current label is $s$. Its stochastic
update gives
\[
 L_{t,a}b_{t,s}=\sum_{s'}T_{t+1,a}(s,s')b_{t+1,s'}.
\]
Taking convex hulls and using the initialization proves necessity.
Conversely choose generators $b_{t,s}$, padding by repetitions when
necessary. Express each $L_{t,a}b_{t,s}$ as a convex combination of the
next generators, and use those coefficients as the stochastic row.
Express each root row as a convex combination of the initial generators.
At time $N$ the generators themselves give the binary decoder rows.
Backward induction on the remaining word proves equality of all
responses. The construction is valid on every generator, not only on
reachable mixtures. For approximation apply this equivalence to the
actual response table of a machine, or to a candidate perturbed table;
finite compactness of the stochastic parameter spaces gives attainment.
\end{proof}

This is the finite, controlled, normalized version of the classical
shift-invariant-cone realization principle, not a newly discovered
characterization of positive systems. In particular, the cone generated
by $(1,b)$ for $b\in P_t$ carries the positive restriction maps.
Benvenuti--Farina's Theorems 2--3 give the stationary cone formulation;
the multiple cuts here are essential \cite{BF04}. The same compatibility
issue was already explicit in the earlier lift formulation \cite{GTF33}.
The normal form has exponentially many suffix coordinates. It is exact,
but does not make arbitrary width minimization efficient or eliminate
hidden directions by projecting them into the observed $D$-space.

\begin{proposition}[A finite compatibility certificate]\label{prop:hankel-dual}
For proposed generators $B_t,B_{t+1}$, put $c=L_{t,a}b_{t,s}$ and let
$b'_1,\ldots,b'_{K_{t+1}}$ be the next generators. The least rowwise
uniform defect is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-defect}
 \min_{\theta\in\Delta_{K_{t+1}}}\left\|c-\sum_i\theta_i b'_i\right\|_\infty
 =\max_{\|z\|_1\le1}\left(\ip zc-\max_i\ip z{b'_i}\right).
\end{equation}
It vanishes exactly when that row has a stochastic extension. If root
rows are approximated within $\delta_0$ and every cut restriction within
$\delta_t$, the resulting machine has worst-word binary-TV error at
most $\delta_0+\sum_{t=1}^N\delta_t$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write the infinity norm as the supremum of its pairing with the unit
$\ell_1$ ball. The simplex and this ball are compact and convex, so finite
bilinear minimax interchanges the extrema. Minimizing the pairing over
the simplex gives the maximum over its vertices with the displayed sign.
For composition, restriction does not increase the infinity norm and
stochastic averaging is a contraction. Induction on the suffix length
therefore adds the successive errors. Each binary pair is normalized,
so its total variation is exactly either coordinate error. Cancellation
may reduce the true error; no equality with the local sum is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Constant separate ranks for the planar experiment]\label{prop:rank-three44}
For the four planar seeds, an identity command, and $0<\rho\le1/10$,
at every cut and every horizon,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-ranks}
 \rank H_t=\rank_+H_t=\rank_{\mathrm{st}}H_t=3.
\end{equation}
Each factorization embeds in a complete machine when all other cuts
are unrestricted. Nevertheless the compatible peak can grow as in
Theorems~\ref{thm:metric-main} and \ref{thm:liouville-main}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The entries in \eqref{eq:hankel} are affine functions of the two
coordinates of $\rho U_ux$, so ordinary rank is at most three. Identity
prefixes, identity suffixes, both coordinate queries and the four seeds
give a rank-three submatrix. For the upper factorization, an equilateral
triangle of circumradius $2\rho$ contains the disk of radius $\rho$.
Encode $\rho U_ux$ in its three vertices $v_i$ by barycentric coordinates.
The continuation row at $v_i$ is
$(1+b\ip{e_j}{U_vv_i})/2$, which is legal since $\|v_i\|=2\rho<1$.
This is a normalized three-factor decomposition and proves the equalities.
To embed it, retain the exact history until this cut, draw its factor
label, retain that label and the remaining history afterwards, then use
the factor decoder. Both retained histories are finite at a fixed
horizon. They are not free registers in a simultaneous three-state
realization. The growth assertions follow from the stated all-hidden
converses, not from a claimed new separation of signed and positive
realization in classical stationary theory.
\end{proof}
