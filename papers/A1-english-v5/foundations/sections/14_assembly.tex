\section{From finite assemblies to complete preparations}\label{sec:assembly}
\subsection{A criterion that allows physical cancellations}
Individual branch derivatives can be large while a complete physical assembly is controlled. The primary approximation should therefore be a finite, explicitly assembled object $J_L(a)$ in one Banach reporting space $\cX$. It must be defined from the same mechanical preparation and observation at every cutoff, including all required boundary terms. Different physical faces or incompatible labels cannot be canceled merely to improve a bound.

\begin{theorem}[Complete-preparation response from a Cauchy hierarchy]\label{thm:assembly}
Let $J_L\in C^r(K;\cX)$, where $K$ is a compact interval and each curve extends to a neighborhood. Assume that for every $0\le j\le r$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{L',L''\ge L}\sup_{a\in K}
 \norm{J_{L'}^{(j)}(a)-J_{L''}^{(j)}(a)}\le\epsilon_{L,j}\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:assembly-cauchy}
\end{equation}
Then $J_L$ and all these derivatives converge uniformly, their limit $J$ is $C^r$ on the interior with continuous one-sided endpoint jets, and $J^{(j)}=\lim_LJ_L^{(j)}$. If $J_L$ converges against a determining test family to the actual mechanical preparation law, $J$ represents that law in $\cX$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Completeness gives uniform continuous limits $G_j$ for each derivative sequence. On any subinterval use
$J_L^{(j)}(b)-J_L^{(j)}(a)=\int_a^bJ_L^{(j+1)}(t)dt$ and pass to the uniform limit. The resulting fundamental-theorem identity proves $G_j'=G_{j+1}$. Induction yields $J=G_0\in C^r$. Endpoint limits follow by continuity. Pairing with continuous linear tests commutes with the norm limit, so a determining family identifies the resulting distribution with the physical law. Probability convergence alone would identify only $G_0$, not the derivatives.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Explicit truncation budgets]\label{cor:tail-budget}
A sufficient condition is
$\norm{(J_{L+1}-J_L)^{(j)}}_\infty\le b_{L,j}$ with
$\sum_Lb_{L,j}<\infty$ for every $j\le r$. One can take
$\epsilon_{L,j}\le\sum_{k\ge L}b_{k,j}$. In particular, $b_{L,j}\le C_j(1+L)^{-p_j}$ with $p_j>1$, or a polynomial times $e^{-\alpha_jL}$ with $\alpha_j>0$, gives an explicit convergent tail.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Telescope $J_{L'}-J_{L''}$ and sum the norm bounds. Polynomial tails are bounded by the corresponding integral, and exponential-polynomial tails by a geometric series after absorbing half the exponential in the polynomial. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:assembly}.
\end{proof}

These are implications with checkable estimates, not a proof that arbitrary billiard or hard-sphere histories satisfy them. A probability cutoff $P(B_L^c)\to0$ supplies no derivative envelope. Likewise, inserting an artificial depth weight into a raw geometric current changes the observable rather than proving its unweighted response.

\subsection{Evidence and differentiated truncations}
If an assembled numerator and its normalizing mass satisfy \eqref{eq:assembly-cauchy} through order $r$ and the masses are uniformly at least $z_*>0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:jet-error} passes the response to normalized experiments. A rare-event restriction can make $z_*$ small, and a growing source can magnify every assembly error. Those factors must be charged at the same cutoff and on the same preparation.

A useful alternative for a fixed report class is to establish the Cauchy bounds directly for $J_L(e^Vf)$, $J_L(e^V)$, and their derivatives. This avoids requiring variation convergence of microscopic laws. It still requires one class large enough for the conditional and dynamic uses to follow.

\subsection{What the next sections actually verify}
The prepared Lorentz tube gives a moving terminal interface with explicit nonzero response. More importantly, Section~\ref{sec:lorentz-global} gives a \emph{finite complete assembly} for an entire smooth preparation on the full phase space over a short horizon. That identity already includes every incidence angle; no infinite branch-tail criterion or uniform inverse-grazing bound is needed in that specific regime. For longer times with many correlated collisions, \eqref{eq:assembly-cauchy} remains an additional target. The short-horizon construction is not presented as having proved it for all times.
