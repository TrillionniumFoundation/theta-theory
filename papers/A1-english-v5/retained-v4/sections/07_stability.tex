\section{Stability and finite implementation certificates}\label{sec:stability}
\subsection{Coefficient errors without cancellation}
Suppose $c_i>0$ and an unnormalized approximation satisfies
$(1-\delta)c_i\le\widetilde c_i\le(1+\delta)c_i$ for $0\le\delta<1$.
Since Bernstein basis functions are nonnegative and sum to one, the same
inequality holds for $P_{\widetilde c}/P_c$. Posterior normalization therefore
gives
\begin{equation}
 \TV(\pi_c,\pi_{\widetilde c})\le
             \min\{1,\delta/(1-\delta)\}.
 \label{eq:posterior-error}
\end{equation}
Indeed the normalized density ratio is between
$(1-\delta)/(1+\delta)$ and $(1+\delta)/(1-\delta)$; integrating its
absolute difference and dividing by two proves the stated conservative
bound. Zero coefficients of an approximant are not permitted when claiming
a relative-error certificate with $\delta<1$.

More generally suppose each positive update is performed with a componentwise
relative error in $[1-\rho_j,1+\rho_j]$, with $\rho_j<1$. Work with
unnormalized coefficient vectors; exact rescaling by a common scalar does
not affect the posterior. Positivity of \eqref{eq:bern-product} propagates
componentwise bounds. After $n$ updates the true and computed polynomial
ratio is between $L_n=\prod_j(1-\rho_j)$ and
$U_n=\prod_j(1+\rho_j)$. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \TV(\pi,\widetilde\pi)\le
              \min\{1,(U_n-L_n)/(2L_n)\}.
 \label{eq:accumulated-error}
\end{equation}
Errors in command selection or detector evaluation are not automatically
coefficient roundoff and must be bounded separately. The estimate does not
assert that an arbitrary floating-point implementation meets its hypothesis.

\subsection{Values are stable under prior perturbation}
For any two posterior probabilities $\pi,\widetilde\pi$ at the same resource
state, the ideal admissible continuation-policy set is the same. For a
fixed remaining-$b$ policy, the conditional expected payoff as a function of
$R$ lies in $[G_-e^{-b|\lambda|B},G_+e^{b|\lambda|B}]$. Integration against
the two priors gives
\begin{equation}
 |V_b(\pi)-V_b(\widetilde\pi)|\le
 \bigl(G_+e^{b|\lambda|B}-G_-e^{-b|\lambda|B}\bigr)
                         \TV(\pi,\widetilde\pi).
 \label{eq:value-stability}
\end{equation}
The bound holds for suprema because the same bound holds uniformly for
every fixed policy; a policy may be optimized separately for each prior.
It also holds for a common restricted finite command menu. Combining it
with \eqref{eq:posterior-error} or \eqref{eq:accumulated-error} gives a
state-rounding value certificate. This is a bound on values from the two
priors, not an automatic optimality bound for every history-dependent
rounded-state implementation. Choosing a policy optimal for the approximate
prior gives a bound of twice the displayed error on its regret at that
single decision problem, by adding and subtracting its value under the
approximate prior.
