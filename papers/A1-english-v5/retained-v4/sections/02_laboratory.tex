\section{A parameter-independent laboratory preparation}\label{sec:lab}
\subsection{Mechanical table and cartridges}
Let $\Lambda$ be the triangular lattice generated by $(1,0)$ and
$(1/2,\sqrt3/2)$. Put
\[
 a_0=\sqrt3/2,\qquad I=[l,u]=[9/20,47/100],\qquad
 A(R)=a_0-\pi R^2,
\]
and let $Q_R=\T_\Lambda^2\setminus\overline B(0,R)$. A released point
particle moves at speed one and reflects specularly on the disks. Distinct
lifted disks are at distance at least $1-2u=3/50$. After a regular reflection
an outgoing ray cannot re-enter the same convex disk before another
collision. Consequently a physical trajectory has at most one collision
in any window $T<3/50$.

Each cartridge contains independent variables
\begin{equation}
 q\sim\frac{dq}{a_0}\text{ on }\T_\Lambda^2,\qquad
 \vartheta\sim\frac{d\vartheta}{2\pi},\qquad U,W\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1].
 \tag{P}\label{eq:prep}
\end{equation}
Cartridges are independent; the table radius is the same fixed unknown
parameter in all cartridges. The commanded mode is $a=(T,\epsilon,\theta)
\in\cA$ and is chosen before exposing the cartridge variables.

A \emph{guarded insertion} attempts to place the particle at $q$. It releases
a unit-speed particle in direction $\vartheta$ exactly when $q\in Q_R$;
otherwise it produces the placement-failure symbol $\partial$. This is a
specified deterministic preparation instrument. One physical idealization is
vertical insertion over rigid cylinders: a top-face encounter goes to the
failure collector, while an open vertical channel reaches the horizontal
release plane. The release mechanism and its momentum exchange belong to
the apparatus. Edges and tangencies are null events under \eqref{eq:prep}.
We use neither a trajectory inside a solid obstacle nor repeated uncharged
placement attempts. An unsuccessful insertion consumes the cartridge.

On successful insertion, let $H$ record whether a collision occurs before
$T$. When $H=1$, let $z$ be the vector from the center of the impacted lifted
disk to the impact point. Its squared length is read by the detector. The
physical record is taken at the first collision, or at $T$ if none occurs;
subsequent motion and detector back-action are not used in the trial. The
local center chart is unambiguous because $u<1/2$. A smooth ambient function
can agree with $|z|^2$ throughout the closed annular neighborhood containing
all possible impact points.

\subsection{A detector implemented by a prepared coordinate}
For $0\le c<1$ define, on $0\le y\le2\pi$,
\begin{equation}
 F_{c,\theta}(y)=
 \frac{y+c\{\sin(y-\theta)+\sin\theta\}}{2\pi}.
 \label{eq:detector-cdf}
\end{equation}
It is strictly increasing, has endpoints zero and one, and has derivative
$(1+c\cos(y-\theta))/(2\pi)$. At a collision the apparatus reports
\begin{equation}
 Y=F_{\epsilon |z|^2,\theta}^{-1}(U).
 \label{eq:detector-map}
\end{equation}
Without a collision it reports $Y=2\pi U$. Firmware uses the measured
coordinate $z$ and the commanded mode, never the unknown $R$. Since
$\epsilon |z|^2\le Eu^2<1$, the inverse exists uniquely and is smooth in
its arguments away from the irrelevant endpoint identification. Thus this
channel is a deterministic function of the mechanics and the prepared
apparatus coordinate; it is not a postulated Gaussian likelihood.

The readout can be included in the usual ideal classical pointer apparatus.
If $G$ is a smooth local readout of the terminal mechanical variables and
prepared coordinates, a canonical pointer pair $(Q,P)$ with impulse
Hamiltonian $PG$ gives $\dot Q=G$. On the explicitly prepared sheet $P=0$
the mechanical variables do not move during this readout impulse, since
their Hamiltonian variation is proportional to $P$. A pointer initially at
$Q=0$ records $G$. With general pointer momenta the corresponding back-action
must be retained. The conditional branches and uniform comparator are
specified hybrid operations, and the next cartridge is fresh regardless of
that back-action. This is an ideal classical instrument construction, not a
claim of a finite-energy quantum or noisy finite-precision implementation.

\subsection{Whole-preparation collision integration}
Write $n_\alpha=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$,
$t_\alpha=(-\sin\alpha,\cos\alpha)$ and
\begin{equation}
 v^-=-\cos\phi\,n_\alpha+\sin\phi\,t_\alpha,\quad
 q_0=Rn_\alpha-sv^-,\quad
 0<s<T,\quad |\phi|<\pi/2.
 \label{eq:tube}
\end{equation}
Every regular colliding initial state has exactly one such representation
modulo lattice and angular identifications. Conversely, the backward segment
from the impact of length $s$ cannot hit another disk, because $s<3/50$;
it lies outside its own convex disk. The same separation excludes a second
collision after reflection. This proves coverage and nonoverlap, not merely
local parameterization.

At fixed incoming laboratory angle $\vartheta=\alpha+\pi-\phi$, the position
Jacobian has columns $Rt_\alpha$ and $-v^-$. Its absolute determinant is
$R\cos\phi$. The angular change of variables has determinant of absolute
value one. Thus for any bounded collision-mark test $f$ the full ambient
preparation gives
\begin{equation}
 \E_R[\one_{\{\mathrm{placed},H=1\}}f]
 =\frac{R}{2\pi a_0}
 \int_0^T\int_0^{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}
 f(Rn_\alpha,v^-,s)\cos\phi\,d\phi\,d\alpha\,ds.
 \label{eq:ambient-tube}
\end{equation}
There is no removed positive-mass grazing neighborhood. In particular,
placement failure has probability $\pi R^2/a_0$, and collision after an
attempt has probability $2RT/a_0$. The allowed-position mass is $A(R)/a_0$.
Conditioning on successful placement therefore recovers precisely the
natural equilibrium preparation and its collision probability
\begin{equation}
 p_R=\frac{2RT}{A(R)}.
 \label{eq:conditional-equilibrium}
\end{equation}
The conditional experiment and the cartridge experiment are different
records and different resource counts. We never discard the conditioning
probability when describing the cartridge law.

Let
\[
 X=\{\partial\}\sqcup(\{0,1\}\times\mathbb S^1),\qquad
 \nu=\delta_\partial+\sum_{h=0}^1\delta_h\frac{dy}{2\pi}.
\]
The total mass of this reference measure is three.
\begin{proposition}[An explicit positive polynomial kernel]\label{prop:raw}
The raw readout has density $k_R^a=H_a(R,\cdot)/a_0$ with respect to $\nu$,
where
\begin{align}
 H_a(R,\partial)&=\pi R^2,\notag\\
 H_a(R,0,y)&=a_0-\pi R^2-2TR,\notag\\
 H_a(R,1,y)&=2TR\{1+\epsilon R^2\cos(y-\theta)\}.
 \label{eq:raw-kernel}
\end{align}
These densities integrate to one. Their coefficients depend continuously
on the mode and readout on each component of $X$. There are explicit
constants $0<\kappa\le K<\infty$ such that
$\kappa\le k_R^a(x)\le K$ for all $R,a,x$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:ambient-tube} gives the hit mass. On the collision shell
$|z|^2=R^2$, so the inverse-CDF apparatus gives the displayed hit density.
The other two masses were computed above and the no-hit pointer is uniform.
Adding the integrals of the three components gives
$(\pi R^2+A(R)-2TR+2TR)/a_0=1$.

For a usable lower bound set
\begin{equation}
 h_* =\min\{\pi l^2,\ a_0-\pi u^2-2T_+u,
                   \ 2T_-l(1-Eu^2)\},\qquad \kappa=h_*/a_0.
 \label{eq:lower-bound}
\end{equation}
The middle entry is positive: even replacing $T_+$ by $3/50$, using
$a_0>0.86$ and $\pi<22/7$ gives a strictly positive value at $u=0.47$.
The other entries are positive by assumption. An upper bound is
$K=a_0^{-1}\max\{\pi u^2,a_0,2T_+u(1+Eu^2)\}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The ideal record is still distinct}
If a complete collision record retains $(q_0,v_0,s)$, the
parameter-independent statistic
$\widehat R=d_{\T_\Lambda^2}(q_0+sv_0,0)$ equals $R$ on its collision tag.
For $R\ne R'$ the set $\{H=1,\widehat R=R\}$ has positive mass under $R$
and zero under $R'$, in either the whole equilibrium or cartridge experiment.
The relative entropies in both directions between these complete-record
laws are infinite. The laws need not be mutually singular, because their
no-collision pieces can overlap. Their total variation distance is bounded
below by the larger of the two collision masses.

This explicit separation is retained from finding S1 of the referee report.
The positive density in Proposition~\ref{prop:raw} belongs to the specified
coordinate detector, not to the ideal record. An arbitrary threshold of
$\widehat R$ would not give this theorem. Section~\ref{sec:response} proves
regular inference directly for the same detector used in control.
