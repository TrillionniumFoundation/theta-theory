\section{A continuous-alphabet prediction theorem}\label{sec:continuous}
\subsection{Conditions that replace finite point probabilities}
In a continuous observation space, $P(Y=y)$ is usually zero. Formula~\eqref{eq:prediction-update} cannot be copied using point-event probabilities. We give a distinct sufficient theorem with an actual quotient construction.

Fix a finite horizon $N$. Let $K,U,Y$ be compact metric spaces, $K$ the parameter space, and let $\lambda$ be a probability on $Y$ with full support. Assume every open-loop experiment has strictly positive joint densities
\[
 q_{a,m}(y_{1:m}\mid u_{0:m-1}),\qquad 0\le m\le N,
\]
which are jointly continuous in all arguments and normalized. Require causal consistency:
\begin{equation}
 \int q_{a,m+1}(y_{1:m},y\mid u_{0:m-1},u)\lambda(\dd y)
 =q_{a,m}(y_{1:m}\mid u_{0:m-1}),
 \label{eq:density-consistency}
\end{equation}
for every prefix and every last action. The empty density is one. Positivity on the compact domains supplies a separate positive minimum for each finite $m$; no bound uniform in $m\to\infty$ is claimed.

For $h=(u_{0:n-1},y_{1:n})$, conditional open-loop future densities are the ratio of the complete density to $q_{a,n}(h)$. They are jointly continuous and positive. The history space $H_n=(U\times Y)^n$ is compact. Choose countable dense $K_*,U_*$ and a countable algebra generating $\mathcal B(Y)$; its products determine measures on every $Y^k$.

For every dense parameter, dense future action string, and algebra event $B\subset Y^k$, record the conditional probability $p_h^a(B\mid\mathbf u)$. Add the past likelihood ratios $e_h(a)=q_{a,n}(h)/q_{a_*,n}(h)$ on $K_*$ if inference with a prior is required. Transform a likelihood coordinate $e$ to $e/(1+e)$ to place it in $[0,1]$. Denote the resulting countable array by $S_n(h)$ and its image by $\mathsf S_n$.

\begin{theorem}[Compact measurable experiment-family state]\label{thm:continuous-state}
Under the preceding assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S_n:H_n\to\mathsf S_n$ is continuous and $\mathsf S_n$ is compact metrizable.
\item Equal state arrays determine the same continuous future densities for every parameter, every open-loop action string, and every readout string.
\item There is a continuous update $T_n:\mathsf S_n\times U\times Y\to\mathsf S_{n+1}$ with $T_n(S_n(h),u,y)=S_{n+1}(huy)$, and a continuous positive predictive density $k_{n,a}(s,u,y)$.
\item The same state is sufficient for all Borel adaptive continuation policies. When past-likelihood coordinates are included, it also determines posterior prediction for every fixed prior on $K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each coordinate probability is the integral over a fixed Borel event of a uniformly continuous conditional density on a compact domain. Uniform convergence of integrands proves continuity in $h$. Likelihood coordinates are ratios of continuous positive functions. Hence the countable product map is continuous and its image is compact metrizable.

If two arrays coincide, their future measures coincide first for dense parameters and action strings, since the countable algebra determines measures. At these choices, equality almost everywhere of the densities implies equality everywhere: a nonzero continuous difference would have fixed sign on an open set of positive $\lambda^k$ measure, contradicting equality of measures. Joint continuity then extends equality from dense parameters and actions to all of them. This use of a common positive continuous density avoids choosing unrelated null sets for uncountably many interventions.

The next predictive density is the one-step conditional density. It is constant on the fibers of $S_n$. The future density after a readout is
\[
 \frac{q_{a,h}^{(u,\mathbf v)}(y,\mathbf z)}{k_{n,a}(h,u,y)}.
\]
The positive denominator and the preceding equality show that updated arrays are constant on those same fibers. The map $(h,u,y)\mapsto S_{n+1}(huy)$ is continuous. The continuous surjection
$H_n\times U\times Y\to\mathsf S_n\times U\times Y$ is a quotient map because its domain is compact and target Hausdorff. A continuous function constant on its fibers therefore descends continuously. The same argument applies to $k_{n,a}$. Its normalization follows from \eqref{eq:density-consistency}.

For deterministic feedback, the density of a future readout string is the causal product, or equivalently the open-loop density evaluated at the realized feedback actions. These expressions agree pointwise for equal states. For randomized policies, integrate this density against the successive policy kernels in the common action--readout chronology. Equality is retained by each kernel integration. Positivity and continuous versions ensure that no intervention-dependent exceptional readout set invalidates this substitution. For a prior, use the retained likelihood curve to reconstruct its posterior and integrate the candidate-specific future laws. Dense parameter values determine the entire continuous likelihood curve.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Response coordinates}
If $K$ is a compact interval and the above densities extend $C^r$ in $a$ to a neighborhood, with derivatives jointly continuous, then conditional densities and their updates are $C^r$ in $a$. Uniform positive denominators permit the quotient-jet recursion of Appendix~\ref{app:jets}. Equality of the complete parameter curves implies equality of derivatives. One can also include derivative evaluations on a countable dense parameter set in the state array. The resulting map remains continuous in history when those derivatives are jointly continuous, though derivative coordinates need bounded rescaling to fit a common compact product.

Only finite-order jets at one parameter produce finite-order equivalence, not equality of the full parameter family. Conversely, equality of a predictive experiment at one parameter produces no derivative equality without additional information, as shown in Section~\ref{sec:predictors}.

\subsection{A nonempty deterministic apparatus class}
The hypotheses can be verified without stochastic collision dynamics. Take a compactly supported initial mechanical state, a compact control set, and a finite sequence of smooth deterministic operations staying in a common compact domain. Before the experiment prepare a finite bank of pointer position coordinates $D_j\in\T$ with strictly positive smooth densities $g_j$, independently of the system and of the pointer momenta. The entire apparatus bank is then part of the complete initial state. At operation $j$, let a known canonical interaction produce a readout
\[
 Y_j=D_j+\kappa_j Q_j\pmod1,
\]
where $Q_j$ depends continuously on the mechanical initial state, previously used apparatus momenta, and the open-loop actions, but not on unused $D_j$. Reflection-free shears \eqref{eq:pointer} in a coordinate chart, followed by reduction of the displayed pointer value modulo one, are one example. Smooth flows before and after the interaction can be included.

For a fixed open-loop action string and hidden initial data $z$ other than the $D_j$, the density is
\begin{equation}
 \prod_{j=1}^m g_j(y_j-\kappa_jQ_j(a,z,\mathbf u)).
 \label{eq:pointer-density}
\end{equation}
Integration against the fixed hidden preparation gives $q_{a,m}$. Every factor is continuous and bounded above and below on a compact circle, so the product and its integral are positive continuous. Last-readout integration gives causal consistency. Parameter derivatives are dominated whenever the finite mechanical derivatives are uniformly bounded. Back-action through the prepared pointer momenta is retained in $Q_j$, and independent output noise is not inferred from the mechanical equations. The independent initial pointer-position preparation is explicitly responsible for the product in \eqref{eq:pointer-density}.

This verifies an actual sufficient class for continuous observations and actions. It does not prove the same density regularity across arbitrary grazing singularities. Those require the geometric analysis later in the paper.

\subsection{Beyond compact positive-density models}
General standard Borel instruments still admit posterior construction, as in Section~\ref{sec:instruments}. The compact image, continuous quotient, and uniform positive evidence proved here need not survive on noncompact spaces or with vanishing densities. One must replace compactness by tightness, prove a determining family, and supply the relevant posterior continuity. Results on Borel-space partially observed control \cite{FeinbergEtAl} are relevant to such extensions, but are not cited as if they automatically prove the present experiment-family quotient.
