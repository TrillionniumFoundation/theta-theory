\section{Detailed finite examples and boundary tests}\label{app:examples}
\subsection{A hidden preparation loss on four atoms}
Let $P$ be uniform on four atoms and let $S$ group the first two and last two atoms. Take $e^V=(1,3,2,2)$. Then $Pe^V=2$. The projection of the tilted law has equal group weights because both conditional exponential means are two. By contrast, the coarse-preparation source is
$m=(\frac12\log3,\log2)$, so its optimum has normalizer
$(\sqrt3+2)/2$ and different group weights. The realizability deficit is
\[
 \log2-\log((\sqrt3+2)/2)>0.
\]
Thus projection of a selected law and restricted preparation are distinct even in a finite exact experiment.

\subsection{A density cap with explicit active set}
On two equally weighted atoms take $e^m=(1,9)$ and $M=3/2$. The uncapped density is $(1/5,9/5)$ and violates the cap. Formula~\eqref{eq:cap} gives $q_M=(1/2,3/2)$, with $c=1/2$: its mean density is one and the second coordinate is capped. The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cap} verifies optimality against every feasible density, not only a sampled grid. When $m$ crosses an active-set threshold, the optimizer can have a derivative kink; this is why Proposition~\ref{prop:cap-response} claims first-order value differentiability rather than a universal classical second derivative.

\subsection{An explicit transverse collision calculation}
Consider $\dot x=1$ with a wall at $x=a$, starting from $x_0<a$. The event time is $\tau=a-x_0$, so $\tau'=1$. After specular reversal $\dot x=-1$, the position at a fixed later time is
$x_T=2a-x_0-T$, whose derivative is two. The reset position map itself is identity and would give zero parameter derivative at a fixed nominal event point if time displacement were omitted. Formula~\eqref{eq:saltation} gives
$s^+=0+(1-(-1))\tau'=2$, exactly the fixed-time derivative. This tests the sign of the post-event correction.

\subsection{Numerical constants are separated from analytic geometry}
For the local tube, the conservative bounds used in Lemma~\ref{lem:tube} follow from rational inequalities for $|\sin\phi|$, $\cos\phi$, and squared positive quantities. Floating point checks on a grid are supplementary only. For the whole preparation, the essential inequalities are analytic:
$R\ge9/20>\sqrt3/4$, $R\le47/100<1/2$, and $T<3/50$.
The incoming Jacobian is $R\cos\phi$, and
\[
 \int_0^T\int_0^{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}R\cos\phi\dd\phi\dd\alpha\dd s=4\pi RT.
\]
This gives an independent mass normalization check for the signed tube assembly. At $F\equiv1$ the correction cancels exactly; at $F=\one_{\rm hit}$ only its positive term remains.

\subsection{Direct geometric checks for the no-collision component}
For equilibrium, the unnormalized no-collision mass is
$2\pi A_R-4\pi RT$. It is positive because the mean roof is at least $d_*>T$. Its radius derivative is $-4\pi^2R-4\pi T$. The collision mass derivative is $4\pi T$, while the full phase-volume derivative is $-4\pi^2R$. Adding the two derivatives therefore recovers the derivative of the preparation normalizer. Normalizing afterward gives derivatives of probabilities summing to zero. This separates moving-domain response from the response of a normalized event probability.

\subsection{Conditional trial output under a hard count budget}
With independent trials and success $s$, the probability of first success at $j$ is $(1-s)^{j-1}s$. The law of the successful record is the same tilted law at every $j$. Consequently conditioning on success before a fixed cap does not bias that successful record beyond the declared tilt. It does change the overall output space: with probability $(1-s)^B$ the protocol outputs a failure symbol. Dropping that failure symbol and claiming an unconditional successful probability law would misrepresent the budgeted experiment.

\subsection{A parameter derivative is information not present in nominal data}
The two Bernoulli families $1/2+a$ and $1/2+2a$ at $a=0$ provide an exact two-history test of response-aware prediction. Their future nominal laws coincide, yet their Fisher informations are respectively $4$ and $16$. This does not disprove nominal sufficient-state construction: it proves that nominal sufficiency and experiment-family sufficiency are different tasks. In an unknown-parameter problem, past likelihood coordinates are also required to recover prior-dependent posterior weights.

\subsection{What finite regressions cannot prove}
No finite set of radius samples proves a uniform geometric theorem. No passing finite derivative check proves an infinite-time response series converges. No successful cap optimization on a grid establishes arbitrary mechanical realizability. The accompanying checks are organized around exact identities and clearly labeled numerical approximations, and their execution receipt is distinct from the mathematical proof ledger.
