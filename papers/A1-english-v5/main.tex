\begin{abstract}
We determine the state required for finite future decisions in a calibrated
polynomial experiment. When the attainable failure factors contain an open
subset of the degree-$q$ polynomials, the exact continuous state after $n$
observations for $m$ further observations has dimension $q\min(n,m)$.
A causal implementation has a tent-shaped memory profile. For an observable
squared-prediction-score experiment, finite memory with $M$ stored symbols
has matching upper and lower regret rates of order $M^{-2/(q\min(n,m))}$,
including randomized codes without historical side information. The same
exponent holds for unconditional regret under one fixed randomized
exploration protocol. The law is
realized by a fixed four-symbol detector and four-entry comparator in a
counted Lorentz laboratory, rather than by arbitrary readout programming.
Erasing one collision bit leaves the preparation and collision masses
unchanged but lowers the dimension from $3\min(n,m)$ to $2\min(n,m)$ under
the same query protocol. Retaining that bit also gives a strictly positive,
explicit prediction gain with two cartridges and no acceptance cost.
The general apparatus retains its full positive likelihood filter, attained
boundary-complete control, and fixed-controller response. Its tagwise-reward
optimal policies admit at most two collision acceptance arcs at every finite
stage. Positive normalized surrogates and complete-policy error bounds treat
smooth departures from the exact polynomial calibration.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} calibrated experiments; predictive state; finite-horizon
information; finite-memory decisions; positive polynomial likelihood;
controlled censoring; Lorentz laboratory.

\paragraph{The distinct role of this last comparison.}
The preceding acceptance-cost separation remains a valid full-class theorem.
Its nominal optima are also those of the binary signal ``no collision versus
its complement'', with the same two-point prior and acceptance multiplier.
Its selected policies ignore the angular mark. It is therefore a cost-saving
mechanism, not a proof that cubic state geometry causes that advantage.
The decision-state law, common-protocol sign ablation, and zero-cost bit
value in Sections~\ref{sec:decision-horizon}--\ref{sec:mark-value} supply
that separate structural connection. Neither comparison replaces the other.
