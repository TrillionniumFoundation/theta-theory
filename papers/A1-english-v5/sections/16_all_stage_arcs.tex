\section{Two collision arcs at every finite stage}\label{sec:all-stage-arcs}
The last-cartridge formula of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-arcs} remains useful as
an explicit formula. Its boundary-count conclusion extends to the full
horizon. This strengthening, including the frozen-failure-continuation
argument, is attributed to Section 8 of the controlling referee report.
We give the complete argument because selecting from an unspecified space
of policies would not by itself prove a Borel feedback implementation.

\begin{theorem}[Full-horizon two-arc realization]\label{thm:all-stage-arcs}
In the original circular Lorentz detector, suppose at each stage the current
accepted reward is constant on each raw tag. Retain the compact decisions,
continuous positive terminal payoff and bounded observable future rewards
of Section~\ref{sec:control}. For every finite budget there is an optimal
Borel feedback controller whose collision high-acceptance set at every
state and selected mode is a union of at most two circle arcs. Its
no-collision gate is angle-constant. Endpoint values are $\eta$ and
$1-\eta$, including $\eta=0$. A boundary-count theorem is not an algorithmic
bound for finding the arc endpoints or for partitioning the belief space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a posterior, a mode and $b$ remaining cartridges. Choose an optimal gate
$g^*$ by Theorems~\ref{thm:control} and \ref{thm:boundary}. Starting at its
failure posterior, choose the Borel optimal remaining feedback rule supplied
by the same induction. Hold that entire failure-branch rule fixed and let
$L(R)$ be its conditional expected remaining payoff. On a null failure
branch use any admissible rule. Put
\[
 A(x)=e^{\lambda r_a(x)}\mathcal W_{b-1}(k_R^a(x)\pi(dR)),\qquad
 C(x)=e^{\lambda r_a^\dagger}\int L(R)k_R^a(x)\pi(dR).
\]
For any alternative gate $g$, optimal accepted continuations and this frozen
failure continuation realize the feasible value
$J(g)=\int\{gA+(1-g)C\}\,d\nu$. At $g^*$ this is the optimal fixed-mode
value. Its pointwise maximizer is the endpoint gate taking $1-\eta$
where $A>C$ and $\eta$ otherwise. Its feasible value cannot exceed the
original optimum and cannot be below $J(g^*)$; thus it is optimal. Allowing
its failure continuation to reoptimize preserves this equality.

On the collision tag the measure $k_R^a(1,y)\pi(dR)$ is affine in
$z=\cos(y-\theta)$. The functional $\mathcal W_{b-1}$ is a supremum of
linear functionals on nonnegative measures. Since the current accepted
reward is tagwise constant, $A$ is convex in $z$ and $C$ is affine. The
nonpositive set of $A-C$ is an interval. Its positive set is at most two
end intervals, whose inverse images under cosine are at most two arcs.
Both functions are angle-constant on the no-collision tag.

For measurability, begin with the selected Borel $g^*(c,a,x)$ and the Borel
optimal feedback maps on the later compact state strata. The failure
posterior of $g^*$ is Borel, with any fixed extension on its common-null
branch. Running the chosen later feedback from that initial posterior
makes $L(R,c,a)$ jointly Borel by finite chronological integration of the
Borel kernels and rewards. Thus the coefficients of $C$ are Borel.
The accepted envelope has the continuity already proved for homogeneous
continuation. Therefore $\{A>C\}$ is jointly Borel in state, mode and raw
record. Apply this construction backward at each stage and choose the
mode by the existing Borel maximizer. The physically pasted feedback then
has the asserted arc structure. No new topology on a space of policies or
unproved measurable policy-space selection has been used.
\end{proof}
