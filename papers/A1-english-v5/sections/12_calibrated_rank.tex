\section{A fixed finite detector and its attainable factor space}\label{sec:calibrated}
The admissible observation class in this section is smaller than the
measurable-comparator class. Fix one mode with $T>0$ and $\epsilon>0$.
The detector releases only four raw symbols, indexed by
$\mathcal X_4=\{\partial,0,+,-\}$. On a collision it reports the sign of
$\cos(Y-\theta)$ and discards the angle. The controller has no access to
$z$, to $|z|$, to $U$, or to the discarded angle. The firmware and the mode
remain fixed. Its only commands are four lookup probabilities
$g_j\in[\eta,1-\eta]$, where $0<\eta<1/2$. A rejected symbol is replaced by
$\dagger$. Thus the implemented record has five symbols, including two
different kinds of failure. Each trial still consumes one ambient cartridge.

The same four-symbol device can be implemented without evaluating an
inverse CDF: on collision set $+$ precisely when
$U\le 1/2+\epsilon |z|^2/\pi$. This fixed comparator has the required
probability. It reads a physical coordinate internally but does not expose
it or allow a controller to choose an arbitrary function of it. This
restricted-interface statement does not assert a lower bound on all
possible classical apparatuses.

Write $k_j(R)$ for the four probabilities. Whole-preparation integration
and the half-circle integrals give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:four-cells}
\begin{split}
k_\partial(R)&=\pi R^2/a_0,\qquad
k_0(R)=1-\pi R^2/a_0-2TR/a_0,\\
k_+(R)&=TR/a_0+2T\epsilon R^3/(\pi a_0),\qquad
k_-(R)=TR/a_0-2T\epsilon R^3/(\pi a_0).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
All four are strictly positive on $I$ and their sum is one. Positivity of
the hit probabilities also follows by integrating the strictly positive
circle density, so no stronger bound on $\epsilon$ is being introduced.
The gate accepts $j$ with probability $g_j k_j(R)$, and its failure
polynomial is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-failure}
F_g(R)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal X_4}(1-g_j)k_j(R),\qquad
\eta\le F_g(R)\le1-\eta.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Finite calibration and exact attainable rank]\label{thm:calibrated-rank}
Let $C$ be the matrix whose columns are the power coefficients of the four
polynomials in \eqref{eq:four-cells}, in the order
$\partial,0,+,-$ and degrees $0,1,2,3$. Then
\[
 |\det C|=4T^2\epsilon/a_0^3>0.
\]
The entire attainable failure family is exactly
$C[\eta,1-\eta]^4$, expressed in coefficient coordinates. In particular it
contains a neighborhood of the constant polynomial $1/2$ in $\mathcal P_3$,
where $\mathcal P_j$ denotes the real polynomials of degree at most $j$.
A coefficient displacement $v$ from that constant is realized by the single
four-entry command
\[
 g(v)=\tfrac12\mathbf 1-C^{-1}v
 \quad\hbox{whenever}\quad
 \|C^{-1}v\|_\infty<\tfrac12-\eta.
\]
If the sign is erased before the controller and comparator, only the three
tags $\partial,0,1$ remain. Their probabilities span exactly $\mathcal P_2$,
and their attainable failure polynomials contain a neighborhood of $1/2$
in $\mathcal P_2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient matrix is
\[
 C=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&1&0&0\\
 0&-2T/a_0&T/a_0&T/a_0\\
 \pi/a_0&-\pi/a_0&0&0\\
 0&0&2T\epsilon/(\pi a_0)&-2T\epsilon/(\pi a_0)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Expansion in its first row and then its quadratic row proves the determinant
identity. Equation~\eqref{eq:finite-failure} proves both necessity and
sufficiency of the cube image; the same gate is used at every radius.
Since $C\mathbf1$ is the coefficient vector of one, the displayed inverse
formula proves the interior claim without an unrestricted functional gate.
After sign erasure the probabilities are $k_\partial,k_0,k_++k_-$.
Their coefficients have rank three in degrees zero through two: the
placement term supplies $R^2$, the hit term supplies $R$, and normalization
supplies one. The identical three-dimensional inverse argument applies.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A finite probe menu shared by the two apparatuses.}
Choose $c=1/2$ and $0<\delta<1/2-\eta$. For each raw index $j$ let the
rejection probabilities be $u^{(j)}=c\mathbf1+\delta e_j$, and put
$B_j=F_{1-u^{(j)}}=c+\delta k_j$. In the sign-erased device a four-entry
command is interpreted by replacing $u_+,u_-$ by their common average;
this public command convention is fixed in advance. Its four probe factors
are then $c+\delta k_\partial$, $c+\delta k_0$, and two copies of
$c+(\delta/2)(k_++k_-)$. In both devices the sum of the four factors is the
constant $4c+\delta$. Their spans are respectively $\mathcal P_3$ and
$\mathcal P_2$: the coefficient transformation in the first device is
$\delta I+c\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top$, which is invertible; in the second,
normalization and the three tag probabilities give the claimed span.
For $m$ future cartridges the same public index set
$\mathcal J=\{\partial,0,+,-\}^m$ selects the same sequence of these
commands. It has $s=4^m$ elements. Only the selected $m$ cartridges are
executed, not $m4^m$ cartridges. No claim of polynomial-size enumeration is
made.

\subsection{The fixed-space rank formulation}
For completeness we give the rank-dependent extension of the product
argument. Its fixed-mode form was derived in Section 7 of the controlling
referee report; the proof below records that attribution. The finite-horizon
decision and finite-memory results in the following sections are additional
statements, not consequences attributed to that report.

\begin{theorem}[Normalized products in a rank-$r$ factor space]\label{thm:rank-deficient}
Let $W\subset\mathbb R[t]$ have dimension $r$, and let $U\subset W$ be
nonempty and relatively open, consisting of polynomials positive on a
compact interval. For a fixed point $t_0$ in that interval, the image of
\[
 (F_1,\ldots,F_n)\longmapsto
 \prod_iF_i(t)/\prod_iF_i(t_0),\qquad F_i\in U,
\]
has generic local dimension $n(r-1)$ and contains a smooth patch of that
dimension with a continuous local section. Every continuous likelihood-determining
encoding of factor histories needs at least $n(r-1)$ real coordinates; storing
the $n$ individually normalized factors attains this number. The assertion
includes a common polynomial divisor and $r=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Remove a greatest common divisor $G$ of $W$. It has no zero on the interval,
since $U$ contains a positive polynomial. The quotient space $\widehat W$
has dimension $r$ and no common complex zero. If $r\ge2$, let $d$ be its
maximum degree. An exact-degree polynomial can be chosen in the image of
$U$. To choose the next factor, avoid the leading-coefficient hyperplane
and the evaluation kernels at the finitely many roots already chosen.
Each kernel is a proper real linear subspace, including evaluation at a
nonreal root. A finite union of proper subspaces has empty interior.
Iteration gives $n$ exact-degree pairwise coprime factors in the open set.
The nonvanishing conditions are open and dense.

At such a tuple the multiplication differential is
$\sum_i\dot F_i\prod_{j\ne i}F_j$. Reduction modulo $F_i$ gives
$F_i\mid\dot F_i$ in its kernel, and the degree bound gives
$\dot F_i=\lambda_i F_i$. The sole relation is $\sum_i\lambda_i=0$.
Its rank is therefore $nr-(n-1)$. Normalization removes one scalar
direction. The rank is locally constant at these tuples, and the
constant-rank theorem gives the patch and local section. Multiplication by
the known nonvanishing function $G^n/G(t_0)^n$ changes neither conclusion.
Composing any exact continuous encoding with the section is injective;
invariance of domain excludes fewer than $n(r-1)$ coordinates. Conversely,
$F_i/F_i(t_0)$ lies in a fixed affine hyperplane of $W$ of dimension $r-1$,
so recording its coordinates for all $i$ suffices. Permutations need not be
identified by a factor-history encoding. For $r=1$ every normalized factor
is fixed and the conclusion is immediate.
\end{proof}

This theorem concerns a fixed factor space, not an arbitrary union of
mode-dependent spaces. For a finite calibrated detector with coefficient
matrix of rank $r$, the cube image has relative interior in its range;
a right inverse on that range supplies the hypotheses. A fixed positive
rational denominator can be retained as a known factor for likelihood
encoding. The decision theorem below is first proved for normalized
polynomial channels with constant denominator; it does not erase a
parameter-dependent denominator.
