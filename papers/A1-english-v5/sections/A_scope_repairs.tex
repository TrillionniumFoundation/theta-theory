\subsection{Inherited M1: constraints with a retained bias}
\begin{proposition}[Biased mean constraint and event constraint]\label{prop:biased}
Let $P$ be a probability, let $C$ be a bounded vector observable and $B$ a
bounded real observable, and put
$Q^{\xi,B}=e^{\xi\cdot C+B}P/Z$, $Z=P e^{\xi\cdot C+B}$.
If $Q^{\xi,B}C=c$, then for every $Q\ll P$ with $QC=c$,
\begin{equation}
 D(Q\Vert P)-QB=D(Q\Vert Q^{\xi,B})+\xi\cdot c-\log Z.
 \label{eq:biased-gap}
\end{equation}
Thus $Q^{\xi,B}$ uniquely minimizes $D(Q\Vert P)-QB$ on this constraint
class. In the explicitly bias-free case $B=0$, it uniquely minimizes bare
relative entropy. The same bare-entropy conclusion holds when $QB$ is
constant on the entire constraint class. Separately, if $P(A)>0$, the
unique minimum-entropy law supported on $A$ is $P_A=P(\cdot\mid A)$ and
$D(Q\Vert P)=D(Q\Vert P_A)-\log P(A)$ there.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The tilted density is bounded above and below relative to $P$. Its logarithm
is $\xi\cdot C+B-\log Z$. Integrating the identity for the logarithm of the
density ratio gives \eqref{eq:biased-gap}, including infinite entropies:
only bounded quantities are subtracted. Nonnegativity of relative entropy,
with equality exactly at equal laws, proves uniqueness and the special
cases. On $A$, the density of $P_A$ relative to $P$ is $1/P(A)$; the same
calculation gives the event formula. An exact event of zero probability
requires a separately specified disintegration, not this formula.
\end{proof}

For $P=(1/2,1/2)$, $C=0$ and $B=(0,\log3)$, the tilt $(1/4,3/4)$ is
not the bare-entropy optimizer. It is precisely the optimizer of the biased
objective in \eqref{eq:biased-gap}. No change is made to the bounded-source
Gibbs identity itself.

\subsection{Inherited M2: both density traces at a moving interface}
Let a bounded domain be partitioned into finitely many moving smooth
subdomains $D_\gamma(a)$. Internal interfaces have a normal from their minus
side to their plus side and normal parameter velocity $v_\Sigma$. Assume
$C^1$ local transports, branchwise $C^1$ extensions of
$\rho_{a,\gamma},V_{a,\gamma},F_{a,\gamma}$, and bounds sufficient to
differentiate their transported integrals and boundary traces. A dot below
means the Eulerian derivative $\partial_a$ at fixed spatial position.
Fixed external boundaries have zero normal velocity. For a moving outer
boundary $\Gamma$, let $v_\Gamma$ be its outward normal velocity and take
the interior branch trace there. Define
\[
 J_a(F)=\sum_\gamma\int_{D_\gamma(a)}
                \rho_{a,\gamma}e^{V_{a,\gamma}}F_{a,\gamma}\,dx.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Branch-density shape response]\label{thm:two-trace}
With the two one-sided density traces retained,
\begin{align}
 J_a'(F)&=\sum_\gamma\int_{D_\gamma(a)}e^{V_\gamma}
  \{\dot\rho_\gamma F_\gamma+\rho_\gamma\dot F_\gamma
                         +\rho_\gamma F_\gamma\dot V_\gamma\}\,dx\notag\\
 &\quad+\sum_\Sigma\int_\Sigma v_\Sigma
    \{\rho_-e^{V_-}F_--\rho_+e^{V_+}F_+\}\,dS\notag\\
 &\quad+\sum_\Gamma\int_\Gamma v_\Gamma\rho_{\rm in}
                      e^{V_{\rm in}}F_{\rm in}\,dS.
 \label{eq:two-trace}
\end{align}
The last sum is zero for a fixed outer domain, or when the fields vanish
near the outer boundary. If the internal density traces agree, the internal
sum reduces to the common-density formula. For $Z_a=J_a(1)>0$, the normalized
derivative is $\{J_a'(F)-(J_a(F)/Z_a)J_a'(1)\}/Z_a$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pull each branch integral back by its transport $\chi_a$ to a fixed domain.
For $f=\rho_\gamma e^{V_\gamma}F_\gamma$ and transport velocity
$w=\partial_a\chi_a\circ\chi_a^{-1}$, its derivative is
$\int_{D_\gamma}(\partial_af+\nabla f\cdot w+f\operatorname{div}w)$.
The last two terms are $\operatorname{div}(fw)$. Apply the divergence
theorem. On a common internal interface the outward normals are opposite,
so the two fluxes add to $v_\Sigma(f_--f_+)$. Substituting each branch's
own density gives the internal term in \eqref{eq:two-trace}. On an external
boundary there is only the interior contribution $v_\Gamma f_{\rm in}$.
The dot in the bulk is $\partial_a$ rather than the material derivative,
so this flux is not counted twice. The normalized formula is the quotient rule.
\end{proof}

The review's compactly supported example
$J(a)=\int_0^a\psi^2+2\int_a^1\psi^2$ gives
$J'(a)=-\psi(a)^2$, exactly the two-trace term. The ambient smooth density
in the inherited whole-equilibrium collision calculation already has a
common trace and remains a valid special case.

\subsection{Inherited M3 and current M1: trajectories, reports, and preparations}
A regular finite-event chamber has a fixed itinerary of at most $M$ events,
common compact neighborhoods, $C^{r+1}$ flights, guards and resets, positive
separation from competing guards and endpoint events, and guard
transversality $|g_t+D_zg\,f^-|\ge\kappa>0$.

\begin{theorem}[Chamber response with an explicit preparation]\label{thm:transport-prep}
In such a chamber, event times, one-sided event states, and the terminal
\emph{state} are $C^r$ jointly in the physical parameter and initial state,
with uniform bounds on the relevant compact set. A terminal report has this
regularity when its specified map from these quantities and the parameter
is jointly $C^r$ on a common neighborhood, with bounded derivatives through
order $r$. No regularity is asserted for an arbitrary Borel readout.

For integration, assume either a fixed finite preparation measure, or explicitly
\begin{equation}
 \mu_a=Z_a^{-1}(X_a)_\#(w_a\lambda),\qquad
 Z_a=\int w_a\,d\lambda\ge z_*>0,
 \label{eq:transport-prep}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is fixed, $w_a\ge0$, and $X_a$ takes values in the common
chamber. The transports and weights are jointly measurable and $C^r$ in
the parameter for $\lambda$-almost every source point. Require a common
integrable envelope for every derivative product through order $r$ of the
composed weighted tests. Then preparation integrals are $C^r$, with all
transport, weight, observable and normalizer derivatives retained.

For the law, let the smooth report take values in a fixed compact smooth
$d$-dimensional manifold without boundary, with a fixed Sobolev structure;
or let its range lie in a compact subset of a fixed $d$-dimensional ambient
torus after a specified smooth embedding. Here $d$ is the dimension of the
space in which $H^{-s}$ is taken, not necessarily the intrinsic dimension of
an embedded range. Require the analogous integrable envelopes for the
weighted report jets. The report law is strongly $C^r$ in $H^{-s}$ when
$s>d/2+r$. Finite tags use a fixed finite direct sum of such spaces.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A flight has the joint smooth dependence given by its variational ODE.
At an event the implicit function theorem gives a smooth event time,
because the guard time derivative is bounded away from zero. In particular,
for an incoming sensitivity $s^-$ evaluated at the nominal event time,
\[
 \tau'=-\frac{g_a+D_zg\,s^-}{g_t+D_zg\,f^-},\qquad
 s^+=D_z\mathcal R\,s^-+\mathcal R_a
       +(D_z\mathcal R f^-+\mathcal R_t-f^+)\tau'.
\]
The last term includes comparison at the same physical time; this is the
standard saltation mechanism \cite{KongEtAl}. Repeating the implicit
function theorem and reset composition finitely many times proves the
joint $C^r$ state statement and its bounds. Positive guard separation
prevents an unnoticed itinerary change. Composing with the specified smooth
report map, with all its parameter derivatives retained, proves the report
statement. Smooth mechanics alone cannot supply this last hypothesis.

Let $Y_a(z)$ denote the report from the initial point $X_a(z)$. Before
normalization the integral is $J_a=\int w_a(z)F_a(Y_a(z))\lambda(dz)$.
Its first derivative is
\[
 J_a'=\int\{\dot w_aF_a(Y_a)
       +w_a\dot F_a(Y_a)+w_a DF_a(Y_a)\,\dot Y_a\}\,d\lambda,
\]
where $\dot Y_a$ includes the initial transport derivative $\dot X_a$.
At order $j$ use the finite Leibniz and chain rules in this same fixed-domain
integral. The stated envelopes justify dominated differentiation and
continuity at every order. Differentiate $Z_a^{-1}J_a$ by the quotient
recursion; do not identify fixed support with fixed weights.

Finally, on a torus the $j$th derivative of $x\mapsto\delta_x$ has squared
$H^{-s}$ norm bounded by a constant times
$\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^d}(1+|k|^2)^{-s}|k|^{2j}$, which is finite for
$s>d/2+r$. Fourier dominated convergence gives strong $C^r$ dependence.
A finite smooth atlas and partition of unity give the same assertion on the
fixed compact manifold. The stipulated ambient torus handles an embedded
range, using its ambient dimension in the inequality. Finite direct sums
handle tags. Apply the chain rule and the assumed integrable jet envelopes
to $\int w_a\delta_{Y_a}\,d\lambda$, then normalize. This proves the strong
distributional assertion. A fixed preparation is the special case of
parameter-independent weights and transport.
\end{proof}

The cusp preparation
$(1/2+|a|)\delta_0+(1/2-|a|)\delta_1$ is excluded by the weight hypothesis,
not by a fictitious failure of smooth identity dynamics. Likewise the
current referee's no-event example $\dot x=a$, $x(0)=0$, $T=1$ has a smooth
terminal state, but $Y=\one_{\{x(T)>0\}}=\one_{\{a>0\}}$ is discontinuous.
It is excluded from the smooth-report clause, not from the measurable
instrument construction. The cartridge model instead integrates the fixed
preparation \eqref{eq:prep} and handles its moving physical guard by the
exact whole-preparation flux calculation, rather than applying a
regular-chamber assertion at grazing. Its integrated detector response is
proved independently by Theorem~\ref{thm:response}.
