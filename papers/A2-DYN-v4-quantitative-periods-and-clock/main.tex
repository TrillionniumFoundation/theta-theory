\begin{abstract}
We study the joint displacement, collision count and flight time of actual
first-return records in triangular periodic billiards with disk radius in
$[0.45,0.47]$. A convex optical action constructs a family of true periodic
orbits whose positive excess-length increments tend to zero. Together with
explicit winding cycles, this determines the complete joint periodic
annihilator. Uniform two-sided exponential bounds for the increments give
an explicit polynomial phase separation at every large roof frequency,
using three periods with logarithmic collision length, and a lower bound
for continuous circle-valued approximate phases. The real period determinant
is nonzero and independent of the radius.

We derive the complete marked suspension law, the raw critical-edge
coefficient and a local Fourier inversion criterion allowing nonvanishing
edge mass away from a central window. The original density is recovered
by an exact decomposition, rather than a smoothed observation model.
For each fixed return count the raw mixed density is continuous in total
variation and the record is continuous in first moment as the radius
varies. Exact return means and compactness give uniform integrability.
A compact subadditive argument then yields a parameter-uniform functional
law for the first-order mechanical clock, including unfinished returns over
an entire growing observation interval. We give the resulting geometric
comparison of collision rates and retain the weighted positive-denominator
and Gaussian-scale interfaces. A full uniform raw density local limit
requires the common operator, measurable-cohomology, covariance and
all-branch residual estimates specified in the article; none is inferred
from periodic separation or a first-order clock law alone.
\end{abstract}
