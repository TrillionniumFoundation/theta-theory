\title[Hankel compatibility and finite-bit memory]{General Theta Foundations I: Hankel Compatibility, Distortion Rates, and Finite-Bit Memory}
\author{Qian Qi}
\date{26 September 2026; revision 44}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We place numerical orthogonal-word experiments in the finite-horizon
positive Hankel realization framework and compare two previously
separate complexity profiles. The optimized loss of any finite word
packet, divided logarithmically by its length, is bounded by the
logarithmic dilation of every compatible common polytope of the same
cardinality. The proof allows arbitrary hidden stochastic states and
removes initialization loss by repetition. For planar alphabets we
construct a uniform table-free simulator using finite random bits and
$O(\log N+\log(1/\varepsilon))$ total work bits, including parameter
construction, counters and arithmetic. The explicit badly approximable
families require $\Theta(\log N)$ work bits at fixed positive accuracy.
The atomic label-width exponents, minimal-type metric bounds and
Liouville fluctuations of the preceding development are retained with
complete proofs. Atomic nonuniform width, clean register cardinality
and the full finite-bit workspace are distinguished throughout. The
profile comparison is one-sided; it is not a general solution of
minimal positive realization or of arbitrary arithmetic width.
\end{abstract}
