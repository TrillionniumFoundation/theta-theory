\section{Realization theory, arithmetic and computational semantics}\label{sec:comparison}
\paragraph{Positive order and cone invariance.}
In the classical stationary problem one seeks a fixed nonnegative
state-space realization of a transfer function. Minimal signed order
need not equal minimal positive order. Benvenuti--Farina's Theorems
2--3 formulate positive existence by an invariant polyhedral cone,
either in a minimal linear realization or in a response-sequence space
\cite{BF04}; the 2022 survey concerns positive minimality \cite{Ben22}.
Our enclosure construction is such a positive-cone construction after
homogenization. The competing machines are different: their rows may
vary with the cut and their entire realization may be redesigned for
$N$. Proposition~\ref{prop:hankel-form} makes the corresponding finite
controlled normal form explicit. Classical stationary order cannot
be substituted for its least simultaneous profile.

\paragraph{Hankel rank and signed weighted automata.}
For fixed seed and query the mean series is
$w\mapsto\rho\ip{e_j}{U_wx}$, with a signed linear representation of
dimension at most $D$. Adding the constant probability offset requires
at most $D+1$ dimensions. Balle--Panangaden--Precup's Theorem 2 identifies
minimal weighted-automaton dimension with infinite Hankel rank
\cite{BPP15}. That is the signed, all-length invariant, not stochastic
label cardinality. Their Theorem 11 bounds an $\ell^2$ approximation
of absolutely summable rational series. Here the unweighted all-word
probability series is not summable, and the objective is the maximum
numerical error on a fixed length. A small weighted mean-square error
under a chosen word law does not control rare-word conditional error.
For an explicitly unweighted finite table, Frobenius error does bound
entrywise error, but its optimizer still need not be stochastic or
compatible. Equation~\eqref{eq:three-ranks} exhibits the precise failure
of separate ranks to detect the present growing resource.

\paragraph{Stochastic and hidden Markov realization.}
Finesso--Grassi--Spreij use pseudo-Hankel nonnegative factorizations and
an informational-divergence criterion to approximate a stationary
output process at prescribed hidden order \cite[Sections III--IV]{FGS10}.
Their second realization step is itself evidence that a static
factorization is not the complete dynamic specification. Our alphabet
is externally controlled rather than generated as an observation
process. Seed resets and selected query columns are separately specified;
worst-word conditional probabilities replace a divergence between
output laws. These are explicit changes of datum and objective, not
a claim that Hankel positivity was absent from HMM theory.

Ohta's reachable and null-space reductions, Theorems 6--9 and Remark 10,
identify tensor and generalized-Hankel dimensions in the effective
linear space of an HMM \cite{Ohta20}. We use the same need to distinguish
observable rank from hidden state count, but do not infer a positive
stochastic basis from a signed quotient. His Section 4.4 also discusses
rotational examples descended from earlier HMM realization work.
Rotation-based rank/positive-order separations are therefore not claimed
new here. Our quantitative theorems concern all horizon-specific
stochastic rows and preserve bounded numerical responses on every
controlled word.

\paragraph{Kronecker quasi-uniformity.}
Dick--Goda--Larcher--Pillichshammer--Suzuki \cite{DGLPS}, Theorem~3.9, characterize quasi-uniformity of the multidimensional sequence $n\alpha\pmod{\Z^r}$ by the simultaneous bound $\eta_\alpha(q)\ge c q^{-1/r}$. Their Lemma~3.11 bounds ratios of successive best simultaneous denominators; Section~3.3 also contains the vector $(2^{1/(r+1)},\ldots,2^{r/(r+1)})$. These are direct arithmetic antecedents, not new examples of this paper.

Our packet points instead have the form $m\cdot\alpha\pmod1$. The relevant separation is dual. The residue argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:weak-denominator}, with an arbitrary nearest-integer numerator and $\eta_\alpha(q)$ in place of $Q^{-1}$, proves directly that a dual bound with exponent $r$ implies
\[
             \eta_\alpha(q)\ge b r^{-r-1}q^{-1/r}.
\]
Thus the cited quasi-uniformity theorem applies to the uniform badly approximable class used in the predecessor. Its denominator control supplies the arithmetic scales for a polygon upper bound. It supplies neither a compatible stochastic transition table nor a lower bound against discarded hidden history. The logarithmic separation of Lemma~\ref{lem:metric-separation} is weaker than a uniform badly approximable inequality; it is proved by a summable tail estimate, not asserted to imply quasi-uniformity.

\paragraph{What is dynamic in the paired profiles.}
The geometry of nearest-center distortion and convex hull approximation is classical \cite{GL,Vidyasagar}. A static $k$-center factorization does not specify how its encoder is calculated without retaining discarded inputs. Conversely, a convex codebook does not force arbitrary hidden states to possess coordinates in that codebook. The two implications in Section~\ref{sec:profiles} avoid both identifications: the converse conditions on the actual state, and the upper theorem provides the same legal rows throughout the run. The packing--dilation balance in Corollary~\ref{cor:balance} is a conditional theorem on independently specified finite profiles, not a claimed equality of static and dynamic optima.

The circle program makes one of these profiles exactly finite, whereas the all-harmonic action-gap optimization of the predecessor remains infinite-dimensional. A finite word length can be exhaustively listed; an unbounded harmonic degree cannot be certified by a finite truncation without a tail theorem. No general action-gap algorithm follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-profile}.

\paragraph{Branching programs and probability preservation.}
At fixed horizon our machine is a probabilistic ordered read-once layered program with arbitrary stochastic rows and bounded real output columns. We distinguish width from total size, uniform description and random-bit complexity. Fourier growth for regular, permutation and width-three branching programs concerns different hypotheses and objectives \cite{RSV,SVW,LPV}. Circle-walk harmonic damping and its arithmetic behavior are also established subjects \cite{BB}. Our converse can use correlated letters inside independently sampled complete packets; it is not a theorem about average error under fair independent letters.

Preserving a strict cutpoint sign is weaker than preserving a numerical probability. The predecessor's constant-width sign construction contracts the output amplitude with length and hence does not contradict the present width growth. Here signal and tolerance are fixed, one query is selected after the word, and neither joint answers nor anytime correctness is required. The exact arithmetic upper rows are atomic real kernels. Section~\ref{sec:bits44} gives a separate finite-bit approximation theorem; it does not represent those exact rows by a finite number of fair bits. A row with three successors can still have costly or irrational coefficients.

\paragraph{Conclusions.}
The finite optimized word profile and the compatible enclosure profile retain quantitative information which qualitative gap data discard. They yield a minimal-type exponent and an almost-everywhere logarithmic estimate, and they exhibit fluctuating numerical width for every Liouville single angle. The reflected versions show that these conclusions survive a particular noncommuting finite extension, without asserting a classification of arbitrary noncommuting nongapped alphabets.

Exact finite widths, the Liouville upper logarithmic limit, optimal enclosure shapes and equality in the distortion--dilation comparison remain distinct quantitative problems. The new profile comparison uses no external spectral gap. For fixed computable planar angles, storing all command counts already gives a classical $O(\log N)$ finite-accuracy work-space upper bound. We do not claim novelty for that upper order alone. The compiler proved here additionally maintains an arithmetic-scale clean stochastic register, generates circulant dyadic rows without a large advice table, and explicitly prices every arithmetic and random-bit register. The earlier expansion, calibration and representation theorems retain their original hypotheses in the unchanged supporting article.
