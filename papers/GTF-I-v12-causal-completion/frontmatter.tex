\begin{abstract}
We study marked causal experiments used by a common finite-register
encoder. Executable comparison charges persistent state and public
randomness, while complete-state fusion identifies when this cost
cancels. On finite supported trees, exact common-task width profiles are
characterized by causally closed compatible covers, without positive
report probabilities or unique optimal actions. Randomization does not
change these profiles, and infeasibility has a positive risk witness.
For summable infinite-horizon criteria we prove compact risk bodies,
attained minimax duality, and finite-seed approximation from dominated
finite-prefix effects, without domination of the complete path law.
An unbounded-generator construction retains the orthogonal dynamics
and initial forcing in compressed memory. A collision-compatible
hard-sphere graph core yields a Galerkin approximation whose actual
marked observation error is uniform over all finite-register consumers.
Costed comparison then gives stable decision certificates for exact
resource profiles. The finite duality, marked quotient, synthesis,
Gaussian, filtering and measure-valued consequences are retained.
\end{abstract}
