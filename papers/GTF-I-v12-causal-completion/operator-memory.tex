\section{Unbounded generators, forced memory, and physical decision limits}
\label{sec:v12-memory}

We now connect the causal comparison results to the microscopic transport
and compressed-memory objects. The analytic input is a generator-domain
construction, not a claim that a finite positive model has replaced the
physical dynamics. The finite-rank Mori--Zwanzig mechanism is classical;
in particular the domain and bounded-perturbation issues are treated in
\cite{WidderZimmerSchilling2025,WidderSchilling2026}. We give the block
proof in the form needed for a quantitative marked-acquisition comparison,
including the unresolved initial component and the full observable.

\subsection{A common domain for resolved and orthogonal dynamics}
Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space, with inner product linear in the first
argument. Let $L$ generate a strongly continuous contraction semigroup
$U(t)$, and let $P$ be an orthogonal finite-rank projection such that
\begin{equation}
 R=PH\subseteq D(L)\cap D(L^*),\qquad Q=I-P.
 \label{eq:v12-domain}
\end{equation}
The restriction $LP$ is bounded. The restriction $PL$ on $D(L)$ extends
to a bounded map on $H$: if $e_1,\ldots,e_k$ is an orthonormal basis of
$R$, its extension is $f\mapsto\sum_i\langle f,L^*e_i\rangle e_i$.
Define
\begin{equation}
 A=PLP:R\to R,\quad B=\overline{PL}Q:QH\to R,
 \quad C=QLP:R\to QH,\quad D=QLQ\big|_{QD(L)}.
 \label{eq:v12-blocks}
\end{equation}
Here $D$ acts in $QH$. The bar denotes the bounded extension, not a
new choice of a generator domain.

\begin{theorem}[Forced memory with an explicit orthogonal generator]
\label{thm:v12-memory}
Under \eqref{eq:v12-domain}, $D$ generates a strongly continuous
contraction semigroup $T_Q(t)$ on $QH$. For every $f\in H$, the resolved
trajectory $x(t)=PU(t)f$ is continuously differentiable and satisfies
\begin{align}
 x'(t)&=Ax(t)+\int_0^t K(t-s)x(s)\,ds+\eta_f(t),
       & x(0)&=Pf,\label{eq:v12-volterra}\\
 K(t)&=BT_Q(t)C,
       &\eta_f(t)&=BT_Q(t)Qf.\label{eq:v12-kernel}
\end{align}
For $\sigma>0$, with $k=\|B\|\|C\|$,
\begin{equation}
 \|K(t)\|\le k,\qquad
 \int_0^\infty e^{-\sigma t}\|K(t)\|\,dt\le k/\sigma.
 \label{eq:v12-kernel-bound}
\end{equation}
The compressed resolvent $\mathsf C(z)=P(z-L)^{-1}P|_R$ is invertible for
$\Re z>0$, and
\begin{align}
 \mathsf C(z)^{-1}&=zI_R-A-B(z-D)^{-1}C,\label{eq:v12-schur}\\
 \|\mathsf C(z)^{-1}\|&\le |z|+\|A\|+k/(\Re z).
 \label{eq:v12-schur-bound}
\end{align}
The forcing transform is both $B(z-D)^{-1}Qf$ and
$\mathsf C(z)^{-1}P(z-L)^{-1}Qf$. If $L$ is skew-adjoint, $D$ is
skew-adjoint on $QH$, $B=-C^*$, and
\begin{equation}
 K(t)=-C^*T_Q(t)C,\qquad K(0)=-C^*C.
 \label{eq:v12-sign}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $P H\subseteq D(L)$, $D(L)=R\oplus QD(L)$. On this domain
$L$ has blocks $A,B,C,D$. The off-diagonal operator
$F=PBQ+QCP$, with the evident inclusions of its ranges in $H$, is
bounded. Equivalently it acts by $F(u+v)=Bv+Cu$.
The bounded-perturbation construction shows that
$L_0=L-F$ on $D(L)$ is a generator. One can obtain its semigroup directly
by the norm-convergent Dyson series on bounded time intervals; the
$n$th perturbation term is bounded by $\|F\|^n t^n/n!$.

For $u\in R$ and $v\in QD(L)$,
\[
 \Re\langle L_0(u+v),u+v\rangle
   =\Re\langle Lu,u\rangle+\Re\langle Lv,v\rangle\le0.
\]
For domain initial data the derivative of the squared norm of the
$L_0$ orbit is nonpositive. Density extends the contraction bound to all
initial data. The projection $P$ preserves the domain and commutes with
$L_0$, and hence with its resolvent and semigroup. Its restrictions are
$e^{tA}$ and a contraction semigroup on $QH$ with generator precisely
$D$. This proves the asserted existence and domain, without writing an
undefined exponential of $QLQ$.

Bounded off-diagonal perturbation and variation of constants now give,
for $x(t)=PU(t)f$ and $y(t)=QU(t)f$,
\begin{align*}
 x(t)&=e^{tA}Pf+\int_0^t e^{(t-s)A}By(s)\,ds,\\
 y(t)&=T_Q(t)Qf+\int_0^t T_Q(t-s)Cx(s)\,ds.
\end{align*}
The first expression is continuously differentiable because $A$ is
bounded and $By(s)$ is continuous. Substituting the second proves
\eqref{eq:v12-volterra}--\eqref{eq:v12-kernel} for every $f\in H$.
Contractivity gives \eqref{eq:v12-kernel-bound}. The operator-valued
kernel is norm continuous, since $C$ has finite-dimensional domain and
$T_Q$ is strongly continuous.

For $\Re z>0$, $z-D$ has a bounded inverse of norm at most
$(\Re z)^{-1}$. Solving the lower block of $(z-L)(u+v)=g+h$ yields
$v=(z-D)^{-1}(h+Cu)$. The upper block is
\[
 [zI_R-A-B(z-D)^{-1}C]u=g+B(z-D)^{-1}h.
\]
The bracket is injective: a null vector would give a null vector of
$z-L$ in its domain. It is therefore invertible in the finite-dimensional
space $R$. Taking $h=0$ identifies its inverse with $\mathsf C(z)$;
taking $g=0$ proves the forcing identity. The norm estimate follows from
the resolvent bound for $D$.

If $L^*=-L$, the off-diagonal operator $F$ is bounded and skew-adjoint,
so $L_0=L-F$ is skew-adjoint on $D(L)$. Its reducing restrictions are
skew-adjoint, and the adjoint relation of the off-diagonal blocks gives
$B=-C^*$. This proves \eqref{eq:v12-sign}.
\end{proof}

Two distinctions in this statement matter. First, $Qf=0$ suffices to
remove the forcing, but this cannot be assumed for an arbitrary physical
observable. Second, \eqref{eq:v12-kernel-bound} is a discounted bound on
the right half-plane. It does not assert decay of $K(t)$ or meromorphic
continuation across the imaginary axis. Those require additional
spectral information. In particular no finiteness of left-half-plane
transmission zeros is inferred from finite rank alone.

\subsection{A stability estimate which retains the full observable}
Let $\widehat x$ solve a finite-dimensional Volterra equation with data
$\widehat A,\widehat K,\widehat\eta,\widehat x_0$, where the kernel and
forcing are locally integrable. Suppose $\widehat x$ is continuous on
$[0,T]$ and put $M_T=\sup_{t\le T}\|\widehat x(t)\|$. Define
\begin{align}
 B_T={}&\|Pf-\widehat x_0\|+T\|A-\widehat A\|M_T
       +\int_0^T\|\eta_f(s)-\widehat\eta(s)\|\,ds\nonumber\\
 &+M_T\int_0^T(T-s)\|K(s)-\widehat K(s)\|\,ds.
 \label{eq:v12-BT}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Resolved and reconstructed error]
\label{prop:v12-stability}
For $k=\|B\|\|C\|$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\le T}\|x(t)-\widehat x(t)\|
       \le B_T\cosh(\sqrt{k}\,T).
 \label{eq:v12-cosh}
\end{equation}
If $\widehat y(t)\in QH$ satisfies
\[
 \chi_T=\sup_{t\le T}
 \left\|\widehat y(t)-T_Q(t)Qf
               -\int_0^t T_Q(t-s)C\widehat x(s)\,ds\right\|<\infty,
\]
then the reconstructed full observable obeys
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\le T}\|U(t)f-\widehat x(t)-\widehat y(t)\|
 \le (1+T\|C\|)B_T\cosh(\sqrt{k}\,T)+\chi_T.
 \label{eq:v12-full-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write the difference equation with the unperturbed $A$ as its local
part and use the contractivity of $e^{tA}$. Fubini and
$\|K(s)\|\le k$ show that $e(t)=\|x(t)-\widehat x(t)\|$ satisfies
\[
 e(t)\le B_T+k\int_0^t(t-s)e(s)\,ds\quad(0\le t\le T).
\]
Iteration yields the series
$B_T\sum_{n\ge0}k^n t^{2n}/(2n)!$; the remainder tends to zero because
$e$ is bounded on the compact interval. This is \eqref{eq:v12-cosh}.
Subtract the expression for $y(t)$ in the preceding proof from the
specified reconstruction. Contractivity of $T_Q$ gives
$\|y(t)-\widehat y(t)\|\le T\|C\|\sup e+\chi_T$.
Adding the resolved error proves \eqref{eq:v12-full-error}.
\end{proof}

The last term cannot be dropped merely because a resolved trajectory is
accurate. A projected observable $PU(t)f$ is not the actual observable
$U(t)f$ unless its orthogonal component is accounted for. This is the
operator counterpart of preserving the actual marked target in an
experiment comparison.

\subsection{Galerkin convergence from the microscopic graph core}
We specialize to the weighted symmetric $L^2$ space of the almost-everywhere
hard-sphere flow in Lemma~\ref{lem:v11-flow}. The measure $\mu$ is an
invariant probability; it may be the normalized invariant measure of a
finite particle sector or a summable mixture of such sectors with the
stated weights. The Koopman group is unitary and its generator $L$ is
skew-adjoint. The graph core in Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-core} supplies a
countable real core: take countable dense smooth interior inputs,
rational orbit-smoothing scales, and their finite linear combinations.
Separability of the graph norm allows a countable selection. Gram--Schmidt
on an enumeration gives nested finite-dimensional spaces $V_n$ such that
\[
 V_n\subset D(L),\qquad \bigcup_n V_n\text{ is a graph core for }L.
\]
Let $P_n$ be their orthogonal projections and set $G_n=P_nLP_n$ on $H$.
It is bounded and skew-adjoint; on $V_n^\perp$ it is zero.

\begin{theorem}[Microscopic graph-core Galerkin limit]
\label{thm:v12-galerkin}
For every $f\in H$ and every finite $T$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}\|e^{tG_n}P_nf-U(t)f\|_2\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:v12-galerkin}
\end{equation}
Moreover $(z-G_n)^{-1}$ converges strongly to $(z-L)^{-1}$ for
$\Re z>0$. Every finite $V_n$ satisfies the common-domain hypothesis
\eqref{eq:v12-domain}, and hence has the forced-memory representation
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-memory}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $f$ belong to the union of the $V_n$. For sufficiently large $n$,
$G_nf=P_nLf\to Lf$. The resolvent bounds for skew-adjoint operators and
the identity
\[
 (z-G_n)^{-1}(z-L)f-f
       =(z-G_n)^{-1}(G_n-L)f
\]
give convergence to zero. The set of vectors $(z-L)f$ for $f$ in the
core is dense in $H$: approximate $(z-L)^{-1}g$ in the graph norm.
The common resolvent bound extends convergence to every $g\in H$.

We also give an exponential-formula argument for the time-uniform
conclusion. For any contraction generator $A$ and $f\in D(A)$,
\[
 (I-tA/m)^{-m}f=\E[e^{sA}f]\quad
 \text{with }s\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(m,t/m).
\]
The formula follows by iterating the Laplace resolvent representation.
Since $\E s=t$, $\operatorname{Var}s=t^2/m$, and
$\|e^{sA}f-e^{tA}f\|\le |s-t|\|Af\|$, it gives
\begin{equation}
 \|(I-tA/m)^{-m}f-e^{tA}f\|
       \le t\|Af\|/\sqrt m.
 \label{eq:v12-euler}
\end{equation}
For a fixed core vector, $\|G_nf\|\le\|Lf\|$ eventually.
Strong resolvent convergence gives convergence of the $m$th powers in
\eqref{eq:v12-euler} for fixed $m$ and $t>0$; their factors are uniformly
bounded. First let $n\to\infty$ and then $m\to\infty$ to obtain pointwise
time convergence. The orbit Lipschitz bounds for this core vector are
uniform in $n$, so a finite time net makes the convergence uniform on
$[0,T]$. Density and contractivity extend it to all $f\in H$.
Finally $P_nf\to f$, and $e^{tG_n}$ is unitary, giving
\eqref{eq:v12-galerkin}. Since $D(L^*)=D(L)$, each $V_n$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:v12-domain}.
\end{proof}

This proof is a graph-core version of the classical resolvent-to-semigroup
approximation argument. It is included to verify, rather than assume,
the approximation used in the next theorem. It is not a uniform estimate
in particle number, a Boltzmann--Grad limit, or a nonlinear kinetic
Trotter--Kato theorem.

\subsection{The common-encoder limit for actual physical observations}
Fix a family of initial densities $f_\theta$ with respect to $\mu$ such
that
\begin{equation}
 f_\theta\ge0,\quad\int f_\theta\,d\mu=1,
 \qquad \sup_\theta\|f_\theta\|_2\le D_0<\infty.
 \label{eq:v12-preparation}
\end{equation}
At checkpoint $t$, control $a$ selects a real observable $F_{t,a}\in H$
and noise level $\sigma_{t,a}>0$. It does not change the microscopic
flow. The exact and approximating reports are, respectively,
\begin{align}
 Y_t&=(U(\tau_t)F_{t,a})(Z_0)+\sigma_{t,a}\xi_t,\nonumber\\
 Y_t^{(n)}&=(e^{\tau_tG_n}P_nF_{t,a})(Z_0)+\sigma_{t,a}\xi_t,
 \label{eq:v12-observations}
\end{align}
where the displayed normal variables are independent conditional
observation noises, not a requirement that the two laws use a common
additive-noise coupling. The control is selected before its report.
There are finitely many controls at each time. Actual stage marks are
sampled by one fixed Borel channel from $Z_0$ and any specified latent
randomness and are shared in a comparison coupling. These latent marks
are not observed by an encoder unless the interface expressly supplies
them. A mark may be an observable of the microscopic future; the latent
sample and the flow determine it before the comparison is made.

Put
\begin{align}
 d_{t,a}^{(n)}&=\|U(\tau_t)F_{t,a}
                    -e^{\tau_tG_n}P_nF_{t,a}\|_2,\nonumber\\
 \zeta_t^{(n)}&=\frac{D_0}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
                   \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_t}\frac{d_{t,a}^{(n)}}{\sigma_{t,a}},
 &\varepsilon_t^{(n)}&=\min\{1,\textstyle\sum_{s\le t}\zeta_s^{(n)}\}.
 \label{eq:v12-zeta}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Width-uniform physical decision limit]
\label{thm:v12-physical}
For the instruments in \eqref{eq:v12-observations}, identity report
channels give two-sided, state-cost-one, marked prefix comparison with
errors $\varepsilon_t^{(n)}$. The comparison is uniform in the parameter,
the common-encoder width profile, all admissible feedback designs, and
each private or independent public seed signature.
For the weights in \eqref{eq:v12-tail}, define
\begin{equation}
 E_n=\sum_{t\ge0}a_t\varepsilon_t^{(n)}.
 \label{eq:v12-physical-error}
\end{equation}
Then $E_n\to0$. For every finite width profile, and indeed uniformly over
all such profiles, the full and approximating risk bodies have Hausdorff
distance at most $E_n$, their lower support values differ by at most
$E_n$, and their centered minimax spectra differ by at most $2E_n$ when
the full-history baselines use the same information pattern. These
statements also hold for a finite acquisition horizon by setting later
weights to zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple the two experiments with the same $Z_0$, the same actual marks,
and the same independent policy randomization. Until the first report
mismatch, their controls and retained states agree. Two real normal laws
of equal variance $\sigma^2$ and means $u,v$ have total variation distance
at most $|u-v|/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$. This follows by integrating the
absolute derivative of their translated density, with the factor one
half in the total variation norm. A measurable maximal coupling of the
successive report kernels therefore has this conditional mismatch bound.

The conditional law of $Z_0$ given a past report can have a large density;
we do not apply \eqref{eq:v12-preparation} to that posterior. Instead,
integrate the first-mismatch contribution under the original marginal
$f_\theta\mu$, bound the indicator of previous matching by one, and
sum over the finitely many possible controls at the step. Cauchy--Schwarz
gives the unconditional contribution at time $s$ at most
$\zeta_s^{(n)}$. The union bound proves \eqref{eq:v12-zeta}. The actual
marks remain matched whenever the transcript does, because they were
coupled through the same latent sample. The bound includes the joint
public seed, which is identical and independent in the two experiments.
The identity comparison channels themselves require no persistent state
or additional seed and do not reveal this proof coupling to a consumer.

For each fixed $t,a$, Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-galerkin} gives
$d_{t,a}^{(n)}\to0$, and hence $\varepsilon_t^{(n)}\to0$.
Because $0\le\varepsilon_t^{(n)}\le1$ and the $a_t$ sum to one,
finite-prefix truncation proves $E_n\to0$. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-transport} in both directions. The same argument
applies to unrestricted full-history consumers, so the optimal
full-history baselines differ by at most $E_n$ without changing their
control or information pattern. The final centered bound follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Combining compact duality with microscopic approximation]
\label{cor:v12-physical-duality}
Assume in addition that the density family is continuous in $L^1(\mu)$
on a compact parameter space, and that the bounded row losses are
uniformly continuous in their row index in supremum norm. With the
finite task alphabets of Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-infinite}, both physical
instruments satisfy its finite-prefix effect hypotheses. They therefore
have attained infinite-horizon public duality, and their attained values
obey the uniform error bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-physical}.
A full observable approximation certified by
\eqref{eq:v12-full-error} can replace the Galerkin approximation in
\eqref{eq:v12-zeta} with the corresponding $L^2$ error.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed finite control word, the joint report law is a mixture over
$Z_0$ of products of positive normal densities. Choose at each report time
any fixed strictly positive probability density as reference. The
resulting loss effects are in product $L^1$, since the bounded loss is
integrated against a probability density. Changing $f_\theta$ changes
any such effect in $L^1$ by at most $\|f_\theta-f_{\theta'}\|_1$ times
the loss bound: integrate out the conditional normal and marked kernels.
A supremum-norm change in the row loss gives the analogous bound.
Thus the required effect map is continuous. The arguments do not require
bounded observables or a density on the infinite report path. The last
assertion follows because the coupling proof uses only the full
observable error in $L^2$, not how it was obtained.
\end{proof}

The two dimensions in this construction must not be identified.
$\dim V_n$ is an analytic approximation parameter of the physical
observation presentation. The retained register remains $S_t$; no access
to a Galerkin coefficient vector is supplied to the consumer. The theorem
proves a uniform comparison before optimizing those registers. The
historical kinetic program additionally requires nonlinear action-sublevel
bounds, hierarchy correctors, and a joint microscopic limit. Neither a
finite-rank memory identity nor strong $L^2$ approximation proves those
additional assertions.
