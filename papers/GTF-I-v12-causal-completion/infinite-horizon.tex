\section{Infinite-horizon risk bodies and coherent comparison}
\label{sec:v12-infinite}

The resource profile and the comparison channel can be defined for an
unbounded time interval without a single reference measure dominating
all infinite trajectories. What is needed for the compactness argument
below is a coherent family of dominated finite-prefix loss effects and a
uniform tail bound for the criterion. This is a different assertion from
weak closedness of arbitrary filtered strategic measures.

\subsection{Finite-prefix hypotheses and executable policies}
Time is indexed by $t\in\N_0$. Fix finite private widths $S_t$, finite
control and decision alphabets at each time, and a finite set of task
labels available to controllers but not to the common encoder. Reports
range over standard Borel spaces $(Y_t,\mu_t)$. The parameter and task rows
form a compact metric space $J$. The controlled instrument and its actual
marks are specified by coherent stochastic kernels. A mark used in the
stage-$t$ loss is part of the actual prefix experiment at that checkpoint.
No independently regenerated target is substituted for it.

For each $N$, assume the finite-prefix loss effects satisfy the hypotheses
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-compact}: after integrating out the actual mark,
each effect for a fixed control, register, and decision string is an
$L^1(\mu_0\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_N)$ function, continuous in the row
$j$, and the losses take values in $[0,1]$. These hypotheses are local in
$N$; no uniform density bound in $N$ is required. Effects for shorter
prefixes agree with those obtained from the specified instrument. The
policy information pattern is the finite-register pattern in
Section~\ref{sec:v11-comparison}.

Let $K_t$ be the compact metrizable space of stage-$t$ weak-star encoder
rows and finite controller/readout simplexes. The initial stage has its
corresponding initial-encoder rows. Put
\[
 K=\prod_{t\ge0}K_t.
\]
This notation fixes time-dependent policies, not stationary policies.
For $\pi\in K$, denote the expected stage loss by $r_{j,t}(\pi)$. It
depends only on the first $t+1$ blocks and is a continuous map into
$C(J)$ by the finite-prefix tensor-testing argument.

Choose $a_t\ge0$ with $\sum_{t\ge0}a_t=1$, and define
\begin{equation}
 r_j(\pi)=\sum_{t\ge0}a_t r_{j,t}(\pi),\qquad
 A_N=\sum_{t>N}a_t.
 \label{eq:v12-tail}
\end{equation}
Geometric discounting is the case $a_t=(1-\beta)\beta^t$, $0<\beta<1$.
More generally all arguments apply to a row family with a common
summable dominating weight sequence.

\begin{lemma}[Coherent policy realization]
\label{lem:v12-executable}
Every point of $K$ has executable Borel representatives, and the
representatives can be chosen jointly Borel in the policy and its report
arguments. One independent uniform seed executes any Borel probability
on $K$ as a publicly randomized policy. For a fixed parameter and
feedback design, changing the chosen representatives on the reference
null sets changes none of its finite-prefix or infinite path laws.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:v10-representatives} to each of the countably many
encoder rows. Conditional averages on increasing finite report partitions
and the Borel convergence set give jointly measurable simplex-valued
representatives; on the exceptional set choose a fixed simplex point.
For each prefix there are finitely many relevant rows. Product domination
makes their reference-null exceptional sets null for every actual
open-loop law; the finite control-string expansion gives the same fact
under feedback. A countable union over prefixes remains null for the
fixed parameter and design. The resulting Borel kernels define the
infinite law by successive kernel extension. Equality on every prefix
implies equality of the infinite path laws.

The space $K$ is compact metrizable, hence standard Borel. A Borel map
from a uniform variable to $K$ realizes any probability on $K$. Evaluating
the jointly Borel representatives at the sampled policy supplies all
successive encoder rows. The seed is sampled independently of the source
and is available as specified at each time. This is an ideal public
resource; no finite storage bound is inferred from this realization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Infinite-horizon common-encoder duality]
\label{thm:v12-infinite}
Under the finite-prefix hypotheses above, the following statements hold.
The private risk body $\mathcal R(S,1)=\{r(\pi):\pi\in K\}$ is compact
in $C(J)$. For each finite public seed bound $q$, the body
$\mathcal R(S,q)$ is compact. The ideal public body is the compact convex
set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R(S,\infty)
  =\left\{\int_K r(\pi)\,\lambda(d\pi):\lambda\in\mathcal P(K)\right\}.
 \label{eq:v12-bary}
\end{equation}
For $b\in C(J)$ its minimax excess satisfies the attained duality
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\lambda\in\mathcal P(K)}\max_{j\in J}
       \left[\int r_j\,d\lambda-b_j\right]
 =\max_{w\in\mathcal P(J)}\min_{\pi\in K}
       \int_J[r_j(\pi)-b_j]\,w(dj).
 \label{eq:v12-dual}
\end{equation}
There is a saddle pair of mixed policies and row probabilities.
The truncated and full risk bodies, at the same width and public seed
signature, have Hausdorff distance at most $A_N$ in $C(J)$, after
truncation is identified with arbitrary continuation of its policy.
Consequently their minimax excesses differ by at most $A_N$ for a common
baseline, and by at most $A_N+\|b-b^{(N)}\|_\infty$ for different baselines.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The countable product $K$ is compact metrizable. Its finite-prefix risk
maps are continuous into $C(J)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:v12-tail} gives the
uniform bound
\[
 \left\|r(\pi)-\sum_{t=0}^{N}a_t r_{\cdot,t}(\pi)\right\|_\infty
       \le A_N,
\]
independently of $\pi$ and of the widths. Thus $\pi\mapsto r(\pi)$ is
continuous into $C(J)$, proving compactness of the private body. For finite
$q$, the map from $\Delta_q\times K^q$ which takes weights and policies
to their weighted risk is continuous and has exactly the $q$-seed body
as its image.

For completeness, norm continuity of the barycentre map in
\eqref{eq:v12-bary} does not follow from scalar weak convergence alone.
Cover $K$ by finitely many sets on which $r$ oscillates by at most
$\varepsilon$ in the $C(J)$ norm. A continuous partition of unity
subordinate to the cover approximates $r$ uniformly by a finite sum
$\sum_i\psi_i(\pi)r(\pi_i)$. Its barycentre depends continuously in norm
on $\lambda$ for weak convergence of probabilities. The error is at most
$\varepsilon$. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves norm continuity,
so the image of $\mathcal P(K)$ is compact and convex. Executability is
Lemma~\ref{lem:v12-executable}.

The payoff obtained by integrating $r_j-b_j$ against $\lambda$ and $w$
is continuous and affine in each probability on the two compact convex
spaces. The compact minimax theorem gives equality and attainment.
Minimizing a linear functional over $\mathcal P(K)$ is the same as
minimizing it over $K$, which gives \eqref{eq:v12-dual}. A primal minimizer
and a dual maximizer form a saddle pair by their two defining inequalities.
Equivalently the compact upper risk body is separated by positive row
functionals, as in the finite-prefix proof.

Every infinite policy restricts to a prefix policy and every prefix
policy has an arbitrary admissible continuation. The uniform tail bound
therefore gives both Hausdorff inclusions for the private bodies and,
after integrating, for every public signature. Taking suprema, infima,
and subtracting the respective baselines proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite seed cost independent of truncation time]
\label{cor:v12-seed}
The family $\{r(\pi):\pi\in K\}$ has a common modulus of continuity
$\omega$ on $J$. If $N(J,h)$ is the size of a finite $h$-net of $J$,
every ideal public risk can be approximated, in supremum norm, within
$2\omega(h)$ by a mixture of at most $N(J,h)+1$ private policies. Its
mixture index can instead be stored privately by enlarging every register
to at most $(N(J,h)+1)S_t$. The number of policies does not grow with the
number of time checkpoints at this fixed accuracy.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A compact subset of $C(J)$ is uniformly equicontinuous: take a finite
supremum-norm approximation and use the uniform continuity of its
finitely many members. This supplies $\omega(h)\downarrow0$.
For a net of size $n$, project the private risk body into $\R^n$.
Its convex hull is compact and Carath\'eodory's theorem expresses any
projected public barycentre using at most $n+1$ projected private risks.
The resulting mixture agrees exactly at the net points. The modulus for
the original risk and for the finite mixture bounds their difference at
an arbitrary row by $2\omega(h)$. To replace the public index by private
storage, initialize the joint state $(i,m_0)$, keep $i$ unchanged, and
use its selected policy at all later times. The task readouts use the
same index. This is the explicit product-state construction, and has
no finite-horizon restriction.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[No dominating measure for the complete path]
\label{ex:v12-bernoulli}
Let the reports be independent Bernoulli variables with parameter
$p\in[1/4,3/4]$. With finite decision alphabets and bounded row-continuous
stage losses, every finite prefix satisfies the effect hypotheses.
Yet the full path laws have no common $\sigma$-finite dominating measure.
Indeed the disjoint Borel sets
$E_p=\{y:\lim_n n^{-1}\sum_{i<n}y_i=p\}$ have probability one under their
respective laws. If a $\sigma$-finite measure dominated all these laws,
it would give positive measure to uncountably many disjoint sets.
Intersecting with its countable finite-measure cover and then with positive
lower measure thresholds shows this is impossible. The convergence of the
empirical mean here is the ordinary strong law, not a new ingredient.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-infinite} therefore has genuinely infinite-path
applications without assuming a dominating measure on the complete path.
\end{example}

\subsection{Costed comparison at all times}
An infinite simulator is \emph{prefix coherent} if one set of causal
kernels defines all its restrictions. At time $t$ it has state cost $K_t$.
A public simulator seed, if used, is explicitly part of the output
interface: the comparison tests its joint law with the simulated
transcript and actual marks against the target experiment supplied with
an independent copy of that seed. The same applies to an independent
consumer seed. This joint-law convention prevents a small marginal
simulation error from hiding dependence revealed by a public seed.
For deterministic or privately randomized stateless observation channels
there is no additional public interface to test.

\begin{example}[Why the simulator seed belongs to the interface]
\label{ex:v12-seedinterface}
Let the source observe a fair bit $W$, let $U$ be an independent public
fair bit, and output $Y=W\mathbin{\oplus}U$. As a law of $(Y,W)$ this
is exactly the uninformative experiment in which $Y$ is independent of
$W$. But a consumer reading $U$ can use $Y\mathbin{\oplus}U$ to recover
$W$ from the simulated interface, whereas that decision has error $1/2$
in the true uninformative experiment supplied with an independent $U$.
The total variation distance of the two joint $(Y,W,U)$ laws is $1/2$,
not zero. Thus a zero marginal error cannot justify a zero error for
this seed-aware composition. Testing the joint interface, as required
here, excludes this ambiguity without forbidding public randomization.
\end{example}

Suppose the prefix-$t$ error, uniform in parameter and output feedback
strategy in this seed-aware sense, is at most $\varepsilon_t\le1$.

\begin{theorem}[Coherent resource comparison]
\label{thm:v12-transport}
An infinite target consumer with width $S_t$ and seed bound $q$, composed
with such a simulator of width $K_t$ and seed bound $r$, has an executable
source realization with widths $K_tS_t$ and seed bound $rq$. For the
criterion \eqref{eq:v12-tail}, every row-risk difference is at most
\begin{equation}
 E_a=\sum_{t\ge0}a_t\varepsilon_t.
 \label{eq:v12-weighted-error}
\end{equation}
The upper-risk inclusion and lower-support inequality of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-comparison} hold with error $E_a$. Two prefix-coherent
simulations compose with multiplied state and seed costs and prefix
errors at most $\min\{1,\varepsilon_t+\varepsilon'_t\}$.
For two-sided cost-one simulations all private and public risk bodies
have Hausdorff distance at most $E_a$; their support values differ by at
most $E_a$. If the respective full-history baselines also differ by at
most $E_a$, their centered minimax spectra differ by at most $2E_a$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At each checkpoint retain the reachable simulator/consumer state pair.
This single infinite machine has every finite-prefix product realization
in Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-comparison}. The joint seed convention makes its
feedback rule admissible for the stated prefix comparison. A stage loss
is a $[0,1]$-valued measurable function of the prefix, the actual mark,
and the terminal randomization. The total variation bound therefore
bounds its expectation difference by $\varepsilon_t$. Sum through $N$
and bound the two remaining weighted tails by their common summable
majorant; letting $N\to\infty$ gives \eqref{eq:v12-weighted-error}.
The risk and support statements follow by the same explicit
implementation, before any optimization over consumers. Composition is
prefixwise contraction of total variation and its triangle inequality.
Two-sided comparison gives both inclusions. Subtracting a baseline with
the stated bound adds at most one further $E_a$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Geometric error accumulation]
\label{cor:v12-geometric}
If a conditional coupling at each step fails with probability at most
$e$, uniformly on previously matched prefixes, then one may take
$\varepsilon_t\le1-(1-e)^{t+1}$. Under geometric discounting,
\[
 E_a\le \frac{e}{1-\beta+\beta e}.
\]
This statement includes any stage mark in the coupled prefix. A bound
only on its marginal, with no coupling to the actual reports, does not
meet the hypothesis.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The conditional success bound iterates to probability at least
$(1-e)^{t+1}$ of matching through time $t$. Summing the resulting bound
against $(1-\beta)\beta^t$ is a geometric series. This is stronger than
using $\min\{1,(t+1)e\}$ when a conditional coupling is available.
\end{proof}

The infinite-horizon extension uses compact policy blocks, a uniform
criterion tail, and classical convex separation. It neither asserts
compactness for arbitrary average-cost problems nor charges an infinite
public design index as a finite register. The next section on physical
operator approximation will supply a different, unconditional prefix
error bound for which Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-transport}, but not the
conditional estimate in Corollary~\ref{cor:v12-geometric}, applies.
