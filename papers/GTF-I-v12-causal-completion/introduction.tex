\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:v12-introduction}

A causal experiment supplies observations at specified times. A decision
procedure with a finite retained register cannot in general replace this
interface by an equivalent full-history experiment at no cost. Information
which is supplied again by one presentation may have to be carried across
time by the other. Moreover, a single encoder serving several tasks cannot
be optimized separately for each task. These two constraints make the
attainable risk body depend on both the experiment and its resource
signature.

This paper studies that dependence for marked experiments. A mark is the
actual target variable appearing in a loss, including its coupling with
the observations and any latent parameter. The observation controller
may know the task; the common encoder does not. Private retained labels,
public seed information, and side observations supplied afresh by the
physical interface are specified separately. This distinction is needed
both for positive approximation results and for exact minimum-state
statements.

\subsection{The structural results}
The first comparison theorem charges a simulator's persistent state.
If a simulator of widths $K_t$ and seed size $r$ approximates the target
experiment with marked feedback error $\varepsilon$, then an $S_t$-state,
$q$-seed target consumer has a source implementation of widths $K_tS_t$
and seed size $rq$. The construction retains reachable pairs and gives
risk-body inclusion before optimization over consumers. Composition
multiplies the state costs and adds the errors. When a consumer's state
already determines the marked continuation class, the class register can
instead be fused with the consumer's register. This gives the exact
same-width complete-state comparison in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-fusion}. General consumers need not admit this
cancellation.

Our main exact completion result, Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-support}, identifies
which width profiles preserve a declared family of decisions throughout
a supported history tree. Its object is a causally closed compatible
cover. Each cell must admit one optimal action for every task, and all
its actual successors under a common control and report must fit in one
next cell. Histories with no such successor impose no fictitious
constraint. The existence of these covers is equivalent to an exact
causal common encoder, whether deterministic, privately randomized, or
publicly randomized. Thus neither a coarsest partition nor a positive
next-report hypothesis is needed. Corollary~\ref{cor:v12-gap} places this
combinatorial characterization on the zero face of the minimax risk body:
infeasibility has a positive decision witness $\alpha\gamma$, explicitly
specified by the finite task table. Completing Brier prediction tests
recovers the universal marked continuation quotient.

The nonfinite theory has two complementary extensions. The dominated
finite-horizon compactness theorem uses weak-star policy rows and
$L^1$-continuous loss effects. A conditional-kernel version permits a
physically redisplayed nonatomic covariate, and hence singular full
report laws. Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-infinite} then treats an unbounded time
interval under coherent dominated finite-prefix effects and a summable
criterion. With $r_{j,t}\in[0,1]$ and $\sum a_t=1$, it proves compact
attainable risk bodies and the attained identity
\[
 \min_{\lambda}\max_j\left\{\int\sum_{t\ge0}a_t r_{j,t}(\pi)
                    \,\lambda(d\pi)-b_j\right\}
 =\max_w\min_\pi\int\left\{\sum_{t\ge0}a_t r_{j,t}(\pi)-b_j\right\}w(dj).
\]
No measure dominating the entire infinite path is assumed. For a fixed
accuracy the finite public-seed approximation depends on row covering,
not the truncation horizon. Its index is priced explicitly when stored
in the private register. Prefix-coherent comparison has error
$\sum a_t\varepsilon_t$, preserving the same product resource law.

A physical consumer connects these assertions to unbounded microscopic
transport. For a contraction generator $L$ and a finite resolved space
in $D(L)\cap D(L^*)$, we construct the orthogonal generator on its actual
domain and prove the forced-memory and Schur-complement formulas,
including the contribution of the unresolved initial observable. A
collision-compatible hard-sphere graph core gives strong, locally
time-uniform Galerkin approximation of the full Koopman orbit. For
Gaussian noisy physical acquisitions with uniformly $L^2$-bounded
initial densities, Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-physical} converts this analytic
approximation into one marked comparison bound uniform over every finite
register profile and feedback design. The same bound passes to the
infinite-horizon dual values. Crucially, neither the Galerkin coefficients
nor a resampled mark are supplied to the encoder.

Finally Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-certificate} joins the two sides. If an
approximate experiment has resource-preserving risk and benchmark errors
at most $\varepsilon<\alpha\gamma/4$, its minimax value separates exact
causal cover feasibility from infeasibility by the threshold
$\alpha\gamma/2$. The comparison is performed before optimization and
its cost remains visible in the one-sided version. This is a precise
sense in which analytic experiment approximation can certify an exact
common-encoder resource profile.

\subsection{Antecedents and the comparison boundary}
Classical experiment comparison and randomization criteria are due to
Blackwell, Le Cam and their successors
\cite{Blackwell1953,LeCam1964,Torgersen1991}. Filtered statistical
experiment comparison is itself an established subject
\cite{Norberg2002}; the observation timing is not introduced here as a
new concept. The weak-star policy topology and strategic-measure
compactification used in the finite-prefix theory have close antecedents
in stochastic teams \cite{Saldi2017Topology,YukselSaldi2017}.
Common-information dynamic programming and finite-memory causal coding
are also established tools
\cite{NayyarMahajanTeneketzis2013,Witsenhausen1979,YukselCausal2010}.
Our encoder remains coupled across decision rows, and every memory or
seed conversion has an explicit implementation cost.

The continuation-space equivalence engine is classical weighted-automaton
linear algebra \cite{Kiefer2020,Tzeng1992}. Positive reachable state
cardinality, in contrast, need not equal linear rank. The synthesis
results below include polynomial count-vector families, exponential
positive width at rank two, and a finite-task graph-coloring reduction.
The compatible-cover device likewise belongs to partial-machine
minimization. Here its support propagation is proved directly for
randomized common encoders, and the resulting exact profiles are related
to positive risk witnesses and marked comparison. We make no claim that
the cover device or graph coloring by itself is new.

The operator section uses the classical finite-rank projection method,
not a new general resolution of orthogonal dynamics. The semigroup and
domain assumptions in \cite{WidderZimmerSchilling2025,WidderSchilling2026}
are particularly relevant: a finite-rank projection does not make $PL$
bounded unless its range lies in the adjoint domain. We impose that
condition and provide the finite-block proof, the forcing and
reconstruction estimates, and the width-uniform physical decision
consequence. The graph-core resolvent argument, product compactness,
Carath\'eodory's theorem and convex minimax are classical ingredients.
The result proposed for assessment is their resource-sensitive
connection, not a priority claim for any of these ingredients.

\subsection{Scope and organization}
All hypotheses remain attached to their statements. The infinite-horizon
compactness result concerns summable criteria and dominated finite
prefixes, not arbitrary average-cost control or arbitrary filtered
strategic-measure closure. The exact cover theorem concerns supported
restart decision families; an arbitrary task-dependent control law is
not silently replaced by an open-loop test. Public seed information is
part of the compared joint interface whenever a consumer may read it.
A marginal simulation bound which discards that seed is insufficient.
The physical approximation proves a microscopic $L^2$ decision limit,
not a nonlinear kinetic action-sublevel or Boltzmann--Grad limit.

The theory also retains the finite common-membership separation, delayed
Gaussian resource exponents, positive filtering, finite nonlinear
semigroups, hidden-phase decisions, predictive trees and measure-valued
consequences. The historical program is a dependency graph: a proved
model consumer, an acquisition adapter and a primary theorem dependency
are different relationships. In particular the primary algebraic-geometric
A2 line is independent of the present comparison theory. The complete
development preserves the earlier mathematical bodies with their local
hypotheses. Reproducibility and source-identity records are separate from
the mathematical arguments and are not used as proof premises.
