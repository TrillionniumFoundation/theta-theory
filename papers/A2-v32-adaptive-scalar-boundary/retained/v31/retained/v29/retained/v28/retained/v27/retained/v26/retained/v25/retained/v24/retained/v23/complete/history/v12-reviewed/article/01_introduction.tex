\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:geometric-results}
A collision record observed near its first possible occurrence follows
a nearly normal alternating channel between two obstacles. As the
number of flights increases, the probability of the record becomes
exponentially small, but its stationary action retains only two free
endpoints. The resulting relative approximation problem is geometric:
can the nonlinear physical law be controlled on a contact neighborhood
which does not shrink with the record length, and what contact
information survives in that law?

We prove such a relative law for separated, positively curved periodic
dispersing billiards. The theorem is local about an arbitrary smooth
configuration and requires neither symmetry nor finite horizon.
The decisive estimate concerns the mixed endpoint derivative, not
just the action. This derivative supplies the physical flux and is
itself exponentially small. We normalize its cofactor formula before
passing to the long-bridge limit. The nonlinear Hessian perturbation
is localized near the two ends in trace norm; its relative determinant
therefore separates into two half-line factors without a factor
proportional to the number of interior collisions. Residual-time
integration preserves this relative estimate, including every fixed
geometric and offset derivative on a common closed collar.

The two oriented even subsequences determine a pair of nonlinear
boundary energy profiles. Each is the normalized pushforward of a
half-line determinant-weighted endpoint measure by its excess action.
This is a full function-valued invariant, rather than a collection
of leading amplitudes or a formal Taylor series. Its two branches
in the contact coordinate are symmetrized. The odd limiting law is
then fixed by an exact Volterra identity; separately observed odd
records test this prediction rather than supply another identifying
coordinate.

The principal geometric consequence is determination of two
independent even contact graphs. The graphs need not coincide, and
their curvatures may differ or coalesce. At fixed gap and labelled
contact curvatures, the map from the two graph jets of degree $2m$
to the two even-law coefficients of degree $m-1$ has diagonal block
\[
 -\begin{pmatrix}P_m&Q_m\\Q_m&P_m\end{pmatrix}
                  \diag(K_{m,0},K_{m,1}).
\]
The positive constants and their explicit formulas are given in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-two-contact}. The coefficient that separates
the two contacts is
\[
 P_m-Q_m=m\tanh((m-1)\gamma)-(m-1)\tanh(m\gamma)>0.
\]
Strict concavity of the hyperbolic tangent gives its positivity.
Consequently every finite pair of even graph jets is recovered by a
block-triangular inverse, with no curvature-separation denominator.
The two normalized even-law germs determine both analytic contact
graphs, and hence each participating connected analytic boundary in
its contact frame. The evenness hypothesis belongs to this geometric
inverse, not to the general relative law or the energy-profile inverse.
The separate contact curvatures in this theorem are supplied; a single
linear multiplier is not used to infer them separately.

This independent-contact direction occurs in physical tables. For each
fixed $M$, Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-realization} constructs a
$2(M-1)$-parameter analytic support-function family with fixed gap,
free area and both curvatures. The two sets of nonlinear law
coefficients through degree $M-1$ form coordinates on that family,
whereas its entire selected leading hierarchy is constant. This gives
an open finite-jet image and describes the finite-order fibres in
larger such families. A further consequence is an actual finite
observation theorem: one fixed even flight number and finitely many
strictly positive physical windows already give local coordinates.
Independent binary preparations achieve squared risk of order $N^{-1}$
on each fixed family, and physical alternatives with identical leading
data give the matching lower bound in that finite-window experiment.
Thus the contact separation is not confined to access to limiting
coefficients. The constants depend on the fixed jet order; this result
is not an infinite-dimensional minimax assertion.

For general contacts we also recover the full energy invariant from
finitely many selected-event bits, without derivative observations.
The integrated physical flux is
\[
 H_{j,b}(d)=2A\sinh(j\gamma)p_{j,b}(d)=d^2F_{j,b}(d).
\]
A weighted Abel transform of this flux gives a two-sided nonlinear
Lipschitz coordinate for the profile. Its nullspace on general inputs
is exactly the affine functions, and it is a norm on physical flux
differences. The exact convolution identity supplies two additional
derivatives: profiles bounded in $C^{m-1,1}$ produce integrated
fluxes bounded in $C^{m+1,1}$. A positive-node regularizer uses this
gain without asking for extra smoothness of the finite-bridge error.
For $m\ge4$, Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-acquisition} gives the sufficient
preparation bound
\[
 C\varepsilon^{-\left(2+6/(m-1/2)+\gamma/|\log\tau|\right)}
                         \log\frac{C}{\eta\varepsilon}
\]
for uniform error $\varepsilon$ at confidence $1-\eta$.
Every failed preparation is charged. The certificate $\tau$ specifies
relative convergence; the displayed exponent is not claimed to be an
intrinsic or optimal information law. The same regularity yields a
conditional uniform-data modulus of exponent $(m-1/2)/(m+2)$.

The actual values of the gap, area and multiplier need not be supplied
in a specified smooth physical class. A charged one-/two-flight pilot
estimates them while allowing unknown smooth remainder functions.
A gap error translates the integrated flux, rather than introducing
a singular offset normalization. Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-self-calibrated}
therefore retains the same sufficient exponent with the supplied upper
multiplier bound $\gamma_+$. Patch labels, positive compact parameter
boxes, a coarse gap bracket, common collars, regularity bounds and a
relative convergence certificate remain part of the information set.
The target is the pair of symmetrized energy profiles, including their
origin, not arbitrary unsymmetrized graphs or an unlabelled table.

\subsection{Geometric setting and observations}
Fix a full-rank lattice $\Lambda\subset\R^2$ and a positive finite number of bounded
$C^\infty$ strictly convex obstacles $D_1,\ldots,D_s$ with everywhere
positive curvature. All distinct translates $D_a+\lambda$, $\lambda\in
\Lambda$, have disjoint closures. Let $A>0$ be the free area per lattice
cell. The unit-speed billiard is prepared with normalized phase volume
$\dd q\dd\vartheta/(2\pi A)$ on the torus complement. We observe the
collision count $N_t$ and, when specified, the first and last collision
positions. Finite horizon, congruence of the obstacles, and symmetry
are not assumed for the general forward and boundary-law results.

Consider a fixed such configuration and a smooth finite-dimensional
family $D_{a,\xi}$ through it. The parameter $\xi$ ranges over a compact
set $K_0$ in a sufficiently small neighborhood of the reference parameter.
The general geometric conclusions are local about an arbitrary reference configuration,
not only about circles. A compact family is covered by finitely many
such neighborhoods, with common constants after taking finite extrema.
Boundaries may be represented by smooth support functions. At every
fixed derivative order, constants can depend on the corresponding
higher norms of this family, on its positive separation and curvature
bounds, and on the lattice. They do not depend on the collision order.

Let $\mathcal E$ be the finite set of oriented closest pairs attaining
the minimum gap in the reference configuration, modulo common lattice
translation. An index $e=(a,b,\lambda)$ represents a segment from
$D_{a,\xi}$ to $D_{b,\xi}+\lambda$; reversing it gives
$\bar e=(b,a,-\lambda)$. Section~\ref{sec:g-localization} proves that
these pairs continue smoothly, remain unobstructed, and include every
channel contributing to a common ground-onset collar. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-general-data}
 \begin{split}
 g_*&=\min_{e\in\mathcal E}g_e,\qquad
 c_{e,b}=1+g_e\kappa_{e,b}\quad(b=0,1),\\
 c_e&=\sqrt{c_{e,0}c_{e,1}},\qquad \gamma_e=\arcosh c_e,
 \qquad d_{j,e}=t-jg_e.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $\kappa_{e,0}$ and $\kappa_{e,1}$ are the positive curvatures at
its two contact points. They need not be equal. A channel event
$E_{j,e}(d)$ consists of the $j+1$ impacts alternating between the
specified facing patches in the window $t=jg_e+d$. These patches are
fixed around the reference contacts and chosen before observation.
They identify a measurable part of a physical collision record, not an
extra latent observation. At a nonminimal channel's own onset this
selection is required; the unselected count law there is a different
observation.

Parameter derivatives are multi-index derivatives in a fixed Euclidean
parameter chart; offset derivatives hold $d$ fixed as an independent
coordinate. Matrix norms are Euclidean operator norms unless a trace
norm is explicitly indicated. The growing record arrays later use the
maximum norm. These conventions distinguish uniform normalized
amplitudes from physical-time derivatives, which can carry powers of $j$.


\subsection{The nonlinear relative law}
Fix an oriented channel $e$ and abbreviate $g=g_e$, $\gamma=\gamma_e$.
Use transverse endpoint coordinates $u,v$ at its two contacts. Let
$W_j(u,v)$ be the stationary length of its $j$ alternating flights,
$E_j=W_j-jg$, $d_j^0=-W_{j,uv}(0,0)$, and $b_j=-W_{j,uv}/d_j^0$.
For $p=j\bmod2$ put
\[
 a_b=\frac{\sqrt{c_0c_1}\sinh\gamma}{g c_{1-b}},\qquad
 q_p=\sqrt{a_0a_p},\qquad d_j^0=\frac{q_p}{\sinh(j\gamma)}.
\]
The stationary bridge and these formulas are proved in
Section~\ref{sec:g-action}. All norms in the next theorem are on fixed
common neighborhoods and include the indicated geometric derivatives.

\begin{theorem}[Relative boundary law]\label{thm:v8-main-relative}
There are positive smooth functions $B_b$ and strictly convex smooth
functions $S_b$, $b=0,1$, with
\[
 S_b(0)=S_b'(0)=0,\qquad S_b''(0)=a_b,\qquad B_b(0)=1,
\]
a common endpoint box, a number $d_*>0$, and $0<\tau<1$ such that,
for every fixed derivative order $k$ and every $j\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v8-relative}
 \|E_j-S_0\oplus S_p\|_{C^k}
 +\|b_j-B_0\otimes B_p\|_{C^k}\le C_k\tau^j,
                    \qquad p=j\bmod2.
\end{equation}
The action difference vanishes to second order at the origin. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v8-boundary-integral}
 \mathcal F_p(d)=\frac{q_p}{\pi d^2}
  \int(d-S_0(u)-S_p(v))_+B_0(u)B_p(v)\,\dd u\dd v.
\end{equation}
Then $\mathcal F_p$ extends smoothly to $[0,d_*]$, is positive there,
and has value one at zero. The exact selected-channel probabilities obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v8-relative-integral}
 \left\|\frac{2A\sinh(j\gamma)}{d^2}
           \Pr(E_{j,e}(d))-\mathcal F_p(d)\right\|_{C^k([0,d_*])}
                                  \le C_k\tau^j.
\end{equation}
The norm includes every fixed mixed geometric and offset derivative.
The limiting conditional endpoint--residual law at $d>0$ has density
proportional to
\[
 B_0(u)B_p(v)\one_{\{r>0,\ S_0(u)+S_p(v)+r<d\}}.
\]
All assertions hold uniformly on the compact local families specified
above. For analytic families the endpoint constructions are analytic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof and dependencies]
Lemma~\ref{lem:g-relative} constructs the finite bridge and its relative
determinant. Lemma~\ref{lem:v4-halfline} constructs $S_b,B_b$ with their
normalizations. Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-factorization} proves
\eqref{eq:v8-relative} by comparing two separated perturbation blocks in
trace norm, including their mixed derivatives. Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-law}
proves \eqref{eq:v8-relative-integral} using the common Morse domain of
Lemma~\ref{lem:g-radial}, and identifies the conditional density.
These proofs are supplied in full in the following sections.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Relation to other inverse data}
The estimates use classical determinant and convolution tools for a
specific physical target. Hill identities relate action Hessians and
monodromy~\cite{Hill}; our relative estimate concerns nonlinear
boundary perturbations followed by physical residual-time integration.
Section~\ref{sec:v9-operators} separates these roles. Spectral and
marked-length inverse problems~\cite{Zelditch,DSKL,FinamoreLeguil}
use different observations. The present geometric data are two
oriented near-onset probability laws, with a specified contact class
and labelled leading geometry. Neither those problems nor arbitrary
asymmetric contact determination are identified with this one.

Causal regularization of autoconvolution is classical
\cite{DaiLamm,HofmannWernerDeng}. The present kernel
$x^{-1/2}V(x)$ is locally integrable but not in ordinary $L^2$ at
zero. Section~\ref{sec:v10-deautoconvolution} specifies the weighted
functional setting and the difference between regularization and
physical data acquisition. This distinction is not, by itself, a
priority claim. The geometric block calculation, relative physical
law and their stated observation consequences supply the mathematical
content of the present results.

\subsection{The proof chain and the auxiliary results}
The main argument has two parts. The first proves the relative physical
law, identifies its energy invariant, and proves the independent-contact
inverse and its physical realization. The second gives the Abel
coordinate and the strongest complete-profile observation theorem.
The geometric inverse uses even contact graphs; the observation of
energy profiles does not. Analytic continuation of a contact germ is
an exact determination statement, not a stability estimate for distant
boundary points from noisy local observations.

The appendices give complete auxiliary results and earlier acquisition
constructions. They include the original identical-contact triangular
inverse and its one-flight comparison, all finite-jet realizations,
integer-norm and lower-order Abel acquisitions, and the smooth-class
pilot used in the main theorem. Endpoint and residual-time experiments
are kept separate from binary records. Exact-family fixed-window
curvature recovery, smooth-nuisance minimax theory, and count-only
calibration each retain their own assumptions, losses and allowed
designs. Their lower bounds are not imported into the full-profile
experiment. Collision-record response formulas and circular benchmark
proofs are also included in full, with the two-collision companion.

The finite-jet theorem does not assert that long records are always
necessary for geometric identification. The retained one-flight
calculation already determines an identical even analytic contact.
Here the independent two-contact block identifies the additional
antisymmetric direction, while the relative law provides its uniform
long-bridge origin and its general function-valued extension.
The main geometric conclusions are
Theorems~\ref{thm:v12-two-contact}, \ref{thm:v12-realization}
and~\ref{thm:v12-finite-observation}; the general physical input is
Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-main-relative}. The preparation exponent is an
observation consequence, not the principal geometric significance
claim.
