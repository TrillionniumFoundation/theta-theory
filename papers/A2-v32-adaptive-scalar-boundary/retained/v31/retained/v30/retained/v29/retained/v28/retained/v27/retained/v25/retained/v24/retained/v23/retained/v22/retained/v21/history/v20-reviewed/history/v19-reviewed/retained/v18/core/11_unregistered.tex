\section{Synchronization without contact registration}
\label{sec:unregistered}
The signed registration in Definition~\ref{def:network} can be removed
when a reconstructed obstacle has no Euclidean symmetry. More generally,
noncircular obstacles leave only finitely many assembly choices. The
mechanism is equality of entire analytic images, not equality of an
unlabelled curvature value at a contact.

For a compact convex body $C$ write $\mathcal G(C)$ for its Euclidean
isometry group. We call $C$ \emph{asymmetric} if $\mathcal G(C)=\{1\}$.
This is an assumption on the unknown table, not supplied knowledge of
its boundary. It can be checked on the images recovered from the laws.

\begin{definition}[Unregistered channel data]\label{def:unregistered}
An unregistered network has the graph, obstacle labels, contact-occurrence
labels, and integer edge labels of Definition~\ref{def:network}, but no
signed distances between occurrences and no relative orientation of
different experiments. On each edge the entire local law is taken modulo
its own simultaneous endpoint reversal. Thus the data are the product
of the edge orbits, not the orbit of their product under one reversal.
Equality means equality of the labelled graph and integer labels, and
separate equality of these edge orbits. Contact origins remain local
marks. Lattice copy labels remain observed discrete data.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Unregistered periodic rigidity and finite ambiguity]
\label{thm:unregistered}
Consider a connected full-lattice-rank network visiting every obstacle of
an analytic periodic dispersing table.
\textup{(i)} If every obstacle is asymmetric, its unregistered channel
data determine the whole labelled table and marked lattice up to one
Euclidean isometry. In particular, all relative edge signs and all
contact registrations are recovered, rather than supplied.
\textup{(ii)} If every obstacle is noncircular, the same data admit only
finitely many such tables up to isometry. Choose a rooted spanning tree,
orient its edges away from the root, and orient the other edges arbitrarily.
An upper bound for the number of candidates is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finiteassembly}
 \prod_{e\in E}|\mathcal G(C_{\operatorname{tail}(e)})|.
\end{equation}
Each candidate is obtained by matching recovered boundary images and
solving the two independent lattice-cycle equations. Remaining shape,
cycle, area, and clearance conditions can only remove candidates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Unique matching of an asymmetric image]\label{lem:match}
If two congruent compact bodies $C,D$ have trivial isometry group, exactly
one Euclidean isometry maps $C$ to $D$. If $\mathcal G(C)$ is finite,
the set of such maps has cardinality $|\mathcal G(C)|$. A strictly convex
planar body has an infinite isometry group only if it is a disk.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Given one matching map $U$, all the others are $Ug$, $g\in\mathcal G(C)$.
Every symmetry fixes the Steiner point, so after centering the group is
a closed subgroup of $O(2)$. If this group is infinite, its rotation
subgroup is infinite: the product of two reflections is a rotation.
An infinite closed subgroup of the circle contains rotations arbitrarily
close to the identity and hence all rotations. Its invariant support
function is constant. The body is a disk.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:unregistered}]
For each edge independently choose a representative of its reversal
orbit. Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic}(i)--(ii) reconstructs the two entire
obstacle images, their relative placement, and the two marked contact
points in a local channel frame. A different representative reflects
this entire pair. Call this a \emph{pair realization}; its congruence
class is independent of the choice. We never identify the independent
edge signs before this reconstruction.

Use integer representative changes to make the spanning-tree labels zero,
as in \eqref{eq:cyclelinear}. Place a reconstructed root body arbitrarily.
For an outward tree edge, match its source body in the pair realization
to the already placed source body. Lemma~\ref{lem:match} gives a unique
map in (i), and at most the corresponding group order in (ii). Apply
that same map to the partner body and both contact points. This places
the child representative. Iteration places every vertex. Backwards
traversal merely interchanges the two bodies of the pair realization.
A common choice of the initial root placement is an ambient isometry;
in the finite case keeping all root-symmetry choices only overcounts.

For a non-tree edge, match its source body to its placed representative.
Let $\widehat C_{w,e}$ be the resulting target image and $C_w$ the
already placed target representative. In a physical assembly these
images must differ by a translation. This translation is unique:
a nonempty compact set cannot be invariant under a nonzero translation.
Writing $s(C)$ for the Steiner point, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unregisteredcycle}
 d_e=s(\widehat C_{w,e})-s(C_w),\qquad
 \widehat C_{w,e}=C_w+d_e.
\end{equation}
The second equality is a consistency test, not an extra orientation
choice. It includes loops, whose source and target vertex coincide.
The transformed integer labels satisfy $d_e=Lk'_e$. Two independent
labels give \eqref{eq:latticerecover}. All other edges, the free-area
normalization, and the physical channel conditions are checked against
this same matrix and these same placed bodies. A physical table producing
the data occurs in this construction, so it is not discarded.

In (i), every matching step is unique. Consequently the construction is
independent of every initial edge-reflection choice: replacing a pair
realization by its reflection simply composes its unique matching map
with that reflection. All contact points and orientations now live in one
plane. Their intrinsic registrations follow by integration along the
reconstructed boundaries. The original independent edge signs are thus
synchronized up to the one ambient reflection. In (ii) every edge offers
at most its source group order, giving \eqref{eq:finiteassembly}; the
cycle solution has no further freedom. This proves both assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[An open physical class without registration]
\label{prop:asymmetricphysical}
There is a nonempty open set of analytic periodic tables satisfying
Theorem~\ref{thm:unregistered}(i), with independent edge reversal orbits
and without any supplied contact registration.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Consider a body with support function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:asymmetricsupport}
 p(\theta)=1+\varepsilon\cos(2\theta)
              +\varepsilon\cos(3\theta+\pi/4),\qquad
                 0<|\varepsilon|<1/11.
\end{equation}
Since $p+p''>0$, it is strictly convex and analytic. Its first Fourier
coefficients vanish, so its Steiner point is the origin. A rotational
symmetry must preserve the nonzero second and third Fourier coefficients;
it therefore has angle zero modulo $2\pi$. A reflection about angle $a$
would require simultaneously $2a\in\pi\mathbb Z$ and
$3a+\pi/4\in\pi\mathbb Z$, which is impossible. Thus the body is asymmetric.
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, place its translates on
$3\mathbb Z\times4\mathbb Z$. The two neighboring channels in
Proposition~\ref{prop:networkexamples} persist, and their labels have
rank two. They now need no registration.

For completeness asymmetry is open near any asymmetric $C^2$ support
function. Reflection axes are compact, so approximate reflection
symmetries would limit to an actual one. For rotations with angles
bounded away from zero the same compactness argument applies. If
nontrivial symmetry angles tend to zero for a sequence converging in
$C^1$, division of the equality of the translated support functions
by the angle implies $p'=0$ at the limit. That limit would be a disk,
not the given asymmetric body. Convexity, isolated normal contacts and
clearance are also open. This proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

The theorem does not equate channel laws with a marked length spectrum.
Its additional conclusion is instead an information reduction within
the same local scattering observation: continuous cross-channel
registration and the relative signs have ceased to be inputs. Symmetric
tables retain the fully registered theorem without any restriction.
