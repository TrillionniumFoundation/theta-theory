\title[Robust compatible realization]{General Theta Foundations I: Robust Compatibility of Numerical Word Realizations}
\author{Qian Qi}
\date{27 September 2026; revision 47}

\begin{abstract}
We study finite-horizon numerical experiments realized by stochastic
registers with prescribed layer widths. For antisymmetric targets,
two labels at every command cut admit an exact scalar tensor normal
form: deleting affine offsets does not increase the optimal uniform
error. A finite parity certificate detects failure of sign compatibility
at arbitrary horizon. For constant-magnitude target entries it decides
whether two labels can improve on the fair-coin error, and provides a
matching constructive alternative. A planar one-command experiment has
a complete two-cut error frontier: either cut alone can use two labels
at error $\rho/4$, whereas using two labels at both forces error
$\rho/2$. The obstruction survives arbitrary intervening hidden states
and fixed perturbations. Compatible positive Hankel factors, finite
all-word Gram certificates, convergent error bounds, arithmetic width
laws and a separately priced finite-bit compiler are retained.
The primary resource is nonuniform clocked atomic-row label width;
we distinguish actual numerical error from average error and local
compatibility certificates. Classical realization and tensor mechanisms
are explicitly separated from the finite compatibility conclusions.
\end{abstract}
