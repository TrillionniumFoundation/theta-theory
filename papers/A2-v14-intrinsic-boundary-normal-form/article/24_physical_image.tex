\section{Physical coordinates and finite-window observation}
\label{sec:v12-physical-image}
The block inverse is a statement about actual boundary laws. We now
show that its two sets of coordinates vary independently in physical
tables while the complete selected leading hierarchy remains fixed.
This also gives physical alternatives for a finite-dimensional
observation theorem. The dimension is fixed throughout this section;
none of its statistical bounds is a lower bound for the full
infinite-dimensional profile experiment.

\subsection{Independent realization at fixed leading data}
\begin{theorem}[A physical open image of two-contact jets]
\label{thm:v12-realization}
Fix $M\ge2$. For any two curvature radii $R_0,R_1$ sufficiently
close to one, there is a real-analytic family of positively curved
periodic billiards on $3\Z\times4\Z$, with $2(M-1)$ parameters,
for which the selected horizontal channel has
\[
             g=1,\qquad A=12-\pi,\qquad \kappa_b=R_b^{-1}.
\]
Both contact graphs are even, but their higher jets are independent.
The parameters can be taken to be either
\[
       (q_{b,2m})_{b=0,1;\,2\le m\le M}
       \quad\hbox{or}\quad
       (f_{b,k})_{b=0,1;\,1\le k\le M-1}.
\]
Each of these maps is a local analytic diffeomorphism, with inverse
derivative bounded on a sufficiently small compact neighborhood.
Every member has the same leading selected count and endpoint-metric
coefficients at every collision order, in both orientations. The
statement includes $R_0=R_1$ and requires no equality of higher jets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use one obstacle per cell and its support function. Put
\[
 w_0(\theta)=\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2},\qquad
 w_1(\theta)=\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2},
\]
and consider the finite-dimensional analytic family
\begin{align}
 h(\theta)={}&1+\alpha\sin^2\theta+
                 \beta\cos\theta\sin^2\theta\notag\\
 &+\sum_{m=2}^M
       \{s_{0m}w_0(\theta)+s_{1m}w_1(\theta)\}\sin^{2m}\theta
           +z\sin^{2M+2}\theta.\label{eq:v12-support}
\end{align}
At the two horizontal normals the support values equal one, the
support derivatives vanish, and the curvature radii are
\[
 h(0)+h''(0)=1+2\alpha+2\beta,
 \qquad h(\pi)+h''(\pi)=1+2\alpha-2\beta.
\]
Thus set $\alpha=(R_0+R_1-2)/4$ and
$\beta=(R_0-R_1)/4$. No $s$ or $z$ changes these data.
The obstacle area is
\[
                 \mathcal S(h)=\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}
                                      (h^2-(h')^2)\,\dd\theta.
\]
At the unit disk,
$\partial_z\mathcal S=\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^{2M+2}\theta\,\dd\theta>0$.
The analytic implicit-function theorem therefore gives a unique small
$z=z(\alpha,\beta,s)$ for which $\mathcal S=\pi$. This function is
analytic jointly in all the displayed parameters.

For small parameters, $h+h''$ is positive, so the support function
defines a strictly convex analytic closed curve. Its right and left
horizontal support points remain $(1,0)$ and $(-1,0)$.
The selected chord from this obstacle to its translate by $(3,0)$
has length one and normal incidence. The unit-disk configuration has
positive clearance and a strict gap between its horizontal nearest
neighbors and all other translates; these properties persist on a
small neighborhood. For the infinitely many distant translates this
follows from a uniform bound on the obstacle diameter, leaving only
finitely many translates to check. Thus the constructed chords are
physical first hits and form the selected ground channel with its
reverse. The free cell area is $12-\pi$.
The function $h$ is even in $\theta$, so the curve is invariant under
reflection across the horizontal axis. Each horizontal contact graph
is consequently even in its transverse coordinate. Reflection across
the vertical axis is not imposed.

At normal zero the $s_{0m}$ perturbation starts in order $2m$,
whereas its $s_{1m}$ counterpart starts in order $2m+2$.
At normal $\pi$ these roles are interchanged. The area compensator
starts in order $2M+2$ at both normals. For completeness, the
support-envelope calculation in a contact frame gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-support-jacobian}
 \frac{\partial q_{b,2m}}{\partial s_{bm}}
                  =-(2m)!\kappa_b^{2m},\qquad
 \frac{\partial q_{b,2m}}{\partial s_{1-b,m}}=0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, at the right contact write the boundary as $x=x(y)$.
Stationarity of $(h(\theta)-y\sin\theta)/\cos\theta$ at its
supporting normal gives, at fixed $y$,
$\partial_s x=\partial_s h(\theta(y))/\cos\theta(y)$.
Since the radius is $R_b$, $\theta(y)=\kappa_b y+O(y^3)$.
The contact graph is $\psi_b=1-x$, giving the negative coefficient
in \eqref{eq:v12-support-jacobian}. At the left contact use the
outward horizontal normal and the reflected longitudinal coordinate;
the same calculation applies. Even powers make the transverse
orientation immaterial.

A perturbation of order $2m$ cannot change graph jets of smaller
order. Derivatives of the compensator also have order $2M+2$.
Hence the map from the $2(M-1)$ variables $s$ to the displayed graph
jets has a block lower triangular Jacobian, with the invertible
blocks \eqref{eq:v12-support-jacobian}. It is an analytic local
diffeomorphism. Compose it with Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-two-contact}
to obtain the asserted statistical coordinates. Compactly restricting
the coordinate neighborhood bounds both inverse derivatives.
Finally, the quadratic endpoint Hessian and its normalized mixed
derivative depend only on $g,\kappa_0,\kappa_1$.
The full-phase leading count coefficient also uses only $A$.
These four data are fixed. The endpoint-metric leading coefficients
and the selected count leading coefficients are therefore fixed at
every order, by \eqref{eq:g-effective}--\eqref{eq:g-ratio} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:v4-saturation}. This assertion concerns this
channel and its reverse, not the leading data of every other channel.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-order physical fibres]
\label{cor:v12-fibres}
For $N>M\ge2$, use the family of Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-realization}
with $N$ in place of $M$. In a sufficiently small coordinate
neighborhood, each nonempty fibre of the two-law jet map through
order $M-1$ is an analytic submanifold of dimension $2(N-M)$.
The full two-law jet map through order $N-1$ is locally injective.
All these statements hold with the selected leading hierarchy fixed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In the statistical coordinates furnished by the theorem the first
map is the coordinate projection onto its first $2(M-1)$
coordinates. Its fibres have the stated dimension; the full map is
the coordinate chart itself. These are fibres of specified finite
physical families, not a classification of the fibres of arbitrary
smooth asymmetric contact geometry.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A fixed physical experiment}
Fix $M$ and one compact coordinate neighborhood from the preceding
theorem. Write $n=M-1$ and
$\vartheta=(f_{b,k})_{b=0,1;\,1\le k\le n}\in\R^{2n}$.
The exact family, its labels, and $g,A,\kappa_0,\kappa_1$ are supplied
in the following experiment. A preparation returns the indicator of
one selected alternating event, at a specified orientation and time.
It does not return an endpoint, a derivative, or a limiting law.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-window observation and physical separation]
\label{thm:v12-finite-observation}
After shrinking the parameter neighborhood, there are $h_0>0$ and
one even integer $j_0\ge2$ such that the $2n$ physical means
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-window-map}
 \mathcal P(\vartheta)=
 \left(\Pr_{\vartheta}
       E_{j_0,b}(\ell h_0)\right)_{b=0,1;\,1\le\ell\le n}
\end{equation}
form a bi-Lipschitz coordinate on a compact parameter ball $\Theta$
with nonempty interior. Here the physical observation time is
$j_0g+\ell h_0$. The constants and the windows depend on the fixed
family and $M$, but not on the requested accuracy.

For $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$ and $0<\eta<1/4$, a Borel
estimator based on independent binary preparations in these windows
satisfies
\[
 \sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}
 \Pr_{\vartheta}\{\|\widehat\vartheta-\vartheta\|>
                 \varepsilon\}\le\eta,
 \qquad N_{\rm tot}\le C\varepsilon^{-2}\log(C/\eta).
\]
The count is deterministic and includes all failures. The same order
holds for the corresponding finite graph-jet vector.

Moreover, for all sufficiently large $N$, the minimax squared risk
in this fixed finite-window experiment satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-physical-risk}
 \frac{c}{N}\le
 \inf_{\widehat\vartheta,\,\mathcal D_N}
       \sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta}
          \mathbb E_{\vartheta}
                    \|\widehat\vartheta-\vartheta\|^2
       \le\frac{C}{N}.
\end{equation}
The infimum allows adaptive and randomized choices of these windows
and at most $N$ fresh preparations. Its lower bound uses pairs of
physical tables in the stated family, with exactly the same selected
leading hierarchy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Analyticity of the boundary law in the physical parameters and offset,
on a common neighborhood, gives
\[
 \mathcal F_{bb}(d;\vartheta)
     =1+\sum_{k=1}^n\vartheta_{b,k}d^k
                            +d^{n+1}R_b(d;\vartheta),
\]
where $R_b$ and its first parameter derivatives are uniformly bounded.
At $d=\ell h$ the Jacobian with respect to $\vartheta$ is the block
diagonal matrix with blocks
$V_h=((\ell h)^k)_{1\le\ell,k\le n}$, plus a matrix of norm
$O(h^{n+1})$. The matrix $V_h=V_1\diag(h,\ldots,h^n)$ is
invertible: its determinant is a nonzero Vandermonde determinant
multiplied by $\prod_{\ell=1}^n\ell$. Also
$\|V_h^{-1}\|\le C h^{-n}$ for $h\le1$.
Choose a fixed $h=h_0$ so small that $nh_0$ lies in the common
collar and the transformed remainder norm is less than $1/4$.

The differentiated relative theorem bounds the difference between
$F_{j,b}$ and $\mathcal F_{bb}$, including its first parameter
derivatives, by $C\tau^j$. Choose a fixed even $j=j_0$ so large
that $\|V_{h_0}^{-1}\|C\tau^{j_0}<1/4$ (with the fixed block
norms included in $C$). The Jacobian of the actual normalized finite
law is then invertible. Passing to the physical means multiplies
its rows by the positive constants
$(\ell h_0)^2/(2A\sinh(j_0\gamma))$. These constants do not depend
on $\vartheta$, since all leading geometric data in this family are
fixed. Thus the physical Jacobian is invertible as well.

Choose a small convex ball about an interior reference parameter.
The Jacobian, after multiplication by its inverse at the center, is
uniformly within $1/2$ of the identity on the ball. Integrating along
a line segment shows that the physical mean map is bi-Lipschitz
there. Take its compact closure as $\Theta$. By further restriction
of the offset collar if needed, each of its finitely many probabilities
is strictly between zero and one; compactness gives common positive
margins. These margins may be very small, but are fixed constants of
this experiment.

Take the same number $L$ of fresh preparations at each of the $2n$
windows, and let $Y$ be the vector of sample means. For any $t>0$,
Hoeffding's elementary bounded-summand bound and a union bound give
\[
 \Pr\{\|Y-\mathcal P(\vartheta)\|_\infty>t\}
                         \le4n\exp(-2Lt^2).
\]
Choose a finite ordered net of $\Theta$ fine enough that its image
approximates $\mathcal P(\Theta)$ within $t$ in Euclidean norm.
Minimize the Euclidean discrepancy from $Y$, resolving ties by the
first index. This is a Borel estimator. On the displayed event its
image error is at most $C_n t$; the inverse Lipschitz bound gives
parameter error at most $Ct$. Set $t=c\varepsilon$ and
$L=\lceil Ct^{-2}\log(4n/\eta)\rceil$. This proves the deterministic
charge and confidence statement. The graph-jet inverse is Lipschitz
on this neighborhood, so the assertion transfers to that vector.
For squared risk, use $L=\lfloor N/(2n)\rfloor$ and a net of mesh
$N^{-1/2}$. The same deterministic discrepancy inequality and
$\mathbb E\|Y-\mathcal P(\vartheta)\|^2\le C/L$ give the upper
bound in \eqref{eq:v12-physical-risk}.

For the lower bound choose an interior point $\vartheta_*$, a unit
vector $v$, and the two parameters
$\vartheta_\pm=\vartheta_*\pm aN^{-1/2}v$.
They represent actual analytic obstacles from
Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-realization}. Every allowed window has a
probability in a fixed interval $[p_*,1-p_*]$. The Bernoulli
relative entropy satisfies
\[
 D(\operatorname{Ber}(p)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(q))
       \le\frac{(p-q)^2}{q(1-q)}.
\]
The smooth mean map therefore bounds this entropy by $C a^2/N$
for each window. In an adaptive experiment, condition on the complete
past and any auxiliary random seed. The rule for choosing the next
window is the same under the two parameters; the conditional entropy
is bounded by the same uniform bound. The chain rule, with padding
by parameter-independent observations after an early stop, gives
entropy at most $Ca^2$ for the entire record. Choose $a>0$ fixed
and small so that the total variation is at most $1/2$, using
$\operatorname{TV}^2\le D/2$.

Any estimator induces the test selecting the nearer of the two
parameters. On a test error its squared parameter loss is at least
$a^2/N$. The sum of the two testing error probabilities is at least
$1-\operatorname{TV}\ge1/2$. The maximum risk is therefore at
least $a^2/(4N)$. This proves the lower bound, including adaptive
window choices. All the alternatives keep $g,A,\kappa_0,\kappa_1$
and the selected leading hierarchy exactly fixed.
\end{proof}

The fixed-window theorem resolves a physical finite-jet inverse
problem, not just a formal coefficient problem: a fixed finite record
length and finitely many strictly positive offsets already give local
coordinates on independently varying contact jets. The windows and
conditioning constants can deteriorate with $M$. The theorem neither
passes this bound to an increasing-dimensional model nor substitutes
its supplied exact family for the unknown smooth remainder experiment
of Section~\ref{sec:v12-regularized-observation}.
