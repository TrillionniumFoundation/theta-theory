\section{Regularized observation of the complete invariant}
\label{sec:v12-regularized-observation}
We return to general contacts, without evenness, congruence or equal
curvature assumptions. The observations in this section recover the
full symmetrized energy profiles. The physical class is not replaced
by the finite-dimensional family of the preceding section.
The integrated-flux identity supplies two additional derivatives on
exactly the profile class used below. This gain and its quantitative
consequences were pointed out in the author-requested independent
memorandum~\cite{A2ReviewV11}. We include the proof and the complete
modified observation argument; the earlier, weaker constructions
remain in Sections~\ref{sec:v10-acquisition}--\ref{sec:v11-calibration}.

Fix $m\ge4$, $D>0$, $B>0$ and $\beta>0$. Write
\[
 \|V\|_{r,1}=\sum_{a=0}^r\|V^{(a)}\|_\infty+
                         \operatorname{Lip}(V^{(r)}),
\]
\[
 \mathcal K_m=\{V\in C^{m-1,1}([0,D]):
      V(0)=1,\ V\ge\beta,\ \|V\|_{m-1,1}\le B\}.
\]
The sum convention is part of this definition. The two actual profiles
belong to this class. For two such functions define
\[
 H(V,W)(d)=d^2T(V,W)(d)
   =\frac2\pi\int_{x+y<d}(d-x-y)
                  \frac{V(x)W(y)}{\sqrt{xy}}\,\dd x\dd y,
 \qquad H_V=H(V,V),
\]
where the integral is over positive $x,y$. The target laws are
$T(\mathcal V_b,\mathcal V_c)$. Constants below may depend on
$m,D,B,\beta$ and the specified physical bounds, but not on the
accuracy, mesh or flight number.

\subsection{The regularity of integrated flux}
\begin{lemma}[Two-derivative gain]
\label{lem:v12-gain}
For $V,W\in\mathcal K_m$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-gain}
 \|H(V,W)\|_{m+1,1}\le C B^2,
 \qquad H(V,W)(0)=H(V,W)'(0)=0.
\end{equation}
In particular, the candidate fluxes belong to a bounded
$C^{m+1,1}$ class even though the profiles are only bounded in
$C^{m-1,1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fubini's theorem and two integrations of a continuous convolution give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-second-derivative}
 Q(d):=H(V,W)''(d)=\frac2\pi\int_0^1
             \frac{V(ds)W(d(1-s))}{\sqrt{s(1-s)}}\,\dd s.
\end{equation}
For $0\le r\le m-1$, dominated differentiation gives
\[
 Q^{(r)}(d)=\frac2\pi\sum_{a=0}^r\binom ra
  \int_0^1\frac{s^a(1-s)^{r-a}
       V^{(a)}(ds)W^{(r-a)}(d(1-s))}{\sqrt{s(1-s)}}\,\dd s.
\]
The weight is integrable, including at both endpoints, and every
profile derivative displayed is bounded by $B$. Thus
$\|Q^{(r)}\|_\infty\le2^{r+1}B^2$. Every derivative of either
profile through order $m-1$ is Lipschitz with constant at most $B$:
for a lower derivative use its next derivative, and for the last use
the norm assumption. The difference of each product in the formula
at $d,e$ is consequently bounded by $C B^2|d-e|$.
It follows that $Q^{(m-1)}$ is uniformly Lipschitz.
Finally
\[
 H'(d)=\int_0^d Q(s)\,\dd s,\qquad
 H(d)=\int_0^d(d-s)Q(s)\,\dd s.
\]
These identities control the two lower derivatives and prove
\eqref{eq:v12-gain}. No extension of the profiles across zero,
additional derivative observation, or higher profile norm is used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Positive-node reconstruction}
Put $q=m+2$, let $L\ge q$, and write $h=D/L\le1$.
The reconstruction $J=J_h^{[q]}$ uses the positive nodes
$h,2h,\ldots,Lh$ only. To specify it, set
\[
 \omega(u)=\begin{cases}e^{-1/(1-u^2)},&|u|<1,\\0,&|u|\ge1,
              \end{cases}\qquad
 \theta_\ell(x)=\frac{\omega(x/h-\ell)}
                         {\sum_{r=0}^L\omega(x/h-r)}.
\]
For $0\le\ell\le L$, interpolate the $q$ nodal values starting at
$s_\ell=\max\{1,\min\{\ell-\lfloor(q-1)/2\rfloor,L-q+1\}\}$
by a polynomial $P_\ell z$ of degree at most $q-1$, and define
$Jz=\sum_{\ell=0}^L\theta_\ell P_\ell z$.
This is a globally smooth function on the closed interval. Its first
stencil is $h,\ldots,qh$; the value at zero is extrapolated and is
not inserted as an additional observation.

\begin{lemma}[Three reconstruction estimates]
\label{lem:v12-reconstruction}
Uniformly for the indicated meshes,
\begin{align}
 |JH_V-H_V|_{\mathscr A}&\le C h^{m-1/2},
                                      &&V\in\mathcal K_m,
                                     \label{eq:v12-approx}\\
 |Jf|_{\mathscr A}&\le C\|f\|_{C^3},
                                      &&f\in C^3([0,D]),
                                     \label{eq:v12-bias}\\
 |Jz|_{\mathscr A}&\le Ch^{-5/2}\max_k|z_k|.
                                     \label{eq:v12-noise}
\end{align}
The seminorm is the linear Abel seminorm of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-abel-inverse}, including its endpoint term.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At each point at most two weights are positive. A nearest grid index
lies within rescaled distance $1/2$, so the denominator is bounded
below uniformly. Derivatives of the weights of order $r$ are bounded
by $C_rh^{-r}$. Every active stencil is within distance $(q+1)h$.
The rescaled Lagrange polynomials and their fixed-order derivatives
are bounded on these compact intervals. Since $q$ is fixed, the
product rule gives
$\|Jz\|_{C^r}\le Ch^{-r}\max|z_k|$ for $r\le3$.
The reconstruction reproduces every polynomial of degree at most
$q-1=m+1$. Subtracting the Taylor polynomial of that degree at
the evaluation point, using a Lipschitz last derivative, gives
\[
 \|(JH_V-H_V)''\|_\infty\le Ch^m,
 \qquad
 \|(JH_V-H_V)'''\|_\infty\le Ch^{m-1}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:v12-gain} bounds the remainders uniformly. These
estimates are valid at zero and $D$, since the rescaled stencils
remain bounded there. For $f\in C^3$, subtract instead its quadratic
Taylor polynomial. Its nodal remainder is $O(h^3)\|f\|_{C^3}$,
so $\|Jf\|_{C^3}\le C\|f\|_{C^3}$.

Apply the mixed Abel estimate
\[
 |G|_{\mathscr A}\le\frac\pi2|G''(0)|+
  2\pi\sqrt D\sqrt{\|G''-G''(0)\|_\infty\|G'''\|_\infty}.
\]
The approximation product has square root $h^{m-1/2}$, and its
endpoint term is $O(h^m)$. The nodal-error product has square root
$h^{-5/2}$, dominating its $h^{-2}$ endpoint term. The $C^3$
bound treats the smooth input with no mesh amplification. This proves
all three claims. In particular, the gain is used only for limiting
candidate fluxes, not to assume two extra derivatives of the physical
finite-bridge error.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Calibrated binary observations}
For the moment suppose $g,A,\gamma$ are supplied, as well as the
selected patch labels, a common collar, and a relative convergence
certificate. For even $j\ge2$ assume the physical selected means have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-physical-assumptions}
 c_j=2A\sinh(j\gamma),\quad H_{j,b}=c_jp_{j,b},\quad
 0\le H_{j,b}(d)\le M_0d^2,\quad
 \|H_{j,b}-H_{\mathcal V_b}\|_{C^3([0,D])}\le M_1\tau^j.
\end{equation}
These are consequences of the relative physical law on the supplied
compact class. The values $M_0,M_1$ and $\tau\in(0,1)$, rather
than the unknown profiles or finite-flight remainders, are supplied.

\begin{theorem}[Complete-profile preparation bound]
\label{thm:v12-acquisition}
For $L\ge m+2$, $h=D/L\le1$, even $j\ge2$ and
$0<t,\delta\le1$, use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-allocation}
 N_{b,k}=\left\lceil
   2c_j(M_0d_k^2+t/3)t^{-2}\log\frac{4L}{\eta}\right\rceil,
 \qquad d_k=kh,
\end{equation}
fresh binary preparations in orientation $b$ at time $jg+d_k$.
There is a Borel estimator with values in $\mathcal K_m^2$ such
that, with probability at least $1-\eta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-error}
 \max_b\|\widehat V_b-\mathcal V_b\|_\infty
     \le C\{h^{m-1/2}+\tau^j+t h^{-5/2}+\delta\}.
\end{equation}
The predicted odd law has at most $2B$ times this error. The total
number of preparations is deterministically bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-total}
 N_{\rm tot}\le2L+C A\sinh(j\gamma)h^{-1}t^{-2}
                                      \log\frac{4L}{\eta}.
\end{equation}
In particular, uniform accuracy $\varepsilon$ for both profiles and
the odd-law prediction, at confidence $1-\eta$, is attained using
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-rate}
 N_{\rm tot}\le C\varepsilon^{-\left(2+\frac6{m-1/2}
                                +\frac\gamma{|\log\tau|}\right)}
                                \log\frac{C}{\eta\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
Every failure is charged. No value at zero or derivative is observed.
This is a sufficient preparation bound, not a minimax or
computation-time bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The class $\mathcal K_m$ is compact in $C^{m-1}$ by
Arzel\`a--Ascoli and the closed sum-norm bound. For fixed $h$, the
map $V\mapsto\mathscr A JH_V$ is continuous through its finite
vector of nodal values, so its image in $C^0$ is compact. Choose
a finite ordered dictionary $\mathcal D_{h,\delta}\subset\mathcal K_m$
whose transformed nodal images form a $\delta$-net. For a data
vector $z$, take the first minimizer over the dictionary of
$|JH_W-Jz|_{\mathscr A}$. Each discrepancy is continuous in the
finite data vector. The first-index rule therefore defines a Borel
map, including ties. No physical realization of dictionary elements
is required for this upper bound.

Let $Z_{b,k}=c_j\overline Y_{b,k}$. Its mean is
$H_{j,b}(d_k)$ and
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Z_{b,k})
   =\frac{c_jH_{j,b}(d_k)(1-p_{j,b}(d_k))}{N_{b,k}}
                   \le\frac{c_jM_0d_k^2}{N_{b,k}}.
\]
A centered summand is bounded by $c_j$. The Bernoulli exponential
moment bound consequently gives
\[
 \Pr\{|Z_{b,k}-H_{j,b}(d_k)|>t\}
 \le2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{N_{b,k}t^2}
                            {2c_j(M_0d_k^2+t/3)}\right\}.
\]
The allocations and a union bound make every nodal error at most
$t$ with probability $1-\eta$. On this event,
\eqref{eq:v12-bias} treats the smooth bridge error and
\eqref{eq:v12-noise} treats only the centered scalar errors, giving
\[
 |JZ_b-JH_{\mathcal V_b}|_{\mathscr A}
                           \le C(t h^{-5/2}+\tau^j).
\]
The minimizing property and the dictionary net bound the discrepancy
of the reconstructed candidate images by twice this quantity plus
$\delta$. Apply \eqref{eq:v12-approx} to the true and fitted
candidate fluxes. The triangle inequality for the linear seminorm
gives their unregularized Abel discrepancy at most
$C(h^{m-1/2}+t h^{-5/2}+\tau^j+\delta)$.
The two-sided inverse of Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-abel-inverse}
proves \eqref{eq:v12-error}. The positive mass-one kernel of $T$
gives the $2B$ bound for the odd-law prediction.

Summing \eqref{eq:v12-allocation} uses
$\sum d_k^2\le D^2L$ and $L=D/h$, and includes each ceiling.
This proves \eqref{eq:v12-total}. Put $\nu=m-1/2$.
Choose an integer mesh with $h\asymp\varepsilon^{1/\nu}$,
$t=c\varepsilon h^{5/2}$, and $\delta=c\varepsilon$.
Take the least even $j\ge2$ with $\tau^j\le c\varepsilon$.
Then
\[
 h^{-1}t^{-2}=c^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2}h^{-6},\qquad
 e^{j\gamma}\le C\varepsilon^{-\gamma/|\log\tau|}.
\]
Integer rounding changes only fixed constants. Substituting in
\eqref{eq:v12-total}, and absorbing the smaller ceiling term,
proves \eqref{eq:v12-rate}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A uniform-data conditional modulus]
\label{cor:v12-modulus}
For $V,W\in\mathcal K_m$, put $e=\|H_V-H_W\|_\infty$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-modulus}
             \|V-W\|_\infty\le C e^{(m-1/2)/(m+2)}.
\end{equation}
This is a conditional estimate on a fixed regularity class of forward
images. No optimality of its exponent is asserted.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the inverse theorem, insert $JH_V,JH_W$, and apply
\eqref{eq:v12-approx} and \eqref{eq:v12-noise}. This gives
$\|V-W\|_\infty\le C(h^{m-1/2}+e h^{-5/2})$.
For sufficiently small $e>0$, choose an integer mesh with
$h\asymp e^{1/(m+2)}$. If $e=0$, let $h\downarrow0$;
for larger $e$, the bound $\|V-W\|_\infty\le2B$ permits
an enlargement of $C$. These cover all cases.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Unknown gap, area and multiplier}
We next remove the actual values of $g,A,\gamma$, not the supplied
labels or class certificates. Assume precisely the pilot model
\eqref{eq:v11-calibration-model}: a supplied positive coarse gap
bracket of width at most $d_c$, positive compact area and multiplier
boxes, and one- and two-flight physical probabilities with exact
leading coefficients $[2A\sinh(r\gamma)]^{-1}$ and unknown
$C^{m-1,1}$ remainders $F_r$, normalized at zero and bounded below.
Their common norm and positivity constants are supplied. In addition,
assume the relative $C^3$ estimates on $[0,D_+]$, a uniform
$C^{3,1}$ bound for $H_{j,b}$ there, and a fixed $D<D_+$.
These are the same assumptions as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-self-calibrated}; the profile class is not
replaced by $\mathcal K_{m+2}$.

\begin{theorem}[Self-calibrated complete-profile observation]
\label{thm:v12-self-calibrated}
Under these supplied smooth physical bounds, for sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$ and $0<\eta<1/4$, a Borel procedure recovers
both complete energy profiles on $[0,D]$ and their predicted odd
law to uniform error $\varepsilon$, with probability at least
$1-\eta$. It uses a separately charged one-/two-flight pilot and
fresh positive-offset bits at one subsequent even flight number.
Its deterministic total preparation count is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-self-rate}
 C\varepsilon^{-\left(2+\frac6{m-1/2}
                              +\frac{\gamma_+}{|\log\tau|}\right)}
                                 \log\frac{C}{\eta\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
The estimate includes the energy origin and does not infer separate
contact curvatures or unsymmetrized contact branches.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We give the plug-in argument because an error in the programmed time
must be handled before regularization. Suppose a pilot yields, on
an event of probability $1-\eta_0$,
\[
 0\le\widehat g-g\le\rho,
 \quad |\widehat A/A-1|\le\alpha,
 \quad |\widehat\gamma-\gamma|\le\zeta,
 \quad j\zeta\le1,\quad j\rho\le D_+-D.
\]
All estimates are projected onto their supplied compact boxes even
on bad histories. Program time $j\widehat g+d$ and scale a new
bit by $\widehat c_j=2\widehat A\sinh(j\widehat\gamma)$.
Conditionally on the pilot its mean is exactly
\[
 R H_{j,b}(d+\Delta),\qquad
 \Delta=j(\widehat g-g),\qquad
 R=\frac{\widehat A}{A}
                         \frac{\sinh(j\widehat\gamma)}{\sinh(j\gamma)}.
\]
Since $\gamma\ge\gamma_->0$, differentiation of $\log\sinh(j\gamma)$
gives $|R-1|\le C(\alpha+j\zeta)$. The one-sided gap estimate
keeps every translated argument in $[0,D_+]$. The $C^{3,1}$
flux bound gives a $C^3$ translation error $O(\Delta)$, including
at zero. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-plugin-bias}
 \|R H_{j,b}(\cdot+\Delta)-H_{\mathcal V_b}\|_{C^3([0,D])}
                         \le C(\tau^j+\alpha+j\zeta+j\rho).
\end{equation}
No factor $\Delta/d$ occurs, since the bits have not been divided
by $d^2$.

On the good pilot event the mean is bounded by a supplied constant
$M_H$. Allocate at each node
\[
 \left\lceil2\widehat c_j(M_H+t/3)t^{-2}
                            \log(4L/\eta_1)\right\rceil
\]
fresh preparations. Conditional Bernoulli concentration gives nodal
error at most $t$ with probability $1-\eta_1$. Applying
\eqref{eq:v12-bias} to \eqref{eq:v12-plugin-bias}, and using the
same dictionary argument, yields error at most
\[
 C\{h^{m-1/2}+t h^{-5/2}+\delta+
                       \tau^j+\alpha+j\zeta+j\rho\}.
\]
The smooth calibration term has no inverse power of $h$. The charge
on every history is bounded by
$2L+C e^{j\gamma_+}h^{-1}t^{-2}\log(4L/\eta_1)$,
since the pilot estimates are always in their compact boxes.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:v11-calibration} with
$\xi=c\varepsilon/j$ and $\eta_0=\eta/2$.
Its proof, retained in full below, brackets the onset using charged
one-flight trials and extrapolates the two unknown smooth remainders
from positive nodes; it does not assume an exact-family probability
oracle. It supplies $\alpha+\zeta\le C\xi$ and
$\rho\le C\xi^{1+1/m}$ with pilot charge
\[
 C(j/\varepsilon)^{2+2/m}
               \log\frac{C\log(Cj/\varepsilon)}{\eta}.
\]
Choose the least even $j$ with $\tau^j\le c\varepsilon$,
$\eta_1=\eta/2$, and the mesh, $t$ and $\delta$ from
Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-acquisition}. For small enough $c$ and
$\varepsilon$, all the displayed pilot constraints hold and the
profile error is at most $\varepsilon$. Conditioning and the two
failure allocations give total failure probability at most $\eta$.
Finally
\[
 2+\frac6{m-1/2}+\frac{\gamma_+}{|\log\tau|}>2+\frac2m.
\]
This strict power gap absorbs the pilot cost and its fixed powers
of $\log(1/\varepsilon)$ into \eqref{eq:v12-self-rate}.
The allocation is bounded independently of pilot success, proving
the unconditional deterministic preparation claim.
\end{proof}

For $m=4$, the profile-stage accuracy power, excluding the common
bridge factor, is $26/7$ rather than the power $6$ of the earlier
$m$-node Abel construction. Both are sufficient bounds for specified
procedures. A larger admissible value of $\tau$ weakens the displayed
bridge factor without changing the billiard. Neither this factor nor
the complete exponent is asserted to be an intrinsic information law.
The physical finite-dimensional lower bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-finite-observation} and the separate nuisance
lower bounds below do not imply a lower bound for this full-profile
experiment. The two even laws identify the invariant; separately
measured odd records can validate, rather than further identify, its
predicted compatibility law.
