\section{Critical boundary experiments and support separation}
\label{sec:v7-critical}
The relative comparison has a statistical scale which is not captured by
weak convergence of a single record. We determine it first for the
quadratic experiments, then transfer it to actual positive-offset laws
in an explicit joint limit. The calculation allows unequal facing
curvatures and either parity of the flight number. Throughout this
section the channel and table are fixed; constants are uniform on the
compact geometric families specified in Section~\ref{sec:geometric-results}.
The two hypotheses compared here are the finite law and its boundary
model for that same table, not two unknown billiard tables.

Write $\iota=j\bmod2$, $a_0=S_0''(0)$, $a_\iota=S_\iota''(0)$, and
$D=\diag(\sqrt{a_0},\sqrt{a_\iota})$. Lemma~\ref{lem:g-jacobi} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-whitening}
 H_j=D M_jD,\qquad H_{\partial,\iota}=D^2,\qquad
 M_j=\begin{pmatrix}\coth(j\gamma)&-\csch(j\gamma)\\
                    -\csch(j\gamma)&\coth(j\gamma)\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Both determinants equal $a_0a_\iota$. On the common endpoint--residual
coordinate space define
\[
 \mathcal D(H)=\{(z,s)\in\R^2\times\R:s\ge0,
                         \ s+z^{\mathsf T}Hz/2<1\}.
\]
Its volume is $\pi/\sqrt{\det H}$, as follows by first integrating
$s$ and then making a linear change to a disk. Let $\Pi_j^0$ and
$\Pi_{\partial,j}^0$ have the common density $\sqrt{a_0a_\iota}/\pi$
on $\mathcal D(H_j)$ and $\mathcal D(H_{\partial,\iota})$, respectively.
The second subscript in $\Pi_{\partial,j}^0$ retains parity.
The actual conditional laws $\Pi_{j,d},\Pi_{\partial,j,d}$ and the
raw laws $P_{j,d},P_{\partial,j,d}$ are those of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-transfer}; the boundary raw law retains the finite
normalizing twist. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-tangent-mass}
 p_{j,d}^0=\frac{d^2}{2A\sinh(j\gamma)},\qquad
 P_{j,d}^0=(1-p_{j,d}^0)\delta_{\dagger}+p_{j,d}^0\Pi_j^0,
 \quad
 P_{\partial,j,d}^0=(1-p_{j,d}^0)\delta_{\dagger}
                         +p_{j,d}^0\Pi_{\partial,j}^0.
\end{equation}
Shrink the common collar so these are probability measures. We use
$\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}=\sup_B|P(B)-Q(B)|$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact support separation]\label{thm:v7-exact-tv}
For every $j\ge1$, independently of the two facing curvatures except
through $\gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-delta}
 \delta_j:=\|\Pi_j^0-\Pi_{\partial,j}^0\|_{\rm TV}
  =\frac2\pi\arcsin(e^{-j\gamma}).
\end{equation}
For independent observations at any finite list $(j_i,d_i)_{i=1}^n$,
\begin{align}
 \left\|\bigotimes_i\Pi_{j_i}^0-
             \bigotimes_i\Pi_{\partial,j_i}^0\right\|_{\rm TV}
     &=1-\prod_i(1-\delta_{j_i}),\label{eq:v7-cond-product}\\
 \left\|\bigotimes_iP_{j_i,d_i}^0-
             \bigotimes_iP_{\partial,j_i,d_i}^0\right\|_{\rm TV}
     &=1-\prod_i(1-p_{j_i,d_i}^0\delta_{j_i}).\label{eq:v7-raw-product}
\end{align}
As a simple binary experiment, each displayed pair of product laws is
exactly equivalent, by measurable randomizations independent of the
binary hypothesis, to an erasure experiment: with probability equal
to the corresponding product on the right, the observation is an
erasure, and otherwise it reveals the hypothesis. Consequently its
minimum equal-prior testing error is half that product.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The common transformation $x=Dz/\sqrt2$ and a subsequent orthogonal
rotation reduce every residual section to a disk and an equal-area
ellipse. The eigenvalues of $M_j$ are
\[
 \lambda=\tanh(j\gamma/2),\qquad \lambda^{-1},
 \qquad 0<\lambda<1.
\]
For the unit section the ellipse is
$\lambda x^2+\lambda^{-1}y^2<1$. Its boundary crosses the unit circle
in the first quadrant at $\theta_0=\arctan\sqrt\lambda$.
Polar integration gives the intersection area
\[
 2\left\{\theta_0+\int_{\theta_0}^{\pi/2}
       \frac{\dd\theta}{\lambda\cos^2\theta+
                                    \lambda^{-1}\sin^2\theta}\right\}
   =4\theta_0.
\]
For the equality, set $t=\tan\theta$ in the integral; it becomes
$[\arctan(t/\lambda)]_{\sqrt\lambda}^{\infty}=\theta_0$.
Every residual section is obtained by the same dilation for both
supports, so integration in $s$ leaves the overlap fraction
$4\theta_0/\pi$. Equal densities on the intersection imply
$\delta_j=1-4\theta_0/\pi$.
If $q=e^{-j\gamma}$, then $\lambda=(1-q)/(1+q)$ and
$\pi/2-2\arctan\sqrt\lambda=\arcsin q$; this proves
\eqref{eq:v7-delta}. The whitening in \eqref{eq:v7-whitening} was common
to both laws and loses no information. In particular the computation
does not require equal curvatures or an even flight number.

For a pair with equal densities on its overlap, the measure with
density the minimum of the two densities is exactly their common
restriction. In the product the same property holds, and its mass is
the product of the individual common masses. This proves
\eqref{eq:v7-cond-product}. In the raw observation space the common
restriction also includes the failure atom; its mass in observation
$i$ is $1-p_{j_i,d_i}^0\delta_{j_i}$. This proves
\eqref{eq:v7-raw-product}.

Here is the asserted equivalence, including the reverse randomization.
Send the common support to an erasure symbol, the support exclusive to
the first law to $0$, and the support exclusive to the second to $1$.
The common restricted measures of the two laws coincide. Given an
erasure, draw from their normalized common restriction; given $0$ or
$1$, draw from the corresponding normalized exclusive restriction.
These kernels recover the two original laws and do not depend on which
of the two hypotheses is true. Zero-mass restrictions may be assigned
an arbitrary fixed probability. Under equal prior probabilities there
is no error on an exclusive observation, and the posterior probabilities
on an erasure remain equal. The minimum error is therefore half the
common mass. This construction is for the fixed simple pair specified
above; its kernel may depend on the known table and design.
\end{proof}

The next lemma controls the approximation error in the same topology
before allowing the sample size to grow. Define
\[
 \omega(d)=\sqrt d,
 \quad\text{or }\omega(d)=d\text{ when both contact graphs are even.}
\]
Evenness here does not require the two graphs to be identical.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform tangent approximation]\label{lem:v7-tangent}
On a common positive collar, uniformly in $j$ and in the specified
compact geometric family,
\begin{align}
 \|\Pi_{j,d}-\Pi_j^0\|_{\rm TV}
  +\|\Pi_{\partial,j,d}-\Pi_{\partial,j}^0\|_{\rm TV}
      &\le C\omega(d),\label{eq:v7-tangent-cond}\\
 \|P_{j,d}-P_{j,d}^0\|_{\rm TV}
  +\|P_{\partial,j,d}-P_{\partial,j,d}^0\|_{\rm TV}
      &\le C p_{j,d}^0\omega(d).\label{eq:v7-tangent-raw}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $E_j=W_j-jg$ and $b_j=-W_{j,uv}/[-W_{j,uv}(0)]$.
The uniform Taylor estimates in Lemma~\ref{lem:g-relative} give, on
bounded scaled endpoint sets,
\[
 \frac{E_j(\sqrt d\,z)}d=\tfrac12z^{\mathsf T}H_jz
                                    +O(\sqrt d\,|z|^3),\qquad
 b_j(\sqrt d\,z)=1+O(\sqrt d\,|z|).
\]
All sublevels under discussion lie in a common bounded set by uniform
coercivity. At each $z$, the actual and quadratic residual intervals
start at zero and have lengths $(1-E_j(\sqrt d\,z)/d)_+$ and
$(1-z^{\mathsf T}H_jz/2)_+$. Their symmetric-difference length is at
most $C\sqrt d\,|z|^3$. Integrating it and the amplitude error over
that bounded set bounds the total variation norm of the unnormalized
densities by $C\sqrt d$. Their normalizing integrals are bounded away
from zero; dividing therefore proves the first finite-law estimate.

The same argument applies to $S_0(u)+S_\iota(v)$ and $B_0(u)B_\iota(v)$.
Their uniform derivatives follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:v4-halfline}.
If both graphs are even, uniqueness of the stationary segment makes
$E_j$ and $b_j$ invariant under simultaneous endpoint sign reversal.
Their remainders improve to $O(d|z|^4)$ and $O(d|z|^2)$, respectively;
the half-line expressions have the same improvement. This proves
\eqref{eq:v7-tangent-cond} with either choice of $\omega$.

The successful subprobabilities have the common scalar prefactor
$d^2[-W_{j,uv}(0)]/(2\pi A)$. The quadratic integral is
$\pi/\sqrt{a_0a_\iota}$, and the Hessian identity gives
$[-W_{j,uv}(0)]/\sqrt{a_0a_\iota}=1/\sinh(j\gamma)$.
Multiplying the unnormalized estimate by this prefactor bounds the
successful-measure difference by $Cp_{j,d}^0\omega(d)$.
The difference of the failure masses is no larger than that bound.
Restoring these atoms proves \eqref{eq:v7-tangent-raw}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joint physical experiment limit]\label{thm:v7-critical}
For any finite independent schedule in the common collar, the actual
positive-offset laws satisfy
\begin{align}
 \left|\left\|\bigotimes_i\Pi_{j_i,d_i}-
       \bigotimes_i\Pi_{\partial,j_i,d_i}\right\|_{\rm TV}
       -\left(1-\prod_i(1-\delta_{j_i})\right)\right|
      &\le C\sum_i\omega(d_i),\label{eq:v7-physical-cond}\\
 \left|\left\|\bigotimes_iP_{j_i,d_i}-
       \bigotimes_iP_{\partial,j_i,d_i}\right\|_{\rm TV}
       -\left(1-\prod_i(1-p_{j_i,d_i}^0\delta_{j_i})\right)\right|
      &\le C\sum_i p_{j_i,d_i}^0\omega(d_i).
                                      \label{eq:v7-physical-raw}
\end{align}
In particular, let $j\to\infty$, $d_j>0$, and set $q_j=e^{-j\gamma}$.
For $k_j$ conditional successes, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-critical-cond-scales}
 k_jq_j\longrightarrow b\in[0,\infty],\qquad
                 k_j\omega(d_j)\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
then their total variation distance from the boundary experiment tends
to $1-e^{-2b/\pi}$. For $n_j$ raw preparations the same limit holds if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-critical-raw-scales}
 n_jp_{j,d_j}^0q_j\longrightarrow b\in[0,\infty],\qquad
                 n_jp_{j,d_j}^0\omega(d_j)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
At $b=\infty$ the limit is interpreted as one. The minimum equal-prior
testing errors tend to $e^{-2b/\pi}/2$. These limits hold for unequal
facing curvatures and across both parity subsequences.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Telescoping product measures gives
$\|\bigotimes_iP_i-\bigotimes_iQ_i\|_{\rm TV}
\le\sum_i\|P_i-Q_i\|_{\rm TV}$. Apply this separately on both
sides of each simple binary comparison, use the reverse triangle
inequality for their distances, and invoke Lemma~\ref{lem:v7-tangent}
and Theorem~\ref{thm:v7-exact-tv}. This proves the finite inequalities.

Now $\delta_j=(2/\pi)q_j+O(q_j^3)$. If $k_jq_j$ has a finite limit,
$k_j\delta_j^2\to0$ and
$k_j\log(1-\delta_j)\to-2b/\pi$; if it diverges, use
$(1-\delta_j)^{k_j}\le e^{-k_j\delta_j}$. This proves the conditional
limit. The raw calculation is identical with
$p_{j,d_j}^0\delta_j$ in place of $\delta_j$; this quantity tends to
zero, and the required first-order sum is
$(2/\pi)n_jp_{j,d_j}^0q_j(1+O(q_j^2))$.
The error terms vanish by the second condition in each displayed
scaling assumption. For any simple pair $P,Q$, integration of the
smaller of their two densities gives minimum equal-prior error
$(1-\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV})/2$. This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

The conditions are nonempty. In the general smooth case take
$d_j=q_j^{2+\epsilon}$, with any fixed $\epsilon>0$, and
$k_j\sim b/q_j$, or $n_j\sim b/(p_{j,d_j}^0q_j)$, for $0<b<\infty$.
In the even case $d_j=q_j^{1+\epsilon}$ suffices. Thus the critical
number of successes is of order $e^{j\gamma}$, while the critical
number of raw preparations is of order $d_j^{-2}e^{2j\gamma}$.
These are distinct resources. In the tangent experiment the probability
that every observation lies in the common support is the product in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v7-exact-tv}; its limiting exponential is the source
of the testing profile, rather than a Gaussian fluctuation argument.

More generally, in a triangular schedule, if the largest $\delta_{j_i}$
tends to zero, $\sum_i\delta_{j_i}\to\lambda$, and
$\sum_i\omega(d_i)\to0$, the conditional distance tends to
$1-e^{-\lambda}$. Replace $\delta_{j_i}$ by
$p_{j_i,d_i}^0\delta_{j_i}$ and the last sum by
$\sum_i p_{j_i,d_i}^0\omega(d_i)$ for raw preparations.
The logarithm argument in the proof applies without change.
The identity maps to the tangent laws and the kernels in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v7-exact-tv} also give two-way randomizations whose
errors tend to zero under these same assumptions. This last statement
concerns the specified simple binary experiments only.

The uniform relative nonlinear theorem is still needed outside this
joint tangent regime: at fixed positive $d$ it compares the actual
nonlinear boundary law, whereas replacing it by its quadratic law
would retain an $O(\omega(d))$ error in each success. The rate $\tau$
in Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-transfer} is not asserted to be optimal.
Nor do the preceding identities extend total variation to full
embedded growing collision arrays. They show, in an actual physical
joint limit, why exponentially small support differences and the
number of successful preparations must be controlled together.
