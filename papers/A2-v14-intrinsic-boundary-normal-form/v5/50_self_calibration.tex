\section{Local calibration from the onset probabilities}
\label{sec:v5-calibration}
The known-gap experiment of Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-acquisition} and an
experiment which must calibrate that gap are different. We first compute
the sensitivity of the former statistic, and then give a count-based
calibration procedure whose preparation cost can be included explicitly.
Throughout this section $m$ is fixed and $j$ is odd. Constants may depend
on $m$ and on the compact geometric family, but not on $j$.

\begin{proposition}[The harmonic timing coefficient]\label{prop:v5-harmonic}
Let $V(d)$ denote either of the normalized physical endpoint variances
at the chosen flight number, and let $v=V(0)$. For the programmed-offset
normalization in Proposition~\ref{prop:v4-timing}, put $\tau=j\Delta g$.
The extrapolated population statistic is
\[
 W_m(\tau)=\sum_{l=1}^m\omega_l
                \left(1-\frac{\tau}{lh}\right)V(lh-\tau).
\]
Its first timing derivative satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-harmonic}
 W_m'(0)=-\frac{vH_m}{h}+O_m(1),\qquad
 H_m=\sum_{l=1}^m\frac1l.
\end{equation}
The remainder is uniform in $j$. In particular extrapolation does not
cancel the leading normalization error.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate the displayed statistic to obtain
$-\sum_l\omega_l[V(lh)/(lh)+V'(lh)]$. The uniform estimates
$V(d)=v+O(d)$ and $V'(d)=O(1)$ reduce the leading term to
$-v\sum_l\omega_l/lh$. The binomial identity
\[
 \sum_{l=1}^m\frac{(-1)^{l-1}\binom ml}{l}
 =\int_0^1\frac{1-(1-x)^m}{x}\dd x
 =\sum_{l=1}^m\frac1l
\]
proves \eqref{eq:v5-harmonic}. The last equality follows on setting
$y=1-x$ and summing $1+y+\cdots+y^{m-1}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A population calibration lemma}
Suppose that a coarse location $g_0$ is supplied with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-coarse-gap}
                 |j(g-g_0)|\le h/4.
\end{equation}
This is a local bracketing assumption, not a fine calibration at scale
$h^{m+1}/j$. The cost of locating this coarse bracket is not included
in the experiment below. Write $\tau=j(g-g_0)$ and observe the selected
channel at the $m+1$ programmed times $jg_0+lh$, $1\le l\le m+1$.
Let $p_l$ be the respective probabilities. By the uniform threshold law,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-calibration-probability}
 p_l=K_j(lh-\tau)^2F_j(lh-\tau),\qquad
 K_j=\frac1{2A\sinh(j\gamma)}.
\end{equation}
All actual offsets are positive. The common factor $K_j$ is unknown
and will not have to be estimated.

Given positive approximations $\widehat p_l$, interpolate their square
roots at $z=lh$ by a polynomial $\widehat P$ of degree at most $m$.
If it has a unique real zero in $[-h/2,h/2]$, define $\widehat\tau$ to
be that zero. Otherwise set $\widehat\tau=0$. This convention always
keeps $\widehat\tau$ in the indicated interval.

\begin{lemma}[Stable extrapolation of the onset zero]\label{lem:v5-calibration}
There are $h_m,\epsilon_m>0$ such that, if $0<h\le h_m$ and
$\max_l|\widehat p_l/p_l-1|\le\epsilon\le\epsilon_m$, then
$\widehat P$ has exactly one zero in $[-h/2,h/2]$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-root-error}
            |\widehat\tau-\tau|
                   \le C_m(h^{m+1}+h\epsilon).
\end{equation}
After decreasing $h_m,\epsilon_m$, the right side is at most $h/8$.
The assertion holds uniformly in $j$ without knowing $K_j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $H_j(d)=\sqrt{F_j(d)}$. Positivity and the uniform differentiated
threshold law give $H_j(0)=1$ and bounded right derivatives through
order $m$. At the positive nodal arguments $d_l=lh-\tau$,
\[
 K_j^{-1/2}\sqrt{p_l}=d_lH_j(d_l).
\]
Let $Q(z)$ be the degree-$m$ Taylor polynomial obtained by multiplying
$z-\tau$ by the degree-$(m-1)$ right Taylor polynomial of $H_j$ at zero.
Then $Q(\tau)=0$, and $Q'(z)=1+O_m(h)$ for $|z|\le h/2$.
Taylor's integral remainder at the positive $d_l$ shows that the exact
nodal values differ from $Q(lh)$ by at most $C_mh^{m+1}$.

For the fixed nodes $1,\ldots,m+1$, let $L_l$ be their Lagrange basis.
On $[-1/2,1/2]$ both $\sum|L_l|$ and $\sum|L_l'|$ are bounded by
constants depending only on $m$. Therefore interpolation of the nodal
Taylor remainders changes the value by at most $C_mh^{m+1}$ and the
$z$ derivative by at most $C_mh^m$ throughout $[-h/2,h/2]$.
This argument uses Taylor coefficients and polynomial evaluation only;
it does not evaluate a physical probability at a negative offset.

If the probability errors are at most $\epsilon$ relatively, the
square-root errors are at most $C\epsilon$ relatively. The normalized
nodal square roots have size $O_m(h)$. The same Lagrange bounds thus
add at most $C_mh\epsilon$ to the value error and $C_m\epsilon$ to the
derivative error. Hence $K_j^{-1/2}\widehat P$ has derivative at least
$1/2$ on the interval for sufficiently small $h,\epsilon$, and its value
at $\tau$ is at most $C_m(h^{m+1}+h\epsilon)$ in absolute value.
Since $|\tau|\le h/4$, its values at the two interval endpoints have
opposite signs after decreasing these constants' allowed neighborhood.
Monotonicity proves existence and uniqueness of the root and the
mean-value theorem proves \eqref{eq:v5-root-error}. The common unknown
positive factor does not change any zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Calibration and conditional recovery in one cost bound}
\begin{theorem}[Locally self-calibrated curvature acquisition]
\label{thm:v5-self-calibration}
Assume the coarse bracket \eqref{eq:v5-coarse-gap}, a fixed selected
channel, and independent full-phase preparations. For $0<h\le h_m$
and $0<\eta<1/2$, there is an experiment with the following properties.
It estimates the gap by $\widehat g=g_0+\widehat\tau/j$ and the two
contact curvatures by $\widehat\kappa_b$, and, with probability at
least $1-\eta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-joint-error}
 |\widehat g-g|\le C_mh^{m+1}/j,\qquad
 \max_b|\widehat\kappa_b-\kappa_b|\le C_mh^m.
\end{equation}
Its total number $T$ of unconditioned preparations, including calibration
and conditional-position acquisition, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-joint-cost}
 \E T\le C_me^{j\gamma}h^{-(2m+2)}\log(C_m/\eta),\qquad
 T\le C_me^{j\gamma}h^{-(2m+2)}\log(C_m/\eta)
\end{equation}
with the latter bound holding together with \eqref{eq:v5-joint-error}
with probability at least $1-\eta$. Thus for curvature accuracy
$\varepsilon$ a sufficient preparation order remains
\[
       C_me^{j\gamma}\varepsilon^{-(2+2/m)}\log(C_m/\eta).
\]
Unlike Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-acquisition}, this statement includes the
fine-gap calibration experiment. It still presupposes the local coarse
bracket, fixed channel patches, and noiseless collision recording.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each of the $m+1$ calibration windows, start independent preparations
until $r$ selected events have occurred. Let $T_l$ be this stopping time
and put $\widehat p_l=r/T_l$. We record the waiting-time estimate used
here. For independent Bernoulli trials of probability $p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-negative-binomial}
 \Pr\{|(r/T_r)/p-1|>\epsilon\}
                    \le 2e^{-c r\epsilon^2},\qquad
 \E T_r=r/p,
\end{equation}
for $0<\epsilon<1/4$ and $r\ge C\epsilon^{-2}$, with absolute
constants. Indeed $T_r>n$ is equivalent to fewer than $r$ successes
in $n$ trials, and $T_r\le n$ is equivalent to at least $r$ successes.
Apply the binomial exponential Markov bound at the two integers nearest
$r/(p(1-\epsilon))$ and $r/(p(1+\epsilon))$. Rounding errors are absorbed
by $r\epsilon\ge C$. The relative deviations of the binomial means
from $r$ are comparable to $\epsilon$, which gives the exponential
bound. The expectation is the sum of $r$ independent geometric waiting
times of mean $1/p$.

Choose $\epsilon=c_mh^m$ with a sufficiently small fixed $c_m$ and
\[
       r=\left\lceil C_mh^{-2m}\log(C_m/\eta)\right\rceil.
\]
Use \eqref{eq:v5-negative-binomial} and a union bound over the calibration
windows. Except on an event of probability at most $\eta/4$, their
relative errors are at most $c_mh^m$. Lemma~\ref{lem:v5-calibration}
then gives $|\widehat\tau-\tau|\le C_mh^{m+1}$ and the first estimate
in \eqref{eq:v5-joint-error}.

Use fresh independent preparations for the conditional-position stage,
at the $m$ programmed windows $j\widehat g+lh$. At each window collect
$2K$ selected records and form the paired variance estimator of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v3-sampling}, normalized by the programmed $lh$.
Take
$K=\lceil C_mh^{-2m}\log(C_m/\eta)\rceil$ and use the extrapolation
weights \eqref{eq:v4-richardson}. Conditional on the entire calibration
experiment, these new preparations are independent, and all the
variance concentration bounds are uniform in the chosen windows.
On the calibration success event,
\[
 \frac{j|g-\widehat g|}{h}=\frac{|\tau-\widehat\tau|}{h}
                                                     \le C_mh^m.
\]
For small $h$ this is in the timing collar of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v4-timing}. That proposition and the conditional
concentration bound give the second estimate of
\eqref{eq:v5-joint-error}, with a further failure probability at most
$\eta/4$. Use a fixed compact reconstruction box enlarged slightly to
include all the supplied nearby gap values.

We check cost also outside the successful calibration event. The root
rule always gives $|\widehat\tau|\le h/2$, whereas $|\tau|\le h/4$.
Consequently every actual endpoint-stage offset is
\[
          lh+\widehat\tau-\tau\in[h/4,(m+3/4)h].
\]
The calibration offsets are in $[3h/4,(m+5/4)h]$. Decrease $h_m$ so
both ranges lie in the common threshold collar. In both stages every
success probability is bounded above and below by fixed multiples of
$h^2e^{-j\gamma}$, uniformly over all calibration outcomes. In
particular a failed polynomial fit cannot produce a nonpositive offset
or an infinite waiting-time expectation. The expectation identity in
\eqref{eq:v5-negative-binomial}, conditioning on the pilot in the second
stage, gives
\[
           \E T\le C_me^{j\gamma}h^{-2}(r+K).
\]
For the high-probability cost, the equivalence with binomial counts and
$\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)<np/2\}\le e^{-np/8}$ show that waiting
for $r$ or $2K$ successes costs at most
$C p^{-1}(r+K+\log(C_m/\eta))$, simultaneously over the fixed number
of windows. These bounds also hold conditionally on the pilot, with
the same constants. A union bound, allocating the remaining $\eta/2$
to the waiting-time tails, proves the simultaneous assertion in
\eqref{eq:v5-joint-cost}. Substituting $r,K$ proves its order, and
$h=c_m\varepsilon^{1/m}$ gives the final accuracy statement.
\end{proof}

The calibration gain comes from estimating the zero of the square root
of an onset probability, not from cancelling the nonzero harmonic
coefficient in \eqref{eq:v5-harmonic}. The unknown area and the rare-event
prefactor cancel in that zero. The factor $e^{j\gamma}$ nevertheless
persists in the number of preparations needed to locate it. No minimax
claim, independence of successive impacts, or uncharged fine timing
measurement is used.
