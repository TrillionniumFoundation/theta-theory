## Verified references and scope of use

**G. N. Nair and R. J. Evans.** “Stabilization with data-rate-limited feedback: tightest attainable bounds.” *Systems & Control Letters* 41 (2000), 49–56. DOI `10.1016/S0167-6911(00)00037-2`. Author-hosted listing and publisher metadata verified. Used for historical positioning; no detailed theorem from this paper is imported into a new proof.

**S. Tatikonda and S. K. Mitter.** “Control Under Communication Constraints.” *IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control* 49 (2004), 1056–1068. DOI `10.1109/TAC.2004.831187`. The primary paper at `https://www.cmi.caltech.edu/events/controlunder_ieee.pdf` was inspected, including its information-pattern diagrams and the proposition statements. The comparison uses the stated rate resource and recursive construction, not a claim that their hypotheses match the present regenerative experiment.

**A. S. Matveev and A. V. Savkin.** *Estimation and Control over Communication Networks*. Birkhäuser, 2009. DOI `10.1007/978-0-8176-4607-3`. Publisher metadata and description verified. Used as a monograph reference, not as a silently imported theorem.

**E. Lindenstrauss and M. Tsukamoto.** “From Rate Distortion Theory to Metric Mean Dimension: Variational Principle.” *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* 64 (2018), 3590–3609. DOI `10.1109/TIT.2018.2806219`; arXiv `1702.05722`. Primary theorem text and metadata checked. The tame-growth hypothesis and variational optimization over invariant measures are retained in the comparison.

**J. V. Bondar and P. Milnes.** “Amenability: A survey for statistical applications of Hunt–Stein and related conditions on groups.” *Zeitschrift für Wahrscheinlichkeitstheorie und Verwandte Gebiete* 57 (1981), 103–128. DOI `10.1007/BF00533716`. Publisher metadata and abstract checked. Cited to acknowledge the classical invariant-minimax method; the retained Gaussian proof is self-contained and no new general group theorem is claimed.

**P. Heinrich and J. Kahn.** “Optimal rates for finite mixture estimation.” Fixed arXiv version `1504.03506v1` (2015). The primary preprint and Theorem 3.2 were checked. The reference deliberately fixes this edition rather than mixing a later publication title and an earlier theorem number. Its unknown-weight likelihood and Wasserstein parameter metric differ from the present equal-weight noisy-moment experiment. The new collision proof does not rely on the preprint's theorem.

## What is and is not claimed as new

The use of conditional expectations, Hilbert quantization, renewal reward, Gaussian invariance, Newton identities, root perturbation, two-point testing and finite covers is classical. The paper proves a specific posterior-orbit/finite-state realization relation under the stated resource model, computes a matched noisy expanding-renewal profile including uniform crossover and exact state accounting, and derives the calibrated collision-moment risk rather than assuming its singular geometry. It does not claim to replace the data-rate theorem, the metric-mean-dimension variational principle, Hunt–Stein theory, or the general theory of mixture singularities.
