\section{Posterior orbits and finite-state realization}\label{sec:v4-orbits}

Let $E$ and $\mathcal Y$ be standard Borel spaces, let $\mu$ be a
probability on $E$, and let $K$ be a probability kernel from $E$ to
$\mathcal Y$. Let $T:E\to E$ and $f:E\to\R^r$ be Borel, with
$\|f\|\le F<\infty$. At each renewal a fresh pair $(U,Y)$ has law
$\mu(du)K(u,dy)$. The observer acquires $Y$, not necessarily $U$.
Between renewals the hidden state follows $T$. The successive cycle
lengths $L_0,L_1,\ldots$ are independent copies of a positive
integer-valued random variable $L$, independent of all acquired pairs,
and
\begin{equation}
 \ell=\E L<\infty,\qquad s_k=\Pp(L>k),\qquad
 w_k=\frac{s_k}{\ell},\qquad W(n)=\sum_{k\ge n}w_k.                 \label{eq:v4-renewal-weights}
\end{equation}
Thus $\sum_{k\ge0}w_k=1$. There is an acquisition at time $0$.
Subsequent acquisition times are $\tau_j=L_0+\cdots+L_{j-1}$.
At time $t=\tau_j+k<\tau_{j+1}$, the target is $f(T^kU_j)$.
The renewal indicator is observed, but the length of the current cycle
is not announced at its beginning.

An $M$-state observer retains $I_t\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$. Its update is
an arbitrary Borel stochastic kernel of the preceding state, absolute
time, renewal indicator, and, at a renewal only, the new observation.
After the update, the decoder has the state, absolute time, and
independent public randomness. It does not separately retain the last
observation, the renewal indicator, or the renewal age. Private
randomness is allowed, but any data-dependent persistent register,
including an age or a random-seed position, is part of $I_t$.
Program descriptions, real constants, and transient computation are
not charged to $M$. This is a persistent-cardinality constraint,
not a bound on total computational storage.

Choose simultaneous Borel versions
\begin{equation}
 m_k(y)=\E[f(T^kU)\mid Y=y],\qquad
 Z(y)=\bigl(\sqrt{w_k}\,m_k(y)\bigr)_{k\ge0}\in
 \mathcal H:=\ell^2(\N_0;\R^r).                                  \label{eq:v4-posterior-orbit}
\end{equation}
A countable union of null sets makes these versions simultaneous;
values there may be set to zero. Write
\begin{equation}
 e_M^2(Z)=\inf_{c_1,\ldots,c_M\in\mathcal H}
       \E\min_{i\le M}\|Z(Y)-c_i\|_{\mathcal H}^2,
 \qquad
 B=\sum_{k\ge0}w_k\E\|f(T^kU)-m_k(Y)\|^2.                       \label{eq:v4-bayes-floor}
\end{equation}
The number $B$ is the unavoidable error of the complete acquisition
history. Let $A_t=t-\max\{\tau_j:\tau_j\le t\}$, and let $Y_t^*$
be the observation at that last renewal. Define
\begin{align}
 E_M^{\rm av}
 &=\inf_{\mathcal A}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
       \sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\E\|m_{A_t}(Y_t^*)-\widehat f_t\|^2,
                                                               \label{eq:v4-excess-risk}\\
 R_M^{\rm av}
 &=\inf_{\mathcal A}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
       \sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\E\|f(X_t)-\widehat f_t\|^2.
\end{align}
Here and below an infimum is over the specified machine class; it need
not be attained. All statements about $e_M^2$ use squared distortion.

\begin{lemma}[Kernel realization]\label{lem:v4-kernels}
The preceding machine class can be realized using a countable family
of independent uniform random variables, independent of the experiment.
After fixing this family and the history before a renewal, the first
$n$ outputs along a continuation with no further renewal belong to a
set of at most $M$ vectors in $(\R^r)^n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a finite-state transition kernel the inverse cumulative
probabilities give a Borel function of the input and one uniform
variable. For a general standard Borel output, embed its range as a
Borel subset of $[0,1]$, use the measurable inverse distribution
function, and map back; a fixed default value handles the null
exceptional set. Applying this construction to each time-indexed
kernel uses only countably many uniforms. The product probability
space supplies them jointly. Public uniforms are included in the
same construction, with their prescribed sharing between update and
decoder. Once they are fixed, the update and decoder are deterministic.
Given the state immediately after a renewal, the no-renewal input
sequence and absolute times determine the subsequent states and
outputs. There are only $M$ possible starting states. The argument
uses a fixed time-indexed family, not an uncharged data-dependent
position in a random tape.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Posterior-orbit converse and realization]\label{thm:v4-orbit}
For the experiment just defined,
\begin{equation}
 R_M^{\rm av}=B+E_M^{\rm av},\qquad
 e_M^2(Z)\le E_M^{\rm av}.                                      \label{eq:v4-orbit-lower}
\end{equation}
In fact every randomized, time-dependent $M$-state machine satisfies
the latter lower bound with its $\liminf$ time-average excess loss.
Conversely, for all integers $N,n\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 E_{Nn+1}^{\rm av}
 \le e_N^2(Z^{<n})+F^2W(n)
 \le e_N^2(Z)+F^2W(n),                                          \label{eq:v4-orbit-upper}
\end{equation}
where $Z^{<n}$ is the first $n$ coordinates of $Z$.
The upper bound is realized, up to an arbitrary positive error, by a
stationary deterministic machine with states
$\{1,\ldots,N\}\times\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\cup\{\partial\}$.
In particular, for $M\ge2$,
\begin{equation}
 e_M^2(Z)\le E_M^{\rm av}
 \le\inf_{1\le n\le M-1}
 \left\{e_{\lfloor(M-1)/n\rfloor}^2(Z)+F^2W(n)\right\}.          \label{eq:v4-orbit-sandwich}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on the full acquired history, the renewal indicators, and
independent coding randomness, the conditional mean of $f(X_t)$ is
$m_{A_t}(Y_t^*)$. Indeed, the current acquired pair is independent of
previous cycles, and survival of its cycle is independent of that
pair. Conditional orthogonality gives, for every machine,
\[
 \E\|f(X_t)-\widehat f_t\|^2
 =\E\|f(X_t)-m_{A_t}(Y_t^*)\|^2
  +\E\|m_{A_t}(Y_t^*)-\widehat f_t\|^2.
\]
The Ces\`aro renewal-reward identity makes the average of the first
term tend to $B$. Bounded rewards and $\E L<\infty$ suffice, including
arithmetic or deterministic cycle lengths. This proves the equality
in \eqref{eq:v4-orbit-lower}.

Fix $n$. At a renewal at time $s$, condition on the past and then use
Lemma~\ref{lem:v4-kernels}. Along the first $n$ no-renewal steps the
outputs are chosen from at most $M$ decoder strings. The new $Y$ has
its original marginal law under this conditioning. Since survival to
age $k$ has probability $s_k$, the expected excess reward in those
steps is at least
\[
 \inf_{c_1,\ldots,c_M}\E\min_i
       \sum_{k<n}s_k\|m_k(Y)-c_{i,k}\|^2
 =\ell e_M^2(Z^{<n}).
\]
Randomizing the selection of a string cannot reduce its pointwise
minimum. Integrating the conditioned variables preserves the bound.
Let $u_s=\Pp(s\text{ is a renewal})$. Rewards arising from distinct
last renewals are disjoint. Summing over $0\le s\le N-n$ therefore
gives a lower bound
$\ell e_M^2(Z^{<n})\sum_{s=0}^{N-n}u_s$ for the total excess loss up
to time $N-1$. The elementary renewal theorem, in its Ces\`aro form,
gives $N^{-1}\sum_{s<N}u_s\to1/\ell$. Consequently the lower limit
of average loss is at least $e_M^2(Z^{<n})$.

Truncation cannot increase quantization error. Conversely, extending
a codebook for the truncation by zero gives
\[
 e_M^2(Z^{<n})\le e_M^2(Z)
 \le e_M^2(Z^{<n})+F^2W(n).
\]
Letting $n\to\infty$ proves the converse, without a compactness
assumption on the orbit image or a restriction to on-image centres.

For the upper bound choose an $\varepsilon$-optimal $N$-centre
codebook for $Z^{<n}$. Coordinatewise projection permits its $k$th
coordinate to be written $\sqrt{w_k}c_{i,k}$ with $\|c_{i,k}\|\le F$;
a zero-weight coordinate is immaterial. At a renewal, choose the first
nearest centre, a Borel choice, and set the state to $(i,0)$.
At a nonrenewal change $(i,k)$ to $(i,k+1)$ until $k=n-1$, after
which the state is $\partial$. The latter state is absorbing between
renewals. Decode $(i,k)$ as $c_{i,k}$ and $\partial$ as zero.
The average excess reward is at most
$e_N^2(Z^{<n})+\varepsilon+F^2W(n)$ by the same renewal-reward
identity. This is an actual finite-state update; its age information
is precisely the charged second coordinate. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
Unused states are permitted, which proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Invariance of the quantization exponent]\label{cor:v4-exponent}
Suppose $W(n)\le A\exp(-cn^\beta)$ for some $A,c,\beta>0$, and
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{-\log e_M^2(Z)}{\log M}
 \quad\hbox{exists in }(0,\infty).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{-\log E_M^{\rm av}}{\log M}=\alpha.
                                                               \label{eq:v4-exponent}
\end{equation}
If $e_M^2(Z)\asymp M^{-\alpha}$, more explicitly
\[
 c_1M^{-\alpha}\le E_M^{\rm av}
 \le C_1M^{-\alpha}(\log M)^{\alpha/\beta}\qquad(M\ge2),
\]
with constants depending on the displayed hypotheses.
For geometric renewals with parameter $p>0$, the exponent conclusion
also holds for the infimum of the supremum-in-time excess risk.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose any $s>\alpha$ and
$n=\lceil((s/c)\log M)^{1/\beta}\rceil$. Then
$\log\lfloor(M-1)/n\rfloor/\log M\to1$, whereas $W(n)\le AM^{-s}$.
Apply \eqref{eq:v4-orbit-sandwich}; the two logarithmic bounds converge
to $\alpha$. Under the two-sided power bound the same substitution
gives the explicit inequalities, enlarging constants for finitely
many small $M$.

For geometric renewals put $q=1-p$, so $w_k=pq^k$. A stationary
reset-based machine has a nonnegative age-loss sequence $d_k$. With
an initial acquisition at zero its loss at time $t$ is
\[
 p\sum_{k<t}q^kd_k+q^td_t\le p^{-1}\sum_{k\ge0}w_kd_k.
\]
Use the upper machines in Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-orbit}; the converse
follows because a supremum is at least the lower time-average.
\end{proof}

The corollary is an exponent theorem, not a uniform equivalence of
errors for arbitrary orbits. It applies to arbitrary Borel dynamics
and arbitrary noisy or partial reset observations. Its geometric
invariant is the quantization exponent of the \emph{posterior} future
orbit; the tail assumption controls the cost of making a finite
codebook causal. Computing that invariant in a particular dynamical
system remains a separate mathematical task.

\begin{proposition}[Exact autonomous realization]\label{prop:v4-closure}
Let a ray mean a sequence $c=(c_k)_{k\ge0}$ in the ball
$\{z\in\R^r:\|z\|\le F\}$. Among stationary deterministic machines,
the optimal average excess risk is
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{\substack{|\mathcal C|\le M\\ S\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal C}}
       \E\min_{c\in\mathcal C}\sum_{k\ge0}w_k\|m_k(Y)-c_k\|^2,
 \qquad (Sc)_k=c_{k+1}.                                        \label{eq:v4-closed-codebooks}
\end{equation}
The reset update may depend on the previous state; allowing that
dependence does not alter this infimum.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed autonomous no-renewal update $a$ and decoder $d$, a state
$i$ determines the ray $(d(a^ki))_{k\ge0}$. These at most $M$ rays
are closed under $S$. Any reset rule selects one of them, possibly
using the previous state; its reward is no smaller than the pointwise
minimum over all the rays. Conversely, for a finite shift-closed set,
retain the chosen ray as the state, apply $S$ on a nonrenewal, and
decode its first coordinate. The first nearest ray is a Borel reset
rule independent of the previous state. Renewal reward proves the
formula. This is a characterization of the stated autonomous class,
not an assertion that all optimal randomized nonstationary machines
are autonomous.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Orbit cardinality does not include an age register]\label{ex:v4-gap}
Let $E=\{0,1\}$, $U=1$ deterministically, $T(0)=T(1)=0$, and
$f(x)=x$. Use geometric renewals with $0<p<1$ and a constant reset
observation. The posterior orbit is the single ray $(1,0,0,\ldots)$,
so $e_1^2(Z)=0$ and $B=0$. Nevertheless a one-state decoder, having
only absolute time and independent public coins, does not know
whether a renewal occurred at that time. For every $t\ge1$ the
minimum squared error is $p(1-p)$, since the target is an independent
Bernoulli($p$) renewal indicator. Two states suffice for zero error:
write $1$ at each renewal and $0$ otherwise. Thus the orbit converse
cannot be reversed with the same cardinality without a realizability
condition. The finite-chain overhead in \eqref{eq:v4-orbit-upper}
and the closure in \eqref{eq:v4-closed-codebooks} address a genuine
obstruction.
\end{example}
