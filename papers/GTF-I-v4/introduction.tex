\section{Introduction}\label{sec:v4-introduction}

A finite representation of an acquired state need not be a finite
representation of its future. A coordinate which is inexpensive to
forget now may become visible after expansion, and a codebook for a
whole future may require an additional register to be executed one
step at a time. The purpose of this paper is to separate these two
questions and then relate them quantitatively. The first concerns
the geometry of the posterior future orbit. The second concerns its
realization by a finite-state observer.

The resource is a hard cardinality bound $M$ on the observer's
persistent state. We do not replace it by mutual information, by an
average coding length, or by a communication rate. Updates and
outputs may be randomized and time dependent. An absolute clock
is permitted, but the time since the last random acquisition is not
available unless represented in the persistent state. This distinction
will be essential even in a two-point example.

\subsection{The main results}
Consider a dynamical system $T$ which is renewed at independent
random times. A fresh hidden state $U$ is observed through a kernel
$K(U,dy)$ at each renewal. Between renewals there are no new data.
Let $w_k$ be the stationary age probabilities, and let
$m_k(y)=\E[f(T^kU)\mid Y=y]$ for a bounded vector readout $f$.
The posterior orbit is
\[
 Z(y)=(\sqrt{w_k}\,m_k(y))_{k\ge0}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-orbit} proves a converse for every finite-state
observer and a finite-state realization bound: an $N$-centre
codebook, truncated at depth $n$, is executed by $Nn+1$ states.
If $e_M^2(Z)$ is its Hilbert-space quantization error, $E_M^{\rm av}$
is the optimal average prediction error above the full-history Bayes
floor, and $W(n)=\sum_{k\ge n}w_k$, then
\begin{equation}
 e_M^2(Z)\le E_M^{\rm av},\qquad
 E_{Nn+1}^{\rm av}\le e_N^2(Z)+\|f\|_\infty^2W(n).              \label{eq:v4-intro-sandwich}
\end{equation}
Consequently every finite positive quantization exponent is preserved
by causal realization under a stretched-exponential renewal tail
(Corollary~\ref{cor:v4-exponent}). This conclusion requires neither
smooth dynamics nor an invertible or noiseless observation map.
Proposition~\ref{prop:v4-closure} gives the exact finite-codebook
condition for autonomous realization: closure under the left shift.
Example~\ref{ex:v4-gap} shows why such a condition cannot be discarded.

For expanding torus systems the state overhead can be removed at the
level of sharp rates. In dimension $d$, let $T_bx=bx$ modulo one,
with $b\ge2$, Haar resets, a wrapped Gaussian acquisition of noise
level $\sigma$, and the circle-coordinate readout $\Phi$.
The posterior Fourier attenuation is explicit. With
$v_k=w_k\exp(-4\pi^2\sigma^2b^{2k})$ and
\[
 \Psi_v(n)=\sum_{k<n}v_kb^{-2(n-k)}+\sum_{k\ge n}v_k,
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-noisy} identifies the optimal excess risk, up to
constants, as $\Psi_v(n_M)$, where
$1+b^d+\cdots+b^{dn_M}\le M<1+b^d+\cdots+b^{d(n_M+1)}$.
The renewal law need not be geometric: a uniform bound on mean
residual lifetime suffices. The lower bound permits arbitrary
randomized nonstationary encoders and off-image codebook centres.
The upper bound is an explicitly counted suffix machine.

In the geometric case $w_k=(1-q)q^k$, the profile is
\[
 (1-q)b^{-2n}\sum_{k<n}(b^2q)^k+q^n.
\]
This gives the three rates in \eqref{eq:v4-three-regimes} and a
uniform critical window $q=b^{-2}e^{x/n}$ with crossover
$H(x)=(e^x-1)/x$. The exact suffix risk has a leading-coefficient
limit; the optimal risk is uniformly comparable to that profile.
The distinction is stated in Corollary~\ref{cor:v4-critical}.
The same profile governs the noise floor, so noise level and memory
budget can vary jointly. Smooth conjugates furnish nonconstant-slope
expanding examples, and bi-Lipschitz readouts preserve the raw-risk
law (Proposition~\ref{prop:v4-conjugacy}).

A second application concerns observation geometry rather than
transport. Suppose the first $d$ power sums of $d$ ordered real roots
are measured through a correlated Gaussian pair with an unrestricted
additive calibration. Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-roots} proves
\[
 \mathcal R_M\asymp\min(1,\sigma^{2/d})+M^{-2/d}.
\]
On domains with separated clusters and at most $r_*$ roots in a
cluster, the statistical exponent becomes $2/r_*$, while the
representation exponent remains $2/d$. Both exponents are proved
from the actual moment map: Newton identities and a multiplicity-aware
root argument give the upper modulus, and moment-matched colliding
configurations give the lower bound. No global contact equivalence
is inserted as a hypothesis.

\subsection{Relation to earlier theories}
Quantization of dynamical trajectories and the cost of preserving
unstable directions are established themes. Nair and Evans
\cite{NairEvans2000}, Tatikonda and Mitter \cite{TatikondaMitter2004},
and the monograph of Matveev and Savkin \cite{MatveevSavkin2009}
study estimation and control through communication channels. In
particular, Propositions 3.1 and 5.3 of
\cite{TatikondaMitter2004} compare the channel rate with expansion
of unstable linear modes, using a geometric converse and a recursive
quantizer for a specified information pattern. Our geometric converse
is related in method. Its resource is different: no fresh channel
symbol is available between renewals, and the complete persistent
state has only $M$ values. A recursive channel code with real-valued
centres or scale variables is not automatically an $M$-state machine.
We prove the finite-state realization and its overhead instead of
identifying these constraints.

Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.10 of Lindenstrauss and Tsukamoto
\cite{LindenstraussTsukamoto2018} relate metric mean dimension to
small-distortion information rates over invariant measures, under a
tame covering-growth assumption. Here the law of an acquired orbit
is fixed, its future has renewal weights, and the constraint is a
single hard persistent cardinality rather than asymptotic mutual
information per unit time. The two invariants need not agree.
Our exponent theorem does not assert a new variational principle
for metric mean dimension; it identifies when a weighted-orbit
quantization exponent survives finite-state causal realization.

Predictive states and predictive compression are discussed in
\cite{ShaliziCrutchfield2001,LittmanSuttonSingh2002,
MarzenCrutchfield2016,LoomisCrutchfield2022}. Finite-state filter and
control approximations, including their stability assumptions, are
compared in the retained quantitative development with
\cite{PagesPham2005,KalogeriasPetropulu2016,SaldiYukselLinder2017,
KaraYuksel2022,KBY2024,DKY2024}. The present posterior-orbit theorem
uses regeneration in place of a global contracting filter metric.
It does not remove the stability assumptions from those different,
continuously observed models.

The nuisance argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-quotient} belongs to
invariant minimax theory. The role of asymptotically invariant
probabilities is classical; see Bondar and Milnes
\cite{BondarMilnes1981}. In our Gaussian proof diffuse nuisance priors
make the residual observation asymptotically parameter free. Its
specific conclusion preserves each hard label budget $M$ and the
cross-covariance of the observed pair. This is not a new general
Hunt--Stein principle, nor full experiment equivalence. Finally,
singular moduli and testing bounds are classical tools
\cite{DonohoLiu1991}; the moment theorem derives their hypotheses in
a coupled, changing-rank example. The mixture-estimation theorem
of Heinrich and Kahn \cite{HeinrichKahn2015} is not invoked as a
substitute for this derivation, since its sample experiment and
unknown-weight parameter space differ.

\subsection{Organization and dependencies}
Sections~\ref{sec:v4-orbits}--\ref{sec:v4-collisions} contain the
principal theorem chain and its complete proofs. The noisy expansion
law uses the posterior-orbit converse, not the earlier scalar
expansion theorem. The root-collision theorem uses only the exact
Gaussian nuisance quotient from the retained results, together with
the two lemmas proved in its own section. None of these new results
uses a historical billiard spectral theorem, a particle large-deviation
principle, a nonlinear semigroup limit, or an unbounded-operator
closure assertion.

The subsequent quantitative development retains the scalar checkpoint
and causal phase separation, correlated calibration, nonuniform
causal resolution, positive-refresh hidden Markov laws, acquisition
examples, and unrestricted-control comparison, with their proofs.
The appendices retain the measurable experiment calculus, predictive
quotients, information inequalities, and model-interface boundaries.
Thus the principal arguments can be read independently, while the
full mathematical development remains available in this edition.
The title denotes the common programme of causal experiments and
resource-aware reductions; it does not identify an abstract reduction
with the model-specific theorem needed to instantiate it.
