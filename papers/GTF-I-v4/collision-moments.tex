\section{Calibrated moment observations at root collisions}\label{sec:v4-collisions}

We next derive, rather than postulate, a singular observation modulus.
Let
\[
 \Theta_d=\{\theta\in[0,1]^d:\theta_1\le\cdots\le\theta_d\},
 \qquad P_j(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^d\theta_i^j,\quad 1\le j\le d,
\]
and write $P=(P_1,\ldots,P_d)$. The data are the actually observed pair
\begin{equation}
 Y=P(\theta)+\eta+\varepsilon_Y,\qquad
 W=\eta+\varepsilon_W,\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_Y\\\varepsilon_W\end{pmatrix}
 \sim N(0,\sigma^2\Sigma_0),                                   \label{eq:v4-moment-pair}
\end{equation}
where $\eta\in\R^d$ is unrestricted, $\sigma\ge0$ is known, and
$\Sigma_0$ is a fixed positive-definite $2d$ by $2d$ matrix.
Let
\[
 V_0=(\Sigma_0)_{YY}+(\Sigma_0)_{WW}
              -(\Sigma_0)_{YW}-(\Sigma_0)_{WY}>0.
\]
An encoder of the pair retains one of $M$ labels. Its decoder estimates
$\theta$, with squared Euclidean loss. Public and private randomness
are permitted under the same hard label constraint as above. Denote
the minimax risk, taking a supremum over both $\theta$ and $\eta$,
by $\mathcal R_M(\Theta,\sigma)$ when the parameter set is $\Theta$.
Projection permits decoder values to lie in $\Theta_d$, so the loss
is bounded by $d$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-quotient}, proved in the retained development,
reduces this problem \emph{exactly for every $M$} to
\begin{equation}
 D=Y-W\sim N(P(\theta),\sigma^2V_0).                             \label{eq:v4-moment-difference}
\end{equation}
For $\sigma>0$ this is its correlated Gaussian nuisance quotient;
for $\sigma=0$ the difference is noiseless and the reverse comparison
follows by fixing the nuisance to any constant. The reduction is an
equality of minimax risks for decisions about $\theta$. It is not a
Blackwell equivalence for the full parameter $(\theta,\eta)$.

\begin{theorem}[Collision strata and label resolution]\label{thm:v4-roots}
For every fixed $d\ge1$ and positive-definite $\Sigma_0$, there are
$0<c\le C<\infty$, independent of $M$ and $\sigma$, such that
\begin{equation}
 c\left\{\min(1,\sigma^{2/d})+M^{-2/d}\right\}
 \le\mathcal R_M(\Theta_d,\sigma)
 \le C\left\{\min(1,\sigma^{2/d})+M^{-2/d}\right\}.               \label{eq:v4-global-root-rate}
\end{equation}
More generally, let $I_1,\ldots,I_s\subset[0,1]$ be ordered closed
intervals of positive lengths, with positive gaps between consecutive
intervals. Prescribe $r_j\ge1$ roots in $I_j$, with $\sum_jr_j=d$,
and let $\Theta(I,r)$ be the resulting ordered parameter set.
If $r_* =\max_jr_j$, then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_M(\Theta(I,r),\sigma)
 \asymp_{d,V_0,I,r}
       \min(1,\sigma^{2/r_*})+M^{-2/d}.                         \label{eq:v4-stratified-root-rate}
\end{equation}
The constants in the stratified assertion may deteriorate when an
interval width or a gap tends to zero. Neither assertion assumes a
two-sided global contact equivalence.
\end{theorem}

The model is genuinely coupled. The determinant of the derivative of
$P$ at distinct ordered roots is
\begin{equation}
 \det DP(\theta)=d!\prod_{i<j}(\theta_j-\theta_i).                \label{eq:v4-vandermonde-rank}
\end{equation}
Thus the rank changes on the collision strata. There is no invertible
smooth global coordinate change that makes this a uniformly regular
$\theta$-experiment. The next lemma supplies the inverse modulus
needed for the statistical theorem. Its root-perturbation argument
is classical in method; the point here is to carry it through the
actual calibrated observation and hard-label decision problem.

\begin{lemma}[Derived inverse moment moduli]\label{lem:v4-root-modulus}
There is a finite $C_d$ such that for all $\theta,\theta'\in\Theta_d$,
\begin{equation}
 \|\theta-\theta'\|\le C_d\|P(\theta)-P(\theta')\|^{1/d}.
                                                               \label{eq:v4-global-modulus}
\end{equation}
For $\theta,\theta'\in\Theta(I,r)$ and $\delta=\|P(\theta)-P(\theta')\|$
sufficiently small, the coordinates in cluster $j$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \|\theta^{(j)}-\theta'^{(j)}\|
 \le C_{d,I,r}\delta^{1/r_j}.                                  \label{eq:v4-local-modulus}
\end{equation}
Both statements include multiple roots and preserve their
multiplicities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $e_j(\theta)$ be the elementary symmetric polynomials, with
$e_0=1$. Newton's identities give
\[
 j e_j=\sum_{i=1}^j(-1)^{i-1}e_{j-i}P_i.
\]
On $[0,1]^d$, $|P_i|\le d$ and $|e_j|\le\binom dj$.
For an explicit finite Lipschitz bound define $L_0=0$ and
\[
 L_j=\frac1j\sum_{i=1}^j\left(dL_{j-i}+\binom d{j-i}\right),
 \quad 1\le j\le d.
\]
Induction gives $|e_j(\theta)-e_j(\theta')|\le L_j\delta$.
For the monic polynomials
$Q_\theta(z)=\prod_i(z-\theta_i)$ and $Q_{\theta'}$, it follows that
\begin{equation}
 |Q_\theta(z)-Q_{\theta'}(z)|\le A_d\delta\quad(|z|\le2),
 \qquad A_d=2^d\max_{1\le j\le d}L_j.                           \label{eq:v4-polynomial-perturbation}
\end{equation}
If $\delta=0$, Newton's identities identify the two multisets of roots,
and their ordered vectors coincide. Suppose $\delta>0$ is small and
put $\rho=(2A_d\delta)^{1/d}<1$. Take the union of the complex disks
of radius $\rho$ about the roots of $Q_\theta$. At its boundary every
factor has modulus at least $\rho$, so
$|Q_\theta|\ge\rho^d=2A_d\delta>|Q_\theta-Q_{\theta'}|$.
Rouch\'e's theorem on each connected component gives the same number
of zeros, counted with multiplicity, for the two polynomials.
One may increase $\rho$ arbitrarily slightly to avoid tangent
boundaries and then take a limit; the piecewise smooth boundary
version follows equivalently from the argument principle.
A component containing at most $d$ disks has diameter at most
$2d\rho$. Match the roots within each component. For real ordered
vectors, sorting a matching cannot increase its maximum displacement:
an inverted pair can be uncrossed with no larger maximum distance.
Thus $\|\theta-\theta'\|\le2d\sqrt d\rho$. For larger $\delta$,
compactness and the bound $\|\theta-\theta'\|\le\sqrt d$ extend the
inequality with a larger constant.

For the stratified assertion let $\Delta>0$ be the smallest gap;
when $s=1$ use the preceding argument with $r_1=d$.
Around roots in $I_j$ use disks of radius
\[
 \rho_j=\left[2A_d(2/\Delta)^{d-r_j}\delta\right]^{1/r_j}.
\]
For small $\delta$ these radii are less than $\min(1,\Delta/3)$.
At the boundary of their union the $r_j$ factors from this cluster
have modulus at least $\rho_j$, and all other factors have modulus
at least $\Delta/2$. Hence the product again exceeds
$A_d\delta$. Rouch\'e gives equal root counts in each component, and
its diameter is at most $2r_j\rho_j$. Matching and sorting within
the cluster proves \eqref{eq:v4-local-modulus}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Indistinguishable colliding configurations]\label{lem:v4-root-testing}
For every $r\ge1$ there exist two distinct real vectors
$a_1<\cdots<a_r$ and $b_1<\cdots<b_r$ in $(-1/4,1/4)$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i a_i^j=\sum_i b_i^j\quad(0\le j<r),\qquad
 \sum_i a_i^r\ne\sum_i b_i^r.                                  \label{eq:v4-moment-cancellation}
\end{equation}
If these vectors are scaled by $h$ and translated to an interior
point $x_0$ of a prescribed cluster, their root separation is a
positive constant times $h$, whereas the difference of all moments
of orders $1,\ldots,d$ is $O(h^r)$, for fixed $d\ge r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose any distinct $a_i$ in the indicated interval and let
$Q(z)=\prod_i(z-a_i)$. Around each $a_i$ choose a small closed
interval, with disjoint interiors and endpoints still in $(-1/4,1/4)$,
on which $Q$ has opposite nonzero signs at the two endpoints.
For any sufficiently small nonzero constant $c$, the polynomial
$Q+c$ retains these sign changes. It therefore has one real root in
each interval, and these are all its $r$ roots. Call them $b_i$.
The monic coefficients through degree one of $Q$ and $Q+c$ agree;
only the constant coefficient changes. Newton's identities imply
\eqref{eq:v4-moment-cancellation}, with the difference at order $r$
equal up to sign to $rc\ne0$.
For every $j\le d$, expand
$\sum_i(x_0+ha_i)^j-\sum_i(x_0+hb_i)^j$ by the binomial theorem.
All terms of degree less than $r$ in $h$ vanish. The remaining
terms are $O(h^r)$. The Euclidean separation of the two root vectors
is $h\|a-b\|>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-roots}]
Work first in the difference experiment
\eqref{eq:v4-moment-difference}. On either compact parameter set take
a measurable least-squares minimizer
\[
 \widetilde\theta\in\argmin_{\vartheta\in\Theta}
           \|D-P(\vartheta)\|.
\]
Existence follows from compactness and continuity; the measurable
minimum theorem gives a Borel selection. With
$D=P(\theta)+\sigma V_0^{1/2}G$, $G\sim N(0,I_d)$, minimality gives
\begin{equation}
 \|P(\widetilde\theta)-P(\theta)\|
 \le2\sigma\|V_0^{1/2}G\|.                                    \label{eq:v4-minimum-distance}
\end{equation}
The global modulus and Gaussian moments yield mean squared error
at most $C\sigma^{2/d}$, also bounded by $d$.
On $\Theta(I,r)$, \eqref{eq:v4-local-modulus}, extended by a bounded
constant outside its small-$\delta$ range, yields an upper bound
$C\sum_j\sigma^{2/r_j}\le C'\sigma^{2/r_*}$ for $0<\sigma\le1$.
For clarity, that extension is valid since
$\|\theta^{(j)}-\theta'^{(j)}\|^2\le r_j$ and
$\delta\ge\delta_0$ implies
$r_j\le r_j\delta_0^{-2/r_j}\delta^{2/r_j}$.
The cap by the diameter handles $\sigma>1$.

Let $N=\lfloor M^{1/d}\rfloor\ge M^{1/d}/2$. The $N^d$ midpoint
product grid in $[0,1]^d$ has squared covering radius at most
$d/(4N^2)$. Project its centres onto $\Theta$, which in both cases
is a closed convex subset. This does not increase their distances
to points of $\Theta$. Quantize $\widetilde\theta$ to the first
nearest projected centre and retain its index. The squared triangle
inequality adds at most $C_dM^{-2/d}$ and multiplies the statistical
bound by at most two. This proves the upper bounds with at most
$M$ labels, including the noiseless case.

For a statistical lower bound take a cluster of size $r_*$, or the
single cluster of size $d$ for the global assertion. Place the
configurations of Lemma~\ref{lem:v4-root-testing} at an interior
point of that cluster; fix all other roots in their intervals.
For sufficiently small $\sigma>0$ take $h=c_0\sigma^{1/r_*}$.
Their squared parameter distance is at least $c_1h^2$ and the
Kullback--Leibler divergence of the two difference laws is
\[
 \frac1{2\sigma^2}
 \|V_0^{-1/2}(P(\theta)-P(\theta'))\|^2
 \le C h^{2r_*}/\sigma^2.
\]
Choose $c_0>0$ so that this is at most $1/8$. Pinsker's inequality
and the two-point testing bound give risk at least
$c_2\sigma^{2/r_*}$ even without a label constraint. For example,
classifying an estimator by the closer of the two parameters
shows that its maximum squared risk is at least one eighth of their
squared distance times one minus their total variation distance.
For larger $\sigma$, the Gaussian experiment is a garbling of the
one at any smaller positive fixed noise level: add an independent
normal with covariance $(\sigma^2-\sigma_0^2)V_0$ before encoding.
Minimax risk is therefore nondecreasing in $\sigma$, giving the
cap $c\min(1,\sigma^{2/r_*})$.

It remains to prove a representation lower bound independent of
noise and calibration. The parameter set contains a $d$-dimensional
box of positive volume, obtained by placing all roots in disjoint
small subintervals inside their prescribed intervals. Put the uniform
prior on that box. Conditional on independent public randomness,
a decoder has at most $M$ possible deterministic centres; private
output randomness can be replaced by its mean for squared loss.
A union of $M$ Euclidean balls of radius
$c_3M^{-1/d}$ covers at most half the box, with $c_3>0$ depending
only on its volume and $d$. Pointwise, every distribution of an
encoded label has loss at least the squared distance to the nearest
centre. Integrate first over the box and then over the public
randomness. The Bayes, hence minimax, risk is at least
$c_4M^{-2/d}$. Combining the two lower bounds by
$\max(u,v)\ge(u+v)/2$ proves the result. Finally apply the exact
nuisance quotient to return to the observed pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Statistical meaning of the collision exponent]\label{rem:v4-collision-meaning}
The exponent $1/r_*$ is derived from moment cancellation and
multiplicity-sensitive root stability; it is not an assumed contact
order. The representation exponent remains $2/d$ because each
allowed stratum-domain contains an open set of $d$ distinct roots.
The paired experiment has equal known weights and noisy observations
of its first $d$ power sums. It is not the experiment of independent
samples from a finite mixture with unknown weights. Rates in the
latter experiment, such as those of Heinrich and Kahn
\cite{HeinrichKahn2015}, concern a different likelihood and different
local parameter sets. Nor is a polynomial root argument a substitute
for an unsmoothed billiard local limit theorem. The present theorem
verifies an actual changing-rank observation geometry within the
calibrated reduction, without importing either of those assertions.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Uniformity in the retained contact theorem]\label{rem:v4-contact-uniformity}
The constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-contact} depend on its displayed
metric-equivalence constants $\lambda,\Lambda$. Their independence
of contact orders, widths, or noise scales is conditional on uniform
control of $\lambda,\Lambda$ in those regimes. It is not a claim of
uniform physical conditioning. In the moment model the dependence
on dimension, covariance conditioning, cluster gaps, and interval
widths is made explicit at the level of the hypotheses in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-roots}; no uniform limit through a collapsing
cluster gap is inferred from the stratified statement.
\end{remark}
