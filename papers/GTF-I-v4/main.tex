\begin{abstract}
We relate the geometry of acquired information to its realization by a
finite-state observer. For arbitrary Borel dynamics with noisy renewal
observations, the quantization error of the weighted posterior future
orbit is a lower bound for every randomized, time-dependent causal
machine. Conversely, an $N$-centre orbit code truncated at depth $n$
is realized by $Nn+1$ states. Under stretched-exponential renewal tails
this preserves every finite positive quantization exponent. For noisy
expanding torus experiments in every dimension, a shift-closed suffix
code yields matching error bounds with all persistent states counted.
The resulting profile treats nongeometric renewal laws, separates the
Bayes noise floor from memory loss, and gives a uniform crossover at
the expansion--renewal threshold. We also derive global and stratified
minimax laws for calibrated Gaussian observations of colliding-root
moments, proving the singular modulus from the moment map rather than
assuming a contact geometry. The earlier checkpoint, filtering,
control, and measurable-experiment results are retained with their
proofs as complementary quantitative and foundational development.
\end{abstract}
