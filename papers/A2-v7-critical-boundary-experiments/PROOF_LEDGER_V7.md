## Asymptotic distinctions

The long-bridge comparison is a finite-law versus boundary-law binary experiment at a fixed table. Its critical profile uses growing j and growing sample size with an explicitly controlled tangent remainder. It is not a two-unknown-table rigidity experiment.

The fixed-jet inverse uses finitely many normalized nonlinear coefficients, supplied g and curvature, and identical-even contacts. Its uniform variable is j, not jet order M. No acquisition cost follows solely from its coefficient Lipschitz bound.

The amplitude acquisition uses J=3 in the constrained physical family, or J=4 for an independent normalizer. Its inverse matches unordered roots through coalescence. The selected-position experiment observes different data and estimates the two actual facing curvatures with its separate rate.

The lower bound concerns Bernoulli reports, not the complete lower collision counts. Its adaptive version requires the stated query collar on every history. The newly acquired fixed bracket does not satisfy that shrinking-collar restriction in its initial phase; only the upper-rate conclusion is extended to this larger procedure.
