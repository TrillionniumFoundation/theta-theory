\section{Round-twenty-three reconstruction: superquadratic compactness and Wasserstein-stable kinetic semigroups}
\label{sec:r23-b4}

The state topology in the preceding revision tested all quadratic-growth
functions while assuming only a bounded second moment.  A vanishing amount of
mass could then carry a nonvanishing unit of energy to infinity.  We replace
that noncompact shell by a superquadratic moment shell and use the
$2$-Wasserstein topology, which is strong enough both to retain energy and to
pass to the quadratic product collision measure.

\subsection{The kinetic state and action topology}

Fix $p=2+\delta>2$.  On $\mathbb T^3\times\mathbb R^3$ let
\[
 \mathcal X_{M,p}=\left\{f\in\mathcal P:
 \int |v|^p f(dx,dv)\le M,
 \quad H(f\mid dx\,M_0(v)dv)\le M,
 \quad C(f)=c_0\right\},                                 \tag{B4.1}
\]
where $C$ records mass, momentum, and energy and $M_0$ is a fixed Maxwellian.
The metric is $W_2$ together with the entropy sublevel topology.  Since the
spatial torus is compact and the $p$th moment is uniformly bounded, the second
moments are uniformly integrable.  Prokhorov compactness plus convergence of
second moments therefore gives $W_2$ compactness of (B4.1).

A controlled path satisfies
\[
 \partial_tf+v\cdot\nabla_xf=\mathcal C(qA_f),
 \qquad q\ge0,                                           \tag{B4.2}
\]
with action
\[
 \mathcal I_{[0,T]}(f,q)
 =\int_0^T\int A_f\ell(q),
 \qquad \ell(q)=q\log q-q+1.                             \tag{B4.3}
\]
The path topology is $C([0,T];W_2)$ after embedding the transport part in a
negative Sobolev space and retaining the uniform $p$th moment.

\begin{lemma}[Compact action sublevels]
\label{lem:r23-b4-compact}
For every compact $K\subset\mathcal X_{M,p}$ and $L<\infty$, the set of
solutions of (B4.2) with $f_0\in K$ and
$\mathcal I_{[0,T]}(f,q)\le L$ is relatively compact in
$C([0,T];W_2)$ after the standard transport interpolation, and every limit
solves (B4.2).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The entropy inequality with the test
$1+|v|^p+|v_*|^p$ propagates a uniform $p$th moment on $[0,T]$ for the prepared
source ball.  For a smooth compactly supported test $\phi$, the balance
equation and the inequality
$ab\le A_f\ell(q)+A_f(e^a-1)$ give
\[
 |\langle f_t-f_s,\phi\rangle|
 \le C_{L,M,\phi}|t-s|^{1/2}.                            \tag{B4.4}
\]
A countable dense family gives equicontinuity in a negative Sobolev metric.
Uniform $p$th moments make the second moment uniformly integrable, so narrow
compactness upgrades to $W_2$ compactness at each time and then uniformly in
time.  For the nonlinear term, $W_2$ convergence implies
$f_n\otimes f_n\Rightarrow f\otimes f$ and gives uniform integrability of the
linear-growth factor $|(v-v_*)\cdot\omega|$.  Truncate velocities, pass to the
limit for the bounded continuous kernel, and remove the truncation using the
$p$th moment.  Hence $A_{f_n}\Rightarrow A_f$.  Lower semicontinuity of
relative entropy identifies the limiting control and closes (B4.2).
\end{proof}

The referee's sequence
$f_n=(1-n^{-2})\varphi+n^{-2}\varphi(\cdot-ne_1)$ has
$\int|v|^{2+\delta}f_n\sim n^\delta$ and therefore does not lie in any fixed
shell (B4.1).  This is precisely the uniform-integrability information absent
from the old state.

\subsection{Strong control transfer in the metric actually assumed}

For cutoff hard-sphere collision kernels and paths in (B4.1), synchronous
coupling of the nonlinear jump representation gives
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}W_2(f_t,g_t)
 \le C_T W_2(f_0,g_0)                                    \tag{B4.5}
\]
when the same bounded relative multiplier $q$ is used.  For unbounded
finite-action controls, truncate $q$, apply (B4.5), and remove the truncation
by (B4.3).  Moreover,
\[
 |\mathcal I(f,q)-\mathcal I(g,q)|
 \le\omega_{M,L}(W_2(f_0,g_0)),                           \tag{B4.6}
\]
for a modulus $\omega_{M,L}(r)\downarrow0$.  The proof couples free transport,
uses Lipschitz dependence of the cutoff collision map in $W_2$, and estimates
the tail by the $p$th moment.  No weak countable-test metric is converted into
a weighted $L^1$ estimate.

\begin{proposition}[Product collision continuity]
\label{prop:r23-b4-product}
If $f_n\to f$ in $W_2$ and
$\sup_n\int|v|^p f_n<\infty$, then for every continuous contact test with at
most linear velocity growth,
\[
 \int\psi\,dA_{f_n}\longrightarrow\int\psi\,dA_f.       \tag{B4.7}
\]
If, in addition, $q_n\to q$ in the entropy topology relative to $A_{f_n}$,
then $q_nA_{f_n}\Rightarrow qA_f$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Product weak convergence follows from weak convergence of probability
measures.  On $|v|+|v_*|\le R$, the contact kernel is bounded and continuous
away from a null set in $\omega$, so (B4.7) follows by approximation.  The
complement is bounded by
$C R^{-(p-1)}\int(1+|v|^p+|v_*|^p)f_n f_{n,*}$, uniformly in $n$.
Let $R\to\infty$.  The final assertion is the variational characterization of
relative entropy plus uniform integrability.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Nisio semigroup and implicit nonlinear resolvent}

Let $\mathrm{BUC}_{M,p}$ be the bounded uniformly $W_2$-continuous functions
on $\mathcal X_{M,p}$.  Define
\[
 (V_t\phi)(f)=\sup_{(f_s,q_s):f_0=f}
 \left\{\phi(f_t)-\mathcal I_{[0,t]}(f,q)\right\}.        \tag{B4.8}
\]
Concatenation of controlled solutions and additivity of (B4.3) give
$V_{t+s}=V_tV_s$.  Compactness from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r23-b4-compact} gives existence of optimizers after relaxed
control closure, while (B4.5)--(B4.6) show that $V_t$ preserves
$\mathrm{BUC}_{M,p}$ and is strongly continuous there.

For a running reward $h\in\mathrm{BUC}_{M,p}$ define the discounted resolvent
\[
 (R_\lambda h)(f)=\sup_{(f_t,q_t):f_0=f}
 \int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}
 \left(h(f_t)-\int A_{f_t}\ell(q_t)\right)dt.            \tag{B4.9}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Implicit nonlinear resolvent identity]
\label{thm:r23-b4-resolvent}
For $\lambda,\mu>0$,
\[
 R_\lambda h
 =R_\mu\bigl(h+(\mu-\lambda)R_\lambda h\bigr).          \tag{B4.10}
\]
The maps are monotone, translation covariant, and
$\|\lambda R_\lambda h-\lambda R_\lambda g\|_\infty
\le\|h-g\|_\infty$.  Consequently the graph
\[
 \mathcal A=\{(R_\lambda h,\lambda R_\lambda h-h):h\in
 \mathrm{BUC}_{M,p}\}                                   \tag{B4.11}
\]
is independent of $\lambda$ and is maximal dissipative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Split the payoff (B4.9) at a bounded stopping time $s$, condition on the state
$f_s$, and use the memoryless discount factor.  Apply this dynamic programming
identity first with rate $\lambda$, then integrate the identity
$e^{-\lambda t}=e^{-\mu t}+(\mu-\lambda)
\int_0^te^{-\mu r}e^{-\lambda(t-r)}dr$.  Optimizing the inner continuation
payoff gives exactly (B4.10).  No composition product
$R_\lambda R_\mu$ is formed.  Monotonicity and the contraction estimate follow
by comparing the same controls.  The range of
$\lambda-\mathcal A$ is all of $\mathrm{BUC}_{M,p}$ by construction; the
nonlinear Lumer--Phillips theorem yields maximal dissipativity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hamiltonian, core, and comparison}

For a cylindrical test
$\phi(f)=F(\langle f,\varphi_1\rangle,\ldots,
\langle f,\varphi_m\rangle)$ with smooth subquadratic $\varphi_j$, the formal
Hamiltonian obtained from (B4.2)--(B4.3) is
\[
 \mathcal H\phi(f)=
 \langle f,v\cdot\nabla_xD\phi(f)\rangle
 +\int A_f\left(e^{\Delta D\phi(f)}-1\right).             \tag{B4.12}
\]
The optimizer is $q=e^{\Delta D\phi}$.  Let $\mathcal D$ be the algebra of
such cylinders after multiplication by cutoffs in
$\Upsilon(f)=1+\int|v|^p f$.  It separates points and is stable under the
resolvent regularization.

A doubling-of-variables proof in $W_2$, with containment function $\Upsilon$,
gives comparison for
$\partial_tu=\mathcal Hu$ on each moment shell.  The collision term is
continuous by Proposition~\ref{prop:r23-b4-product}; the exponential source
is bounded on the core.  Penalization by
$\alpha W_2(f,g)^2+\epsilon(\Upsilon(f)+\Upsilon(g))$
keeps maximizing pairs compact, and the synchronous coupling estimate makes
the transport/collision modulus vanish as $\alpha\to\infty$.  Thus the
viscosity equation has a unique bounded uniformly continuous solution, equal
to $V_t\phi$.

\subsection{Microscopic graph convergence}

Let $V_t^\varepsilon$ be the exact finite-volume exponential semigroup acting
on symmetric observables.  B2 supplies the full collision-history hierarchy
and its cutting estimate.  For every $\phi\in\mathcal D$, solve the finite
backward specular terminal problem on histories of size at most $K$ and denote
the resulting microscopic corrector by $\phi_{\varepsilon,K}$.  It belongs to
the exact BBGKY generator domain and satisfies
\[
 \lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
 \sup_{f\in\mathcal X_{M,p}}
 |\mathcal H_\varepsilon\phi_{\varepsilon,K}(f)
   -\mathcal H\phi(f)|=0.                                \tag{B4.13}
\]
For fixed $K$, only finitely many loop minors occur; B2's surplus gain controls
them.  Choose a diagonal $K(\varepsilon)\uparrow\infty$ after these finite
bounds, rather than assuming one uniform Łojasiewicz exponent over all
histories.

\begin{theorem}[Nonlinear semigroup limit]
\label{thm:r23-b4-limit}
For every $T< T_*$ and $\phi\in\mathrm{BUC}_{M,p}$,
\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}\sup_{f\in\mathcal X_{M,p}}
 |V_t^\varepsilon\phi(f)-V_t\phi(f)|\longrightarrow0     \tag{B4.14}
\]
after the exact-number preparation of B1--B2.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use (B4.13) on the core to prove the viscosity sub- and supersolution
inequalities for the upper and lower half-relaxed limits.  The containment
function yields compactness despite the non-locally-compact ambient measure
space.  The comparison theorem identifies the two limits with $V_t\phi$.
Density of the resolvent-regularized core and contraction of both semigroups
extend the result to every $\phi\in\mathrm{BUC}_{M,p}$, uniformly on compact
time intervals.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Round-Twenty-Two regression}

The zero-cost escaping-energy sequence is excluded by the propagated
$(2+\delta)$ moment and hence cannot violate
Lemma~\ref{lem:r23-b4-compact}.  Stability is proved directly in $W_2$, the
metric assumed by the theorem, rather than initialized from a weaker topology
and upgraded to weighted $L^1$.  Product collision measures converge by
Proposition~\ref{prop:r23-b4-product}.  Finally, graph convergence is paired
with an explicit core, containment function, and comparison proof, so maximal
dissipativity is not used as a substitute for the viscosity argument.
