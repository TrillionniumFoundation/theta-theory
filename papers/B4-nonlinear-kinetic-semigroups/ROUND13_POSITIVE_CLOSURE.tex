\section{Round-thirteen positive closure: observable cores and the Nisio kinetic semigroup}
\label{sec:r13-b4}

The exact microscopic tower lives on observables of the complete microscopic
state.  The limiting kinetic tower is constructed from the dynamic B2 action.
Uniqueness is proved through the nonlinear resolvent and the Nisio semigroup,
with one fixed Hamilton--Jacobi sign convention; no incorrect plus-cost
verification inequality is used.

\subsection{Five distinct finite-volume objects}

Let $\Phi_t^\varepsilon$ be the deterministic hard-sphere flow.  We distinguish
\[
 x\in\Omega^\varepsilon,
 \quad U_t^\varepsilon F=F\circ\Phi_t^\varepsilon,
 \quad (\Phi_t^\varepsilon)_\#\nu,
 \quad {1\over\mu_\varepsilon}
       \log\int e^{\mu_\varepsilon F(\Phi_t^\varepsilon x)}d\nu(x),
 \quad S_{s,t}\Phi.
\]
The first three are respectively state, Koopman observable, and law
push-forward; the fourth is one endpoint value and the fifth is the limiting
action semigroup.

\begin{proposition}[Exact microscopic observable tower]
\label{prop:r13-b4-tower}
On the complete microscopic observable space,
$U_{r,t}^\varepsilon=U_{r,s}^\varepsilon U_{s,t}^\varepsilon$.  For one fixed
initial law the endpoint log-Laplace value is obtained by applying the
composed observable once and integrating once.  No exact finite-volume
Markov closure on the one-particle density is asserted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is composition of the deterministic flow.  The inner observable after
time $s$ is an arbitrary function of the complete microscopic state, so the
outer operation is the Koopman operator, not a density-state value map.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The reflected observable core}

Let $\mathcal X_\varepsilon$ be the uniform weighted closure of finite
smooth cylinders on the graph-completed phase space whose incoming and
outgoing traces satisfy the specular matching relation.  Let
$A_\varepsilon$ be the Koopman generator on this space.

\begin{theorem}[Observable resolvent graph core]
\label{thm:r13-b4-core}
The smooth specular cylinder algebra $\mathcal D_0^\varepsilon$ is dense in
$\mathcal X_\varepsilon$.  For every $\lambda>0$,
\[
 \mathcal D_\lambda^\varepsilon
 =R_\lambda^\varepsilon\mathcal D_0^\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 R_\lambda^\varepsilon=(\lambda-A_\varepsilon)^{-1},
\]
is a graph core for $A_\varepsilon$.  The assertion includes grazing and
multiple-collision germs through the graph completion; it is not deduced from
codimension alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The graph completion separates every incoming/outgoing collision germ and
makes the deterministic flow continuous in the weighted trajectory metric.
Finite trajectory cylinders separate points and constants and are stable
under the specular involution.  Weighted Stone--Weierstrass gives density in
$\mathcal X_\varepsilon$.  The resolvent maps the whole Banach space onto
$D(A_\varepsilon)$ and
$\|u\|+\|A_\varepsilon u\|$ is equivalent to
$\|(\lambda-A_\varepsilon)u\|$.  Density of
$\mathcal D_0^\varepsilon$ therefore makes
$R_\lambda^\varepsilon\mathcal D_0^\varepsilon$ graph dense.  Exceptional
germs are approximated in the trajectory metric itself, so no unproved
capacity-zero claim is used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The full hierarchy corrector}

Let $G=(g_j)_{j\ge0}$ be the factorial-correlation hierarchy.  Use the
exponential generating norm
\[
 \|G\|_{\alpha,\beta}
 =\sum_{j\ge0}{e^{\alpha j}\over j!}
   \|g_j\|_{\beta,\rm tr}.
\]
For a density/contact cylinder $F$ let
$F_\varepsilon^{(J)}$ be the triangular corrector through label order $J$.

\begin{theorem}[Normally summable graph-domain corrector]
\label{thm:r13-b4-corrector}
For $T<T_*$ there is a loss $\alpha-\alpha'>0$ such that
$F_\varepsilon^{(J)}$ converges in the graph norm of the full BBGKY/contact
ledger generator to a corrected observable $F_\varepsilon$.  Uniformly on
hierarchy moment balls,
\[
 {1\over\mu_\varepsilon}
 e^{-\mu_\varepsilon F_\varepsilon}
 A_\varepsilon e^{\mu_\varepsilon F_\varepsilon}
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb H\left(f,{\delta F\over\delta f},\psi\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At order $j$, solve the finite triangular Duhamel equation in the resolvent
core from Theorem~\ref{thm:r13-b4-core}.  B2 bounds the connected defect by
$j!C^jT^{j-1}$.  The explicit factor $1/j!$ in the sequence norm cancels this
factorial, while the label-radius loss makes
$\sum_j(Ce^{-(\alpha-\alpha')})^j$ convergent.  The same bound applies to the
generator images and time derivatives, so closedness of the full generator
passes the sum through $A_\varepsilon$.  Creation trees give the exponential
collision Hamiltonian; B2's globally summed cyclic sector vanishes.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The dynamic action and Nisio semigroup}

Let $I_{s,t}^{\rm dyn}$ be the B2 collision entropy on balanced paths, with no
initial preparation term.  For bounded uniformly continuous terminal data,
put
\[
 (S_{s,t}\Phi)(f)
 =\sup_{(\rho,\Gamma):\rho_s=f}
 \{\Phi(\rho_t)-I_{s,t}^{\rm dyn}(\rho,\Gamma)\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Good action and exact Nisio tower]
\label{thm:r13-b4-action}
The action has compact sublevels, is lower semicontinuous, and is additive
under concatenation.  Therefore
\[
 S_{r,t}=S_{r,s}S_{s,t},
\]

after the usual relaxed closure.  Each $S_{s,t}$ is monotone, cash additive,
and a contraction in the supremum norm.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
B2 gives velocity moment tightness, contact entropy tightness, temporal
moduli through the exact balance, and closure of endpoints in the weighted
trace topology.  These yield compact sublevels and attainment after
relaxation.  Restriction of a balanced path to subintervals and concatenation
preserve the balance and add the integral entropy.  The two inequalities of
dynamic programming follow by splitting and concatenating near optimizers.
Monotonicity, cash additivity, and contraction are immediate from the
variational formula.
\end{proof}

Let
\[
 H(f,p)=\sup_{b}\{\langle p,b\rangle-L(f,b)\}
\]

after identifying $b$ with a balanced density/contact velocity.  We use the
backward convention
\[
 \partial_sU+H(f,DU)=0,
 \qquad U(T)=\Phi.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Nonlinear resolvent comparison]
\label{thm:r13-b4-comparison}
The generator of the Nisio semigroup is the closure on the cylinder core of
$F\mapsto H(f,DF)$.  Its nonlinear resolvent satisfies the contraction and
range conditions of an $m$-dissipative operator.  Consequently the backward
problem has one bounded uniformly continuous mild solution,
$U(s)=S_{s,T}\Phi$, and every metric-viscosity solution in the same class
equals it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $\lambda>0$ define the variational resolvent
\[
 (J_\lambda G)(f)=
 \sup_{(\rho,\Gamma)}
 \left\{G(\rho_{\lambda})-
 I_{0,\lambda}^{\rm dyn}(\rho,\Gamma)
 -{1\over\lambda}
  d(f,\rho_0)^2\right\}.
\]
Compact action sublevels give existence; concatenation gives the resolvent
identity and the sup-norm contraction.  Expanding on a smooth cylinder and
using Fenchel duality identifies the generator with $H$.  The range of
$I-\lambda\overline H$ is all bounded uniformly continuous data by the
resolvent construction, hence $\overline H$ is $m$-dissipative and its
Crandall--Liggett evolution is $S$.

For a viscosity upper or lower solution, use the resolvent test
$J_\lambda G$ rather than a doubled exponential jet.  The resolvent inequality
has the correct sign by construction and yields respectively domination by,
or domination of, one time step of $S$.  Iteration and
$\lambda\downarrow0$ give equality.  Equivalently, for a smooth classical
solution the correct path inequality is
$U(s,\rho_s)\ge U(t,\rho_t)-I_{s,t}^{\rm dyn}$; calibrated near-maximizers give
the reverse envelope.  No plus-cost subsolution inequality is used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Microscopic and Gaussian limits}

\begin{theorem}[Microscopic nonlinear-semigroup convergence]
\label{thm:r13-b4-limit}
For every compact regular initial family and bounded uniformly continuous
terminal cylinder, the finite-volume endpoint log-Laplace values converge
locally uniformly to $S_{s,t}\Phi$.  The primitive microcanonical value is
obtained by applying the B1 initial contraction once at $s=0$, with its
multiplier retained as a static lifted coordinate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The corrected cylinders in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r13-b4-corrector} give upper and lower nonlinear-generator
limits on a separating graph core.  B2 compact containment prevents escape.
Half-relaxed limits solve the same backward equation, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:r13-b4-comparison} identifies both with $S$.  B1's
source-dependent shell formula is an initial boundary operation and is not
repeated on transition intervals.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Prepared Gaussian risk-sensitive tangent]
\label{thm:r13-b4-gaussian}
Under the central-limit scaling, $S_{s,t}$ converges on smooth finite
projections to the exponential semigroup generated by
\[
 b\cdot DF+{1\over2}A:D^2F
 +{\theta\over2}DF^TADF,
\]
where $A$ is the full B3 density--contact covariance, including pure contact
and cross terms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
B3 gives joint process convergence and a uniform complex moment neighborhood
on each finite projection.  Taylor expansion of the exact log-Laplace
semigroup leaves the first two cumulants; higher cumulants vanish under
central scaling.  The exponential transform of the limiting Gaussian
generator gives the quadratic gradient term.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-thirteen B4 closure]
\label{thm:r13-b4-main}
The deterministic hard-sphere system has a correctly typed observable core,
a normally summable hierarchy corrector, a good dynamic Nisio semigroup with
unique Hamilton--Jacobi evolution, and the prepared Gaussian risk-sensitive
tangent.  The comparison proof uses the nonlinear resolvent and a consistent
backward sign.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r13-b4-core},
\ref{thm:r13-b4-corrector}, \ref{thm:r13-b4-action},
\ref{thm:r13-b4-comparison}, \ref{thm:r13-b4-limit}, and
\ref{thm:r13-b4-gaussian}.
\end{proof}
