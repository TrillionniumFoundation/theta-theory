\section{Round-thirty-one reconstruction: ballistic energy compactification and shellwise nonlinear semigroups}
\label{sec:r31-b4}

Relative entropy with respect to a Gaussian and a bounded second moment do not
make second moments uniformly integrable.  The kinetic state is therefore
compactified by retaining energy that escapes to infinite velocity.  At the
velocity boundary the spatial phase is collapsed, which is forced by ballistic
motion and removes the high-speed counterexample to small-time continuity.
The original entropy running action is unchanged.  Ordinary $W_2$ statements
are recovered on the zero-recession face under a genuine superquadratic
uniform-integrability condition.

\subsection{Joint rate and preparation--dynamics factorization}

For an exactly balanced path $\Gamma=qA_f$ starting from $x$, define
\[
 J_T^x(f,\Gamma)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\int_0^T\!\int\ell(q)\dd A_f,&f_0=x,\\
 +\infty,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \ell(q)=q\log q-q+1.                              \tag{B4.1}
\]
The joint microscopic rate is
\[
 I_T^{\rm joint}(f,\Gamma)
 =H(f_0\mid f_0^{\rm ref})+J_T^{f_0}(f,\Gamma),            \tag{B4.2}
\]
and the density rate is the contraction
\[
 I_T^{\rm dens}(f)=\inf_\Gamma I_T^{\rm joint}(f,\Gamma). \tag{B4.3}
\]
A nonzero B3 contact cycle can change the joint cost without changing the
density path, so (B4.3) is not replaced by the cost of an arbitrary chosen
contact control.

\subsection{Ballistic energy compactification}

Let
\[
 \jmath(v)={v\over1+|v|}\in B_1,
\]
and let $\overline B_1$ be the closed unit ball.  Form the compact quotient
\[
 \widehat E_v=
 (\mathbb T^3_x\times\overline B_1)/\sim,                 \tag{B4.4}
\]
where $(x,\theta)\sim(x',\theta)$ whenever $|\theta|=1$.
Thus spatial position is forgotten only at infinite velocity, while velocity
direction is retained.

For a probability $f$ with finite kinetic energy define its mass image
\[
 m_f=(x,\jmath(v))_\#f                                   \tag{B4.5}
\]
on $\mathbb T^3\times\overline B_1$ and its energy image
\[
 e_f=[x,\jmath(v)]_\#(|v|^2f)                             \tag{B4.6}
\]
on $\widehat E_v$.  A compactified kinetic state is
\[
 \mathbf f=(m,e,P),                                       \tag{B4.7}
\]
where $m$ is a probability, $e$ is a finite positive measure,
$P=\int v f$ when represented by finite velocities, and the compatibility
relations hold on every compact velocity ball.  Boundary mass of $e$ records
energy recession.

\begin{definition}[Ballistic energy topology]
\label{def:r31-b4-topology}
A sequence $f_n$ converges to $\mathbf f=(m,e,P)$ when $m_{f_n}\Rightarrow m$,
$e_{f_n}\Rightarrow e$ on $\widehat E_v$, and momenta converge.  Equivalently,
we test mass by bounded continuous functions of $(x,\jmath(v))$ and energy by
continuous functions on $\widehat E_v$, whose boundary values are independent
of $x$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Compact energy shells]
\label{prop:r31-b4-compact-shell}
For every $E_0<\infty$, the set
\[
 \widehat\cX_{E_0}=\{\mathbf f:e(\widehat E_v)\le E_0,
                      \ m(\mathbb T^3\times\overline B_1)=1\}            \tag{B4.8}
\]
is compact in the ballistic energy topology.  Its zero-recession face
$e(\{|\theta|=1\})=0$ consists of ordinary finite-energy measures.  A family
in that face is relatively compact in $W_2$ exactly when its quadratic energy
is uniformly integrable; a sufficient condition is a common bound on
$\int\Psi(|v|^2)\dd f$ for some superlinear $\Psi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both ambient spaces in (B4.5)--(B4.6) are compact, so Prokhorov compactness and
the total-energy bound give subsequential limits.  The local compatibility
relations are closed after truncating $|v|$, and momentum is retained as a
separate bounded coordinate on a fixed energy shell.  Boundary energy is
exactly the defect in quadratic uniform integrability.  The final statement
is the standard characterization of $W_2$ compactness by weak compactness and
uniform integrability of $|v|^2$.  No entropy--energy implication is asserted.
\end{proof}

The Gaussian preparation gives, for $0<\lambda<a$,
\[
 \sup_\varepsilon{1\over\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log E\exp\{\lambda\mu_\varepsilon E_0^\varepsilon\}<\infty,           \tag{B4.9}
\]
and elastic microscopic dynamics satisfies
$E_t^\varepsilon=E_0^\varepsilon$ pathwise.  Hence energy-shell exponential
containment follows directly from the initial law.

\subsection{Path compactness in the supported topology}

For a mass test $\phi$ that is Lipschitz in $x$ and $\jmath(v)$,
free transport on $[s,t]$ changes its value by at most
$C|t-s|\int(1+|v|)\dd f$.  On an energy shell this is
$C_{E_0}|t-s|$.  For an energy test $\psi$ on $\widehat E_v$, its boundary
value is independent of $x$, so the high-velocity spatial displacement is
invisible; uniform continuity on the compact quotient controls the remaining
part.  Collision increments are integrated against $qA_f$ and split at
$q\le R$ and $q>R$, with the latter controlled by the running entropy.

\begin{theorem}[Ballistic energy path compactness]
\label{thm:r31-b4-path-compact}
For every $E_0,L,T$, the set of exactly balanced joint paths satisfying
\[
 E(f_0)\le E_0,\qquad J_T^{f_0}(f,\Gamma)\le L             \tag{B4.10}
\]
is relatively compact in
$C([0,T];\widehat\cX_{E_0})$ and in the weak contact-current topology.
Every limit retains total energy, momentum, mass, and its boundary recession
measure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The preceding free-flight estimates give equicontinuity for a countable dense
family of mass and energy tests.  For collisions, on $q\le R$ use the energy
bound to control the hard-sphere factor $|v-v_*|^2$; on $q>R$ use
$q1_{q>R}\le C\ell(q)/\log R$ and then let $R\to\infty$.  Tests on the energy
compactification have no spatial oscillation at $|v|=\infty$, so the estimate
is uniform in high velocities.  Proposition~\ref{prop:r31-b4-compact-shell}
and Arzela--Ascoli give path compactness.  The same truncation controls contact
mass on compact velocity sets, while the energy coordinate records the tail.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Running-action Nisio semigroup}

For $\phi\in\operatorname{BUC}(\widehat\cX_{E_0})$ define
\[
 (V_t^{E_0}\phi)(x)=
 \sup_{(f,\Gamma):f_0=x,\ E(f_0)\le E_0}
 \{\phi(\mathbf f_t)-J_t^x(f,\Gamma)\}.                  \tag{B4.11}
\]
The one-shot prepared value remains separate:
\[
 \cP_t\phi=
 \sup_x\{V_t^{E(x)}\phi(x)-H(x\mid f_0^{\rm ref})\}.     \tag{B4.12}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Shellwise strongly continuous semigroup]
\label{thm:r31-b4-semigroup}
For every fixed $E_0$, $(V_t^{E_0})_{t\ge0}$ is a max-plus contraction
semigroup on $\operatorname{BUC}(\widehat\cX_{E_0})$:
\[
 V_0^{E_0}=I,\qquad V_{t+s}^{E_0}=V_t^{E_0}V_s^{E_0},      \tag{B4.13}
\]
and
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\norm{V_t^{E_0}\phi-\phi}_\infty=0. \tag{B4.14}
\]
No global sup-norm strong continuity is asserted over the union of all
unbounded energy shells.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Running actions concatenate and carry no initial entropy, proving (B4.13).
For zero-cost free motion, a bounded-Lipschitz mass test changes by at most
$t\sqrt{E_0}$ in $W_1$ duality.  Energy tests are uniformly continuous on the
compact quotient and are spatially constant at infinity; split at a velocity
threshold and then let $t\downarrow0$.  Controlled collisions with action at
most one have the same uniform modulus by
Theorem~\ref{thm:r31-b4-path-compact}; controls with larger action cannot
improve a bounded payoff.  Approximation of a uniformly continuous $\phi$ by
finite test cylinders gives (B4.14).  The monokinetic velocity $1/(2t)$
counterexample is excluded uniformly only after fixing $E_0$, exactly as the
theorem states.
\end{proof}

On smooth cylinders the Hamiltonian remains
\[
 \cH\phi(f)=\ip{f}{v\cdot\nabla_xD\phi(f)}
 +\int A_f\left(e^{\Delta D\phi(f)}-1\right).             \tag{B4.15}
\]
No recession penalty is added to the rate or Hamiltonian; the compactification
only records limits already permitted by the microscopic energy law.

\subsection{Varying-reference closure and comparison}

A sequence $(f_n,\Gamma_n)$ converges in collision-entropy topology when its
compactified density paths converge, $A_{f_n}\Rightarrow A_f$ locally in
velocity, $\Gamma_n\Rightarrow\Gamma$, and
\[
 \int\phi\dd\Gamma_n-
 \int(e^\phi-1)\dd A_{f_n}
 \longrightarrow
 \int\phi\dd\Gamma-
 \int(e^\phi-1)\dd A_f                                  \tag{B4.16}
\]
for every compactly supported collision test.

\begin{proposition}[Joint-action closure]
\label{prop:r31-b4-closure}
On a shell (B4.10), every limit has
$\Gamma=qA_f$ on finite velocities and
\[
 \int\ell(q)\dd A_f\le
 \liminf_n\int\ell(q_n)\dd A_{f_n}.                       \tag{B4.17}
\]
Any collision energy escaping to infinity is represented by the boundary
energy measure and is subject to the exact global energy conservation law.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Truncate velocity.  On the truncated set, convergence of the mass coordinate
gives product convergence and the collision kernel is uniformly integrable.
The variational representation of perspective entropy and (B4.16) give the
liminf.  Increasing the truncation identifies the finite-velocity current;
Proposition~\ref{prop:r31-b4-compact-shell} records the remaining energy.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Comparison on compact ballistic shells]
\label{thm:r31-b4-comparison}
On $[0,T]\times\widehat\cX_{E_0}$, the Hamilton--Jacobi equation
$\partial_tu=\cHu$ has at most one bounded uniformly continuous viscosity
solution on the cylindrical test core, and that solution is
$V_t^{E_0}\phi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use a doubled-variable penalty generated by a finite determining family of
mass and energy tests on the compact space
$\widehat\cX_{E_0}$.  Maximizing pairs therefore exist.  Truncate velocities
in the collision Hamiltonian; the finite part is locally Lipschitz, while the
energy-boundary part is controlled by tests whose boundary values are
spatially constant and by the contact entropy tail.  Send the doubling
parameter to infinity, then remove the truncation.  Dynamic programming gives
existence.  The proof no longer invokes a noncompact entropy--energy subset of
$W_2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Zero-recession and microscopic realization}

If a shell also satisfies
\[
 \sup_f\int\Psi(|v|^2)\dd f<\infty                       \tag{B4.18}
\]
for a superlinear $\Psi$, then its boundary energy is zero and the compactified
topology agrees with $W_2$ on that shell.  This is an additional proved or
imposed uniform-integrability gate, not a consequence of entropy plus energy.

For a finite determining family $\Phi_n$, define
$\mathfrak R_{\varepsilon,N}^{(n)}(f)$ as the unique entropy projection of the
symmetric hard-core Maxwellian law subject to exact conservation and matching
expectations of $\Phi_n$.  Let $n(\varepsilon)\uparrow\infty$ slowly enough for
the B1 saddle estimates.

\begin{theorem}[Microscopic semigroup limit on ballistic shells]
\label{thm:r31-b4-limit}
For $T<T_*$, $E_0<\infty$, and
$\phi\in\operatorname{BUC}(\widehat\cX_{E_0})$,
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}\sup_{f\in\widehat\cX_{E_0}}
 \left|V_t^\varepsilon\phi(
       \mathfrak R_{\varepsilon,N}(f))-V_t^{E_0}\phi(f)\right|
 \longrightarrow0.                                      \tag{B4.19}
\]
On a zero-recession shell satisfying (B4.18), the same statement may be read
in $W_2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At a fixed B2 vertex cutoff, the loop theorem removes recollisions and the
tree terms converge to (B4.15); the Ovsyannikov factorial tail is uniform.
B1 controls the exact constraint multipliers in the entropy projection.
Theorem~\ref{thm:r31-b4-path-compact} supplies half-relaxed-limit compactness
and Theorem~\ref{thm:r31-b4-comparison} identifies both limits.  Contraction
and cylindrical-core density complete the proof.  Under (B4.18), quadratic
uniform integrability removes the boundary energy and upgrades convergence to
$W_2$.
\end{proof}

The kinetic theory thus preserves the original entropy action and nonlinear
Hamiltonian while using the compact topology actually supported by finite
energy.  Strong continuity and comparison are stated on fixed compact energy
shells, and ordinary $W_2$ is recovered exactly when recession energy is
absent.
