\section{Round-three positive closure: correlation-state generators and the kinetic semigroup}
\label{sec:r3-b4}

The finite deterministic gas is not replaced by a Markov process on smooth
one-particle densities.  Its exact ensemble state is the correlation
hierarchy.  Density cylinders are lifted by correlation correctors; after this
lift the nonlinear generators converge to the Hamilton--Boltzmann Hamiltonian.
The limiting semigroup is also constructed directly from the additive B2
action, which supplies dynamic programming and comparison.

\subsection{The exact correlation state}

Let
\[
 \mathfrak X_\alpha
 =\left\{G=(g_k)_{k\ge0}:
 \|G\|_\alpha=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{e^{\alpha k}}{k!}
 \int |g_k(Z_k)|e^{\beta|V_k|^2}dZ_k<\infty
 \right\}.
\]
Here \(g_k\) is the rescaled \(k\)-particle correlation density and
\(g_0=1\).  The BBGKY hierarchy defines a strongly continuous linear
semigroup \(\mathscr U_\varepsilon(t)\) from
\(\mathfrak X_\alpha\) to \(\mathfrak X_{\alpha'}\) for
\(0<\alpha'<\alpha\) on the short-time interval of B2.  This hierarchy is
equivalent to the Liouville evolution of the full grand-canonical law.

For a finite cylinder
\[
 F(f)=\varphi(\langle f,\phi_1\rangle,\ldots,
 \langle f,\phi_m\rangle),
 \qquad \varphi\in C_b^3,
\]
define its exponential correlation functional by expanding
\(e^{\mu_\varepsilon F(\pi)}\) into factorial moments.  Denote the resulting
exact value on an initial hierarchy \(G\) by
\[
 \mathscr S_\varepsilon(t)F(G)
 =\frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\mathbb E_G e^{\mu_\varepsilon F(\pi_t^\varepsilon)}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact hierarchy semigroup]
\label{prop:r3-b4-hierarchy}
The value \(\mathscr S_\varepsilon(t)F\) is determined exactly by
\(\mathscr U_\varepsilon(t)G\).  On complete correlation states,
\[
 \mathscr S_\varepsilon(t+s)
 =\mathscr S_\varepsilon(t)\circ\mathscr S_\varepsilon(s)
\]
with the usual exponential normalization.  No closure on \(g_1\) alone is
used in this identity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Factorial correlation functions determine every symmetric expectation and
are equivalent to the Liouville density.  The BBGKY semigroup therefore
propagates the complete law.  Exponentiating the value and applying the linear
Liouville tower gives the semigroup identity.  Since all hierarchy components
are retained, no propagation-of-chaos statement is needed at finite
\(\varepsilon\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Correlation-corrected test functions}

Write the BBGKY generator as
\[
 \mathscr A_\varepsilon
 =\mathscr A_0+\mathscr C_\varepsilon,
\]
where \(\mathscr A_0\) is free transport and
\(\mathscr C_\varepsilon\) is the hard-sphere boundary operator.  A density
cylinder has a formal derivative \(p=\delta F/\delta f\).

\begin{lemma}[Recursive correlation correctors]
\label{lem:r3-b4-corrector}
For every cylinder \(F\) and every integer \(K\), there are symmetric
correctors \(\mathfrak c_j[F]\), \(2\le j\le K\), depending on at most
\(j\) trajectories, such that
\[
 F_\varepsilon^{(K)}(G)
 =F(g_1)+\sum_{j=2}^K\mu_\varepsilon^{1-j}
 \mathfrak c_j[F](g_1,\ldots,g_j)
\]
satisfies
\[
 \mathscr H_\varepsilon F_\varepsilon^{(K)}
 =\mathbb H\left(g_1,\frac{\delta F}{\delta f},0\right)
 +R_{K,\varepsilon}[F,G],
\]
where
\[
 \mathscr H_\varepsilon F
 =\frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 e^{-\mu_\varepsilon F}
 \mathscr A_\varepsilon e^{\mu_\varepsilon F}
\]
and, on hierarchy moment balls,
\[
 |R_{K,\varepsilon}[F,G]|
 \le C_F\left(\varepsilon^\alpha+
\rho^K
\right).
\]
With a collision work \(\psi\), the right-hand side is
\(\mathbb H(g_1,\delta F/\delta f,\psi)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand the exponential cylinder in factorial cumulants.  Acting with the
boundary operator on the one-particle term creates the binary collision
factor
\[
 e^{\Delta p+\psi}-1.
\]
The remaining terms are connected correlation defects.  Order them by the
number of labels in their minimal collision tree.  Define
\(\mathfrak c_j[F]\) as the negative time integral of the connected
\(j\)-label defect transported by the free hierarchy.  This cancels the defect
of order \(j\) and moves the remainder to order \(j+1\).

The real-trajectory cluster norm of B2 bounds the \(j\)-label connected term
by \(j!C^jT^{j-1}\); choose the hierarchy weight so that its normalized tail
is \(C\rho^K\).  Recollision terms are bounded by the first-cycle gain
\(C\varepsilon^\alpha\), uniformly after the finite number of derivatives
created by \(F\).  The same recursion with one marked contact inserts
\(\psi\) inside the collision exponential.  This proves the estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Upper and lower nonlinear-generator convergence]
\label{thm:r3-b4-generator}
Let \(F\) range over the separating cylinder core and let smooth hierarchy
states converge to a density \(f\) with a uniform moment bound.  There are
corrected tests \(F_\varepsilon\to F\) such that
\[
 \limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
 \mathscr H_\varepsilon F_\varepsilon
 \le\mathbb H\left(f,\frac{\delta F}{\delta f},\psi\right),
 \qquad
 \liminf_{\varepsilon\to0}
 \mathscr H_\varepsilon F_\varepsilon
 \ge\mathbb H\left(f,\frac{\delta F}{\delta f},\psi\right).
\]
The convergence is locally uniform on regular moment balls.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First let \(K\to\infty\) in
Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-b4-corrector}, then let \(\varepsilon\to0\).  The hierarchy
moment bound makes the corrector series Cauchy and
\(F_\varepsilon\to F(g_1)\).  Approximate an arbitrary regular hierarchy from
above and below by finite connected truncations; positivity of the factorial
moment representation preserves the two inequalities.  The error estimate is
uniform on moment balls, giving local uniformity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The weighted density state and containment}

Let
\[
 \mathcal E_M=\left\{f\ge0:
 \langle1+|v|^m,f\rangle\le M\right\}
\]
with the weighted weak metric
\[
 d_w(f,g)=\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}
 \left(1\wedge|\langle f-g,\phi_j\rangle|\right),
\]
where \((\phi_j)\) is dense in the unit ball of weighted Lipschitz tests.
Let \(\mathscr D\) be the cylinder core used above.  A viscosity sub- or
supersolution is tested only by members of \(\mathscr D\) plus the containment
function
\(\Upsilon(f)=\langle1+|v|^m,f\rangle\).

\begin{lemma}[Exponential compact containment]
\label{lem:r3-b4-containment}
For every compact regular initial family and every \(A>0\), there is \(M_A\)
such that the microscopic empirical paths leave \(\mathcal E_{M_A}\) with
probability at most \(e^{-A\mu_\varepsilon}\), uniformly in small
\(\varepsilon\).  The same bound holds under every source in a compact
complex ball.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The B2 marked cluster theorem is analytic after adding a small positive
multiple of \(1+|v|^m\) to the particle source.  Chernoff's inequality gives
the uniform moment bound.  Time equicontinuity on the countable test family
follows from the exact balance, the free-transport moment, and the exponential
collision-mass bound.  The weighted compactness criterion gives
\(\mathcal E_{M_A}\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The action semigroup and comparison}

For \(s<t\), let
\[
 \mathcal A_{s,t}(f,\Gamma)
 =\int_s^t\ell(d\Gamma/dA_f)dA_f
\]
on balanced pairs.  For bounded uniformly continuous terminal data \(\Phi\),
define
\[
 (S_{s,t}\Phi)(f_s)
 =\sup_{(f,\Gamma):f(s)=f_s}
 \left\{\Phi(f_t)-\mathcal A_{s,t}(f,\Gamma)\right\}.
\]
The initial rate is not included in this transition semigroup.

\begin{theorem}[Constructed kinetic Lax--Oleinik semigroup]
\label{thm:r3-b4-action}
On the regular short-time domain, \(S_{s,t}\) maps
\(\mathrm{BUC}(\mathcal E_M)\) to itself, is monotone and cash additive, and
satisfies
\[
 S_{r,t}=S_{r,s}S_{s,t}.
\]
For \(\Phi\in\mathscr D\), \(U(s,f)=S_{s,T}\Phi(f)\) is a viscosity solution
of
\[
 \partial_sU+
 \mathbb H\left(f,\frac{\delta U}{\delta f},0\right)=0,
 \qquad U(T,f)=\Phi(f),
\]
and a bounded collision work enters as the \(\psi\)-argument of
\(\mathbb H\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The B2 action has compact sublevels on moment balls.  Hence the supremum may be
restricted to a compact set and is attained after relaxation.  Concatenating
two balanced density--collision pairs preserves balance and adds the entropy
action, proving the semigroup law; splitting a path proves the reverse
inequality.  Stability under the weighted weak metric follows from lower
semicontinuity of the action and recovery by smooth positive approximations.

For a smooth cylinder touching \(U\), restrict the variational formula to a
short interval.  Fenchel duality from B3 gives the upper viscosity inequality.
For the lower inequality, use the B2 biased collision law with source
\(p=\delta F/\delta f\) and the corresponding regular recovery path.  Division
by the interval length and passage to zero gives the Hamiltonian.  A collision
reward simply adds \(\psi\) to the same duality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hamiltonian continuity on moment balls]
\label{lem:r3-b4-hcont}
For cylinder cotangents \(p,q\) with a common weighted \(C^1\) bound and
\(f,g\in\mathcal E_M\),
\[
 |\mathbb H(f,p,\psi)-\mathbb H(g,q,\psi)|
 \le C_M\left(d_w(f,g)+
 \|p-q\|_{C^1_w}\right)
\]
after replacing \(d_w\) by an equivalent metric containing the finitely many
tests appearing in \(p,q\).  The containment function satisfies
\[
 \mathbb H(f,D\Upsilon,0)\le C(1+\Upsilon(f)).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The transport term is linear.  Expand the difference of the collision terms
as \((ff_*-gg_*)\) plus the difference of exponentials.  Weighted moment
bounds dominate the relative velocity and every derivative of
\(\Delta p\).  Approximate the resulting finitely many weighted continuous
tests by the metric family.  For \(\Upsilon\), elastic collisions conserve
kinetic energy and the polynomial weight grows only through free transport,
which gives the final bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Comparison and uniqueness]
\label{thm:r3-b4-comparison}
Bounded upper-semicontinuous subsolutions and bounded lower-semicontinuous
supersolutions on the regular moment domain, with the same terminal data,
satisfy \(u\le v\).  Hence the action value in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-b4-action} is the unique viscosity solution in this class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose \(\sup(u-v)>0\).  Double variables with the finite-dimensional metric
penalty
\[
 \frac1{2\eta}\sum_{j=1}^J
 |\langle f-g,\phi_j\rangle|^2
 +\delta(\Upsilon(f)+\Upsilon(g))
 +\frac{|t-s|^2}{2\eta}.
\]
Choose \(J\) so that the omitted metric tail is smaller than the alleged gap.
The containment term keeps maximizers in one moment ball.  Apply the viscosity
inequalities with the cylinder derivatives of the penalty.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-b4-hcont} makes the Hamiltonian difference tend to zero as
\(\eta\downarrow0\), then \(J\to\infty\) and \(\delta\downarrow0\).  The time
penalty and a standard strict subsolution perturbation yield a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Microscopic semigroup convergence}

\begin{theorem}[Kinetic excess-pressure convergence]
\label{thm:r3-b4-limit}
For every bounded uniformly continuous terminal cylinder and every compact
regular initial family, the hierarchy values converge locally uniformly to
\(S_{s,t}\Phi\).  The same is true with bounded collision work.  The limit
passes the nonlinear tower from the exact hierarchy state to the density
Lax--Oleinik semigroup.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The primary convergence route is the good joint density--actual-collision LDP
of B2.  Condition its initial rate on a regular initial density and apply the
Laplace principle to a bounded terminal cylinder.  The resulting variational
formula is exactly \(S_{s,t}\Phi\), because the transition part of the B2 rate
is \(\mathcal A_{s,t}\).  Uniform exponential compact containment makes this
convergence locally uniform on compact regular initial families.  Bounded
collision work is handled by the same joint Laplace principle with the
collision coordinate retained.

Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-b4-generator} is now a consistency theorem on the
separating core: it identifies the infinitesimal Hamiltonian of the already
constructed action value.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-b4-containment} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-b4-comparison} extend the cylinder result to bounded
uniformly continuous terminal data and identify the unique viscosity
solution.  Local uniform convergence and the exact hierarchy tower give the
limiting semigroup law.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Microcanonical preparation as a lifted static saddle}

For an initial multiplier \(\lambda\), let
\(S_{s,t}^\lambda\) denote the kinetic semigroup whose initial activity is
tilted by \(\lambda\).  Define the lifted semigroup on functions
\(V(\lambda,f)\) by
\[
 (\mathbf S_{s,t}V)(\lambda,f)
 =S_{s,t}^\lambda(V(\lambda,\cdot))(f).
\]
The multiplier is a static state coordinate and is not reselected inside each
time block.

Let
\[
 (\mathfrak C_aV)(f)
 =\inf_\lambda\{V(\lambda,f)-\lambda\cdot a\}
 -\inf_\lambda\{V_0(\lambda)-\lambda\cdot a\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Source-dependent microcanonical semigroup interface]
\label{thm:r3-b4-micro}
The lifted family \(\mathbf S_{s,t}\) is a nonlinear semigroup.  The
microcanonical finite-horizon value is
\[
 U_a^{\rm mc}(0,f)
 =\mathfrak C_a\big(\mathbf S_{0,T}\Phi\big)(f),
\]
with the unique minimizing multiplier equal to the B1
\(\lambda_{\Phi}\).  This formula is the Boltzmann--Grad limit of the primitive
microcanonical values.  At an intermediate time, dynamic composition is
performed in the augmented state \((\lambda,f)\); minimizing \(\lambda\)
independently at every block is not part of the model.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The semigroup law holds at fixed \(\lambda\), so it holds for
\(\mathbf S\).  B1 gives, uniformly for every terminal and collision source,
\[
 \inf_\lambda\{Q(H,\lambda)-\lambda\cdot a\}
 -\inf_\lambda\{Q(0,\lambda)-\lambda\cdot a\}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-b4-limit} identifies \(Q(H,\lambda)\) with the fixed-
\(\lambda\) kinetic value.  Strict constraint convexity gives the unique
\(\lambda_\Phi\).  Since the initial constraint is chosen once, retaining
\(\lambda\) in the state gives the correct tower before the terminal
contraction \(\mathfrak C_a\) is applied.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Calibrated rays and the Gaussian tangent}

Let \(G\) be a mechanical work direction and let a prepared exposed value
select \(\theta=I_G'(a)\).  On the canonical cocycle generated by this same
work, define
\[
 \mathcal E_G^\theta(F)
 =\theta^{-1}
 \left(Q(\theta G+\theta F)-Q(\theta G)\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Canonical-ray coefficient and Gaussian contraction]
\label{thm:r3-b4-theta}
If the nonlinear value is required to use the prepared canonical
Radon--Nikodym cocycle and to linearize after exponentiation, its coefficient
is uniquely \(\theta=I_G'(a)\).  Under the central-limit scaling of B3, the
kinetic semigroups converge to the risk-sensitive diffusion semigroup with
generator
\[
 \mathfrak G^\theta F
 =b\cdot DF+\frac12A:D^2F
 +\frac\theta2\,DF^TADF.
\]
The covariance \(A\) includes the B1 constrained initial covariance and the
B3 collision noise.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient statement is the canonical-cocycle uniqueness argument:
applying the value to the work generator identifies its exponent with the
mechanical likelihood ratio, forcing equality of coefficients.

For the tangent, the round-three finite-volume normalization theorem of Paper B3 identifies
the pressure Hessian with the covariance of the
\(\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}\)-field, and B3 proves joint Gaussian convergence.
Expand the limiting log-Laplace semigroup on a complex ball of radius larger
than the central-limit perturbation.  Third and higher cumulants vanish under
the scaling, while the first two give \(b\) and \(A\).  Exponential moment
bounds make the likelihood ratios uniformly integrable.  The exponential
transform of the diffusion generator gives the displayed quadratic term.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-three B4 closure]
\label{thm:r3-b4-main}
The deterministic hard-sphere hierarchy has an exact nonlinear tower; its
correlation-corrected generators converge to the Hamilton--Boltzmann
Hamiltonian; the limiting action semigroup is the unique viscosity solution
on the weighted density state; primitive microcanonical preparation is a
source-dependent contraction of a lifted static-multiplier semigroup; and the
quadratic theta generator is the proved Gaussian tangent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Proposition~\ref{prop:r3-b4-hierarchy} and
Theorems~\ref{thm:r3-b4-generator}, \ref{thm:r3-b4-action},
\ref{thm:r3-b4-comparison}, \ref{thm:r3-b4-limit},
\ref{thm:r3-b4-micro}, and \ref{thm:r3-b4-theta}.
\end{proof}
