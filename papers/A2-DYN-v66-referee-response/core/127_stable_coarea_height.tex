\section{Pointwise roof inversion by stable coarea and cylinder bounds}
\label{sec:v59-stable-coarea}
We prove a density theorem, and not just a scalar spectral expansion, for
the preceding Markov baker map. In this section the roof is $\tau_0$;
the uniform pressure-contact assertion for the perturbed roofs
$\tau_\epsilon$ is separate. Let $p_m^{\rm M}$ be the density of
$S_m\tau_0$ under the original probability $\mu$, and put
\[
 \bar\tau=24/7,\qquad \sigma^2=204/343,\qquad
 z_m(t)=(t-m\bar\tau)/\sqrt m.
\]
All boundary lines of the baker partition remain in the physical
coding. No noise, roof convolution, or exceptional roof set is introduced.

\subsection{A lattice estimate with a stable cylinder}
Write $A_m=\sum_{r=1}^m I_r$. For a full word
$w=(i_0,\ldots,i_m)$ let
\[
 w_\mu=\pi_{i_0}\prod_{r=0}^{m-1}P_{i_ri_{r+1}},\qquad
 y_m=b_w+a_wy_0,\qquad 0<a_w\le\rho_0^m.
\]
Conditional on the word, $y_0$ is uniform on $[0,1]$; it is independent
of the future $x$ cylinder. Here $b_w$ is a scalar endpoint of a stable
cylinder, not a physical remainder density. Put $b_m=b_w$ on that word.

\begin{lemma}[Exact-state lattice local limit]
\label{lem:v59-markov-lattice}
For every pair of states $i,j$, uniformly in $k\in\Z$ and $m\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 \left|\sqrt m\,\Pr_i(A_m=k,I_m=j)
       -\pi_jg_{\sigma^2}\left(\frac{k-3m/7}{\sqrt m}\right)\right|
                &\le C m^{-1/2},\label{eq:v59-markov-lattice}\\
 \sqrt m\,\Pr_i(A_m=k,I_m=j)&\le C.\notag
\end{align}
The same estimates hold after averaging the initial state by $\pi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the matrix $P_{ij}e^{izj}$ and Fourier inversion on $[-\pi,\pi]$.
At zero its leading eigenvalue is simple. The centered logarithm has
quadratic term $-\sigma^2z^2/2$ by
\eqref{eq:v59-joint-covariance}, and a bounded cubic remainder; the
projection entry equals $\pi_j+O(|z|)$.
There is no other unit-modulus eigenvalue. Indeed equality in the
triangle inequality for an eigenvector, followed by irreducibility,
forces all its coordinate moduli to agree and every transition phase
to agree with its eigen-equation. The $00$ loop gives eigenvalue one;
the $11$ loop then gives $e^{iz}=1$. The only such point on the torus
is zero. Compactness gives exponential decay away from zero.

On a small fixed interval the leading modulus is at most $e^{-cz^2}$.
The projection error and the logarithmic cubic error in the inverse
integrate to $C/m$: use $|z|e^{-cmz^2}$ and
$m|z|^3e^{-cmz^2}$, respectively, after reducing the interval so that
the real quadratic damping absorbs the cubic remainder. The omitted
Gaussian tail and complementary terms are exponential. Multiplication
by $\sqrt m$ proves \eqref{eq:v59-markov-lattice}; the bound is uniform
in $k$ because its Fourier phase has modulus one. Increase $C$ for
finitely many small $m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Stable interval probabilities at an exact lattice label]
\label{lem:v59-cylinder-local}
For every interval $J\subset\R$, every $k\in\Z$ and $1\le L\le m/2$,
\begin{align}
 \left|\sqrt m\,\mu\{A_m=k,b_m\in J\}
   -g_{\sigma^2}\left(\frac{k-3m/7}{\sqrt m}\right)
                              |J\cap[0,1]|\right|
     &\le C\left(\frac{1+L}{\sqrt m}+\rho_0^L\right),
                                               \label{eq:v59-cylinder-LLT}\\
 \sqrt m\,\mu\{A_m=k,b_m\in J\}
     &\le C(|J\cap[0,1]|+\rho_0^L).
                                               \label{eq:v59-cylinder-upper}
\end{align}
Open, closed and half-open intervals obey the same bounds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each terminal state $j$, the last $L$ transitions partition its
stable unit interval into cylinders $I_w$ of lengths
$a_w=\prod P_{i_{r+1}i_r}\le\rho_0^L$. Their interiors are disjoint.
A full word with that suffix has $b_m\in\overline I_w$.
If $i=i_{m-L}$ and $s_w=\sum_{r=m-L+1}^mi_r$, the probability of
this suffix and $A_m=k$ is exactly
\[
 \Pr_\pi\{A_{m-L}=k-s_w,I_{m-L}=i\}
                         \prod_{r=m-L}^{m-1}P_{i_ri_{r+1}}.
\]
Detailed balance identifies $\pi_i$ times the last product with
$\pi_j|I_w|$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:v59-markov-lattice} to the prefix.
Changing its Gaussian scale and center to $(m,3m/7)$ costs
$C(1+L)/\sqrt m$ after normalization, uniformly in $k$, since
$|s_w|\le L$ and both $g'$ and $zg'$ are bounded. Summing over any
collection of suffixes does not multiply this error by their number:
the sum of their transition products is at most two, the number of
possible initial states. Consequently the normalized mass of every
such collection differs from its total $\pi_j|I_w|$ times the full
Gaussian by at most $C(1+L)/\sqrt m$.

Approximate $J\cap[0,1]$ from inside and outside by complete stable
cylinders, separately for each terminal state. At most two boundary
cylinders per state are needed, with total length at most $C\rho_0^L$.
This proves \eqref{eq:v59-cylinder-LLT}. For the upper bound, use just
the uniform upper estimate for the prefix, paying $\pi_i^{-1}\le7/3$;
the resulting mass is at most a constant times the total cylinder
length. There is no local-limit error in this positive upper bound.
Boundary atoms are contained in the same boundary cylinders. This also
proves all the endpoint conventions claimed above.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A pointwise density, including every word}
The exact conditional roof on a word with $A_m=k$ is
\[
 S_m\tau_0=3m+k+b_w-(1-a_w)y_0.
\]
The derivative in the original stable variable is bounded away from
zero by $1-\rho_0^m$. Thus the complete density, for almost every $t$,
is the finite positive coarea sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v59-exact-markov-density}
 p_m^{\rm M}(t)=\sum_w\frac{w_\mu}{1-a_w}
    \one_{[b_w+a_w-1,b_w]}(t-3m-A_m(w)).
\end{equation}
In particular its integral is one. There are no omitted words near
any cut, and no derivative of a long inverse density is being assumed.

\begin{theorem}[A continuous-roof pointwise local limit]
\label{thm:v59-markov-density}
For $m\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v59-pointwise-density}
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in\R}
 \left|\sqrt m\,p_m^{\rm M}(t)-g_{\sigma^2}(z_m(t))\right|
                         \le C\frac{\log(2+m)}{\sqrt m}.
\end{equation}
Moreover $\sup_m\sqrt m\|p_m^{\rm M}\|_\infty<\infty$.
For bounded Lipschitz functions $f,d$ on $[0,1]$, the actual weighted
roof density $p_m^{f,d}$, with insertion $f(y_0)d(y_m)$, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v59-weighted-density}
 \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in\R}
 \left|\sqrt m\,p_m^{f,d}(t)
       -g_{\sigma^2}(z_m(t))\Theta_{f,d}(t-3m)\right|
       \le C\|f\|_{\rm Lip}\|d\|_{\rm Lip}
                            \frac{\log(2+m)}{\sqrt m},
\end{equation}
where the arithmetic endpoint factor is
\[
 \Theta_{f,d}(r)=\sum_{k\in\Z}\int_{[0,1]\cap[r-k,r-k+1]}
                       f(v-r+k)d(v)\,dv.
\]
It is one-periodic and is not replaced by the product of endpoint means.
For $f=d=1$, it is identically one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed integer $k$ put $\delta=t-3m-k$. Since
$0<a_w\le\rho_0^m$ and $0\le b_w\le1-a_w$, the contribution of
words with $A_m=k$ in \eqref{eq:v59-exact-markov-density} is between
\[
 \mu\{A_m=k,b_m\in[\delta,\delta+1-\rho_0^m]\}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 (1-\rho_0^m)^{-1}\mu\{A_m=k,b_m\in[\delta,\delta+1]\},
\]
up to the immaterial finite set of roof endpoints. Choose
$L=\lceil\log(m)/(2\log(4/3))\rceil$ for large $m$, so that
$L\le m/2$ and $\rho_0^L\le m^{-1/2}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v59-cylinder-local} identifies the normalized contribution
with
\[
 g_{\sigma^2}\left(\frac{k-3m/7}{\sqrt m}\right)(1-|\delta|)_+
                  +O(\log(2+m)/\sqrt m).
\]
Only at most three integers have $|\delta|\le1$. On this set their
Gaussian argument differs from $z_m(t)$ by at most $m^{-1/2}$.
The exact partition identity
$\sum_{k\in\Z}(1-|r-k|)_+=1$ proves
\eqref{eq:v59-pointwise-density}, uniformly in $t$. The all-count
height bound follows directly from \eqref{eq:v59-exact-markov-density},
$1-a_w\ge1-\rho_0$, and the positive lattice upper bound, again summing
at most three integer labels.

For the weighted assertion, at the coarea point
\[
 y_0=(b_w-\delta)/(1-a_w),\qquad y_m=b_w+a_wy_0.
\]
On its support these differ from $b_w-\delta$ and $b_w$, respectively,
by at most $C\rho_0^m$. Lipschitz continuity changes the normalized
weighted sum by at most $C\|f\|_{\rm Lip}\|d\|_{\rm Lip}\rho_0^m$.
The remaining function of $b_w$ is
$f(b_w-\delta)d(b_w)\one_{[\delta,\delta+1]}(b_w)$, with a uniform
bounded-variation norm. The interval estimate
\eqref{eq:v59-cylinder-LLT} controls its integral by integration by
parts: a signed measure whose mass on every interval is at most $e$
acts on such a function with absolute value at most
$e(\|h\|_\infty+\operatorname{TV}(h))$. The two shifted interval
boundaries are handled by \eqref{eq:v59-cylinder-upper} and the same
$L$. The limiting integral is the one displayed in $\Theta_{f,d}$.
Sum the at most three labels and make the same Gaussian-center change.
Complex weights follow by the identical variation estimate. No arbitrary
path-dependent insertion is assigned this amplitude.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Positive essential heights of boundary sources}
Let $U_s$ be an initial vertical strip family: in each square it is the
union of intervals of $x$-distance less than $s$ from $0,1,a_{i1}$.
Let $V_s$ be the analogous terminal stable strip about
$y=0,1,b_{j1}$. They include the forward and backward partition cuts.
Let $p_{m,s}^{\rm vert}$ and $p_{m,s}^{\rm stab}$ be the roof densities
of the positive, unnormalized sources $\one_{U_s}\mu$ and
$\one_{V_s}\circ F^m\,\mu$, respectively. Define also
$p_{m,s}^{\rm age}$ by the source $\one_{\{y_0<s\text{ or }y_0>1-s\}}\mu$.

\begin{theorem}[Actual boundary-source height estimates]
\label{thm:v59-markov-boundary-height}
For $0<s<1/8$ and $m\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v59-markov-boundary-height}
 \sqrt m\bigl(\|p_{m,s}^{\rm vert}\|_\infty
       +\|p_{m,s}^{\rm stab}\|_\infty
       +\|p_{m,s}^{\rm age}\|_\infty\bigr)
                            \le C(s+\rho_0^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}).
\end{equation}
In particular the ordered limit of each essential height is zero as
$m\to\infty$ and then $s\downarrow0$. A union of these sources and
any bounded insertion supported on it have the same domination bound,
with its supremum norm included.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $L=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$. Cover the initial vertical strips from
outside by full forward cylinders of length $L$. Their mesh is at most
$\rho_0^L$, so their total stationary mass is at most $C(s+\rho_0^L)$.
For each such prefix, the remaining $m-L$ transitions have exact-count
probability at most $C/\sqrt m$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:v59-markov-lattice}. The conditional coarea density on a
complete word is at most $(1-\rho_0)^{-1}$ and a prescribed roof uses
at most three count values. Sum the unchanged positive prefix weights.
This proves the vertical estimate without requiring the strip to be a
union of exact cylinders.

For the terminal stable strips, $|y_m-b_m|\le\rho_0^m$, uniformly
at every coarea point. Enlarge their finitely many intervals by this
amount and apply the positive bound \eqref{eq:v59-cylinder-upper} to
each of the at most three relevant counts. For the initial age strips,
the coarea identity makes $y_0<s$ imply
$b_m\in[\delta,\delta+s]$, and $y_0>1-s$ imply
$b_m\in[\delta+1-s-\rho_0^m,\delta+1]$.
The same interval bound proves the assertion. Constants absorb rounding
and $\rho_0^m\le\rho_0^L$. Source domination proves the weighted and
union claims. This proof bounds essential heights directly; it does not
infer them from the small masses of the strips.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What the realization tests, and what it does not transfer.}
The map has correlated state observations, a nontrivial complementary
operator, a continuous roof, and exponentially many singular word
boundaries. Its density calculation remains on the original deterministic
source. The decisive additional input for its height theorem is the
uniform stable coarea derivative $1-a_w\ge1-\rho_0$, together with
positive local bounds for cylinders of length up to $m/2$.
These inputs are proved here, not assumed as consequences of pressure
contact. The roof is nevertheless an explicitly solvable coboundary
extension of a finite-state additive record; this is not a claim that
all singular hyperbolic density problems reduce to it.

For the Lorentz return record, the incidence and competing-hit sources
in Corollary~\ref{cor:v51-positive-criterion} do not yet have this
cylinder/coarea comparison with a uniform derivative and preserved
exact labels. An orbit is not continued across either physical seam by
this example. Their two essential-height estimates and the original
unrestricted four-coordinate pointwise theorem therefore remain separate
requirements. The same-roof Lorentz consequences are not promoted by a
pointwise theorem for the Markov model.
