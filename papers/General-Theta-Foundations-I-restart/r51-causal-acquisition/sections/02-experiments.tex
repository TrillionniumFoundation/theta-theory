\section{Prepared experiments and the paid interface}\label{sec:experiment}
All physical, action and report spaces are standard Borel. A prepared experiment consists of a preparation law and jointly measurable positive probability kernels
\[
 K_a(dx',dy\mid x),\qquad a\in\calA,
\]
where the nonempty action set $\calA$ is finite. Positivity means nonnegative measures; a uniform positive likelihood is an additional condition only when explicitly stated. After each call an observer may stop or continue. A protected audit $Z\in\R^d$ is performed \emph{after} the prediction is committed. Its law and physical evolution are fixed by the experiment, not by the decoder.

Write $\calF_{t-1}$ for the complete past, including previous private randomizations, before action $A_t$ is chosen. The current report is $Y_t$. The present main class has known Borel functions $f_a$ and compact sets $K_a\subset\R^d$ such that, at every legal stopped history,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:audit}
 X_t=f_{A_t}(Y_t),\qquad
 \E[Z\mid\calF_{t-1},A_t,Y_t,\text{current stop coins}]=X_t,
 \qquad \E[\norm{Z-X_t}^2\mid\cdots]=B.
\end{equation}
The constant $B\ge0$ is common to actions and histories. The audit is bounded. The dots denote the same pre-audit information, not a future audit result. Thus every decoder $r_J$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pythagoras}
 \E\norm{Z-r_J}^2=B+\E\norm{X_\tau-r_J}^2.
\end{equation}
Let $K=\bigcup_a K_a$ and $H=\conv K$; $H$ is compact. Decoder values may be projected to $H$ without increasing the excess in \eqref{eq:pythagoras}.

\begin{definition}[Regenerative task and conditional envelopes]\label{def:classes}
A task is \emph{regenerative} if probability measures $\nu_a$ on $K_a$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regen}
 \law(X_t\mid\calF_{t-1},A_t=a)=\nu_a
\end{equation}
for every surviving history, every time and every legal choice of action. These measures are actual one-call laws under the preparation. They are not a formal reachable-set measure.

The \emph{conditional-envelope class} replaces \eqref{eq:regen} by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelopes}
 \underline b_a\nu_a\le
 \law_\theta(X_t\mid\calF_{t-1},A_t=a)
 \le\overline b_a\nu_a,
 \qquad 0<\underline b_a\le1\le\overline b_a<\infty.
\end{equation}
The fixed parameter $\theta$ is unknown to the observer. Bounds hold for every $\theta$ and every legal history outside its null set, before optimizing the program. Each $\nu_a$ must be exhibited as an actual one-call law in a specified prepared reference member. Known maps, bounds and reference laws may enter a common program; the true $\theta$ may not. The full future predictive quotient need not be the current task vector $X_t$; Appendix~\ref{app:predictive} separates them.
\end{definition}

A checkpoint procedure may use the whole history to choose actions and a randomized stopping time $1\le\tau<\infty$, then encode that history once into at most $m$ output labels. An autonomous observer has disjoint finite live and terminal sets $S,J$, a fixed initial live state, an action kernel $p(a\mid s)$, and a routing kernel
\[
 \gamma(s'\text{ or }j\mid s,a,y).
\]
Every choice uses only the current live state, current report and fresh independent coins. A retained seed, an external phase counter, and rereading earlier reports are disallowed. Its entire state budget is $|S|+|J|\le M$, including all stopping and output labels. The protected audit does not update this state.

The primary acquisition budget is $\E\tau\le N$, $N\ge1$. We also state a scalar priced version with fixed known costs $c_a>0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
 \E\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}c_{A_t}\le N.
\end{equation}
Here $N$ has cost units, not automatically raw-call units; the raw count is recovered only by $c_a=1$. Preparation and audit costs are listed separately in Section~\ref{sec:errors}. Risk infima are denoted by $\calR_\cp(N,m;c)$ and $\calR_\aut(N,M;c)$. In \eqref{eq:envelopes} these are infima of the worst-model risk over common legal programs with a worst-model budget. Fresh output randomization cannot improve a squared loss, so fixed decoder values suffice.

The legal class is empty for an autonomous observer with $M<2$, since a call and a subsequent stop require a live and a terminal label. No prescribed deterministic horizon is imposed. In particular, the fixed-horizon clock deduction $M-n$ from informative filtering is not applicable to this mean-budget stopping interface.
