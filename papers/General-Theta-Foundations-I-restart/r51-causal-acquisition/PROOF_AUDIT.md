# Adversarial proof audit — R51

## Quantifier and implementation checks

- The lower bound optimizes all legal history strategies, not a fixed acquisition controller. The upper constructs one program for the entire conditional-envelope parameter class.
- The current task response is not declared to be the complete future predictive state. For unknown parameters that quotient can require an updated model posterior. The theorem uses conditional row inequalities, not free access to that posterior.
- The action counts are the expectations of **actual calls before stopping**. Terminal submeasures are never normalized separately before the cost calculation.
- A feasible profile can contain rejected-only actions. Its exact inverse still has the specified counts; they can be discarded only when optimizing, where cost cannot increase.
- Both signs of a null perturbation are used in the two-action LP extremality proof. This is the usual finite constraint argument, not a new general MDP theorem.
- Strong duality is first established with centers fixed. A single (z,λ) is constrained by every action. There is no unsupported exchange of infimum/supremum.
- At N=min c, compact right-continuity proves only a dual supremum, not attainment.
- The envelope upper sums a uniform lower stop hazard over actual survival. It does not assume independence after rejection.
- At N=1 the linear repair stops all formerly continuing primed outcomes. It does not add a clock or a state. Repair depends on known primed mass and is not represented as a common unknown-model operation.
- The regular-rate proof includes global covering and off-support centers. It treats N<1/lower-envelope by a one-call cover instead of invoking an infeasible comparison profile.

## Singular and Gaussian checks

The continuous mass r and density cap Nb are retained. The single-output formula uses the explicit b≤1/8 bound to locate the centroid below 1/2; it is not asserted at arbitrary b. For finite m a zero infimum is a minimum by compactness, which is needed in the zero-risk converse. A marked quantum mixture has pure outputs on both components, preserving its protected audit variance.

The Gaussian model is transition-then-report. Its occupation equations use the stationary first report and include the correlation induced by all rejections. Reflecting routing leaves cost and squared risk unchanged. Neyman–Pearson domination holds at fixed negative-state acceptance mass, where the derivatives have strict signs. A threshold with smaller mean is raised until the budget is saturated. This gives a lower for every Borel randomized M=2 program, not a lower certified by a numerical search.

The checkpoint code is deliberately reported as a construction. The certified table uses a rational **lower** threshold so the actual mean, not a nominal N substituted in its occupation equations, is at most N. Rational decoders are evaluated by their actual loss, not identified with exact irrational conditional means.

## Opposite stability certificates

For the report-first Gaussian example, the curvature is evaluated at p=11/1000 inside the acquired predictive interval. Restricting the integral to y≤2 gives a rigorous lower larger than one. The Hilbert derivative has a separate uniform strict upper 49/50. In the converse hold/reset example the positive-probability all-hold word is an identity map; uniformly comparable contracting metrics would contradict this. That atomic example does not purport to supply positive-dimensional small balls.

## No claim silently transferred

No TV-to-LDP equivalence, finite-to-infinite horizon extrapolation, finite-matrix-to-unbounded-operator closure, or same-controller-to-unrestricted-control substitution is used. Generic stopped occupation and its square-root modulus remain in the retained article under their original assumptions; the new linear modulus uses regeneration. The new article does not certify all historical proofs or assign itself a top-four acceptance decision.
