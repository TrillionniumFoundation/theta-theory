\begin{abstract}
We determine the joint acquisition--memory frontier of prepared causal experiments whose current task response is regenerated by a chosen acquisition action. The response laws may depend on the action, have different supports, and contain singular or atomic components. An actual occupation profile, rather than an ambient parameter dimension, characterizes both arbitrary-history checkpoint procedures and autonomous finite-state observers. Every feasible terminal occupation is realized by one live state and the prescribed output labels; no additional clock or retained random seed is used. A common mass--cost dual and a two-action reduction make the acquisition optimization explicit. Conditional domination extends the matching bounds to correlated, controlled and model-indexed experiments, including classical polynomial instruments and noncommuting quantum instruments. The regenerative frontier has a linear same-budget perturbation modulus, in contrast with the square-root modulus needed for general stopped experiments. An atomic--continuous acquired law exhibits a uniform mass-dependent memory transition. Beyond the regenerative class, we prove an exact all-randomized-observer two-state Gaussian frontier and give certified checkpoint constructions for the same stopped physical experiment. Predictive realization, finite precision, causal deficiency and all persistent and temporary resources are distinguished throughout.
\end{abstract}
