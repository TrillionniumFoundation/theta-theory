## What changes mathematically

1. **General finite-group contrast structure (Theorems 5.1–5.2).** For every finite observable group and finite terminal output alphabet, sufficiently small positive contrast attains the minimum permutation-module degree with legal positive preparation and decoder tables. The theorem identifies the exact live-memory value and proves attained budget thresholds. It does not assume that one qubit or one fixed prior represents all experiments.
2. **Positive quotient structure and dual certificates (Lemma 6.1, Theorems 6.2–6.3).** Normal stabilizers allow exact absorption of preparation into quotient response tables. When every subgroup is normal, a finite family of linear programs characterizes the unrestricted minimum, with explicit realizations and necessary-and-sufficient payoff inequalities. The nonnormal case retains its stated restricted scope. The abelian character cover, the sharp four-bit transition and its robust lower certificate follow within that framework.
3. **Matching positivity transitions (Propositions 5.3 and 6.5).** The `S_3` law has mixed-core transition `3` to `6` at contrast `2/3`, and counted live memory `4` to `6`. The four-bit law keeps predictive dimension six and the same active module while the core changes from `10` to `12` at `3/5`, with counted memory `11` to `13`. Exact upper constructions match the lower certificates; all boundary conventions are printed in the propositions.
4. **Conditional versus averaged quantum memory (Theorem 9.1).** An explicit family of qubit instruments with uniformly positive outcome probabilities has an exactly depolarizing average, a stationary one-dependent observed law and Hankel rank three. For phase order `q >= 3`, exact mixed classical memory is `q` and exact pure-start memory is `q+1`; irrational phase gives no finite exact classical model. The proof covers arbitrary real nonnegative hidden models and includes matching polygon realizations and pure-start rigidity. The earlier FRDN phase mechanism and earlier one-dependent non-HMM examples receive explicit priority credit.
5. **Constructive sufficient theory and retained results.** Theorem 8.1 quantifies a classical common-contraction polytope construction with counted initialization. R68's stronger instrument-interiority theorem and R69's period-four boundary are inherited, not relabelled as new discoveries. The causal transfer, predictive compression, finite-order measurement, reset, memory/communication, rank, discrimination, variational/dual, spectral-geometric and strategy-realization developments remain in the current source, technical supplement and pinned companions.
