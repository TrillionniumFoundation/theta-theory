\begin{abstract}
A report may specify a physical transformation without revealing the transformed state. We prove a causal energy identity for finite predictive test spaces transported by state-independent reports. At every finite-state update, information is lost twice: within each incoming row and when incoming conditional means are pooled. The identity gives a lower bound for all Borel randomized observers and an exact compatibility criterion for attaining its product bound. For compact-group relative transport with explicit orbital mass, global cover and stabilizer certificates, the optimal causal excess is comparable to the sum of the per-stage quantization scales, truncated at one. Spherical and higher-dimensional pure-state quantum orbits verify those certificates. On the circle the bound is exact. With an internally enforced deadline, every reachable phase is charged; balanced integer allocation gives the exact total-state resource curve. Positive classical transport and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the same theorem directly. Flagged hidden transformations yield a spherical--atomic acquired law with its true surviving mass and changing predictive rank. We also prove a fixed-start accessibility transfer for marked occupation certificates, explain cancellation without observable access, and compose same-task simulator and finite-precision errors without identifying labels with total bit complexity.
\end{abstract}
