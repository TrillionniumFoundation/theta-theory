\section{The causal cost of acquired geometry}
The basic object is a performed experiment, not a parameter manifold. Preparation and positive causal kernels determine the executable future tests. Those tests determine a predictive quotient; a legal observer must update a finite representation of that quotient using only reports already delivered. The law actually obtained by this procedure, rather than a formal reachable set, supplies the geometric measure relevant to risk. A comparison of experiments must in addition implement the reports before their deadlines and pay for the simulator's retained state.

The difficulty addressed here is spatial and causal. An optimal quantizer of each acquired law need not be recursively executable. Even when the acquired law is identical at every time, repeated observation can force a new information loss at every finite-state update. This obstruction is different from a small probability of reaching a useful region or a long physical return time. It persists when the full-history update is an invertible isometry and every report has the same state-independent distribution.

Our central theorem identifies the obstruction at the level of conditional means of executable tests. Its exact identity separates the ordinary quantization loss of each incoming row from the additional loss caused by merging those rows into one next-state label. The latter term vanishes precisely when the incoming conditional centroids agree. Thus the same formula provides both an all-program lower bound and the compatibility condition required by its attaining causal construction. It is not an assertion that an arbitrary prescribed codebook can be updated for free.

For reported isotropic transport on a predictive sphere of dimension $d$, let $m_t$ be the number of labels retained after call $t$. The theorem and its geometric consequences give
\[
 \calR_{\rm seq}(n;m_1,\ldots,m_n)-B
 \asymp_d \min\left\{1,\sum_{t=1}^n m_t^{-2/d}\right\},
 \qquad
 \calR_{\rm cp}(n,m)-B\asymp_d m^{-2/d}.
\]
The constants are independent of $n$ and all the $m_t$. The report reveals a relative transformation, not the current direction; no intermediate physical preparation occurs. The full-history baseline $B$ belongs to the same terminal audit. This is a tracking experiment: $n$ is a prescribed physical exposure, not an interchangeable number of independent samples of a static parameter.

On the circle, put $\beta_m=(\sin(\pi/m)/(\pi/m))^2$ for $m\ge2$ and $\beta_1=0$. We obtain the exact sequential value $B+1-\prod_t\beta_{m_t}$ for arbitrary Borel randomized observers, not only for a quantizer architecture. If the observer must stop by its own state exactly after $n$ reports, its initial state and all reachable phases are disjoint and charged. Writing $M-1=nq+r$, $0\le r<n$, gives the exact value
\[
 B+1-\beta_q^{\,n-r}\beta_{q+1}^{\,r},\qquad M\ge n+1.
\]
There is no legal observer for $M<n+1$. In particular, feasibility is distinct from retaining any nonzero predictive energy. The optimal allocation is obtained by one integer division, rather than exhaustive synthesis.

The main proof starts from report instruments and finite test pullbacks. Sphere and circle calculations occur only after that theorem. Classical word-transport and noncommuting quantum instruments then verify its hypotheses independently. The homogeneous version includes higher-dimensional projective orbits, not only spheres. A flagged hidden-transform extension produces a true acquired mixture of a sphere and an atom, retaining the surviving mass in both the baseline and excess. A separate accessibility proposition converts inherited all-row physical-occupation certificates to a prescribed-start, observably gated task; a stationary mixture shows why this qualification cannot be removed.

Conditional projection, quantization and centroid identities are classical tools. We do not claim their invention. The contribution proposed here is the all-Borel causal product converse, its exact pooling compatibility, and the complete-state deadline law for relative rather than absolute observations. Section~\ref{sec:literature} compares the information pattern and conclusion with zero-delay coding and finite-memory partially observed control. The earlier marked physical-time theorem remains a distinct complete result, with its duration--exit dependence, prefix normalization and actual-return remainder; it is neither used to prove the new energy theorem nor discarded in favor of a model-specific calculation.

The higher-dimensional quantum realization derives the acquired dimension $2(D-1)$ from Haar instruments and their executable audits, rather than substituting the ambient $D^2-1$ real matrix coordinates. Its mass lower is uniform over every possible incoming mixed conditional mean, including changes of eigenvalue multiplicity.
