\section{Matching geometric laws without reset}\label{sec:orbit-laws}
The geometric consequence extends beyond spheres. Let $G$ be a compact matrix group acting orthogonally on the centered raw test space $V$, with Haar reports and prepared unit test point $x_*$. Let $\mathcal O=Gx_*$ and $\mu=(g\mapsto gx_*)_\#h$. All points and measures below are induced by the performed instruments. Assume: (a) $V$ has no nonzero $G$-fixed vector; (b) the stabilizer of $x_*$ has fixed space exactly $\R x_*$ in $V$; (c) for every unit $v\in V$, every Euclidean center $z$ and $0<r\le r_0$,
\[
 h\{g:\norm{gv-z}\le r\}\le C_0r^d;
\]
and (d) $\mathcal O$ has global $m$-point unit covers of radius at most $C_1m^{-1/d}$. These are primitive orbital mass and cover certificates, uniform over all possible incoming conditional means; a bound only on the prepared orbit is not enough for the lower.

\begin{theorem}[Homogeneous acquired-geometry transfer]\label{thm:homogeneous}
Under (a)--(d), for all $n\ge1$ and $m_t\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:homogeneous-law}
 \calR_{\rm seq}(n;\boldsymbol m)-B
 \asymp\min\left(1,\sum_t m_t^{-2/d}\right),\qquad
 \calR_{\rm cp}(n,m)-B=q_m(\mu)\asymp m^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
The infimum is over all Borel randomized label updates. An autonomous internal $n$-report deadline is feasible exactly for $M\ge n+1$, and in that range
\begin{equation}\label{eq:homogeneous-clock}
 \calR_{\rm aut}(n,M)-B\asymp
 \min\left(1,n^{1+2/d}(M-1)^{-2/d}\right).
\end{equation}
Constants depend only on the displayed raw geometric certificates. Report laws have zero state-dependent total-variation distance, updates are isometries on the entire test space, and the stored observer uses global covers of $\mathcal O$. Thus stability, cover validity and report continuity are part of the proved implementation, not an assumed oracle filter.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Condition (c), with radius a fixed constant times $m^{-1/d}$, leaves at least half the actual orbital mass outside any $m$ balls. Therefore $q_m(\operatorname{Law}(gv))\ge c m^{-2/d}$ uniformly in unit $v$, including all normalized incoming observer means. The energy theorem proves the lower product and hence the lower in~\eqref{eq:homogeneous-law}. For a nearest-direction global cover $Q_m$ on $\mathcal O$, let $\kappa_m=\int\langle u,Q_m(u)\rangle\mu(du)$. The zero Haar mean in (a) and the covering identity give $0\le\kappa_m\le1$ and $1-\kappa_m\le C m^{-2/d}$.

For any $x,\widehat x\in\mathcal O$, average $gx$ conditional on $g\widehat x=u$. Haar disintegration on the stabilizer coset and (b) give
$\E[gx\mid g\widehat x=u]=\langle x,\widehat x\rangle u$.
The recursive cover therefore multiplies the correlation by $\kappa_{m_t}$ at each step. The legal terminal readout $(\prod_t\kappa_{m_t})\widehat x_n$ has predictive error $1-\prod_t\kappa_{m_t}^2$, proving the upper. This readout lies in $\conv\mathcal O$ because zero is its Haar barycenter. The checkpoint equality follows by conditioning and its two bounds follow from (c) at $v=x_*$ and (d). Finally the disjoint-phase proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:clock} applies to the same independent Haar report law on $G$. The budget $1+\sum_t m_t\le M$, Jensen's inequality and balanced integer widths prove~\eqref{eq:homogeneous-clock}, including the saturated range. No enumeration of histories enters the lower.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Acquired spherical mass and global covers]\label{lem:sphere-geometry}
For each integer $d\ge1$ there are positive constants $c_d,C_d$ such that, for all $m\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sphere-q}
 c_dm^{-2/d}\le q_m(\sigma_d)\le C_dm^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
There are $m$ or fewer unit directions and a Borel nearest-direction map $Q_m$ with
\[
 \kappa_d(m):=\int\langle u,Q_m(u)\rangle\,\sigma_d(du)
 \ge 1-C_dm^{-2/d},\qquad 0\le\kappa_d(m)\le1.
\]
The cover is global on the sphere, not merely on the support of a selected candidate trajectory.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The spherical-coordinate area formula gives constants $c,C>0$ such that
\[c r^d\le\sigma_d(B(u,r))\le C r^d\qquad(u\in\mathbb S^d,\ 0<r\le1).\] The upper bound also holds, after increasing $C$, for Euclidean centers in the unit ball: a nonempty ball is contained in a spherical ball of twice its radius around a point of its intersection. For $r=(2Cm)^{-1/d}$, at least half the actual mass lies outside any $m$ radius-$r$ balls. This gives the lower bound. A maximal $h$-separated set is an $h$-cover and, by disjoint radius-$h/2$ balls, has at most $C'h^{-d}$ points. Choose $h$ of order $m^{-1/d}$; boundedly many small values of $m$ are absorbed in the constants. Nearest-direction assignment is Borel after breaking ties by index, and its squared distance is at most $h^2$. Since $\norm u=\norm Q_m(u)=1$, its mean inner product is at least $1-h^2/2$. It is nonnegative because the maximum inner product among the chosen directions dominates any single direction, whose mean inner product is zero. The centroid codebook can only improve the distance upper, giving~\eqref{eq:sphere-q}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nonreset acquired-geometry resource law]\label{thm:sphere}
For the raw isotropic transport interface and the same bounded terminal task, all integers $n\ge1$ and $m_1,\ldots,m_n\ge1$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sphere-law}
 c_d\min\left(1,\sum_{t=1}^n m_t^{-2/d}\right)
 \le\calR_{\rm seq}(n;m_1,\ldots,m_n)-B
 \le C_d\min\left(1,\sum_{t=1}^n m_t^{-2/d}\right).
\end{equation}
The infimum is over all legal Borel randomized finite-state observers. The upper is attained to the displayed constants by explicit deterministic recursive covers. The checkpoint identity in~\eqref{eq:orbit-product-lower} holds at the same horizon and task. Thus the acquired law alone does not determine the online value: the relative-report interface and its repeated compression loss are essential.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The lower follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:energy} and Lemma~\ref{lem:sphere-geometry}, using
$1-\prod_t(1-a_t)\ge1-\exp(-\sum_ta_t)$ for $0\le a_t\le1$.
For $d\ge2$ store a unit code direction $\widehat p_t$, initialized at $x_*$, and update
\[
 \widehat p_t=Q_{m_t}(R_t\widehat p_{t-1}).
\]
Fix entering unit vectors $p,\widehat p$. Conditional on $R\widehat p=u$, the stabilizer $SO(d)$ averages every perpendicular component to zero. Therefore
\[
 \E[Rp\mid R\widehat p=u]=\langle p,\widehat p\rangle u,
\]
and averaging over $u$ gives
\[
 \E\langle p_t,\widehat p_t\rangle
 =\kappa_d(m_t)\E\langle p_{t-1},\widehat p_{t-1}\rangle.
\]
Writing $a_n=\prod_t\kappa_d(m_t)$, use the terminal response $a_n\widehat p_n$, whose scalar is a precomputed program constant. Its squared predictive error is
$1+a_n^2-2a_n^2=1-a_n^2$.
It is at most $\sum_t(1-\kappa_d(m_t)^2)$ and at most one, so the geometric bounds prove the upper. On the circle the regular-arc map has zero mean sine error and mean cosine error $\sin(\pi/m)/(\pi/m)$; direct rotation integration proves the same correlation identity. Section~\ref{sec:circle} also proves the exact value there. No full-history posterior is present in the executing recursion.
\end{proof}
The full-history update has Lipschitz constant one, not a strict contraction. In the space of test predictions the report kernels are state independent, and every linear audit probability is Lipschitz. Cover validity and report continuity are therefore verified from the primitive interface. Error does not disappear between preparations, because there are no intermediate preparations. The matching law measures precisely its accumulation.
