                   GENERAL THETA FOUNDATIONS I:
         ACQUIRED GEOMETRY AND CAUSAL RESOURCE TRANSFER

                                                     QIAN QI


        Abstract. We prove a two-sided resource transfer theorem for prepared causal experiments equipped
        with a paid reference certificate and a common acquisition program. The comparison is made on
        unnormalized report and task measures, along actual pre-call occupation, rather than on a postulated
        parameter geometry or a fictitious reference path. A single affine order combines relative calibration
        with accumulated absolute defects and yields implementable finite-state upper bounds and all-
        strategy lower bounds at the same physical budget. Relative task order alone preserves the acquired
        resource curve at every scale, including zero-loss strata, without the acquisition-amplified floor
        associated with an absolute error estimate. We characterize this order by measurable positive matrix
        measures on changing-rank supports, and construct Borel predictive quotients for compact-exhausted
        continuous-response experiments. The same theorem gives a classical controlled-channel realization
        and a noncommuting quantum realization with pure posterior strata; these concern post-command
        audits. Nonintervening directional sensing and the earlier absolute-calibration theory are retained
        separately in full. Event-stopped observers may reuse live labels for terminal outputs, while state-
        stopped observers pay for disjoint roles. Causal simulator state, stopping clocks, numerical work,
        calibration, raw acquisitions and physical time remain separate resources.




                                               1. Introduction
   An experiment does not acquire a manifold. It acquires reports through preparations and
interventions, each of which has a causal position and a physical cost. The future tests executable
after those reports determine which distinctions matter. Only after this experiment and its tests
have been fixed does a geometric question arise: how much of the resulting predictive information
can be retained and used with finite resources?
   The central difficulty is not the definition of a predictive quotient. It is the passage from a
quotient and an acquired law to an algorithm that uses the permitted interface, and to a converse
that applies to all competing algorithms. Three obstacles occur together. Selection changes the
acquired law and must pay for rejected observations. An error harmless for an ordinary report can
be amplified by a rare stopping event. Finally, a label used to route a live protocol need not be
unavailable as a terminal output. A geometric argument that omits any of these features can give
an incorrect resource curve.
   We address these obstacles by comparing unnormalized task measures. Given a genuinely prepared
joint report–audit law K0 (dx, dz), let ν be its report law and derive

global covering. They permit singular location measures, a variable-rank task carrier and a positive

controlled channels and finite-dimensional noncommuting quantum instruments. Their targets are
post-command audits. Neither is an estimation theorem for an untouched pre-intervention state.
The full preceding theory, including nonintervening directional Gaussian sensing, absolute-calibration
occupation bounds, finite-description examples and stopped-flow realization, is supplied as the
complete technical companion [8]. It is preserved, not relabelled as new mathematics.

Relation to experiment comparison and predictive representations. Classical experiment
comparison [1] and predictive-state minimality and controlled transducers [4, 5] provide important
antecedents. Quantum statistical sufficiency is likewise an established experiment-comparison
problem [7]. They do not by themselves give the particular paid stopping profile, a finite-state
strategy lift, or a separate simulator clock and workspace account. Our theorem uses a reference/effort
witness and a common task-order construction. It is not a necessary-and-sufficient classification of
all causal strategies. The exact order characterization below is for a declared family of localized
affine-square tests, not for the existence of arbitrary history-dependent balancing programs. These
distinctions are part of the mathematical statement, not qualifications added after the proof.

                     2. Prepared kernels, acquired task laws and observers
   All hidden-state, action and report spaces are standard Borel. A fixed unknown index θ ∈ Θ
specifies an actual preparation and positive normalized causal kernels. Positivity means nonnegative
measures, not an everywhere strictly positive density. Dependence on the entire physical past
may be included in the hidden state. A legal policy sees past visible reports and its admitted
randomization, but not θ or a future audit. Iteration of these kernels defines its finite-prefix laws;
the consistent countable path law is their kernel extension.
   Each call has a cost ci ≥ c∗ > 0. The decision to make the next call and its productive flag are
predictable. Commitment is permitted only after a productive report, and occurs exactly once
almost surely in each model. At that time a specified future audit Z ∈ H is scored, where H ⊂ Rk
is nonempty, compact and convex. The prediction is committed before that audit is observed. A
prospective joint report–audit kernel is defined at every productive call, including rejected calls.
The stopping coin is independent of the audit conditional on the entire current visible report and
the past. Write L = diam(H)2 ; the case L = 0 is trivial whenever a legal rule exists.
   The core cost Cτ includes initial preparation charged to the core, failed calls and all productive
calls through commitment. A separately executed final audit must be added to the raw budget.
This convention is fixed before taking a risk infimum. Physical time, stored program constants and
temporary computation are not identified with call count.


Three resource interfaces. A checkpoint procedure may use its whole precommit history, but
must output one element of a fixed dictionary of at most M values. This counts its output width
only; it is not a claim of log2 M total working bits.
   An autonomous observer has an alphabet S, |S| ≤ M , and one fixed parameter-independent
program. At an intercall boundary its only persistent observer memory is its label s ∈ S. Its next
command distribution is a fixed function of s. A physical port name, elapsed time, subprogram
index, random seed or former report is not an extra free input. During a call its current command,
current report and fresh coins are available. Whatever persists to another call must be encoded in
S. A fixed decoder r : S → H reads only the committed label.
   In the event-stopped interface a post-report kernel emits (s′ , e) ∈ S × {0, 1}. An event e = 1 ends
the experiment and retains s′ for the decoder; no subsequent command is executed. This public
commitment event is not a stored bit used in continuing computation. The same label may be used
live on one path and terminal on another. In the state-stopped interface a fixed map h∗ : S → {0, 1}
determines halt status. The initial label is live, and type-one labels are globally absorbing terminal
labels with no executed command rows. Entry into a terminal label is allowed only after a productive
report. Thus live and terminal types are disjoint in this interface, and only in this interface by
definition.
   Write RiΘ (N, M ), i ∈ {cp, ev, st}, for the infimum of worst-case expected squared loss over
programs with robust almost-sure commitment and supθ Eθ Cτ ≤ N . An empty legal class has value
+∞. A program chosen once at random is not a free persistent seed. The ideal measurable class
may have fixed real constants; effective implementation is stated separately in Section 8.

Lemma 4.1 (Profile attainment). For every finite m ≥ 1 and b ≥ 1, the minimum in (2.2) is
attained. The profile is nonincreasing in m, b and left-continuous in b > 1.
Proof. Write a dictionary as an ordered tuple in the compact set H m , permitting repetitions. The
feasible densities are a weak-star closed subset of the radius-b ball of L∞ (ν): positivity, the upper
bound and the mass equality are each closed under pairing with L1 (ν). They are therefore weak-star
compact. As the tuple varies, its squared-distance function varies uniformly on H, since all points
belong to a bounded set. Along a convergent product net, split the objective difference into this
uniform variation and the weak-star pairing against the fixed bounded measurable loss at the limit
tuple. This proves continuity of the objective and attainment. Nets avoid an unwarranted sequential
compactness assumption. Monotonicity follows from inclusion of feasible sets. For left continuity
take an optimizer h at b > 1 and let hλ = (1−λ)h+λ. It has unit mass and cap bλ = (1−λ)b+λ < b.
Its objective tends to the optimum as λ ↓ 0, while monotonicity bounds the optimum at bλ between
the optimum at b and this objective.                                                                □

What is and is not generalized. The central theorem generalizes exact and absolute-calibrated

consequences. The positive-matrix characterization concerns localized task order; the quotient

a fixed independent command sampler. These results do not establish necessity of a common
acquisition program for every causal experiment, nor the minimal possible simulator state under
all implementations, nor matching lower bounds for program length, scratch and arithmetic time.
Those stronger mathematical questions are distinct from the fully stated sufficient-class theorem
proved here. The general theorem does not depend on a conclusion taken from either realization.
