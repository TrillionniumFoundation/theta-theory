\section{A sharp joint resource law}\label{sec:sharp}
The defect modulus concerns an attained experiment difference, not an arbitrary permissible error tolerance. The following single task realizes it together with a geometric memory coordinate and an inaccessible calibration coordinate.

\begin{proposition}[Memory, calibration and a long-cycle defect]\label{prop:joint}
Let $\pi$ be a probability law on a compact subset $K\subset\R^m$, and let $\theta\in\{0,1\}$ and $u\in\{-\delta,\delta\}$ be fixed inaccessible parameters, $0\le\delta\le1$. At each cycle prepare $V\sim\pi$, independently of the parameters and clock. Charge one zero-score preparation. With probability $1-e$ there is one data call; with probability $e$ there are $L\ge1$ data calls. The type is visibly tagged, all later data reports are blank, and the observer is overwritten at the next preparation. The target first report is $(V,\theta)$ in either type. The source first report is $(V,\theta)$ in the short type and $(V,\perp)$ in the long type. At every data call the actual audit is $(V,\theta,u)$, not feedback. Its loss is
\[
 \norm{d_V-V}^2+(d_b-\theta)^2+(d_u-u)^2.
\]
Let $\mu=2+e(L-1)$ and $a=(\mu-1)/\mu$. For every $M\ge3$, with $J=\lfloor M/3\rfloor$, the source's optimal robust average risk obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-sandwich}
 a q_M(\pi)+\frac{eL}{4\mu}+a\delta^2
 \le\mathcal R_\av(M,e,L,\delta)
 \le a q_J(\pi)+\frac{eL}{4\mu}+a\delta^2.
\end{equation}
The upper is implemented by one common finite-state encoder whenever the chosen $J$-center classifier and centers have the declared executable precision; otherwise it is the exact Borel upper and is finitely approximable for computable regular or Cantor codebooks.

Under task-preserving, cycle-aligned causal simulation, with the target first report required before any later acquisition, the source-to-target completed-cycle deficiency is exactly $\varepsilon=e/2$. The simulator may not use audit marks as feedback. For $0<p\le1$, choose $e=2\varepsilon$ and $L=\lfloor e^{-1/(1+p)}\rfloor$. If $q_M(\pi)\asymp M^{-2/d}$, then for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-rate}
 \mathcal R_\av(M,\delta,\varepsilon)
 \asymp M^{-2/d}+\delta^2+\varepsilon^{p/(1+p)},\qquad M\ge3,
\end{equation}
with a uniform $1+p$ cycle moment. The constants depend on the declared geometric law and $p$, not on $M,\delta,\varepsilon$. In particular the exponent in \eqref{eq:holder-defect} is sharp in its moment class.

For the same stationary observer class at finite real acquisition budget $N$, Theorem~\ref{thm:renewal} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-N}
 |\mathcal R_N(M,\delta,\varepsilon)-\mathcal R_\av(M,\delta,\varepsilon)|
 \le 2HK N^{-p},
\end{equation}
where $H=\operatorname{diam}(K)^2+2$ after restricting decisions to the corresponding convex ranges. An analogous bound holds for vanishing discount. The term in \eqref{eq:joint-N} is a signed finite-acquisition comparison, not an asserted positive learning penalty.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Any $M$-state observer has at most $M$ vector readouts, so its vector loss is at least the nearest-center loss. The clock is independent of $V$, giving the occupied vector law $a\pi$. Average the fixed parameters $\theta=0,1$ and $u=\pm\delta$ only to prove a minimax lower. This averaging does not change them during a path. On a long source cycle, no visible report contains information about $\theta$, and no information survives from another cycle; a fair bit therefore contributes at least $1/4$ per data call. The unobserved calibration sign contributes at least $\delta^2$ at every data call. Squared coordinates add, proving the lower in \eqref{eq:joint-sandwich} for every randomized program.

For the upper, use $J$ vector centers and bit readouts $0,1,1/2$, with calibration output zero. A load report selects a center and either the observed bit or the erasure readout. Holding reports retain that label. There are $3J\le M$ labels; the distinguished reset label is one of them and has zero-score preparation, so no extra phase state is needed. The vector loss is $a q_J(\pi)$, the bit loss is exactly $eL/(4\mu)$ for each fixed $\theta$, and the calibration loss is exactly $a\delta^2$ for each fixed $u$. Near-optimal centers suffice if the infimum notation is used; compactness also gives exact centers.

On a source erasure, a causal simulator can issue an independent fair bit, and otherwise issue the reported bit. Everything else is copied with its actual cycle type, length and audit law. For either fixed $\theta$, the probability that its first bit is wrong is $e/2$. Hence the full-cycle total variation is at most $e/2$. Conversely, conditioned on the erasure type, the simulator's first-bit distribution is common to both values of $\theta$. At least one parameter has an incorrect-bit probability at least $1/2$. The type has probability $e$ and the target bit is certain, giving deficiency at least $e/2$. The task-preserving requirement prevents rewriting the later true audit. The first-report timing prevents using a future acquisition to infer the bit. No assertion is made for a different cross-cycle, nonreset simulation interface.

For $e$ small, $L$ is between one half and one times $e^{-1/(1+p)}$, so $eL\asymp e^{p/(1+p)}$ and $2\le\mu\le3$. Also
\[
 \E\tau^{1+p}=(1-e)2^{1+p}+e(L+1)^{1+p}\le 2^{2+p}.
\]
Thus $a$ stays between $1/2$ and $2/3$, $q_J(\pi)\asymp q_M(\pi)$ under the declared power law, and \eqref{eq:joint-rate} follows. Setting the vector and calibration tasks to zero leaves an exact loss $eL/(4\mu)$ even against unrestricted within-cycle history. This supplies the defect lower order independently of quantization. Finally rescale the bounded summed loss by $H$, apply the uniform renewal theorem and undo the scaling to prove \eqref{eq:joint-N}.
\end{proof}

The lifetime clock in this example is a physical experimental resource. It is not a counter secretly stored by the observer: the observer merely follows visible load, hold and reset reports. Simulating the clock requires its own stated resources. Similarly, the audit is an actual marked kernel operation in this formulation; implementing it as a separate physical call changes the acquisition ledger and must be charged accordingly. The example proves a selected matched slice of the resource region. It does not prove joint optimality of program description, workspace, physical time and minimal simulator state.
