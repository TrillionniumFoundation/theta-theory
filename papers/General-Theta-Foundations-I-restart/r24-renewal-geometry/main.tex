\begin{abstract}
The geometry obtained by a continuing experiment is weighted by the time for which its predictions are retained, not merely by the law used to prepare it. We prove a causal transfer theorem for regenerative experiments operated by one parameter-independent finite-state controller. The joint physical preparation and observer reset is an explicit, charged protocol. Finite-history product domination and a controller-uniform return-tail envelope yield an attained robust average risk, determination by finite model families, and uniform comparison with finite-acquisition and vanishing-discount values at the unchanged state budget. A return moment of order $1+p$ gives the sharp uniform orders $N^{-p}$ and $(1-\beta)^p$. A second sharp transfer exponent, $p/(1+p)$, converts a scored-cycle causal defect into average-risk error. Primitive kernel and instrument errors give effective lower certificates against every same-budget Borel controller and upper certificates implemented by finite tables. On the actual renewal-occupation law, small-ball mass, global covering and occupation-weighted predictive stability give matching checkpoint and online memory curves. Hidden-state experiments and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the assumptions independently; heavy-tailed and rank-changing classes are included. The theorem does not identify a compulsory observer reset with physical mixing, and does not infer an unrestricted average-cost theorem by formally removing a discount.
\end{abstract}
