\begin{abstract}
Physical regeneration need not erase what an observer has learned. We prove a causal resource-transfer theorem in which one parameter-independent finite-state program retains its state across every physical regeneration. The actual boundary chain, including its transient states, periodic classes and multiple closed classes, determines the long-run time change. A bounded group inverse is equivalent, on compact boundary families, to cost-universal uniform Cesaro regularity. Together with finite-history product domination and a uniform return-tail certificate, it yields attained robust average risk, finite-model determination, and uniform finite-acquisition and vanishing-discount comparisons at unchanged memory. For a finite model family, increasing the recurrence bound exhausts the unrestricted fixed-memory average value. Complete marked-cycle errors admit a recurrence-sensitive transfer modulus; support-stable controller classes have implementable same-memory upper certificates and all-Borel lower certificates. Actual class-weighted occupation, rather than a pooled ratio or a preparation law, controls predictive compression. A counted controller--predictor lift gives joint control/geometry bounds and matching consequences under stated mass and stability hypotheses. Controlled queue busy periods and noncommuting heralded quantum excursions verify the general theorem independently. Singular holding laws and a task-preserving erasure experiment give sharp memory and tail-sensitive error laws. Uniformity over unrestricted controllers is not inferred from physical mixing, and fixed-exploration geometry is distinguished from optimized control.
\end{abstract}
