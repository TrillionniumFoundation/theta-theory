\section{Measurability, occupation timing, and explicit constants}\label{sec:details}
\subsection{Kernel versions}
\begin{lemma}[A measurable dominated kernel density]\label{lem:rn}
Let $U,Y$ be standard Borel and $\sigma$ a probability on $Y$. For a measurable finite kernel $u\mapsto K_u\ll\sigma$, there is a jointly measurable density representing each $K_u$ up to its own $\sigma$-null set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a countable algebra generating the Borel field of $Y$ and let $\mathcal P_n$ be the finite partition generated by its first $n$ sets. On a positive-mass cell $C$ set $f_n(u,y)=K_u(C)/\sigma(C)$, and set it to zero on null cells. Each $f_n$ is jointly measurable. For fixed $u$, these are the conditional expectations of any Radon--Nikodym density of $K_u$. The martingale convergence theorem gives its almost-sure limit and $L^1$ convergence. The joint liminf, set to zero wherever infinite, is a jointly measurable version. The exceptional set may depend on $u$; no common null set over all parameters is claimed. Standard Borel disintegration, as developed in \cite{Kallenberg,BertsekasShreve}, then gives sequential finite-word densities by applying this construction to successive conditional hidden-history kernels. Conditioning on an entire hidden path as if it were independent of earlier reports is unnecessary. This proof explicitly supplies the generating-partition hypotheses rather than relying on an unspecified kernel-version assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The queue moments}
For the biased walk with up probability $p<1/2$, down probability $q=1-p$, put $b=q-p$. Starting at workload $w$, the hitting time $T_w$ of zero has mean $m_w=w/b$. Its second moment is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:queuemom}
 \E T_w^2={w^2\over b^2}+{4pq\,w\over b^3}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the equations with zero boundary values are
\[
 m_w=1+pm_{w+1}+qm_{w-1},\qquad
 s_w=1+2(pm_{w+1}+qm_{w-1})+ps_{w+1}+qs_{w-1}.
\]
Substitution gives the displayed polynomial solutions. Stopping on a finite interval and then using the exponential Lyapunov bound proves their integrability and identifies them with the hitting-time moments, rather than merely formal solutions of a recurrence. At $p=1/4$, $w=1$, they give $m_1=2$, $s_1=10$, and the preparation-adjusted bounds used in \eqref{eq:queue17}.

\subsection{Load timing and nonexpansive propagation}
For the iid-lifetime load models of Corollary~\ref{cor:rawgeometry}, assume $\tau=L+1$ has mean $\mu$ and the first data law is independent of the lifetime. Its average occupation weight is $1/\mu$. At a finite external horizon $N\ge2$, renewal epochs $S_k\le N-2$ each give one first-data report. If $K_x=\min\{k:S_k>x\}$, then $\E K_x\ge x/\mu$ by the stopped sum identity and $S_{K_x}>x$, while there is always the initial epoch. Hence the weight of that first-data law is at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstweight}
 {1\over N}\max\{1,(N-2)/\mu\}\ge {1\over2\mu}.
\end{equation}
For $N=2,3$ use the first term and $\mu\ge2$; for $N\ge4$ use the second. At normalized discount the same weight is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discountweight}
 (1-\beta)\sum_{k\ge0}\E\beta^{S_k+1}
 ={\beta(1-\beta)\over1-\E\beta^\tau}
 \ge{\beta\over\mu},
\end{equation}
because $1-\beta^\tau\le\tau(1-\beta)$. Thus it is at least $1/(2\mu)$ for $\beta\ge1/2$. A continuous quantum branch multiplies each weight by its actual factor $1-\omega$.

For the corresponding nonexpansive recursive upper, error at data age $t$ is at most $C r_J t$. For finite $N$, the expected total of its squared gains is bounded by summing over at most $N$ possible renewal epochs, each contributing at most $C^2\E\tau^3$. Dividing by $N$ gives a uniform constant; the positive Abel mixture gives the discount version. For retained exploration with label-dependent lifetimes the main average theorem uses the class-weighted law \eqref{eq:occupation}; the simple exact first-weight formulas above are stated only for the iid-lifetime subclass. They are not silently applied to arbitrary Markov-renewal lifetimes.

\subsection{Informational completeness and physical back-action}
For $d=q^2-1$ linearly independent traceless Hermitian $H_j$, the effects
$F_{j,\pm}=(I\pm cH_j)/(2d)$ satisfy
$\sum_{j,\pm}F_{j,\pm}=I$.
On the traceless Hermitian space with Hilbert--Schmidt norm, let $s_0>0$ be the least singular value of $X\mapsto(\tr H_jX)_j$. The audit map $f(X)=(\tr F_{j,\pm}X)_{j,\pm}$ therefore obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:icconstant}
 \norm{f(X)}_2\ge {c s_0\over\sqrt2\,d}\norm{X}_2.
\end{equation}
All norms on this finite-dimensional difference space are equivalent, giving the inverse Lipschitz constant required in Corollary~\ref{cor:rawgeometry}. The performed audit changes the subsequent physical state by its completely positive average channel; it is not a simultaneous readout of incompatible unperformed measurements.
