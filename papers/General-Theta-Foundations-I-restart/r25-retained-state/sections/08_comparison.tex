\section{Comparison, scope, and consequences}\label{sec:comparison}
The theorem is a nonreset observer theorem, not a theorem without physical regeneration. At a boundary the physical innovations restart conditional on one fixed parameter; the observer's label survives and can carry evidence and control information. Multiple closed observer classes are handled by their separate physical-time ratios. No filter contraction or irreducibility of every boundary matrix is assumed. Uniformity instead has the exact cost-universal boundary criterion of Theorem~\ref{thm:regularity}, together with the raw physical-tail condition.

The established neighboring results answer different questions. Table~\ref{tab:comparison} summarizes the distinctions; ``fixed memory'' means an externally specified persistent-state cardinality, not memory chosen after an accuracy target. Recent preprints are identified as such and no priority conclusion is inferred from their omission of our notation.

The group inverse, hitting identities, renewal reward and directed forest expansion are standard tools \cite{Meyer,Asmussen,ChebotarevAgaev}. Convexity is not used to make a nonlinear feedback program space convex. Product response continuity supplies a legitimate compact topology for one reused Borel row; recurrence sublevels then prevent loss of average continuity when a closed class appears. The finite-model identity is the finite-intersection consequence in this one common space. It does not say that each model may select its own controller.

The synthesis theorem is global at a fixed label count on a support-stable architecture. Its lower certificate covers every Borel program in that architecture, and its upper table uses the original experiment and the same labels. Uniformly computable marked response errors, return tails, model nets and integrations are nevertheless \emph{inputs}. The queue and instrument verifications derive such inputs from primitive data in explicit classes. Enumeration \eqref{eq:count} proves effectivity, not a polynomial upper bound, and not a complexity classification for unrestricted partially observed control. This separation is especially important in light of the neighboring revealing-POMDP complexity result \cite{ACL}.

For predictive geometry, \eqref{eq:occupation} is the measure that is actually acquired in physical time. A local small-ball bound can give a lower rate only with its actual mass. The recursive upper also needs a global candidate-valid cover and an occupation-weighted stability bound. These sufficient conditions permit noncontractive propagation, singular strata and long holding times. They are not claimed to be a necessary-and-sufficient classification of recursively attainable checkpoint geometry. The optimized inequalities \eqref{eq:optimizedlower}--\eqref{eq:optimizedupper} state exactly where control enters and where an additional common attaining exploration is needed.

The resulting general resource description has two levels. On a declared recurrence class,
\[
 \Phi_{\mathfrak E}(M,H)
 =\min_{\gam\in\Gamma_{M,H}^\Theta}\sup_\theta
     e_1\Pi_{S_\theta^\gam}(D_\theta^\gam)^{-1}r_\theta^\gam
\]
is attained, has the finite-model characterization, and differs from the same-budget finite-acquisition risk by at most $b_N$. On the classes of Theorem~\ref{thm:compile} it has arbitrarily tight implementable and information-theoretic certificates. Geometry supplies explicit matching rates under Corollary~\ref{cor:matching}; Theorem~\ref{thm:joint} realizes memory, calibration and an attained task-preserving defect in one task. These statements do not assert a universal exponent in the ambient parameter dimension.

Remaining mathematical questions are genuine. The fixed-memory unrestricted value can be exhausted for finite model families, but uniform rates and attainment need not survive $H\to\infty$. General nondominated adaptive response families, a complete intrinsic recursive-geometry criterion, efficient synthesis, and optimal joint lower bounds for program description, workspace, arithmetic precision, physical time and minimal simulator state are not established here. Neither TV-to-LDP equivalence nor an unbounded-operator closure follows from our finite-dimensional comparisons. These limitations do not alter the experiment-to-prediction-to-resource question; they delimit the proved transfer precisely.
