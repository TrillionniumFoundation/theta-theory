\section{Acquired occupation and counted causal geometry}\label{sec:geometry}
\subsection{Predictive quotient and the task being compressed}
A history determines the conditional laws of all its legal future experiments, including available actions, report events and charged stopping tests. Two histories are predictively equivalent if all these quantities agree. On a standard Borel history space, a countable determining family of future tests gives the measurable map
\[
 h\longmapsto (\E[T_j\mid h])_{j\ge1}\in[0,1]^{\N}.
\]
Its fibers are precisely that equivalence relation. Its image need not be Borel solely because this map is Borel. Whenever a standard Borel realization is needed below, it is an explicit hypothesis verified by the raw model: the image has a standard Borel structure, a Borel section, and report and update kernels descend measurably. No measurable quotient theorem is inferred from positivity alone.

\begin{lemma}[Minimal realized prediction]\label{lem:quotient}
Suppose a countable test family determines all executable continuations and is closed under the stated one-step report and action continuations. Suppose its evaluation image $S$ is standard Borel and has a Borel section. Then the evaluation map is a measurable predictive quotient. Any statistic that determines all the tests factors to this quotient on its attained range whenever its own evaluation readouts are measurable. A report kernel and update kernel constant on the evaluation fibers descend to $S$ by composition with the section; the descended kernels do not depend on the chosen section.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equality of coordinates is exactly equality of all determining tests and hence all permitted continuations. The quotient map is Borel coordinatewise. If $Z$ determines the tests by Borel functions $f_j(Z)$, its factor is $z\mapsto(f_j(z))_j$, which lies in $S$ on the attained range. The section composes Borel kernels with Borel maps; fiber constancy makes the result section-independent. Including action-availability and visible clock coordinates prevents identification of histories with different legal future interfaces. This proves the stated minimality, not a prior-free sufficient statistic for every unknown-model family.
\end{proof}

The task coordinates in the geometric inequalities may be a closed response factor of the full future-test quotient under a specified execution; they are not asserted to identify histories having different uncompressed report laws. The remainder of this section is under one specified physical law and exploration, or under a common predictive realization separately verified for an entire model family. For a bounded Hilbert-valued performed audit $Y_t$, let $p_t=\E[Y_t\mid\mathcal H_t]$ at a designated scored data call. A zero-score within-cycle report can still update the predictive state. All updates used by the observer are stationary functions of its counted controller state, stored center, and current visible report type. A phase variable needed for this property must be either presently reported or included among the counted labels. The known-law predictive prior at a physical preparation is fixed; an unknown-parameter posterior carried across preparations is \emph{not} discarded under this convention.

Fix a $Q$-state exploration controller $\gam$. Conditional on each entering controller label $i$, let $\nu_i$ be the expected sum of point masses at the data-call predictive means during that cycle. With the closed-class quantities of \eqref{eq:classratio}, the actual acquired occupation submeasure is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:occupation}
 \nu^\gam=\sum_C w_C\,{\sum_{i\in C}\pi_C(i)\nu_i
                                  \over\sum_{i\in C}\pi_C(i)d_i}.
\end{equation}
Preparation and other zero-score calls remain in the denominators, so $\nu^\gam$ can have mass below one. Transient occupation has zero average mass under the return and recurrence bounds. Let $B^\gam$ be the corresponding average of $\E[\norm{Y_t-p_t}^2\mid\mathcal H_t]$. For any finite submeasure $\nu$, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quant}
 q_J(\nu)=\inf_{c_1,\ldots,c_J}\int\min_j\norm{v-c_j}^2\,\nu(dv).
\end{equation}
This is defined on the actual task-response space, not on an ambient parameter chart.

\begin{theorem}[Occupation geometry with retained control]\label{thm:geometry}
For the specified controller and task, a checkpoint encoder with one fixed codebook of $J$ responses has excess risk exactly $q_J(\nu^\gam)$. Every online observer with at most $J$ fixed response values has the same lower bound, even if it retains information across physical regenerations.

For a counted implementation let $\nu_q^\gam$ be the part of \eqref{eq:occupation} where the current control label is $q$. If the exploration label is retained and each of its $Q$ values has $J$ conditional response labels, total storage is $QJ$ and the checkpoint identity becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditionalq}
 R_{\rm cp}^{\gam}(Q,J)-B^\gam=\sum_{q=1}^Q q_J(\nu_q^\gam).
\end{equation}
Every online implementation with this same conditional labeling convention has this lower bound.

Suppose a global $J$-center cover in every control fiber has radius $r_J$, including a valid preparation center. Let $U_{q,a,y}$ be a Borel candidate-valid predictive update and $f$ a task readout of Lipschitz constant $L_f$. For \emph{every} pair of exact and candidate states used by this recursion suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stable}
 d(U_{q,a,y}s,U_{q,a,y}s')\le L_{q,a,y}d(s,s').
\end{equation}
At each preparation the predictor coordinate, but not the exploration label, may be initialized to the known prior. Let $G_t$ be the propagated sum of unit errors since this initialization, so $G_t=1+L_tG_{t-1}$ on an updated call. Assume its actual class-weighted data occupation satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stabmass}
 \mathcal S_\gam=
 \sum_Cw_C{\sum_{i\in C}\pi_C(i)\E_i\sum_{t\ {\rm data}}G_t^2
                          \over\pi_Cd}<\infty.
\end{equation}
There is one stationary $QJ$-state controller--predictor with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geomupper}
 R_{\rm on}^{\gam}(Q,J)-B^\gam\le L_f^2 r_J^2\mathcal S_\gam.
\end{equation}
If the control boundary matrix has $h(P)\le H$, the joint implementation has $h(P_*)\le H+2$. With update numerical error at most $\delta$ in the state metric per updated call and readout error at most $\xi$, the square root of the right side of \eqref{eq:geomupper} can instead be bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numericalgeom}
 L_f(r_J+\delta)\sqrt{\mathcal S_\gam}
       +\xi\sqrt{\nu^\gam(S)}.
\end{equation}
The approximate states must remain in the same candidate-valid domain.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conditional projection gives
$\E\norm{Y_t-c}^2=\E\norm{Y_t-p_t}^2+\E\norm{p_t-c}^2$.
For any random label, the last term is at least the squared distance to the fixed codebook. Conditional cycle summation and class-by-class renewal reward give \eqref{eq:occupation} and the lower bounds. A full-history checkpoint can choose a nearest center, measurably after breaking ties by the smallest index, proving the identities as infima. On compact task support the optimizing centers may be restricted to its compact closed convex hull in finite dimension; in general an arbitrarily close codebook suffices for the equality of infima. Formula~\eqref{eq:conditionalq} is the same argument with separate fixed codebooks in the control fibers. No codebook is chosen retrospectively for the realized history.

The online machine stores $(q,j)$. It runs the original exploration using $q$ alone, computes $U$ at the stored center, and chooses its nearest covered center in the new control fiber. The exact state is used only in this proof. From \eqref{eq:stable}, $e_t\le L_te_{t-1}+r_J$ and hence $e_t\le r_JG_t$. Sum its squared readout error under \eqref{eq:occupation} to obtain \eqref{eq:geomupper}. The same induction with error $r_J+\delta$, followed by the triangle inequality in the actual occupation $L^2$ space, proves \eqref{eq:numericalgeom}. This is a root-error statement; the full excess bound is its square.

It remains to count and verify the retained boundary state. At preparation the predictor is voluntarily initialized from the known physical prior; the exploration label is unchanged, and later actions ignore the predictor label. Thus the next joint boundary law depends on the entering pair only through $q$. There are stochastic matrices $A$ (forget the predictor) and $K$ (one full cycle from $q$ to the exiting pair) with $P_*=AK$ and $KA=P$. Consequently
\[
 \Pi_{P_*}=A\Pi_PK,\qquad
 G_{P_*}=I-\Pi_{P_*}+AG_PK.
\]
These follow either by finite Cesaro sums and the group-inverse identities, or by multiplying them directly. Their norms give $h(P_*)\le2+h(P)$. There are exactly $QJ$ possible pairs; neither $A$ nor the proof state supplies additional memory. This is a partial predictor initialization permitted by the raw interface, not an imposed reset of the retained control information.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Mass, singular strata and optimized control]\label{cor:matching}
If an actual submeasure $\lambda\le\nu^\gam$ has mass $w>0$ and
$\lambda(B(x,r))\le Cr^d$ for all centers and $0<r\le r_0$, then for all sufficiently large $J$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smallball}
 q_J(\nu^\gam)\ge {w\over2}\left({w\over2CJ}\right)^{2/d}.
\end{equation}
A global cover $r_J\le C_1J^{-1/d}$ and a uniform bound in \eqref{eq:stabmass} give matching conditional checkpoint and online rates for fixed $Q$ whenever the lower submeasure occurs in at least one control fiber. Its mass is never normalized away. Finite strata, atoms and nonuniformly stable paths are allowed.

For a joint decision problem over all retained $M$-state controllers with recurrence bound $H$, the general lower bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimizedlower}
 V_{\rm joint}(M,H)\ge
 \inf_{\gam\in\Gamma_{M,H}}\{B^\gam+q_M(\nu^\gam)\}.
\end{equation}
For each $QJ\le M$, every implementable exploration controller with $h(P)\le H-2$ and the preceding uniform geometric hypotheses supplies the upper bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimizedupper}
 V_{\rm joint}(M,H)\le
 \inf_{\gam\in\Gamma_{Q,H-2}^{\rm geom}}
       \{B^\gam+L_f^2r_J^2\mathcal S_\gam\}.
\end{equation}
Unused labels can be padded as isolated states. If all admissible explorations have $B^\gam\ge B_*$ and a uniform small-ball lower, and one fixed-size $Q_*$ controller attains $B_*$ with uniform cover and stability constants, then for $M\ge Q_*$,
$V_{\rm joint}(M,H)-B_*\asymp M^{-2/d}$, subject to the displayed recurrence margin and with small $M$ absorbed into constants. These attainment and uniform-mass hypotheses are additional conditions, not consequences of a fixed-policy example.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The union of $J$ balls of radius $r=(w/(2CJ))^{1/d}$ has $\lambda$-mass at most $w/2$. The remaining mass is at distance at least $r$ from every center, proving \eqref{eq:smallball}. Apply the upper bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:geometry} in each finite fiber; an actual lower fiber suffices for a lower bound on the sum. Every joint observer's actual policy determines $B^\gam$ and $\nu^\gam$, and its $M$ fixed readouts give \eqref{eq:optimizedlower}. The counted product construction proves \eqref{eq:optimizedupper}; the group-inverse margin is exactly the bound just proved. The final matching assertion follows with $J=\lfloor M/Q_*\rfloor$ and the stated uniform lower. Nothing in this reasoning replaces the infimum over control policies by a fixed policy without the extra hypotheses.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact nonuniform holding geometry without observer overwrite]\label{prop:holding}
At each paid preparation draw a fresh vector $V$ from a given compactly supported law $\pi$ and an integer lifetime $L\ge1$. A visible load reveals $V$; it is scored at that call and at the following $L-1$ blank holding calls. Preparation has zero score. The target at each scored call is $V$, and $\E(L+1)<\infty$. The incoming observer label is arbitrary and is not forcibly overwritten. Then, over all stationary $M$-state observers,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holding}
 R_{\rm on,\av}(M)=R_{\rm cp,\av}(M)=q_M(\nu),\qquad
 \nu(dv)={\E[L\mid V=v]\pi(dv)\over\E(L+1)}.
\end{equation}
For a family of preparations the robust value is $\inf_{|C|\le M}\sup_\theta\int d(v,C)^2\nu_\theta(dv)$, with a single codebook. This direct statement does not require common domination of the preparation family. The achieving boundary rows are identical, so $h(P)\le2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At every scored call, regardless of the incoming state or earlier cycles, a fixed $M$-response observer has squared loss at least its pointwise nearest-codeword error. The physical preparation and holding process are independent of its actions, so its actual time average has precisely the length-biased law in \eqref{eq:holding}. At load choose a nearest center and retain its label through blank reports. This is one stationary $M$-state program: the load/hold/preparation tags are visible, and the preparation has zero score. Old information is not deleted by the interface, but cannot improve the pointwise bound. Its boundary distribution is independent of the incoming label, giving $P=\Pi_P$ and $G_P=I-P$, of norm at most two. The identical pointwise argument with one fixed codebook proves the robust assertion for arbitrary preparations. It is not a proof of general nondominated Borel-controller compactness.
\end{proof}
