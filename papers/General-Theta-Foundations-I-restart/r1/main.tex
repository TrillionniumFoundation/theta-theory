\begin{abstract}
A prepared causal experiment determines both a space of future predictions and a probability law on that space. We prove a resource-transfer theorem in which the finite-memory scale is derived from this acquired law, rather than from an ambient parameter dimension. The hypotheses are certificates on future tests, raw-history mass, valid representative domains, and the propagation of errors introduced by actual updates. An acquisition-weighted transport estimate converts anisotropic small-ball bounds into matching checkpoint and online risk curves, for every positive label budget. Exact waiting transitions introduce no new quantization error. Consequently the constants can remain uniform through arbitrarily long inactive periods, vanishing acquisition probabilities, and changes of acquired rank. Calibration and numerical errors enter before squaring; causal simulation defects enter through the common bounded decision loss, with simulator, controller, and clock states charged separately. We verify the theorem directly for a binary hidden-state filter with continuous reports and for a stratified geometric sensor with absorbing acquisition. The latter has an explicit joint acquisition--memory law through all rank collapses. Classical prediction, quantization, and experiment-comparison results are separated from the acquisition-weighted causal synthesis.
\end{abstract}
