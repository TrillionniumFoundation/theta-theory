\begin{abstract}
We study finite-resource prediction in prepared causal experiments with a fixed admissible reference and a common task-balancing acquisition program. An all-strategy acquired-count converse and an all-model conditional-hazard construction identify the stopped minimax risk with a marked trimmed-quantization profile. The persistent-state law depends on the stopping interface: with $B$ mandatory live states, globally halt-typed observers have terminal width $M-B$, whereas observers that emit a final commitment event can reuse live labels and have width $M$. We prove both statements, an exact role-packing lemma, and a resource-aware composition rule. A state-dependent approximate-balance theorem gives an explicit budget correction and a sharp amplification of task error by the expected number of attempts; neither independence of attempts nor a normalized-filter contraction is required. Classical controlled kernels and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the same theorem, including singular acquired laws and pure-state strata. These two exact realizations score post-command audits. Separately, a nonintervening multidimensional Gaussian sensing experiment yields a matching waiting--memory law, an exact finite-output obstruction, and a sharp asymptotic noise constant. All geometric quantities are derived from actual report--audit laws; computation, workspace, numerical precision, physical calls and simulator state are charged separately.
\end{abstract}
