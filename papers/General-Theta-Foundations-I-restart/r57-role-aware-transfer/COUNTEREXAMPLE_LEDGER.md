# Counterexample and sharpness ledger

1. **R56 live/terminal overlap:** gate G and acquisition A are two live labels; productive acceptance may emit halt retaining G or A as either of two decoder values. No persistent mode register is present. This invalidates an untyped M-2 subtraction.
2. **Typed restriction is a real resource:** the fixed state-only halt map forbids the preceding cross-role alias. Both results are proved, not conflated.
3. **Report balance without first moments:** three observationally identical models audit 0,+1,-1. Reference profile zero but every common program has worst loss >=1. Full history and more finite states do not reveal the hidden alternative.
4. **Rare task-error amplification:** mark D of actual mass 1/b; changing residual variance on D from 0 to be gives one-attempt loss defect e and exact worst risk be at expected b calls.
5. **Sharp waiting denominator:** a zero-loss region of mass 1/beta perturbed down by a forces expected wait 1/(1/beta-a) for every zero-risk rule.
6. **Finite-output directional obstruction:** m<=d centers leave some unit direction at distance >=1. Even unlimited paid waiting cannot improve risk one with that fixed decoder width.
7. **Actual atom mass:** saturation requires b*lambda>=1. Conditioning the atom to mass one erases its real acquisition cost.
8. **History-dependent success:** different success probabilities on histories cannot be represented by a single exact driver row. Conditional hazard bounds do not require such a row.
9. **Uncharged stateful recoding:** a dispatcher that reads physical phase stores information and is counted; duplicated action names alone do not increase mandatory live contexts.
10. **Small output-TV and long duration:** rare very long waits can preserve terminal distributions while changing expected cost. Complete-law error still needs a separate cost certificate.
11. **Conditional coin leakage:** approximate control weights allowed to sense the current hidden input can destroy the fair reference. cor:raw-approx explicitly excludes this unproved case.
12. **One-point atom boundary:** below saturation one cannot allocate an m-1=0 dictionary; the fixed-center upper is stated separately.

These examples delimit proven claims and identify necessary resource distinctions; none is presented as a reason to abandon generalization.
