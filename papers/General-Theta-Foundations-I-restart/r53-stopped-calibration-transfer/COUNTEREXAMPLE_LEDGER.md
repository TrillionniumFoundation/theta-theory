# Counterexample ledger

1. Unreachable recurrence: add an inaccessible self-loop to an immediate-stop mode. Balance permits arbitrary circulation there; no real terminal measure is created. The proof explicitly removes it.
2. Free-stage fallacy: deterministic L-stage actions with disjoint names, constant proxy and zero mark need L live labels although a one-point cover is exact.
3. Zero hazard: gate calls cannot stop. A per-call hazard floor fails even when p>0 and total mean cost is finite. The finite potential succeeds.
4. Waiting mass normalization: at gate rate p an active report costs kappa=1+1/p calls. Replacing the raw experiment by its active conditional law incorrectly gains kappa.
5. Nonuniform endpoint: p=0 makes acquisition inaccessible. The theorem requires feasible N>=kappa(p) and asserts no finite endpoint value.
6. Model register: a two-mass hidden-state representation is for a fixed model. Unknown transition parameters require a family or a prior; no parameter is supplied to the observer.
7. Allowed tolerance versus attained defect: delta and epsilon lower terms use indistinguishable alternatives and actual erasure. A numerical upper tolerance alone has no such lower bound.
8. General absorbing-model compactness: finite-cost slice and fixed domination are used here; no generic weak compactness of arbitrary stopped strategic laws is assumed.

These examples delimit actual theorem hypotheses. None is a reason to abandon the foundations mother problem. R52's exact Gaussian, revealing and two-state examples are preserved, not reclassified as new R53 results.
