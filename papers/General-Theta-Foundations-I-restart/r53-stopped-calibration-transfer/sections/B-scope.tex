\section{Dependencies and extent of the theorem}
The main proof uses conditional projection, positive finite-flow realization, weak-star compactness on a bounded count slice, and a geometric packing argument. Neither physical realization is used to prove the central theorem. The finite protocol projection is exact for the primary report, not a consequence of mere positivity or a claim that the additional report is neutral. Uniform conditional calibration is verified on raw invariant families; it can fail when accumulated history makes the residual uncertainty smaller than every current-report mark. The live-state lower uses legal action signatures rather than an uncharged phase signal.

The full stopped-experiment companion~\cite{GTF52} is retained as a separate mathematical article. It contains the one-context density-envelope theorem, exact mass--cost dual, Gaussian whole-history lower and three-state construction, revealing-mass phase transition, and complete two-state ROC including atoms. Those statements keep their original hypotheses and are not reclassified as newly proved here. Its own complete companion chain preserves earlier predictive, posterior-pooling, continuation, finite-horizon, stationary and stopping results. The present central theorem is self-contained and does not require the reader to accept that entire archive as hidden premises.

The finite protocol class supplies a necessary and sufficient flow condition for baseline risk and matching full profiles when mandatory incompatible modes force the live-state charge. It does not supply a near-necessary classification for all raw causal kernels. General unknown-kernel learning, vanishing hidden-state positivity floors, nonclosed infinite observable drivers, efficient global synthesis and joint optimal lower bounds for program description, workspace, precision and physical time remain separate proof obligations. In particular, allowing $p\downarrow0$ in a paid finite driver is not a proof of uniformity as the hidden filtering parameter $q$ vanishes. These distinctions concern the proven statements, not a change of the foundational mother problem.
