\section{Actual mass, global covering and metastable acquisition}
\label{sec:geometry}
We first record the geometric ingredient, with its proof, to distinguish it from the protocol completion. For a primary probability $\nu$ let $\sigma=(f,\sqrt w)_\#\nu$ be its \emph{actual} marked law. Suppose $f\in K\subset H$, $0\le\sqrt w\le1$, $K$ compact, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectangles}
 c_0r^ds^\alpha\le\sigma(B(x,r)\times[0,s])\le C_0r^ds^\alpha
 \quad(x\in K,\ 0<r\le r_0,\ 0<s\le1),
\end{equation}
where $d,\alpha,c_0,C_0,r_0$ are positive. This is not an assumption about the ambient hidden-state dimension or a postselected normalized stratum. Define
\[
 \mathcal Q_m(b)=\min_{C\in H^m,\ 0\le h\le b,\ \int h\,d\nu=1}
     \int\bigl(w+\min_j\|f-r_j\|^2\bigr)h\,d\nu,
 \qquad b\ge1.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Reliability--resolution geometry]\label{prop:geometry}
Under \eqref{eq:rectangles}, uniformly for $b\ge1$ and $m\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
 \mathcal Q_m(b)\asymp F_{d,\alpha}(b,m)
 :=\max\{b^{-2/\alpha},(bm)^{-2/(d+\alpha)}\}.
\end{equation}
Both global covering and the requisite packing follow from the same acquired measure. This geometric proposition is the marked-mass result of the stopped-experiment companion~\cite{GTF52}, restated here to make the protocol theorem self-contained.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Setting $s=1$ gives $d$-regularity of the location marginal. Covering $K$ by finitely many $r_0$-balls bounds the total low-mark mass by $Cs^\alpha$. A selected density at most $b$ leaves at least half its mass above $w=c^2b^{-2/\alpha}$ for a fixed small $c$. This proves the first lower term.

The sublevel of distortion at most $t^2$ lies in $m$ rectangles of radius $t$ and height $t$. A center outside $K$ with an intersecting ball can be replaced by an intersection point and the radius doubled. Its reference mass is at most $Cmt^{d+\alpha}$. Taking $t=c(bm)^{-1/(d+\alpha)}$ leaves at least half the selected mass outside, proving the other lower term.

A maximal $r$-separated subset is an $r$-cover and has at most $Cr^{-d}$ points by the lower mass bound on disjoint $r/3$-balls. Conversely the upper mass bound guarantees $k$ points separated by at least $ck^{-1/d}$. For large $b$ select
\[
 k=\min\{m,\lfloor c_1 b^{d/\alpha}\rfloor\},\qquad
 t=C_1(bk)^{-1/(d+\alpha)},
\]
with a zero floor replaced by one. Choose $C_1$ large enough for the $k$ marked rectangles of radius and height $t$ to have total mass at least $1/b$, and then $c_1$ sufficiently small to keep their location balls disjoint. Both choices can be made uniformly, since $k\le c_1b^{d/\alpha}$. A constant density on their union gives terminal mass one with bound $b$ and distortion at most $2t^2$. Since $k\asymp\min(m,b^{d/\alpha})$, this proves the upper. The compact bounded-budget interval is handled by the full law and one center; there $F$ is bounded below by a positive constant.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A genuine preparation--measurement protocol}
There are two modes, $G$ (gate) and $A$ (acquisition), with disjoint action names. The preparation starts in $G$. A unit-cost gate call reports failure and returns to $G$ with probability $1-p$, or reports success and moves to $A$ with probability $p$, where the protocol parameter $0<p\le1$ is known. Uniformity in $p$ compares these known protocols; it does not presume that an unknown gate rate has been learned. Stopping at a gate call is prohibited. At $A$, a unit-cost call produces a primary mark with law $\nu$ and an additional informative report. The observer either stops there or returns to $G$. Hidden physical dynamics are not reset by these protocol events. All actions within either phase, when present, have the same displayed primary projection and may control the hidden dynamics subject to the calibration certificate.

\begin{theorem}[Waiting-rate-uniform causal resource law]\label{thm:gate}
For the preceding raw protocol let $\kappa(p)=1+1/p$. The profile is feasible exactly for $N\ge\kappa(p)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gate-profile}
 \Psi_m(N)=\mathcal Q_m\bigl(N/\kappa(p)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Under \eqref{eq:rectangles} and calibration, for every $N\ge\kappa(p)$, $m\ge1$ and $M\ge3$,
\begin{align}
 R_\cp^\Theta(N,m)-B_0&\asymp F_{d,\alpha}\bigl(N/\kappa(p),m\bigr),\label{eq:gate-cp}\\
 R_\aut^\Theta(N,M)-B_0&\asymp F_{d,\alpha}\bigl(N/\kappa(p),M-2\bigr).\label{eq:gate-aut}
\end{align}
The constants are independent of $p,N,m,M$ and the hidden model, provided the calibration and geometry constants are fixed. For $M<3$ no autonomous program is legal. Thus the result remains uniform along $p\downarrow0$, including budgets $N$ that vary with $p$; it does not assign a legal finite-cost procedure at $p=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $x_G,x_A$ be the aggregate action counts. Gate termination is forbidden, so the acquired-flow equations give $x_A=px_G$. Total terminal mass one gives $x_A\ge1$. Therefore
\[
 \E(\text{raw calls})=x_G+x_A=\kappa(p)x_A.
\]
This identity also follows directly for every history-dependent rule by predictable gate-success counting; no deterministic number of cycles or independent stopping time is presumed.

The terminal primary density is bounded by $x_A$ and integrates to one. This gives the lower profile in \eqref{eq:gate-profile}. Conversely any density $0\le h\le b=N/\kappa(p)$ of mass one is feasible with $x_A=b$ and $x_G=b/p$. At acquisition accept with probability $h/b$, otherwise return to the gate. The actual acceptance probability per acquisition is $1/b$, so its expected active-call count is $b$ and its expected raw cost is exactly $N$. Both gate and acquisition modes occur before every commitment, and their action sets are disjoint. Lemma~\ref{lem:labels} therefore charges two live labels even when all task values coincide. Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} and Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry} prove the claims.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Singular preparation and nonuniform waiting}
Let $K$ be a finite product of intervals and middle-third Cantor sets with their uniform and fair Cantor probability measures. The location exponent is the sum of one for each interval and $\log2/\log3$ for each Cantor factor. Indeed at $3^{-(j+1)}<r\le3^{-j}$ a ball meets a bounded number of level-$j$ cylinders, each of mass $2^{-j}$, while a cylinder a fixed number of levels deeper through its center lies inside the ball. This proves both mass bounds for one Cantor coordinate. Product Euclidean balls are sandwiched between fixed rescalings of product rectangles, proving regularity on $K$. Independently draw $T$ with $\Pp(T\le s)=s^\alpha$ and use $w=T^2$. Then \eqref{eq:rectangles} holds for the \emph{actual} prepared law.

The singular support and the metastable parameter $p$ enter different places: $d,\alpha$ determine the geometric loss, while $\kappa(p)$ counts the physical calls needed to acquire that law. Neither is discarded by conditioning on gate success. At fixed feasible $N/\kappa(p)$, increasing the output dictionary cannot remove the reliability floor. The proof thus treats a nonuniformly waiting singular class, rather than appending a singular interface to a smooth theorem.
