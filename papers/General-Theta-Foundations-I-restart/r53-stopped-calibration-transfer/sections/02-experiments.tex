\section{Prepared experiments and an observable protocol}
\label{sec:experiments}
All spaces are standard Borel. A preparation, a family of positive normalized causal kernels and a fixed bounded terminal audit are specified. A model parameter $\theta\in\Theta$ is fixed throughout a trajectory and is not available to the observer. The hidden physical state may include the entire microscopic past. The visible protocol mode belongs to $I=\{1,\ldots,L\}$ and starts at the known mode $i_0$. Mode $i$ admits a finite nonempty set of actions $A_i$. Costs $c_{ia}>0$ are known and are paid for every call, including a rejected one. Write $c_*=\min_{i,a}c_{ia}>0$.

The raw report is $(J,Y,V)$: $J$ is the next protocol mode, $Y$ is a primary report and $V$ is an additional informative report. Its joint law and the new hidden state are generated by the physical kernel, not by an observer. Let $\mathcal H_{t-1}$ include the complete visible past and all earlier private randomizations. We require an \emph{exact observable protocol projection}: for every model, every surviving legal history with mode $i$, and every selected action $a\in A_i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection}
 \law_\theta(J_t,Y_t\mid\mathcal H_{t-1},A_t=a)=P_{ia}(dj,dy).
\end{equation}
The probability measures $P_{ia}$ are known and common. No analogous independence is required for $V_t$ or for the post-report hidden state. There need be no common report support across different modes or actions. Equation~\eqref{eq:projection} is checked by marginalizing the original kernel on an invariant family of prepared physical laws; it is not inferred merely from the name ``protocol mode.'' Every row used on a reachable mode is an actual conditional acquisition law of that physical experiment.

Stopping is allowed only on a specified Borel set $D_{ia}\subset I\times Y_{ia}$. It is decided after the whole current report; outside $D_{ia}$ the observer must continue in the reported mode $J_t$. The audit $Z\in H$ is performed after commitment, with no intervening uncontrolled transition. Here $H\subset\R^k$ is compact and convex and contains all legal proxy and audit values. Projecting predictions onto $H$ cannot increase squared loss. Denote the conditional audit mean and total variance, given the entire pre-audit history including $(J,Y,V)$, by $\mu$ and $v$. These are regular conditional quantities, not probabilities assigned to continuous singletons.

\begin{definition}[Conditional calibration]\label{def:calibration}
Known Borel functions $f_{ia}(j,y)\in H$, $0\le w_{ia}(j,y)\le W$, and constants $B_0\ge0$, $a_v>0$, $A_v,a_b\ge0$ satisfy, after every legal surviving history and every model, on the stop-allowed domain,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:calibration}
 a_vw\le v-B_0\le A_vw,\qquad
 \|\mu-f\|^2\le a_bw.
\end{equation}
The inequalities hold almost everywhere under the conditional full-report law. In particular they remain true after arbitrary selection using the additional report $V$. Fresh routing coins are conditionally independent of the audit. Values of $f,w$ outside $D_{ia}$ can be chosen arbitrarily within the displayed bounds.
\end{definition}

For a raw report density $\lambda$, audit numerator $U$ and audit squared-norm numerator $T$, relative to any local dominating full-report measure, the verification consists of
\[
 a_vw\lambda^2\le T\lambda-\|U\|^2-B_0\lambda^2
       \le A_vw\lambda^2,\qquad
 \|U-\lambda f\|^2\le a_bw\lambda^2.
\]
These are imposed on a raw invariant family before any program or budget is chosen. At zero likelihood bounded tests force $U=T=0$ and no posterior is assigned. Uniformity here concerns actually possible conditional histories, not a formal ambient state manifold. The proxy $f$ and mode $i$ are not asserted to be the complete predictive quotient.

A checkpoint uses arbitrary legal history-dependent randomized actions and stopping, with $1\le\tau<\infty$ almost surely, and at commitment selects among at most $m$ fixed values in $H$. An autonomous observer has disjoint live and terminal labels, starts at one fixed live label, chooses its action from that label alone, and routes using only its live label, current action and current report. Each terminal label has one fixed response. All rules are parameter independent; no retained seed, old report, real posterior or free external mode/clock register is allowed. A physical protocol mode is available for future action selection only insofar as its report has been encoded in the live label. Illegal actions and unauthorized stops are excluded, not offered as free probes. A visit to mode $i$ means a live pre-call occurrence $I_{t-1}=i$ with $t\le\tau$; a next-mode mark on an accepted report is not by itself such a visit. Thus a positive-mass mode visit means a strictly positive expected call count there.

Let $R_\cp^\Theta(N,m)$ and $R_\aut^\Theta(N,M)$ be the infima of worst-model squared audit risk under
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
 \sup_{\theta}\E_\theta\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}c_{I_{t-1},A_t}\le N,
\end{equation}
with $M$ counting \emph{all} live and terminal labels. Infeasible values are $+\infty$. The baseline $B_0$ is the same fixed task constant before optimization, not a difference of two separately optimized Bayes risks. Preparation and audit costs are added explicitly when a total physical budget is stated.
