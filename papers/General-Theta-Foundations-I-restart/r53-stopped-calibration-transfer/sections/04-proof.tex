\section{Proof of the central theorem}
\begin{lemma}[Conditional risk and actual occupation]\label{lem:occupation}
At a permitted stop, for every legal committed prediction $r$,
\[
 k_-\{w+\|f-r\|^2\}\le
 \E[\|Z-r\|^2\mid\text{pre-audit information}]-B_0
 \le k_+\{w+\|f-r\|^2\}.
\]
Every finite-cost history-dependent procedure induces a feasible flow in \eqref{eq:flow-box}--\eqref{eq:flow-cost} with its actual terminal submeasures.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Conditional projection writes the middle expression as $v-B_0+\|\mu-r\|^2$. The upper follows from $\|\mu-r\|^2\le2\|f-r\|^2+2a_bw$. For the lower,
\[
 w+\|f-r\|^2\le(1+2a_b)w+2\|\mu-r\|^2
 \le k_-^{-1}\{v-B_0+\|\mu-r\|^2\}.
\]
Conditioning on a fresh response coin does not change the audit law.

For the second assertion fix a model and put
\[
 x_{ia}=\E\sum_{t\le\tau}\one_{\{I_{t-1}=i,A_t=a\}},\quad
 \zeta_{ia}(B)=\Pp(I_{\tau-1}=i,A_\tau=a,(J_\tau,Y_\tau)\in B).
\]
The count bound follows from $c_*>0$. Predictability before the report and \eqref{eq:projection} give the full acquired report-count measure exactly as $x_{ia}P_{ia}$. The terminal count is dominated by that count, so $\zeta_{ia}=h_{ia}P_{ia}$ with $0\le h_{ia}\le x_{ia}$ and the stop restriction gives its support. The number of visits to mode $j$ is its initial visit plus the number of \emph{continued} reports announcing $j$. Taking expectations proves \eqref{eq:flow-balance}. Almost-sure termination gives mass one. No conditional law after rejection has been substituted for a raw law.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-flow realization without phantom circulation]\label{lem:realization}
Every finite feasible $(x,h)$ is realized in terminal law by a stationary randomized protocol program whose mode--action counts are at most $x_{ia}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a mode with $x_i>0$ choose action $a$ with probability $p_i(a)=x_{ia}/x_i$. If $x_{ia}>0$, accept primary report $(j,y)$ with probability $h_{ia}(j,y)/x_{ia}$; otherwise that action is never selected. Upon acceptance route to a nearest codepoint, with a fixed Borel tie rule. Upon rejection store mode $j$. Ignore the additional report $V$. On zero-count modes choose any legal row; they will not be reached from $i_0$.

The substochastic continuation matrix on positive-count modes is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
 Q_{ij}=\frac1{x_i}\sum_a\int_{\{j\}\times Y_{ia}}(x_{ia}-h_{ia})\,dP_{ia}.
\end{equation}
The balance equation is $x=e_{i_0}+xQ$, with row-vector convention. Hence
\[
 \bar x:=\sum_{n\ge0}e_{i_0}Q^n\le x.
\]
Its finite total mass proves finite expected duration and almost-sure stopping for the constructed program. Equation~\eqref{eq:projection} makes this continuation law the same for all hidden models.

The nonnegative residual $z=x-\bar x$ satisfies $z=zQ$. Summing its coordinates gives
\[
 \sum_i z_i(1-Q_i\mathbf1)=0.
\]
Every summand is nonnegative. Thus $z_i=0$ whenever mode $i$ has positive acceptance probability; when its acceptance probability is zero, every $h_{ia}$ is zero almost everywhere. Consequently the realized terminal density
\[
 \bar x_i p_i(a)\frac{h_{ia}}{x_{ia}}
       =\frac{\bar x_i}{x_i}h_{ia}
\]
is exactly $h_{ia}$ in all cases, with zero-count cases read as zero. Actual action counts are $\bar x_i p_i(a)\le x_{ia}$. Unreachable closed classes may therefore have appeared in the finite balance witness, but they contribute neither terminal mass nor risk and are removed in its execution.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Risk bounds and attainment]
For a checkpoint dictionary $C$, Lemma~\ref{lem:occupation} bounds the risk of its actual response below by the integral of $k_-d_{ia,C}$ against its actual terminal law. Taking the profile minimum, then the worst model and the infimum over common programs proves the first inequality of \eqref{eq:cp}. Any autonomous program has at least one live label, so its terminal dictionary has at most $M-1$ elements. This proves the first inequality of \eqref{eq:aut}.

For existence, $0\le x_{ia}\le N/c_*$ and $0\le h_{ia}\le N/c_*$. Use the compact count box and the finite product of weak-star compact balls in $L^\infty(P_{ia})$. Pointwise inequalities, the permitted-stop restriction, balance and resource constraints are weak-star closed by testing against nonnegative $L^1$ functions and the finitely many mode indicators. The dictionary lies in compact $H^m$. Boundedness of $f$ implies uniform convergence of the squared-distance functions when centers converge. The bounded objective is therefore jointly continuous in these topologies. A nonempty feasible set has a minimizer. This argument does not require compactness of a space of arbitrary infinite stopped trajectories.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:realization} to that minimizer. Its terminal submeasures are exactly the chosen $h_{ia}P_{ia}$, its costs are no greater, and conditional risk is at most $k_+d_{ia,C}$. This proves the checkpoint upper and the autonomous upper with $L$ live labels and $m$ terminal labels. Under \eqref{eq:exact-risk} the two conditional comparisons are identities, giving the exact checkpoint formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Mandatory action signatures charge live states]\label{lem:labels}
Suppose $b$ modes are visited with positive probability before commitment by every legal observer under one common model, and their permitted action sets are pairwise disjoint. Every such autonomous observer uses at least $b$ live labels.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a live label $s$, the support of its fixed action row must lie in $A_i$ whenever that label is actually used in mode $i$ with positive probability. The same label cannot serve two of the stated modes: the support would lie in an empty intersection. Each mode has a positive-mass live label because the alphabet is finite. These labels are distinct. The argument does not use a free current-mode register, and an illegal action is not a permissible diagnostic experiment.
\end{proof}

Applying the lemma with $b=L$ gives the strengthened lower in Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}; the exact autonomous conclusion follows from \eqref{eq:exact-risk}. More generally it gives $k_-\Psi_{M-b}$ whenever $b$ mandatory incompatible modes are certified.

\begin{example}[Two false relaxations]\label{ex:circulation}
First, take an initial mode that stops on its first report and a second, unreachable self-looping mode. The balance equations allow any finite positive occupation in the second mode, with zero terminal mass there. It is a circulation, not a second acquired population. Lemma~\ref{lem:realization} removes it exactly. Assigning terminal mass to it would violate balance.

Second, let a deterministic protocol require actions $a_1,\ldots,a_L$ in that order, with disjoint singleton action sets and stopping allowed only after the last action. All reports can have constant task proxy and zero risk mark. A one-point geometric cover is then exact, but fewer than $L$ live labels cannot execute the protocol. A cover or rowwise zero distortion alone is not a causal implementation.
\end{example}
