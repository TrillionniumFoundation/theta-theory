\section{Executable tests and predictive realization}
\label{sec:quotient}
The protocol projection is not a replacement for the predictive quotient. Starting from the prepared kernel, let $\mathcal T_i$ be a family of bounded executable future tests closed under legal one-call continuation, finite randomization and the bounded monotone limits being used. Include report events, audit coordinates and the legal action signature. For a visible history $h$ define its evaluation vector $q(h)=(\E[T\mid h])_{T\in\mathcal T_i}$. Two histories are equivalent only if their signatures and all these predictions agree. Unknown-model and fixed-prior interpretations are distinct: uniform predictions retain the family indexed by $\theta$, while a Bayesian quotient requires a stated prior on the joint model and hidden state.

\begin{proposition}[Realized predictive quotient]\label{prop:quotient}
Assume a countable determining family of tests, Borel evaluation, a Borel attained image $Q$, and a Borel section of the evaluation map on that image. Suppose one-step report kernels and next evaluations depend only on the current evaluation and compatible action, as verified by the unnormalized test closure. Then $Q$ carries a Borel predictive update and is minimal among Borel statistics that determine the same executable tests and admit the corresponding measurable factorization. This statement does not assert that every measurable quotient is standard Borel.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Countably many Borel evaluations form a Borel map into a countable product of bounded intervals. Test closure implies that equivalent histories have the same next-report probabilities and the same normalized next-test values at positive evidence. Select a representative with the assumed Borel section and apply the raw update there. The result is independent of the representative, and the section makes it Borel jointly with the permitted action. Zero-evidence reports do not require an arbitrarily chosen posterior. Induction establishes equality of every finite future test law; the declared monotone closure extends this to the stated test class. Conversely different evaluations are separated by a determining test. A statistic sufficient for that class has fibers contained in evaluation fibers, so the quotient factors through it; measurability uses the factorization condition in the statement.
\end{proof}

In the finite hidden-state and finite Hermitian realizations, unnormalized positive coordinates and explicit finite audit effects give a concrete compact carrier. Quotient directions that no admitted future test separates are removed; visible mode and active audit registers are retained. Constant density or finite-dimensionality alone is not invoked as a measurable-image theorem. If a global standard Borel quotient has not been established, the full posterior on the stated standard Borel hidden space remains a valid implementation for defining conditional tests, but is not handed to a finite observer for free. Generalization from finite matrices to unbounded operator closures requires additional mathematics.
