\section{Introduction}
A preparation, waiting period, measurement and terminal readout need not have interchangeable roles. A favorable observation may be reachable only after a long causal protocol. A law placed directly on favorable observations forgets the cost of reaching them; an observer that is handed the current protocol stage without retaining it forgets a different resource. The distinction persists when the protected physical state evolves and the full report remains informative.

We study the passage from positive prepared kernels and executable future tests to actually acquired task geometry, a finite-state causal implementation and a common decision risk. The central object is not a prescribed manifold or covariance. It is the terminal part of a finite-cost occupation flow, marked by an observable prediction proxy and its certified residual risk. Flow conservation records which reports can actually follow which protocol stages. A raw conditional calibration certificate compares this marked distortion with the protected future test after every legal history, including histories selected by earlier rejections.

Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} gives both an unrestricted-history lower bound and a common finite-state upper bound. The experiment has $L$ observable protocol modes, whose marginal transition/report law is closed at the raw-kernel level. The remaining report can depend on the hidden state and on its posterior. The implementing program uses at most $L$ live labels and $m$ fixed terminal values. No approximate posterior, old report tape, external clock or persistent random seed is supplied. When every mode must occur and distinct modes require incompatible actions, $L$ live labels are also necessary. In that case the same marked-flow profile bounds both architectures at exactly the same acquisition and terminal-label budgets.

The converse realization requires care. A finite solution of balance equations may include circulation in an unvisited recurrent class. Such circulation is not acquired information. We prove that its removal preserves \emph{all terminal submeasures} and decreases every nonnegative additive resource. Thus the profile is not merely a formal optimization on reachable points, and no stationary-distribution or uniform-absorption assumption is inserted to identify it with an execution law.

The geometric consequence retains two independent obstructions. Low residual uncertainty of order $s^2$ may have mass of order $s^\alpha$, while locating a representable prediction inside radius $r$ has mass of order $r^d$. These actual masses give the two terms of the matching law. We then place this geometry behind a preparation gate of success probability $p$. Each measurement attempt costs exactly $1+1/p$ raw calls in mean, including rejected attempts. Theorem~\ref{thm:gate} preserves that factor rather than absorbing it into a constant. It therefore applies uniformly along metastable families with $p\downarrow0$, provided the scaled budget remains feasible. Cantor product preparations give noninteger $d$ in the same proof.

Two raw realizations retain information that the finite program does not store: a controlled hidden Markov sign and a coherently evolving qubit observed by noncommuting weak instruments. In both, the protocol projection is exact but the posterior is not recovered from that projection or the current proxy. The protected risk comparison, not posterior recovery, is the link used by the central theorem. A separate killed-potential estimate permits finite-precision implementation even in protocol modes where stopping is forbidden.

\subsection*{Relation to established methods}
Occupation measures and stationary randomized realization are classical tools of constrained Markov decision theory~\cite{Altman,DP}. We do not claim those tools or conditional projection as new. Compactness of occupation measures is delicate outside the finite, bounded-cost setting~\cite{PZ}; the proof below uses bounded finite-mode counts and domination by fixed raw report measures, not a general compactness theorem for stopped strategic laws. Trimmed quantization and selective classification already optimize distortion or uncertainty under mass restrictions~\cite{CGM,Franc}. The present theorem couples those restrictions to causal balance, unknown hidden dynamics, legal action signatures and a complete observer state count.

Value-directed belief compression~\cite{PB} uses a task-sensitive representation; Bayes-risk quantization has a Bregman interpretation~\cite{Varshney}. These facts do not alone provide the stopped all-history converse or pay for a protocol controller. Policy-conditional finite-memory approximation~\cite{Kim} compares values under a specified execution, whereas the lower bound here permits arbitrary legal history-dependent sensing and stopping. The full predictive quotient remains a separate, explicitly realized information object~\cite{SC}.

The one-protocol marked comparison, the reliability--resolution exponent, and the Gaussian all-history and two-state consequences are retained with complete proofs in the stopped-experiment companion~\cite{GTF52}. The present contribution is their finite-protocol causal completion, including circulation removal, necessary live-state charges, a waiting-rate-uniform realization and drift-based resource perturbation. The raw projection and calibration hypotheses are substantive sufficient conditions. They are not a classification of arbitrary causal experiments or a claim to have learned an unknown kernel.
