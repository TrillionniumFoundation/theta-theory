\begin{abstract}
For prepared causal experiments with a finite observable protocol projection and a history-uniform conditional calibration certificate, we prove a two-sided resource--risk transfer theorem. The full report may remain informative about an evolving hidden state; neither the current proxy nor the protocol mode is assumed to recover the posterior. Actual stopped occupation satisfies a killed-flow constraint. Conversely every finite feasible flow has a common causal realization with the same terminal measure and no larger cost, after unvisited circulations are removed. One live label per protocol mode and the stated output labels suffice. Mandatory incompatible actions give a matching lower count. For a metastable preparation--measurement protocol the resulting sharp law is the maximum of $(N/\kappa)^{-2/\alpha}$ and $(Nm/\kappa)^{-2/(d+\alpha)}$, with the physical waiting cost $\kappa=1+1/p$ explicit, uniformly as the gate probability $p$ tends to zero. Controlled hidden-state and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the same theorem, also on singular acquired supports. A killed-potential perturbation estimate controls numerical error and mean cost without a positive stop hazard at every call. Calibration, simulation deficiency, persistent labels, program description and temporary computation remain separately accounted for.
\end{abstract}
