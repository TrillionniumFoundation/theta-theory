\section{Normalized experiments and the central theorem}\label{sec:setup}
\subsection{Raw tests and their affine closure}
Fix a known preparation and a prescribed finite report protocol of length $n\ge1$. All physical state, action and report spaces are standard Borel. The visible report law is generated by normalized positive causal kernels; positive operator instruments are also allowed through their ordinary classical report laws. One preparation, $n$ reports, and one environment-owned terminal audit are $N=n+2$ paid physical calls. The answer is committed before the audit. That audit is not a report available to the observer.

A finite \emph{executable affine closure certificate} consists of the constant test and $k$ bounded test coordinates, a compact convex prediction domain $K_t\subset\R^k$ for each phase, a Borel realization of those coordinates, and, for $\lambda_t$-almost every report $y$, affine maps
\[
 \ell_t(y,u)=\ell_t^0(y)+c_t(y)u,\qquad
 U_t(y,u)=a_t(y)+A_t(y)u.
\]
The raw joint report/test identities give, for every realized incoming prediction $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:instrument}
 \Pp(Y_t\in dy\mid u)=\ell_t(y,u)\lambda_t(dy),\qquad
 F_t(y,u)=\frac{U_t(y,u)}{\ell_t(y,u)}\in K_t.
\end{equation}
In particular $\int\ell_t(y,u)\lambda_t(dy)=1$. The reference measure may be sigma-finite. Likelihoods are positive on a common full-measure set for the main resource theorem. The identity theorem permits zero likelihood, using the closed convex perspective and setting its value to zero when both numerator and likelihood vanish. The identities hold on the whole declared domain, not only at the states of a proposed quantizer. They can be verified by pulling the raw instrument back on the finite tests. All matrix coordinates are obtained from those tests; they are not postulated state geometry.

Write $\mathcal H_t$ for the complete report history and $X_t$ for its predictive vector. Then $X_t=F_t(Y_t,X_{t-1})$, with known $X_0$. A performed bounded vector audit $Z_n$ satisfies $\E[Z_n\mid\mathcal H_n]=X_n$. Its Euclidean squared norm is the fixed task metric, with response set $K_n$; a larger closed convex response set containing $K_n$ gives the same optimum. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baseline}
 \nu_t=\Law(X_t),\qquad
 B_n=\E\norm{Z_n-X_n}^2.
\end{equation}
These are laws and risks under the actual preparation and protocol, independent of the observer. There is no infimum over a different sensing protocol hidden in $B_n$.

\begin{lemma}[Realized predictive quotient]\label{lem:quotient}
Suppose the declared future tests contain the coordinate tests and their legal report continuations, and their evaluations are determined by the affine instrument certificate. If the evaluation image is standard Borel and has a Borel realization, equality of the coordinates and of the visible action/clock signature is exactly predictive equivalence for these tests. The quotient is measurable, realizable and minimal among Borel statistics sufficient for them. Jointly Borel action-indexed certificates descend with the legal action signature unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Iterating the unnormalized instrument identities determines the probabilities of all finite report words and all declared terminal test expectations. A monotone-class argument extends from a countable generating family of report events to its sigma-field. Conversely the included coordinate tests distinguish different vectors. Any sufficient Borel statistic carries each coordinate as a Borel function, and hence carries their finite vector. Evaluation at the assumed Borel realization gives descended kernels, independently of the representative; composition preserves joint action measurability. The interface signature prevents identifying histories with different legal continuations. These assertions concern the declared test family, not unmodeled physical tasks.
\end{proof}
For the finite hidden-state and density-operator models below, mixtures realize the entire compact convex domain. The deterministic map sending a state coordinate to its physical preparation is a Borel realization. Restricting to a Borel attained image gives the same task quotient; a formal ambient set is never substituted for its acquired probability law.

\subsection{Finite observers and certificates}
A scheduled observer has finite alphabets $S_t$, $|S_t|\le m_t$, and Borel rows $k_t(j\mid s,y)$. Only $S_{t-1}$, $Y_t$ and fresh independent randomness are available at the update. A persistent seed, history tape, current exact posterior or real-valued auxiliary register is not free. Put
\[
 V_t=\E[X_t\mid S_t],\qquad e_t=\E\norm{V_t}^2,
 \qquad r_t=\E\norm{X_t-V_t}^2.
\]
The best response based on $S_n$ is $V_n$, and total risk is $B_n+r_n$. Zero-mass labels are discarded in sums. A fixed Borel program may contain exact constants; its bit implementation is a separate resource statement in Section~\ref{sec:resources}.

An autonomous observer has one finite label set, one reused report row, one initial law and a halt/readout map. It must itself halt exactly after $n$ acquisitions almost surely. The physical experiment continues to make reports available until it halts. No external end marker or phase is supplied. All labels, including phases and halt labels, count toward $M$. The report spaces are then one common space with equivalent positive conditional laws at every possible phase and history. This is a support condition, not equality of report probabilities.

For $u\in K_{t-1}$ let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-law}
 \mu_{t,u}=(F_t(\cdot,u))_\#\bigl(\ell_t(\cdot,u)\lambda_t\bigr),
 \qquad \Phi_t(u)=\int\norm z^2\,\mu_{t,u}(dz).
\end{equation}
At an actual incoming label with $V_{t-1}=u$, $\mu_{t,u}$ is the law of the prediction after seeing the report but before replacing the label. A uniform certificate on $K_{t-1}$ is a way of controlling every actual such row, not a claim that every point of $K_{t-1}$ has been acquired.

Fix $d>0$. The geometric certificates, with constants independent of $n,t$ and the observer, are:
\begin{align}
 \mu_{t,u}(B(z,h))&\le C_{\rm mass}h^d
 &&(u\in K_{t-1},\ z\in\R^k,\ 0<h\le h_0),\label{eq:mass}\\
 \mathcal N(K_t,\operatorname{dist}_t,h)&\le C_{\rm cov}h^{-d}.
 \label{eq:cover}
\end{align}
The cover is global and consists of legal representatives in $K_t$; by enlarging constants it supplies an at-most-$m$ codebook of radius $C m^{-1/d}$ for every integer $m\ge1$. Distances and diameters are uniformly bounded and $\norm{u-v}\le C\operatorname{dist}_t(u,v)$. In the curvature alternative below, $\operatorname{dist}_t$ is Euclidean. Report continuity is certified separately by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:report-continuity}
 \norm{\ell_t(\cdot,u)\lambda_t-\ell_t(\cdot,v)\lambda_t}_{\TV}
 \le L_{\rm rep}\norm{u-v}.
\end{equation}
It is needed for robust implementation and composition, not to replace the actual measure in~\eqref{eq:mass}.

One of the following \emph{stability certificates} is required for the matching upper.
\begin{description}
\item[(C)] $\Phi_t$ is twice differentiable on an open relative neighborhood of $K_{t-1}$ (or by continuous extension from its relative interior), with
\[
 \tfrac12D^2\Phi_t(u)[v,v]\le b_t\norm v^2,
 \qquad \prod_{j=s+1}^{t}b_j\le C_0\rho^{t-s},\quad 0<\rho<1.
\]
The derivative interchange in~\eqref{eq:hessian} is justified by a declared integrable bound. Convexity supplies the lower sign; no positive Hessian lower is required.
\item[(P)] The executable normalized update satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_t(F_t(y,u),F_t(y,v))
 \le a_t\operatorname{dist}_{t-1}(u,v),
 \qquad \prod_{j=s+1}^{t}a_j\le C_0\rho^{t-s}
\]
for common nonexceptional reports and all $u,v$. Individual $a_t$ may equal or exceed one. A global Borel nearest-cell rule for~\eqref{eq:cover} is part of the certificate.
\end{description}
Empty products equal one; $C_0\ge1$. These are experiment-level conditions. They do not assert that every positive experiment is stable.

\begin{theorem}[Bayesian causal acquired-geometry transfer]\label{thm:main}
For every prepared experiment satisfying the affine test certificate, every horizon $n\ge1$, and every scheduled Borel randomized finite observer, the following exact identity holds at each $1\le t\le n$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-identity}
 r_t=\E\Phi_t(X_{t-1})-\E\Phi_t(V_{t-1})
       +D_t^{\rm row}+D_t^{\rm pool}.
\end{equation}
The last two terms are the nonnegative conditional-square losses defined in~\eqref{eq:defects}. The first term is a nonnegative conditional Jensen defect. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-converse}
 r_t\ge\sum_s\Pp(S_{t-1}=s)q_{m_t}(\mu_{t,V_{t-1}(s)}).
\end{equation}
When differentiation is justified, the likelihood-induced curvature is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hessian}
 \tfrac12D^2\Phi_t(u)[v,v]
 =\int \ell_t(y,u)\norm{D_uF_t(y,u)v}^2\,\lambda_t(dy).
\end{equation}

Assume, in addition,~\eqref{eq:mass}--\eqref{eq:report-continuity}, the global domain/cover conditions, and (C) or (P). There are constants $0<c\le C<\infty$, depending only on the displayed certificate constants, such that for all $n\ge1$, $m\ge1$ and $M\ge n+1$,
\begin{align}
 \calR_{\rm cp}(n,m)&=B_n+q_m(\nu_n),
 &c m^{-2/d}&\le q_m(\nu_n)\le C m^{-2/d},\label{eq:checkpoint}\\
 c(M-n)^{-2/d}&\le\calR_{\rm aut}(n,M)-B_n
 & &\le C(M-n)^{-2/d}.
 \label{eq:autonomous}
\end{align}
For~\eqref{eq:autonomous} require the autonomous common-support interface just specified. The autonomous class is empty for $M<n+1$ and nonempty for $M\ge n+1$. Its infimum is over all legal Borel randomized programs, not just the quantizers constructed in the proof. Both upper bounds are achieved up to the displayed constants by legal finite-state programs. In (P) the state carries a codepoint label; in (C) it carries a calibrated-cell label. Their programs and output precision are not included in the number of labels.
\end{theorem}
The first part is proved in Section~\ref{sec:defect}; the matching statements in Section~\ref{sec:transfer}. The theorem permits positive informative likelihoods, nonhomogeneous acquired laws and block, rather than per-step, stability. It does not claim an unrestricted optimal-control theorem: the preparation and physical report protocol are fixed before optimizing the observer. A controller reading additional history would change the experiment and requires a separate proof.
