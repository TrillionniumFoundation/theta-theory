## Intrinsic completion rather than only a stable sufficient route

Theorem 3.1 now gives necessary and sufficient allocation conditions on a compact metrizable predictive carrier, possibly infinite-dimensional. The external observer's finite law must be a barycentric contraction of its own acquisition law. For a stationary autonomous observer, the same report-to-label kernel must solve all phase moment constraints simultaneously. A support-function inequality tests exactly this common-program requirement. The maximum lies outside the phase sum. Separate per-phase convex-order comparisons do not suffice; Remark 5.2 gives a strict counterexample.

The criterion does not assume density, strong concavity or contraction. Its variables are finite occupancy laws, actions and predictive barycenters rather than an unavailable full-history posterior tape. The proof supplies measurable implementing kernels by compact separation and all-incoming calibration. It characterizes the declared compact predictive class, not every standard-Borel quotient; Appendix A proves a raw-kernel sufficient realization and explicitly separates the extra weak-Feller property used for continuous value functions.

Theorem 4.4 strengthens candidate-wise separation. For fixed external widths an optimum is attained. Finite polyhedral test relaxations have values increasing to that optimum, and any strictly smaller target is excluded by a finite collection of tests uniformly over the entire candidate parameter set. There is no claimed effective bound on the number of tests, and no analogous general compact-attainment claim is made for arbitrary stationary report kernels.

## Timing interface and genuine predictive memory

Definition 2.1 writes one stationary tuple, including its halt set, action probabilities, next-label kernel and readout. Fresh coins are erased; seed, instruction pointer, readable output and clock are persistent if retained. Legality must hold at every positive-mass occurrence of a reused label.

Proposition 5.3 proves both interface conversions and, under common report null sets, an exact variational conversion. The conventional externally clocked rate is displayed alongside the internally timed rate in Theorem 6.1. Mutual absolute continuity, not a quantitative likelihood-ratio floor, is what the clock converse needs. Report time stamps do not invalidate the central simultaneous-allocation theorem; they only remove that special clock lower.

Theorem 8.1 responds to the request for a converse beyond a deadline count. In a capture-and-blind-transport experiment, every intermediate alphabet must preserve the acquired response. Even with a free external clock, the optimal risk is the quantization error at the smallest intermediate alphabet. Under the internal clock, the exact risk is `q_floor((M-1)/n)(mu)`. This is not the R15 `(M-n)` law: predictive resolution itself must be carried at every stage. The actual law may be fractal or countably atomic, and the update is noncontracting. Corollary 8.2 proves both a fractal power law and an exponential countable-atomic law. This does not purport to solve an externally recognized open nonlinear filtering problem.

## Quantitative consequences, conventional tasks and preservation

Theorem 6.1 retains and re-proves the R15 smoothing/block-stability mechanism. The all-state/all-action density and full-curvature assumptions are not weakened silently. They are now sufficient conditions for estimating the exact frontier, not premises of the general criterion. The boundary-valid curvature argument keeps the off-carrier grid center distinct from the on-carrier programmed barycenter. Its constants, Euclidean volume and norm comparison are stated.

Theorems 7.2 and 7.3 verify the same regular theorem for untruncated Gaussian hidden-state reports under ordinary categorical Brier filtering, and for a positive noncommuting instrument under Brier prediction of a fixed informationally complete POVM. Finite dimension, known kernels, positive preparation and spanning effects remain explicit. We claim neither arbitrary quantum-control completion nor a nonlinear/nonparametric filtering theorem. The report-readable finite-stratum result keeps actual submass, and the blind example adds countably accumulating singular acquisition beyond that finite-stratum corollary.

## Execution and the multi-resource boundary

Propositions 9.1–9.2 keep parameter-independent action/report/clock/readout compatibility and the complete stopped scored law in the error statements. Persistent predictor and simulator labels multiply; bounded-loss complete-law errors add. A one-way simulator does not yield a reverse lower. Continuous-kernel numerical execution remains conditional on explicit routines and their certificates.

Theorem 9.3 gives a further effective result for finite rational raw experiments. The admissible finite machine set is a compact semialgebraic set; a risk minimizer can be selected by real-algebraic decision methods. Support-preserving rational rounding creates no new illegal or premature-stopping path and has an explicit finite-horizon total-variation loss. Programme bits, workspace, expected random-bit generation and bounded-trial fallback are separate. This is a decidability and feasible-execution theorem, not polynomial-time synthesis or a matched optimal complexity region.

Corollary 9.4 uses attained erasure and unobservable calibration alternatives in one scored task. The final physical audit occurs after the predictor has fixed its decisions and halted. Therefore `N=n+2` physical calls correspond to `N-1` predictor transitions; this convention is explicit, not an uncharged terminal signal inside the engine. The early output is write-only and cannot store information for later use.
