## New general mathematics and proof boundary

Theorem 3.1 proves an exact causal-allocation and resource-risk criterion on the declared compact metrizable convex predictive class, with finite legal action sets, standard Borel reports, affine unnormalized instruments and a continuous bounded terminal loss affine in the predictive state. Its central realization criterion does not require acquired densities, contraction, finite-dimensional closure or full task curvature. Appendix A, Theorem A.1, constructs a measurable minimal predictive quotient from compact raw experiments and a separating, pullback-stable continuous executable-test space; it does not assert such a quotient for arbitrary Borel kernels.

For an externally clocked observer the actual pre-pooling acquired law lambda at each cut must dominate the proposed finite retained law nu in convex order. The finite-target allocation mechanism is explicitly attributed to Strassen and re-proved. For an autonomous machine a single phase-independent action/stop/report-transition/readout tuple is used: the simultaneous dual inequality has the maximum over destination labels AFTER summing phase contributions. Separate one-step convex-order feasibility is insufficient when an unobserved phase is reused. The proof constructs a common measurable transition kernel or a finite affine separating witness. Auxiliary conditional predictive states are programme constants, not a free persistent posterior register.

For either declared interface the optimized risk equals B_n^* plus its feasible acquired-allocation frontier. Under the actually deployed law its excess is exactly the sum of nonnegative Bellman action slacks and continuation Jensen defects, plus any terminal readout slack. This is not a comparison of separately optimized shadow controllers. General measurable synthesis is not efficient finite-bit synthesis.

Theorem 4.4 adds a global result for the compact weakly continuous EXTERNALLY CLOCKED class: finite polyhedral-test relaxations have optimal values increasing to the true optimum. Every strictly suboptimal risk target is excluded by a finite family of acquired-law inequalities applying to every candidate flow, not merely to one proposed encoder. No effective size bound or general autonomous attainment is claimed.

Proposition 5.3 states the exact interface conversion. Under common report null sets, positive cut supports of an internally exact-n stationary machine are disjoint, so its risk is the infimum of external profile risks over 1+sum(m_t)<=M. The autonomous class is nonempty exactly when M>=n+1. A timestamp-bearing report or a free external stage index invalidates this clock converse, not the general simultaneous-allocation criterion.

## Quantitative consequences, realizations and singular acquired laws

The inherited R15 mass-curvature/stability mechanism is re-proved as Theorem 6.1 and derived from the general allocation theorem. Under all-state/all-action acquired density, full terminal Bayes-risk curvature, report continuity and summable block sensitivity, checkpoint and external excess are of order M^(-2/d), while internally exact-n autonomous excess is of order (M-n)^(-2/d), uniformly in n and M. These additional quantitative hypotheses remain visible and are not consequences of positivity alone.

Theorem 7.2 verifies the assumptions from untruncated Gaussian hidden-state kernels, including the global posterior density bound up to the simplex boundary and explicit transition/contrast nondegeneracy. ## Resource and error composition

Propositions 9.1-9.2 give parameter-independent typed causal simulation and numerical first-mismatch bounds in the same complete stopped/scored task. Action compatibility, report transformation, strategy lift, internal simulator state, clock and task readout are explicit. Defects add while predictor and simulator state alphabets multiply; a one-way upper does not imply a reverse lower. Programme bits, workspace, precision, raw calls and physical time are distinct resources.

Theorem 9.3 adds an effective compiler for finite rational raw kernels: fixed-budget feasibility and minimum risk reduce to the standard real-closed-field decision machinery. Support-preserving denominator-Q rounding introduces no forbidden path and therefore preserves legality and the exact deadline, with explicit risk, programme, workspace and sampling bounds. The decision machinery is standard and credited. The theorem is not a polynomial-time synthesis or a matched full bit-complexity result. Continuous-report algorithms still need the stated integration/Bellman/coordinate certificates.

Corollary 9.4 attains N, M, delta and epsilon in one task using an actual early erasure and an unobservable calibration alternative. N=n+2 counts the early observation, n engine calls and the final paid audit; the predictor halts after N-1 calls, before the audit. For M>=N>=3 the regular autonomous excess is of order (M-N+1)^(-2/d)+delta^2+epsilon. In the blind case it is exactly q_floor((M-2)/(N-2))(mu)+delta^2+epsilon/2. The early causal deficiency is exactly epsilon. These are actual interfaces and alternatives, not arbitrary error tolerances automatically converted into lower bounds.
