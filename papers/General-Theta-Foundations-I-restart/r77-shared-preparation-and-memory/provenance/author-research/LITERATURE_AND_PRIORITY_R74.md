## 2. Symmetry, positive factorizations, and terminal contrast

### GPT13 — symmetric cone lifts are established theory

J. Gouveia, P. A. Parrilo and R. R. Thomas, *Lifts of convex sets and cone factorizations*, Mathematics of Operations Research **38**(2) (2013), 248–264. DOI: [10.1287/moor.1120.0575](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.1120.0575). [Primary author text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.3164).

The full primary preprint was opened; Theorems 2.4 and 2.12 were inspected. They connect cone lifts with factorizations of slack operators and treat equivariance. R74 therefore does not claim to introduce symmetric positive factorizations. Its assertions concern the particular all-word stochastic realization invariant inherited from R73: a common finite group action, probability-valued preparation and decoder, the exact minimum permutation-module value at small terminal contrast, and the prescribed-start charge. A finite response table is not automatically the complete slack operator of its residual body; that identification is not silently assumed.

### KPT10 — the full journal article is from 2012

V. Kaibel, K. Pashkovich and D. O. Theis, *Symmetry matters for sizes of extended formulations*, SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics **26**(3) (2012), 1361–1382. DOI: [10.1137/110839813](https://doi.org/10.1137/110839813). [Primary author text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0911.3712).

The key `KPT10` is retained as an identifier, not a publication-year assertion. The preliminary IPCO version appeared in 2010, LNCS 6080, 135–148. The [publisher record](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/110839813) confirms the full article's 2012 volume and pages. Definition 11 and Lemma 12 discuss coordinate-symmetric sections. Symmetry constraints can change positive-lift complexity; that broad phenomenon is established. The specific R74 step is that normal orbit stabilizers allow preparation to be absorbed into a bounded quotient decoder without increasing the counted core. This yields the exact subgroup-family LP, rather than a generic claim that all symmetric factorizations are linear programs.

### GillisGlineur12 — restricted and unrestricted positive factorizations differ

N. Gillis and F. Glineur, *On the geometric interpretation of the nonnegative rank*, Linear Algebra and its Applications **437**(11) (2012), 2685–2712. DOI: [10.1016/j.laa.2012.06.038](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2012.06.038). [Primary author text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.0880).

The primary preprint and its restricted-rank formulation were inspected. A factorization whose hidden columns are confined to the target column space imposes an additional restriction; general positive realizations cannot be identified with that geometry without proof. R74 retains the R73 common-action normal form and does not project every hidden response into the reached observable plane. The contrast construction embeds the observable module into a permutation module and builds legal probabilities there. The quotient LP is justified by a separate normality argument. The DOI ends in **038**; an erroneous **031** suffix encountered in a development bibliography was corrected.

### BocciCarliniRapallo11 — mixing and perturbation persistence are not new principles

DOI: [10.1137/110825455](https://doi.org/10.1137/110825455). [Primary author text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.3566).

Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 3.2 were inspected; the [publisher record](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/110825455) was checked. The paper treats local nondecrease and barycentric perturbations of ordinary nonnegative rank. R74 makes no general first claim for positivity under mixing or persistence of a rank obstruction. Its additional conclusions are the exact group permutation-module saturation value, attained action-budget endpoints, and explicit quotient payoff certificates. The C2^4 perturbation radius is a sufficient quantitative certificate for tables on the same command quotient; it is not asserted sharp, nor does it give a matching upper for all nearby tables.

## 4. Physical dictionaries are an inherited resource, distinct from abstract memory

### AubrunSzarek17 — convex approximation of density matrices is established and stronger in dimension

G. Aubrun and S. J. Szarek, *Dvoretzky's theorem and the complexity of entanglement detection*, Discrete Analysis (2017), paper 1, 20 pp. DOI: [10.19086/da.1242](https://doi.org/10.19086/da.1242). [Primary author text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.00578).

Section 6 and Proposition 10 were inspected. The latter gives pure-state convex approximations with exponential dependence on dimension for a fixed shrinkage. R68's elementary net bound is therefore not presented as the best available dimension dependence. Its declared small-noise exponent concerns one physical dictionary covering every state in the shrunken density body, and the R68 proof remains current. An arbitrary stochastic simulator for one response may use nonphysical intermediate response vectors, so that dictionary lower bound cannot be substituted for an unrestricted memory lower. The rediscovered R74 dictionary draft added no independent contribution and was removed from the active increment.

### Fawzi21 — extension complexity is a separate resource

H. Fawzi, *On polyhedral approximations of the positive semidefinite cone*, Mathematics of Operations Research **46**(4) (2021), 1479–1489. DOI: [10.1287/moor.2020.1077](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.2020.1077). [Primary author text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.09649); [publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.2020.1077).

Theorems 1 and 2 and the related-work discussion were inspected. They prove extension-complexity lower bounds for polyhedral approximations of a positive-semidefinite trace-one section. The paper also distinguishes vertex counts and lifted descriptions. R74 retains that distinction: a universal physical dictionary, the number of live stochastic labels in a particular experiment, and the size of a lifted linear description are different optimization problems. No extension-complexity lower is transferred automatically to a hidden-memory lower, and no optimal dimension claim is inferred from the elementary dictionary estimate.
