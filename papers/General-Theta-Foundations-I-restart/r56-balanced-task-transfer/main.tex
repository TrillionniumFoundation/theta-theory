\begin{abstract}
We prove a stopped acquisition--memory theorem from raw causal experiments by balancing their report measures and unnormalized task-loss measures together. A single admissible reference experiment supplies the converse against every history-dependent procedure, while one parameter-independent finite-state program supplies the upper for all hidden dynamics. With exact task balance and compatible physical ports, the minimax risk is exactly a density-constrained quantization profile of a genuinely acquired law, at the paid productive budget; all live and terminal states are charged. Finite report transformations give a controlled terminal-alphabet expansion, rather than a free decoder seed. The result does not require a positive lower bound on every conditional variance, a common primary row under every strategy, or a contracting normalized filter. A raw moment criterion characterizes finite balancing mixtures, and an integrated instrument estimate controls rank-changing updates without division by small report probabilities. Controlled classical kernels and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the same theorem, including zero positivity floors and singular acquired supports. A nonintervening Gaussian sensing problem has an exact one-value obstruction and a uniform, asymptotically sharp two-value waiting law. Relative acceptance certificates and typed causal simulation complete the resource and error composition. The inherited marked covering calculation is reproduced with its actual mass factors; it is not a new quantization formula.
\end{abstract}
