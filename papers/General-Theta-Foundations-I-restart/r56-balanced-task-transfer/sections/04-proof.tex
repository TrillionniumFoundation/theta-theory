\section{Proof of the transfer and physical memory bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Attainment and continuity of the acquired profile]\label{lem:attain}
The minimum in \eqref{eq:profile} is attained. For each fixed $m$, $\Qm_m$ is nonincreasing and left-continuous on $(1,\infty)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Represent a dictionary by an ordered $m$-tuple in the compact space $H^m$, permitting repetitions. The feasible densities are a weak-star compact subset of the radius-$b$ ball of $L^\infty(\nu)$: positivity, the upper bound and the mass equality are weak-star closed. Nets suffice; no unmentioned sequential compactness is used. The bounded loss functions vary uniformly continuously with the tuple, since squared distance on $H$ is uniformly Lipschitz in its second argument. Along a convergent product net, split the objective difference into this uniform difference and the weak-star pairing with one fixed bounded measurable loss. This proves attainment. Monotonicity is immediate. If $h$ is optimal at $b>1$, use $h_\lambda=(1-\lambda)h+\lambda$, whose cap is $b_\lambda=(1-\lambda)b+\lambda<b$. Its loss tends to the optimal loss as $\lambda\downarrow0$. Monotonicity sandwiches the values and proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A terminal measure obtained by any competitor]\label{lem:lower}
Under $\theta_0$, every legal terminating procedure of expected cost at most $N$ has a terminal mark measure $\zeta$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dominated-terminal}
 \zeta(X)=1,\qquad
 0\le\zeta\le (\E_{\theta_0}A_\tau)\nu\le (N/\kappa)\nu.
\end{equation}
If its terminal dictionary is $C$, its expected loss is at least $\int d_C\,d\zeta$, where $d_C=w+\operatorname{dist}(f,C)^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a Borel set $D$, predictability of productive calls and the conditional reference row imply, by Tonelli,
\[
 \E_{\theta_0}\sum_{t\le\tau}\one_{\{t\text{ productive}\}}\one_{\{X_t\in D\}}
   =\E_{\theta_0}A_\tau\,\nu(D).
\]
The terminal report is one of these reports, so its measure is dominated by this acquired report-count measure. Almost-sure commitment gives mass one, and \eqref{eq:effort} gives the last inequality. Only now take its Radon--Nikodym density, choosing a Borel version clipped to the displayed cap.

Condition on the entire current report, the preceding history and the chosen output. Under the witness, the conditional squared loss is $w(X)+\|f(X)-r\|^2$. The fresh stopping coin leaves this identity unchanged. Randomizing among a fixed dictionary cannot reduce the value below its nearest point. Summing over the disjoint commitment events proves the claim. No independence between different calls was assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One balanced rejection program]\label{lem:upper}
Let $(C,h)$ be feasible in \eqref{eq:profile}. After each attempt of $\mathcal G$, accept virtual mark $x$ with probability $h(x)/b$ and export $T_jr_C(x)$, where $r_C$ is the first nearest point to $f(x)$ in a fixed ordering of $C$. Otherwise return to the start label. This program has expected core cost at most $\kappa b$, risk exactly $\int d_C h\,d\nu$ under every $\theta$, and at most $B+D_0|C|$ states.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Borel representatives and the finite nearest-point rule make the program measurable. On every surviving pre-attempt history, \eqref{eq:mass-balance} gives acceptance probability $1/b$. For each nearest-point cell, apply \eqref{eq:loss-balance} with its constant center, first to simple acceptance functions and then by bounded approximation to $h/b$. The expected terminal loss contributed by that attempt is
\[
 b^{-1}\int d_C(x)h(x)\nu(dx).
\]
Consequently the probability of surviving $j$ complete attempts is $(1-1/b)^j$. This is a consequence of the conditional hazard, not an assumed independent renewal structure. Summing the predictable attempt costs gives $\E C\le\kappa\sum_{j\ge0}(1-1/b)^j=\kappa b$. Summing the terminal contributions gives the stated risk. For $b=1$ the first attempt is accepted almost surely. The physical state need not return to its former distribution on rejection. Only the $B$ routing labels and the exported terminal dictionary persist. The current choice $j$ is discarded after its effect has been encoded in that terminal value or the routing label. Thus the state count follows from \eqref{eq:export}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Semantic, presentation-invariant live-state bound]\label{prop:physical}
Consider finitely many mandatory pre-call contexts $I$ with no additional free context input to the observer. A physical command includes its legal histories, executed kernel, port, visible reports and costs, not merely its printed name. Assume that the legal physical command set at every history in context $i$ is exactly $\mathcal U_i$. Suppose that, under one fixed admitted model, every legal terminating procedure visits each context with positive probability. Let $b_{\rm phys}$ be the least number of subsets covering $I$ such that each subset $J$ has $\bigcap_{i\in J}\mathcal U_i\ne\varnothing$. Every autonomous observer needs at least $b_{\rm phys}$ live states. This count is invariant under duplicating command names or bijective stateless recoding of the same physical commands.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a live label that occurs in a collection $J$ of mandatory contexts, its fixed command distribution must be supported on commands legal in every member of $J$. Null exceptional commands can be removed simultaneously because $J$ is finite. Thus the intersection is nonempty. The collections served by its live labels cover the mandatory contexts, proving the bound. Duplicating a name changes neither the physical intersections nor this covering number; a stateless bijection preserves them. A translator that detects an unavailable phase is not stateless and must be charged as a simulator. The proposition does not characterize the full minimal automaton in general.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:lower} and minimization over the fixed dictionary give the checkpoint lower. At least one state is live, so an $M$-state autonomous observer has at most $M-1$ terminal values. Use Proposition~\ref{prop:physical} for the sharpened width when its witness is $\theta_0$. Lemmas~\ref{lem:attain} and \ref{lem:upper} give the common upper and attainment. Its checkpoint version simply uses the same program with the exported dictionary. If $D_0=1$, its width agrees with the lower and proves \eqref{eq:exact}. Under the witness, one productive call is necessary and costs at least $\kappa$ in expectation, so budgets below $\kappa$ are infeasible. The mandatory live states and at least one terminal state prove the other infeasibility assertion.
\end{proof}
