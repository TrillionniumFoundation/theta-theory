\section{The acquired-geometry transfer theorem}
For $b\ge1$ and integer $m\ge1$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile}
 \Qm_m(b)=\min_{\substack{C\subset H,\ 1\le|C|\le m\\
                    0\le h\le b,\ \int h\,d\nu=1}}
             \int [w(x)+\operatorname{dist}(f(x),C)^2]h(x)\nu(dx).
\end{equation}
Set $\Qm_m(b)=+\infty$ when $b<1$ or $m<1$. The density cap is relative to the actually acquired $\nu$. It does not condition on a favorable event without retaining its mass.

\begin{theorem}[Balanced causal acquisition and finite-state completion]\label{thm:main}
Suppose the raw conditions of Section~\ref{sec:experiment} hold, with the reference and effort inequality using the same fixed admissible model. Let $b=N/\kappa\ge1$. Then
\begin{align}
 \Qm_m(b)&\le R_\cp^\Theta(N,m),\label{eq:cp-lower}\\
 R_\cp^\Theta(N,D_0m)&\le\Qm_m(b),\qquad
 R_\aut^\Theta(N,B+D_0m)\le\Qm_m(b),\label{eq:upper}\\
 \Qm_{M-1}(b)&\le R_\aut^\Theta(N,M).\label{eq:aut-lower}
\end{align}
If, under that witness, every terminating legal observer must use at least $B$ live states in the physical sense of Proposition~\ref{prop:physical}, replace $M-1$ in \eqref{eq:aut-lower} by $M-B$. In particular, when $D_0=1$ and this lower is certified, for every $N\ge\kappa$ and $m\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact}
 R_\cp^\Theta(N,m)=\Qm_m(N/\kappa),\qquad
 R_\aut^\Theta(N,B+m)=\Qm_m(N/\kappa).
\end{equation}
The infima in these equalities are attained in the declared Borel model. For $N<\kappa$, no legal robust observer exists. The autonomous class is also empty for $M\le B$ when the mandatory-state certificate applies.
\end{theorem}

This is a minimax theorem with one common upper program, not a family of modelwise reductions. The two ingredients are logically different: the reference quantifies over \emph{all} competing strategies at one actual model, while balance quantifies over \emph{all} models and histories for one common program. The latter need not be a full predictive-state simulator. For nontrivial export multiplicity, an invariant optimal dictionary $C$ satisfying $\bigcup_jT_jC=C$ gives the corresponding upper with $|C|$, not $D_0|C|$, terminal values.

\begin{corollary}[Regular resource orders with exported values]\label{cor:order}
Suppose the actual marked geometry satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry} with $d,\alpha>0$, and the mandatory live-state count is $B$. For fixed $D_0$ and $M-B\ge D_0$, both bounds in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} are comparable, with constants independent of $N,M$, to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-rate}
 F(N/\kappa,M-B)
 =\max\left\{(N/\kappa)^{-2/\alpha},
       ((N/\kappa)(M-B))^{-2/(d+\alpha)}\right\}.
\end{equation}
For checkpoint risk use $m$ in place of $M-B$, with $m\ge D_0$. The constants depend on the stated geometry and $D_0$, not on an ambient parameter dimension or on a hidden positivity floor.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:geometry} to the lower width $M-B$ and the upper virtual width $\lfloor(M-B)/D_0\rfloor$. This integer is at least $(M-B)/(2D_0)$ in the declared range. The displayed function changes by at most a fixed factor under that width change. The checkpoint argument is identical.
\end{proof}

Atoms must be handled using \eqref{eq:profile}, not by replacing it with a power law. Proposition~\ref{prop:atom} preserves their exact saturation threshold when $D_0=1$. Neither the lower nor the upper calls a conditional acquired law a free prepared sample.
