\section{Prepared experiments and balanced task measures}
\label{sec:experiment}
All state, action and report spaces are standard Borel. An unknown index $\theta\in\Theta$ specifies a prepared law and positive normalized causal kernels. It is fixed for an entire execution; it may index an arbitrary history-dependent physical evolution. Policies see only past reports, legal physical commands and their own allowed coins. They never see $\theta$. All assertions below are for the declared experiment and test, not for a formal parameter manifold.

A call has a nonnegative cost bounded below by $c_*>0$. A predictable flag declares whether the call is productive. Commitment is allowed only after a productive report. Conditional on commitment, a fixed bounded future audit $Z\in H$ is executed, where $H\subset\R^k$ is compact and convex. This audit is not observed before the prediction is committed. The loss is $\|Z-r\|^2$, $r\in H$. Each productive call has a potential audit specified by the same kernel, whether or not the observer commits there. A fresh stopping coin is independent of that audit conditional on the current physical report and history. All other informative components of the current report must be included in the conditional laws below.

The cost $N$ in the core theorem counts preparation, failed gate calls, productive calls, and all calls preceding commitment. A separately required final physical audit has its own explicit cost. Thus a protocol with one initial preparation call and one final audit uses total raw budget $N+2$, not $N$. Time, bit operations and scratch space are not identified with raw-call count.

A checkpoint procedure may retain the full precommit history, but its committed value belongs to a single fixed dictionary of at most $m$ elements. An autonomous observer has a fixed alphabet of at most $M$ states, including every live routing state and every terminal value. Its command law and readout are determined by its state and its current permitted input. At the interface between calls its only observer memory is that state. Its fixed program and real constants are part of the ideal Borel model; finite description is treated separately. Fresh coins can be used within a call but no seed, clock, chosen dictionary or subprogram persists for free. In particular, randomly selecting a different read-only program for the whole execution is not an uncharged random policy. With robust almost-sure commitment and robust expected cost at most $N$, write the infimal worst-case risks as $R_\cp^\Theta(N,m)$ and $R_\aut^\Theta(N,M)$; the infimum over an empty class is $+\infty$.

\subsection*{A genuinely acquired reference}
Fix one admissible $\theta_0\in\Theta$ and a known, explicitly certified probability kernel $K_0(dx,dz)$ on $X\times H$. Under $\theta_0$, after \emph{every} legal productive command and every surviving pre-call history, the conditional mark law is $\nu(dx)=K_0(dx,H)$. Conditional on the full current report, the audit has the same first and second moments as $K_0(dz\mid x)$. Known within-call command coins or auxiliary reports supply no further audit information under this witness. Define, $\nu$-almost everywhere,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derived}
 f(x)=\int zK_0(dz\mid x),\qquad
 w(x)=\int\|z-f(x)\|^2K_0(dz\mid x),\qquad
 d_r(x)=w(x)+\|f(x)-r\|^2.
\end{equation}
Take Borel representatives, with $f\in H$, $w\ge0$. Thus the geometry is derived from an executed report--audit law. It is not a covariance or a reference volume appended independently to the experiment.

Assume that every legal observer satisfies, under this \emph{same} witness,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effort}
 \E_{\theta_0} C_\tau\ge\kappa\E_{\theta_0} A_\tau,
 \qquad A_\tau=\text{number of productive calls through commitment},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa>0$ and $C_\tau$ is its actual accumulated core cost. This is an experiment-level all-strategy inequality. It may follow from a predictable compensated count or a bounded potential, and will be proved directly in the realizations. Since $A_\tau\ge1$, $N<\kappa$ is infeasible under the witness.

\subsection*{One common upper program}
Fix a parameter-independent acquisition program $\mathcal G$ using $B$ live states. From its start state it obtains one productive report, almost surely, at conditional expected cost at most $\kappa$ on \emph{every} surviving history and every $\theta$. After rejecting that report it returns to the same program start. This is a reset of the observer's routing label, not an assumed physical reset of hidden dynamics.

Within the productive call, $\mathcal G$ is allowed a fresh finite choice $j\in J$, a Borel report map to a virtual mark $x$, and a Borel export map $T_j:H\to H$. No information about $\theta$ is used. For every pre-attempt history $h$, every $\theta$, every Borel $D\subset X$ and every $r\in H$, require
\begin{align}
 \Pp_\theta(x\in D\mid h,\mathcal G)&=\nu(D),\label{eq:mass-balance}\\
 \E_\theta[\one_{\{x\in D\}}\|Z-T_jr\|^2\mid h,\mathcal G]
   &=\int_D d_r(x)\nu(dx).\label{eq:loss-balance}
\end{align}
The physical reference mark used in the lower and the virtual mark used in the upper may be different report functions; they take values in the same declared space and their compared law is the same actually prepared $\nu$.

The averaging in these identities includes the fresh choice $j$, gate history inside the attempt, and all hidden states, but the rule later applied to the virtual mark may not retain the averaging coin except through the exported terminal value. The identities are verified from the raw kernels \emph{before} selecting $m,N$ or a dictionary. They neither require equality of the full hidden law nor assert equal conditional variances after conditioning on $j$.

There is a declared export multiplicity $D_0\in\mathbb N$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:export}
 \left|\{T_jr:r\in C,\ j\in J\}\right|\le D_0|C|
 \quad\text{for every finite }C\subset H.
\end{equation}
The important exact case is $T_jr=r$, $D_0=1$. Finite isometry groups give $D_0\le|J|$, but are not required by the theorem. All routing needed to perform these operations between calls is already included in $B$. A random pulse performed in a separate call needs its own retained control state and cost.

The loss identity is an identity of unnormalized measures, not of posterior variances after averaging a normalized filter. It is equivalent to the corresponding order-zero, first and squared-norm moment identities when $T_j$ is an isometry, as made precise in Proposition~\ref{prop:moment}. It allows arbitrary report dependence and zero residual variance on individual actual histories.
