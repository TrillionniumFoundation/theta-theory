\begin{abstract}
A causal observer must pay for knowing when to act as well as for remembering what its observations imply. We prove a joint acquisition--state law for experiments admitting a finite affine realization of their executable future tests. Under block contraction of the acquired-state kernel and uniform bounds on actual acquired densities, the optimal excess risk is of order $M^{-2/d}$ at a checkpoint and $(M-n)^{-2/d}$ for an autonomous observer making exactly $n$ acquisitions. Constants are uniform in $n$; all phases and controller states are charged. The lower follows from equivalence of report traces and actual-law small balls. The upper combines conditional-barycenter calibration, an exact continuation-value Jensen identity, a boundary-valid mass--curvature estimate, and an unequal integer allocation of states. The decision loss need not be quadratic. Gaussian hidden-state sensors and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the same theorem from their raw kernels. A stratified extension permits repeated transitions between pure-state atoms and full-dimensional laws. Numerical execution and causal simulation are separately certified, and a common scored experiment gives attained calibration and deficiency terms.
\end{abstract}
