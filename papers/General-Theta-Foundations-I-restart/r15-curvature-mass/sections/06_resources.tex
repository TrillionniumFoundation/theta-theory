\section{Execution resources and common-task transfer}\label{sec:resources}
The cardinality theorem is not a total-bit-complexity theorem. We record the resources before combining errors. One call in the preceding sections means one actual instrument report, including failed or validation reports. A physical preparation or reset within that call is a separately recorded operation, not a free successful-sample oracle. The irreversible audit used to score the final forecast costs one additional measurement when physically implemented; it is not visible to the predictor before scoring. Thus $n$ sensing calls correspond to $n+1$ total measurements in such a realization. The difference is a fixed, explicit cost, not a hidden asymptotic convention.

\subsection{Finite descriptions and numerical execution}
For the construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the phase sets are part of the single persistent label set. Hence retained information costs $\lceil\log_2M\rceil$ bits. At precision $p$, storing $d$ coordinates, a finite action instruction and a terminal readout for each label costs at most $C M(dp+\log_2|\mathcal A|)$ bits, in addition to the model description and integration programmes. A grid index can be computed in temporary workspace, which is erased at the next cut. The theorem alone does not bound the complexity of computing Bellman integrals or certified barycenters.

\begin{proposition}[Certified numerical observer]\label{prop:digital}
Consider a full-dimensional instance and a specified allocation $L_t$, with grid widths $h_t$. A programmed observer uses actual-law barycenters for its own programmed actions. Suppose these actions have Bellman slack at most $\gamma_t$ uniformly over their labels. Suppose a numerical evaluation of the ideal post-report coordinates has sup error at most $b_t$ except on an event of probability at most $\xi_t$ under the ideal observer law. Let the conditional complete physical kernels, including the scored audit, admit a causal coupling with total mismatch probability at most $\sum_t\zeta_t+\zeta_{\rm audit}$. This is a bound on physical kernels or complete scored laws, not merely separate report marginals. Let the terminal readout incur uniformly at most $\omega(u)$ additional conditional loss when its approximation is within $u$ in the specified output norm. Then the implemented risk is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:digital}
 B_n^*+C\sum_ta_tL_t^{-2/d}+\sum_t\gamma_t+\omega(u)
 +H\left[\zeta_{\rm audit}+\sum_t\left(\zeta_t+\xi_t+C_{K,d}\rho\frac{b_t}{h_t}\right)\right].
\end{equation}
The bracket may be replaced by its minimum with one for the mismatch contribution. The digital machine has the same persistent labels. Additional programme bits, report precision, workspace and runtime must be supplied by the numerical certificates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the exact programmed actions, the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:telescope} has a nonnegative Bellman slack at each phase, adding at most $\sum_t\gamma_t$. Their actual all-incoming barycenters are recomputed using those actions; thus changing the action programme does not silently retain barycenters from a different law. The continuation Lipschitz and actual density estimates are uniform in the actions.

Couple the exact and digital executions. Before their first differing label or physical trajectory, they issue the same programmed action. Coordinate error can change a grid index only when an ideal coordinate lies within $b_t$ of a grid hyperplane. In a fixed bounding cube there are at most $C_{K,d}/h_t$ such hyperplanes; the total volume of their slabs is at most $C_{K,d}b_t/h_t$. The unconditional ideal acquired law has density at most $\rho$, giving the stated probability. Intersecting this event with the absence of earlier disagreements can only decrease its unconditional probability; no density estimate conditional on survival is asserted. Add the exceptional numerical and physical-coupling events by a union bound. Off their union the terminal label agrees, and the loss increases by at most $\omega(u)$. On their union the bounded scored loss increases by at most $H$. This proves \eqref{eq:digital}.
\end{proof}
For a Brier audit the ideal label readout is the conditional mean, and changing it by an output vector $e$ adds exactly $\norm{e}_2^2$. More generally a $C^2$ generator $\Phi$ with bounded Hessian gives $\omega(u)\le Cu^2$ for the proper losses above, by the integral remainder formula. For an arbitrary $C^1$ loss, a quadratic output-error bound is not automatic. This distinguishes task-norm output precision from coordinate precision used in cell selection. On strata the same proof is applied typewise with the corresponding intrinsic slab estimates, provided the numerical certificate also preserves the readable type.

\subsection{Resource-certified causal morphisms}
\begin{proposition}[Typed common-task composition]\label{prop:morphism}
Let a parameter-independent causal simulator implement every policy in a declared target class using the source experiment. Specify its strategy lift, report transformation, legal action interface, internal state, physical clock and task readout. Suppose its complete stopped and scored joint law is within total variation $\varepsilon$ of the target law, uniformly over that class and the model parameter. Here $\TV(P,Q)=\sup_E|P(E)-Q(E)|$. If the target predictor uses $M$ labels and the simulator uses $S$ labels, their direct causal implementation uses at most $SM$ labels and transfers a common loss in $[0,H]$ with additive error $H\varepsilon$. All additional clock states must already be in $S$ or be multiplied in separately. Acquisition, preparation and physical-time costs follow the simulator's declared cost map.

For two composable certificates, complete-law errors add and state counts multiply. Calibration and numerical errors that are themselves complete-law mismatch bounds add to this error. Errors measured in a task norm must first be converted using the loss modulus; they are not interchangeable with total variation. A reverse risk lower requires a reverse certificate covering every source policy being lower bounded.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Run the simulator and predictor in the product of their labels. The strategy lift defines the next legal source action from this product, and the report transformation supplies the target report before its prescribed deadline. The readout is part of the same scored law. For a bounded loss, the layer-cake representation bounds expectation difference by $H\TV$. For composition, append the second causal simulator and use contraction of total variation by Markov kernels and the triangle inequality; multiply the three retained state components. Costs are accumulated according to the specified maps, not inferred from statistical distance. Applying the construction to every policy in a reverse certificate and then taking infima gives the stated condition for a lower. Without that quantifier, an upper simulator does not imply a converse.
\end{proof}
This proposition is the elementary constructive comparison mechanism of classical experiment theory~\cite{blackwell,lecam}, with the resource interface made explicit. It is not a new total-variation inequality. Neither a fixed-time certificate nor finite total-variation proximity proves an infinite-horizon, stopping-time or large-deviation equivalence.

\subsection{An attained four-coordinate resource law}
The following experiment shows that the geometric, calibration and deficiency coordinates can occur together in one scored task. It is a sharpness realization, not a claim that an arbitrary permitted error tolerance must incur a positive risk.

Take an engine from Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, with $n\ge1$ sensing calls. Independently prepare a bit $b\in\{0,1\}$ and an inaccessible calibration offset $v\in\{-\delta,\delta\}$ with equal probabilities. The known amplitude satisfies $0\le\delta\le1$. Before the engine, make one mandatory channel call: it reports $b$ with probability $1-2\varepsilon$ and reports an erasure symbol with probability $2\varepsilon$, where $0\le\varepsilon\le1/2$. Immediately after this report and before any engine observation, the observer must issue an irreversible estimate $\widehat b$. This early scored output is write-only: it cannot later be read as an external register and has no feedback. It may use the current report in temporary workspace. The final forecast includes the engine decision and an estimate $\widehat v$. The common loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jointloss}
 (b-\widehat b)^2+L_{\rm eng}(\widehat u)+(v-\widehat v)^2,
\end{equation}
where $L_{\rm eng}(\widehat u)$ denotes the actual engine audit loss, including its quantum audit outcome when appropriate. All three summands are scored under this one product preparation. The initial channel action is legal only at that initial interface; subsequent sensing actions are the engine actions.

\begin{corollary}[Same-task acquisition--memory--error law]\label{cor:joint}
Put $k=n+1$, the number of calls before the irreversible terminal audit. There are constants independent of $n,M,\delta,\varepsilon$ such that
\begin{align}\label{eq:joint}
 \mathcal R_\cp(k,M,\delta,\varepsilon)-B_n^*
 &\asymp M^{-2/d}+\delta^2+\varepsilon,\\
 \mathcal R_\aut(k,M,\delta,\varepsilon)-B_n^*
 &\asymp(M-k)^{-2/d}+\delta^2+\varepsilon,\qquad M\ge k+1.\notag
\end{align}
The source-to-exact-bit causal deficiency, with its mandatory early deadline, is exactly $\varepsilon$. If the final audit is counted as a measurement, total acquisitions are $N=k+1$ and the autonomous geometric term is $(M-N+1)^{-2/d}$ for $M\ge N$. No audit measurement or internal reset is omitted from the resource account.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On a revealed bit the early squared error can be zero, whereas on erasure its conditional variance is $1/4$. Thus the unavoidable bit risk is $\varepsilon/2$. The offset is independent of every available observation, so its unavoidable risk is exactly $\delta^2$. Giving the full-history engine policy all this independent side information cannot lower its engine risk below $B_n^*$: condition on that side information and minimize over engine policies. The final acquired-density lower is uniform over all such policies.

For an autonomous procedure, the initial channel label cannot appear at an engine phase, since it would issue an illegal channel action. Within engine phases, the clock-support proof still applies. There are therefore at least $k$ nonterminal labels and at most $M-k$ terminal labels. Lemma~\ref{lem:lower} proves the engine contribution, and the three lower terms add because they are components of the same loss under the same preparation. The analogous checkpoint argument uses $M$ terminal labels.

For an upper, estimate a revealed bit by its value and an erased bit by $1/2$, issue that value irreversibly, and merge immediately into a single engine initial label. Estimate the offset by zero. No bit status is retained during engine sensing. Allocate $M-1$ labels to the engine, including its initial label. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives the upper because $(M-1)-n=M-k$. The extra channel interface uses one label. This early-output interface is deliberately specified; the same count is not asserted for a task that requires the bit to be recalled at the terminal cut.

For the deficiency lower, regard $b$ as an unknown parameter. The two source channel laws have total-variation distance $1-2\varepsilon$. The two exact target-bit laws have distance one. If a parameter-independent simulator had defect $e$ for both parameters, contraction and the triangle inequality would give $1\le2e+1-2\varepsilon$, hence $e\ge\varepsilon$. Reporting a fair guessed bit on erasure attains $e=\varepsilon$ for each parameter. The guess is required before the engine or terminal audit, so subsequent observations cannot repair the deadline. Appending the same controlled engine cannot increase this complete-law defect. This proves the asserted attained deficiency and \eqref{eq:joint}.
\end{proof}
The offset coordinate represents an explicitly unobserved calibration ambiguity, not online estimation of an unknown kernel. The geometric term comes from actual sensing laws; the two independent coordinates establish additivity in one task. Their role does not convert arbitrary simulator-error allowances or numerical tolerances into information-theoretic lower bounds.
