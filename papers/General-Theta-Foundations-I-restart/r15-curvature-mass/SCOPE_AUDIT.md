# Scope audit and genuine remaining proof gaps

## Established in the native source at stated quantifiers

A boundary-valid acquired mass–curvature upper; exact barycentric causal information-loss identity; uniform block-sensitivity allocation; a report-equivalence converse forcing n nonterminal states for an internal exact-n deadline; matching checkpoint and autonomous curves for a finite affine predictive class; nonquadratic strongly proper tasks; unbounded Gaussian reports with observed dimension at least acquired dimension; finite noncommuting positive instruments; a repeatedly rank-changing validation/noise experiment; certified numerical execution; typed common-scored-law transfer; an attained same-task four-coordinate example.

These are sufficient-class theorems with complete written proofs. Their validity, priority and significance remain subject to independent mathematical review. No journal decision or formal proof certification is claimed.

## Not yet established

1. A necessary-and-sufficient intrinsic completion characterization unifying arbitrary overlapping-noise, separated refinement and continuation-cut obstructions under substantially weaker primitive hypotheses.
2. A matching law without finite affine closure, for growing/infinite hidden state, or for all weakly mixing/non-summable continuation-sensitivity profiles. The displayed weighted upper survives without block summability, but its general lower is not matched.
3. Unknown-kernel learning or causal adaptive-calibration optimality. Known raw models and numerically certified approximations are different from learning them from data.
4. A full optimal joint region for programme length, planning, scratch, physical time, calibration precision, reset count and minimal simulator state. Those coordinates have explicit feasible accounts, not all matching lower bounds.
5. Arbitrary countable/degenerating strata, general lower-rank or non-strongly-concave decision exponents, unrestricted stopping times, infinite-horizon scored laws or LDP equivalence.

## Important task/interface distinctions

The central risk is excess over B_n* for the same exact-n terminal task. In a dynamic engine X_n or the quantum terminal state changes with n. This is not automatically a monotone estimation curve for one fixed latent variable as acquisition grows. The audit is paid and inaccessible before the forecast. M-n applies only to a clock-neutral, internally exact-deadline machine; a free external terminal request is a different resource.

The product task includes an irreversible early bit output; no claim is made that a bit needed again at the terminal cut can be discarded without extra state. Its delta coordinate is an explicit inaccessible offset, and epsilon is a proved attained channel defect. Numerical errors are upper certificates in their declared norms.

Gaussian positivity is not a contraction theorem: the 3*kappa block hypothesis is substantive. The quantum realization permits noncommutativity but is finite dimensional and deliberately calibrates an instrument family. The standard Gaussian model strengthens the natural application case; it is not described as solving a previously recognized open problem without evidence.

The title and G1/G3 mother problem are retained. The manuscript is a general theorem plus proofs and realizations, not a survey or a claim that every original ambition is already closed.
