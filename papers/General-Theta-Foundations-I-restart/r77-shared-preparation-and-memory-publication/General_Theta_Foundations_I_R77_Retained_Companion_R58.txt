                   GENERAL THETA FOUNDATIONS I:
         ACQUIRED GEOMETRY AND CAUSAL RESOURCE TRANSFER

                                                     QIAN QI


        Abstract. We study finite-resource prediction in positive prepared causal experiments using
        an actually prepared comparison law that need not belong to the operating model family. A
        reference-side converse replaces only each productive report–audit row while retaining the actual
        pre-call occupation. The resulting positive submeasure has controlled missing mass, a cost-certified
        density cap and controlled task moments, giving a lower bound uniform over all legal strategies.
        A common parameter-independent approximate balancing program gives a matching upper at the
        same expected physical budget. The bounds recover exact stopped risk profiles at zero error and
        matching acquired-geometry orders on a quantified two-sided calibration regime. Drift certificates
        extend the converse to metastable and singular calibration with explicit preparation mass and
        residence time. Classical controlled channels and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the
        same theorem without an exactly fair operating reference or a posterior-rank floor. A same-task
        extension combines attained calibration and causal erasure error. Event-stopped observers may
        reuse live labels for outputs, whereas intrinsically state-stopped observers pay for disjoint live and
        terminal roles; both resource laws are proved. We retain nonintervening Gaussian sensing with a
        joint waiting–output geometry. Numerical precision, program description, workspace, physical time
        and simulator states are accounted for separately.




                                               1. Introduction
   A prepared causal experiment determines which future tests can be predicted from its reports.
The predictive quotient records this equivalence of executable tests, but does not by itself determine
the geometry that a finite observer can acquire. Waiting for a rare informative report changes both
the attainable law and the physical cost. Persistent memory constrains the route to that report as
well as its final description. A further difficulty is calibration: a comparison law may describe a
physically prepared ideal experiment accurately without being an exact member of the operating
model family. A converse that silently treats it as an admitted model is then unjustified.
   We establish a two-sided resource–risk transfer under explicit raw experiment certificates. One
actual witness bounds the effort of every competing strategy and the accumulated discrepancy
of its productive report–audit rows from a prepared comparison law. Separately, one parameter-
independent acquisition program approximately balances the report and task measures in every
operating model. Our center is Theorem 4.1. It uses neither an exact comparison model in the
uncertainty family nor a comparison execution that stops under the competing program.
   The main construction in the converse is an accepted comparison submeasure. Retain the actual
distribution of each pre-call history, but replace its current productive report–audit row only. The
resulting positive measure has a controlled mass defect and a density cap set by actual expected
acquisition count. It need not be an executable terminal law. Its unnormalized task moments
nevertheless give an all-strategy risk lower. This separates a valid local comparison from the generally
invalid replacement of an entire selected path law.

                      2. Prepared experiments and stopping resources
   All state, action and report spaces are standard Borel. An unknown index θ ∈ Θ specifies a
prepared law and positive normalized causal kernels (positivity means nonnegative measures, not a
strictly positive density at every report). It is fixed for an entire execution; it may index an arbitrary
history-dependent physical evolution. Policies see only past reports, legal physical commands and
their own allowed coins. They never see θ. All assertions below are for the declared experiment and
test, not for a formal parameter manifold.
   A call has a nonnegative cost bounded below by c∗ > 0. A predictable flag declares whether
the call is productive. Commitment is allowed only after a productive report. Conditional on
commitment, a fixed bounded future audit Z ∈ H is executed, where H ⊂ Rk is compact and
convex. This audit is not observed before the prediction is committed. The loss is ∥Z − r∥2 , r ∈ H.
Each productive call has a potential audit specified by the same kernel, whether or not the observer
commits there. A fresh stopping coin is independent of that audit conditional on the current physical
report and history. All other informative components of the current report must be included in the
conditional laws below.
   The cost N in the core theorem counts preparation, failed gate calls, productive calls, and all
calls preceding commitment. A separately required final physical audit has its own explicit cost.
Thus a protocol with one initial preparation call and one final audit uses total raw budget N + 2,
not N . Time, bit operations and scratch space are not identified with raw-call count.

The stopping interface and retained labels. A checkpoint procedure may retain the entire
precommit history, but its committed value belongs to one fixed dictionary of at most m elements.
This convention counts only its terminal dictionary, not its full-history working memory. Its physical
acquisition and audit costs are still charged.
   A finite-state observer has an alphabet S of at most M labels and one fixed parameter-independent
program. At every intercall boundary its only observer memory is the retained label. Its next
physical command distribution is a fixed function of that label. In particular, the physical port
identity, elapsed time, prior report and subprogram index are not additional free inputs. During
a call the current command, its report and fresh independent coins are available; the post-report
transition can depend on them. No such coin, report, real register or clock persists unless encoded
in S. A fixed terminal decoder r : S → H reads only the committed label; it never reads the report
or the audit. The two stopping interfaces are as follows.
Definition 2.1 (Event and state stopping). In the event-stopped interface, a post-report kernel
emits (s′ , e) ∈ S × {0, 1}. The value e = 1 is permitted only after a productive call and commits
exactly once: the experiment stops, the label s′ is retained, and the decoder returns r(s′ ). No next
command is executed. The commitment event belongs to the public stopped transcript; it is not
an extra stored bit available during subsequent computation. On branches with e = 0, execution
continues at s′ . A label may therefore be used both live and terminal on different branches.

executed command row. Type-zero labels are live. The halt status is thus completely determined
by the retained state, and live and terminal labels are disjoint.

                          Appendix B. Scope and boundary examples
   The center of the paper is a general sufficient-class acquired-geometry theorem, not a classification
of all positive causal experiments. Its calibrated two-sided assertion uses an actual effort witness
and bounded task-row discrepancies from a separately prepared comparison, which need not be
an operating model. It does not assume that a competing program stops under the comparison
dynamics. The exact specialization uses a fixed admissible witness with a common productive
task law and an all-strategy effort inequality, with a matched upper effort constant for exactness,
together with one common task-balancing program. It does not require a common primary row for
all models or all strategies. If two histories in the same alleged driver state have success probabilities
1/4 and 3/4, a single exact driver row cannot represent both. The present upper still works when a
proved conditional attempt bound and the task-measure balance remain valid; otherwise neither the
finite-driver formula nor the new theorem is licensed.
   Pointwise calibration is not necessary for these minimax laws. Static hidden signs have zero
residual variance after fully revealing histories, and nearly pure quantum states can do the same.
Positive current-report marks then fail on those fibers. The raw reference-plus-balance argument
avoids that lower-variance premise; it does not assert that every such failed calibration can be
repaired. Conversely, report-only balance and an unaffordable reference preparation are insufficient,
as is a reference model whose supposedly unpredictable productive state is already leaked by a
preceding report.
   Action aliasing is harmless only when it preserves physical ports, costs, observable inputs and
legal continuation kernels. Duplicating one physical command into two names does not increase
Proposition 10.4’s count. A phase-dependent dispatcher that can see an otherwise unavailable port
state is an extra resource, not a relabeling. The proposition is not a universal minimal-controller
characterization. Nor does a live lower exclude terminal reuse. Definition 2.1 and Lemma 10.5 keep
the two semantics separate; Proposition 18.2 charges any stateful translator before permitting role
identification.
   The finite-state upper is an explicit recursion: at the two physical port labels, issue the declared
command; at the productive call, compute the virtual mark and finite decision; retain only the
next label. It has no history tape, accurate hidden posterior, free real register, free time counter
or externally randomized persistent program. Exact Borel descriptions and finite bit descriptions
are distinguished by Proposition 17.2. Optimal joint program/time/workspace complexity remains
unproved.
   The two intervention realizations score the post-command audit. The Gaussian theorems preserve
the original current sign or direction and use no intervention on it. The directional theorem
combines a fresh-reference variance lower with a static-direction output-covering lower; it does not
add separately witnessed lower losses as if one model realized both. These are different declared
tasks and are never exchanged by an untyped reduction. The reference-law geometry is derived from
actual preparation and its cost is included, but its existence remains an experiment-class assumption.
General learning of an unknown gate lower bound, unrestricted kernel learning, arbitrary unbounded
losses, nonterminating protocols, infinite-dimensional unbounded operators, and large-deviation
equivalence are not conclusions of the paper. The stopped results proved here use nonnegative costs
36                                              QIAN QI


and explicit conditional hazards; they are not substitutions of a random time into a fixed-horizon
theorem.
   The retained predictable-geometry manuscript contains the earlier pointwise calibration and
conditional-envelope route, including nonexact acquired-mass bounds. It remains valid under its
stated assumptions and is preserved in full as an optional companion. Earlier curvature, continuation,
adaptive refinement, causal morphism and independent realization papers likewise keep their own
hypotheses. None is used backwards to establish the central theorem here, and no historical archive
is silently recertified by the present proof.
