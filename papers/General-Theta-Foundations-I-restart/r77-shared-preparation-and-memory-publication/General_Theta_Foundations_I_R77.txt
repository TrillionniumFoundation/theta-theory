                   GENERAL THETA FOUNDATIONS I:
         ACQUIRED GEOMETRY AND CAUSAL RESOURCE TRANSFER

                                                      QIAN QI


        Abstract. We study the finite positive memory required to realize causal experiments and im-
        plement their optimal policies. Multiple preparations sharing one controller and decoder admit a
        common permutation normal form under finite-group convex specifications. An invariant-polytope
        formula counts mixed memory and every prescribed pure initializer exactly. Joint Fourier multi-
        plicities give the exact small-contrast core resource for preparations with a common group average,
        and shared memory can grow arbitrarily while each preparation alone needs two labels. For finite
        reset instruments with positive failure effects, positive costs and no closed failure set, a transversal
        formula classifies memory for robust optimal control with mandatory success stopping and tied
        actions. Attained variational certificates detect every insufficient memory, and explicit rational
        instruments realize arbitrary action-cover problems. Finally, a robust nonnegative-factorization
        argument gives memory lower bounds for arbitrary classical terminal decoders. The original algebraic
        qubit instrument has unbounded approximation memory with an explicit logarithmic-in-logarithm
        lower rate, without a badly-approximable premise or a Born representation of hidden labels. The
        general causal comparison and the complete reset, discrimination, variational, dual, spectral and
        geometric developments retain their individual hypotheses and full proofs.




                                                1. Introduction
   A predictive state records the information in an experimental past that can affect future laws. Its
linear dimension measures one form of compression. A device storing a probability distribution on
finitely many labels faces additional constraints: its transition and readout tables must be positive,
its initialization must be implemented, and a single device may have to serve several preparations
and later requests. These constraints are also present when the aim is to implement an optimal
experimental policy rather than to reproduce a prescribed law. The resulting resource can be much
larger than the predictive dimension or the resource of any one preparation considered separately.
   The question of this paper is how predictive, convex and spectral structure controls such finite
positive implementations. We work within a theory of prepared causal experiments, acquired task
laws and finite-resource statistical decisions. A terminal response assigns a probability row to each
command word. An instrument response describes joint command–report histories and the state
on which a subsequent terminal test acts. All controllers have one fixed parameter-independent
program. Persistent labels, policy storage, physical state, program bits and arithmetic work are
different resources. An initializer is counted when it is a pure label of the device; freely prepared
mixed starts are always stated as a separate convention.
   Three structural results organize the current article. First, we classify the common positive
realization of multiple preparations under finite-group response constraints. The exact initialization
term is geometric, and the joint rather than individual Fourier multiplicity governs small-contrast
memory. Second, we identify the exact number of fixed command rows needed for robust optimal
control of finite reset experiments with tied actions. This number is a finite transversal invariant

                 2. Prepared kernels, acquired task laws and observers
   All hidden-state, action and report spaces are standard Borel. A fixed unknown index θ ∈ Θ
specifies an actual preparation and positive normalized causal kernels. Positivity means nonnegative
measures, not an everywhere strictly positive density. Dependence on the entire physical past
may be included in the hidden state. A legal policy sees past visible reports and its admitted
randomization, but not θ or a future audit. Iteration of these kernels defines its finite-prefix laws;
the consistent countable path law is their kernel extension.
   Each call has a cost ci ≥ c∗ > 0. The decision to make the next call is predictable. In the
geometric route, the declared productive-call flag is also predictable; in the primitive-instrument
route, productive reports are specified outcomes and can be random. Commitment is permitted
only after a productive report, and occurs exactly once almost surely in each model. At that time
a specified future audit Z ∈ H is scored, where H ⊂ Rk is nonempty, compact and convex. The
prediction is committed before that audit is observed. A prospective joint report–audit kernel is
defined at every productive call, including rejected calls. The stopping coin is independent of the
audit conditional on the entire current visible report and the past. Write L = diam(H)2 ; the case
L = 0 is trivial whenever a legal rule exists.
   The core cost Cτ includes initial preparation charged to the core, failed calls and all productive
calls through commitment. A separately executed final audit must be added to the raw budget.
This convention is fixed before taking a risk infimum. Physical time, stored program constants and
temporary computation are not identified with call count.

Three resource interfaces. A checkpoint procedure may use its whole precommit history, but
must output one element of a fixed dictionary of at most M values. This counts its output width
only; it is not a claim of log2 M total working bits.

   An autonomous observer has an alphabet S, |S| ≤ M , and one fixed parameter-independent
program. At an intercall boundary its only persistent observer memory is its label s ∈ S. Its next
command distribution is a fixed function of s. A physical port name, elapsed time, subprogram
index, random seed or former report is not an extra free input. During a call its current command,
current report and fresh coins are available. Whatever persists to another call must be encoded in
S. A fixed decoder r : S → H reads only the committed label.
   In the event-stopped interface a post-report kernel emits (s′ , e) ∈ S × {0, 1}. An event e = 1 ends
the experiment and retains s′ for the decoder; no subsequent command is executed. This public
commitment event is not a stored bit used in continuing computation. The same label may be used
live on one path and terminal on another. In the state-stopped interface a fixed map h∗ : S → {0, 1}
determines halt status. The initial label is live, and type-one labels are globally absorbing terminal
labels with no executed command rows. Entry into a terminal label is allowed only after a productive
report. Thus live and terminal types are disjoint in this interface, and only in this interface by
definition.

              3. Reversible response groups and common controlled memory
   The fixed-phase results concern one transition matrix. Several commands require one family of
matrices on the same retained labels, and checking every command in isolation is insufficient. We
give an intrinsic finite-realization criterion for responses whose minimal linear dynamics is reversible
and compact, including any finite family of unitary commands on a prepared finite-dimensional
quantum system. We then obtain a matching arithmetic memory law for a pair of noncommuting
involutions. This is a theorem about exact controlled response realization, not an assertion that a
simulator’s private label is an observed sufficient statistic for arbitrary feedback optimization.
   Positive realization through invariant polyhedral sets is classical [48, 47, 46]. The argument
below uses a section of the realizing simplex before passing to the observable quotient; auxiliary
hidden-state responses need not belong to the minimal response space. Compact reversibility then
turns containment into equality. The resulting finite-group obstruction concerns all command words,
rather than a single spectral restriction.

          5. Shared preparation, invariant polytopes, and noncyclic memory
   A device that reproduces one prepared response need not reproduce a family of preparations

preparation was selected. This convention is what makes joint realization a resource question.

         8. Finite memory certificates without physical decoder assumptions
   The physical-dictionary lower bound uses the geometry of density matrices at individual hidden
labels. An arbitrary classical simulator need not have that geometry. We now give a different
certificate, valid for arbitrary classical decoder tables. Its conclusion is quantitatively weaker, but
applies to the original algebraic qubit instrument without an unproved Diophantine hypothesis.
More generally it applies whenever experimentally accessible preparations have separated exposing
tests.
   Nonnegative factorizations, support antichains and their relation to memory and extension
complexity are classical. In particular the exact zero-diagonal support bound is already in the
literature on nonnegative rank; see [67, Section 4.1 and Corollary 4.13] and [85, Section 3.1]. The
argument below supplies an explicit error margin and derives its hypotheses from joint stopped
records. It does not treat an approximate zero as an exact zero, or assume that a rare conditional
distribution inherits the unconditional approximation error.

                       12. Scope and further mathematical questions
   The shared-preparation normal form applies to every finite group, every finite preparation and
outcome alphabet, and closed convex wordwise specifications. The input controller need not satisfy
group relations. The invariant polytope may extend beyond the target response span. The exact
low-contrast formula requires a common mean law; the displayed nonnegative-rank counterexample
explains why this hypothesis cannot simply be removed. These are shared-device results with one
fixed decoder, not a claim that independently optimized devices can always share their memory at
no cost.
   The reset classification allows multiple commands and tied optimal actions, but uses positive
failure effects and the finite reset property. The explicit positive-semidefinite counterexample shows
the role of reachability under an exposing preparation. The finite gap certificate detects failure to
attain the unrestricted optimum; it is not the full memory-constrained cost frontier. The complexity
reduction supplies a concrete limitation on uniform efficient synthesis, not an impossibility of useful
algorithms for particular experimental families.
   The new approximation lower permits arbitrary classical terminal decoder tables but requires
delayed terminal test requests and no uncounted access to the preparation history. At fixed positive
target budget and the original algebraic phase it leaves a gap between logarithmic-in-logarithm and
logarithmic memory growth. The sharper matching logarithmic physical-dictionary law retains its
Born-readout and badly-approximable hypotheses. Neither law is the original general-M squared-sign
minimax-risk frontier. The latter, general nonrigid optimal feedback without the reset/reachability
hypotheses, and the intermediate phase-type timing frontier remain distinct mathematical problems.
   The inherited mean–contrast law is exact for its displayed growing symmetric-group family, and
the general finite-group error principle is an attained finite optimization. No closed formula for
every group or universal polynomial-time synthesis is inferred. The return-polytope construction
concerns explicitly verified conditional branches, not average mixing. Its target-cost bound does
not control the simulator’s unbounded expected duration. The stationary-initialization theorem
concerns exact predictively stationary laws; an approximate truncation does not automatically
inherit its extra-label lower bound.
   All original causal, predictive-state, information-compression, finite resource, finite-order measure-
ment, two-stage reset, rank-constrained discrimination, communication, dual, spectral and geometric
developments retain their hypotheses. Local geometry remains local. Independent A1, A2 and fur-
ther Theta construction problems require their own arguments. Program bits, arithmetic precision,
working storage and sampling work are not silently identified with persistent label counts. The
paired supplement contains complete technical and independent proofs; the separately compilable
R58 companion retains its own scope.
