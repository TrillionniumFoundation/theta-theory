\section{Actually acquired geometry and a nondominated singular class}\label{sec:geometry}
The raw-response value is a task invariant, not automatically a dimension formula. Matching numerical rates require additional mass and implementation structure. We give a completely proved singular case and retain the broader finite-horizon acquired-mass and stability results in the companion \cite{FiniteHorizon} with their original hypotheses.

Let $S$ be a compact subset of $\R^d$ and let $D$ be a compact convex set containing $S$. The prepared target $X$ has law $\mu_\theta$ on $S$. The first paid acquisition reports $(\mathtt{prepare},X)$. Every subsequent paid call reports only $\mathtt{blank}$. At every time the task is squared prediction of the same $X$. Rescale the diameter, if necessary, so the loss is at most one. The visible preparation tag is part of the stated physical experiment, not a secretly supplied clock. The controller is exactly the stationary machine of Definition~\ref{def:controller}.

\begin{theorem}[Robust blind acquired geometry]\label{thm:blind}
For every arbitrary family $(\mu_\theta)_{\theta\in\Theta}$ on $S$, every $M\ge1$, and every $0<\beta<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blind}
 V_\beta^\Theta(M)
 =\min_{c_1,\ldots,c_M\in D}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
      \int\min_{1\le j\le M}\|x-c_j\|^2\,d\mu_\theta(x).
\end{equation}
One parameter-independent nearest-center encoder followed by self-loops attains the value. The same formula is the optimal checkpoint risk immediately after the first acquisition. No domination of the preparation family is required. In particular, it includes the uncountable nondominated family $\mu_\theta=\delta_\theta$ on a continuum, when the right side is the squared covering radius of that actual family of preparations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any Borel controller, its output at every time belongs to its fixed set of at most $M$ readouts. Conditional on $X=x$, even a randomized internal trajectory incurs expected loss at least $\min_j\|x-d_j\|^2$. Integrate this pointwise inequality, sum the discount weights, and take the parameter supremum. This proves the lower bound for every controller, including randomized reuse of its states.

For any centers, choose the least-index nearest center at the preparation report. The encoder is Borel by continuity of the distances. On blank reports every state loops to itself. Its risk equals the displayed integral at every time and every parameter. The initial label can be one of these same $M$ labels: the preparation tag, not an extra register, distinguishes the first update. The checkpoint has the same lower and upper construction. Finally, for each parameter the displayed integral is continuous on compact $D^M$ by bounded convergence; its supremum is lower semicontinuous and attains a minimum. This proof is direct and does not invoke product domination from Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in the nondominated case.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Mass, covering and singular memory rates]\label{cor:mass}
For a fixed actual acquired law $\mu$, suppose its support has a global covering bound $N(r)\le C_1r^{-s}$ at small $r$, and it has a submeasure $\lambda\le\mu$ of mass $m>0$ such that
$\lambda(B(x,r))\le C_2r^s$ for every center and sufficiently small $r$. Then for all sufficiently large $M$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
 \frac m2\left(\frac{m}{2C_2M}\right)^{2/s}
 \le V_\beta^{\{\mu\}}(M)=q_M(\mu)
 \le C_3M^{-2/s},
\end{equation}
with constants independent of $\beta$. The exponent may be nonintegral. The factor involving $m$ is retained; a rare stratum is not normalized into a full-probability experiment.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $r=(m/(2C_2M))^{1/s}$. The union of $M$ balls has $\lambda$-mass at most $m/2$, so at least $m/2$ mass has squared error at least $r^2$, regardless of randomization. For the upper, choose a covering with at most $M$ centers at radius bounded by a constant times $M^{-1/s}$ and use the encoder from Theorem~\ref{thm:blind}. These are actual-law lower and globally implementable upper bounds in the same task.
\end{proof}

For the uniform middle-thirds Cantor preparation, a depth-$k$ cylinder has mass $2^{-k}$ and diameter $3^{-k}$. These cylinders give $N(r)\le C r^{-\log2/\log3}$. A ball with radius between $3^{-(k+1)}$ and $3^{-k}$ intersects a uniformly bounded number of depth-$k$ cylinders, giving the matching small-ball bound. Hence \eqref{eq:mass} applies with $s=\log2/\log3$, without a smooth carrier. Products of such preparations and ordinary bounded-density coordinates give anisotropic cylinder coverings; \eqref{eq:blind} retains their entire quantization curve rather than replacing it by ambient dimension. Atomic mixtures and accumulating atoms are included directly by \eqref{eq:blind}; a power law is not asserted when the mass and covering hypotheses fail.

For the general continuing experiment, a singleton Bayes task also gives a useful lower diagnostic. Let $m_t=\E[Z_t\mid H_t]$ for a bounded Euclidean target, under a fixed declared acquisition policy independent of compression. Conditional expectation gives
\[
 \E\|Z_t-d_{I_t}\|^2
 =\E\|Z_t-m_t\|^2+\E\|m_t-d_{I_t}\|^2
 \ge B_t+q_M(\law(m_t)),
\]
provided the full history includes the controller's used randomization, which is independent of the hidden target conditional on its physical reports and actions. A discounted sum preserves this inequality. For adaptive optimization, the acquired law must be recomputed under each deployed policy; this fixed-policy inequality is not an unrestricted control theorem. Nor may one subtract separately optimized Bayes baselines in the robust problem. The exact blind theorem avoids both issues by proving its matching construction and lower bound in the very same experiment.
