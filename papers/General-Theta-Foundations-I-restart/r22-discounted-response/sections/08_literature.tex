\section{Comparison and mathematical boundaries}\label{sec:literature}
The static-reduction method of Witsenhausen \cite{Witsenhausen} moves a dynamic observation law into a cost density under an independent reference. Strategic-measure compactness and existence in decentralized control were developed in \cite{YukselSaldi,Yuksel}; approximation by independently dominated measurements is studied in \cite{Hogeboom}. These are predecessors of the response expansion and tensor argument here. We do not claim the independent-reference reduction, weak-star compactness, or the finite-intersection principle as new. The point requiring verification for the present question is that a single finite row is reused at all times and models, while the physical state is not assumed finite-dimensional. Proposition~\ref{prop:tensor} proves this verification before discounting; Theorem~\ref{thm:compile} keeps the original state budget on both sides of a global approximation.

Robust finite-state controllers for uncertain finite POMDPs are studied in \cite{Cubuktepe}, and inductive finite-controller synthesis in \cite{Andriushchenko}. Those works address controller design and useful algorithms; the present exhaustive certificate is not a replacement for their computational methods. Finite-window near optimality in \cite{Kara} compares increasing windows with an unrestricted POMDP value under filter stability. Here the comparison is against all Borel controllers at a fixed cardinality, without a filter-stability hypothesis, but with product-history domination and supplied effective response data. Average-cost near optimality in \cite{YuBertsekas} has a genuinely different objective and assumptions; no limit $\beta\uparrow1$ in our theorem supplies its conclusion.

The parameter is fixed along a path in every statement about our robust value. A timewise adversary, an internally timed exact deadline, and an unrestricted quantum-memory controller are different comparison classes. Exact Borel controllers may have no finite description. Our effective layer supplies finite tables, but table parsing, numerical report classification and integration have separate cost certificates. The numerical example is evidence that the two endpoints can be instantiated, not a proof of the general theorem.

The following boundaries remain mathematical questions rather than consequences proved here. The exact theorem does not cover arbitrary nondominated report families; the nondominated statement in Theorem~\ref{thm:blind} has its own direct proof. We do not have a general undiscounted average-cost or internally stopped theorem, a uniform $\beta\uparrow1$ compactness conclusion for optimal values, a classification by an intrinsic numerical dimension for all raw experiments, unknown-kernel learning rates, or a matched optimum for description length, workspace and physical time. Nor is a witness-size bound extracted from compactness or a practical complexity guarantee from enumeration. The discount--expansion estimate gives a quantitative consequence for unstable hidden dynamics, not a resolution of every robust filtering problem. The finite-horizon geometric and causal-deficiency results in the complete companion retain their own hypotheses and full proofs. These distinctions isolate the central advance without replacing the original experimental question by a particular realization.
