\section{The causal experiment and its mathematical question}\label{sec:context}
An experiment does not supply a manifold to an observer. It supplies a preparation, interventions, reports, and executable future tests. Predictive equivalence is determined by those tests; predictive geometry is the law actually acquired through a legal execution. The question is which part of that law one finite observer can retain, and how a resource-certified simulation changes the answer. Neither the dimension of a formal parameter space nor a separate optimum at each parameter answers this question.

There are two distinct difficulties. First, repeated use of one report-to-state rule couples all times. Secondly, the model parameter is fixed throughout the experiment but inaccessible to the controller. A sequence of finite-horizon or parameterwise minimizers need not be a single reusable rule. The center of this paper is a compactification which respects both constraints. Its input is the raw response of the experiment to fixed finite intervention words, not a chosen finite-dimensional posterior coordinate system.

The main theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, establishes an attained robust value for every fixed finite alphabet and every strictly discounted infinite horizon. Proposition~\ref{prop:tensor} is its mechanism: a report row is reused at different report coordinates, so finite-history product domination permits tensor testing, even though pointwise multiplication is not weak-star continuous. Theorem~\ref{thm:compile} then gives an implementing finite table and a lower certificate against all Borel controllers of the same size. This is stronger than evaluating a selected controller list, but it is not an efficient general synthesis algorithm.

The interface matters. Here all primitive actions are legal at every continuing call, and the controller does not have to discover an exact internal deadline. Therefore small report errors may create previously impossible report paths without making the controller illegal. This is the precise reason that equivalent active-cell supports, needed for exact interface preservation in the finite-horizon theory, are unnecessary here. Finite-history product domination remains a substantive assumption. We do not infer domination of infinite path laws, average-cost convergence, or arbitrary stopping-time guarantees.

\subsection{Position relative to earlier results}
Static reduction and compactness of strategic measures are established tools \cite{Witsenhausen,YukselSaldi,Yuksel,Hogeboom}. Robust finite-state control is also an established subject \cite{Cubuktepe,Andriushchenko}. Our claim is not that common controllers, compactness, or finite enumeration are new individually. The result combines a fixed reused alphabet, an arbitrary fixed-model family, continuous changing-support reports, and physical presentations not assumed finite-dimensional, with a global same-budget effective comparison. The common row is never replaced by independent rows at successive times.

The finite-horizon allocation, exact-deadline and geometric transfer results are preserved in the complete companion \cite{FiniteHorizon}. They are not unproved premises of the new long-run theorem. In particular, the exact-deadline expression involving $M-n$ is not applied to a continuing $M$-state controller. The companion contains the earlier predictive-realization and acquired-mass hypotheses in full. The next section also states exactly which predictive construction is used here.
