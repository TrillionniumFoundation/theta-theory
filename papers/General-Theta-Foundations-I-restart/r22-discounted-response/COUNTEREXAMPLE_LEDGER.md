# Counterexample and sharpness ledger

1. **Only marginal domination:** Y1=Y2 uniform; alternating dyadic indicators converge weak-star but their diagonal square does not converge to the square of the limit. This blocks a false tensor proof without finite-product domination (rem:diagonal).
2. **Timewise parameter oracle:** allowing separate minimizers at each theta or each stage changes the controller class. The common rows/readouts precede the supremum throughout; the complete R20 separate-versus-common example remains intact.
3. **Support and exact stopping:** norm-small report coarsening may create forbidden paths. New theorem assumes universally legal continuation; it is not an exact-deadline approximation result.
4. **Unstable physical dynamics:** logistic expansion with moving interval observation support has a valid raw discounted modulus despite no asserted filter contraction. Singleton expanding preparation witnesses show the three discount-expansion orders cannot be uniformly improved in that raw class.
5. **Rare stratum:** lower quantization constant contains m^(1+2/s). Omitting actual m would falsely assign a full-mass lower to an arbitrarily rare acquired component.
6. **Nondominated preparations:** a continuum of actual Dirac preparations has a covering-radius robust curve. Single-model dimension zero does not determine its common-controller minimax value; direct blind proof is separate from dominated compactness.
7. **Actual erasure:** persistent failure produces exact risk e/[4(1-beta+beta e)], showing discount amplification. This is not an arbitrary deficiency tolerance promoted to a lower bound.
8. **Coarse Q:** the generic Q=2 rounding allowance is useless in the 729-table certificate. The zero value-rounding allowance is proved specially by the cell optimum, not falsely used as a uniform row-approximation bound.
9. **No free controller clock:** a prefix truncation in the analysis leaves a stationary controller unchanged. An external geometric score selector is separately charged machinery; its expected budget is not a hard deadline.
