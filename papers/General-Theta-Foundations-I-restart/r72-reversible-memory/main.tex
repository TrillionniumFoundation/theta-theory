\begin{abstract}
We study exact finite classical memory for the controlled probability responses of prepared causal experiments. For every finite-dimensional response whose minimal shift dynamics generates a compact group, a finite stochastic realization exists if and only if the observable group is finite. The proof passes through a section of the realizing simplex and does not identify hidden responses with the minimal linear space. It applies to arbitrary finite families of unitary commands after removing directions invisible to all future tests. A pair of noncommuting involutions exhibits a sharp common-memory arithmetic envelope under damping, even though either undamped command separately has finite memory. For periodic mixtures of rational frequencies, we determine the least stochastic memory exactly by a partition of the frequency orders into least-common-multiple costs, with one extra initial label whenever several recurrent classes are used. The deterministic-transition count is their full least common multiple. The exact stochastic count is preserved near zero internal damping, and physical leakage adds exactly one live label to the entire mixture. Both lower bounds range over arbitrary real stochastic realizations and bounded decoders. The periodic laws have the established fixed squared-loss interpretation at the original least expected cost; controlled response simulation is kept distinct from general feedback optimality. The full arbitrary-phase Diophantine theory, performed-law, stopping, reset, rank and acquired-geometry results are retained with their separate hypotheses. Classical positive-realization and Perron--Frobenius methods are credited, not presented as newly invented machinery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and continuing mathematical questions}\label{sec:r72-scope}
The compactly reversible theorem is an exact all-command response criterion. It works on the observable quotient, not necessarily the full physical unitary group, and its deterministic residual count need not minimize stochastic memory. The two-involution theorem supplies one common machine and a matching arithmetic order for every command word, but it does not optimize a controller free to choose a less demanding experiment. The periodic partition law requires nonzero, distinct, nonconjugate nonreal modes and the displayed bounded positive-amplitude mixture. Vanishing coefficients must first be removed. Its endpoint plateau is qualitative in its threshold; its leakage increment is exact. None of these assertions solves the older general-$M$ squared-sign risk or phase-type timing frontier.

The arithmetic theorem determines a constant-factor exact-memory envelope for each nonreal single-frequency probability response, and both logarithmic growth exponents for every irrational phase. Its uniform cubic-root criterion is necessary and sufficient. It does not determine the irrationality exponent of the particular angle with $e^{i\vartheta}=(3+4i)/5$, nor a sharp leading memory constant. In a finite mixture it is the maximum of the component envelopes that governs memory; simultaneous favorable approximation scales are not assumed.

The exact realization uses a prescribed initial live label, stochastic transitions and a bounded decoder. Its physical squared-task interpretation retains the original first-herald cost $1/p$, fixed dictionary and actual quantum audit. Transition-probability descriptions, exact sampling, program storage and arithmetic time are additional resources. An ideal exact-label theorem is not a matching complexity theorem or an algorithm for a noncomputable phase.

The current supplement contains the full instrument-wide state-interior realization, normalized-budget stopping rigidity, robust tied optimality and finite squared-witness proofs. They remain independent results with their own scopes: a latent instrument simulator is not a free observed state, and the boundary criterion is not a classification of several-command feedback control. The inherited performed-law, minimax, reset, timing, finite-width, rank and geometric consequences are retained in full. The separate nonintervening sensing, ordered-measurement, two-stage reset communication and rank-constrained discrimination lines remain at their immutable source identities.

The geometric transfer still requires its own actual-history lower witness and one common paid construction. General unknown-model predictive realization, arbitrary controlled boundary stopping, the sharp general-$M$ squared-sign and phase-type timing risk frontiers, and matched program, precision, workspace and time lower bounds remain distinct research questions. The original title and top-level research objective are retained without identifying the present phase classification with completion of the entire programme.
