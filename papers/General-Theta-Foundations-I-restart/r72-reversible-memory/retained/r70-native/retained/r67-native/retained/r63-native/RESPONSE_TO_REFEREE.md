## Substantive new response to the contribution question

The title remains **General Theta Foundations I: Acquired Geometry and Causal Resource Transfer**, and the research objective remains Annals, Acta, Inventiones and JAMS. The response is not to rename a specialist result, remove historical content, or change the venue target.

The current mathematical center extends the R62 primitive risk route from a finite list of known operating models to a compact, continuously parameterized family of controlled classical or finite-dimensional quantum instruments. Finite model grids alone are not sufficient: a grid-feasible policy can violate its physical cost budget off the grid. The new proof controls report-resolved primitive perturbations through the **actual source pre-call occupation**, derives a uniformly proper fallback, and restores the original uniform expected-cost budget through a counted common mixture. It produces a rigorous lower/upper risk enclosure for one common decoder and one common controller. A converging cost hierarchy removes a crude fallback-cost admission threshold, giving the result at every strictly interior feasible budget. For compact algebraic primitives, a terminating real-algebraic procedure produces arbitrarily accurate same-budget policies and certified risk intervals from the supplied primitive description.

The exact examples are part of this positive theorem's boundary analysis. Small cost error can cause a risk jump at the feasibility boundary even when both models remain feasible. A target failure branch can require a horizon-dependent cost correction even when source occupation is one. Even affine primitive instruments and audit observables on a convex parameter interval do not permit endpoint-only risk verification: the Bernoulli example has endpoint-family risk zero but full-interval risk one-half at every budget at least one. Fixed unknown models are distinguished from switching adversaries by a physical nilpotent-instrument counterexample.

The new upper modulus is not claimed sharp for optimized risk, and the construction-size bounds are not optimal computational bounds. The compact risk theorem does not characterize exactly when the separate geometric reference profile is attained. These distinctions preserve the original foundations problem while supplying a stronger primitive-to-policy bridge.
