\begin{abstract}
We study finite-resource prediction in positive prepared causal experiments using an actually prepared comparison law that need not belong to the operating model family. A reference-side converse replaces only each productive report--audit row while retaining the actual pre-call occupation. The resulting positive submeasure has controlled missing mass, a cost-certified density cap and controlled task moments, giving a lower bound uniform over all legal strategies. A common parameter-independent approximate balancing program gives a matching upper at the same expected physical budget. The bounds recover exact stopped risk profiles at zero error and matching acquired-geometry orders on a quantified two-sided calibration regime. Drift certificates extend the converse to metastable and singular calibration with explicit preparation mass and residence time. Classical controlled channels and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the same theorem without an exactly fair operating reference or a posterior-rank floor. A same-task extension combines attained calibration and causal erasure error. Event-stopped observers may reuse live labels for outputs, whereas intrinsically state-stopped observers pay for disjoint live and terminal roles; both resource laws are proved. We retain nonintervening Gaussian sensing with a joint waiting--output geometry. Numerical precision, program description, workspace, physical time and simulator states are accounted for separately.
\end{abstract}
