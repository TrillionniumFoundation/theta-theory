\subsection*{Decision order, selective prediction and finite optimality witnesses}
The exact matrix-order statement below is task-complete for its declared localized affine-square family. It is not an order for all bounded decision problems. In quantum randomization theory, Jen\v{c}ov\'a \cite{Jencova16} characterizes approximate post-processing of channels by guessing probabilities of all input ensembles coupled to an ancilla. Those tests are substantially richer than the fixed audit losses here; their conclusion is a channel-simulation or deficiency statement, not a bound on the persistent labels and paid calls of an autonomous stopped controller. We neither infer that randomization criterion from the affine-square tests nor replace its ancilla-assisted family by finitely many fitted moments.

Selective prediction optimizes risk subject to coverage, or coverage subject to risk. Franc, Prusa and Voracek \cite{Franc23} identify Bayes classification with randomized Bayes selection as the optimum in their cost-based, bounded-improvement and bounded-abstention formulations. Thus selection by a conditional-risk threshold is not newly introduced here. For i.i.d. repeated acquisition, an acceptance rule of coverage $1/b$ has expected attempt count $b$; the paper retains the physical price of those attempts, a finite common output dictionary, and the uncertainty of a prospective audit. Outside the i.i.d. special case, rejection may change the physical state, so a coverage statement at the initial preparation is not an all-history hazard certificate.

Functional representation and reverse channel coding address a different resource. The strong functional representation lemma of Li--El Gamal \cite[Theorem 1]{LiElGamal18} represents $Y$ by $(X,Z)$ with $Z$ independent of $X$ and controls conditional information overhead; its one-shot application bounds communication for exact channel simulation. Such representation does not supply an uncharged persistent $Z$ to the observers defined here, a legal current-report stopping rule, or a bounded expected physical waiting cost. Conversely our hard number of decoder values is not the entropy or expected code length of a transmitted simulation index.

The primitive risk route now includes its optimality side. Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-attainment} closes the feasible boundary; Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-witness} identifies active physical witnesses and a common Bellman certificate. Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-stopped-order} supplies a complete bounded-score comparison for the actual stopped law at a fixed dictionary and budget, with same-budget finite-label approximation; it does not assert ancillary quantum-channel comparison. Their separation, compactness and finite-support ingredients are classical \cite{Charnes63,Shapiro09,Balder89}. The assertions specific to this stopped experiment are the exact reserved-cost certificate, separate cost and task preparations, counted completion at the original budgets, and the obstruction to convexifying a hard output dictionary. Theorem~\ref{thm:multi-resource} treats several additive expected resources jointly, without calling program bits or scratch space additive costs unless a physical accounting actually has that form. These statements do not assert priority for general semi-infinite programming or resolve arbitrary efficient control synthesis.


\subsection*{Primitive data and the quantifiers of the two routes}
The following logical structure is part of the geometric transfer theorem, not a minimax interchange:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\exists\,\theta_*\quad\forall\,\text{competitors }P,\text{ reached }h,
                  \text{ legal }a:\ \text{lower certificates},\\
 &\exists\,G\quad\forall\,\theta,\text{ surviving }h:
                  \ \text{upper certificates}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first witness is fixed before the competing program; the second program cannot read the hidden model. Their cost constants need not agree. The primitive optimization route instead quantifies over one common feasible program directly, deriving finite lower witnesses from its constrained optimization rather than postulating the geometric reference.

The delayed-release theorem gives a concrete temporal-memory consequence with only one legal physical command. Its exact attainment count does not follow from command-domain incompatibility, and its complete-law loss would be invisible in a terminal-output marginal. It is a scoped application, not a claim that intermediate-state timing optimization or a recognized open control problem has been solved in full.
