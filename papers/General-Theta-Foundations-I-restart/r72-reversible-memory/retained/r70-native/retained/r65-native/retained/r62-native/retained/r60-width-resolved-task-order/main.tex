\begin{abstract}
We study when information physically acquired by a causal experiment admits a sharp finite-resource prediction law, and when the comparison program required by that law can be synthesized. For bounded decision-continuous losses we identify the attained least selected-task defect at each decoder width. For squared prediction this yields an exact moment criterion strictly weaker than affine-square matrix order and a dimension-sharp width hierarchy. The defect is integrated with one common dictionary against actual stopped occupation, giving two-sided paid resource bounds under explicit reference and implementation certificates. For finite-branching classical and quantum interfaces with positive call costs, a common proper program with prescribed bounded task moments exists exactly when every finite-horizon, finite-model semidefinite problem is feasible. Nonexistence has a finite dual obstruction. At fixed classical memory, an exact reachable-support potential certificate supplies a finite algebraic synthesis criterion in time-homogeneous finite models. As a natural spectral application, we determine the entire integer memory--call frontier for two projective detectors in every finite dimension, including a golden-ratio transition and a strict two-label minimax gap. The acquired geometric, singular-instrument, post-command quantum and classical realizations, predictive quotient, calibrated composition and nonintervening sensing theory are retained. Event stopping permits live/output label reuse; state stopping uses disjoint types. Program description, scratch, calibration and time are separately charged. The general transfer remains conditional on its declared certificates; the synthesis classifications and detector formulas have their own explicit scopes.
\end{abstract}
