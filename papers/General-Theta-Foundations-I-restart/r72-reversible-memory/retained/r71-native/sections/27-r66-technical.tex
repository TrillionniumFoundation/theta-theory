
\section{Weighted boundary realization: explicit primitive diagnostics}\label{sec:supp-weighted}
The new weighted theorem applies beyond model-independent report words. For example let a two-dimensional protected state have failure Kraus matrix
$A=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{u},\sqrt{v})$ and herald Kraus matrix
$B=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{1-u},\sqrt{1-v})$, with $(u,v)$ in a compact rectangle inside $(0,1)^2$. One call costs one and a rejected herald retains $B\rho B/\tr(B\rho B)$. The mean herald probability depends on the current state unless $u=v$, and neither branch resets that state. The first-herald potential is exactly
\[
 H=\operatorname{diag}((1-u)^{-1},(1-v)^{-1}),\qquad H-A^*HA=I.
\]
For a fixed prepared state the maximum cost occurs at the upper-right corner, where every finite unary word has positive mass. Thus the cost-support coverage holds. This example verifies the enlarged interface directly and does not replace its theorem by a simulation.

For a prescribed stochastic observer with transitions $Q_y$, its block killed map retains the same total physical failure mass. Indeed summing the output memory blocks removes $Q_y$ by row normalization. Iteration proves the uniform survival bound on positive block inputs. Its score potential is therefore the unique bounded solution of the main article's equation \eqref{eq:positive-score-certificate}; the first block evaluated on the prepared density is the actual performed risk. In contrast, a finite Hankel factorization with negative transition entries has no such probability interpretation.

\section{Scope of exact algebraic memory certification}\label{sec:supp-realization-computation}
The finite test in Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-realization-test} fixes a desired probability response, not merely finitely many empirical samples. Its linear representation is an input identity valid for all words. Equality at words through length $M+r-1$ implies equality everywhere by the invariant-span argument; finite tests on arbitrary fitted data do not carry that conclusion. The number of tested words is at most $\sum_{j=0}^{M+r-1}|Y|^j$, each coordinate equality has polynomial degree at most $M+r$, and the unknown controller has $|Y|M^2+M|C|$ entries before row-sum elimination. These are finite syntactic bounds, not an efficient bound for quantifier elimination.

For the damped qubit the polygon construction supplies a finite search bound, with algebraic vertices and algebraic barycentric weights for algebraic $\alpha$. Real-algebraic feasibility can therefore return an algebraic minimal controller. Its transition-table description, the encodings of those algebraic numbers, and the resources of exact sampling are additional to $\lceil\log_2 M\rceil$ retained-state bits. In the protected classical chain example the displayed rational probabilities already give an exact $k$-label controller, and the probability-valued Hankel rank proves that this label count is minimal. Neither proof gives a matching universal program or working-storage lower bound.

The classical chain and the quantum calibration problem score replication of a terminal probability, not recovery of the realized hidden state. The observer's private random chain is independent of the protected chain; only their terminal output laws agree. This distinction makes clear why no extra observer-owned quantum memory or hidden-state oracle has entered the construction.
