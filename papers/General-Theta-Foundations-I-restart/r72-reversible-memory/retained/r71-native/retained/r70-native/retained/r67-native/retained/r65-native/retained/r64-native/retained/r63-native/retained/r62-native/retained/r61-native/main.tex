\begin{abstract}
We study when the information physically acquired by a causal experiment determines the optimal prediction attainable at finite cost and finite memory. A width-resolved selected-task defect gives two-sided paid transfer for bounded decision-continuous losses, with one dictionary across actual stopped occupation. We determine the exact zero-risk width spectrum; for squared prediction it is the partial sums of the atomic masses of the zero-residual predictive law. For finite-branching classical and quantum interfaces, common proper synthesis is characterized by finite-horizon, finite-model feasibility, with finite dual obstructions. At fixed memory, reachable-support potentials give exact cost and score certificates. A report-resolved operator-span criterion verifies repeated task balance for standard Borel return instruments. Its finite-depth obstruction bound is sharp: a physically realizable dimension-$d$ routine can first fail balance after exactly $d^2-1$ rejections. For finite reset instruments we derive properness, Bellman continuation, a spectral minimax value, a least-favorable preparation and explicit memory certificates from primitive operations; this includes arbitrary finite rank-one detector families. The full integer memory frontier for two projections of arbitrary rank is retained, including its two-label transition and minimax gap. Predictive quotients, singular acquired geometry, post-command classical and quantum realizations, nonintervening sensing and calibrated composition are retained with their separate hypotheses. Exact transfer still requires its paid converse and implementation certificates; neither a local spectral formula nor finite diagnostics settle the unrestricted foundations programme.
\end{abstract}
