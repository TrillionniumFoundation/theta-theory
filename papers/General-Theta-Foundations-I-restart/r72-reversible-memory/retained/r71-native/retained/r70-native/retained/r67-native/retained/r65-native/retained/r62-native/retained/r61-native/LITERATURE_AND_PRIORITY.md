Blackwell comparison, sequence form, compact policy arguments, absorbing Bellman equations, separation/semidefinite duality, quantifier elimination, Pauli/Weyl randomization and raw positive-instrument contractivity remain credited in the native bibliography. Their combination must be evaluated by its precise theorem, not by renaming these ingredients.

The reset theorem is a complete primitive-to-value reduction in its declared model, including a preparation saddle and equality-based memory conditions. Its Bellman and spectral tools are classical. The sharp continuation-depth construction, report-event task formulation and finite-width zero-risk refinement are stated and proved as current author contributions; neither a literature search nor the finite diagnostics proves that these suffice for top-four editorial significance.
