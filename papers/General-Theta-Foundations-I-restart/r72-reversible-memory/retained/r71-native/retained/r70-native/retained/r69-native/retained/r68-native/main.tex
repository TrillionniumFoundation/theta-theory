\begin{abstract}
We study finite classical realization of prepared causal experiments while keeping hidden simulator states distinct from observer memory. For a finite-dimensional quantum instrument, a uniform positive lower bound on every normalized report-branch output yields an exact finite state-positive realization for all adaptive command policies, terminal measurements and stopping rules. In physical dimension $d$, a common pure-state hull uses at most $\lceil(4d/\eta)^{d-1}\rceil$ generators at mixture margin $\eta$, plus prescribed preparation labels; each command/report row needs at most $d^2$ successors. The realization preserves complete stopped laws and actual expected costs, not merely a finite collection of moments. At fixed $d$, the exponent $d-1$ is necessary for uniform state-positive representation, including finite-command examples, while no such bound for unrestricted scalar-response realizations is inferred. A block version permits interiority only after several calls and counts its intervening clock and states. A separate finite spectral argument gives an explicit rational exclusion margin at every classical memory size for the fixed-angle damped qubit. These results extend the original realization programme beyond one-command heralded models without replacing its distinct boundary-optimal-observer problem. The necessary-and-sufficient single-command stopping criterion, robust tied decision face, common squared-audit embeddings, algebraic fixed-memory risk, performed-law duality, geometric transfer, spectral and communication results are retained with complete current proofs and their individual hypotheses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and continuing mathematical questions}\label{sec:r68-scope}
The inherited boundary criterion concerns one globally legal command, finitely many reports, compact continuous model uncertainty, a fixed finite decoder dictionary, and primitive uniform transience. It permits non-scalar herald effects, noncommuting channels and model-dependent word laws. It does not classify boundary feedback with several commands. Where the normalized unused-budget condition fails, the theorem supplies an actual rejecting policy but does not replace its remaining control problem by a decision quotient.

Robust ties are classified by a finite positive stochastic realization of an optimal kernel, not by arbitrary selection from wordwise Bayes ties. The invariant-polytope criterion is complete within the stated rigid class, but is not a general algorithm. Exact risk at each prescribed algebraic memory bound is computable; deciding whether some finite memory attains the unrestricted optimum is a different question. The rational exact-memory hierarchy and rank-three counterexample are physically realized squared-prediction constructions, not claims to settle a separately recognized open quantum-control problem. The new exact instrument simulator keeps its latent state hidden and does not itself construct an optimal finite-memory observer. Its state-positive noise exponent does not bound arbitrary response realizations, and its fixed-angle spectral lower is not asserted sharp. The sharp general-$M$ noisy-qubit risk frontier and the intermediate phase-type timing frontier remain open here.

The geometric transfer still requires its own actual-history converse and common paid construction. Its full finite-width, rank, singular-range, regular, mixed and atomic consequences remain in the current supplement, together with nonintervening sensing ancestry and the fixed-law predictive quotient. The independent ordered-measurement, two-stage reset communication and rank-constrained discrimination sources remain preserved at their immutable identities; they are not inferred from the new protected-channel embedding. General unknown-model predictive realization and matching optimal program, precision, workspace and time bounds remain independent research targets.
