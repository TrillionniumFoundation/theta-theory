# Assumptions and their uses

| Assumption | General theorem use | Raw verification / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Known preparation, normalized Borel controlled kernels | Actual path law, posterior, Bellman comparison | All positive sensor kernels; not unknown-kernel learning |
| Finite nonempty action alphabet, finite horizon | Measurable minimizing action; legal programme | No arbitrary stopping-time substitution |
| Actual posterior law density <= rho for every input/action | Integrated upper and policy-uniform small-ball lower | Raw Jacobian bound, not an assumed Fisher tensor |
| Compact convex interior carrier K_kappa | Barycenters stay legal; finite curvature collar | Explicit kappa=tau(1-lambda)/(1+lambda) |
| Global coordinate grid | Executable label bound and boundary-slab precision | At most L cells, all phases charged |
| Exact conditional barycenter of implemented law | Zero first moment, exact Jensen telescope | All incoming edges merged; no retained shadow filter |
| Final full categorical squared task | Uniform concavity/Lipschitz and terminal rank lower | Different scored tasks may have lower rank |
| Finite face family with relative-interior carriers | Singular extension | Actual face submass and vertex atoms; finite stratum labels |
| Terminal r-face actual mass >= w | Stratified lower with w^(1+2/r) | Intermediate larger rank not automatically a terminal lower |
| Reverse causal strategy-class coverage | Risk lower transport | Forward same-controller simulation alone insufficient |
| Early exact-bit output deadline | Attained deficiency exactly epsilon | No terminal-audit look-ahead |
| Certified digital arithmetic / comparison / kernel errors | Explicit implementable precision bound | No universal synthesis or total bit-optimality claim |

Realization A: positive noisy finite-state sensor selection, arbitrary d. Realization B: validation/noise hybrid, repeatedly changing atomic/continuous acquisition rank. Integral T additionally supplies graph-directed separated acquisition, continuous-query Gaussian continuation and rank-changing regression. They remain mathematically distinct, not mutually circular proofs.
