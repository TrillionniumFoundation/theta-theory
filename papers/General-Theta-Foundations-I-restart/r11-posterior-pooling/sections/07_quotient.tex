\section{Measurable predictive realization}\label{sec:quotient}
We record a sufficient realization result so that the predictive quotient in Section~\ref{sec:problem} is an actual measurable object. Its topological hypotheses are explicit; they are not inferred from the phrase ``standard Borel''. The more general instrument construction and its hypotheses are retained in Supplements S and T.

\begin{proposition}[Compact evaluation realization]\label{prop:quotient}
At each fixed phase let the history space be a countable union of Borel compact metrizable pieces $H=\bigcup_m H_m$. Suppose a countable determining family of bounded executable tests and legal-interface coordinates has continuous normalized expectations $e_k:H_m\to[-1,1]$ on every piece. Assume equality of all these coordinates is equivalent to equality of the legal interface and expectations of every executable continuation test. Put
\[
 e(h)=(e_k(h))_{k\ge1},\qquad S=e(H)\subset[-1,1]^{\mathbb N}.
\]
Then $S$ is standard Borel, $e$ admits a Borel section, and its fibers are exactly predictive equivalence classes. Every Borel history statistic $b$ from which $e$ is Borel recoverable factors onto $S$; conversely every Borel statistic constant on the fibers is a Borel function of $e$.

Suppose additionally that the report kernel and the updated evaluation coordinates after each legal action are jointly Borel in history, action and report and are constant on each predictive fiber (for a fixed jointly defined version). Then they descend to jointly Borel kernels and updates on $S$. The latter assumption holds for the finite-state Bayes realization \eqref{eq:bayesupdate} whenever the future tests separate the relevant beliefs and legal phase is retained.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each image $e(H_m)$ is compact, so $S$ is an $F_\sigma$ subset of the compact countable product. A continuous surjection from a compact metric space onto its image has a Borel section: cover the domain at level $r$ by finitely many closed sets of diameter at most $2^{-r}$, refine the previous chosen closed set, and select the first set whose image contains the desired point. Images of these compact sets are closed. The selected nested nonempty compact sets have diameters tending to zero, and their unique limit is a Borel selection in the fiber. For $S$, first select the least piece whose compact image contains the point, then use its section. This supplies a Borel map $s:S\to H$ with $e\circ s=\mathrm{id}_S$.

The determining-family assumption identifies the fibers exactly, including action availability. If $e=g\circ b$, the statistic $b$ maps onto $S$ through $g$; this is the predictive minimality statement. If $v$ is Borel and constant on the fibers, $v=(v\circ s)\circ e$, giving Borel factorization. The report kernel and update descend by evaluation at $s(z)$. Fiber constancy makes the definition independent of the section; joint measurability follows by composition. No assertion is made about arbitrary null-set versions of regular conditional distributions without the stipulated compatible version.
\end{proof}

For the sensors of Theorem~\ref{thm:sensors}, fixed-phase histories form finitely many compact report cubes indexed by action words. Positivity makes Bayes evaluation continuous on each. If an invertible legal transition is available, next-report means recover the incoming belief as proved there. Terminal categorical audit tests separate terminal beliefs. Equality of beliefs gives equality of every controlled future law by iteration of the physical kernels, so the quotient is the actual acquired belief image, with phase appended. For a parameter family these statements apply to the declared prepared law; a Bayesian quotient for one prior is not automatically a sufficient statistic valid simultaneously for every parameter.

A smaller state used only for a scored task need not satisfy the determining-family premise for the complete experiment. In particular a continuation chart, an observable rank, or a scalar forecast does not by itself yield a simulator of all future tests. Simulator certification in Proposition~\ref{prop:morphism} therefore remains a separate requirement. Nothing here extends a finite-dimensional identity to an unbounded operator closure or converts total-variation comparison into large-deviation equivalence.
