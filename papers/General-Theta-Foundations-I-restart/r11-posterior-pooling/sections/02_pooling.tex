\section{Causal posterior pooling}\label{sec:pooling}
Fix $d\ge1$, a horizon $n\ge1$, and hidden states $\{0,\ldots,d\}$. The preparation $p_0$ and all kernels below are known. At stage $t<n$ an action $a$ in a fixed finite alphabet $\mathcal A_t$ produces a transition matrix $T_t^a$, then a report with strictly positive density $f_{t,j}^a(y)$ given the new hidden state $j$, relative to a specified measure $m_t^a$. These are normalized Borel densities. The primitive cost of this operation is one observation and its declared physical duration. The final scored audit is $Y=e_{X_n}$, revealed only after $\widehat Y\in\Delta_d$, with squared Euclidean loss. Here $\Delta_d$ is the probability simplex on $d+1$ points.

For $p\in\Delta_d$ put $q=pT_t^a$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bayesupdate}
 r_t(p,a,y)=\sum_{j=0}^d q_j f_{t,j}^a(y),\qquad
 F_{t,j}(p,a,y)=\frac{q_j f_{t,j}^a(y)}{r_t(p,a,y)}.
\end{equation}
The report law is $r_t(p,a,y)m_t^a(dy)$. The posterior law generated by this actual report is denoted $\Lambda_t(p,a)$. Densities of posterior laws below use coordinates $(p_1,\ldots,p_d)$, with $p_0=1-\sum_{j=1}^dp_j$, and ordinary $d$-dimensional Lebesgue measure in these coordinates.

\begin{definition}[Uniform acquired smoothing]\label{def:smoothing}
There are $0<\kappa<1/(d+1)$ and $\rho<\infty$, fixed before the policy, for which, for every $t<n$, $p\in\Delta_d$ and $a\in\mathcal A_t$, the law $\Lambda_t(p,a)$ is supported on
\[
 K_\kappa=\{u\in\Delta_d:u_j\ge\kappa\ (0\le j\le d)\}
\]
and has density at most $\rho$. The same requirement includes beliefs arising after earlier compression, not just a chosen exact-filter trajectory.
\end{definition}
This is an experiment-level condition on the pushforward in \eqref{eq:bayesupdate}; Theorem~\ref{thm:sensors} derives it by a raw Jacobian calculation. No optimal codebook, known acquired law of a chosen policy, smooth value function, or separated response support is assumed. The report law itself obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reportcontinuity}
 \TV(J_t(p,a),J_t(p',a))\le\tfrac12\norm{(p-p')T_t^a}_1
 \le\tfrac12\norm{p-p'}_1.
\end{equation}
The substitute for pathwise contractivity is the exact information-loss identity below. It is decision stability on actual laws, not contraction of two filters along arbitrary reports.

Let $V_t(p)$ be optimal full-history risk for the remaining stages, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bellman}
 V_n(p)=1-\norm p^2,\qquad
 V_t(p)=\min_{a\in\mathcal A_t}\int V_{t+1}(F_t(p,a,y))r_t(p,a,y)m_t^a(dy).
\end{equation}
Then $B_n^*=V_0(p_0)$. The action set at every stage is nonempty, and all full-history Borel policies are admitted. In particular this is not a comparison under a controller fixed before optimization.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive acquired-geometry completion]\label{thm:pooling}
Under Definition~\ref{def:smoothing}, there are positive constants $c,C,L_0$ depending only on $d,\kappa,\rho$ with the following properties.

For every $M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cprate}
 cM^{-2/d}\le R_\cp(n,M)-B_n^*\le CM^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
For arbitrary integers $L_1,\ldots,L_n\ge L_0$, a deterministic autonomous machine exists with at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:totalstates}
 S=1+\sum_{t=1}^n L_t
\end{equation}
\emph{total} states and risk at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poolrate}
 B_n^*+C\sum_{t=1}^n L_t^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
All phases, action instructions and the terminal readout are functions of these states. It never rereads a report or retains an exact full-history posterior. Its read-only programme is constructed from the known kernels and the preparation.

More precisely, let $\lambda_t$ be its actual pre-pooling posterior law, partitioned into its grid cells $C_{t,j}$, and let $b_{t,j}$ be the conditional barycenter in that cell. Then its risk satisfies the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poolidentity}
 R_{\rm pool}-B_n^*=
 \sum_{t=1}^n\sum_j\left\{
 \lambda_t(C_{t,j})V_t(b_{t,j})-
            \int_{C_{t,j}}V_t(u)\,\lambda_t(du)\right\}.
\end{equation}
The law $\lambda_t$ is induced by the constructed controller, not borrowed from an optimal full-history policy.

Consequently, for $M\ge1+nL_0$, with $L=\lfloor(M-1)/n\rfloor$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:autrate}
 cM^{-2/d}\le R_\aut(n,M)-B_n^*\le CnL^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
The lower is valid whenever the autonomous class is nonempty. Thus at every fixed horizon the checkpoint and autonomous memory exponents agree. The displayed upper, rather than a horizon-independent constant, is the joint-budget assertion.
\end{theorem}

The programme in this theorem may use exact Borel Bayes instructions and exact known barycenters. Its existence is separate from the complexity of evaluating \eqref{eq:bellman}. A finite-precision implementation with explicitly charged errors is given in Proposition~\ref{prop:digitalpool}. No polynomial planning claim is implicit.

\subsection{An integrated Jensen estimate}
\begin{lemma}[Curvature integrated against actual mass]\label{lem:integrated}
Let $K$ be a compact convex subset of an open convex set $\Omega\subset\R^d$, with positive distance from its boundary. Let $v$ be concave and $L$-Lipschitz on $\Omega$. Partition space into half-open cubes $C_j$ of side $h$, with fixed lexicographic boundary assignment. For all sufficiently small $h$, every finite measure $\lambda$ supported on $K$ with density at most $\rho$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jensenbound}
 0\le\sum_{j:\lambda(C_j)>0}
 \left\{\lambda(C_j)v(b_j)-\int_{C_j}v(x)\lambda(dx)\right\}
 \le C_{K,\Omega,d}\rho Lh^2,
 \qquad b_j=\frac{\int_{C_j}x\lambda(dx)}{\lambda(C_j)}.
\end{equation}
The constant does not require differentiability of $v$, a lower bound on cell masses, or regularity of its switching sets. The measure need not be normalized.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Jensen's inequality gives the lower. We prove the upper first for smooth convex $f=-v$. For a cube $C$ meeting $K$, let $z$ be its center, let $\ell$ be the tangent affine function to $f$ at $z$, and set $w=f-\ell$. Take $R=2\sqrt d\,h$; the ball $B_R(z)$ remains in a fixed compact neighborhood of $K$ when $h$ is sufficiently small. Convexity gives $w\ge0$, $w(z)=0$, and subharmonicity gives, for $x\in C$,
\[
 w(x)\le 2^d\fint_{B_R(z)}w\le2^d A(R),\qquad
 A(r)=\fint_{\partial B_r(z)}w.
\]
For the first inequality average over $B_{R/2}(x)\subset B_R(z)$, using $w\ge0$; for the second, the spherical averages increase. Along each ray convexity and $w(z)=0$ give $w(z+R\theta)\le R\partial_Rw(z+R\theta)$. The divergence theorem therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localcurvature}
 \sup_Cw\le C_dR^{2-d}\int_{B_R(z)}\Delta f(x)\,dx.
\end{equation}
Since $\ell$ is affine and $w(b_C)\ge0$, the cell's Jensen gap for $v$ is at most
\[
 \int_C w\,d\lambda\le C_d\rho h^2
                 \int_{B_R(z)}\Delta f(x)\,dx.
\]
The expanded balls of a lattice grid have bounded overlap depending only on $d$. Choose a smooth cutoff $\chi$ equal to one on their union and supported in a fixed compact subset of $\Omega$. Positivity of $\Delta f$ and integration by parts give
\[
 \int_{\cup B_R(z)}\Delta f\le\int\chi\Delta f
      =-\int\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla f
      \le L\int\norm{\nabla\chi}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:jensenbound}. For a nonsmooth convex $f$, mollify on a slightly larger compact neighborhood. The mollifications remain convex, have Lipschitz constants at most $L$, and converge uniformly on all cells and their barycenters. The same bound is uniform in the smoothing radius; passage to the limit proves the assertion. Equivalently, the estimate integrates the positive distributional curvature measure of $f$, rather than bounding its pointwise Hessian.
\end{proof}
Here and below the notation $\fint$ denotes normalized integration over the indicated set. The argument is a local convexity estimate; no claim of novelty is made for concavity, subharmonic mean inequalities or mollification individually. Its role is to control causal pooling for nonsmooth optimal continuation values.

\subsection{Exact posterior states and optimal actions}
\begin{lemma}[Finite-state posterior aggregation]\label{lem:aggregation}
Suppose a finite label $I$ has known masses $\alpha_i$ and exact conditional hidden distributions $p_i=\law(X\mid I=i)$. Select a known action $a_i$ at each label, take the physical transition and report, and compute $W=F(p_i,a_i,Y)$. If the next label is $J=j$ precisely when $W\in C_j$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barycenter}
 \alpha'_j=\PP(J=j)=\lambda(C_j),\qquad
 \law(X'\mid J=j)=b_j=\frac{\int_{C_j}u\lambda(du)}{\lambda(C_j)},
 \quad\lambda=\law(W).
\end{equation}
Thus the barycenter pools \emph{all} incoming labels, not separate incoming edges. Zero-mass cells may be assigned arbitrary fixed instructions. The next transition uses only $J$, its recorded barycenter and the new report.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Markov kernel and $p_i=\law(X\mid I=i)$ imply $\law(X'\mid I,Y)=W$. For each hidden coordinate $k$,
\[
 \PP(X'=k,J=j)=\E[W_k\mathbf1_{\{W\in C_j\}}]
                =\int_{C_j}u_k\lambda(du).
\]
Divide by the cell's actual positive mass. This proves the exact conditional identity without retaining $I$ or its history at the next cut.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pooling}]
A fixed future policy, including its final simplex-valued forecast, has risk linear in the incoming hidden distribution, with coefficients in $[0,2]$. Taking the infimum shows that every $V_t$ is concave and $2\sqrt d$-Lipschitz in simplex coordinates. The Bayes recursion \eqref{eq:bellman} follows by conditioning. Its integrands are bounded, and the perspective of a continuous bounded function under \eqref{eq:bayesupdate} is continuous in $p$ by dominated convergence; an integrable dominator is $2\sum_j f_{t,j}^a$. Finite action sets therefore admit Borel minimizing selectors, with ties resolved by a fixed order.

Start with one initial label of mass one and posterior $p_0$. Inductively, at a label with posterior $p_i$, choose a minimizing action in \eqref{eq:bellman}. Partition the next posterior's first $d$ coordinates into the grid of side $h_t=1/k_t$, where $k_t=\lfloor L_t^{1/d}\rfloor$. There are at most $k_t^d\le L_t$ occupied cells in $[0,1]^d$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregation} to all incoming labels and record the resulting masses and barycenters in the read-only programme. This is a finite forward construction of integrals under the actual controller law. It is not an oracle that supplies the true full-history posterior during execution.

Let $Z_t$ denote the retained label and $Q_t=\law(X_t\mid Z_t)$ its recorded posterior. Let $W_t$ be the pre-pooling posterior using $(Z_{t-1},Y_t)$. Bellman optimality at the chosen action gives
\[
 \E V_t(W_t)=\E V_{t-1}(Q_{t-1}).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:barycenter} gives $Q_t=\E[W_t\mid Z_t]$. Summing the successive differences proves \eqref{eq:poolidentity}, because the terminal output $Q_n$ has true risk $\E[1-\norm{Q_n}^2]$. In general $W_t$ is \emph{not} the posterior given the entire discarded history. No identification with that posterior is used.

By Definition~\ref{def:smoothing}, each $\lambda_t$ is a finite mixture of laws bounded by $\rho$ on $K_\kappa$. Choose $L_0$ so that the grids in Lemma~\ref{lem:integrated} and their enlarged cubes stay in the simplex interior. That lemma bounds the $t$th summand by $CL_t^{-2/d}$ and proves \eqref{eq:poolrate}. Give all stages disjoint label sets, plus the initial label. Their union has size \eqref{eq:totalstates}; the stage, action, transition routine and stop/readout instruction are determined by the label itself. This proves the full autonomous state count.

For a converse, under \emph{any} full-history policy, condition immediately before the last action and report. Definition~\ref{def:smoothing} bounds the resulting posterior law by $\rho$; averaging gives the same bound for $\nu_n^\pi$. In the first $d$ coordinates, a union of $M$ radius-$r$ balls has mass at most $\rho v_dMr^d$, where $v_d$ is the unit-ball volume. Set $r=(2\rho v_dM)^{-1/d}$. At least half the mass lies outside, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smallballlower}
 q_M(\nu_n^\pi)\ge\tfrac12(2\rho v_dM)^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
The full simplex norm dominates the coordinate norm. Together with $B_n^\pi\ge B_n^*$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:seeds}, this proves the checkpoint lower and hence the autonomous lower. For the checkpoint upper select an optimal full-history policy and quantize its terminal posterior by a uniform grid on the simplex. There are at most $M$ cells after a fixed dimensional adjustment, and squared cell diameter is at most $CM^{-2/d}$. Increasing $C$ handles the finitely many small $M$. This proves \eqref{eq:cprate}. Equal allocation in \eqref{eq:totalstates} proves \eqref{eq:autrate}.
\end{proof}

The lower bound is a bound on an actually obtained posterior law for every admissible policy. It is not a packing of a merely reachable parameter set. The upper uses global cells but charges only their finite labels and programmed constants. Its decision stability holds across arbitrary policy switches because the representatives are exact conditional barycenters. A nearest grid point maintained alongside a hidden exact posterior would neither have identity \eqref{eq:poolidentity} nor satisfy the state model.
