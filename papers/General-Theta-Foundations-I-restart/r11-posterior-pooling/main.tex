\begin{abstract}
A finite causal observer must choose experiments as well as compress their reports. We prove a completion theorem for observation-adaptive experiments with overlapping noise. The construction pools posterior laws by their actual conditional barycenters, so every retained state has its true posterior rather than an uncharged exact filter or an approximate posterior. Its excess decision risk is exactly a sum of acquired Jensen defects of concave continuation values. An integrated curvature estimate makes these defects second order under uniform posterior-density bounds, without differentiability of the optimal value or a margin assumption at policy switches. This yields matching checkpoint and autonomous memory exponents at every fixed observation horizon, with the complete phase and controller cost displayed. Positive finite-state kernels with continuous asymmetric sensors verify the assumptions, and the optimal sensor changes with a noisy observed value. A stratified version permits exact-validation atoms and continuously acquired strata in the same recursion. Calibration, numerical implementation and causal deficiency are transferred in the same task. The separated-refinement and continuation-cut theorems are retained in integral supplements; uniform joint horizon--memory optimality outside those stronger classes is not asserted.
\end{abstract}
