Review object: 589372183966bef1b40ae223b0ab468cb6419182. Report snapshot: 4454ad669ebdbccb93d10d828484600dce58845d. The report's seven major revisions and related numbered comments are addressed below. We retain the mother problem, title and substantive r1 mathematics. A stronger theorem is supplied rather than substituting a survey or a realization paper. No statement here represents a commissioned journal decision or independent referee endorsement.

## MR1 — Predictive realization and conditional versions

`lem:factor` states and proves the standard-Borel factorization with completed sigma fields and an almost-sure conclusion. `prop:quotient` now separates event-instrument closure, common full-support dominating measures, continuous density numerators, positive-denominator normalization and separately verified zero-density extensions. Equal event laws imply equal densities almost everywhere; continuity upgrades this to the declared report domain. The continuation interface is part of the quotient. The sensor proof explicitly verifies its dominating measure and null-report overwrite. This closes the stated version/factorization defect under the displayed certificate, not for arbitrary measurable quotients.

## MR2 — Recurrent nonuniform transport

`thm:kernel` derives conditional first/mixed-second moment profiles from a raw Markov factor. The assumption is the one-step Lyapunov inequality K2 v <= lambda v with bounded positive v, not the old unrolled (T) bound. The proof gives two positive resolvents and an acquired-weight-normalized energy matrix. `cor:finite-kernel` permits recurrent expansion in a two-mode chain; `prop:suspension` proves exact independent holds do not introduce an inverse activity probability into the forced profile. `thm:recurrent` verifies a continuous-state fold/refresh raw kernel with gains 2 and 1/4, actual density bound, and transport constant 3040/667. Main theorem `thm:main` invokes this structural route without circular use of a realization.

The acquired-weight normalization remains necessary in multi-chart classes. The theorem does not assert a uniform rare-component bound when its explicit normalized matrix diverges, nor does it cover arbitrary adaptive suspensions. This is a stated boundary, not a claim that (T) has been derived for all causal kernels.

## MR3 — Full resources and a growing-overhead converse

Section `sec:transfer` defines algorithm classes, parameter-independent microstep simulators, stopping indices, report/task/clock transformations, strategy-class inclusions, all resource coordinates and an explicit monotone cost map. `thm:morphism` proves composition with these types and keeps the complete ledger in its risk inequality. Pathwise, conditional-mean and tail semantics are distinct. Public clocks must be exogenous; prefix dependence cannot be implemented by a free history tape.

`thm:tag` proves total-state necessity from an executable continuation distinction: independent latched tokens must remain exactly answerable after the memory cut. The lower applies to fused and randomized machines, not just a product construction. It permits growing L=R0*D0*Q0, with a matching geometric scale at floor(M/L). This is a nonconstant overhead regime. It does not make every arbitrarily named simulator register mandatory. `thm:robust` retains workspace, program, input precision and physical time in its feasible-region minimax theorem. Optimal shortest-program/runtime/workspace converses below that region remain unproved.

## MR4 — One common minimax class and optimal deficiency

`sec:minimax` defines one eight-member parameter family, one raw protocol, one squared-loss menu, one parameter-independent algorithm class and one oracle baseline independent of the member. All five terms occur simultaneously. Uniform-prior projection and the mandatory-tag cut give the lower; a finite-bit program gives the upper. `prop:floors` proves erasure deficiency equals theta/2 by binary total-variation contraction, not just by an imputation upper bound. The simulator class cannot query the terminal answer before the target forecast. Calibration and numerical ambiguity are compulsory input indistinguishability, not optional implementation inaccuracies.

Section `sec:scope` and `LITERATURE_COMPARISON.md` compare causal-state minimality, controlled predictive representations, bisimulation, finite belief-MDP approximation, filter quantization, positive moment stability, Le Cam/Torgersen comparison and singular quantization. Fifteen references are used in the manuscript. Classical projection, resolvents, Blackwell randomization and minimal predictive states are not claimed new. Specific theorem comparisons are limited to primary text actually checked; no exhaustive priority clearance is asserted.

## MR6 — Exact versus finite-bit algorithms

Appendix `sec:algorithms` supplies separate exact Borel pseudocode and finite-bit routines, paid initialization, rational dyadic grids, approximate nearest-center selection, local state/readout errors, and separate persistent, transient, readonly and time accounts. HMM errors use a fixed safe parameter rectangle. Sensor errors distinguish acquired-report error and unresolved-mean approximation. Holds copy an integer label without fresh rounding. `thm:robust` gives an explicit feasible bit-operation/time region. Unknown or noncomputable constants are not free finite programs.

## MR7 — Quantifiers and constants

The main theorem explicitly indexes horizon-dependent domains, charts and widths, lists constant dependence, distinguishes exact minimizers from computable constant-factor allocations, and states the zero-weight transport condition. Rank-zero/equal-zero assertions use inequalities, not ratios of zeros. The intersecting-strata budget threshold remains explicit. Full ledger raw calls include the selected terminal probe; the main prediction horizon counts preforecast calls. Stopping results remain bounded-horizon and controller certificates do not silently expand to unrestricted control.
