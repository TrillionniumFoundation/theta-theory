\begin{abstract}
A prepared causal experiment generates a predictive quotient and an actual probability law on it. We connect the geometry of this law to implementable finite-memory prediction and to resource-bounded comparison of experiments. The central theorem combines anisotropic acquired-mass estimates with an innovation-localized error process. A positive-kernel resolvent derives its second-moment bound from a one-step Lyapunov inequality, allowing recurrent expanding updates and arbitrarily long exact waits. The resulting checkpoint and online bounds match on the certified class; finite intersecting strata and disappearing widths are included. Causal morphisms transfer a common bounded loss while retaining acquisition, persistent state, workspace, program, precision, and physical-time accounts. A continuation-separation theorem proves a matching total-state lower bound with growing mandatory interface memory. A single explicitly specified robust experiment simultaneously realizes acquisition, geometric resolution, calibration, numerical-information, and optimal-deficiency terms in one minimax risk. Three raw-kernel verifications distinguish Bayesian filtering, singular absorbing acquisition, and recurrent fold-and-refresh dynamics. The geometric and moment arguments precede and do not depend on these realizations.
\end{abstract}
