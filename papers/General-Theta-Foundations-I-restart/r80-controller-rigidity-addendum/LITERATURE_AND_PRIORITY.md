# Priority and scope of the addition

The direct mathematical predecessor is the exact R80 source `c0ac246181c2f4214e3e830952386adffb7d82df`, Section 57, not the earlier independently prepared beta=1/2 exponent proof. R80 already establishes the sharp value asymptotic, complete baseline equality set, local coercivity and matching constructive program. Those results are inherited and explicitly cited. The only added claim is a necessary-and-sufficient description of all first-order nearly optimal six-parameter programs, including the stronger little-o leakage and update-deficit rates. Equality cases of AM-GM, compactness, lower semicontinuity, positive resolvents and Taylor expansion are classical methods, not new discoveries.

The current reading also checked the primary author text [Clarkson--Lin--Glazebrook](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.09310), especially its Theorem 10 and Section 6.1/Appendix C: interior search saddles, consistent-priority mixtures and common-power cycles are pre-existing search theory and are correctly credited by R80. [Bui--Lidbetter--Lin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10908) studies optimal pure search strategies under its common-power convention. Pure word existence and implementation by a counted stochastic controller are different questions. These comparisons do not supply the new equality-case proof and are not a worldwide novelty search.

The [full R80 literature ledger](../r80-stability-and-saturation/LITERATURE_AND_PRIORITY.md) and its primary-source records remain authoritative for the inherited manuscript's wider history. This addition makes no claim of firstness for fractional-power optimization, sensitivity analysis, or finite-state control. There is no new independent human specialist priority opinion. The original four-journal goal is retained, but the modest scope of this additional optimizer classification must be judged honestly alongside the full inherited paper.
