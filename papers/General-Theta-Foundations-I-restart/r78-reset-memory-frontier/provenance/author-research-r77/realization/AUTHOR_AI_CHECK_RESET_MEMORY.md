## Checked steps

1. **Optimal first row.** The true cost of any controller is bounded below by its first-row start operator, evaluated at every initial density matrix. A robust optimum therefore has initial row in `P_*`; this does not assume the optimum continuation is followed by the given controller.
2. **Dual exposure.** At an attaining dual preparation, the first start-operator term is at least `V_*` and the full robust-optimal cost is at most `V_*`. Nonnegative Bellman residuals must vanish. Every first failure report has positive probability because each `E_ai` is positive definite, even when the dual state is singular or the first row has a proper support. Thus every reset state is actually exposed at the same equality preparation.
3. **Ties and counted labels.** For every reached label after report `i`, its row has support in `A_i^*`. A row compatible with several reset states has its whole support in the intersection of their optimal-action sets; choosing any support action therefore hits all those sets. Reports outside `I(p^1)` cannot use label 1. At most `M-1` selected support actions yield the necessary transversal. No assumption of unique optimal actions or deterministic updates was used.
4. **Attaining construction.** One optimal initial row and a smallest transversal of the remaining optimal-action sets suffice, with report-only deterministic routing. Every continuation is Bellman optimal at the actual reset state. Finite-prefix Bellman equality bounds expected cost by `v_i`; the strictly positive cost floor gives finite expected duration. The bounded terminal potential vanishes in the limit, proving exact continuation cost and properness rather than assuming them.
5. **Attainment and algebraic procedure.** `P_*` is nonempty by the inherited compact optimization. The integer cover expression depends on finitely many supports, so its minimum is realized. Enumeration of deterministic proper selectors recovers `v`, since one selector is simultaneously optimal. Positivity of Hermitian matrices and exact support inequalities are real-algebraic conditions after splitting real and imaginary parts. The claimed finite procedure is valid without a claim of efficient or already executed general elimination.
6. **Gap lower.** After the first failure `i`, every possible chosen label has expected next residual at least `r_i=min_k sum_a p_a^k d_ia`. Keeping this residual and dropping the later nonnegative ones gives the operator `K_M` at every initial state. Taking its largest eigenvalue and then minimizing over a compact product of simplexes gives an attained nonnegative `D_M`. Controllers of infinite worst-state cost satisfy the lower trivially.
7. **Gap iff insufficient memory.** If `D_M=0`, the initial row is optimal and evaluation at an attaining dual state forces every `r_i` to vanish, using the same strictly positive first-report coefficients. The minimizing rows can then be connected by the previously verified routing construction to implement an optimum with `M` labels. Conversely optimum rows make all `r_i` zero; unused rows can pad the controller when `M` is larger. This proves the exact positivity criterion, not equality with the whole constrained cost frontier.
8. **Quantum instrument and hidden internal measurement.** Effects `(r/s)I` and success effect `(1-r)I` form a valid trace-preserving instrument via the displayed measure-and-prepare Kraus maps. Diagonal-basis measurement with hidden outcome and the declared cost realizes `G_a`. The intermediate basis outcome and realized cost are explicitly unavailable to the control port; otherwise extra observed information would change the problem.
9. **Exact reduction.** With `v_i=1/(1-r)`, the reset-state eigenvalues of the first operator are at most `2+r/(1-r)`, while the additional coordinates have values `3+r/(1-r)+p_j`. They strictly dominate for every row `p`. Minimizing `max_j p_j` gives the unique uniform row. Since every `A_i` is proper, none accepts that full support; the cover theorem gives `m_*=t+1`. Fixing rational `r` gives polynomial-size matrix data from the incidence table. Adding an unused action makes the input sets proper without changing their transversal number.
10. **Failure of unqualified exposure.** In the displayed two-dimensional example, `v=2`, `L_a=diag(2,6)` and `L_b=diag(3,5)`. A row placing weight `x` on `a` has first operator `diag(3-x,5+x)`, so the unique optimum is `x=0` and value five. Constant `b` has cost four from the reset state and five from the other basis state, hence operator `diag(4,5)`. It is one-label robust optimal. The dual worst-case state has zero failure probability, explaining exactly why its continuation residual need not vanish at a reached reset state.
