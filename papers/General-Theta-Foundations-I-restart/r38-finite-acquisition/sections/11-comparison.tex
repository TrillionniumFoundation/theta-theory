\section{Comparison, consequences and scope}\label{sec:comparison}
Sequential quantization has long treated causal encoders rather than independent static codebooks; Borkar, Mitter and Tatikonda \cite{BMT2001} supply a central comparison. The present observer also chooses the acquisition action. Its retained labels are the entire online information available for both control and prediction. A belief-grid approximation accompanied by a separately maintained exact filter would not have this resource interpretation.

The existence of near-optimal finite-state controllers is not new. Yu and Bertsekas \cite{YuBertsekas2008} establish such an average-cost approximation result for a finite POMDP under their constant-optimal-average-cost hypothesis. General finite approximations of Borel MDPs are treated by Saldi, Y\"uksel and Linder \cite{SYL2016}. Those results motivate, but do not themselves give, the joint acquired-density lower, the total-label rate, the continuous-report calibrated implementation and the budget-independent entrance and perturbation certificates proved here. Our synthesis proof is a finite exhaustive approximation argument, not a computational improvement on those theories.

For partially observed average control, Demirci, Kara and Y\"uksel \cite{DKY2024} derive average-cost optimality and approximation conclusions through nonlinear-filter contraction. We use a standard span-contraction Bellman argument after proving its feedback-uniform acquired-overlap premise. The step not supplied by that Bellman equation is the invariant calibration and mixing of the executed finite observer. A filter can be stable while an observer's memory stays trapped in incompatible classes. Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} concerns the joint finite-label instrument, not just the full-history filter.

Value-directed compression \cite{PB2002} focuses on preserving the decision-relevant geometry of a POMDP. The Bayes-risk/Bregman connection \cite{Varshney2011} explains why curvature is naturally task dependent. Here the protected squared task makes conditional means exact and gives a uniform strong-concavity lower. The upper uses a nonsmooth Bellman bias and actual mass per grid cell. We do not assert that a different loss has the same exponent without an appropriate replacement curvature certificate.

The finite-memory belief approximation analysis of Kim \cite{Kim2026} controls errors using a contracting compressor and fixes the policy and closed-loop execution in its comparison. We distinguish that comparison from the infimum over all adaptive acquisition policies in $J_*(n)$. We do not claim that an arbitrary compressor is contractive. The common-grid implementation in this paper receives a separate positive-cone proof of joint mixing, before its optimal stationary calibration is selected.

The general hypotheses remain sufficient, not necessary. Whole-base interiority is used exactly when the remainder in a positive-cone comparison is passed through a raw instrument. Local acquired overlap without robust accessibility does not give our uniform observer minorization. Common small-ball mass without a global legal recursive cover does not supply an implementable upper. Section~\ref{sec:audit} records these distinctions. The two raw realizations do not reverse these implications.

For the stated class, the finite-acquisition conclusion improves the erasure-dependent entrance term and precision tolerance of the retained stationary article \cite{GTFStationary}: there is no vanishing erasure rate and no extra label lane. The conditional geometry remains that of the actually executed law. The singular result retains acquired mixture weights and permits the support dimension to change at every call. It does not remove every degeneracy: arbitrary nondominated kernels, infinite-dimensional predictive closure, unknown-kernel learning, nonsummable inaccessible classes, arbitrary singular stratifications, optimal program/workspace/time tradeoffs and unrestricted unbounded stopping require additional theorems. No general impossibility claim is made about those tasks.
