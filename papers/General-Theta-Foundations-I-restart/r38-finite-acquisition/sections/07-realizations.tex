\section{Two raw experiment realizations}\label{sec:realizations}
The general theorem was proved without using either model below. The finite test closures, acquired laws and accessibility certificates are now derived from their physical kernels. Several density calculations are inherited from \cite{GTFBayes,GTFControlled,GTFStationary}; they are included to expose every hypothesis of the new joint-ensemble lift.

\begin{theorem}[Controlled Gaussian observations]\label{thm:gaussian}
Let the physical state have $q\ge2$ values. Let $P$ be invertible and column stochastic with $P_{ij}\ge a_0>0$. An action chooses one of finitely many Gaussian sensors: conditional on pre-transition state $i$, its report is $N(m_i^a,\Sigma_a)$ on $\R^{q-1}$, with $\Sigma_a$ positive definite and full-rank likelihood-difference matrix. After the report the state transitions by $P$. A protected categorical audit of the current state is scored by squared probability loss. Then Theorem~\ref{thm:main} holds with $d=q-1$; the common-cell observer needs no erasure mechanism and has an entrance term $C/n$ uniform in $M$. The comparison permits every full-history adaptive sensor choice.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The physical probability vector $u$ is separated by the executable categorical audit. Every further finite instrument word has a linear expectation in $u$; hence its coordinates are exactly the predictive quotient, with the mixture simplex as base and a Borel preparation section. The raw instrument, written relative to Lebesgue measure, is
\[
 L_a(y)u=P\diag(f_1^a(y),\ldots,f_q^a(y))u.
\]
Replacing Lebesgue measure by any positive normalized mixture of these Gaussian densities gives the reference probability formulation without changing this instrument. It is positive and integrable. The likelihood is $\sum_i u_i f_i^a(y)$, and the normalized output is $P$ times the ordinary Bayes probability. The task has $g(u)=1-\norm u^2$ on the simplex hyperplane, with its actual audit sampling noise retained.

Take $K=P\Delta_{q-1}$ and $D=\{u\in\Delta_{q-1}:u_i\ge a_0/2\}$. These have the required relative buffer. Every positive physical input, even a boundary preparation outside $D$, has normalized output in $K$; this verifies \eqref{eq:raw-interior} on the entire physical cone. The likelihood is positive on $D$ and its total-variation dependence on $u$ is Lipschitz. Dominated integration gives weak continuity. For $x,q'\in K$, $x_i\ge a_0$ and $q'_i\le1$, so \eqref{eq:cone-comparison} holds, for example, with $\kappa=a_0/2$.

Before applying $P$, write $z_i=\log(w_i/w_q)$ for the Bayes vector $w$. Then
\[
 z_i=\log(u_i/u_q)+(m_i^a-m_q^a)^T\Sigma_a^{-1}y
 -\tfrac12\bigl[(m_i^a)^T\Sigma_a^{-1}m_i^a-(m_q^a)^T\Sigma_a^{-1}m_q^a\bigr].
\]
Under the actual report law this is a finite mixture of nonsingular Gaussians. The compact input domain and finite sensor set give a uniform bound
$C\exp(-c\norm z^2)$ on their density. Constants include a positive determinant lower bound for the likelihood-difference matrices and bounds on the covariance eigenvalues. The inverse logistic Jacobian is a fixed multiple of $(\prod_iw_i)^{-1}\le C\exp(C\norm z)$. Gaussian tails dominate that factor throughout the simplex, including near every face. The invertible affine map $P$ supplies the density upper on $K$.

On a fixed full-dimensional compact interior simplex $W_*$, the same Gaussian mixtures have a uniform strictly positive density lower. Thus $\mu_{a,u}\ge\epsilon\zeta$ on $PW_*$, with $\zeta$ its normalized relative volume, uniformly for $u\in D$ and every sensor. We may set $G=K$, $k=0$. This is an actual mass bound; no tail or unsuccessful acquisition is discarded. Hyperplane grids supply the boundary-free cells. Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} now yields a one-step joint ensemble minorization for every local common-grid observer. Neither contraction of the sensor selector nor contraction of each posterior path is assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Noncommuting controlled quantum instruments]\label{thm:quantum}
Let $D_q\ge2$, $0<\beta,\lambda<1$, and let $U_a$ be any finite nonempty family of unitaries. On the unit ball of traceless Hermitian matrices, with normalized Hilbert--Schmidt volume, set
\[
 E_H=I+\beta H,\quad K_H=E_H^{1/2},\quad \tau=I/D_q,
\]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantum-instrument}
 L_a(H)\sigma=\lambda U_aK_H\sigma K_HU_a^*
          +(1-\lambda)\tr(E_H\sigma)\tau.
\end{equation}
Choose the action before the report $H$, and use a fixed informationally complete finite POVM for the single protected audit with categorical squared loss. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies with $d=D_q^2-1$ in its induced probability chart, uniformly in $n,M$ for each fixed experiment. The block length and constants may deteriorate as $\lambda$ or $\beta$ approach a degenerate endpoint. No common eigenbasis or free quantum observer memory is available.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The effects integrate to $I$, since $\int H=0$, and satisfy
$(1-\beta)I\le E_H\le(1+\beta)I$. Equation~\eqref{eq:quantum-instrument} is a completely positive normalized instrument. It models an unsharp measurement followed by the chosen unitary and a depolarizing channel. Its report density $1+\beta\tr(H\sigma)$ is genuinely state dependent. Informational completeness separates the density matrix; every future instrument-word expectation is affine in it. This derives the predictive quotient and positive test cone from raw experiments. The probability chart is an injective affine map; use its Euclidean Brier norm. Estimates in Hilbert--Schmidt coordinates change only fixed chart constants.

Set $K=\{\sigma\ge(1-\lambda)\tau:\tr\sigma=1\}$ and
$D=\{\sigma\ge(1-\lambda)\tau/2:\tr\sigma=1\}$. The raw image of every positive matrix lies in $C_K$, including rank-deficient input matrices. Report continuity and operator domination follow from compactness and linearity. Since every normalized $q\in K$ satisfies $q\le I$, input $x\in K$ dominates $\kappa q$ with, for example, $\kappa=(1-\lambda)/(2D_q)$.

For fixed interior $\rho$, the map
$E\mapsto\sqrt E\rho\sqrt E/\tr(E\rho)$ from positive trace-$D_q$ effects to positive trace-one matrices has inverse
\[
 B_{\rho,\sigma}=\rho^{-1/2}(\rho^{1/2}\sigma\rho^{1/2})^{1/2}\rho^{-1/2},
 \qquad E=D_q B_{\rho,\sigma}^{\,2}/\tr B_{\rho,\sigma}^{\,2}.
\]
The positive solution of $B\rho B=\sigma$ is unique, which verifies both inverse identities. On $\rho\in D$ and the compact effect ball, all relevant eigenvalue floors are positive. The inverse derivative is uniformly bounded. Thus the forward Jacobian is bounded away from zero; the final affine depolarizing/unitary map is invertible on the trace hyperplane. Its actual report density is bounded, so the acquired row has a uniform full-dimensional upper density. Noncommuting effects are included.

At $\rho=\tau$ the normalized output is
$\tau+(\lambda\beta/D_q)U_aHU_a^*$. An interior effect ball therefore maps over a common neighborhood $B_*$ of $\tau$. The smooth inverse and compactness show that for all $\rho$ in a sufficiently small closed ball $G$ about $\tau$, every action's actual row dominates a common $\epsilon\zeta$, where $\zeta$ is normalized volume on a smaller $B_*$. This proves local actual overlap; it is not asserted globally in one step.

Use $V(\rho)=\norm{\rho-\tau}_{\HS}$ and $E_a=\{\norm H_{\HS}\le s\}$, the same quiet report ball for all actions. The matrix square-root estimate and the likelihood lower bound give
\[
 V(F_a(H,\rho))\le\lambda V(\rho)+C\lambda\beta s
       \quad(H\in E_a,\ \rho\in K).
\]
Indeed expand numerator and denominator using
$\norm{\sqrt{I+\beta H}-I}_{\rm op}\le C\beta s$ and
$\tr(E_H\rho)\ge1-\beta s$. Each quiet report has actual probability at least $(1-\beta)s^d$, independently of the earlier actions and reports conditional on their history. Choose $s$ so that the drift floor is below one quarter of the radius of $G$, choose $k$ so that $\lambda^k\sup_KV$ is below one quarter of it, and then choose $s_0$ so that accumulated grid error is below one quarter of it. The fixed chart equivalence supplies the $L_V$ in \eqref{eq:quiet-margin}. All certificates are now uniform over arbitrary action rows and all sufficiently fine common grids. The good reports are paid acquisitions; the proof has not postselected a new physical preparation. Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-mixing} gives the joint minorization and finite-acquisition conclusion. No infinite-dimensional operator closure is used.
\end{proof}
