\section{Finite precision without a shrinking mixing gap}\label{sec:compilation}
\begin{proposition}[Effective finite-state realization]\label{prop:compilation}
In addition to Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, assume effective compact nets in every relative cell closure, computable instrument integrals, effective certificates for $b,\gamma,s_0$, and uniform effective boundary/integration moduli for the chosen grid. For $M\ge M_0$ there is a finite search over actual $M$-label programs, with within-cell codepoints in finite $\sigma$-nets and dyadic action rows of denominator $Q$, returning an observer with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compiled}
 R_\infty\le\rho+CM^{-2/d}
       +\frac{L_0b}{\gamma}\tau_{\rm tab}
       +\eta+\delta_{\rm out}^2.
\end{equation}
Here $\tau_{\rm tab}$ is a certified uniform ensemble-map approximation error, $\eta$ is the finite evaluation/search slack, and $\delta_{\rm out}^2$ is a certified weighted squared readout error. Every enumerated exact common-grid table has the same $b,\gamma$ mixing certificate, without erasure. A legal finite-arithmetic execution with per-call ensemble error $\tau_{\rm run}$ adds at most $L_0b\tau_{\rm run}/\gamma$ to its finite-average risk; if homogeneous and $b\tau_{\rm run}<\gamma$, it also has an attracting invariant ensemble. The same program uses at most $M$ retained labels.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Uniform interiority of $K$ in $B$ gives both $z\le_C C_B(1,q)$ for normalized physical $z$ and $q\in K$, and a two-sided bound
\[
 -C_B\norm{q-q'}(1,q)\le_C(0,q'-q)
       \le_C C_B\norm{q-q'}(1,q).
\]
After applying the positive instrument and normalizing, these imply a report-uniform Lipschitz bound for $F_a(y,q)$ in $q\in K$. They also dominate the physical report law at any normalized input by a fixed multiple of its report law at the programmed representative. A representative error $\sigma$ changes a grid decision only within a strip of width $C\sigma$ of a boundary. The actual density bound and the supplied boundary modulus bound the physical mass of that strip. On a fixed $m$-cell grid a computable bound of the form
\[
 \tau_{\rm tab}\le C_m\sigma+C|A|/Q
\]
is available. Both constants include norm equivalence with the $C_K$ base norm. To see that this norm causes no lost factor depending on $m$, each misrouted positive physical contribution is in $C_K$; routing it to two different labels costs at most twice its mass in the sum base norm. Action-row rounding obeys the same estimate. More general supplied strip moduli can be used instead.

Choose each approximating representative in the closure of its own fixed cell. All update tables then remain local at the original mesh, not at an accumulated numerical-history radius. Enumerate these nets and all dyadic action rows. Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-mixing} applies to every candidate, whether or not its actions minimize the Bellman equation. The family contains a table approximating the calibrated fixed-point observer. Proposition~\ref{prop:perturbation}, initially with that observer's fixed readouts, bounds its invariant risk increase by $L_0b\tau_{\rm tab}/\gamma$.

For each finite candidate, compute its actual invariant ensemble by certified finite instrument integration and positive iteration. The truncation error after $t$ calls is at most $2(1-\gamma)^{\lfloor t/b\rfloor}$; integration errors are amplified by at most $b/\gamma$. Recenter the readouts at the conditional means of this actual candidate ensemble. This cannot increase risk. Very small-mass labels need not be divided by an interval containing zero: when their total upper mass is below an assigned squared-error allowance divided by $L_0$, use arbitrary legal readouts there. Refine all other mass intervals until positive lower bounds allow division. Because there are finitely many labels, the positive masses are eventually resolved and the zero masses can be charged by their shrinking upper bounds. Approximate the remaining means to achieve total weighted squared error $\delta_{\rm out}^2$. Finite interval evaluation of all candidates and further refinement supplies slack $\eta$ and proves \eqref{eq:compiled}.

A running finite-arithmetic interpreter still sees the original physical report kernel. Approximate comparisons, tails and fallbacks are bounded by a certified uniform per-call ensemble discrepancy. They do not redefine the experiment by conditioning on a bounded report region. Proposition~\ref{prop:perturbation} supplies the uniform risk estimate; its block contraction argument gives attraction when the error is small enough. A homogeneous implementation must not retain a growing precision counter or an uncounted random seed.
\end{proof}

The search size is at most
\[
 \prod_{s=1}^m|\operatorname{Net}(\overline C_s,\sigma)|\,
                 (Q+1)^{m|A|}
\]
before finite readout refinement. No efficient global synthesis claim is made. To retain the geometric risk order it is sufficient to take
$\tau_{\rm tab}+\tau_{\rm run}\lesssim M^{-2/d}$ and
$\eta+\delta_{\rm out}^2\lesssim M^{-2/d}$, with constants depending on $b/\gamma$. Unlike a vanishing-erasure construction, this does not require an additional factor $M^{-2/d}$ in the ensemble error tolerance.

The persistent label uses $\lceil\log_2M\rceil$ bits. A direct table description has upper size
\[
 O\bigl(Md\log(1/\sigma)+M|A|\log Q+Md\,p_{\rm out}\bigr)
       +\text{shared primitive and grid description}.
\]
Its workspace is bounded by $W_{\rm primitive}(p)+O(dp+\log M+|A|\log Q)$ for a declared primitive precision $p$; time includes primitive evaluation, cell search and fresh coin generation. Offline enumeration and integration have their own costs. The readout precision $p_{\rm out}$ must be certified using actual label masses; it is not automatically a universal function of $\delta_{\rm out}$. The external audit clock and simulator states are separate resources. Effective Gaussian tail/log-odds and compact quantum spectral-floor certificates provide the stated integration and strip assumptions when their model parameters are effectively given. Arbitrary real kernels are not presumed to have finite descriptions.
