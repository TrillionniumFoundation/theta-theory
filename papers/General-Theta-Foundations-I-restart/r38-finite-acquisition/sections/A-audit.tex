\section{Interfaces and distinctions used in the proof}\label{sec:audit}
The predictive-quotient assertion assumes a finite separating test realization and a Borel realization section. It does not infer either from a formal coordinate count. Positivity gives the affine unnormalized instrument, and likelihood division is made only off a common zero set. The Gaussian and quantum constructions explicitly verify that the protected audit separates the physical state, so these are predictive coordinates rather than an arbitrary enlargement.

Local calibration is not global calibration. For example, let two incoming rows be point masses at distinct values $x_0,x_1$ and let each use a single output label. Each row's one-center quantization error is zero, but merging the two rows into the same label has strictly positive conditional variance whenever both rows have positive mass. Even vanishing pooling error at one update does not prove that the centroids repeat at the next update. Lemma~\ref{lem:fixedpoint} solves the entire invariant ensemble equation, and Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-mixing} proves attraction for the resulting fixed program.

Mixing of the physical instrument alone is insufficient for mixing of an arbitrary observer. A program that never changes its incoming label has several invariant label classes, even when the physical kernel is independent fresh sampling at each call. The common-grid locality hypothesis rules out this construction by forcing labels to name cells of their programmed acquired response. The positive-cone remainder in Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} lies initially in $C$, not necessarily in $C_K$; positivity only on a restricted normalized domain would not justify its image. This is why \eqref{eq:raw-interior} is stated on the entire physical cone.

The joint minorization describes ensembles of executable test responses. It does not identify their base norm with total variation on an unrepresented microscopic path space. Proposition~\ref{prop:morphism} therefore assumes a separate complete stopped-law deficiency when transferring between experiments. Conversely, finite-arithmetic report rounding is not claimed to be close in total variation to a continuous report law; the relevant bound is on the instrument after its label decision and on the scored readout.

A homogeneous observer uses one table at every call. The uniform audit request is generated by an independent paid external mechanism and is not an internally exact deadline. A visible end marker, a timer supplied to the observer or a readout depending on an uncounted phase would change the legal resource model. The preparation and final audit are physical calls. The finite program, its work memory and computation are not measured by $\log_2 M$ alone.

Finally, the mass density and lower-dimensional component laws used for lower bounds are actual acquired probabilities. Rare report paths in the accessibility proof remain subprobabilities of their original mass. They are not postselected free preparations. A similarity between two formal supports gives none of these mass, recurrence or implementation statements.
