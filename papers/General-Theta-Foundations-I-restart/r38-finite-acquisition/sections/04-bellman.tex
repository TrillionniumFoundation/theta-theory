\section{Average-cost bias and acquired curvature}\label{sec:bellman}
\begin{lemma}[Actual feedback-uniform entrance]\label{lem:feedback-overlap}
Definition~\ref{def:class} implies \eqref{eq:overlap}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After the first report the exact predictive state is in $K$. When $k>0$, require the next $k$ reports to lie in $E_a$ for their actual chosen actions. Successive conditional integration gives mass at least $v^k$, uniformly over every feedback policy. The exact recursion satisfies the drift bound with mesh error zero, so its state is in $G$. Its next acquired row dominates $\epsilon\zeta$. When $k=0$, that next row has this overlap directly. All $k+2$ calls occur in the actual experiment; the restricted report words are only a subprobability witness. No policy is selected after observing a future report.
\end{proof}
For the following inherited Bellman argument set $b_*=k+2$ and $\epsilon_*=\epsilon\theta$.
\begin{lemma}[Block span contraction]\label{lem:span}
Define $\mathcal T f=g+\min_aK_af$ on $C(D)$. Under \eqref{eq:overlap},
\[
 \osc_D(\mathcal T^{b_*}f-\mathcal T^{b_*}q)
 \le(1-\epsilon_*)\osc_D(f-q).
\]
There is a solution of \eqref{eq:acoe}, unique modulo constants in $h$, with the bounds in \eqref{eq:bias-bounds}. It is strongly concave in the stated task chart.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed starting state, compare a $b_*$-step policy minimizing the terminal-$f$ objective with one minimizing the terminal-$q$ objective. Finite actions and weak continuity give Borel selectors by finite backward induction; arbitrarily minimizing policies give the same inequalities. Running costs cancel when the same policy is used in this comparison. Thus their difference is bounded above and below by terminal expectations of $f-q$ under legal policies. Every such terminal law is
$\epsilon_*\zeta+(1-\epsilon_*)\widetilde\mu$. Therefore every value of $\mathcal T^{b_*}f-\mathcal T^{b_*}q$ lies between
\[
 \epsilon_*\zeta(f-q)+(1-\epsilon_*)\inf_D(f-q)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \epsilon_*\zeta(f-q)+(1-\epsilon_*)\sup_D(f-q).
\]
This proves the contraction, including feedback policies with different state-dependent minimizing actions.

The quotient $C(D)/\R$ with oscillation norm is complete. The translation-equivariant $\mathcal T$ induces a map whose $b_*$th iterate is a strict contraction. Its unique fixed class is also fixed by $\mathcal T$, since $\mathcal T$ commutes with its iterate. Choose its representative $h$ to vanish at a fixed point of $D$. Then $\mathcal T h=h+\rho$. Since $\osc_D(\mathcal T^{b_*}0)\le b_*\osc_Dg$, the contraction gives $H\le b_*\osc_Dg/\epsilon_*$. This is the usual span-contraction argument, applied here to feedback-uniform acquired laws rather than to a latent transition alone \cite{Dobrushin1956,Kurano1986}.

If $f$ is concave, its perspective $w f(v/w)$ is concave on the positive cone of its domain. Hence $K_af$ is concave by \eqref{eq:raw}. The minimum of finitely many concave functions is concave. Iteration from zero and uniform convergence modulo constants show that $h$ is concave. Equation~\eqref{eq:acoe} gives
$h-g=\min_aK_ah-\rho$ concave; since $g=c-\norm{\cdot}^2$ with affine $c$, this proves strong concavity without a Hessian assumption.

For a concave function on $D$, extending the line through two points of $K$ by length $r$ at either end bounds each intermediate slope in absolute value by $H/r$. Thus $h$ is $H/r$-Lipschitz on $K$, including nonsmooth switching surfaces. Bellman minimizers in \eqref{eq:acoe} exist and have a Borel selection. Summing the Bellman inequality along any full-history strategy gives a lower average cost $\rho-H/n$ at cuts $0,\ldots,n-1$; a minimizing selector gives an upper $\rho+H/n$. Shifting these cuts to $1,\ldots,n$ changes the average by at most $\osc_Dg/n$. This proves the asserted bound for $J_*(n)$ and full-history average optimality of $\rho$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Boundary-valid acquired-curvature estimate]\label{lem:curvature}
Let $v$ be concave and $L$-Lipschitz on compact convex $K\subset\R^d$, and let $\mu$ be a probability with density at most $\Lambda$. A single grid partition $\mathcal P_m$ of $K$ with at most $m$ cells satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curvature}
 \sum_{C\in\mathcal P_m}\mu(C)v\left(\frac{\int_Cx\,d\mu}{\mu(C)}\right)
       -\int v\,d\mu\le C_{K,d}\Lambda Lm^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
Zero-mass cells are omitted. The grid is independent of $v,\mu$; the centroids are not.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the acquired-mass curvature mechanism of \cite{GTFControlled}, recalled to make the stationary proof self-contained. The convex function $-v$ extends to a convex $L$-Lipschitz function $f$ on $\R^d$: take the supremum of its supporting planes at interior points of $K$. Interior slopes have norm at most $L$, and continuity gives equality on the boundary. Its distributional Laplacian $\kappa=\Delta f$ is a positive locally finite measure.

For a cube $C$ of side $s$ and center $z$, mollify $f$ and subtract its tangent plane at $z$, obtaining a nonnegative convex $w$ with $w(z)=0$. On a ball of radius $R=2\sqrt d\,s$, submean estimates bound $w$ on $C$ by a fixed multiple of the average of $w$ on the surrounding sphere. Convex radial slopes give $w(z+R\theta)\le R\partial_Rw(z+R\theta)$. Integration and the divergence theorem therefore yield
\[
 \sup_Cw\le C_dR^{2-d}\int_{B_R(z)}\Delta f.
\]
For $d=1$ the sphere consists of the two endpoints and the same calculation applies. Tangent contributions cancel at the conditional centroid, and its value of $w$ is nonnegative. Passing through mollification with a slightly enlarged ball proves
\[
 \int_C f\,d\mu-\mu(C)f(\E[X\mid C])
 \le C_ds^{2-d}\mu(C)\kappa(\overline B_{3\sqrt d s}(z)).
\]
A smooth cutoff on a fixed neighborhood of $K$ bounds its curvature mass by
$\int\chi\,d\kappa=-\int\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla f\le L\int|\nabla\chi|$.
The enlarged balls have bounded overlap and $\mu(C)\le\Lambda s^d$. Summation gives $C_{K,d}\Lambda Ls^2$. Divide a bounding cube into $\lfloor m^{1/d}\rfloor$ parts per coordinate, intersect with $K$, and discard empty cells. This proves \eqref{eq:curvature}, with the case $m=1$ absorbed by its fixed constant. No positive distance of the acquired support from the boundary of $K$ is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Actual-law small-ball lower]\label{lem:smallball}
Every mixture of the acquired rows in Definition~\ref{def:class} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smallball}
 q_M(\mu)\ge c_d\Lambda^{-2/d}M^{-2/d},\qquad
 c_d=\tfrac12(2v_d)^{-2/d},
\end{equation}
where $v_d$ is the Euclidean unit-ball volume.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The density bound survives mixing. The union of radius-$r_0$ balls about $M$ readouts has probability at most $\Lambda Mv_dr_0^d$. For $r_0=(2\Lambda Mv_d)^{-1/d}$ at least half the actual mass lies outside. Randomized assignment cannot improve the pointwise minimum over its finite readouts. This is a bound on an acquired law, not on a formally reachable set.
\end{proof}
