\section{Prepared experiments and the protected task}\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{From executable tests to positive instruments}
Fix a time-homogeneous prepared causal experiment on standard Borel spaces, a finite nonempty action set $A$, and its legal finite future experiments and bounded readouts. A strategy uses only past reports, actions and independent private randomness. Histories are predictively equivalent when they have the same legal action interface and all executable future-test expectations agree.

We require a finite separating affine test realization. More precisely, the constant test and finitely many executable tests give a Borel vector $u(h)\in\R^d$ whose attained image is Borel and has a Borel realization section. Equality of these coordinates is equivalent to equality of all future tests. The unnormalized expectation of every coordinate test jointly with a next report is affine in the incoming coordinates. Realizable random mixtures form a compact convex base $B\subset\R^d$ with nonempty interior in this chart. We write
\[
 C=\{(w,wu):w\ge0,\ u\in B\}\subset\R^{d+1},\qquad e(w,v)=w.
\]
This is a closed pointed generating cone, and $e$ is its normalization functional. The associated base norm is
\[
 \norm z_B=\inf\{e(z_+)+e(z_-):z=z_+-z_-,\ z_\pm\in C\}.
\]
On a finite direct sum of label cones we use the sum base norm.

For each action $a$ let $\lambda_a$ be a reference probability and $L_a(y)$ a Borel, integrable family of positive linear maps on $C$, with $\bar L_a=\int L_a(y)\lambda_a(dy)$ preserving $e$. Positivity is positivity on this cone; complete positivity is separately verified for quantum instruments. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw}
 L_a(y)(1,u)=(\ell_a(y,u),U_a(y,u)),\qquad
 F_a(y,u)=U_a(y,u)/\ell_a(y,u).
\end{equation}
Both $\ell_a$ and $U_a$ are affine in $u$. All identities hold for every incoming state off one reference-null set for each action. At zero likelihood the numerator is zero by positivity and bounded tests, and no normalized state is used on that event. The actual acquired row and its integration operator are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row}
 \mu_{a,u}=(F_a(\cdot,u))_\#(\ell_a(\cdot,u)\lambda_a),\qquad
 K_af(u)=\int f(v)\mu_{a,u}(dv).
\end{equation}
A concrete experiment may first be given on a larger physical state space. Its asserted finite test image and positive maps must then be derived from that experiment, as in Section~\ref{sec:realizations}.

\begin{lemma}[Predictive realization]\label{lem:quotient}
Under the preceding hypotheses, the attained test image realizes the predictive quotient as a Borel causal state space. It is minimal among sufficient Borel statistics whose attained images have Borel realization sections. Report and update kernels descend jointly in the finite action coordinate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equal test coordinates imply equal future tests by the separating realization hypothesis; the converse holds because the coordinates are executable tests. For another sufficient statistic, compose its realization section with $h\mapsto u(h)$. Sufficiency makes this map independent of representatives, proving Borel factorization. The common null sets and the affine instrument identities give Borel descended kernels. Successive integration proves realization of every legal finite continuation. This proves the asserted minimality; it does not assert that an arbitrary measurable quotient is standard Borel. The larger mixture domain $B$ need not be the support of any one acquired law.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Prediction, acquisition and audit}
A fixed bounded executable audit $Z$ has conditional mean $u$ and affine second moment $c(u)$. A response $r\in B$, committed before the audit, has squared loss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
 \E[\norm{Z-r}^2\mid u]=c(u)-2\langle u,r\rangle+\norm r^2,
 \qquad g(u)=c(u)-\norm u^2.
\end{equation}
An injective affine probability chart of a categorical or informationally complete measurement gives the same formula on its affine image, with the induced Euclidean norm. Orthogonal components of the actual audit noise remain in $c$; they are not discarded. Assume the loss is bounded by $L_0$ on the stated response set.

An infinite unaudited reference execution is used to define costs, not to perform incompatible audits at all times. For an integer $n\ge1$, an external auditor independently chooses $T$ uniformly from $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, hides $T$, and requests the response immediately after report $T$. It then performs the single protected audit and ends the experiment. Before that request the controller sees only the prescribed acquisition reports. The expected risk is the average of the potential single-audit risks at cuts $1,\ldots,n$. For a quantum experiment no intermediate audit is physically inserted.

This protocol uses at most $n$ raw acquisition calls, one initial preparation and one audit, hence a pathwise physical-call budget $N=n+2$. Its expected raw-report count is $(n+1)/2$, not $n$. The auditor pays for its uniform draw and an $\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$-bit counter. The observer is not given that counter or an impending-last-report signal. This external clock is recorded separately from observer memory and physical time.

Write $X_t$ for the full-history predictive vector on the reference execution. The full-history comparator may use every legal adaptive action strategy, including its own time and workspace; its risk is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full}
 J_*(n)=\inf_\pi \frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n\E_\pi g(X_t).
\end{equation}
A checkpoint has the same unrestricted acquisition history and, when requested, encodes it into $M$ labels with one label-only decoder. Its acquisition workspace is not identified with $M$.

A homogeneous finite observer has a single set $S$ of at most $M$ labels, an initial label, a fixed action distribution $p_s$ at each label, a Borel update distribution $\gamma(j\mid s,a,y)$ and a fixed readout $r_s$. Its only retained dynamic variable is its label. A fresh action/update coin is independent of the physical state given that label; a retained seed would count as state. Neither a history tape, a real posterior register, a phase counter nor the previous report is available at the next call. The external request reads the existing label and ends the run. Put $\calR_{\rm hom}(n,M)=\inf R_n$ over these programs. Their tables may depend on the known experiment and on $M$; the controller constructed below works for every $n$ and every initial preparation. Known real table entries are counted as program data, not as costless bit strings.
