\section{Lifting acquired mass to an executed observer}\label{sec:calibration}
The full-history process and a finite observer need not have the same mixing properties. The following lift is the step that connects them.

\begin{lemma}[Positive-cone lift of a local acquired component]\label{lem:lift}
Fix a common partition $\{C_j\}_{j=1}^m$ and representatives
$q_s\in\overline C_s\subset K$, with arbitrary action distributions $p_s$. Send report $y$ under action $a$ to the unique cell containing $F_a(y,q_s)$. Define
\[
 (Z_m^\circ)_j=\int_{C_j}(1,x)\,\zeta(dx).
\]
Then $Z_m^\circ\in C_K^m$ is normalized. For every nonnegative ensemble supported on labels with $q_s\in G$, its next ensemble satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-lift}
 PZ\ge_{C_K^m}\kappa\epsilon e_{\rm tot}(Z)Z_m^\circ.
\end{equation}
No calibration of the input ensemble is needed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For completeness, choose $a>0$ with $K+B_a\subset B$ and let $D_K$ be the diameter of $K$. Taking $\kappa>0$ sufficiently small that
$\kappa D_K/(1-\kappa)\le a$ gives
$(x-\kappa q)/(1-\kappa)\in B$, proving \eqref{eq:cone-comparison}.
For a positive-mass input row $z_s=w_s(1,x_s)$ with $x_s\in K$, write
\[
 z_s=\kappa w_s(1,q_s)+z_s^{\rm rem},\qquad z_s^{\rm rem}\in C.
\]
The remainder need not lie in $C_K$. Precisely for this reason \eqref{eq:raw-interior} was imposed on the whole physical cone: its image under every report instrument does lie in $C_K$. The contribution to output label $j$ is therefore at least, in the $C_K$ order,
\[
 \kappa w_s\sum_a p_{sa}
 \int_{\{F_a(y,q_s)\in C_j\}}L_a(y)(1,q_s)\,d\lambda_a(y)
 =\kappa w_s\sum_a p_{sa}\int_{C_j}(1,x)\,\mu_{a,q_s}(dx).
\]
When $q_s\in G$, subtract $\kappa\epsilon w_s(Z_m^\circ)_j$. The remaining measure is positive and supported on $K$, so its barycentric integral is again in $C_K$. Sum over the incoming labels. Zero-mass rows contribute zero. The integral identity uses the actual likelihood in the pushforward, not the volume of an image. Grid boundaries have zero $\zeta$ mass because $\epsilon\zeta\le\mu_{a,q}$ for any $q\in G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform mixing of every local common-grid table]\label{lem:uniform-mixing}
Under Definition~\ref{def:class}, take any such table with cell diameter at most $s_0$ when $k>0$. Regardless of its action rows and without assuming stationary calibration, its ensemble map satisfies \eqref{eq:ensemble-minor}, with $m$ in place of $M$ and $b=k+1$, $\gamma=\kappa\epsilon\theta$. Hence it has a unique invariant normalized $C_K$ ensemble and contracts equal-mass differences by $1-\gamma$ every $b$ calls. These constants are independent of the number of cells.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $k=0$, every representative lies in $G$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} proves the assertion. Otherwise consider the subexecution on which the first $k$ reports belong to $E_{A_t}$. Its actual conditional probability at each step is at least $v$, whatever the incoming physical state in $K$, the label, the chosen action and the earlier reports. The total subensemble consequently has mass at least $v^k e_{\rm tot}(Z)$; this statement follows by successive conditional integration, not independence of the reports.

On this subexecution the programmed representatives obey
\[
 V(q_{S_{t+1}})\le\lambda V(q_{S_t})+c+L_Vs_0.
\]
Indeed $F_{A_t}(Y_{t+1},q_{S_t})$ and $q_{S_{t+1}}$ belong to the closure of the same cell. Equation~\eqref{eq:quiet-margin} puts the final representative in $G$. The actual physical ensemble, which may be quite different from the programmed representative, remains in $C_K$ by \eqref{eq:raw-interior}. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} to this subensemble on the next report. The complementary executed subensemble is also positive. Thus the full $(k+1)$-step map dominates
$\kappa\epsilon v^k e_{\rm tot}(Z)Z_m^\circ$.

On $C_K^m$ write
\[
 P^b=\gamma\mathcal S+(1-\gamma)Q,
 \qquad \mathcal S Z=e_{\rm tot}(Z)Z_m^\circ.
\]
The residual $Q$ is positive and mass preserving. Such maps contract the base norm, while $\mathcal S$ vanishes on zero-total-mass differences. This proves the contraction. The normalized ensemble base is compact and complete. The contraction gives the unique fixed point of $P^b$; applying $P$ to it gives another fixed point, so it is also fixed by $P$. The geometric estimate follows. This is convergence of represented executable-test ensembles, not total-variation convergence of an arbitrary unrepresented microscopic state or of an infinite transcript.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Global calibration without erasure]\label{lem:fixedpoint}
For each permitted common grid there is one homogeneous table of the preceding form whose invariant positive-mass label means equal its programmed representatives, and whose action rows are supported on $\arg\min_a K_ah(q_s)$. The table is defined at all labels, including zero-mass invariant labels, and uses exactly the grid labels.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $D_s=\overline C_s\subset K$ be the compact convex cell closures. Use the compact convex ensemble base
\[
 \mathcal E=\{(z_s):z_s\in C_{D_s},\ \sum_s e(z_s)=1\}.
\]
At positive mass put $z_s=w_s(1,q_s)$ and allow every probability row supported on the finite minimizing set of $K_ah(q_s)$. At zero mass choose any $q_s\in D_s$ and any action row; its contribution is zero. For each allowed choice define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixedj}
 \mathcal F_j(z,p)=\sum_{s,a}p_{sa}
   \int\one_{\{F_a(y,q_s)\in C_j\}}L_a(y)z_s\,d\lambda_a(y).
\end{equation}
For positive rows the integral is a mass-weighted average of $(1,x)$ with $x\in C_j$. Thus $\mathcal F_j\in C_{D_j}$ and total mass is one. Its values are nonempty, convex and compact: for fixed $z$ the map is linear in the product of the minimizing-action simplices; arbitrary zero-row choices have no effect.

The graph is closed. Along a convergent sequence, a positive limiting mass gives convergent normalized representatives and, after taking a subsequence, convergent action probabilities supported on limiting minimizers because $K_ah$ is continuous. The normalized positive instrument is continuous in its input off the common null set. The boundary preimage has zero reference mass, since the acquired row gives the boundary zero mass and its likelihood is positive. Dominated convergence, using the integrable operator norm, handles the cell indicator integrals. At a zero limiting mass every row contribution is bounded by a constant times that mass, uniformly in its allowed representatives. No normalization at zero is used.

The finite-dimensional Kakutani theorem \cite{Kakutani1941} gives $z^*\in\mathcal F(z^*)$. Fix one witnessing action selection and choose representatives in their own cell closures for zero-mass labels. The equations are precisely the invariant equations of the executed positive instrument. Its invariant means are therefore the positive-mass $q_s$ themselves, and it is realizable by the permitted prepared mixtures. Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-mixing} gives uniqueness and attraction for this fixed table, including from a start not calibrated by $z^*$. The proof does not assert convergence of an iteration that repeatedly recomputes the nonlinear fixed-point correspondence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Risk of the calibrated table]\label{prop:invariant}
The observer of Lemma~\ref{lem:fixedpoint} satisfies
\eqref{eq:stationary-bound} and \eqref{eq:entrance}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At its actual invariant ensemble put $Q=q_S$, choose $A$ by the programmed minimizing row, and set $W=F_A(Y,Q)$. The actual law
$\mu=\sum_{s,a}w_sp_{sa}\mu_{a,q_s}$ has density at most $\Lambda$. Its output mean $Q'$ is the conditional centroid of $W$ in the common cell. Sum the Bellman equation over the calibrated invariant ensemble. Stationarity cancels the incoming and outgoing bias terms and yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationary-jensen}
 R_\infty-\rho=\E h(Q')-\E h(W)
       =\mathcal J_{\mathcal P_m}(h,\mu).
\end{equation}
The curvature estimate in Lemma~\ref{lem:curvature} bounds this by $Cm^{-2/d}$. Strong concavity bounds it below by
$\E\norm{W-Q'}^2\ge q_m(\mu)\ge cm^{-2/d}$.
Choose a grid obtained by splitting a fixed bounding cube with
$\lfloor M^{1/d}\rfloor$ subdivisions per side. Its number of cells is at most $M$, and its mesh and curvature bound are $O(M^{-1/d})$ and $O(M^{-2/d})$, even if some cells are empty. The lower with $M$ follows from the actual number being at most $M$.

Every initial preparation in $D$ is sent to $K$ after one acquired report. Thereafter Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-mixing} bounds the ensemble distance by
$2(1-\gamma)^{\lfloor(t-1)/b\rfloor}$. For the fixed readouts, expected audit loss is affine in the unnormalized ensemble and bounded by $L_0$ on its normalized base. Summing the resulting expectation differences over $t=1,\ldots,n$ costs at most $2L_0 b/(n\gamma)$. This proves \eqref{eq:entrance}. Calibration is needed at the invariant law only; no claim of exact calibration during entrance has entered the argument.
\end{proof}
