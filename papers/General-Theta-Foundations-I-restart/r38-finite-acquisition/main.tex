\begin{abstract}
We derive finite-acquisition risk laws for known prepared positive causal instruments with a finite separating affine family of executable tests. A positive-cone comparison lifts actual acquired overlap to a minorization of the joint physical--observer ensemble. Robust access to a common report region makes its constants independent of the number of retained labels. Together with an invariant calibration correspondence and an average-cost Bellman equation, this constructs one homogeneous observer with at most $M$ total labels and excess predictive risk between $cM^{-2/d}$ and $C(M^{-2/d}+n^{-1})$ against every full-history adaptive acquisition strategy in a protected random-audit task. The same observer works for all horizons and preparations, without artificial erasure, physical reset or an uncounted posterior register. The lower uses actual acquired mass; the upper uses a global legal cover and nonsmooth Bellman curvature. Controlled Gaussian filters and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the same theorem from raw kernels. A weighted acquired-stratum extension, an explicit binary benchmark, an attained calibration--deficiency composition, and finite-precision execution with budget-independent perturbation amplification are proved. All call, clock, program and workspace costs remain distinct.
\end{abstract}\maketitle
