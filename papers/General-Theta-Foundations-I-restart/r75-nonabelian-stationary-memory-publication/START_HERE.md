## Mathematical advances and their scope

### General finite-group structure

Theorem 6.2 turns the positive small-contrast problem for arbitrary finite groups and finite output alphabets into explicit irreducible-multiplicity inequalities over permutation modules. It supplies legal preparation and decoder tables, including complex and quaternionic representation types through a real embedding argument. The classical representation-theoretic ingredients are credited separately from the positive realization and resource conclusions.

Theorem 6.3 proves that the binary response set on a fixed transitive action is convex **if and only if its stabilizer is normal**; equivalently, the convexity holds for every finite output alphabet. A normalized permutation-character witness detects every nonnormal action at positive contrast. This characterizes the stated fixed-action response set, without asserting a classification of every nonabelian optimization problem.

### A closed nonabelian resource transition and equality classification

For every integer `n >= 5`, consider the binary symmetric-group response with first coordinate

`beta_t(g) = (1-t)/n + t * Fix(g)/n`, for `0 <= t <= 1`.

Theorem 6.6 proves the complete minimum permutation-core law, with
`tau_n = (sqrt(n)-1)/(sqrt(n)+1)`:

| Contrast | Minimum permutation core | Minimum counted pure-start memory |
|---|---:|---:|
| `t = 0` | `1` | `1` |
| `0 < t <= tau_n` | `n(n-1)` | `n(n-1)+1` |
| `tau_n < t <= 1` | `n^2` | `n^2+1` |

For every positive contrast the observable group remains `S_n` and the predictive dimension remains `1+(n-1)^2`. The strict extra positivity cost therefore persists in every dimension in this family. Its absolute jump is `n`; the ratio `n/(n-1)` tends to one. A two-generator command alphabet suffices. The paper gives a physical realization without claiming minimal quantum dimension or a common-resource quantum advantage.

The proof rests on Lemma 6.5, a sharp extremum for **all real entrywise nonnegative singular matrices** satisfying the specified mass and double-centering constraints. It assumes neither symmetry, positive semidefiniteness nor a nonnegative factorization. The equality case forces a unique coefficient matrix up to simultaneous coordinate permutation. Zero-pattern and ratio arguments then classify every minimum permutation-core factorization at the threshold, including the full allowed family of nonuniform orbit weights. The statement does not extend that classification to every stochastic realization before reduction to a core. The small-contrast upper construction, arbitrary-core lower reduction, sharp inequality, boundary realization and equality classification form one explicit proof chain.

### Stationary initialization and a complete noisy instrument

Theorem 9.1 proves a counted-initialization law for arbitrary stationary finite-alphabet response laws, with its controlled extension and terminal-readout hypotheses. Outside the one-label case, a finite pure-start realization needs exactly one more label than the smallest mixed-start realization. The infinite-value case is stated explicitly. The proof deletes the initial state using predictive stationarity and preserves every word law; the converse processes the first actual command while entering the core.

Theorem 10.3 applies this principle to the complete three-outcome qubit instrument, whose report probabilities are uniformly positive and whose average is exactly depolarizing. For `M >= 3`, `0 < theta <= 2*pi/M` and `0 < alpha <= 1`, it gives the necessary-and-sufficient criterion

`N_mix(alpha,theta) <= M` iff `alpha <= 1/(cos(theta)+sin(theta)*tan(pi/M))`.

The boundary has exactly `M` mixed labels and `M+1` pure labels, with the stated zero-report Perron-block rigidity. For `0 < alpha < 1`, one common dictionary with
`K(alpha) = max(3,ceil(pi/arccos(alpha)))`
realizes all commanded angles and all observed branches. It is minimal for the full angle family; the same upper bound applies to each finite alphabet, with equality when it contains a maximizing phase. The `alpha=0` two/three-state endpoint, the undamped finite-order case and the irrational obstruction are treated separately. The scalar spectral geometry and older phase/non-HMM phenomena receive explicit priority credit; the current conclusion concerns complete instruments, simultaneous commands and counted initialization.

### Uniform stopped-law approximation

Theorem S1.1 supplies one finite simulator for all common visible adaptive stopping policies with target expected-call budget `B`. With `alpha=1-epsilon`, the complete stopped-law total variation is at most `epsilon*B/8`, including terminal POVMs and a scalar nontermination mass. The resulting pure-memory upper bound for `0 < delta < B/8` is

`1 + max(3,ceil(pi/arccos(1-8*delta/B))) = (pi/4)*sqrt(B/delta) + O(1)`.

The policy uses the visible record and its classical randomness, without an additional entangled quantum register. The budget belongs to the target process. The theorem does not assert that the approximation satisfies the same expected-cost budget or terminates almost surely, and it does not prove a matching general approximation lower bound.
