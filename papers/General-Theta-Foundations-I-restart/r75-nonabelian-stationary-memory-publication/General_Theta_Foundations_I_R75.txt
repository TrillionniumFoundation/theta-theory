        Abstract. We study the classical memory required to reproduce complete causal response laws.
        For a finite observable group, we express the exact small-contrast memory through irreducible
        multiplicities in permutation modules. On a fixed transitive action, the binary response set is convex
        if and only if its stabilizer is normal. A permutation-character witness detects every failure of
        normality. For every n ≥ 5, we determine the complete contrast law of a binary Sn response: its
                                                                                √         √
        minimum pure-start memory is one at t = 0, n(n − 1) + 1 for 0 < t ≤ ( n − 1)/( n + 1), and n2 + 1
        above that threshold. Its observable group and linear dimension remain unchanged for t > 0. A
        sharp extremum for nonnegative singular matrices also classifies the minimum permutation cores
        at the transition. For stationary finite-alphabet processes admitting a finite classical realization,
        we prove that a counted pure initialization costs exactly one state beyond the smallest mixed-start
        realization unless the process is independent and identically distributed. We apply this principle to
        a qubit instrument with uniformly positive report probabilities and an exactly depolarizing average
        channel. One invariant polygon realizes all its observed branches and, under an explicit noise bound,
        all commanded angles simultaneously. Relative spectral bounds give matching memory counts in the
        first sector and in the worst phase. A single finite simulator also approximates all adaptive stopped
        laws for policies with bounded target expected calls, with an explicit resource bound. The complete
        causal comparison, optimal discrimination, reset, variational, dual, spectral and geometric results
        are retained with their individual hypotheses and proofs.




                                               1. Introduction
   A predictive state records everything about a past experiment that can affect its future laws.
Its linear dimension does not, by itself, determine the memory needed to realize those laws with
nonnegative probabilities. Commands can change the next observation, an observation changes the
state on which later commands act, and retaining or communicating information uses resources.
This paper studies the resulting positive-realization problem within a theory of causal resource
comparison and optimal statistical discrimination.
   We distinguish two response objects. A terminal response assigns a probability row b(w) to each
finite command word. An instrument response assigns a joint probability to each finite command–
outcome history. An exact classical realization uses one fixed collection of stochastic tables on
finitely many live labels. Except in an explicitly stated mixed-start comparison, the initial label
is pure and counted. All lower bounds allow arbitrary real probabilities. Program-table length,
arithmetic precision, working storage and the protected physical system are separate resources.
   The central questions are structural. Which linear predictive actions admit finite positive
realizations? When does linear representation theory determine the exact resource, and when do
probability constraints add a strict cost? How does the preparation contribute to that cost? Can
one controller reproduce a whole commanded instrument, rather than a scalar subsequence of its
observations? The results below answer these questions on distinct, explicitly specified classes.

Priority, dependence and the original causal theory. Residual automata, stochastic realiza-
tion, Hankel rank, invariant cones and observable operator models are classical [44, 48, 49, 45, 46, 47].
The structure of stochastic matrix groups [61, 62, 60, 66], finite-group representation theory [75],
symmetric positive factorizations [67, 68] and nonnegative-factorization perturbations [69, 70] supply
established ingredients. We credit multiplicity decomposition, Frobenius reciprocity and Fourier
orthogonality as classical facts, with the steps needed here proved below. The new group asser-

symmetric-group rigidity law. They extend the already proved reversible normal form and contrast
construction rather than replacing them.
   Invariant-polytope simulation of quantum instruments, including adaptive composition, has
explicit antecedents [47, 71]. Fanizza, Lumbreras and Winter [50] treat phase obstructions, finite
quantum processes without finite classical realizations and noisy classical-memory lower bounds.

a first. The present assertions concern the general counted-initialization identity and the displayed
qubit family’s exact, simultaneous full-instrument resource laws and stopped-law approximation.
Finite diagnostics below are checks of identities and constructions, not substitutes for the proofs or
for a complete literature-priority assessment.

                 2. Prepared kernels, acquired task laws and observers
   All hidden-state, action and report spaces are standard Borel. A fixed unknown index θ ∈ Θ
specifies an actual preparation and positive normalized causal kernels. Positivity means nonnegative
measures, not an everywhere strictly positive density. Dependence on the entire physical past
may be included in the hidden state. A legal policy sees past visible reports and its admitted
randomization, but not θ or a future audit. Iteration of these kernels defines its finite-prefix laws;
the consistent countable path law is their kernel extension.
   Each call has a cost ci ≥ c∗ > 0. The decision to make the next call is predictable. In the
geometric route, the declared productive-call flag is also predictable; in the primitive-instrument
route, productive reports are specified outcomes and can be random. Commitment is permitted
only after a productive report, and occurs exactly once almost surely in each model. At that time
a specified future audit Z ∈ H is scored, where H ⊂ Rk is nonempty, compact and convex. The
prediction is committed before that audit is observed. A prospective joint report–audit kernel is
defined at every productive call, including rejected calls. The stopping coin is independent of the
                          ACQUIRED GEOMETRY AND CAUSAL RESOURCE TRANSFER                                          5


audit conditional on the entire current visible report and the past. Write L = diam(H)2 ; the case
L = 0 is trivial whenever a legal rule exists.
  The core cost Cτ includes initial preparation charged to the core, failed calls and all productive
calls through commitment. A separately executed final audit must be added to the raw budget.
This convention is fixed before taking a risk infimum. Physical time, stored program constants and
temporary computation are not identified with call count.

Three resource interfaces. A checkpoint procedure may use its whole precommit history, but
must output one element of a fixed dictionary of at most M values. This counts its output width
only; it is not a claim of log2 M total working bits.
   An autonomous observer has an alphabet S, |S| ≤ M , and one fixed parameter-independent
program. At an intercall boundary its only persistent observer memory is its label s ∈ S. Its next
command distribution is a fixed function of s. A physical port name, elapsed time, subprogram
index, random seed or former report is not an extra free input. During a call its current command,
current report and fresh coins are available. Whatever persists to another call must be encoded in
S. A fixed decoder r : S → H reads only the committed label.
   In the event-stopped interface a post-report kernel emits (s′ , e) ∈ S × {0, 1}. An event e = 1 ends
the experiment and retains s′ for the decoder; no subsequent command is executed. This public
commitment event is not a stored bit used in continuing computation. The same label may be used
live on one path and terminal on another. In the state-stopped interface a fixed map h∗ : S → {0, 1}
determines halt status. The initial label is live, and type-one labels are globally absorbing terminal
labels with no executed command rows. Entry into a terminal label is allowed only after a productive
report. Thus live and terminal types are disjoint in this interface, and only in this interface by
definition.
   Write RiΘ (N, M ), i ∈ {cp, ev, st}, for the infimum of worst-case expected squared loss over
programs with robust almost-sure commitment and supθ Eθ Cτ ≤ N . An empty legal class has value
+∞. A program chosen once at random is not a free persistent seed. The ideal measurable class
may have fixed real constants; effective implementation is stated separately in Section S34.

              3. Reversible response groups and common controlled memory
   The fixed-phase results concern one transition matrix. Several commands require one family of
matrices on the same retained labels, and checking every command in isolation is insufficient. We
give an intrinsic finite-realization criterion for responses whose minimal linear dynamics is reversible
and compact, including any finite family of unitary commands on a prepared finite-dimensional
quantum system. We then obtain a matching arithmetic memory law for a pair of noncommuting

involutions. This is a theorem about exact controlled response realization, not an assertion that a
simulator’s private label is an observed sufficient statistic for arbitrary feedback optimization.
   Positive realization through invariant polyhedral sets is classical [48, 47, 46]. The argument
below uses a section of the realizing simplex before passing to the observable quotient; auxiliary
hidden-state responses need not belong to the minimal response space. Compact reversibility then
turns containment into equality. The resulting finite-group obstruction concerns all command words,
rather than a single spectral restriction.

                          13. Scope and further mathematical questions
   The exact small-contrast formula applies to every finite observable group. The normality theorem
characterizes convexity for a fixed transitive permutation action; it does not classify every possible
linear reduction of an arbitrary nonabelian realization problem. The symmetric-group family
has a closed optimal resource law at every contrast, including the transition and its extremal
permutation-core factorizations. A closed high-contrast formula for arbitrary finite groups remains
open.
   The stationary initialization theorem applies to arbitrary finite-alphabet stationary response laws
and makes no quantum, reversibility, or finite-rank assumption. The noisy-instrument formulas
concern the displayed qubit family. Its single controller works for every word over the supplied
command alphabet, including adaptive visible policies. One-dependence and stationarity are
assertions about the fixed-command process; adaptive choice of commands can itself transmit
dependence. The stopped-law approximation is a uniform upper bound under a target expected-call
budget, not a matching lower bound for every approximation problem.
   The retained general instrument theorem assumes uniform interiority of normalized branches.
It supplies a simulator of the experiment; its private label is not a new observation available to
the original policy. Consequently neither that theorem nor the new simultaneous construction
classifies optimal feedback or tied boundary decisions for every causal experiment. The general-M
squared-sign minimax-risk frontier and the intermediate phase-type timing frontier remain distinct
unresolved problems. Program length, arithmetic precision, working storage and exact sampling
costs are also distinct from the number of live labels.
   The full actual-history comparison theorem below and the independent causal, reset, discrimination
