\begin{abstract}
For a prepared positive causal experiment with a finite affine closure of executable tests, we characterize and construct optimal finite-state observers under a bound on expected acquisition. The unknown model is fixed during a run, and one program must control, stop, and decode under every model. A joint occupation criterion yields exact realization and attainment without uniform absorption. We then prove that effective raw-instrument certificates suffice to approximate this optimum from both sides while preserving exactly the same mean-acquisition and persistent-state budgets. The essential step is a stopping regularization that buys a strictly positive budget margin without adding a label. Occupation-weighted perturbation estimates separately control terminal risk and mean cost, and give a quantitative same-budget modulus for calibration and numerical errors without posterior contraction. Actual acquired mass determines a sharp geometric consequence: for report-recoverable tasks, a mass-constrained quantization profile gives matching checkpoint and autonomous laws, including noninteger-dimensional and changing-rank acquisition. Correlated classical and noncommuting quantum experiments verify this geometric theorem; informative Gaussian filters and general finite-dimensional quantum instruments verify the new exact-budget construction. Program descriptions, temporary workspace, precision, physical calls, and simulator state are accounted for separately.
\end{abstract}
