## The new mathematical contribution

R75 extends the positive-realization theory in two connected directions: the resource constraints imposed by the continuing action, and those imposed by preparation of a stationary response.

1. **Arbitrary finite groups.** `thm:r75-orbit-multiplicity` gives an integer multiplicity-cover formula for the exact small-contrast permutation resource, and the exact number of copies of any prescribed transitive action. The proof treats multi-output responses, missing representation types, zero output columns, constant responses and real descent. Classical representation decomposition and Frobenius reciprocity are credited; their use is combined with the inherited positive construction, not relabelled as new representation theory.
2. **A necessary and sufficient convexity boundary.** `thm:r75-normality-convexity` proves that the binary response set on one transitive action is convex precisely when the stabilizer is normal. A normalized permutation-character witness detects every nonnormal stabilizer at every positive contrast. A Fourier-rank argument supplies an explicit positive distance from the one-orbit response set, so the obstruction is robust rather than merely an exact equality failure. This identifies the precise scope of the earlier orbit-absorption mechanism; it does not claim that no special nonnormal problem can have some other linear formulation.
3. **A complete nonabelian contrast law and sharp rigidity.** `thm:r75-symmetric-return` treats every `S_n`, `n>=5`, at every contrast. Put `tau_n=(sqrt(n)-1)/(sqrt(n)+1)`. Its positive core is exactly `n(n-1)` for `0<t<=tau_n` and exactly `n^2` for `tau_n<t<=1`. Pure-start minima are respectively `n(n-1)+1` and `n^2+1`; at `t=0` both minima are one. Predictive dimension `1+(n-1)^2`, residual count `n!` and observable group remain unchanged throughout positive contrast. Subgroup-index estimates and absorption of noncarrying orbits reduce every smaller arbitrary stochastic controller to a singular nonnegative coefficient matrix. The new sharp matrix bound `lem:r75-singular-coefficient` proves the exact transition and classifies its coefficient-matrix equality case. The minimum permutation-core factorizations at the transition are then classified, including their nonuniform orbit weights and decoder rescaling; uniqueness of every hidden stochastic implementation is not asserted. Explicit matching tables and a physical unitary realization use two command types and binary terminal readout for every n.
4. **A general initialization theorem.** `thm:r75-stationary-initializer` proves that every predictively stationary controlled response requiring more than one label has pure-start minimum equal to mixed-start minimum plus exactly one, including the infinite-value convention. Its ordinary-process corollary applies to every non-i.i.d. stationary finite-alphabet process, without reversibility, finite predictive rank, irreducibility, a quantum model or a phase assumption. The proof explicitly eliminates the pure initial state while preserving every future word and optional terminal readout.
5. **A complete noisy instrument and simultaneous control.** `thm:r75-noisy-memory` gives matching first-sector and worst-phase memory counts for the whole three-outcome qubit process. One physical polygon realizes all branches, and under the explicit inradius bound it realizes all commanded angles simultaneously. The sector inequality and its scalar thresholds are inherited, and earlier noise-dependent process lower bounds are credited. The additional content is the common full-instrument realization, its equality structure and the general initialization charge. `thm:r75-stopped-approximation` gives one finite simulator approximating every visible adaptive stopped law at target expected-call budget `B`, with error at most `epsilon B/8` and an explicit `O(sqrt(B/delta))` label upper. It does not claim the missing general approximation lower bound or preservation of an unbounded stopping-time expectation by total variation alone.

These are author-proved claims with complete arguments in the current main/supplement pair. The mathematical status is separate from finite diagnostics, source identity, native compilation, human assessment, and an editorial judgment about significance.
