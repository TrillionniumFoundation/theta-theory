\section{Introduction}\label{sec:current-overview}\label{sec:introduction}

A predictive state records the information in an experimental past that
can affect future laws.  Its linear dimension measures one form of
compression.  Implementing those laws with finite positive memory also
requires stochastic transitions, a common decoder and an admissible
initialization.  Implementing an optimal policy introduces a further
question: how much does the best attainable cost change when the device
is allowed one additional persistent label?  Predictive dimension,
the number of physical states and the number of optimal decision values
need not answer that question.

This paper relates predictive, convex and spectral structure to finite
positive implementations of prepared causal experiments.  A controller
has one fixed parameter-independent program.  It sees only the declared
reports, and retains no uncounted history, clock or private seed.
Persistent labels, the protected physical state, program-table bits and
working arithmetic storage are separate resources.  A prescribed pure
initial label is counted.  Freely prepared mixed starts are identified
whenever that different convention is used.

The central question is now both quantitative and structural: how
stable is the optimized memory frontier under perturbation of the
physical experiment, and when does it reach the unrestricted-history
optimum at a finite budget?  Same-label regularization proves a general
continuity theorem, uniform in the number of labels and valid through
changes of reachable support.  We prove its square-root exponent
optimal, with an exact leading coefficient in a fully optimized
two-label example.  For any finite number of imperfect search locations and arbitrary
positive costs, a complete arithmetic criterion distinguishes finite
saturation from strict gaps at every finite budget, including families
with both commensurate and incommensurate pairs.  A quantitative
construction and an exact rational-data test supplement this criterion.
The reset subclass, shared-preparation realization theory and original
causal comparison retain their stronger or independent structures;
their quantifiers and mathematical scopes remain distinct.

\subsection*{Regularization without additional memory or physical reset}

Let $\Phi^\theta_{a,y}$ be the completely positive nonterminal branches
of a finite instrument, with commands $a$, reports $y$, and one hidden
model $\theta$ fixed throughout the experiment.  The initial density
matrix is arbitrary.  Success terminates immediately; no other stopping
is allowed.  Suppose the mean call-cost operators satisfy
$c_*I\preccurlyeq G^\theta_a\preccurlyeq c^*I$ with $c_*>0$.
A common autonomous $M$-label controller has command rows $p^k$ and
updates $u^{k,a,y}_\ell$, and starts at the counted label $1$.
Write $V_M$ for its least worst-model, worst-initial-state expected cost.

Suppose a controller has cost at most $b$ in this sense.  After each
failure replace its update by
\[
 u^{k,a,y,\varepsilon}_\ell
 =(1-\varepsilon)u^{k,a,y}_\ell+
                   \varepsilon\mathbf1_{\{\ell=1\}}.
\]
The physical state is unchanged.  Theorem~\ref{thm:r79-label-restart}
proves, whenever $\varepsilon b<c_*$, an initial cost bound and a
global continuation bound
\[
 U_\varepsilon=\frac{b}{1-\varepsilon b/c_*},\qquad
 K_\varepsilon=\frac{c^*}{\varepsilon}+U_\varepsilon.
\]
The latter applies from every physical state at every label, even when
the original controller had inaccessible dark labels.  Both estimates
hold uniformly over the fixed hidden model.  The proof uses finite
restart generations and the all-initial-state guarantee, without
presuming the finiteness it is to establish.

Measure instrument distance by half the trace-norm distance of its
normalized output, including success, and measure cost distance in
operator norm.  For two feasible compact model families at mutual
distances at most $(\eta,\zeta)$, let $B$ bound their common one-row
fallback costs.  For sufficiently small distances, with explicit
conditions in Theorem~\ref{thm:r79-frontier-stability},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r79-intro-stability}
 \sup_{M\ge1}|V_M(\mathcal U)-V_M(\mathcal V)|
 \le \frac{10B^2+4Bc^*}{c_*}\sqrt\eta
                   +\frac{4B}{c_*}\zeta.
\end{equation}
If every command has success effect at least $\kappa I$, the stronger
Corollary~\ref{cor:r79-positive-success} replaces the right side by
$\zeta/\kappa+c^*\eta/\kappa^2$.  Scalar geometric-waiting examples
prove local sharpness of both coefficients.  This additional success
hypothesis is not imposed on the general theorem.
The same $M$ is used on both sides.  The argument transfers the bounded
global witnesses of the regularized controller, rather than enlarging
memory to record a longer history.  Reachable supports may change and
unregularized continuation potentials may diverge.  The inequality
also holds for unrestricted visible-history values, which are the
limits of $V_M$ with an explicit counted-prefix approximation.

\subsection*{A sharp singularity of the optimized frontier}

The square-root exponent in \eqref{eq:r79-intro-stability} is sharp even
when the physical dimension, the number of models and the memory budget
are fixed.  Consider two scalar models, commands $a,b$, unit call cost
and one failure report.  Their success probabilities are respectively
$(1-\varepsilon,0)$ and $(0,1/4)$.  Put
\[
 r_0=\frac{\sqrt{65}-1}{32},\qquad
 D=\frac14+\frac34r_0,\qquad
 c_0=\frac{\sqrt3\,r_0^2}{D^2+r_0^2/4}>0.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:r80-sharp-modulus} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r80-intro-sharp}
 V_2(\varepsilon)=\frac{1+\sqrt{65}}2
                    +c_0\sqrt\varepsilon+o(\sqrt\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
The primitive discrepancy is exactly $\varepsilon$, the costs do not
change, and one fixed mixed command row has cost at most $6$ throughout
$0\le\varepsilon\le1/2$.  This is an optimized two-label value, with
all stochastic command and update rows admitted.  The one-label value
$V_1(\varepsilon)=1/(1-\varepsilon)+4$ is smooth at zero.

The proof first establishes an exact positive-polynomial inequality
and a unique effective boundary optimizer, including the opposite
orientation of the two command rows.  Its quantitative rigidity forces
the escape rate from a formerly inaccessible dark label to have the
scale $\sqrt\varepsilon$.  A lower bound for all six controller
parameters matches an explicit repairing program.  This closes the
exponent question for the general primitive class.  It does not assert
sharpness of the general upper constant or contradict the Lipschitz law
under a positive per-command success floor.

\subsection*{When finite memory reaches the history optimum}

Let $n\ge2$, $r_i\in(0,1)$ and $c_i>0$.  In hidden model $i$,
only command $i$ can succeed, with probability $q_i=1-r_i$; every
command $j$ costs $c_j$ in every model.  There is one failure report.
The hidden model is selected once and is not given to the controller.
Put $\tau_i=c_i/q_i$, $B=\sum_i\tau_i$, $u_i=-\log r_i$ and
$H=\sum_i1/u_i$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r80-finite-search-one-label} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:r80-finite-search-saturation} prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r80-intro-saturation}
 V_1=B,\qquad
 \bigl(\exists M<\infty:\ V_M=V_{\rm hist}\bigr)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{\log r_i}{\log r_j}\in\mathbb Q\quad\text{for every }i,j.
\end{equation}
Otherwise every finite $M$ has an attained strict gap, although
$V_M\downarrow V_{\rm hist}$.  Rational primitive examples already
occur for two locations with failure probabilities $1/2$ and $1/3$.

The greedy search ordering, interior preparation saddle, mixture of
at most $n$ consistent-priority words and common-power cycles are
classical \cite{Matula1964,ClarksonLinGlazebrook2023};
Lemma~\ref{lem:r80-finite-search-saddle} supplies a complete proof of
the ingredients needed here.  For the converse, partition commands
by rational commensurability of their failure logarithms.  Different
classes have only finitely many cross-class ties.  Every optimal word
therefore has the same deterministic class suffix, with class
frequency ratios that are irrational.  A finite stochastic
controller emitting a deterministic class suffix forces that suffix
to be ultimately periodic, by a recurrence on positive label
supports.  This excludes all finite stochastic implementations,
including randomized updates and mixtures within each class.  It
does not assume that an arbitrary finite Markov chain has rational
frequencies.

On the positive side, finite logarithmic cosets give ultimately
periodic consistent-priority words.  Their optimal mixture is
implemented from a counted pure initial label through the sampled
command and its conditional update; every persistent branch label
is charged.  The sufficiency combines classical cyclic search with
this resource accounting.

The quantitative conclusions do not require a known saddle prior.
Lemma~\ref{lem:r80-finite-search-prior-cap} gives an explicit
primitive-data lower bound $\pi_i\ge\delta>0$ for every
least-favourable prior.  If
$C=\max_i\tau_i/(\delta\min_i\tau_i)$,
$A=C\max\{C,1/\min_i r_i\}$ and $\kappa=e^{-1/H}$, then
Theorem~\ref{thm:r80-finite-search-resource-bounds} gives
\[
 0\le V_M-V_{\rm hist}\le BA\kappa^{\lfloor M/n\rfloor},
 \qquad M\ge n.
\]
At most $n$ counted finite prefixes and one common fallback realize
the bound.  For rational primitive data, signed prime-exponent
vectors decide saturation exactly.  In the affirmative case an
explicit sufficient label cap reduces the history value to inherited
fixed-memory algebraic synthesis
(Corollary~\ref{cor:r80-finite-search-effective}).  Neither a minimal
cap, an efficient elimination algorithm nor a matching lower
approximation rate is claimed.  The full two-location argument and
its sharper normalized constants remain in
Section~\ref{sec:r80-arithmetic-saturation} of the supplement.

\subsection*{Exact certificates, model quantifiers and an algebraic frontier}

For fixed controller and model let $\mathsf T^\theta_\gamma$ be its
nonterminal map on $M$ blocks of $d$-dimensional matrices, and let
$K^\theta_\gamma$ be its cost operator.  The inherited support theory
is restated with a complete proof in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r79-exact-certificate}.  A finite cost bound $b$ is
equivalent to projections $P$ and a positive supported potential $W$
with
\[
 P_1=I,\quad P\mathsf T(P)P=\mathsf T(P),\quad
 W=PWP\succeq PKP+P\mathsf T^*(W)P,\quad W_1\preccurlyeq bI.
\]
The exact robust quantifier order is
$\exists\gamma\,\forall\theta\,\exists(P^\theta,W^\theta)$.
The common controller is implementable; model-dependent proof witnesses
are not additional observations.  Theorem~\ref{thm:r79-quantum-frontier}
gives attainment for every $M$ and extends the finite-model algebraic
certificate to compact semialgebraic families with unrestricted legal
feedback.  No general efficient elimination algorithm is claimed.
The support and potential construction, finite-model synthesis and
compactness mechanism are inherited from
Theorems~\ref{thm:memory-certificate} and~\ref{thm:face} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:memory-algebraic}; they are not counted as separate
new discoveries.

For two fixed hidden models, two commands and a single failure report,
Proposition~\ref{prop:r79-golden-ratio-memory} determines every budget:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r79-intro-golden}
 V_1=2,\qquad V_2=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
 V_M=\frac32\quad(M\ge3).
\end{equation}
Its two-label optimizer has a dark label that is inaccessible in one
model and essential in the other.  The example tests both the support
quantifier and the charge for a prescribed pure initial label.  A
separate positive-probability example shows that deterministic
report-only routing of a fixed row palette can fail for a fixed hidden
model, and has a known-model nonreset quantum realization.  Thus the
following reset normal form has a precise structural boundary.

\subsection*{The complete reset memory frontier}

Let $a\in\mathcal A$ be a command and $i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}$ a failure
report.  The physical failure branch is
\[
 \mathcal I_{a,i}(\rho)=\tr(E_{a,i}\rho)\sigma_i,
 \qquad E_{a,i}\succeq0,\quad \sum_iE_{a,i}\preccurlyeq I.
\]
The report itself prepares the known state $\sigma_i$.  The observer
does not receive an additional state measurement or free reset.  A
successful call must terminate and failure forbids termination.  The
mean call cost is $\tr(G_a\rho)$ with
$c_*I\preccurlyeq G_a\preccurlyeq c^*I$ and $c_*>0$; a realized cost is
not an extra observation.  The initial density matrix is arbitrary and
unknown.  Write $V_M$ for the least worst-initial-state expected cost
over autonomous controllers with at most $M$ live labels and a prescribed
pure initial label.

Absence of a closed failure set is necessary and sufficient for a
finite-cost controller, even with one randomized command row
(Theorem~\ref{thm:reset-control}).  Put
\[
 Q_{iaj}=\tr(E_{a,j}\sigma_i),\qquad c_{ia}=\tr(G_a\sigma_i).
\]
For a row palette $p^1,\ldots,p^M\in\Delta(\mathcal A)$ arising from
a controller with finite worst-initial-state cost, consider the finite
decision problem whose actions are exactly these rows.  Its
least Bellman potential is
\[
 x_i=\min_{1\le k\le M}
           \sum_a p_a^k\left(c_{ia}+\sum_jQ_{iaj}x_j\right).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:r78-palette-normal-form} proves that every finite-cost
controller is dominated, separately at every initial density matrix,
by one with the same rows, the same initial row $p^1$, and deterministic
report-only routing $i\mapsto\kappa(i)$.  The argument uses the
all-initial-state requirement at an essential point: starting the
original controller with its fixed initial label from each $\sigma_i$
gives a finite comparison policy for every state of the row-palette
problem.  It does not assert transience of unreachable pairs in the
original product of physical and controller states.

Let $C_0$ be an explicit finite worst-state cost of a full-support
one-row controller.  Theorem~\ref{thm:r78-reset-frontier} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r78-intro-frontier}
\begin{split}
 V_M=\min\{b:\;&p^k\in\Delta(\mathcal A),\quad
      \kappa:\{1,\ldots,s\}\to\{1,\ldots,M\},\quad
      x\in[0,C_0]^s,\quad b\in[0,C_0],\\
 &x_i\ge\sum_a p_a^{\kappa(i)}
                  (c_{ia}+\sum_jQ_{iaj}x_j)\quad(1\le i\le s),\\
 &\sum_a p_a^1(G_a+\sum_i x_iE_{a,i})\preccurlyeq bI\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The minimum is attained and each feasible point constructs an actual
controller.  Conversely, every cost bound below $C_0$ realized by any
stochastic controller gives such a point.  Only $s$ scalar continuation
potentials are needed, independently of $M$; no $sM$-state product
resolvent is imposed.  Singular effects and arbitrary Bellman ties are
allowed.  For exact algebraic primitive data, this compact finite union
of semialgebraic sets decides every cost threshold and gives an exact
minimizer.  No polynomial running time or executed general elimination
is asserted.

Proposition~\ref{prop:r78-frontier-stability} proves continuity of each
$V_M$ in the admissible primitive instrument.  The lower argument uses
the bounded potentials and compact row palettes; the upper argument
uses stability of a fixed proper realizing controller.  Thus a strict
finite resource gap persists under sufficiently small perturbations,
including perturbations that remove commutation among effects and
cost operators.

There are exact growing-dimensional instances.  For any nonempty proper
action subsets $A_1,\ldots,A_s\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}$, the inherited
rational construction has $E_{a,i}=(r/s)I$ and costs in $[1,4]$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:r78-cover-frontier} computes $V_M$ by a finite minimum
over at most $M-1$ continuing actions and a nonempty initial support.
Its optimum has pure continuing rows and an initial row uniform on
that support.  This purity conclusion is for this displayed family;
the general normal form preserves arbitrary mixed command rows.
For $A_i=\{i\}$, $s=q=n\ge2$ and $r=1/2$, the full formula becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r78-intro-hierarchy}
 V_M=4+\frac1n+
      \frac{(n-1)\max\{n-M+1,0\}}{n^2},\qquad M\ge1.
\end{equation}
It gives $V_1>V_2>\cdots>V_{n+1}=V_*$ in dimension $2n$, with
one exact decrement $(n-1)/n^2$ at every step.  In this family the full
gap is exactly twice the older first-residual certificate $D_M$.
The distinction between a lower certificate and the true constrained
optimum is therefore quantitative, not only terminological.

\subsection*{The optimum threshold and positive exposure}

The unrestricted reset optimum is the spectral minimum
$V_*=\min_p\lambda_{\max}(\sum_a p_aL_a)$, where
$L_a=G_a+\sum_i v_iE_{a,i}$ and $v$ is the primitive Bellman optimum.
When one attaining dual preparation $\rho_*$ satisfies
$\tr(\rho_*E_{a,i})>0$ for every command $a$ and report $i$,
the optimal-action sets $\mathcal A_i^*$ give a simpler exact memory
threshold.  Write $\mathcal P_*$ for the optimal initial rows,
$I(p)=\{i:\supp p\subseteq\mathcal A_i^*\}$, and $t(J)$ for the
least number of actions meeting all $\mathcal A_i^*$ with $i\in J$.
The inherited Theorem~\ref{thm:r77-reset-cover} states
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r77-intro-reset}
 m_*=1+\min_{p\in\mathcal P_*}
                 t(\{1,\ldots,s\}\setminus I(p)).
\end{equation}
Positive definite effects are a direct sufficient exposure hypothesis.
The explicit semidefinite example has a one-label optimum that this
cover formula would overcount without exposure.  It remains a valid
counterexample to removing that hypothesis; it is also correctly
handled by the new general frontier.  The arbitrary-cover realization
and its NP-hardness consequence are inherited unchanged.  Their
unrestricted value may be simple even though memory synthesis is hard.

\subsection*{Shared preparations and causal response realization}

Fix a finite group $G$ and finite response laws $b_i:G\to\Delta(C)$.
All preparations must use one command program and one decoder; the
decoder does not receive the preparation identity for free.  For
closed convex constraints on every command word,
Theorem~\ref{thm:r77-shared-normal-form} gives a common permutation
core, including exact charges for prescribed pure initializers.
For the distinct target tables $\mathcal B$, let $P$ range over
$G$-invariant polytopes in the full response simplex containing
$\mathcal B$, and let $v(P)$ count their vertices.  Then
Theorem~\ref{thm:r77-shared-polytope} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r77-intro-polytope}
 \kappa_G(\mathcal B)=\min_P v(P),\qquad
 m_G(\mathcal B)=\min_P\{v(P)+|\mathcal B\setminus\operatorname{vert}P|\}.
\end{equation}
The polytope may extend beyond the target span.  These are different
resource conventions: a freely prepared mixed row and a prescribed
pure device label have different initialization costs.

Horizontal concatenation of the preparation Fourier blocks gives the
joint, potentially noncyclic response module.  With a common group
mean, its permutation multiplicity cover is the exact sufficiently
small positive-contrast memory, with an explicit simultaneous positive
decoder.  Weighted singular values give robust lower certificates.
Exact families on the fixed group $C_2$ require $2q$ shared labels
although every preparation separately needs two, or have mixed memory
two and unbounded pure initialization overhead.  Unequal means have
a nonnegative-rank obstruction.  The full finite-group normal form,
nonabelian multiplicity, quotient duality, and the displayed complete
symmetric-group mean--contrast law retain their proofs and scopes.

\subsection*{Operational approximation and the qubit boundary}

Finite causally prepared heralds followed by delayed terminal tests
give a robust nonnegative-factorization obstruction without requiring
a physical density matrix at each hidden simulator label.  The
decoder may have arbitrary stochastic test columns, but cannot see an
uncounted copy of the earlier history.  Theorem~\ref{thm:r77-operational-antichain}
first controls joint herald and test events, and only then conditions.
For the original qubit phase $z=(3+4i)/5$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:r77-unrestricted-qubit} implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r77-intro-arithmetic}
 \delta\le\frac{25\zeta}{128\cdot200^J J^2}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad N_{\rm cl}(\delta;B)\ge
 \min\{m:\tbinom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\ge J\},
 \qquad\zeta=\min\{1,7B/8\}.
\end{equation}
At each fixed $B>0$ the unrestricted lower is of order
$\log\log(1/\delta)$; the retained constructive upper is logarithmic.
The matching leading logarithm for physical density dictionaries keeps
its Born-decoder and badly-approximable-phase hypotheses.  These rates
concern stopped response approximation, not the original general-$M$
squared-sign decision-risk frontier.  That separate problem remains
open and is not replaced by the exact reset cost hierarchy.

\subsection*{Classical ingredients, priority and the proof architecture}

Bellman verification and stochastic-shortest-path theory are classical
\cite{BertsekasTsitsiklis91}.  Finite stochastic controller search,
bounded policy iteration and nonlinear optimization of fixed controller
size have an established literature
\cite{Meuleau99,PoupartBoutilier03,AmatoBernsteinZilberstein10}.
Parameter synthesis, robust undiscounted goal costs and fixed hidden
models with common finite-state controllers are also established
\cite{JungesEtAl18,CubuktepeEtAl21,GaleslootEtAl25}; recent algebraic
complexity results further delimit effectivity claims
\cite{BovyEtAl26}.  Quantum reachable supports and expected-cost
observables have their own prior theory
\cite{YuYing12,YingFengYuYing13,LiuZhouBartheYing25,AvanziniEtAl22}.
Continuity under model perturbation has an established discounted
control theory \cite{KaraYuksel2020}.  The present estimates concern
undiscounted success stopping with a fixed counted memory budget and
possibly changing reachable support.
Neither writing a nonlinear program nor invoking real algebraic
elimination is claimed as a new discovery.  The general step here is the same-label regularization and its
uniform-in-memory stability across changing reachable supports.
The new sharpness theorem establishes the exact worst-case exponent
within that class, rather than restating its upper estimate.  The inherited compact
risk enclosure in Theorem~\ref{thm:compact-risk} uses a finite-prefix
completion whose memory can grow; it does not supply this fixed-memory
comparison.  For the reset subclass, the structural content is simultaneous domination with the same counted rows,
removal of all stochastic routing for the all-state reset problem,
the $s$-potential compact certificate in the undiscounted model,
and the full exact resource law and stable hierarchy.

Residual automata, positive realization, representation theory,
equivariant factorizations and antichains retain their classical
attribution \cite{RabinScott59,Rabin63,Heller65,CarlylePaz71,
BenvenutiFarina04,Schwarz64,Flor69,Serre77,GPT13,VandaeleGillisGlineur17}.
In classical search, an infinite pure search sequence and a finite
autonomous stochastic implementation are different strategy classes
\cite{BuiLidbetterLin2023}.  The search saddle and common-power cyclic
ordering are attributed to their existing theory.  The additional
claims submitted here are all-stochastic necessity through projected
commensurability classes, explicit primitive-data resource bounds,
and counted implementation with an effective cap.
Set-cover hardness is used through its explicit physical reduction
\cite{Karp72}.  Targeted primary-source comparisons distinguish these
ingredients from the theorem statements; they do not certify
exhaustive priority or independent editorial significance.

The general frontier, stability and sharpness chain uses primitive feasibility,
reachable-support cost verification, and same-label restart.  It needs
no group or phase-arithmetic assumption.  The search saturation proof
uses the stated scalar family, a classical greedy saddle and a finite
support-set obstruction; it is not a universal instrument classification.
The reset frontier depends
on the experiment semantics and the finite
reset existence theorem.  It does not require the group realization or
qubit arithmetic results.  The shared-preparation chain uses the
finite-group convex normal form and its Fourier premises.  The
approximation chain uses operational factorization and phase separation.
The general actual-history comparison and paid construction remain in
the main article; their lower witnesses use the competitor's actual
histories.  The supplement preserves full independent proofs for
finite-order quantum measurement, two-stage reset, memory and
communication, rank-constrained discrimination, performed-law
compactness, variational and dual attainment, spectral memory and local
acquired geometry.  The R58 companion remains independently compilable.
No model-specific value formula is promoted to a solution of arbitrary
causal feedback, global geometry, or the independent A1/A2 programme.
