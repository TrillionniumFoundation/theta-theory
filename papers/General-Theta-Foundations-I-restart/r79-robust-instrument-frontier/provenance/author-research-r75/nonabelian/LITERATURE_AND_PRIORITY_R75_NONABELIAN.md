# R75 nonabelian author mathematics: primary-source comparison

## Scope and internal inheritance

This research draft continues the native R74 manuscript at source commit `29b999abf11c43709e64be58765aa1984d13ac91`, whose complete publication is `c2c05ee3dccd23588790cd92d2aa2b0b3dac00e0`. The inherited authorities used here are Sections 31, 33, 37 and 38. The full source restoration and controlling-referee audit are managed by the root revision task; this addendum does not replace that provenance audit.

R73 already established the reversible permutation-core normal form, the module lower bound, and the counted-pure-start formula. R74 already established positive realization at the minimum permutation-module size for every sufficiently small positive terminal contrast, and closed attained action-budget contrast intervals. R74 also proved normal-stabilizer preparation absorption and the corresponding quotient linear programs. Those are inherited results, not R75 discoveries. The current draft reproduces the short positive construction only to make the prescribed-action extension independently checkable.

The proposed R75 increment is:

- An explicit Fourier multiplicity cover for arbitrary finite groups, including a complete real-descent argument, and the exact small-contrast number of specified transitive-orbit copies.
- An exact converse to normal-stabilizer convexification: a transitive orbit's binary response set is convex if and only if its stabilizer is normal. Permutation-character return laws provide the counterexample for every nonnormal stabilizer, at every positive contrast. A quantitative sup-norm margin is proved.
- A full-contrast return-law rigidity lemma.
- A sharp extremal inequality for every entrywise nonnegative singular real matrix `A` of total sum one with `PAP=(t/n)P`: for `n>=5`, `t<=(sqrt(n)-1)/(sqrt(n)+1)`. Equality determines the coefficient matrix completely up to simultaneous coordinate permutation, without assuming symmetry or positive semidefiniteness.
- An unbounded nonabelian family, for every `n >= 5`, with exactly two unrestricted positive-core values, `n(n-1)` and `n^2`, separated by the **explicit** attained threshold `(sqrt(n)-1)/(sqrt(n)+1)`. The smaller value holds at the threshold. The proof covers all permitted stochastic controllers through the inherited normal form; the natural-orbit construction is not imposed as a lower-bound restriction. Every minimum permutation-core factorization at the threshold is classified, including its allowed nonuniform orbit weights.

Native-source searches found no permutation-character convexity theorem, prescribed-orbit multiplicity formula, or this all-`n` symmetric-group family in the restored R74 text and retained R74 author research. This is an internal duplication check, not an exhaustive worldwide priority clearance.

## Classical representation theory: Serre

J.-P. Serre, *Linear Representations of Finite Groups*, Graduate Texts in Mathematics **42**, Springer-Verlag, 1977. DOI: [10.1007/978-1-4684-9458-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-9458-7). The publisher's [book record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4684-9458-7) and chapter records for [character theory](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-9458-7_2), [induction](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-9458-7_3), and [the group algebra](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-9458-7_6) were checked on 2026-10-21 UTC. Subscription chapter bodies were not accessed.

Complete reducibility, matrix-coefficient orthogonality, the decomposition of a finite group algebra into irreducible matrix blocks, and Frobenius reciprocity are classical inputs. The formula `m_rho(H)=dim rho^H`, the interpretation of a permutation character as a fixed-point count, and the standard representation of `S_n` are not claimed as new. The draft supplies the operational deductions and a direct proof of real descent, rather than assuming that a complex factorization is automatically stochastic. The elementary subgroup-index inequalities and full-contrast rigidity used in the new memory converse are proved locally.

## Symmetric positive factorizations: Gouveia–Parrilo–Thomas

J. Gouveia, P. A. Parrilo and R. R. Thomas, *Lifts of convex sets and cone factorizations*, Mathematics of Operations Research **38**(2) (2013), 248–264. DOI: [10.1287/moor.1120.0575](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.1120.0575). [Primary author text, arXiv:1111.3164v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.3164v2).

The primary text and Theorems 2.4 and 2.12 were inspected. Cone lifts and symmetric cone factorizations are established theory. The present response-table family is not silently identified with an entire slack operator, and its live-label resource is not identified with extension complexity. The new result identifies the exact convexity boundary for one fixed transitive probability factorization, and gives exact all-controller memory for a specified experimental family. The common-action condition and bounded terminal decoder are part of the definition being optimized.

## Symmetry can change resource size: Kaibel–Pashkovich–Theis

V. Kaibel, K. Pashkovich and D. O. Theis, *Symmetry matters for sizes of extended formulations*, SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics **26**(3) (2012), 1361–1382. DOI: [10.1137/110839813](https://doi.org/10.1137/110839813). [Primary author text, arXiv:0911.3712v3](https://arxiv.org/html/0911.3712v3).

The primary text's coordinate-symmetric extension framework, Definition 11 and Lemma 12, were inspected. Symmetry-sensitive positive constructions and group averaging are prior mechanisms. The R75 statement therefore makes no first claim that symmetry affects positive representation size. Its subgroup-index argument concerns multiplicity of the natural standard representation and exact terminal return probabilities; it is proved without importing an extended-formulation lower bound. The result compares a positive stochastic core with its permutation-module lower bound, not symmetric and nonsymmetric polyhedral extensions.

## Restricted positive factorization: Gillis–Glineur

N. Gillis and F. Glineur, *On the geometric interpretation of the nonnegative rank*, Linear Algebra and its Applications **437**(11) (2012), 2685–2712. DOI: [10.1016/j.laa.2012.06.038](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2012.06.038). [Primary author record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.0880).

The author record and abstract were rechecked; the earlier R74 ledger documents its own full-text inspection. The distinction between restricted and unrestricted nonnegative rank is relevant: a particular action or natural-orbit ansatz does not certify an unrestricted optimum. R75 keeps `q_H` explicitly action-restricted and supplies a separate subgroup-index proof excluding every smaller action in the symmetric-group family. No unrestricted realization is projected into a chosen visible subspace without the retained subquotient/complete-reducibility argument.

## Positive and completely positive realization: Monras–Winter

A. Monras and A. Winter, *Quantum learning of classical stochastic processes: The completely-positive realization problem*, TQC 2014, LIPIcs **27** (2014), 99–109. DOI: [10.4230/LIPIcs.TQC.2014.99](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.TQC.2014.99). [Primary proceedings and PDF](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.TQC.2014.99).

The primary proceedings PDF, including its quotient-realization discussion and Theorem 6, was inspected. Positive-realization cones, quasi-realizations, and quantum realization precede this revision. R75's physical model is a finite prepared unitary experiment implementing the explicit permutation-character response; no general positive-realization criterion is claimed first. Its optimality theorem counts arbitrary exact classical stochastic labels under the inherited pure-start convention. It does not prove minimal quantum Hilbert-space dimension, or a quantum advantage using a common dimension metric.

## Fourier rank is established outside this application

H. Lawrence, K. Georgiev, A. Dienes and B. T. Kiani, *Implicit bias of linear equivariant networks*, Proceedings of ICML 2022, PMLR **162**, 12096–12125. [Primary proceedings record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/lawrence22a.html).

The primary abstract explicitly treats low-rank Fourier matrix coefficients on arbitrary finite groups. That work concerns optimization bias in linear equivariant networks, whereas the R75 claims concern positive preparations, bounded decoders, and exact orbit/live-label resources. This source is a priority guard against presenting the use of noncommutative Fourier rank as itself a new general method; it is not used as a missing proof or necessarily as a main-text citation.

## Support-pattern bounds are established: Chee–Le–Ta

Y. M. Chee, Q.-T. Le and H. Ta, *On the Asymptotic Nonnegative Rank of Matrices and its Applications in Information Theory*, [primary author text, arXiv:2308.07187v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.07187v3), revised 29 January 2024. Theorem 3.9 and Proposition 3.11 were inspected on 2026-10-21 UTC. They relate nonnegative subrank to an induced matching in the support and use positive diagonal positions with zero cross entries to separate factor supports.

The R75 equality proof uses the same elementary support mechanism once its extremal coefficient matrix is known: one nonnegative rank-one summand cannot cover two such diagonal positions. That mechanism is not claimed as a new lower-bound method. The proposed increment is the sharp scalar-centered singular-matrix extremum, its coefficient equality case, and the resulting bounded-decoder classification and unrestricted memory law. The manuscript proves each step locally.

## What has and has not been established

The manuscript proves its general structural statements, its sharp matrix inequality and equality case, and its specified exact memory law with the stated finite-group and `n >= 5` hypotheses. The proof yields an unbounded **additive** positivity cost `n`; the ratio `n/(n-1)` tends to one. The former open threshold is now resolved by the closed formula `(sqrt(n)-1)/(sqrt(n)+1)`. The earlier rational decoder on `[0,1/(n-1)]` remains in the source as a second implementation, together with the full-contrast return-law proof. Its smaller endpoint is not confused with the exact transition.

No increasing relative separation, general full-contrast linear program for nonnormal actions, or classification of every original stochastic transition realization is established. The equality statement is specifically a classification of minimum permutation-core factorizations at the threshold; orbit weights need not be uniform.

Targeted searches and the primary comparisons above did not establish exhaustive novelty. No independent human specialist endorsement, formal verification, or mathematical-journal editorial decision has been obtained. The requested Annals/Acta/Inventiones/JAMS research goal remains the goal; a list of finite checks is not evidence that that editorial threshold has already been met.
