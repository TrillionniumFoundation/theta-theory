## Programme and proved center

The paper retains its original identity: prepared causal experiments, predictive information and compression, positive finite resources, finite-order quantum measurement, two-stage reset, memory and communication, rank constraints, optimal discrimination, variational and dual principles, spectral structure and acquired local geometry. The current center is chosen from the strongest actual proof chain, not a fixed old qubit dimension or prior. Its target remains the four leading general mathematics journals specified by the author.

The principal additional theorem is **same-M regularization and uniform-in-M primitive stability** for the robust expected-cost frontier of arbitrary finite-dimensional CP failure instruments. A fixed hidden model remains unchanged throughout an execution. Positive call costs, all-initial-state guarantees, globally legal commands and mandatory success stopping are explicit assumptions. Returning a controller to its already counted initial label adds no label and performs no physical reset. This produces bounded full-space cost potentials and transfers one implementable controller across nearby model families even when original reachable supports change.

The exact cost-support certificate, finite-model synthesis, compact attainment, no-dark fallback, centered trace estimate and finite-prefix completion have inherited or classical antecedents. They are stated with full current proofs and attributed accordingly. Their consolidation and compact-semialgebraic arbitrary-feedback extension support the new theorem; they are not relabeled independent discoveries. Under a uniform per-command success floor, a stronger Lipschitz law and exponential-in-horizon completion follow from classical resolvent estimates, with exact scalar examples proving local sharpness of its coefficients.

The exact golden-ratio all-M example is a special-model closed law. The reset same-row theorem and arbitrarily long resource hierarchies are stronger structural results for a narrower model. The common finite-group preparation and Fourier theorems, unrestricted-decoder lower bounds, causal comparisons and independent supplement all remain proved at their stated scopes.
