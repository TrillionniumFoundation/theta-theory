# Literature and mathematical priority — R79 author revision

## Current contribution and inherited mathematics

The principal additional result is **regularization with exactly the same charged live-label budget**, followed by stability of the whole optimal cost frontier with constants independent of that budget. It applies to finite-dimensional quantum failure instruments without reported resetting, one unknown model fixed throughout the experiment, mandatory success stopping, a common positive call-cost floor, and guarantees for every initial physical density matrix.

Section 55 changes only the permitted post-failure label update: with a small probability it returns to the already counted initial label. It does not reset the physical state, add memory, supply a clock or reveal the model. Its finite-segment proof gives explicit initial and global continuation bounds. Transferring the resulting bounded witnesses yields the uniform-in-M comparison, including at changes of reachable support. The square-root modulus is a proved upper estimate, not a claimed optimal exponent. Under an additional uniform per-command success floor, the stronger Lipschitz estimate uses classical geometric contraction and resolvent perturbation; any sharp scalar coefficients belong to that stronger, explicitly stated regime.

## Direct primary comparisons

### Fixed-size controller optimization and parameter synthesis

C. Amato, D. S. Bernstein and S. Zilberstein, *Optimizing fixed-size stochastic controllers for POMDPs and decentralized POMDPs*, Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems **21** (2010), 293–320, DOI [10.1007/s10458-009-9103-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-009-9103-z), already formulates fixed-size stochastic-controller optimization as a nonlinear program. The current revision retains the predecessor comparison and bibliography key `AmatoBernsteinZilberstein10`. Controller variables and a finite nonlinear program are not new inventions of R79.

S. Junges, N. Jansen, R. Wimmer, T. Quatmann, L. Winterer, J.-P. Katoen and B. Becker, *Finite-state Controllers of POMDPs via Parameter Synthesis*, UAI 2018, 519–529, [official proceedings paper](https://www.auai.org/uai2018/proceedings/papers/195.pdf), gives the fixed-memory controller/parametric-Markov-chain correspondence in Theorem 2 and synthesis reductions in Theorem 3. Its specifications include indefinite-horizon rewards; Section 5 discusses zero probabilities. Thus product-state constructions and exact bounded-controller parameter synthesis are established. R79's comparison concerns its operator setting, model-dependent reachable certificates and same-memory stability theorem, not the existence of algebraic controller variables.

### Robust and fixed hidden-model controllers

M. Cubuktepe, N. Jansen, S. Junges, A. Marandi, M. Suilen and U. Topcu, *Robust Finite-State Controllers for Uncertain POMDPs*, AAAI **35**(13) (2021), 11792–11800, DOI [10.1609/aaai.v35i13.17401](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/17401), is a direct predecessor. The examined [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.11459v2), Sections 2–4, requires one FSC to satisfy undiscounted expected-reward-to-goal conditions for all fixed model instantiations. It assumes no dead ends, expressed as eventual goal reachability under every policy, and graph-preserving uncertainty. Its semi-infinite nonlinear formulation and synthesis method must be credited. R79 permits model-dependent reachable supports and support-changing boundaries and proves its own exact certificate and global comparisons; it is not the first robust or undiscounted FSC formulation.

M. F. L. Galesloot, R. Andriushchenko, M. Češka, S. Junges and N. Jansen, *Robust Finite-Memory Policy Gradients for Hidden-Model POMDPs*, IJCAI 2025, 8518–8526, DOI [10.24963/ijcai.2025/947](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/947), optimizes a common FSC across fixed hidden models. The [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09518) combines worst-model evaluation with policy gradients for goal rewards under support/reachability assumptions. A shared robust controller for an unknown fixed model is therefore established. R79 distinguishes its exact boundary certificates and resource-preserving perturbation theorem. Memory counts also require matching the observation interface: a freely available current observation is different from information that must reside in a counted autonomous label.

### Quantum instruments and reachable supports

Reuse `Barry14`. R79's fixed finite-controller effectivity does not contradict unrestricted-policy undecidability and is not an introduction of quantum sequential control.

N. Yu and M. Ying, *Reachability and Termination Analysis of Concurrent Quantum Programs*, CONCUR 2012, LNCS **7454**, 69–83, DOI [10.1007/978-3-642-32940-1_7](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-32940-1_7), [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.1935), proves finite-dimensional reachable-support stabilization. S. Ying, Y. Feng, N. Yu and M. Ying, *Reachability Probabilities of Quantum Markov Chains*, CONCUR 2013, LNCS **8052**, 334–348, DOI [10.1007/978-3-642-40184-8_24](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-40184-8_24), [Lemma 1(2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.0060), records that construction and attributes it to Yu–Ying while developing broader quantum reachability structure. The Md-iterate block-support construction in R79 is a specialization of this finite-dimensional argument and the paper's own earlier support theory, not an independent priority claim.

### Perturbation theory and current neighboring work

The manuscript retains the classical stochastic-shortest-path foundation `BertsekasTsitsiklis91` and earlier robustness references `KaraYuksel20` and `Demirci25`. In particular, Y. E. Demirci, A. D. R79 makes no first-continuity claim for partially observed control. Its stopped positive-cost objective, changing reachable supports and exact same-M comparison are the distinctions to be assessed.

A targeted supplementary check examined D. P. Bertsekas and H. Its perturbation adds positive stage costs to distinguish proper from improper policies. That is a classical regularization precedent; it does not by itself establish the present label-update bound. Similarly, geometric contraction under a uniform one-step success floor and the associated resolvent identity are classical, and are credited as the mechanism of the stronger special-regime estimates.

The search also inspected E. M. Bovy, C. Probine, M. Suilen, U. Topcu and N. It studies finite-horizon robust rewards and time-dependent finite-state controllers, including real-algebraic complexity. These conventions differ from autonomous stopped total cost, but preclude broad novelty claims about real-algebraic robust memory synthesis. It is cited as a preprint, not assigned an inferred publication status.

B. Kraske, Q. H. Ho, F. Rossi, M. Lahijanian and Z. The inspected fixed-policy evaluation results do not supply the proposed optimal-frontier theorem through same-size controller replacement. ## Effective synthesis and theorem scope

The compact semialgebraic extension requires effectively specified real-algebraic coefficients, or rational functions over them without poles on the compact model set. It retains the common-controller quantifier before the unknown model and allows support/cost witnesses to vary with that model. The finite exact elimination result is not a practical algorithm, a polynomial complexity theorem, or a claim that general elimination has been executed in the diagnostics. Attainment comes from the controller space and the lower-semicontinuity argument; it does not follow from a false uniform bound on all continuation witnesses.

The same-memory stability theorem uses all physical initial states, a pure counted initial label, legal feedback, fixed models and positive costs. Its constants are independent of M but depend on the stated one-row cap and cost bounds. A stronger success-floor theorem has that additional hypothesis. The golden-ratio frontier and its sharp realization are exact for one displayed experiment. Reset-only normal forms, closed-cover formulas, other resource laws and local geometry retain their original scope. None of these conclusions completes optional commitment, the original general-M squared-sign minimax-risk problem, the unmatched unrestricted-decoder growth rates, or independent A1/A2 goals.
