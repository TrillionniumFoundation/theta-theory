## Exact boundary between established and added content

**Convex finite-group specification.** Finite Markov closed-class decomposition, aperiodic stationary convergence, nonnegative stochastic group structure, compactness and finite-dimensional Fourier theory are established ingredients. The proof is supplied here because it must work without an exact observable quotient: identity-word averages stay in the allowed set, the sandwiched operators form a finite-group permutation action, and the projected preparation has the correct pure initialization charge. The false stronger equation `E Q_l=E` is not used. The new theorem yields a finite minimax problem over all finite command words, a stable exact-memory gap and quantitative multiplicity obstructions. The prime cyclic example has matching upper and lower error. Targeted searches did not locate this same cost-preserving closed-convex formulation; this is not proof that no equivalent formulation exists elsewhere.

**Bounded centered coefficients.** Representation decomposition, subgroup fixed multiplicity, the subgroup-index estimate, exact normal form and R75 one-output singular extremum are inherited. The new weighted two-output inequality uses elementary range bounds and AM–GM. It implies the full three-piece function `F_n(p)` and explicit equality classification. In particular the curved segment uses a common null vector of both output matrices, not merely singularity of one; at its left endpoint the marginal column is proved unique directly. Existence of a width `n-1` pair for every feasible parameter is distinguished from a claim about every feasible supplied pair. The group corollary inherits its lower action classification and converts the new boundary to all mean/contrast values. The role of `p` as a group mean, rather than a Bayesian prior, is explicit.

**Return geometry and approximate physical memory.** Triangle barycentric coordinates, stars-and-bars counting, coupling, stopped potentials, positivity of density mixtures, orbit polygon geometry and quadratic conjugate-norm estimates are elementary or classical ingredients. The new synthesis gives one simulator for an entire adaptive policy class at fixed target expected budget and relates the count to continuation-orbit growth. The one-angle refinement reuses a finite orbit prefix as return seeds, so the dictionary count is `K+L-1`, rather than `K L`. The lower uses early-abort tests to keep their expected cost uniform in prefix length, obtains fidelity from joint events without assuming conditional accuracy on a rare event, and packs physically valid dictionary points. For a fixed badly approximable phase and fixed positive `B`, the leading logarithmic constant is one. It is not a universal HMM lower, not uniform in two varying parameters, and not a Diophantine claim about `(3+4i)/5`.
