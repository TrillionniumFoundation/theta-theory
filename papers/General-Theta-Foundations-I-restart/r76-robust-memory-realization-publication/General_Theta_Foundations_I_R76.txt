                   GENERAL THETA FOUNDATIONS I:
         ACQUIRED GEOMETRY AND CAUSAL RESOURCE TRANSFER

                                                     QIAN QI


        Abstract. We study the positive memory required to reproduce complete causal response laws.
        For every finite-group target, closed convex constraints on all command words admit a permutation
        realization at no greater memory cost. This reduces optimal uniform approximation over words of
        unbounded length to an attained finite optimization and gives robust multiplicity lower bounds.
        A sharp simultaneous-positivity inequality determines the complete feasibility region for bounded
        centered coefficient matrices with a common null vector. It yields a three-piece exact mean–contrast
        memory law for an unbounded symmetric-group family, together with boundary rigidity and optimal
        strategies. The observable group and predictive dimension stay fixed while the positive resource
        changes. For general finite-command quantum instruments, a physical return polytope and rare
        continuation branches give one finite simulator for every adaptive stopped experiment of bounded
        target expected cost. The memory depends on continuation-orbit growth; commuting branches
        give polylogarithmic bounds. For a fixed badly approximable qubit phase we prove a matching
        logarithmic law, with leading constant one, within the explicit class of physical-state dictionaries
        with Born terminal decoders. The inherited stationary initialization theorem separates the counted
        pure start from the continuing memory. Complete proofs of the general causal comparison, reset,
        optimal discrimination, variational, dual, spectral and geometric results are retained with their
        individual hypotheses.




                                              1. Introduction
   A predictive state contains the information in an experimental past that can affect future laws.
Its linear dimension measures one kind of compression. A controller that stores probabilities on
finitely many labels must also respect positivity, the preparation of its initial state, and the effect
of subsequent observations. These constraints can make its minimum memory much larger than
the predictive dimension. They also distinguish exact realization, uniform approximation of every
command word, and approximation of experiments stopped at bounded expected cost. We study
these distinctions within the original theory of causal resource comparison and optimal statistical
discrimination.
   A terminal response assigns a probability row b(w) to each finite command word. An instrument
response assigns joint probabilities to command–report histories and includes the state on which a
terminal measurement acts. A classical realization uses one fixed collection of nonnegative transition
and readout tables. Its initial label is pure and counted, except in explicitly named mixed-start
comparisons. The physical quantum system, an external policy’s storage, program bits, arithmetic
precision and sampling work are separate resources. A private simulator label is never made visible
to the external policy.
   The central question is how an algebraic or geometric description of future responses controls
their positive implementation. The present results give complementary answers. A finite-group
specification admits a cost-preserving permutation reduction even when the required laws have

                 2. Prepared kernels, acquired task laws and observers
   All hidden-state, action and report spaces are standard Borel. A fixed unknown index θ ∈ Θ
specifies an actual preparation and positive normalized causal kernels. Positivity means nonnegative
measures, not an everywhere strictly positive density. Dependence on the entire physical past
may be included in the hidden state. A legal policy sees past visible reports and its admitted
randomization, but not θ or a future audit. Iteration of these kernels defines its finite-prefix laws;
the consistent countable path law is their kernel extension.
   Each call has a cost ci ≥ c∗ > 0. The decision to make the next call is predictable. In the
geometric route, the declared productive-call flag is also predictable; in the primitive-instrument
route, productive reports are specified outcomes and can be random. Commitment is permitted
only after a productive report, and occurs exactly once almost surely in each model. At that time
a specified future audit Z ∈ H is scored, where H ⊂ Rk is nonempty, compact and convex. The
prediction is committed before that audit is observed. A prospective joint report–audit kernel is
defined at every productive call, including rejected calls. The stopping coin is independent of the
audit conditional on the entire current visible report and the past. Write L = diam(H)2 ; the case
L = 0 is trivial whenever a legal rule exists.
   The core cost Cτ includes initial preparation charged to the core, failed calls and all productive
calls through commitment. A separately executed final audit must be added to the raw budget.
This convention is fixed before taking a risk infimum. Physical time, stored program constants and
temporary computation are not identified with call count.

Three resource interfaces. A checkpoint procedure may use its whole precommit history, but
must output one element of a fixed dictionary of at most M values. This counts its output width
only; it is not a claim of log2 M total working bits.
   An autonomous observer has an alphabet S, |S| ≤ M , and one fixed parameter-independent
program. At an intercall boundary its only persistent observer memory is its label s ∈ S. Its next
command distribution is a fixed function of s. A physical port name, elapsed time, subprogram
index, random seed or former report is not an extra free input. During a call its current command,
current report and fresh coins are available. Whatever persists to another call must be encoded in
S. A fixed decoder r : S → H reads only the committed label.
   In the event-stopped interface a post-report kernel emits (s′ , e) ∈ S × {0, 1}. An event e = 1 ends
the experiment and retains s′ for the decoder; no subsequent command is executed. This public
commitment event is not a stored bit used in continuing computation. The same label may be used
live on one path and terminal on another. In the state-stopped interface a fixed map h∗ : S → {0, 1}
determines halt status. The initial label is live, and type-one labels are globally absorbing terminal
labels with no executed command rows. Entry into a terminal label is allowed only after a productive
report. Thus live and terminal types are disjoint in this interface, and only in this interface by
definition.
   Write RiΘ (N, M ), i ∈ {cp, ev, st}, for the infimum of worst-case expected squared loss over
programs with robust almost-sure commitment and supθ Eθ Cτ ≤ N . An empty legal class has value
+∞. A program chosen once at random is not a free persistent seed. The ideal measurable class
may have fixed real constants; effective implementation is stated separately in Section S40.

            3. Reversible response groups and common controlled memory
   The fixed-phase results concern one transition matrix. Several commands require one family of
matrices on the same retained labels, and checking every command in isolation is insufficient. We
give an intrinsic finite-realization criterion for responses whose minimal linear dynamics is reversible
and compact, including any finite family of unitary commands on a prepared finite-dimensional
quantum system. We then obtain a matching arithmetic memory law for a pair of noncommuting
involutions. This is a theorem about exact controlled response realization, not an assertion that a
simulator’s private label is an observed sufficient statistic for arbitrary feedback optimization.
   Positive realization through invariant polyhedral sets is classical [48, 47, 46]. The argument
below uses a section of the realizing simplex before passing to the observable quotient; auxiliary
hidden-state responses need not belong to the minimal response space. Compact reversibility then
turns containment into equality. The resulting finite-group obstruction concerns all command words,
rather than a single spectral restriction.

           5. The complete mean–contrast law and optimal-factor rigidity
   The mean of a response and the size of its nonconstant part impose distinct positivity constraints.
The return family of Theorem S3.6 fixes the mean at 1/n. We now allow every feasible mean and
both signs of contrast, for every n ≥ 5. A second positivity inequality changes the sharp boundary:
simply rescaling the old threshold gives the wrong answer on an interval of means. The proof yields
the complete boundary factorization on that interval. Throughout, the mean is the average terminal
probability over the group; it is not a prior on an unknown parameter.
   Bounded simplex-structured factorization and stacking complementary nonnegative slacks with a
common factor are established techniques [77, Section IV-C]. Threshold attainment and boundary
identifiability also occur in the geometric theory of nonnegative rank [78, Section 4.3]. Our additional
conclusion is the sharp simultaneous coefficient extremum below, its three-piece parameter region,
and the resulting unrestricted group-memory and boundary-factor laws.

vector. Both outcomes must be represented through the same preparation; this common-null-vector
requirement is stronger than separate rank bounds on the two outcome matrices.

             7. Approximation by truncating rare conditional excursions
   Exact memory can be infinite although one finite simulator approximates every stopped experiment
with a prescribed expected cost. The damping construction of Theorem S7.1 gives such an upper
bound for the entire angle circle. For a fixed finite command alphabet we now obtain a smaller
bound by preserving a long conditional excursion exactly and modifying only its overflow. The
general mechanism depends on the growth of the continuation semigroup, and applies beyond the
qubit family.
   The ingredients have classical antecedents. Polyhedral positive realizations are part of the theory
of Heller and of Monràs and Winter [48, 47, 76]. Martingale calculations for pattern waiting times
are classical [80]. The use made here is a uniform approximation of complete adaptive stopped
laws, with an explicit memory bound from a finite return polytope and the number of distinct
continuation states. It is separate from the exact noisy realization problem of [50].

                      10. Scope and further mathematical questions
   The finite-group convex-specification theorem is general over the stated finite alphabets, finite
groups, closed convex constraints and arbitrary stochastic controllers. Its uniform approximation
quantifier ranges over all finite command words. The resulting finite optimization is not a closed
numerical formula for every group and target. The exact small-contrast multiplicity law and
normal-stabilizer convexity criterion remain general within their stated classes. The complete
mean–contrast formula concerns the displayed symmetric-group family for every n ≥ 5; it does not
classify high contrast for all finite groups. Its matrix synthesis theorem is an existence equivalence
over parameter pairs, not a positive-factorization theorem for every given feasible matrix.
   The return-polytope theorem applies to finite-command instruments on an explicit invariant
physical state set, with a uniform conditional bound on continuing branches and a finite return
polytope. These hypotheses are verified for the undamped qubit application. A fixed finite
angle alphabet gives the new polylogarithmic upper; the retained damping construction works
simultaneously for the entire angle circle. The matching logarithmic lower and leading constant
require fixed B > 0, a fixed badly approximable phase, and physical dictionaries with Born terminal
readouts. They do not apply to arbitrary classical decoder tables without a further argument. The
original algebraic phase has an explicit rational-table logarithmic upper; its analogous Diophantine
lower certificate is not supplied here.
   The stationary initialization theorem applies to arbitrary predictively stationary finite-alphabet
response laws and makes no quantum, reversibility, finite-rank or irreducibility assumption. The
exact noisy instrument laws concern their stated family and spectral regimes. One-dependence
and stationarity are assertions about a fixed command; adaptive command choices can transmit
dependence. A private simulator label is not an observation available to the original policy. The
approximating process need not satisfy the target’s expected stopping budget: total variation alone
does not control an unbounded expectation.
   The general-M squared-sign minimax-risk frontier, unrestricted approximate-decoder lower
bounds, arbitrary nonrigid robust feedback and tied boundary decisions, and the intermediate
phase-type timing frontier remain distinct mathematical problems. None is replaced by a solved
response-memory problem. Program length, arithmetic precision, working storage and exact
sampling costs also remain distinct from live labels.
   The actual-history comparison theorem and the independent causal, reset, discrimination and
geometric results keep their original quantifiers. Local geometric conclusions remain local. Indepen-
dent A1, A2 and further Theta construction objectives require their own proofs. The supplement
contains the complete technical arguments and independent developments; all inherited mathemati-
cal bodies and the separate R58 companion have traceable preserved locations. Historical drafts
and source-recovery records are available without becoming prerequisites for reading the current
theorem chain.
