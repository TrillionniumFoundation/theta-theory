# Counterexample and sharpness ledger

1. **Dropping rare prepared mass.** Preparation (1-b)A+bT has b>0 trapped mass. Charging only the ready component allows an infinite failure clock at no apparent cost. The true budget contains b E T0. The exact value at N=1/p is positive and scales as b^((M-1)/M) for fixed M.
2. **Lipschitz optimal-value claim at fixed resources.** For M=2 the exact prep risk is determined by F=(pb+sqrt(p^2b^2+4(1-p)b))/2; prediction risk is F/2-F^2/4. Nominal risk is zero and prep norm error is2b. A uniform Lipschitz bound fails; square-root order is necessary.
3. **One-call TV/base-norm alone without paid mean.** Move probability h of a failure transition to T. The exact M=2 risk satisfies F^2=(N-1)h(1-F), despite a raw one-call error2h. At large N the expected number of opportunities must enter the bound.
4. **Uncharged external clock.** A homogeneous k-live-state absorption mechanism has the stated phase frontier. A deterministic externally timed cutoff with more stages changes the resource model and need not satisfy a k-state claim.
5. **Fixed-competitor continuity versus reoptimization.** The nominal boundary-optimal wait-for-success program becomes inadmissible when b>0. The theorem transfers a regularized program at the same budgets; it does not assert feasibility of the unmodified program or continuity of the occupation projection.
6. **Norm error versus posterior smoothness.** Rank-changing quantum effects have controlled unnormalized instruments while normalized posteriors can be unstable at small likelihoods. No division by a vanishing likelihood occurs in the transfer.
7. **General M exact prediction.** The all-M failure value is exact; squared prediction is bounded between F/4 and F/2-F^2/4. Equality of the upper for all M is NOT asserted. It is exact for M=2.
8. **Asymptotic quantifiers.** The exponent (M-1)/M is for fixed M,p as b tends to zero. The implicit root is the all-budget statement; the asymptotic is not claimed uniformly for M growing with 1/b.

Inherited ledgers retain separate-model routing impossibility, phantom invariant mass, local/global cover failure, free deadlines, and unsupported fixed-time-to-stopping substitutions. Their original mathematical sources remain unchanged.
