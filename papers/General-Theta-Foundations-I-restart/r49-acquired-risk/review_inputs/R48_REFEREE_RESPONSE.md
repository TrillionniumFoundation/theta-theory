## Principal mathematical response

The earlier causal-stopping development gives an exact common-program occupation criterion and an actual-law geometric profile under a direct expected-acquisition budget. The present revision includes those proofs and adds a constructive completion: for every rational N>=1 and M>=2, finite raw-instrument certificates yield a finitely described common program and arbitrarily tight bounds for the optimal risk **without enlarging N or M**. Competitors remain all legal Borel randomized finite-state observers under the specified controlled, stopped interface. Their original stopping times need not satisfy a uniform absorption condition.

The new proof regularizes a competitor by rerouting a small fraction of live decisions to an already existing terminal label. It proves a paid mean margin and a bounded-risk loss. An occupation-weighted positive-map resolvent then bounds terminal-risk and mean-cost errors separately. Uniform report averaging and dyadic rounding cover every Borel program, not merely regular candidate policies. The margin makes a finite one-sided feasibility test sufficient despite potentially undecidable boundary comparisons. A rigorous finite global lower follows from this approximation of every competitor. The construction never reads the true parameter, exact posterior, past reports, or an external clock.

A new same-budget continuity theorem transfers the optimal value between raw experiment families. The quantitative sufficient modulus is capped by L and is L[sqrt(2(N-1)(delta0+N eta))+delta0+N eta]+alpha. Its exponent is not claimed best possible. The exact endpoint N=1 is proved separately.

## Breadth and realizations

Two independent new raw verifications apply the **same** exact-budget theorem. The classical theorem permits controlled Gaussian observations of a finite hidden state with unknown parameters in an effective compact family; transitions can be identity or reducible and need not mix. It keeps the entire Gaussian tail as a charged digitized report outcome. The quantum theorem treats noncommuting positive instruments with possibly rank-deficient effects and noncontracting channels. The square-root Holder estimate controls raw maps at rank change, without inverting an effect or claiming normalized posterior continuity there. Both proofs derive executable test closure, report approximation, uniform parameter moduli and legal decoder nets from the raw kernels.

Explicit matching geometric rates remain those of the retained general report-recoverable theorem: correlated controlled classical kernels and noncommuting covariant quantum instruments verify that theorem independently. The new informative Gaussian certificate is **not** mislabeled a closed-form acquired-dimension law. Unknown fixed parameters range over a known family; this is not a general unknown-kernel learning/regret theorem.

## Specific scope of the response

The report's request for active acquisition and stopping is addressed by the declared optimization class; its finite implementation issue is advanced to same-budget finite synthesis. Common support is not required to be strictly positive: zero transition entries and rank-changing effects are allowed under a common dominating reference measure. This is not a nondominated theorem. Program bits, workspace and physical calls are separately counted; the finite enumeration is not claimed efficient.

The requested pair of quantitatively contrasting examples for the *old* curvature versus pathwise stability routes (17.8) is not newly supplied. The new construction avoids either route, but this does not retrospectively answer that particular comparison request. A universal informative-filter power law under nonsummable sensitivity, all computational-resource lower bounds, and an intrinsic classification beyond finite affine effective certificates remain unproved. The retained costed binary Gaussian example is not promoted into a new sharp direct-mean Gaussian law. An explicit requested numerical end-to-end Gaussian benchmark with certified computed optimal table is not reported as executed here.

The introduction now separates standard probability/convexity tools, inherited occupation/profile theorems, and the new exact-budget construction. The manuscript includes the theorem-level comparison with value-directed compression, Bayes-risk/Bregman quantization, policy-conditional window-memory approximation, absorbing occupation theory and its 2025 finite-mixture continuation. Mathematical importance and priority remain questions for independent review, not outcomes of this response.
