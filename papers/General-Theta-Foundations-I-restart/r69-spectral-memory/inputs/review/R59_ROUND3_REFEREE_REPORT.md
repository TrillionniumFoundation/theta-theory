## 2. Executive description of the mathematical architecture

### 2.1 The physically acquired marked profile

A genuinely prepared comparison experiment supplies a joint report--audit law

```text
K_0(dx,dz)
```

with report marginal `nu`. Disintegration gives

```text
f(x) = E_0[Z | x],
w(x) = E_0[||Z-f(x)||^2 | x],
d_r(x) = w(x)+||f(x)-r||^2.
```

For a dictionary of at most `m` responses, the profile is

```text
Q_m(b)
 = min ∫ [w+dist(f,C)^2] h dnu
```

subject to

```text
0 <= h <= b,   ∫h dnu = 1,   |C| <= m.
```

The cap `b` records the physical price of selection. If the selected probability law is formed from a proposal law at expected-attempt budget `b`, its density relative to the proposal cannot exceed `b`. The residual mark `w` prevents a report--audit prediction task from being replaced by location quantization alone.

The variational functional is classical in spirit. The manuscript correctly presents the novelty as its causal transfer and resource interpretation, not the invention of trimming or population quantization.

### 2.2 The lower and upper have different, correct quantifiers

The lower fixes one model `theta_*`, independently of the competing program. At every actually reached productive pre-call history and every legal command, it compares the full current report--audit row with a reference row. It also requires one all-program effort lower and actual-occupation bounds on accumulated mass and task defects.

The upper is logically separate. One parameter-independent acquisition program must have a conditional whole-attempt cost bound and mass/task certificates after every surviving history in every model. It also supplies explicit live-role and export-width bounds.

Consequently the theorem has the valid robust structure

```text
one actual witness  -> lower bound on every program,
one common program  -> upper bound in every model.
```

The comparison experiment need not be an operating model. The lower and upper need not use the same operating model. No minimization/maximization exchange is used.

### 2.3 Central inequalities

Put

```text
b_- = N/kappa_-,
b_+ = N/kappa_+,
t_N = (1-A_N)_+/u_-.
```

Then the lower is

```text
R_i^Theta(N,M)
 >= [l_- t_N Q_{m_-(M)}(b_-/t_N)-E_N]_+.
```

For

```text
beta_N = l_+ b_+/(1+b_+ a_+) >= 1,
```

the common program gives

```text
R_i^Theta(N,M)
 <= [u_+(1+b_+a_+)/l_+] Q_{m_+(M)}(beta_N)+b_+e_+.
```

When `beta_N<1`, the cap-one first-attempt construction gives the separate stated upper. The directions, factors, and resource widths are internally consistent.

### 2.4 Relative order versus additive defects

With all additive defects zero, fixed two-sided order factors produce only constant changes in risk and effective selection budget. In particular, a task direction with zero reference loss remains zero under positive two-sided order.

A fixed additive one-call task error behaves differently. Selection can concentrate the error on a rare low-loss region and amplify it by the expected number of attempts. Appendix B gives an explicit witness with amplification `b e`. This is the correct conceptual reason that relative calibration can preserve an arbitrarily fine resource curve while a fixed absolute error may create a floor.

## 3. Independent audit of the lower proof

### 3.1 The actual-history device is essential and correct

A competitor may terminate and satisfy its cost constraint under the operating model but fail to do so under a substituted comparison experiment. Therefore one cannot simply rerun the policy under the comparison law.

R59 avoids this error. At each actual reached pre-call history under `theta_*`, it replaces only the current productive row inside a nonnegative occupation integral. Earlier histories retain their actual distribution.

For each terminal decoder value `c`, the proof defines a finite measure `xi_c` on the comparison mark space. Their sum satisfies

```text
xi <= (E_* A_tau) nu <= b_- nu.
```

The corresponding sum using actual rows has mass exactly one because the competitor commits exactly once almost surely. Applying the lower mass certificate call by call gives

```text
1 <= u_- xi(X)+A_N.
```

Applying the task certificate on the disjoint actual commitment events gives

```text
actual risk >= l_- sum_c ∫d_c dxi_c - E_N.
```

The reference task identities are conditioned on the full current report, not only on its compressed mark. Thus an extra visible coordinate cannot secretly reveal the audit while leaving the mark marginal unchanged.

### 3.2 Normalization and density cap

Let `s=xi(X)`. When `t_N>0`, the mass inequality gives `s>=t_N`. Scale

```text
xi'=(t_N/s)xi.
```

It has mass `t_N` and remains dominated by `b_-nu`. Therefore

```text
h=(d xi'/dnu)/t_N
```

has unit mass and cap `b_-/t_N`.

Nearest-dictionary projection and nonnegative loss yield

```text
sum_c ∫d_c dxi_c
 >= t_N Q_{m_-(M)}(b_-/t_N).
```

No infimum/supremum exchange occurs. When `t_N=0`, the theorem correctly asserts only the trivial zero lower and does not attempt to evaluate the profile at an infinite cap.

### 3.3 Decoder-width converse

A checkpoint or event-stopped observer has at most one decoder value per retained label. A state-stopped observer with at least `B_0` actually reached live labels has at most `M-B_0` terminal labels because the global halt map makes live and terminal types disjoint.

The physical live-role lower is based on intersections of physically legal command sets across mandatory contexts, not on printed command names. Duplicate names and stateless recodings therefore do not evade it.

I find the lower proof correct under its stated certificates.

## 4. Independent audit of the common upper

### 4.1 Conditional hazard

Choose an attained profile optimizer `(C,h)` at cap

```text
beta_N=l_+b_+/(1+b_+a_+).
```

The common program accepts the virtual mark with probability `h/beta_N`. The lower mass certificate for that program gives, conditionally on every surviving history,

```text
P(accept next attempt)
 >= l_+/beta_N-a_+
 = 1/b_+.
```

This conditional statement is the key. It gives a geometric survival bound even if each rejection changes the hidden physical state in an arbitrary history-dependent way. No renewal assumption is used.

### 4.2 Cost

Every begun attempt has conditional expected whole-attempt cost at most `kappa_+`. Since the expected number of begun attempts is at most `b_+`, predictable summation gives

```text
E C_tau <= kappa_+ b_+ = N.
```

The proof does not use bounded task-law discrepancy to infer an unbounded time or cost estimate.

### 4.3 Loss

The one-attempt unnormalized accepted loss is bounded by

```text
u_+ Q/beta_N + e_+.
```

Summing over the disjoint terminal events with the survival estimate gives the stated upper. Export classes and live roles are then packed according to the stopping interface.

The cap-one first-attempt case is also correct: `b_+>=1` implies `N>=kappa_+`, so the whole attempt fits the physical budget.

I find the upper proof correct under its all-history and all-model hypotheses.

## 5. Third-round focal audits

### 5.1 Profile attainment and left continuity

The profile uses an ordered tuple in compact `H^m`, allowing repetitions, and a weak-star compact set of densities in the radius-`b` ball of `L^infinity(nu)`. Uniform convergence of squared-distance functions in the tuple variable and weak-star pairing against the fixed limiting loss give continuity along product nets. This avoids an unjustified sequential compactness assertion.

For left continuity at `b>1`, mixing an optimizer with the constant density one produces caps increasing to `b` from below and objectives converging to the optimum. Monotonicity gives the reverse inequality. I find this argument correct.

### 5.2 Exact affine-square order and singular ranges

The matrix measure

```text
J_P(D)=∫_{D×H} v(z)v(z)^T dP
```

encodes all localized affine-square tests. Positive semidefinite order on every Borel report event is exactly equivalent to two-sided order of those tests. Taking the constant affine function gives report-mass order; summing the coordinate affine squares gives squared prediction loss for each fixed readout. Finite-image readouts and arbitrary bounded selection functions follow by partition and monotone approximation.

This is an exact criterion for the declared affine-square test family. It is only sufficient for the narrower central prediction task, as the manuscript correctly states.

For singular densities, two-sided order implies equality of kernels and a bounded generalized eigenvalue relation on the common range. The Moore--Penrose inverse is used pointwise through Borel spectral calculus; no uniform inverse norm is taken. Hence changing rank and exact null task directions are handled correctly.

### 5.3 Mixed-dimensional profile with overlapping components

The finite-mixture theorem does not require component labels to be visible or the component measures to be mutually singular.

For a feasible global density `h`, defining

```text
s_j=lambda_j ∫h d sigma_j
```

gives a normalized component density `lambda_j h/s_j` with cap `b lambda_j/s_j`. The same global dictionary has at most `m` points on every component, proving the lower.

For the upper, componentwise optimized selected measures are mixed into

```text
zeta=sum_j s_j h_j sigma_j.
```

Because `s_j h_j <= b lambda_j`, this finite measure is dominated by `b sigma`, even when components overlap. Its Radon--Nikodym density with respect to the actual mixture is therefore a legal global selection function. The union of the component dictionaries gives the stated finite-factor upper.

The result is mathematically correct as an ideal Borel statement. Its effective implementation is not automatic when overlapping components have only abstract descriptions; that obligation belongs to the separate effective hypotheses.

### 5.4 Acquired zero-loss atom

Above the threshold `b lambda>=1`, selecting only the zero-loss atom gives zero profile. Below threshold, at least `s=1-b lambda` selected mass must come from the remainder. Scaling the selected remainder down to mass `s` preserves domination and nonnegative-loss ordering, giving the lower. Reserving one center for the atom and optimizing the remaining selected mass gives the upper for `m>=2`.

The special warning for `m=1` is necessary and present. Under positive residual variance on the remainder, attained finite-cap optimization cannot have zero integral below threshold. I find the threshold argument correct.

### 5.5 Effective finite-description upper

The effective corollary assumes, in addition to the qualitative theorem, an upper domination of every actual report law by `bar u nu`, effective finite physical partitions, certified integration, and finite dictionary nets.

An `L^1(nu)` approximation of the ideal finite decision vector then controls the actual conditional decision discrepancy by `bar u` times that error after every surviving history. Choosing a strict hazard margin preserves robust termination, while choosing task and readout approximations at the profile scale preserves the rate.

This is a valid exhaustive-effectivity statement. It is not a polynomial-time synthesis theorem and should not be presented as one.

### 5.6 Finite calibration and deterministic guard

The calibration proposition correctly separates:

- the probability of falsely declaring a certificate;
- the probability of rejecting a genuinely tighter sampler;
- expected deployment cost on a true certificate;
- the deterministic `T`-attempt cost on a false certificate;
- fallback on certificate failure; and
- the residual probability of forced commitment after `T` attempts.

The guard is essential because a small confidence-failure probability alone cannot guarantee almost-sure termination. The proposition explicitly charges calibration count states, attempt-index states, fallback, and terminal roles. I find its probability, expected-cost, and risk bookkeeping consistent.

### 5.7 Predictive quotient: correct fixed-law scope

The quotient appendix is a fixed prepared-law theorem. A countable determining coordinate map is continuous on a compact exhaustion. Each compact image is compact, so the actual image is a countable union of compact sets and hence Borel. The nested compact-cell construction gives a Borel section.

Continuation closure and a countable report-determining family prove that representatives with the same coordinate vector have almost-surely identical next quotient values under the common report law. Impossible reports are completed arbitrarily rather than assigned a false canonical posterior.

The theorem does not give a common executable quotient for an arbitrary unknown-model family. The manuscript explicitly retains this limitation. I find the fixed-law theorem correct.

## 6. Stopping semantics and exact resource laws

### 6.1 Event stopping

A post-report kernel emits `(s',e)`. If `e=1`, the experiment terminates and `s'` is decoded. Since no command is executed after the terminal event, a label can be used as a live command state on continuing paths and as a terminal output state on other paths. The final event is not a continuing memory bit.

Therefore `ell` live roles and `t` terminal roles can be embedded in `max(ell,t)` event-stopped labels.

### 6.2 State stopping

A fixed global halt map types every label as live or terminal. Terminal labels are absorbing and have no command row. Live and terminal roles are disjoint, so `ell+t` labels are required by the direct implementation. A lower certificate showing at least `B_0` reached live labels leaves at most `M-B_0` decoder values.

### 6.3 Exact formulas

Under exact matched certificates and identity exports, the theorem gives separate equalities for checkpoint, event-stopped, and state-stopped interfaces. The subtraction by the live-role count belongs only to state stopping. It is not silently transferred to the event-stopped model.

The role semantics repair the defect identified in the R56 report. I find the R59 formulation internally consistent.

## 7. Raw realizations

### 7.1 Paid gate and productive port

The gate and productive commands have disjoint physical domains and no free port tag. Every productive call is preceded by a successful paid gate call. The actual witness effort lower and common attempt-cost upper are therefore

```text
kappa_- = 1+1/p_1,
kappa_+ = 1+1/p_0.
```

Both mandatory contexts require separate live command roles. Even when gate success is certain, a productive attempt costs two calls, not one.

### 7.2 Classical transitive-group realization

A command applies a group element before a positive report--audit instrument. For the lower witness, componentwise bounds on the hidden symbol distribution are preserved by each permutation, giving full command-conditioned joint-law order for every competitor command.

For the upper, command weights close to uniform and independent of the hidden current input average every input distribution close to the uniform reference. The independence clause is essential and explicitly stated. Positivity of the report--audit kernels transfers the order to the full observable task law.

### 7.3 Quantum Weyl realization

Weyl averaging sends every density matrix to `I/d`. A witness relatively bounded around `I/d` remains so under each conjugation, and positivity of the complete report--audit instrument gives the lower order. Near-uniform Weyl command weights give the common upper for arbitrary incoming states, including pure states.

The fixed witness still has a relative full-rank input condition. The absence of an eigenvalue floor concerns the post-report states and task ranges, where the displayed instrument has genuine pure/full-rank strata. The manuscript does not conflate these two statements.

The classical and quantum examples are legitimate raw verifications. They are nevertheless highly structured balancing families, and this limits the external significance of the current theorem.

## 8. Resource-certified composition and numerical approximation

The simulator proposition correctly retains separate factors for continuing simulator state and terminal sufficient classes. State-stopped and event-stopped resource counts follow from the same role-packing lemma.

Total variation of the complete stopped scored transcript controls a bounded common-task risk, but it does not control unbounded physical time. The theorem therefore requires separate call/time certificates. Reverse risk inequalities require a reverse simulator or an independent lower witness.

For numerical implementation, pointwise acceptance-probability error reduces the conditional hazard, and the cap is adjusted to restore the target `1/b` hazard. Task error is accumulated on attempts, whereas final readout error is added in root-mean-square at the actual terminal event and is not multiplied by the waiting time. This distinction is correct.

The finite center-table bit, scratch, and arithmetic counts are sufficient upper accounts only. No matching computational lower is proved.

## 9. Same-task calibration and deficiency construction

The enlarged experiment combines three losses in one performed task:

1. the original core prediction task;
2. a fresh fair sign that is erased with a declared probability; and
3. a fixed inaccessible calibration sign.

The lower contributions are simultaneous. The erased fresh sign remains fair conditional on all available pre-audit information; averaging the two fixed calibration alternatives gives the `delta_cal^2` term; and the core lower uses the same actual witness. The argument does not combine lower bounds from incompatible models.

The early-sign deficiency is attained: on erasure any immediate simulated sign is independent of the true fair sign, giving the lower, while reporting the true sign off erasure and a fresh fair sign on erasure gives the matching coupling. The report deadline prevents recovery from the later audit.

The state counts in this construction are explicitly sufficient rather than minimal. This is the correct claim.

## 11. Literature positioning

The native bibliography correctly acknowledges Blackwell comparison, trimmed `k`-means, causal-state/predictive-representation antecedents, quantum randomization, and quantum sufficiency. The top-four positioning remains too narrow.

### 11.1 Statistical experiment comparison and deficiency

The manuscript's two-sided matrix-measure order is exact for a designated localized affine-square family. It is not a full randomization or deficiency theorem for all bounded decision problems. This distinction should be made against the broader Le Cam/Blackwell tradition and modern quantum randomization theorems, including work characterizing approximate simulability of quantum experiments or channels by families of guessing tests.

The valuable new question is not whether a general experiment comparison exists, but how one such task-specific comparison interacts with paid causal selection, finite persistent roles, and a protected future audit.

### 11.2 Selective prediction and reject-option risk

The capped density `h`, accepted mass, risk, and randomized acceptance rule have close conceptual neighbors in selective prediction and reject-option classification, where one optimizes selective risk subject to coverage or vice versa. R59 differs because rejection consumes physical calls, the selected object carries a future audit variance mark, the dictionary has a finite width, and the upper must work causally after every surviving history. These differences should be stated explicitly rather than leaving trimmed `k`-means as the only statistical neighbor.

### 11.3 Rejection sampling and channel simulation

The common upper is a rejection-style simulator with a history-uniform hazard. Functional-representation, reverse-channel-coding, and sample-communication literatures analyze related ways of producing a target distribution from a proposal or shared randomness. R59's contribution is not generic rejection sampling; it is the robust all-model causal implementation and the matching all-strategy lower under actual occupation and finite role accounting. This is exactly why the neighboring literature should be compared.

### 11.4 Priority conclusion

I did not locate an existing result that exactly combines:

- one actual-history lower witness against all causal competitors;
- one common all-model upper program;
- a marked trimmed profile with paid expected attempts;
- two-sided relative order preserving exact task-null strata;
- separate event/state stopping resource laws; and
- classical and quantum positive-instrument realizations.

This supports a plausible, narrow novelty claim. It is not an exhaustive priority certificate and does not by itself establish top-four significance.

## 12. Main reasons the top-four recommendation remains negative

### 12.1 The theorem is certificate driven

The decisive lower and upper conditions are supplied at the theorem interface. The manuscript does not derive them from a broad intrinsic property of a prepared causal experiment. In difficult applications, verifying one command-conditioned lower witness for every competitor history and one all-history common upper may contain most of the problem.

### 12.2 Necessity is missing

The matrix-measure order is necessary and sufficient for a declared affine-square family, but the manuscript does not prove that some such order, common acquisition program, or profile representation is necessary for causal resource transfer. The theorem is therefore a strong sufficient architecture, not an intrinsic classification.

### 12.3 The realizations are designed around balancing symmetries

Transitive finite groups and Weyl twirling provide exact, elegant verifications. They are also purpose-built ways to produce a common reference law. The paper does not yet show that the theorem changes the solution of a broadly recognized natural problem with uncontrolled or learned kernels.

### 12.4 The natural-application consequence is limited

No standard selective-prediction, active sensing, POMDP, sequential coding, or quantum discrimination problem receives a previously unknown optimal finite-resource curve as a consequence. The examples validate the framework but do not yet supply the external mathematical impact expected at a top-four general journal.

### 12.5 Effectivity is not complexity

The effective upper uses supplied partitions, integration certificates, dictionary nets, report domination, and exhaustive enumeration. The pilot treats a fixed independent command sampler. These are honest and useful results, but they do not solve general efficient synthesis or calibration for history-dependent unknown kernels.

### 12.6 The title remains broader than the proved native center

The native theorem advances the Foundations program, but it proves a sufficient task-order transfer framework rather than a foundation-wide classification of acquired geometry and causal resources. The introduction and abstract should more visibly identify this scope.
