\section{Introduction and the boundary-memory question}\label{sec:introduction}
A prepared causal experiment consists of physically specified kernels, legal calls, visible reports and an audit that is observed only after commitment. The observer may reject a report, but rejection has its actual cost and may change the protected state. We ask when a constrained prediction optimum exists, how it can be certified against all admitted models, and whether it can be attained by an observer with finitely many persistent classical labels. These questions are distinct even for one algebraic qubit and one command.

\subsection{Complete performed laws, rather than only final outputs}
At a fixed actual decoder dictionary, the probability law of the complete stopped visible word, commitment and prospective audit is the appropriate bounded comparison object. Its finite-prefix coefficients depend continuously on one common sequence-form plan. A positive open-call reserve gives the uniform tail estimate
\[
 \Pr_\theta(\tau>T)\le\min\{1,(N/c_*-1)/T\}.
\]
An open prefix is not declared successful at its cutoff: it still owes one paid call. These two facts make the family of budget-feasible performed laws compact in uniform total variation, even at the least feasible budget. Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-attainment} proves primal attainment; Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-stopped-order} gives a complete bounded-score criterion and exact deficiency. In the finite-score dual, the policy in the inner problem remains feasible in every model, not just in the finite support of the separating score prior.

This compactness is not a claim that expected occupation measures are compact or that total expected duration is continuous. Bounded terminal tails suffice for the performed probability law; they need not control the remaining cost of a stopped policy. Nor is the theorem a quantum-channel randomization result with arbitrary ancillary inputs. It compares legal performed laws on the same declared stopped-outcome space. These distinctions are essential to its application to timing and to finite classical memory.

For a fixed dictionary, the risk frontier has the exact resource-price dual of Corollary~\ref{cor:resource-price}. Compact finite-horizon instrument families also admit necessary-and-sufficient sparse witnesses: Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-witness} uses at most $k_T+1$ total pure cost and risk preparations, where $k_T$ is the affine dimension of the common plan polytope after eliminating flow equalities. Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-bellman} evaluates one such witness by a common unnormalized backward recursion and proves its rowwise slack criterion. Primal feasibility is checked separately. These fixed-dictionary conclusions are not extended to an optimized hard width: Proposition~\ref{prop:width-dual-gap} gives a strict failure of that inference.

\subsection{The additional invariant for exact boundary memory}
Attainment in a compact infinite-plan space does not imply attainment in any finite-label subclass. Interior completion uses a strictly cheaper common fallback; that argument alone says nothing at the least budget. We determine the extra structure on prepared heralded channel experiments, whose failure words have a model-independent positive probability and whose protected state evolves by specified channels.

The identity $p\E\tau=1+\E J$, where $J$ counts rejected heralds, forces commitment at the first herald at budget $1/p$. The remaining optimization is a robust wordwise decision problem, not a free choice of a later physical stopping time. Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-regularity} derives an attained least-favorable measure and a nonnegative decision-slack identity. If all its wordwise Bayes decisions are unique, exact finite stochastic realization is equivalent to finiteness of the residual decision quotient
\[
 w\equiv v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a(wz)=a(vz)
                         \text{ for every failure continuation }z.
\]
Its index is exactly the least live-label count. Randomized transitions cannot lower that count because exact optimality forces every positive-probability path to make the unique optimal decision. Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-ties} supplies the single-model tied version and explains why robust ties need different treatment. This is a quotient for one optimal task, not a hidden claim to have constructed a common predictive state for all unknown-model experiments.

Theorem~\ref{thm:qubit-boundary} realizes the infinite-index case by an algebraic rotating qubit. It gives the exact full-history series, an exact one-label value, a strictly positive gap at every finite memory bound, and exponentially accurate finite-label approximants at the same boundary budget. Corollary~\ref{cor:polarization-boundary} derives a least-favorable member of a compact unknown-polarization family instead of fixing an arbitrary prior. For general finite-dimensional unitary orbits, Corollary~\ref{cor:unitary-period} identifies exact finite memory with pure periodicity of the unique optimal decision sequence. Proposition~\ref{prop:contractive-boundary} supplies exact finite-memory realizations from a contraction and a strict limiting decision gap; Corollary~\ref{cor:noisy-boundary} proves that mixing alone is insufficient. Theorem~\ref{thm:two-label-quantum} determines the exact one- versus two-live-label frontier for every rotation angle and damping strength, including the transition at which the second label first helps. Finally Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-algorithm} gives a polynomial precision-synthesis bound for unary rational channels at a bounded herald rate, with separate label, program and working-storage accounts.

The application is random-horizon prediction of a protected quantum audit, not a predetermined string inserted into a clock. The physical channel determines the task at each history, and the observer has no phase-valued report or coherent-memory access. Irrational rotations and nonregular quantum acceptance have classical antecedents; the claim here is the minimum-budget risk and exact classical-memory theory, not discovery of that automata mechanism.

\subsection{Relation to constrained absorbing control and automata}
The convex-analytic methods are established. Piunovskiy--Zhang \cite[Theorems 1--2 in the cited author text]{PiunovskiyZhang24} identify finite extreme occupation measures with deterministic stationary strategies and, under their hypotheses, obtain mixtures of at most $J+1$ such occupation measures for a constrained total-cost problem. Dufour--Prieto-Rumeau \cite[Theorems 4.3--4.4 and Proposition 4.5]{DufourMixtures} relate faces and minimal mixture order and prove finite-mixture sufficiency for finitely many rewards in uniformly absorbing models. Those objects are primal occupation measures and policies, not the dual model/preparation atoms of Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-witness}.

A stationary policy can still read a continuously valued Markov state; a small number of policies in a mixture therefore does not bound the number of persistent labels needed to implement one of them without that state oracle. In our unary channel example, the exact optimal action is a measurable function of a recurrent quantum orbit. The controller must reconstruct the required decision from its own labels, not receive the orbit point as input. This is precisely the distinction the residual decision quotient measures. The result neither contradicts nor strengthens a primal mixture theorem merely by having a different atom count.

Finite residual equivalence and unary eventual periodicity are classical automata concepts; quantum finite automata and their periodic stochastic events are treated in \cite{Mereghetti24}. A probabilistic automaton with a nonisolated cutpoint can have behavior that is not captured by deterministic language recognition. Our determinization is therefore proved from zero decision slack and uniqueness, not from a false general assertion that every stochastic automaton recognizes only regular languages. The tied robust example makes that proof boundary explicit.

\subsection{The geometric route and the organization of the proofs}
The original task-order route remains a complementary result, with its complete actual-history comparison and common-hazard proofs in Section~\ref{sec:central} and its proof section. Its comparison measure is constructed from the competitor's actual pre-call occupation, not by running the competitor under a fictitious reference law. A separate common paid upper program works in every model. The acquired profile is marked trimmed quantization, with the population-trimming and causal-sampling ancestry credited in the current supplement \cite{CGM97,GG99,LiuVerdu18,GocFlamich24}. The profile is not itself asserted to be a new sampling principle.

The main article concentrates on the performed-law and optimality theory, the new boundary-memory characterization, the exact timing comparison, and the actual-history transfer mechanism. The current technical supplement gives all compact-uncertainty, general-loss, singular-range, zero-spectrum, continuation, reset, detector, geometric and composition proofs and the fixed-law predictive quotient, with cross-document theorem numbers. The full predecessor article and independent companions are preserved separately. Moving a technical proof does not turn its hypothesis into an assumption without proof or discard an older valid conclusion.

The hypotheses that distinguish these statements are summarized here. In particular, physical dimension, controller labels, decoder width and additive expected resources are different quantities.
