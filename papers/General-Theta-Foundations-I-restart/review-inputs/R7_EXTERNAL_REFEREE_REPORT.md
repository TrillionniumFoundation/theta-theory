## Recommendation

**Reject at the level of the Annals of Mathematics, Inventiones Mathematicae, the Journal of the American Mathematical Society, or Acta Mathematica in the manuscript's present form.**

This recommendation is **not based on an identified fatal mathematical error**. Revision R7 is a major and successful response to the R6 report. It does not merely add another verification of the sufficient block theorem. It introduces a genuinely different lower mechanism: a dominated continuation cut turns histories before a causal information cut into conditional future-response functions in a separable Hilbert space, and the quantization width of their actual submeasure obstructs every finite retained alphabet crossing that cut. The same terminal task and Bayes baseline are used on both sides. This is a substantive necessary condition for online/checkpoint matching, even though it is not a characterization.

The revision also proves an optimized acquisition--memory law for a nonhomogeneous anisotropic progressive sensing class, rather than optimizing a policy-dependent acquired law by an illegitimate interchange of infima. The proof gives two policy-uniform lower bounds—unread-tail acquisition variance and physical-law finite-center distortion—and matches their maximum by an exact finite-prefix recursion. The manuscript further supplies a standard categorical filtering-loss theorem, a concrete finite-bit implementation theorem, and an attained erasure-deficiency example in the same scored task.

In the load-bearing arguments checked, I did not find an elementary gap invalidating:

- the continuous-instrument predictive realization on its stated compact and positive-density domain;
- the checkpoint projection identity;
- the dominated-continuation-cut lower for randomized finite-state predictors;
- the integration of that lower with the relation-preserving block upper;
- the delayed-query online/checkpoint separation;
- the merged-list greedy allocation identity;
- the product-cylinder small-ball estimate;
- the adaptive unread-tail variance lower;
- the optimized acquisition--memory theorem;
- the exact erasure-deficiency calculation under the compulsory completion interface;
- the finite-bit feasible implementation account;
- the primitive sparse-Gaussian block certificate;
- the actual posterior Jacobian lower for ordinary categorical squared filtering loss;
- the stopped occupation estimate; or
- the common-task morphism and reverse-transfer implications under their declared classes.

The negative top-four judgment instead concerns the **scope and concentration of the advance**. The new necessary obstruction requires a nonzero product minorization of the actual prefix/suffix law and is not shown sufficient, dual to the block certificate, or quantitatively complete over a broad natural class. Its only sharp online/checkpoint separation in the native article is a classical delayed-query/random-access construction. The optimized sensing theorem is exact and nonasymptotic, but its raw experiment consists of independent fair progressive digit streams. Conditional on the complete acquired history, unread tails remain independent and fair, so observed values cannot improve the deterministic allocation order; the theorem solves a structured multiscale bit-allocation problem rather than general adaptive experiment design. The Gaussian theorem is now stated for a standard loss, but it remains a known-kernel finite-state class with fixed dimension, primitive support and uniform nondegeneracy bounds.

Thus R7 is substantially stronger than R6 and, in my view, constitutes a serious paper. For a strong specialist journal in probability, information theory, nonlinear filtering, stochastic control, quantization, or applied probability, the outlook is **favorable after a focused major revision**. The remaining revision should primarily sharpen the information-theoretic antecedents of the two new mechanisms, apply the continuation obstruction in a genuinely noisy nontrivial model, and make the submission package self-contained about the retained companion.

## 2. Executive assessment of revision R7

R7 changes the logical shape of the paper. The central result is no longer only

```text
raw block stability + a relation-preserving finite cover
    => a recursively executable finite-label upper bound.
```

It is now a two-sided framework:

```text
actual dominated continuation geometry at every cut
    => necessary finite-state lower bounds,

relation-preserving block stability
    => constructive finite-state upper bounds.
```

The lower and upper refer to the same prepared experiment, fixed terminal task and Bayes baseline. Terminal quantization is only the final cut; earlier cuts can be wider because one retained label must select an entire response function of future reports. This is the principal conceptual advance of R7.

The second major addition is an optimized acquisition theorem. For nonhomogeneous anisotropic binary-cylinder coordinates, one greedy scale sequence simultaneously controls:

1. the smallest possible worst remaining physical scale after a fixed number of reads;
2. the physical product law's finite-center quantization;
3. the acquisition variance under every adaptive policy; and
4. an exact finite-prefix online construction.

Consequently the joint acquisition and label constraint is governed by

```text
R_{min(n,floor(log_2 M))}^2,
```

up to constants depending only on the fixed dimension and lower ratio bound. The theorem includes smooth, singular, mixed and oscillatory product geometries without assuming a limiting dimension.

These results materially answer two examples in the R6 report of advances that could alter the assessment: a necessary obstruction and an adaptive acquisition theorem. They do not, however, produce the broader intrinsic classification contemplated there. The continuation certificate is one-sided and may be trivial when no useful common suffix measure is minorized. The adaptive class has exact progressive coordinates and independent fair unread tails. There is still no theorem saying that all nontrivial continuation widths can be recursively realized, no duality between the lower certificate and the admissibility relation, and no broad converse forcing failure of online/checkpoint matching from absence of a regeneration or order structure.

My assessment is therefore materially more positive than for R6 while retaining the same top-four recommendation.

## 4. Predictive realization and the scored task

### 4.1 Experimental model and interfaces

The paper begins with prepared standard-Borel causal kernels, legal visible-history strategies, explicit public interfaces and charged resource increments. A finite public phase is treated fiberwise, while an unbounded or data-dependent external interface is excluded unless separately conditioned upon. This is the correct way to prevent a clock or phase register from becoming an uncounted information channel.

The statement that `M` counts the entire data-dependent tuple at a cut is essential and is used consistently in the new lower bounds. A deterministic phase may be accounted for separately as `Q`, but it cannot encode observations. Controller or simulator state consulted after the cut must be included in the tuple or charged separately.

### 4.2 Continuous-instrument quotient

Proposition `prop:quotient` improves the previous quotient statement in two useful ways. First, the evaluation space is the closed linear span of determining future tests and interface coordinates, so its fibers are exactly the declared predictive equivalence rather than a gratuitously finer partition. Second, joint action/report measurability is assumed and descended through a Borel section of the compact quotient.

The proof is coherent under its assumptions. Compactness makes the evaluation map perfect; positive-density instrument evaluations descend and can be divided only on the open positive-density domain. A common null-report version remains extra data. The measurable-factor assertion is coordinatewise and does not rely on an unspecified quotient sigma field.

For publication, I recommend replacing or supplementing the nested-cover selector paragraph with a standard measurable-selection reference and a precisely stated lemma. The current construction is plausible, but it is compressed relative to the importance assigned to joint versions.

### 4.3 One terminal task

The task is a bounded finite-dimensional vector with a specified weighted Euclidean norm. Independent probe indices are interpreted as a vector of separately executable conditional means, not simultaneous counterfactual measurements. The checkpoint formula and all continuation bounds use this one task. The Gaussian consequence later removes the need for an auxiliary indexed probe altogether.

## 5. The dominated continuation cut

### 5.1 Statement and interpretation

At a deterministic cut `s`, let `H` be the prefix and `U` the complete physical pre-decision suffix. Their actual joint law is `Lambda`. A certificate consists of a finite prefix measure `underline mu` and a suffix probability `eta` satisfying

```text
underline mu(dh) eta(du) <= Lambda(dh,du).
```

For the same terminal outcome `Y`, the conditional mean

```text
z(h,u)=E[Y | H=h,U=u]
```

is viewed, for each prefix, as an element

```text
Z_s(h)=z(h,.) in L^2(eta).
```

The pushforward of `underline mu` is an actual finite measure `xi_s` on the response-function space. A retained label before the suffix chooses at most one of `M` response functions of `U`, even if the decoder is generously given the complete suffix. Therefore

```text
R_on(T,M)-B_T >= q_M(xi_s).
```

This is a clean and useful formulation. It recognizes that the object crossing a cut is not necessarily the terminal conditional mean itself; it may be a function specifying how every possible future report changes the eventual prediction.

### 5.2 Audit of the proof

The proof handles the relevant quantifiers correctly.

- The exploration is fixed independently of the prediction encoder, so the physical law of `(H,U)` is not changed by conditioning on encoder coins.
- Independent shared and private algorithm coins can be fixed. For each realization, the cut label is `i(H)` and the terminal output is a Borel function `a_i(U)`.
- Giving the decoder all of `U` can only improve it and therefore cannot weaken the lower bound.
- Conditional projection subtracts the same full-history Bayes baseline `B_T`.
- The product domination permits replacement of the actual joint integral by the product integral without assuming physical independence.
- The final infimum is exactly an `M`-center quantization problem in the separable Hilbert space `L^2(eta)`.
- Null-version ambiguity is harmless because the dominated product is absolutely continuous with respect to the actual joint law.

I did not find a flaw in this argument.

### 5.3 Strength of the result

This lower is genuinely stronger than terminal acquired-law quantization. A terminal score may be atomic or have a tiny codebook even when an earlier label must preserve a high-dimensional family of responses to a future query. The delayed-query example makes the distinction exact: after the query index is seen, two outputs suffice, while before it is seen the retained state must encode an entire binary response vector.

The result is also more flexible than exact tag recovery. It permits noisy and continuum-valued response functions and measures squared task distortion rather than only zero-error separation.

### 5.4 Limitation of the certificate

The product minorization is a serious hypothesis. If the suffix law depends strongly on the prefix through support, action availability, a data-dependent interface or nearly singular transition kernels, a common `eta` may have zero or vanishing dominated mass. In that case the theorem is inconclusive, not false. The manuscript states this correctly.

What is missing for a broader foundational claim is a theorem deriving useful dominated cuts from intrinsic experiment conditions—Doeblin minorization, regeneration, conditional density lower bounds, common channels, or another measurable decomposition—and showing that the resulting widths are quantitatively close to the true finite-state obstruction. At present the certificate is an elegant lower-bound template with one exact separation example.

### 5.5 Information-theoretic antecedents

The operational pattern is close to classical source coding with decoder side information and to functional source coding: an encoder observes prefix information, a decoder later receives side information, and the desired reconstruction is a task-dependent function of both. R7 is not simply a restatement of Wyner--Ziv or coding-for-computing results. It is one-shot, finite-alphabet, causal-cut based, uses a Hilbert quantization submeasure and retains the actual experiment/task semantics. Those differences are substantive. Nevertheless, the comparison is too close to omit.

The paper should compare Lemma `lem:cut` at theorem level with:

- Wyner and Ziv's decoder-side-information rate--distortion problem;
- Orlitsky and Roche's coding-for-computing framework;
- one-shot or finite-block functional compression and remote-source reconstruction; and
- communication-complexity/random-access lower methods beyond the single cited entropy inequality.

The comparison should identify exactly which side information is available at the cut, why the hard cardinality `M` differs from asymptotic rate, and what the product minorization buys relative to conditional rate--distortion formulations. Without this discussion, the novelty claim is difficult to calibrate against the nearest information-theoretic language.

## 6. Integration with the block upper

The constructive side of Theorem `thm:transfer` retains the strongest feature of R6. A static finite cover preserves a budget-independent relation; physical-law strict stability is required only across a raw block; every individual update may be merely nonexpansive; and the actual implementation still rounds after every report.

The relation-robust drift quantifies over every adapted admissible perturbation, not only over the eventual code. This avoids circularly assuming the desired recursive error bound. The block shadow is initialized at the retained representative and receives the same physical word, but it is not stored. Telescoping charges every local insertion with all later pathwise gains. Minkowski and the all-pair block inequality yield the displayed recurrence and the uniform deterministic-time radius.

The combined theorem defines

```text
W_M=max{q_M(nu_T), sup_s sup_certificates q_M(xi_s)}
```

and proves

```text
B_T+W_M <= R_on(T,M)
             <= B_T+(L_f E_M+v)^2.
```

This is logically sound: the lower certificate need not be related to the relation used for the upper. The theorem does not claim that the two sides automatically match. The new necessary compatibility corollary is therefore meaningful.

The paper would become substantially stronger if it supplied a nontrivial class in which the continuation widths and the relation-preserving upper are shown to be equivalent in order at every relevant cut. At present the Gaussian theorem uses a terminal acquired-law lower, the progressive sensor uses direct acquisition and physical-law lower bounds, and the strict online/checkpoint separation uses delayed random access. The new continuation theorem is not yet the common quantitative engine of the principal natural realization.

## 7. Delayed-query separation

The example prepares `d` independent fair bits, reveals them before the memory cut, and reveals a uniform query index afterward. The checkpoint encoder after the query needs two labels. Before the query, a label represents an entire response vector.

The entropy proof is standard and correctly used. For retained label `C` and independent seed `R`,

```text
I(X;C | R) <= log_2 M.
```

Conditional subadditivity gives the average posterior bit entropy lower. Concavity of binary entropy translates it to average classification error, and Bernoulli posterior variance is at least half the smaller posterior probability. Exact zero error requires a distinct positive-probability state for every bit string.

The example convincingly demonstrates that terminal atomicity is not an online memory theorem. Its coding mechanism is classical, as the paper acknowledges. For top-four impact, a noisy or dynamical example in which `q_M(xi_s)` is computed sharply and is not reducible to exact random access would be much more persuasive.

## 8. Adaptive acquisition on nonhomogeneous cylinders

### 8.1 Raw experiment

The model has independent fair binary digits in `d` coordinates. Coordinate `j` has arbitrary level-dependent ratios in `[a,1/2]`, and a paid read reveals the next unread digit of a chosen coordinate. An idle is paid. The terminal vector is audited only after prediction. This is a clear progressive multiresolution experiment, and the interface rules rule out rereading or obtaining an entire tail at once.

The actual acquired score law under an adaptive policy is correctly recorded as a finite weighted atomic leaf law. The paper does not assert that prefixes are uniform conditional only on random depths; that assertion would be false for data-dependent stopping.

### 8.2 Greedy minimax allocation

For weighted remaining scales

```text
s_j(k)=sqrt(omega_j) ell_{j,k},
```

the greedy rule removes the largest current entry among the decreasing coordinate lists. Every feasible allocation removes a prefix of each list. Consequently the greedy allocation removes the globally largest `b` entries and attains

```text
R_b=min_{sum k_j<=b} max_j s_j(k_j).
```

The one-step relation `a R_b <= R_{b+1} <= R_b` follows because the successor of the removed maximum is at least an `a` fraction of it. This argument is correct.

### 8.3 Product small balls

At the greedy allocation, each active coordinate's terminal weighted cylinder width is at least `aR_b`. A ball of radius `aR_b/4` meets at most three level cylinders per active coordinate, hence at most `3^d` product cylinders. Each product cylinder has mass `2^{-b}`. Increasing the depth by a fixed dimension-dependent number makes the union of `M` balls carry at most half the physical mass. Iterating the one-step scale inequality returns the lower to `R_floor(log_2 M)`.

The handling of touching cylinders is adequate. Interiors are disjoint, and boundary points have zero fair-product mass. Unsplitted coordinates contribute one interval and no extra factor. The upper from product-cylinder conditional means is immediate. I found the proof valid, though the constants are deliberately coarse.

### 8.4 Adaptive acquisition variance

Conditioned on the complete acquired history and policy coins, every unread digit remains independent and fair. Selecting which coordinate to read is a function of revealed information, and revealing its next independent bit preserves the induction. Thus the residual variance in coordinate `j` at depth `k` is bounded above and below by constants times `ell_{j,k}^2`.

Every realized adaptive allocation has total depth at most `n`; its largest weighted remaining scale is at least `R_n`. Therefore every adaptive policy incurs acquisition error at least `R_n^2/16`. This does not use the false claim that random-depth prefixes remain uniformly distributed after marginalizing the full history.

### 8.5 Matching law

For any procedure, two independent lower mechanisms apply:

1. full-history acquisition uncertainty is at least a constant times `R_n^2`;
2. its final `M` possible vector outputs incur at least `q_M(law(X))`, which is at least a constant times `R_floor(log_2 M)^2`.

The physical law of `X` is policy independent, so the second lower remains valid even when acquisition is optimized. Taking the larger term gives `R_K^2` for `K=min(n,floor(log_2M))`. This avoids exchanging a policy infimum with the policy-dependent decomposition of its actual acquired law.

The greedy upper reads exactly the first `K` scheduled digits, stores the binary word and pads the remaining calls with idles. Its output is the conditional mean. This proves the matching online and checkpoint order.

I found this argument correct.

### 8.6 Significance boundary

The theorem is stronger than a fixed-policy quantization result. It allows anisotropic weights, arbitrary nonhomogeneous level sequences, smooth and singular factors, and no limiting dimension. It proves that adaptivity cannot improve the order over a deterministic greedy allocation.

At the same time, the reason is tightly tied to the product experiment: observed digits do not change the distribution or scale of any unread digit. The action can choose only which independent list entry to remove next. Thus the theorem is an exact adaptive statement, but the adaptive observations carry no information relevant to future allocation beyond already counted depth. This should be emphasized more clearly in the introduction and title-level claims.

The closest classical antecedents are not only quantization on Moran sets. The paper should compare the greedy law with classical bit allocation for independent vector-source components and with successive-refinement theory. Again, R7 is not a direct restatement: it is nonasymptotic, handles level-dependent scales, combines paid acquisition with hard persistent state, and supplies an online exact-prefix machine. But those are precisely the distinctions that need to be made explicitly.

A stronger future theorem would permit observation-dependent posterior scales, correlated coordinates, nonproduct tails, unknown coefficients or actions whose information value depends on previous reports. That would test whether the acquired-geometry framework handles genuinely adaptive experimental design rather than optimal allocation among independent refinement streams.

## 9. Common calibration and erasure defect

The joint theorem adds an inaccessible sign to the geometric task and an early bit channel which either reports the true bit or erases it. The exact-bit target has the same later experiment and scored outcome. The completion interface requires the virtual exact-bit report before the progressive commands and forbids use of the terminal audit.

The risk calculation is correct. Under the uniform bit prior, an erasure leaves posterior squared error `1/4`; after the task weight and erasure probability, it contributes a constant times `epsilon`. Sign averaging contributes the calibration term. The geometric lower is independent of the bit interface. The upper retains one of three bit/erasure states and a slightly shorter greedy word; losing at most two binary depths changes the scale by only a fixed `a`-dependent factor.

The complete-law deficiency is also correctly identified. Replacing an erasure by an independent fair bit gives a wrong virtual exact report with probability `epsilon`, yielding an upper defect `epsilon`. The two source hypotheses at the compulsory completion cut have testing error `epsilon`; any virtual exact report induces such a test, giving the reverse lower. Independent erasure implements the opposite direction exactly.

This is a useful attained example and corrects the common mistake of treating any defect allowance as a universal risk floor. It remains a deliberately designed interface rather than a general multi-resource converse.

## 10. Finite-bit implementation

Theorem `thm:digital` is a welcome addition. It does not infer computability from a finite abstract alphabet. The theorem assumes a certified finite greedy schedule and dyadic coefficient table, then separately bounds:

- `K` persistent acquired data bits;
- numerical precision `p`;
- temporary writable space;
- read-only program and calibration description;
- deterministic padded phase/clock states;
- elementary bit-operation time; and
- physical acquisition calls.

The root numerical error `Gamma=(K+1)2^{-p}` follows from coefficient and output rounding. Conditional projection eliminates the cross term with the residual physical tail. The stored clock multiplier is retained in the total persistent cardinality rather than being silently folded into `M`.

The theorem is explicitly a feasible upper region. It does not give lower bounds for program length, workspace or time, and it does not claim to decide exact comparisons of arbitrary computable reals. This is the correct scope.

For publication, the model of a write-only vector output should be stated once in the resource definitions, so readers do not wonder whether previously emitted coordinates constitute a rereadable free tape. The proof does state that they do not.

## 11. Ordinary categorical filtering loss

The Gaussian section now addresses the strongest task-related criticism of R6. The outcome is the actual categorical hidden state, and the loss is ordinary squared error against a simplex forecast. The Bayes baseline is the full-history filtering error.

### 11.1 Upper

The mass-preserving deficit compander gives an `M^{-1/d}` cover in projective distance, preserves spread inwardness and enforces coordinatewise domination. Primitive support and a positive-probability bounded Gaussian report block yield strict projective contraction only after `m` calls. Relation-preserving perturbations retain enough coordinate mass for that product. Exponential likelihood moments control the complement. This verifies the general block theorem uniformly over the declared report-adapted actions at fixed class parameters.

The posterior readout is Lipschitz from projective distance to Euclidean norm by the softmax derivative. Thus the online excess is `O(M^{-2/d})`.

### 11.2 Lower

The lower is independent of the upper construction. On the actual event that the last `m` reports lie in a fixed cube, the predictive weights before the final report have a uniform coordinate floor. The final action is already fixed before that report. Affine log-likelihood ratios make the report-to-posterior map injective, and the softmax Jacobian determinant is the product of all posterior coordinates. The determinant condition on the mean differences yields a uniformly bounded density for an actual positive posterior submeasure.

The event mass is retained rather than normalized away. Euclidean small balls therefore give the `Omega(M^{-2/d})` checkpoint lower for the same categorical task. I found this argument sound under the fixed dimension, primitive support, missingness, covariance, mean and determinant bounds.

### 11.3 Scope

This theorem is a natural and useful consequence. It is not an optimal-control result, an unknown-model theorem or a parameter-uniform statement near degeneracy. The exploration is fixed independently of the predictor, and controller state remains outside the prediction label unless counted.

The finite numerical paragraph gives explicit clipping growth and a relation-preserving interval compander. It correctly stops short of claiming the full Gaussian bit-complexity region proved for the progressive class.

## 12. Stopping, morphisms and the retained singular companion

### 12.1 Stopping

The stopped proposition no longer substitutes a random time into a deterministic bound. An augmented Lyapunov function has conditional block drift. Predictable indicators select entered blocks, and an optional finite sum controls their occupation. A within-block maximum estimate then handles arbitrary visible stopping times. The task, baseline, case `tau=0`, legal virtual continuation and clock cardinality are explicit.

The result is a bounded-horizon upper with a term proportional to the expected number of entered blocks. It is not a matching arbitrary-stopping law, and the manuscript says so.

### 12.2 Measurable coupling and morphisms

The maximal-coupling construction via refining finite partitions gives jointly measurable densities on standard-Borel spaces. The stopped complete-law comparison includes preparation mismatch and sums physical-law shadow errors only over actual continuing calls.

The morphism theorem charges predictor, simulator, controller and clock states in the serial tuple, adds complete-law total-variation defect after task-metric error, and keeps target and source baselines from being separately recomputed. Composition requires closure of lifted policies and stopping rules. Reverse certificates transfer the continuation lower at an explicitly inflated state budget.

The native article states the operational data needed for the theorem, while referring to the R6 companion for the fully typed transcript construction. For a journal submission, the status of that companion must be unambiguous. If it is part of the submission, it should be submitted and cited as such; if not, the native article should contain a complete formal definition sufficient to verify every use.

### 12.3 Singular realization

The appendix records the exact attachment of the completed nonreset Cantor realization. The full proof remains in the retained companion. The relation is the full same-interface completed candidate domain, deletion and prepend gains are charged, cross-mode transport appears in the weighted matrix, and the actual acquired law remains finite atomic. This is mathematically distinct from both the Gaussian block realization and the progressive acquisition theorem.

I accept the preservation and attachment as part of the reviewed repository packet. A conventional referee should not be required to infer whether an unpublished self-cited companion is a supplement, a second submission or merely repository history. ## 14. Assessment against the controlling targets F1--F4

### F1 — Causal acquired-geometry transfer

**Substantially achieved, with both a sufficient construction and a necessary cut obstruction.**

The raw block theorem constructs a legal finite recursion, and the continuation theorem supplies same-task lower bounds at earlier information cuts. This is a material advance over R6.

The remaining qualification is central: the two certificates are not shown dual, jointly complete or equivalent over a broad class. Product domination may be trivial, and passing every such cut is not shown sufficient.

**Achieved.**

The sparse Gaussian block model, the nonreset singular dynamical model and the progressive acquisition model are structurally distinct. ## 15. Novelty and literature positioning

The paper responsibly credits conditional projection, finite-center quantization, predictive representations, comparison of experiments, random-map contraction, random-access entropy, recursive filter quantization, POMDP belief covering and finite-state controllers. The 2025--2026 POMDP references added in R7 are relevant and their high-level distinctions are stated accurately.

The two new native mechanisms nevertheless require a wider theorem-level literature map.

### 15.1 Continuation response functions

The nearest language includes decoder side information, remote or indirect source coding, functional source coding and one-way communication complexity. The authors should explain:

- why `H` is the encoder-side object and `U` is decoder-side information;
- why the response function `z(H,.)` is the task object being compressed;
- how hard one-shot state cardinality differs from asymptotic coding rate;
- why product minorization yields an `L^2(eta)` lower instead of a conditional rate--distortion expression; and
- which aspects are genuinely causal rather than ordinary side-information coding.

A targeted search did not reveal an exact duplicate of Lemma `lem:cut` in the manuscript's experiment-and-resource language. That is not a priority certificate. The classical neighborhood is sufficiently close that omitting it leaves the novelty assessment incomplete.

### 15.2 Progressive allocation

The nearest language includes bit allocation for independent vector sources and successive refinement. The authors should compare:

- the merged-list rule with classical componentwise distortion allocation;
- exact progressive bit revelation with successively refinable descriptions;
- the paid acquisition budget with an ordinary encoding-rate budget;
- level-dependent Moran scales with stationary high-rate formulas; and
- hard persistent label count with entropy-coded average rate.

The R7 theorem's nonasymptotic arbitrary-scale statement and its joint acquisition/memory lower are meaningful differences. They should be displayed against, not in place of, the classical baseline.

### 15.3 Natural filtering and POMDP work

The new comparison with reachable-belief covering, adaptive discretization, approximate information states, finite windows and finite-state-controller synthesis is useful. The paper correctly distinguishes planning value from one fixed prediction task and reachable-set geometry from an actual-law lower. It should retain those distinctions without implying that prior work failed to address memory at all.

## 16. Major issues before specialist-journal publication

### 16.1 Add the missing decoder-side-information and functional-coding comparison

This is the most important remaining literature issue. The continuation-cut theorem should be compared directly with Wyner--Ziv-type side information, coding for computing and one-shot functional compression. A paragraph of field-level citations is not enough; the paper should compare hypotheses, code model and conclusion.

### 16.2 Add the classical bit-allocation and successive-refinement comparison

The optimized progressive theorem should acknowledge that greedy allocation across independent component scale lists has classical source-coding antecedents. The novelty lies in the arbitrary nonhomogeneous scales, paid sensing, hard persistent state and policy-uniform acquisition lower. State that precisely.

### 16.3 Give a nontrivial noisy application of the continuation width

The delayed-query example is effective but classical and exact. Compute or bound `q_M(xi_s)` in a model with noisy future reports, overlapping response functions or a genuine dynamical suffix. Ideally, show a strict separation from terminal `q_M(nu_T)` in a natural stochastic experiment. This would demonstrate that the new lower is more than a repackaging of random access.

### 16.4 Clarify how often useful product domination exists

Give reusable sufficient conditions for a nonzero certificate: common-density lower bounds, regeneration events, Doeblin blocks, finite common report packets, or other natural hypotheses. Quantify how the dominated mass enters the lower. Also give an example where terminal geometry is simple but the certificate fails, to distinguish absence of evidence from ease of compression.

### 16.5 State the adaptive theorem's structural restriction more prominently

The theorem optimizes all policies in its declared class, but the independent fair unread tails make the value of a read independent of previous bit values. The allocation is adaptive in timing and coordinate choice, yet observations cannot improve the deterministic scale schedule. This is not a flaw; it is the theorem's structural reason. It should be foregrounded so that “adaptive acquisition” is not read as arbitrary sequential experimental design.

### 16.6 Make the native/companion submission self-contained

The reviewed packet contains a complete R6 companion, but the native article cites it for full singular and typed-morphism details. State whether the companion is an appendix, a companion submission or a public preprint with stable bibliographic identity. The core claims should not depend on a repository-only self-citation whose submission status is unclear.

### 16.7 Reframe the title or define the umbrella term

The title may be retained, but then “General Theta” should be defined as a mathematical program rather than an internal project name, and “Foundations” should be reconciled with the fact that the necessary and sufficient certificates are not complete. A narrower title centered on causal finite-state prediction, continuation geometry and adaptive acquisition would set a more accurate expectation.

### 16.8 Keep the resource claim exactly separated

The manuscript is currently careful: the matched profile covers `N`, the complete data-dependent `M`, `delta` and an attained `epsilon` in a specified family; `Q`, program, workspace and time receive feasible upper accounts. Preserve that distinction in every abstract, introduction and conclusion statement.

## 18. What would materially change the top-four assessment

Further polishing, more finite checks or another certificate-designed example would not by itself change the recommendation. A top-four case would require one of the following advances, or something comparable.

1. **A near-characterization of causal compatibility.** Show that a family of continuation widths, perhaps together with an intrinsic regeneration/order condition, is not only necessary but sufficient up to universal or structural constants for online/checkpoint matching.

2. **A duality between the lower and upper certificates.** Derive a relation-preserving cover and block drift from a quantitative continuation geometry, or prove a converse showing that failure of every such intrinsic relation forces a width separation.

3. **A natural noisy application of the continuation theorem.** Resolve a finite-memory law for a recognized nonuniformly stable filter, hidden expanding system, regime-switching model or partially observed process in which the earlier response-function geometry—not merely terminal posterior density—is essential.

4. **Genuinely observation-adaptive acquisition.** Extend the matching acquisition theorem to correlated or nonproduct sources whose posterior scales and optimal next action depend on observed values, or to unknown-kernel learning and calibration under the same raw budget.

5. **A matched multi-resource region.** Prove lower as well as upper bounds for several intrinsic coordinates among persistent state, simulator/controller state, clock, program, scratch, precision and physical/computational time.

6. **A broad theorem producing dominated cuts.** Establish a general measurable regeneration/minorization principle yielding sharp continuation widths for an important class of causal kernels.

Any one of these could concentrate the current architecture into a result of exceptional general-mathematics significance.

## 19. Final evaluation

Revision R7 is the strongest and most mathematically consequential manuscript in the restart sequence. It answers the principal R6 challenge in substance. The paper now has a necessary same-task continuation obstruction, not only a sufficient stability theorem. It also has an optimized acquisition--memory law, a standard categorical filtering consequence, a concrete finite-bit implementation and an attained common-task defect example.

The principal proofs checked are coherent. The continuation cut correctly reduces a causal information cut to finite-center quantization of conditional response functions. The adaptive theorem correctly avoids commuting policy optimization with a policy-dependent acquired law. The Gaussian lower is an actual posterior submeasure argument for the ordinary filtering task. The resource accounting and source pipeline are unusually disciplined.

The manuscript nevertheless remains below the exceptional threshold of the four leading general mathematics journals. The new lower is a one-sided certificate under product domination rather than an intrinsic characterization. The adaptive class is exact but highly separable, so observed values do not change the optimal refinement order. The strongest natural realization remains a regularized known-kernel finite-state filter. The central new lower has not yet been applied sharply in a noisy natural model, and the nearest source-coding and bit-allocation antecedents require a fuller theorem-level comparison.

Accordingly:

**Top-four general-journal recommendation:** reject in the present form.  
**Reason:** the paper now contains substantial and apparently sound new mathematics, but not yet the intrinsic completeness, natural-problem resolution or concentrated breadth expected at that exceptional level. This is not an identified technical-correctness rejection.  
**Specialist-journal outlook:** favorable after a focused major revision addressing decoder-side-information/functional-coding antecedents, classical bit allocation and successive refinement, a nontrivial noisy continuation-cut application, the availability of product domination, and the formal status of the R6 companion.
