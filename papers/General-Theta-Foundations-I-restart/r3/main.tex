\begin{abstract}
A prepared causal experiment generates a predictive quotient and an actual law on it. We prove a resource--resolution transfer theorem by propagating finite error measures against the actual acquired measures at the same time. A one-step recharge inequality yields terminal-component error bounds without inverse rare-component probabilities. A raw refresh-flow condition verifies this inequality for nonstationary recurrent experiments with expanding updates and arbitrarily long exact waits. Combined with multiscale covering and small-ball bounds, it gives matching checkpoint and online finite-state risks on finitely or countably many scale-separated components. A singular realization permits infinitely accumulating components and oscillatory quantization profiles without a limiting dimension. Finite anisotropic and intersecting-stratum results, a stationary positive-kernel route, and nonlinear-filter realizations complement this theorem. Resource-certified causal morphisms retain the full acquisition, state, workspace, program, precision and time ledger. Approximate continuation challenges yield a fused-state information converse, while one scalar probe gives a coupled acquisition--memory--calibration--packet-precision minimax law. Exact label machines and effective finite-bit implementations are distinguished throughout.
\end{abstract}
