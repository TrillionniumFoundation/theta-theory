\begin{abstract}
A finite observer must acquire the information that it compresses and must pay for the decision to stop acquiring it. We study positive causal experiments with a finite affine closure of executable future tests, allowing a fixed unknown model and a single model-independent observer. Under a bound on expected acquisition, we characterize the attainable terminal predictive ensembles by a joint report-routing separation condition. No uniform transience or contraction is required. Excess invariant occupation can be removed without changing the terminal law or increasing any nonnegative resource cost; consequently the optimal hard-state risk is attained. The same result gives a finite-model obstruction to every unattainable robust risk level. For experiments whose current task response is recoverable from the current report, a physically prepared reference law yields a sharper geometric characterization. Its mass-constrained quantization profile gives an all-observer converse and a one-live-state implementation. For independent reference reports both checkpoint and autonomous optima are exact; for uniformly comparable controlled reports the bounds match. On an acquired $s$-regular support the excess risk is of order $(NM)^{-2/s}$, with every live and terminal state charged. Correlated classical kernels and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the same theorem. Singular product measures, changing-rank mixtures and attained calibration and simulation errors are treated explicitly.
\end{abstract}
