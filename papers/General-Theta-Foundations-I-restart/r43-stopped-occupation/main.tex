\begin{abstract}
For known prepared positive causal instruments with a finite separating affine family of executable tests, we prove a matching finite-state risk law in a genuinely executed geometric-stop experiment. Preparation-injected calibration identifies the exact stopped physical--observer ensemble by a positive resolvent, without stationary initialization or an observer mixing assumption. Its regret against all full-history adaptive acquisition strategies is exactly an acquired conditional Jensen gap of a discounted Bellman potential. Actual acquisition mass, global cells and feedback-uniform raw overlap make this gap comparable to $M^{-2/d}$ uniformly over every finite mean acquisition budget. An initial label and internal halting labels are explicitly charged. A hard cap has a separately proved tail term. Controlled Gaussian sensors and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the same theorem from raw kernels; marked acquired-rank changes retain their actual masses. An explicit binary code, attained calibration--deficiency alternatives, and finite-precision and simulator accounting complete the same-task transfer. The theorem optimizes acquisition, not the imposed stopping rule, and does not assume a pre-existing statistical manifold.
\end{abstract}\maketitle
