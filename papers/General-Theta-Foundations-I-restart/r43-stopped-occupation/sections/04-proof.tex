\section{Proof of the central theorem}\label{sec:proof}
\subsection{Controlled Bellman oscillation, not observer mixing}
\begin{lemma}[Uniform stopped Bellman potential]\label{lem:bellman}
The function $V_s$ in \eqref{eq:bellman-def} is concave. Writing $U_s=V_s/s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:span}
 \osc_D U_s\le \frac{b\osc_Dg}{\epsilon},\qquad
 \Lip_K\psi_s\le L_*:=\Lip_Kg+\frac{b\osc_Dg}{\epsilon r}.
\end{equation}
Moreover $\psi_s-g$ is concave. Without overlap one may replace the oscillation bound by $\osc_Dg/s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The map $Tf=\min_aK_a(sg+\beta f)$ is a sup-norm contraction with factor $\beta<1$ (constant when $s=1$). Its iterates from zero converge uniformly to a continuous fixed point. The usual finite backward induction and the bounded remainder $\beta^tL_0$ identify it with the infimum in \eqref{eq:occupation}. A Borel minimizing action selector exists because the action set is finite. Thus this infimum ranges over all full-history adaptive policies, not merely policies selected in advance without feedback.

If $f$ is concave on $D$, its perspective $w f(v/w)$ is concave on the cone over $D$. Since the normalized output of each raw instrument lies in $K$, integration in \eqref{eq:instrument} makes $K_af$ concave on $D$. A finite minimum of concave functions is concave. Uniform iteration proves concavity of $V_s$ and hence of $\psi_s-g=\beta U_s$.

For $\mathcal T_\beta f=\min_aK_a(g+\beta f)$, compare the same $b$-call feedback policy when its terminal function is $f$ or $q$. Running terms cancel. The difference of the optimal values is bounded above and below by $\beta^b$ times expectations of $f-q$ under legal terminal laws. By \eqref{eq:overlap}, every such law is $\epsilon\zeta+(1-\epsilon)\widetilde\mu$. Therefore
\[
 \osc_D(\mathcal T_\beta^bf-\mathcal T_\beta^bq)
 \le\beta^b(1-\epsilon)\osc_D(f-q).
\]
Since $U_s=\mathcal T_\beta^bU_s$ and
$\osc_D(\mathcal T_\beta^b0)\le b\osc_Dg$, rearrangement gives
$\osc_DU_s\le b\osc_Dg/[1-\beta^b(1-\epsilon)]$, which implies \eqref{eq:span}. The crude bound follows by summing the discounted ranges of $g$. For a concave function on $D$, extending a line through $K$ by length $r$ in either direction bounds intermediate slopes by its oscillation divided by $r$. Apply this to $U_s$. No observer transition matrix appears in this proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Acquired mass and nonsmooth curvature}
\begin{lemma}[Common-cell curvature and all-center converse]\label{lem:geometry}
If $\mu\le\Lambda\operatorname{vol}_d|_K$ and $f$ is concave and $L$-Lipschitz on $K$, the bounding-cube grids satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grid-gap}
 0\le\calJ_{\mathcal P_m}(f,\mu)\le C_{K,d}\Lambda Lm^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
Every such $\mu$ also satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smallball}
 q_M(\mu)\ge\tfrac12(2\Lambda v_dM)^{-2/d},
\end{equation}
where $v_d$ is the unit-ball volume. Both estimates survive mixing with the original probability weights.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We recall the acquired-curvature argument to include nonsmooth action switches and boundary cells. The convex function $-f$ has a convex $L$-Lipschitz extension to $\R^d$: take the supremum of supporting affine functions at interior points of $K$ and use continuity on its boundary. Its distributional Laplacian $\kappa$ is a positive measure. For a cube of side $h$ and center $z$, mollify the extension and subtract its tangent plane at $z$, obtaining a nonnegative convex function $w$ with $w(z)=0$. On the surrounding ball, the submean inequality bounds $w$ on the cube by a dimensional constant times its spherical average. Convex radial slopes satisfy $w(z+R\theta)\le R\partial_Rw(z+R\theta)$. Integrate this inequality and apply the divergence theorem. After removing mollification on a slightly enlarged ball it gives
\[
 \int_C(-f)\,d\mu-\mu(C)(-f)(\E[X\mid C])
 \le C_dh^{2-d}\mu(C)\kappa(\overline B_{3\sqrt d h}(z)).
\]
Affine terms cancel at the centroid. In dimension one the sphere is its pair of endpoints and the same inequality follows by the two one-sided derivatives. A smooth cutoff on a fixed neighborhood of $K$ bounds the total curvature there by $C_KL$, through integration by parts. The enlarged balls have bounded overlap and $\mu(C)\le\Lambda h^d$. Summation proves $C\Lambda Lh^2$. Dividing a fixed bounding cube into $\lfloor m^{1/d}\rfloor$ parts per coordinate gives at most $m$ cells and proves \eqref{eq:grid-gap}, including coarse meshes by enlarging the fixed constant. No smooth Hessian or separation of acquired mass from $\partial K$ was assumed.

For the converse, $M$ balls of radius $t$ about arbitrary readouts have mass at most $\Lambda Mv_dt^d$. At $t=(2\Lambda Mv_d)^{-1/d}$ at least half the actual mass lies outside. Randomized assignments cannot improve the pointwise nearest-readout loss. This proves \eqref{eq:smallball} also for off-support centers and repeated readouts.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Preparation-injected calibration}
\begin{lemma}[A fixed table calibrated at the stopping call]\label{lem:calibration}
For every common grid there is a homogeneous table with one initial label and its grid labels, whose terminal ensemble is \eqref{eq:resolvent}. At each positive-mass recurrent label its programmed $q_i$ is the exact conditional mean at the geometric stopping call, and its action row is supported on $\arg\min_aK_aH_s(q_i)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $D_j$ be the compact convex closure of cell $C_j$. Work on the compact convex normalized ensemble base
\[
 \mathcal E_m=\{(z_j):z_j\in C_{D_j},\ \sum_j e(z_j)=1\}.
\]
The initial label is never revisited. Choose any minimizing action $a_0$ in \eqref{eq:bellman-def} at $u_0$ and define the genuinely acquired first-call ensemble
\[
 (B_0)_j=\int\one_{\{F_{a_0}(y,u_0)\in C_j\}}
                   L_{a_0}(y)(1,u_0)\,d\lambda_{a_0}(y).
\]
For $z_i=w_i(1,q_i)$ with $w_i>0$, permit any action probability supported on the finite minimizing set at $q_i$. Send report $y$ to the cell containing $F_a(y,q_i)$. At zero-mass rows choose any representative in $D_i$; its contribution is zero. The correspondence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:correspondence}
 \mathcal F_j(Z)=s(B_0)_j+
 \beta\sum_{i,a}p_{ia}\int\one_{\{F_a(y,q_i)\in C_j\}}
                                      L_a(y)z_i\,d\lambda_a(y).
\end{equation}
Every value lies in $\mathcal E_m$: each positive contribution is the actual likelihood-weighted barycenter of predictions in its output cell. The values are nonempty compact convex, since for a fixed $Z$ the action simplices enter linearly.

Its graph is closed. For a positive limiting row mass, representatives converge; a subsequence of its action probabilities converges to a distribution on limiting minimizers, by continuity of $K_aH_s$. The normalized map is continuous in its incoming coordinates off the common null sets. Its boundary preimage has zero reference mass, because the likelihood is positive and its acquired row charges no grid boundary. Dominated convergence with the integrable operator norm proves convergence of the indicator integrals. At a row whose mass tends to zero, every contribution is bounded by that mass in the cone base norm; no normalization at zero is used. Kakutani's theorem \cite{Kakutani1941} now gives a fixed point $Z$ and witnessing action rows. Fix these rows and choose any within-cell representatives and action rows at zero-mass labels. This defines an ordinary Borel table at every label.

For that frozen table define the linear recurrent map $P$ by the integral part of \eqref{eq:correspondence}, using these fixed representatives and action probabilities on every input ensemble. Positive input ensembles over $K$ remain over $K$; their total mass is preserved. Hence $P$ contracts the sum base norm, and $I-\beta P$ has inverse $\sum_{t\ge0}\beta^tP^t$. The fixed-point equation identifies its unique terminal solution as \eqref{eq:resolvent}.

Starting the actual machine at the initial label gives $B_0$ after one report and $P^{t-1}B_0$ after $t$ reports on its unaudited continuation. Independent stopping therefore gives exactly $s\sum_{t\ge1}\beta^{t-1}P^{t-1}B_0=Z$. Thus the $q_i$ are calibrated at the actual stopping call. They are not asserted to equal $\E[X_t\mid S_t=i]$ for each deterministic $t$. The preparation term identifies the first run even when $P$ has several invariant ensembles or is periodic. For $s=1$ the correspondence is constant and the same argument includes the one-call task.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact stopped regret]\label{lem:regret}
The table of Lemma~\ref{lem:calibration} satisfies \eqref{eq:exact-gap}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The probability $\mu_s$ in \eqref{eq:actual-pooled} is the likelihood-weighted mixture of the initial row with weight $s$ and the calibrated recurrent rows with weights $\beta w_ip_{ia}$. The fixed-point equations say that $\mu_s(C_j)=w_j$ and its cell centroid is $q_j$. The true stopped response risk is $\sum_jw_jg(q_j)$ by the affine audit formula and terminal calibration.

Bellman-minimizing action rows give
\[
 \int H_s\,d\mu_s
     =sV_s(u_0)+\beta\sum_iw_iV_s(q_i),
\]
whereas
\[
 \sum_jw_jH_s(q_j)
     =sR(\gamma_{s,m};u_0)+\beta\sum_jw_jV_s(q_j).
\]
Subtract and divide by $s$. This is precisely \eqref{eq:exact-gap}. In particular, no stationarity replacement or optional-stopping substitution in a fixed-time identity has been made. A single incoming row may have an optimal static quantizer without satisfying \eqref{eq:correspondence}; the common cells and global fixed point are essential.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Matching risk and all-program quantifiers}
For the constructed table, \eqref{eq:density} passes to $\mu_s$. Lemmas~\ref{lem:bellman} and \ref{lem:geometry} applied to \eqref{eq:exact-gap} give $C(M-1)^{-2/d}$. Strong concavity of $g$ and concavity of $\psi_s-g$ also imply that its Jensen gap is at least the actual within-cell squared error, although this alone is not the all-program converse.

For an arbitrary legal program, condition the protected audit on its full pre-audit history, including its private coins. Its risk is
\[
 B_s^\pi+\E\norm{X_\tau-r_{S_\tau}}^2.
\]
The decoder has at most $M$ distinct responses. The independent hazard makes $\nu_s^\pi$ a mixture of actual rows, so \eqref{eq:smallball} bounds the second term below by $cM^{-2/d}$. The first term is at least $V_s(u_0)$ even when the action strategy is time-dependent. This proves the lower bounds. For a checkpoint, conditional nearest-center assignment gives \eqref{eq:cp-exact}; an infimum not attained can be approached by measurable finite-center codes. A global grid after an optimal full-history acquisition selector gives its upper. These arguments prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main} without assuming that the finite program's acquisition strategy coincides with the full-history optimal one.
