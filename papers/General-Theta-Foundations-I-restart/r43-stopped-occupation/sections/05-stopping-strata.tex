\section{Stopping resources, hard caps and acquired strata}\label{sec:stop-strata}
\subsection{The independent stopping device is not free memory}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:internal}]
Take $m=\lfloor(M-1)/2\rfloor$ cells in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The initial label executes its programmed first action. At every acquired report compute the same destination cell as that table, and use an independent fresh Bernoulli coin. On continuation enter that cell's recurrent copy; on termination enter its halt copy and invoke the protected audit. The total is $1+2m\le M$. Each halt copy stores the same readout as its recurrent copy. The stopped physical--label law is unchanged, so the upper follows. Conversely the imposed history-independent hazard preserves the actual row mixture at the stop, and any internally stopped $M$-state program still has at most $M$ terminal readouts. The all-program projection proof gives its lower. The signal to invoke the audit comes from the halt label, not from an external countdown.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A separately proved hard-cap transfer]\label{thm:cap}
Fix $H\ge1$ and replace the external geometric request by $\tau_H=\min(\tau,H)$. The auditor keeps its own cap counter, not visible to the controller before requesting the response. Let $V_{s,H}(u_0)$ be the full-history acquisition optimum for this task. With $M\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap-law}
 cM^{-2/d}\le\calR_{\rm hom}^{\rm cap}(s,H,M;u_0)-V_{s,H}(u_0)
 \le C(M-1)^{-2/d}+2L_0\beta^H.
\end{equation}
The upper uses the same frozen table constructed for the uncapped hazard. The cap uses at most $N=H+2$ physical calls and mean raw acquisitions $(1-\beta^H)/s$. If $\beta^H\le M^{-2/d}$ the upper and lower match uniformly. For $s<1$ it suffices that
$H\ge (2\log M)/(d[-\log(1-s)])$; for $s=1$ the tail is zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any fixed pre-request policy and readout, couple the two tasks with the same raw experiment, actions and coins. They agree on $\{\tau\le H\}$. On its complement each bounded audit loss lies in $[0,L_0]$, so risks differ by at most $L_0\beta^H$. Policies may be extended beyond $H$ arbitrarily, which gives the same bound between the full-history infima. Apply these two bounds to Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. At every possible capped cut the actual predictive law is still a mixture of the stipulated rows; the small-ball argument relative to the policy's own Bayes risk proves the lower without subtracting a tail term. Finally sum the survival probabilities $\sum_{t=1}^H\Pp(\tau\ge t)$. An internally counted cap is a different architecture and would require counting its phase memory; it is not granted by this theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Marked acquired-rank law]\label{thm:strata}
Replace \eqref{eq:density} by a finite visible decomposition. Each report carries a mark $j$ whose actual conditional probability is the fixed $w_j>0$, independent of the incoming state and chosen action. Given this mark, the acquired vector lies in a compact convex affine set $K_j\subset K$ of dimension $d_j\ge1$, with relative density at most $\Lambda_j$ on that span, uniformly on $D$. Separately marked atom states are allowed; at least one component has positive dimension. Keep closure, continuity and the feedback-overlap certificate on the predictive coordinate. Relative grid boundaries are null. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi}
 \Xi(M)=\sum_{j:d_j>0}w_jM^{-2/d_j}.
\end{equation}
For all $M$ above a fixed component-dependent threshold, the checkpoint, homogeneous and internally stopped excess risks are comparable to $\Xi(M)$, uniformly over $s\in(0,1]$ and $u_0\in D$. The capped upper is $C\Xi(M)+2L_0\beta^H$. Constants may depend on the fixed positive weights and components; no optimal allocation as the weights vary with $M$ is claimed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Reserve one recurrent label for each atom and divide the remaining labels in fixed positive proportions among the positive-dimensional marked grids. Use their compact relative cell cones in \eqref{eq:correspondence}. An output carries its actual mark, so each raw contribution belongs to its designated relative cell cone. Both the first-call injection and the recurrent rows retain the original $w_j$. Closed-graph and zero-mass arguments are unchanged, using relative rather than ambient boundary nullity. The same positive resolvent identifies the actual terminal ensemble, even when a mean entering an instrument is off the stratum of an earlier report.

The Bellman function is concave and Lipschitz on ambient $K$, so its restriction to each affine span has the same Lipschitz bound. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:geometry} on each positive-dimensional component and sum its conditional gap with multiplier $w_j$. Atoms have zero cell gap. This proves the upper $C\sum_jw_jm_j^{-2/d_j}\le C\Xi(M)$. For any global $M$-center decoder, orthogonally project its centers onto each affine span. This cannot increase distances. The relative small-ball bound gives $c_jM^{-2/d_j}$ there; multiply by the actual $w_j$ and sum. The stopped row mixture has these same weights for every acquisition policy because the prescribed hazard is independent. This proves all lower bounds, including for overlapping affine spans and off-stratum readouts. Internal halt copies change counts only by a fixed factor. The cap proof applies unchanged. No component is conditioned on and then assigned unit mass.
\end{proof}
