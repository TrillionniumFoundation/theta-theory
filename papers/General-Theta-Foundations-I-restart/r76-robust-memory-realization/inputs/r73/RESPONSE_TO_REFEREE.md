## The mathematical changes in this revision

1. **A size-preserving reversible normal form.** Every finite stochastic realization of a compactly reversible response can be replaced by a permutation action on recurrent labels, with randomness confined to a counted first-input initializer when necessary. The proof starts with the actual reachable row space and identity-word loops, constructs an idempotent by a finite lazy stochastic average, and proves the two-sided sandwich identities before extracting a group action. It does not assume that arbitrary hidden-state responses lie in the minimal observable space. The full convergence lemma is proved in the current supplement.

2. **An exact general memory variational principle and rigidity.** If `N_b` is the number of residuals and `kappa_G(b)` is the minimum size of a finite permutation factorization allowing a mixed initial law, then `m(b)=min{N_b,1+kappa_G(b)}` under the prescribed pure initial-label convention. This separates stochastic size from deterministic residual size. It yields sharp factorial group bounds, a permutation-module lower bound, and the rank equality criterion. A two-command family in every dimension `k>=3` has exact stochastic size `k+1` and deterministic-transition size `k!` for distinct preparation weights. Repeated weights and constant or pure preparations are treated explicitly. An explicit prepared-qubit response on `S_3` has the same group, rank three, natural permutation module, and six residuals as a four-label example, but its bounded-decoder constraints force six labels. Thus the probability factorization cannot be replaced by group, rank, or module data alone. The diagonal realization of the factorial family is essentially classical; that factorial comparison is not presented as a quantum advantage.

3. **An effective exact synthesis theorem on algebraic reversible input.** The finite/infinite observable-group alternative is decidable from an exact real-algebraic representation under the stated probability and compact-reversibility promises; prepared algebraic unitary data satisfy those promises by proof. An explicit finite-group order bound follows from the classical integral-lattice and reduction-modulo-three argument. Finite permutation actions and real-algebraic feasibility then yield a minimum-size stochastic implementation. These classical decision ingredients are credited, and no polynomial running time, unknown-system learning guarantee, or general POMDP decidability is asserted.

4. **Finite certificates and a stable exact-memory theorem.** A target of supplied linear dimension `d` and a candidate with `M` labels are equivalent on all words if they are equivalent through length `d+M-1`. The classical finite-word bound combines with compact stochastic parameter space to give an attained positive separation from all smaller exact machines. For algebraic data this separation is effectively computable. The reversible normal form supplies a common stationary row, so damping toward the group-averaged response has a realization of the same size. Finite separation gives a matching lower in a nonempty explicit neighborhood. For the period-four response, an exact three-state polynomial inequality yields `delta_3>1/36` and an independently checkable four-label interval `89/90<=alpha<=1`. The interval is sufficient; its endpoint is not claimed optimal.

The main article develops these results as one proof chain. Existing causal, geometric, decision-theoretic, reset, rank-constrained, and arithmetic results remain in the paired current article/supplement or their explicitly pinned independent source. Their preservation is not a claim that each follows from the new group theorem. The original general-`M` squared-sign minimax risk frontier, arbitrary controlled boundary optimization, and the full Theta pipeline remain distinct open questions.

### 17.2. Make the Moore/Myhill–Nerode comparison direct

**Layer: mathematical priority and quantifiers.** The minimal deterministic output machine is the classical residual quotient. In the original R65 boundary problem, physical zero slack and wordwise unique optimal actions force a single dictionary-specific decision function; positive-successor selection then removes stochastic advantage. Those steps and their restrictions remain visible. The present probability-law problem allows ties and stochastic transitions, and its minimum can be much smaller than the residual cardinality. The new normal form establishes the stronger and different statement that dynamic randomness can be moved to an initializer without increasing the total label budget. Classical stochastic matrix groups, invariant cones, and probabilistic-automaton minimization are credited at the point of use. The task response is not equated with the full predictive state.

**Disposition:** inherited priority correction retained; the new stochastic/deterministic distinction is proved, including factorial separations.

### 17.3. Classify robust tied actions

**Layer: proof scope and structural generality.** R66/R67 already supplied the anchored stochastic optimal-face criterion on the inherited rigid class, together with a square-loss embedding and counterexamples to unqualified determinization. **Disposition:** the previously established closure on its declared rigid class remains closed. Classification of every controlled or nonrigid tied boundary experiment remains a separate question.

### 17.4. Broaden the heralded model and identify the replacement structure

**Layer: generality and applicability.** The inherited weighted-rigidity and state-dependent instrument results remain intact.
