## The new general theorem

**Theorem 3.1 (`thm:modulus`)**, with **Lemma 3.2**, is the new center. From each actual marked excursion use the retained-boundary matrix P, the joint duration–exit matrix T, mean reward r, and the correct classwise physical-time gain g. The residual is b=r−Tg, not generally r−diag(E tau)g. For every family with finite uniformly bounded mean duration mu_bar and every integer N≥1, set

`k_N(x)=inf_u { ||b_x−(I−P_x)u||_infinity + osc(u)/N }`,

`K_N=sup_x k_N(x)`, and

`Delta_N=sup_x max_{1≤t≤N} ||t(J_t(x)−g_x)||_infinity / N`.

The theorem proves

`Delta_N ≤ K_N+B_N`, and `K_N ≤ (mu_bar+2) Delta_N+B_N`,

where B_N is the explicit actual physical-return remainder. No compactness or uniform group-inverse bound is needed for these inequalities. Uniform integrability of all conditional return rows yields equivalence of K_N→0 and uniform physical-acquisition convergence. Transient, periodic and multichain starts are included.

The exact attained finite dual is

`k_N(x)=max { z^T b_x : ||z||_1≤1, ||(I−P_x)^T z||_1/2≤1/N }`.

A feasible signed vector provides a lower certificate for a physical prefix discrepancy. It is not an occupation probability. The converse uses the exact first-excursion renewal identity for u_N=N^(-1) sum_{t≤N} t(J_t−g); duration and exit are never separated falsely. Analytical correctors may depend on the model; the executing observer program may not.

The prefix maximum and return remainder are necessary qualifications: a periodic two-state example has zero terminal discrepancy at even N but positive k_N; a deterministic four-call excursion has zero boundary residual but nonzero physical prefix error. The displayed lower ranges over entering labels; a fixed-initial-law lower requires an accessibility argument. The actual-occupation and decision-risk lower theorems supply their own fixed-law arguments.

**Corollary 3.3** shows that optimizing the older residual/oscillation price loses at most factor two against K_N. Classical approximation functionals, Poisson equations and linear-programming duality are explicitly credited, including Shaw and Glynn–Infanger. New priority is not claimed for those tools.

## Finite-resolution geometry and causal implementation

**Theorem 7.1 (`thm:resolution`)** applies the physical modulus to the actual scored occupation tested by bounded 1-Lipschitz functions. Suppose a genuine submeasure lambda≤nu_infinity has mass w and lambda(B(z,r))≤A(r) for every relevant center. If M A(r)≤w/2, then

`q_M(nu_N) ≥ (w/2) r^2 − 2 r zeta_N`,

where zeta_N=K_N^K+B_N. Thus zeta_N≤wr/8 gives q_M(nu_N)≥wr^2/4. The proof uses truncated squared distances with Lipschitz constant 2r and retains the actual mass. The occupation accuracy needed is of order r rather than r^2. A raw bin-report example explains why occupation convergence cannot simply be ignored at the quantization scale.

**Corollary 7.2** joins this lower to a counted Q floor(M/Q)-state upper with global candidate-valid covers, report continuity, a common executable initialization, predictive stability and the same finite-horizon full-history baseline. For optimization, lower witnesses must hold for each competing observer's own acquired law. A fixed-controller comparison is not promoted to unrestricted control.

**Theorem 9.4 (`thm:controlled-load`)** verifies finite-N mass without a recurrence restriction: a charged preparation draws a fresh hidden V~rho, a charged load reveals it, and subsequent compulsory service scores a retained readout before the next report. Endogenous service L may depend on V, actions and history; assume E[L|entering history,V=v]≤L_* for every allowed adaptive strategy and every v. With mu_*=2+L_*, the actual law satisfies

`(1/mu_*−1/N)_+ rho ≤ nu_N ≤ L_* rho`.

For N≥2mu_*, the optimal M-state scored risk obeys

`q_M(rho)/(2mu_*) ≤ inf_gamma sup_theta R_N^theta(gamma) ≤ L_* q_M(rho)`.

The lower uses predictable starts early enough to guarantee the first scored call, not an unjustified independence of the final unfinished mark. One held-codebook M-state program attains the upper. Preparation, load, service and audit conventions are explicit; the disclosed protocol is not an internal clock supplied for free.

## Sharp acquisition/memory/calibration/deficiency law

**Theorem 11.1 (`thm:profile`)** permits arbitrary continuous revelation intensity lambda(t) and audit amplitude a(t), both zero at the common endpoint. Its exact n-round all-history minimax diagnostic risk is

`D_n = sup_t a(t)/(2n) sum_{j=0}^{n−1}(1−lambda(t))^j`.

One common two-label stationary program attains it for all n and unknown model values. The general modulus becomes

`Psi_n=sup_t a(t) min{1,1/(n lambda(t))}`, with `Psi_n/4≤D_n≤Psi_n/2`.

**Theorem 11.2 (`thm:profile-joint`)** charges four physical calls per round, N=4n, in a fixed common task. It first proves the exact lower/upper sandwich with q_M(rho) versus q_floor(M/2)(rho), the true invisible calibration term delta^2 and the exact erased-profile D_n. Under stated doubling assumptions this gives

`R(4n,M,delta,e) asymp q_M(rho)+Psi_n+delta^2+A_*(epsilon_*(e))`,

where A_*(u)=sup_{lambda(t)≤u}a(t) and the achieved early-report causal deficiency satisfies e/2≤epsilon_*(e)≤e. The runtime uses at most 2 floor(M/2) labels. It has no access to the unknown model or to the audit before its deadline. An arbitrary permitted tolerance is not turned into a necessary lower bound. Erasure can destroy zero-gain task compatibility; the finite direct proof, not a misapplied zero-gain corrector, establishes the erased law.

**Corollary 11.3** includes algebraic profiles a=t^v, lambda=t^k and a logarithmic profile with Psi_n of order 1/log(e n). Numerical root error, program bits, workspace, evaluation time and simulator-state products remain separately accounted. Full optimal computational-resource tradeoffs are not claimed.
