## 2. Executive assessment of Revision R25

R25 changes the mathematical status of the long-run program. R24 treated physical regeneration together with a compulsory overwrite of the observer. R25 keeps the physical fresh-start condition but retains the observer's label. The label can therefore carry evidence about the fixed inaccessible parameter or control information into the next physical experiment.

For each fixed model and program, the complete conditional cycle law now yields

```text
P(i,j) = probability of exiting the cycle in retained label j,
d_i    = conditional expected physical cycle length,
r_i    = conditional expected cycle reward.
```

This is a finite Markov-renewal kernel, not an iid cycle law. The boundary chain may contain transient labels, periodic classes, and several closed classes. The initial label determines absorption probabilities into those classes.

The correct physical-time gain is

```text
g = sum_C w_C (pi_C r)/(pi_C d),
```

where `w_C` is the absorption probability and `pi_C` is the stationary law of the boundary chain restricted to class `C`. The ratio must be formed separately in each class before the classes are mixed. The manuscript gives a simple counterexample showing that pooling the numerators and denominators first is wrong.

The paper then introduces

```text
h(P) = ||(I-P+Pi_P)^{-1} - Pi_P||_infinity,
```

and proves its role as a cost-universal temporal regularity scale. On compact matrix families, a uniform bound on `h(P)` is equivalent to uniform Cesàro convergence, continuity of the ergodic projection, and locally constant rank of `I-P`. This excludes the degeneration in which a new closed class appears in a limit and the average cost jumps.

The central theorem uses this matrix regularity together with finite-response product domination and a controller-uniform physical-return envelope. Its main conclusions are:

1. robust average-risk attainment on every nonempty bounded-recurrence class;
2. exact determination by finite model subfamilies, with recurrence constraints included;
3. uniform same-program comparison with expected `N`-call risk and normalized discount;
4. moment rates with recurrence and physical-return terms separately visible;
5. finite-family exhaustion of the unrestricted fixed-memory value over increasing recurrence bounds; and
6. a conditional coupling to acquired occupation geometry.

This is a substantial answer to the R24 report. It is not merely a relabeling of the compulsory-reset theorem.

## 4. Audit of the retained-state transfer theorem

### 4.1 Information pattern and quantifiers

The model parameter is fixed for the entire execution and is not an argument of the program. One stationary tuple of action rows, report rows, preparation row, and readouts is chosen before the model supremum. The observer's label survives physical regeneration. No extra seed, history tape, exact posterior, cycle number, or uncharged output channel is supplied.

The physical fresh-start condition is conditional on the entering retained label and fixed parameter. Therefore the conditional complete-cycle law depends only on those quantities, while dependence between reward, duration, and exit label is allowed.

The manuscript maintains this information pattern consistently in the exact theorem, the finite-acquisition bridge, the simulator theorem, the compiler, and the sharp examples.

### 4.2 Finite-response continuity

The program space has finitely many simplex-valued `L^infinity` rows. Under the fixed product references, positivity and row sums are weak-star closed. Action rows and readouts lie in compact finite-dimensional spaces.

For a fixed finite intervention word, every response is an `L^1` density paired with a product of row entries evaluated on distinct report coordinates. The rectangle-density argument proves continuity under weak-star convergence, including repeated appearances of the same row. The proof correctly avoids pointwise multiplication on one report coordinate.

This is the necessary topological input inherited from R22/R24. Marginal domination alone would not suffice, and the manuscript continues to state this boundary explicitly.

### 4.3 Boundary regularity theorem

For every finite stochastic matrix, the eigenvalue one is semisimple, so the Cesàro projection and group inverse exist. The identity

```text
sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(P^k-Pi_P) = (I-P^n)G_P
```

gives the `2h(P)/n` bound.

On a compact family, uniform convergence implies continuity of `Pi_P`; continuity implies locally constant nullity because the trace of the projection is integer-valued. Conversely, local rank constancy permits a fixed contour around one in a relative neighborhood, yielding a continuous spectral projection and continuous group inverse. A finite cover gives a uniform bound.

The closedness of `h(P) <= H` follows by passing the polynomial group-inverse identities to a convergent subsequence and using uniqueness. I find this argument sound. The paper properly credits the underlying finite-dimensional mechanism to the classical group-inverse literature.

The theorem characterizes uniformity for all bounded state rewards and all initial labels. It is not a necessary condition for a single reward that happens to be constant along a degenerating family; the paper states this qualification.

### 4.4 Classwise physical-time gain

The time-changed stochastic matrix

```text
S = I - D^{-1}(I-P)
```

holds at state `i` for a geometric number of unit time steps with mean `d_i` and then performs a `P` transition. It has the same closed classes and absorption probabilities as `P`. Within a closed class, its stationary law is proportional to `pi_C(i)d_i`, which gives the class ratio `(pi_C r)/(pi_C d)`.

Mixing these class ratios with the initial absorption probabilities gives the correct physical-time average. The manuscript's counterexample with two absorbing classes of different durations correctly rules out a pooled ratio.

### 4.5 Root hitting and Poisson bias

The transient block of the group inverse is the fundamental matrix `(I-Q)^{-1}`, so its row sums control the expected time to the recurrent set. In a closed class, selecting a state of stationary mass at least `1/M` and applying the classical mean-hitting-time identity gives the stated `2MH` bound. Combining the transient and recurrent portions yields the root-hitting scale `(2M+1)H`.

Time change multiplies the expected root-hitting boundary count by at most the maximum conditional mean cycle length. The stopped-sum construction then bounds the time-changed group inverse and the oscillation of a recurrent Poisson solution.

The constants are conservative but directionally correct. Periodicity is harmless because the argument uses Cesàro projection and hitting times rather than convergence of `P^n`.

### 4.6 Physical-call bridge

Inside a recurrent class, the increment

```text
R_k - g_C tau_k + u(I_{k+1}) - u(I_k)
```

has conditional mean zero. Stopping at the first cycle that crosses physical call `N` is legitimate because reaching cycle `k` is determined before its innovations. The unfinished part is bounded by the overshoot, and the boundary correction is bounded by the oscillation of `u`.

The overshoot estimate conditions at every possible boundary epoch and uses the uniform conditional return envelope over all entering labels. Positive integer cycle lengths ensure that there is at most one boundary at each physical call. This yields the stated sum of tail envelopes.

From a transient label, the proof first reaches a selected recurrent root, paying its expected physical duration, and then applies the recurrent estimate. This establishes the displayed recurrence-plus-tail finite-`N` comparison without assuming a stationary initialization.

### 4.7 Conditional moment rate and the factor `M`

The manuscript correctly avoids the invalid step of replacing a sum of maxima over entering labels by the moment of one lifetime. It uses

```text
sum_s max_i E_i(tau_i-s)_+
<= sum_i E_i[tau_i min(tau_i,N+1)]
```

and therefore pays a factor `M` under only labelwise moment bounds. The factor can be removed if one common stochastically dominating lifetime is supplied. This correction is mathematically necessary and is one of the strongest signs that the revised proof has been audited seriously.

### 4.8 Robust attainment and finite-model identity

For each model, the recurrence condition and risk sublevel define closed sets in the compact ambient program space. Bounded recurrence makes the time-changed ergodic projection continuous. The worst-model risk is therefore lower semicontinuous and attains its minimum.

The finite-intersection argument on the joint risk-and-recurrence constraints gives the finite-model identity. The right-hand side imposes the recurrence bound only on its finite model set, and the compact-intersection proof correctly recovers a program satisfying all constraints. There is no minimax interchange.

### 4.9 Finite-family unrestricted exhaustion

For a finite model family, every individual finite boundary program has a finite maximum group-inverse norm across the finitely many induced matrices. Hence the union of integer recurrence sublevels equals the entire program space, and the unrestricted infimum is the decreasing infimum of the sublevel values.

This is a value exhaustion, not an attainment theorem. The paper correctly states that unrestricted attainment is equivalent to reaching the unrestricted value at a finite level. It also correctly declines to pass the finite-`N` or discount comparison uniformly through `H -> infinity`.

This limitation is central to the editorial assessment: the unrestricted fixed-memory problem remains only partially compactified.

## 5. Audit of recurrence-sensitive defects and effective certification

### 5.1 Complete conditional-cycle comparison

The compared object includes preparation, actions, visible reports and flags, performed audit marks, duration, reward, and exit retained label, conditional on every entering label. This is the correct object for transferring a retained-state average. Report-marginal proximity alone would be insufficient.

Truncation gives the stated perturbation bounds for `P`, `r`, and `d`. Division by durations bounded below by one controls `c=D^{-1}r` and the time-changed matrix `S`.

The projection perturbation identity

```text
Pi_{S'}-Pi_S = Pi_{S'}(S'-S)G_S + G_{S'}(S'-S)Pi_S
```

is valid for arbitrary finite stochastic endpoints and does not require them to share recurrent classes. Together with group-inverse bounds at both endpoints, it gives the recurrence-sensitive risk modulus.

The theorem correctly requires recurrence and tail control at both endpoints. A simulator's private state changes the joint boundary chain, so an observer recurrence bound alone does not suffice.

### 5.2 Primitive classical and quantum comparison

The classical comparison couples the complete next marked transition, including the next physical state and end flag, until first mismatch. Summing the mismatch hazard over the original endpoint's alive calls gives the conditional cycle defect.

The quantum comparison telescopes unnormalized marked instruments. The replacement at step `t` is weighted by the trace mass of the original alive state, and the subsequent complete instrument is trace-norm contractive. This avoids a fictitious classical coupling of noncommuting states and avoids normalization at zero-probability reports.

These arguments support the intended primitive-to-cycle implication.

### 5.3 Graph recurrence certificate

The support-stable architecture fixes a directed graph, keeps all updates inside reflexive reachability, and assigns a positive floor to each designated ending edge. A first-call end probability gives a uniform lower bound on every graph edge in the boundary matrix. Paths of length at most `M-1` then give a geometric root-hitting bound and hence a group-inverse bound.

The architecture can contain multiple terminal components and periodic classes. It does not impose a common reset label. This is a legitimate nonreset sufficient class.

### 5.4 Cell averaging and rational rounding

Averaging one Borel row under the common within-cell reference produces a single cell table reused everywhere. Because the reconstructed instrument retains the exact joint cell, next physical state, audit, flag, and exit-label law, the identity covers the complete conditional cycle.

Averaging preserves convex support restrictions and positive floors. Rational rounding reserves the required floor mass and rounds only the residual distribution, preserving forbidden zeros and closed-class structure. Thus every Borel program in the declared architecture is covered by a finite table at the same `M`.

This support discipline is essential. The manuscript correctly notes that arbitrary rounding of an element in a spectral recurrence sublevel could create a new interclass edge and destroy the recurrence bound.

### 5.5 Forest evaluation and error directions

For a fixed support graph the number of closed classes is known. The coefficient formula for the ergodic projection follows from the small-`z` resolvent and semisimplicity at zero. Directed forest weights give a positive denominator bounded below by the product of the required edge floors after time change.

Certified finite-history integrations and the physical tail therefore yield rigorous intervals for every finite table. All-Borel coverage supports the lower endpoint; a selected table and the controller-uniform model modulus support the upper endpoint. No local optimizer is used as a global lower certificate.

The procedure is mathematically global but computationally exhaustive. The paper is appropriately cautious about complexity.

## 6. Audit of actual occupation geometry

### 6.1 Class-weighted occupation

For a retained controller, transient boundary occupation has zero long-run weight. In every eventual closed class, the predictive occupation numerator is averaged under the class stationary law and divided by the class physical-time denominator. The classes are then mixed by their absorption probabilities.

This is the correct analogue of the multichain gain for predictive occupation. It is not the preparation law and not a pooled numerator-to-denominator ratio.

### 6.2 Checkpoint identity and online lower

Conditional square projection separates irreducible conditional variance from squared error in predicting the conditional mean. With one fixed codebook of `J` responses, pointwise nearest-center distance lower-bounds every randomized encoding. A full-history checkpoint can attain the infimum by a measurable nearest-center assignment.

The same quantization term is therefore an online lower for every observer with those fixed response values, including observers that retain information across physical regenerations.

### 6.3 Counted `QJ` implementation

The online upper stores the pair `(control label, predictive center)`. The exploration uses only the control coordinate; the candidate predictive update and projection use the center coordinate. The total number of labels is exactly `QJ`.

At preparation only the predictor coordinate is initialized from the known physical prior. The retained control label is not erased. This is materially different from R24's compulsory observer reset.

The matrices `A` and `K` satisfy `P_*=AK` and `KA=P`, and the identities

```text
Pi_{P_*}=A Pi_P K,
G_{P_*}=I-Pi_{P_*}+A G_P K
```

are correct. They give `h(P_*) <= h(P)+2`. Thus the recurrence cost of the product implementation is paid explicitly.

### 6.4 Conditional nature of optimized matching

The general joint-control lower takes an infimum over the policy-dependent baseline and actual quantization law. The upper searches among implementable explorations with enough recurrence margin to absorb the predictor lift.

A matching `M^{-2/d}` rate requires:

- a uniform baseline lower across all admissible explorations;
- a uniform actual small-ball submeasure across those explorations;
- a fixed-size exploration attaining the baseline;
- global covers and candidate-valid update stability for that exploration; and
- recurrence margin for the product implementation.

These are strong additional assumptions. The paper now states them honestly. A fixed-policy example alone does not prove an unrestricted optimal-control rate.

### 6.5 Holding geometry

The direct holding theorem is exact even without observer overwrite. A fresh visible mark replaces the useful prediction at the load, and old information cannot improve the pointwise nearest-codeword lower. The actual occupation is length-biased. The boundary rows of the attaining program are input-independent, giving a small recurrence bound.

This supplies a genuine nondominated preparation family by a direct argument. It does not remove domination from the general adaptive compactness theorem, and the paper does not claim otherwise.

## 8. Sharpness and resource accounting

### 8.1 Boundary recurrence is necessary

The slowly communicating two-state boundary chain has average error of order the group-inverse scale divided by the number of cycles. At the degenerate endpoint the average jumps. This demonstrates that compactness of the unrestricted program space alone does not make average risk continuous.

The one-state rare-long-cycle example shows that the `N^{-p}` and `(1-beta)^p` orders cannot be improved uniformly over a bounded `1+p` moment class. It involves no observer reset and therefore belongs genuinely to the retained interface.

## 9. Novelty and literature position

The manuscript now compares itself with important neighboring POMDP and control results, including finite-memory long-run approximation, strong uniform values, average-cost equations, history-space existence, restart and intermittent-observation models, revealing-POMDP complexity, and classical finite-memory learning.

That comparison is useful but not yet sufficient. The mathematical center of R25 is also directly adjacent to a mature multichain Markov-renewal and generalized-inverse literature. At minimum, the next revision should compare theorem by theorem with:

1. Denardo and Fox's multichain Markov-renewal programs;
2. Federgrün and Schweitzer's fixed-point approach to undiscounted Markov-renewal programs;
3. Lamond and Puterman's generalized-inverse treatment of discrete-time Markov decision processes;
4. classical multichain average-cost policy-evaluation and Poisson-equation theory;
5. finite-horizon approximation of Markov-renewal programs; and
6. modern multichain robust average-reward Bellman/vector-value formulations.

The relevant question is not whether those papers use the present notation. It is which exact part of R25 is absent from their conclusions after one fixes a stationary finite controller and regards its conditional physical excursions as semi-Markov transitions.

My current assessment is that the candidate contribution is the following combination:

- a compact topology for reused Borel report rows under finite-product response domination;
- a robust unknown-model recurrence sublevel defined by cost-universal boundary regularity;
- explicit physical-call and discount comparison at the same retained memory;
- complete-cycle defect amplification through the time-changed ergodic projection;
- all-Borel coverage and finite implementation on a support-stable raw architecture; and
- coupling to actual class-weighted predictive occupation.

This combination may be new and is mathematically coherent. The individual linear-algebraic, renewal, semi-Markov, finite-intersection, and forest ingredients are classical. The paper should make the novelty claim at precisely the level of the combination and should not let the breadth of the title suggest a general average-cost or universal acquired-geometry classification.

## 10. Major revisions required before specialist-journal publication

### 10.1 State the recurrence restriction in every headline formulation

The abstract does mention the group inverse, but the principal theorem's scope should be summarized in one sentence every time the result is advertised: robust uniformity and attainment are on bounded recurrence sublevels, not on the unrestricted controller space.

### 10.2 Add a dedicated semi-Markov/multichain comparison subsection

The current literature section is weighted toward POMDP strategy questions. Add a theorem-level comparison with classical multichain Markov-renewal programs, generalized inverses, average-cost policy evaluation, and semi-Markov approximation.

### 10.3 Isolate the genuinely new theorem from classical consequences

The paper should explicitly separate:

- reused-row response continuity;
- recurrence-sublevel compactness;
- standard finite-state multichain/semi-Markov evaluation; and
- robust finite-intersection and same-memory consequences.

This would make the novelty easier to assess and reduce the risk of overclaiming standard group-inverse facts.

### 10.4 Clarify finite-family exhaustion versus unrestricted solution

The equality

```text
V(M)=inf_k V(M,k)
```

is useful, but it is not an unrestricted compactness or attainment theorem. The introduction and conclusion should emphasize that an optimizer may escape through recurrence degeneration and that the `N`/discount bridge is not uniform after exhaustion.

### 10.5 Give an operational route to a finite recurrence level

For applications, explain when a near-optimal unrestricted controller can be shown to lie in a finite recurrence sublevel. The graph architecture supplies one route, but it is narrower than the spectral theorem. A perturbation margin, unichain architecture, or explicit communicating-floor condition would make the value exhaustion more actionable.

### 10.6 Strengthen the natural application

The queue theorem verifies the hypotheses and gives explicit constants. A stronger application would use the retained label to achieve a nontrivial robust control benefit or prove a memory–performance law that is not already encoded by the abstract theorem's architecture.

### 10.7 Distinguish physical regeneration from arbitrary semi-Markov decomposition

Explain exactly which physical fresh-start assumptions are needed to make the conditional excursion law depend only on the entering retained label. This point is central when the hidden parameter remains fixed and the observer accumulates evidence.

### 10.8 Tighten the statement of the defect theorem

The theorem requires recurrence and tail control at both endpoints and a paired common program class. Put those requirements directly in the theorem's first displayed line or a separate assumptions list. Also clarify when the perturbation modulus is meaningful relative to the recurrence scale.

### 10.9 Separate exact spectral classes from effective graph classes visually

The exact theorem uses arbitrary bounded-`h(P)` classes. The compiler uses a support-stable graph architecture. A diagram or two-column table should make clear that the effective theorem is not a compiler for every exact admissible controller.

### 10.10 Expand the optimized-geometry discussion

The matching rate needs uniform actual mass and a fixed-size attaining exploration. Give at least one raw model in which those optimized hypotheses, not merely fixed-exploration hypotheses, are verified.

### 10.11 State the common-task deficiency as part of the model, not a later qualification

The environment-owned audit wire and early-report deadline are mathematically essential. Include them in the theorem title or opening sentence and compare briefly with unrestricted statistical experiment deficiency.

### 10.12 Reduce editorial dependence on the packet

The 26-page native article is largely self-contained, which is an improvement. Ensure that every theorem used in the native proof has either a native proof or an ordinary published citation. Companion references should remain provenance rather than implicit prerequisites.

## 11. Technical comments

1. In the definition of the physical fresh-start property, state explicitly whether the next physical preparation is conditionally independent of the entering observer label given `theta`, or merely generated by a label-dependent prescribed preparation row. The current text appears to intend a label-independent physical preparation and a label-dependent observer update.

2. The uniform return condition is imposed over every entering label, including labels that may be unreachable under a particular program. This is mathematically convenient and supports compactness, but it is stronger than an on-policy condition. Explain whether unreachable rows can be normalized without changing the class.

3. In Theorem `thm:regularity`, cite an explicit perturbation result or give one more sentence showing why fixed nullity excludes any additional eigenvalue inside the contour around one.

4. The proof that the group-inverse sublevel is closed passes to a convergent subsequence of `G_{P_n}`. State explicitly that finite dimensionality makes the norm-bounded sequence precompact.

5. In Lemma `lem:roots`, distinguish boundary-transition count from physical-call time at the point where the factor `mu_bar` is introduced.

6. The root-hitting formula uses a selected state with stationary mass at least `1/M`. If the closed class has fewer than `M` labels, the bound can be sharpened, though the present bound is valid.

7. The time-changed matrix has diagonal entry `1-(1-P_ii)/d_i`. Note explicitly that stochasticity follows from `d_i >= 1`, including the case `P_ii=0`.

8. In the recurrent stopped-cycle argument, define the cumulative physical-call boundary times before introducing `K`; this would make the filtration and stopping logic easier to follow.

9. The transient-prefix discrepancy is bounded by the physical time to a selected root. Spell out why bounded loss and gain in `[0,1]` give that estimate without an additional factor two.

10. The moment specialization pays `M`. The introduction should avoid displaying the polynomial bound without immediately mentioning this finite-label factor and the separate common-dominating-lifetime improvement.

11. The finite-model identity includes recurrence constraints on the finite subfamilies. This subtle point deserves a sentence immediately after the equation in the theorem statement.

12. The exhaustion theorem is stated for finite model families because a program can have unbounded `sup_theta h(P_theta)`. Give a simple infinite-family example in the counterexample ledger.

13. In the defect theorem, the notation `P` is used both for boundary matrices and probability laws elsewhere. Consider using `K_cycle` or another notation for the complete conditional cycle laws in the proof paragraph.

14. The truncation bound `||P-P'|| <= 2 epsilon` follows from the manuscript's total-variation convention. State the convention again in the theorem or proof because both conventions are common.

15. In the projection perturbation identity, indicate dimensions and multiplication order once; the identity is correct but easy to misread.

16. The graph architecture permits updates along reflexive reachability, which can skip multiple graph edges in one cycle. Explain in one sentence why the terminal strongly connected components remain exactly those of the original graph.

17. The graph recurrence bound is extremely conservative. This is acceptable, but the queue example should state that its direct two-state bound replaces the generic exponential-in-`M` graph bound.

18. In support-preserving rounding, state the feasibility condition `m zeta <= 1` for every required row explicitly.

19. The forest denominator lower uses off-diagonal `S` edges at least `c_0 zeta/mu_bar`. Explain how self-loops and reflexive reachability are treated in the forest count.

20. In the compiler, `L` and `U` are described as arbitrarily close after refining numerical intervals. The total certified interval also includes discretization and model-net errors; avoid any sentence that could be read as saying `U-L` alone is the whole optimality gap.

21. The predictive quotient lemma assumes a standard Borel image and a Borel section. This is honest. Keep it separate from the finite-product response theorem, which does not require such a quotient.

22. In the occupation formula, make explicit whether `nu_i` includes preparation reports with zero score. The surrounding text suggests that only designated scored data calls enter the numerator while all calls enter the duration denominator.

23. The checkpoint identity with general Hilbert-valued targets may require an existence qualification for optimal finite codebooks outside finite-dimensional compact support. The proof already uses equality of infima; reflect this in the theorem statement.

24. The numerical geometry bound is written as a square-root estimate. State the final squared excess bound explicitly near the display to prevent accidental linear interpretation.

25. The `QJ` product implementation assumes the predictor coordinate can be initialized from a known physical prior. For robust unknown-model families, specify when this prior is common or when a common predictive realization supplies a model-independent initialization.

26. In the optimized lower, an arbitrary `M`-state observer has at most `M` task readouts but may use those labels jointly for control and prediction. The pointwise codebook lower is still valid; say so explicitly.

27. The queue's noisy workload report is only an example. Clarify which finite report kernels preserve the action-uniform drift and effective parameter modulus.

28. The queue source gives an unbounded hidden physical state but not a proved full predictive-geometry rate for that workload. The paper correctly labels the attached finite task factor separately; preserve this distinction in the abstract and conclusion.

29. In the quantum realization, `|0>` is a unit vector in the same two-dimensional space as `|1>,|2>`, not a third basis state. The text already says this; a different symbol would nevertheless reduce confusion.

30. The Kraus approximation bound is representation-dependent as an input but yields a representation-independent operational error. State whether the compiler receives the Kraus certificate or only the derived instrument-norm certificate.

31. The endogenous-return gain condition is sufficient and can be restrictive. Give one sentence comparing it with expected logarithmic contraction or random Lipschitz-product conditions.

32. The fresh-bit deficiency lower uses a measurable event involving the environment-owned audit. State explicitly that the audit alphabet and target report alphabet are part of the common measurable space.

33. In the joint resource theorem, the upper uses `3 floor(M/3)` labels and leaves unused labels arbitrary. Make clear that unused rows are chosen to preserve the recurrence class if the theorem is applied inside a bounded-`H` optimization.

34. The bounded-loss normalization `diam(K)^2+5` is correct for arbitrary admissible calibration readouts. Retain this corrected constant in every derived packet and companion reference.

35. The native bibliography should add the closest classical multichain Markov-renewal and generalized-inverse references rather than relying only on the POMDP comparison table.

36. Recent 2026 preprints should remain labeled as preprints and should not bear more novelty weight than their verified statements support.

37. The phrase “intrinsic criterion” in the boundary theorem is reasonable for cost-universal Cesàro regularity, but it should not be extended rhetorically to the full causal geometry problem.

38. The finite checks of group-inverse identities, forest formulas, and queue constants are useful regression tests. Continue to state that they do not certify the continuum or arbitrary-Borel arguments.

39. The evidence branch contains a disclosed failed setup run and a completed repaired run. This is acceptable; keep the distinction clear in future packets.

## 12. Directions that could materially change the top-four assessment

The following would materially strengthen the case for a top-four general mathematics journal:

### 12.1 Unrestricted retained fixed-memory compactness

Prove attainment or a useful uniform value theorem beyond bounded recurrence sublevels, or characterize exactly when the exhaustion over `H` attains its limit. A theorem controlling recurrence degeneration from primitive causal data would be especially significant.

### 12.2 Near-necessary causal regularity

Replace the sufficient product-domination plus return-tail plus group-inverse package by a more intrinsic necessary-and-sufficient criterion for continuity of fixed-memory average risk under raw experiment convergence.

### 12.3 A recognized natural application

Resolve a substantial open problem in average-cost POMDPs, robust queue control, causal coding, intermittent observation, or quantum feedback using the retained-state theorem—not merely verify that the theorem applies.

### 12.4 General nondominated adaptive families

Extend the common-program compactness and risk transfer beyond finite-product domination to an important class of mutually singular path laws.

### 12.5 Recursive geometry classification

Give a near-necessary characterization of when actual occupation quantization can be implemented online at the same state budget, rather than sufficient global covers and stability gains.

### 12.6 Non-exhaustive synthesis complexity

Develop an algorithm with a meaningful complexity guarantee for a nontrivial subclass, or prove a sharp complexity lower/upper classification tied to the present certificate.

### 12.7 A matched multiresource region

Establish lower and upper bounds jointly for persistent labels, program description, workspace, numerical precision, physical acquisitions/time, and simulator state, rather than selected slices.

Any one of these, if carried out at the same level of proof discipline, could substantially alter the editorial assessment.

## 13. Editorial conclusion

R25 is a serious revision. It directly answers the most important criticism of R24 by retaining observer state across physical regeneration. It treats the induced boundary process as a genuine multichain Markov-renewal object, introduces the correct classwise physical-time value, isolates a quantitative recurrence scale, proves same-memory finite-acquisition and discount comparison, and connects the long-run theorem to actual acquired occupation and finite implementation.

I found no elementary defect in the central proofs checked. The paper is mathematically coherent, unusually careful about information patterns, and substantially stronger than its predecessors.

My top-four recommendation nevertheless remains negative because the uniform theorem is confined to bounded recurrence sublevels, the unrestricted finite-model problem is only exhausted rather than compactly solved, the geometric matching is conditional, and the conceptual core lies close to classical multichain/semi-Markov and generalized-inverse theory. The raw examples verify the framework but do not yet demonstrate broad external impact through a recognized hard application.

For an appropriate specialist journal, I would encourage a focused revision centered on the retained-state theorem, with a fuller semi-Markov novelty comparison, a sharper natural application, and reduced editorial dependence on the historical packet. The present report should not be interpreted as identifying a fatal mathematical error or as recommending abandonment of the central program.
