\section{Introduction}
A prepared causal experiment determines which future tests can be predicted from its reports. The predictive quotient records this equivalence of executable tests, but does not by itself determine the geometry that a finite observer can acquire. Waiting for a rare informative report changes both the attainable law and the physical cost. Persistent memory constrains the route to that report as well as its final description. A further difficulty is calibration: a comparison law may describe a physically prepared ideal experiment accurately without being an exact member of the operating model family. A converse that silently treats it as an admitted model is then unjustified.

We establish a two-sided resource--risk transfer under explicit raw experiment certificates. One actual witness bounds the effort of every competing strategy and the accumulated discrepancy of its productive report--audit rows from a prepared comparison law. Separately, one parameter-independent acquisition program approximately balances the report and task measures in every operating model. Our center is Theorem~\ref{thm:reference-stability}. It uses neither an exact comparison model in the uncertainty family nor a comparison execution that stops under the competing program.

The main construction in the converse is an accepted comparison submeasure. Retain the actual distribution of each pre-call history, but replace its current productive report--audit row only. The resulting positive measure has a controlled mass defect and a density cap set by actual expected acquisition count. It need not be an executable terminal law. Its unnormalized task moments nevertheless give an all-strategy risk lower. This separates a valid local comparison from the generally invalid replacement of an entire selected path law.

Write $\Qm_m(b)$ for the marked trimmed-quantization profile derived from the comparison's report law $\nu$, audit mean $f$, and residual variance $w$; its cap $b$ retains actual preparation mass. In the matched-effort, identity-export case the central bounds take the form
\[
 \left[t\Qm_m(b/t)-\mathfrak e_N\right]_+
 \ \le R\ \le
 (1+ba_+)\Qm_m\!\left(\frac b{1+ba_+}\right)+be_+,
 \qquad t=(1-\mathfrak a_N)_+.
\]
Here the lower is read as zero at $t=0$, and the upper has its stated feasibility threshold. The lower defect bounds are actual accumulated charges, not a best-policy occupation estimate. Different effort constants and export multiplicities remain explicitly distinct in the theorem. The formula recovers exact equalities at zero errors and matching geometric orders on a quantitative two-sided calibration regime.

The output width depends on the physical stopping convention. A state-stopped observer has a state-only halt map; its live and terminal labels are disjoint, giving width $M-B$ when $B$ live labels are mandatory and sufficient. An event-stopped observer emits its final commitment during a transition and may reuse live labels as final outputs, giving width $M$. We retain both exact resource laws and their proof, rather than excluding reuse silently. All intercall phases, clocks and continuing random seeds are charged.

Theorem~\ref{thm:metastable} replaces small uniform defects by a drift certificate on actual good/bad mode occupation. The initial bad mass and its residence time appear explicitly; singular and changing-rank report kernels are permitted. Theorems~\ref{thm:classical-calibrated} and \ref{thm:quantum-calibrated} verify the same central theorem from a classical controlled sign channel and a noncommuting positive quantum instrument. Their nominal balanced preparations need not be operating models. A joint calibration--erasure extension keeps all errors in the same terminal squared loss, while finite numerical execution and causal simulator costs are stated separately.

We also retain a different, nonintervening raw application. A current hidden unit vector is observed through Gaussian noise and is not altered by the observer. Theorem~\ref{thm:directional} proves a matching law
\[
 (1+\log b)^{-1/2}+m^{-2/(d-1)}
\]
against every legal stopping rule, together with exact finite-output obstructions and a sharp noise constant. This application is not mislabeled as a post-command balanced task, and its physical posterior is derived directly from the Gaussian kernel.

The variational profile itself has an established ancestry. When $w=0$, writing $a=1/b$ and $g=h/b$ gives $0\le g\le1$, $\int g\,d\nu=a$, and the objective is the normalized distortion of a fractionally retained mass $a$. This is the population trimming formulation underlying trimmed $k$-means and impartial trimming \cite{CGM97}, \cite{GG99}. With $w\ne0$ it is the corresponding marked objective. We do not claim the density cap or low-distortion selection principle as new. Classical reject-option prediction \cite{Chow}, \cite{DHZ}, invariant decision procedures \cite{Luschgy}, and quantum randomization \cite{AMTW} supply related tools. Approximate rejection sampling from a fixed independent batch \cite{BP23}, \cite{SPJ26} has a different budget and output objective: here physical attempts can be history dependent, the criterion is an audited decision loss, and a single finite-state program must work for every admitted model.

Almost-sure expected-cost reachability in partially observed control is studied in \cite{CCGK}, and fixed-memory limit-sure reachability in \cite{ACSS}. Sufficiency results for chattering policies in absorbing decision processes are developed in \cite{DPChatter}. These works concern policy classes and reachability or occupation criteria; the present matching assertions require, in addition, the specified report--audit balance and acquisition certificates.


Accumulation of local error along transient occupation and Lyapunov telescoping are standard stochastic-control techniques. In particular, the occupation-weighted rollout bounds of Hansson and Wahlberg \cite{HW26} address policy-specific Bellman approximation and properness. We do not claim occupation weighting, drift control, or the mere appearance of an expected-time factor as new. Here the comparison submeasure controls both its lost mass and its task moment against a trimmed finite-dictionary optimum, yielding a converse uniform over all admissible observers even when the comparison process is not an admitted or terminating execution. This combines a different quantifier structure, a selected geometric objective and explicit stopping-resource widths.

The result is a sufficient-class theorem, not a classification of all positive kernels. Measurable quotient realization has its separate hypotheses in Appendix~\ref{sec:quotient}; arbitrary projective arrays are not declared physical states. Our proofs distinguish new comparison arguments from inherited exact balance and role counting, and from standard conditional projection, compactness, trimming, Gaussian tails and covering. Ideal Borel existence is not finite algorithmic synthesis. The certificates needed to learn unknown kernels, to price every computation optimally, or to treat unbounded losses and nonterminating protocols are not supplied by the present theorem.
