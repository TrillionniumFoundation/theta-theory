\begin{abstract}
We study optimal prediction in prepared causal experiments subject to paid calls and finite output resources. On a common finitely branching interface, a positive call-cost floor and bounded decision-continuous loss give attainment of the constrained optimum at every feasible budget, including the least one. Reserved finite-prefix problems increase to that optimum even at the boundary. For a fixed decoder dictionary the family of complete performed stopped laws is compact in uniform total variation. A finite-score randomization criterion characterizes its exact budget-constrained deficiency, and finite-label laws are dense at every interior budget. The risk frontier is convex and has an exact resource-price dual. On compact continuous families of finite-dimensional instruments, every strictly feasible prefix optimum has a necessary-and-sufficient certificate with at most $k+1$ pure spectral cost and task witnesses, where $k$ is the affine dimension of the common plan polytope; the bound is sharp. A common backward recursion evaluates these witnesses and characterizes equality of all used rows. Coupled with primitive fallback synthesis and budget restoration, the certificates give arbitrarily accurate finite-label strategies at the original interior budget. Cost and task witnesses cannot generally be forced to use one prior, and optimizing a hard dictionary-width constraint can create a strict duality gap. A delayed-release task has an exact budget curve and a sharp live-state attainment threshold, with a quantitative obstruction below it. The complementary task-order route keeps its lower comparison on actual stopped occupation and uses one common upper program, yielding geometric resource laws under explicit certificates. Width defects, zero-risk spectra, singular and mixed acquired geometry, reusable continuation, reset control and two-detector spectral memory laws are retained. Predictive quotients, ordered measurements, reset communication and nonintervening sensing retain their separate scopes; no local formula or finite certificate completes the unrestricted Foundations programme.
\end{abstract}
