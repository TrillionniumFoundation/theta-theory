\section{Exact reference and balance certificates}
\label{sec:exactconditions}
The hypotheses in this section are additional exact conditions, used only in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and its stated applications. They are not imposed on Theorem~\ref{thm:reference-stability}.
\subsection*{A genuinely acquired reference}
Fix one admissible $\theta_0\in\Theta$ and a known, explicitly certified probability kernel $K_0(dx,dz)$ on $X\times H$. Under $\theta_0$, after \emph{every} legal productive command and every surviving pre-call history, the conditional mark law is $\nu(dx)=K_0(dx,H)$. Conditional on the full current report, the audit has the same first and second moments as $K_0(dz\mid x)$. Known within-call command coins or auxiliary reports supply no further audit information under this witness. Use the derived moments and profile of \eqref{eq:derived}--\eqref{eq:profile} for this actual reference law.

Assume that every legal observer satisfies, under this \emph{same} witness,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effort}
 \E_{\theta_0} C_\tau\ge\kappa_-\E_{\theta_0} A_\tau,
 \qquad A_\tau=\text{number of productive calls through commitment},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_->0$ and $C_\tau$ is its actual accumulated core cost. This is an experiment-level all-strategy inequality. It may follow from a predictable compensated count or a bounded potential, and will be proved directly in the realizations. Since $A_\tau\ge1$, $N<\kappa_-$ is infeasible under the witness.

\subsection*{One common upper program}
Fix a parameter-independent acquisition program $\mathcal G$ using $B$ live states. From its start state it obtains one productive report, almost surely, at conditional expected cost at most $\kappa_+$ on \emph{every} surviving history and every $\theta$. After rejecting that report it returns to the same program start. This is a reset of the observer's routing label, not an assumed physical reset of hidden dynamics.

Within the productive call, $\mathcal G$ is allowed a fresh finite choice $j\in J$, a Borel report map to a virtual mark $x$, and a Borel export map $T_j:H\to H$. No information about $\theta$ is used. For every pre-attempt history $h$, every $\theta$, every Borel $D\subset X$ and every $r\in H$, require
\begin{align}
 \Pp_\theta(x\in D\mid h,\mathcal G)&=\nu(D),\label{eq:mass-balance}\\
 \E_\theta[\one_{\{x\in D\}}\|Z-T_jr\|^2\mid h,\mathcal G]
   &=\int_D d_r(x)\nu(dx).\label{eq:loss-balance}
\end{align}
The physical reference mark used in the lower and the virtual mark used in the upper may be different report functions; they take values in the same declared space and their compared law is the same actually prepared $\nu$.

The averaging in these identities includes the fresh choice $j$, gate history inside the attempt, and all hidden states, but the rule later applied to the virtual mark may not retain the averaging coin except through the exported terminal value. The identities are verified from the raw kernels \emph{before} selecting $m,N$ or a dictionary. They neither require equality of the full hidden law nor assert equal conditional variances after conditioning on $j$.

There is a declared export multiplicity $D_0\in\mathbb N$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:export}
 \left|\{T_jr:r\in C,\ j\in J\}\right|\le D_0|C|
 \quad\text{for every finite }C\subset H.
\end{equation}
The important exact case is $T_jr=r$, $D_0=1$. Finite isometry groups give $D_0\le|J|$, but are not required by the theorem. All routing needed to perform these operations between calls is already included in $B$. A random pulse performed in a separate call needs its own retained control state and cost.

The loss identity is an identity of unnormalized measures, not of posterior variances after averaging a normalized filter. It is equivalent to the corresponding order-zero, first and squared-norm moment identities when $T_j$ is an isometry, as made precise in Proposition~\ref{prop:moment}. It allows arbitrary report dependence and zero residual variance on individual actual histories.

The lower and upper effort constants need not agree. Exact risk identities below require the additional match $\kappa_- =\kappa_+=\kappa$. In the raw heralding examples this match is proved by a predictable gate count. In applications without a match, the two distinct rescaled budgets remain in the conclusion.
