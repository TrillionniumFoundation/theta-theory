## Substantive contribution of this revision

The new `sections/28-spectral-memory.tex` addresses the unresolved quantitative exact-response memory problem. It proves that the least pure-start stochastic realization size of a balanced sinusoidal qubit probability is exactly the least dimension of a stochastic matrix having its damped phase as an eigenvalue. Sufficiency is proved with a normalized maximal-modulus eigenvector and a legal decoder, without an extra random initial label. The fixed squared-task embedding makes this the actual minimum-cost optimal-observer memory, not an abstract signed-rank lower bound.

The paper then proves an exact worst-phase size, exact first-sector thresholds and controller rigidity, and matching cubic-root growth at every fixed phase satisfying a global badly-approximable bound. In symbols, the worst varying phase has size `max(3,ceil(pi/acos(alpha)))`, while each certified fixed arithmetic phase has size `Theta_c((1-alpha)^(-1/3))`. The two bounds in the latter theorem are both proved for exact responses at the same original budget. The main text gives an elementary quadratic phase satisfying the condition and does not assume it for the different algebraic rotation `(3+4i)/5`. For that older rotation an explicit logarithmic exclusion improves the former log/log-log lower, but is not advertised as matched.

The spectral regions, classical sector inequality and Type-0 lazy-cycle rigidity are prior mathematics, now cited directly. They are proved in the form needed rather than hidden in a new abstract hypothesis. The new quantitative claims are the response-level consequences and fixed-phase arithmetic comparison. We do not claim that this solves the separate general-M squared-sign risk frontier from R65, nor the phase-type timing problem, nor an externally recognized open problem merely by naming one. Mathematical significance and earliest priority remain for independent assessment.

## Priority, preservation and remaining questions

The main text distinguishes the earlier quantum-process memory bound of Fanizza–Lumbreras–Winter from the exact balanced-response equivalence and fixed-phase arithmetic theorem. The primary Type-0 result of Kirkland–Šmigoc already classifies the lazy-cycle boundary matrices, so R69 credits it rather than advertising the rigidity step as newly discovered. The needed sector inequality and rational approximation are also proved fully, avoiding a hidden load-bearing external reference.

Every inherited current mathematical source remains either unchanged in the main/supplement or byte-preserved under the complete predecessor tree. In particular the absolute/relative actual-history transfer, zero-risk atomic correction, compact/primal-dual theory, rank and spectral geometry, nonintervening sensing and independent ordered-measurement/reset-communication/discrimination lines are not deleted or inferred from the new scalar example. The stronger logarithmic fixed-angle lower supersedes only the sharpness level of the older bound; the old inequality remains valid and is not silently removed.

Open questions include the matched scalar exact-memory rate for the original algebraic phase, arbitrary multi-frequency responses, the general-M squared-sign risk curve, multi-command boundary optimal-memory classification, the phase-type timing frontier, common unknown-model predictive realization, intrinsic geometric exactness and matched program/sampling/precision/workspace/time resources. These distinctions retain the original research aim without awarding the paper its own top-four significance judgment.

The executable verification separates exact finite algebra, floating probes, native source compilation, source identity and remote publication. No proof-assistant certification, newly commissioned human referee, exhaustive priority certification or editorial acceptance is claimed.

The finite-spectral-mixture theorem additionally treats a fixed finite collection of nonzero phase modes on a higher-dimensional protected carrier. Its lower retains every spectral obstruction and the full 2r+1 Hankel rank; its upper counts the initial mixture label and any constant tail block. It yields the same matched cubic-root exponent for certified fixed phase lists, not a universal theorem for arbitrary multi-frequency positive realizations.
