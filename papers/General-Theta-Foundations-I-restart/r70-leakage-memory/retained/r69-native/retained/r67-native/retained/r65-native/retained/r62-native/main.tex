\begin{abstract}
We study finite-resource prediction in prepared causal experiments. A task-order transfer theorem keeps the converse on actual pre-call occupation and uses one common implementation: fixed relative order preserves zero tasks and all resolution scales, whereas additive task error can grow linearly with acquired sample count. For finite time-homogeneous classical and quantum instruments we additionally derive a common proper fallback from the absence of invariant dark subspaces. Finite semidefinite problems then give an explicit two-sided approximation to optimal bounded task risk at the same expected-cost budget, with an $O(T^{-1})$ gap above a certified fallback cost. A preliminary cost hierarchy converges from both sides to the least feasible budget and supplies such a finite fallback at every strictly interior feasible budget. The constructed observers have counted finite memory and fixed output dictionaries; no reset, balancing group or supplied reference model is used in this finite-model synthesis. Compact decision nets preserve the output-width constraint. We retain exact width-dependent defects, the atomic zero-risk spectrum, finite-obstruction and reachable-support synthesis, report-resolved continuation criteria with sharp physical depth, reset-instrument control and the complete two-detector spectral memory frontier. A six-regime selected-mass diagram makes the mixed-dimensional geometric law explicit. Predictive quotients, singular task geometry, post-command realizations and nonintervening sensing keep their respective scopes. The primitive synthesis is not a claim that every experiment has the comparison profile, and neither finite memory existence nor algebraic decidability supplies optimal program, workspace or time complexity.
\end{abstract}
