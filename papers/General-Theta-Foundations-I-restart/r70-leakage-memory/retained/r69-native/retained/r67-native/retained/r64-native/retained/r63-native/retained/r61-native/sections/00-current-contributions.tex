Two further structural results address the gap between a synthesized routine and a reusable acquisition procedure. Theorem~\ref{thm:return-span} characterizes exact marked task identities on every reachable rejected-return history by annihilation of one finite report-resolved operator span. The span stabilizes after at most $d^2-r_0$ enlargements. It is not obtained by averaging the returned channels and is not the full matrix algebra on a reachable Hilbert support. Corollary~\ref{cor:reusable-balance} compiles this identity into the paid rejection construction without observing prospective audits or granting free reset operations. This is a finite exact verification theorem on its declared time-homogeneous interface, not a general efficient synthesis theorem.

Theorem~\ref{thm:reset-control} derives properness and optimal control from the primitive failure branches of any finite reset instrument, allowing mixed reset states and state-dependent expected call costs. It supplies a Bellman continuation, an attained spectral optimization over initial command rows, a least-favorable physical preparation, and a rigidity identity for every finite-cost competing policy. Its rank-one specialization permits any finite number of detectors in any finite dimension. The complete arbitrary-rank two-detector result remains a distinct exact spectral application. These conclusions require no balancing group; nevertheless first detection is an intervening task, not estimation of an untouched unknown state.

The boundary theory is equally sensitive to quantifiers. Theorem~\ref{thm:zero-general} determines zero risk for every compact finite-width loss family. For squared prediction, Theorem~\ref{thm:zero-spectrum} identifies the threshold with the sum of the largest $m$ atomic masses of the zero-residual predictive measure. A selected single zero-loss atom gives only a sufficient threshold when other zero-loss locations are present. The exact spectrum covers all such atoms and excludes nonatomic zero-residual mass at finite width. The fixed-dictionary selection dual is proved with ties and boundary mass retained; its trimming ancestry is explicitly credited.

Theorem~\ref{thm:borel-return} extends this criterion to standard Borel return instruments and proves a finite measurable coarsening with the same exact operator span. Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-return-depth} realizes the matching depth $d^2-1$ with a strictly completely positive channel, a full-rank initial preparation and a bounded two-outcome audit. These are exact dimension-dependent statements about repeated task balance. The Hermitian-span method and its distinction from Hilbert supports are already explicit in prior quantum verification work \cite{Fu24}; the present assertion is not priority for that underlying method.
