\begin{abstract}
We study the relation between bounded-risk optimality and finite classical memory in prepared causal experiments. A positive open-call cost reserve makes the budget-feasible family of complete performed stopped laws compact in uniform total variation at a fixed decoder dictionary. This gives boundary attainment, an exact finite-score comparison, and a resource-price dual; compact finite-prefix problems have sparse physical cost and risk witnesses with a common Bellman equality certificate. We determine the additional structure needed for finite-memory attainment at the least budget in prepared heralded channel experiments. A minimum-cost rigidity identity forces first-herald commitment. Under unique decisions for an attained least-favorable measure, exact finite stochastic memory is equivalent to a finite residual decision quotient, and its index is the exact live-label count. Ties admit a separate single-model criterion and can preserve a genuine need for robust randomization. An algebraic rotating qubit, also with a compact uncertain-polarization family, has an attained full-history optimum but a strictly larger optimum at every finite classical-memory bound. Finite-label policies nevertheless approximate it exponentially at the same boundary budget. A contraction with a strict limiting decision gap gives exact finite memory; depolarizing qubits show why contraction alone does not. The noisy qubit also has an exact two-live-label optimum for every rotation angle and a sharp onset of memory benefit. For unary rational channels a direct synthesis procedure has polynomial bit complexity at a bounded herald rate. Exact delayed-release timing laws and the actual-history geometric transfer theorem are retained as complementary consequences, with their full technical developments in the current supplement. The results distinguish complete probability laws from occupation measures, dual preparations from primal policy mixtures, and fixed dictionaries from nonconvex hard-width optimization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and continuing mathematical problems}\label{sec:r65-scope}
The closed performed-law family and its decision tests concern a common finite-branching interface, a positive conditional call-cost floor, bounded terminal loss and a fixed actual dictionary. They do not imply uniform expected cost-tail integrability or finite classical-memory attainment at the least budget. The heralded-channel results classify that latter question within their prepared-channel interface and prove both positive finite-memory realizations and a quantum obstruction. The prefix algorithm has its own unary rational-data assumptions. No result here turns arbitrary quantum cutpoint acceptance into exact deterministic prediction.

The geometric route remains independent: a commandwise lower witness on actual histories and one common paid upper construction yield the comparison profile, not conversely for every primitive experiment. Its width, rank, singular-range, regular, mixed and atomic consequences are proved in the current supplement. The separately retained nonintervening-sensing, ordered-measurement, reset-communication and rank-constrained-discrimination lines are not consequences inferred from the heralded example. The predictive quotient still has its stated fixed-law scope.

Open questions include an intrinsic general criterion for geometric exactness, common unknown-model predictive realizations, boundary finite-memory density beyond the class treated here, the sharp intermediate-memory phase-type timing frontier, and matching program, precision, workspace and time lower bounds. The present decision quotient supplies one experiment-level exact-memory invariant, not a universal completion of those questions.
