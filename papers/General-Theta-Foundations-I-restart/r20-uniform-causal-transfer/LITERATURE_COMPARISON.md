# Literature comparison and attribution

## Closest comparisons checked in primary sources
Junges et al., UAI 2018 / PMLR R16:518–528: randomized finite-state-controller synthesis via parametric Markov chains. Andriushchenko et al., PMLR 180 (2022), 85–95: oracle-guided inductive FSC synthesis. Amato–Bonet–Zilberstein, AAAI 2010: Mealy/Moore controller conventions. R20 does not claim bounded-controller existence/decidability, common finite rows, or timing conventions as inventions.

Cubuktepe et al., AAAI 35 (2021), 11792–11800, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v35i13.17401: robust FSCs for uncertain finite POMDPs, a semi-infinite nonconvex formulation, dualization and locally optimized synthesis. The paper's model already protects transition support. Primary journal PDF pages 1–2 were read and visually inspected. Thus robustness and support preservation themselves are inherited concepts, not novelty claims. R20's asserted combination is global same-M approximation relative to all Borel controllers on continuous-report positive instruments, together with the exact common-parameter/common-phase dual and finite-model compactness consequence. No superior algorithmic scalability is claimed.

Hudak et al., arXiv:2602.08734v2, revised 1 April 2026 (AAMAS 2026): controller extraction/evaluation from neural policies, including robust controllers over finite hidden-model sets. The consulted version supports that objective; it is not dismissed as merely empirical and it is not claimed to lack controller guarantees. The new theorem instead provides a global fixed-budget value bracket under supplied raw moduli, for potentially uncountable model families.

Leskelä–Vihola, Bernoulli 23 (2017), 2784–2807, DOI 10.3150/16-BEJ827: conditional convex order and measurable martingale kernels. The one-step barycenter argument is classical; the declared additional restriction is actual causal acquisition plus a single reused programme across parameters and phases. Gan et al., AAAI 36 (2022), 5025–5033 and Zhong, Econometrica 90 (2022), 1537–1582 supply strategic/sequential information-design comparisons with different signal and objective constraints.

## Preserved comparisons, with current metadata spot checks
Continuous-observation approximation arXiv:2410.02895v2, refined finite-window bounds arXiv:2409.04351v1, Kim arXiv:2601.03132v1, and Zhu–Lu PMLR 300 (2026), 1423–1431 were checked against primary abstract/proceedings pages. Their version-specific scopes remain intact: filter-stable history policies, fixed-policy performance comparison or offline coverage are not automatically a whole-controller cardinality frontier. Full mathematical comparisons of every referenced article were not repeated in this turn. Existing exact allocation, curvature/mass, blind-transport and compiler arguments are attributed to the predecessor manuscripts and standard tools, not recounted as R20 novelty.

## Narrow claim to be refereed
The new joint maximum couples all parameter and phase constraints before eliminating one row. Finite-dimensional integrated moments give compact common-programme optimization and finite-model global obstructions without changing a fixed model mid-path. Common report reconstruction and a programme-uniform raw modulus then turn a complete finite search into a two-sided certificate for the whole Borel class at the original memory budget. Standard weak-star separation, compact finite-intersection arguments, positive contraction, rounding and enumeration are identified as tools rather than independent discoveries.

This is a positioning audit, not proof that the precise combination has no precedent. Independent priority and importance assessment remains necessary. Formal citations are in references.tex; all entries being cited is a source check, not bibliographic completeness.
