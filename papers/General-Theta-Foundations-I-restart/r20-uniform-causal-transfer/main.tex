\begin{abstract}
A prepared causal experiment determines predictive information through executable future tests, but a finite observer can retain only laws generated by its own acquisitions and one executable programme. We give an exact allocation criterion for a single parameter-independent programme on a family of compact predictive experiments. Its affine dual couples all occupied parameters and phases before selecting a next label. On jointly dominated finite-dimensional positive instruments, a robust optimum is attained and every strict global risk obstruction is already present in finitely many models. With uniformly effective support and continuity certificates, the same finite-label optimum admits computably convergent upper and lower bounds. The construction preserves legal actions, exact stopping and programme reuse; it does not require contraction of normalized filters or interchange the programme infimum with a parameter supremum. Untruncated Gaussian models with nonmixing transitions and noncommuting quantum instruments with unknown channels verify the same theorem. Singular acquired laws, atomic tails and rank changes are included. A calibration experiment has an exact minimax memory threshold. Matching geometric resource curves and common-task simulation are retained with separate accounts for labels, programme description, workspace, precision, acquisitions and time. The effective search is finite but need not be efficient.
\end{abstract}
