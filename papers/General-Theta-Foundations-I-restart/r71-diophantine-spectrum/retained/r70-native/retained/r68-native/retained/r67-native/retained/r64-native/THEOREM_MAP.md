## Logical dependencies and scope

The inherited actual-history comparison and common-program upper remain the geometric route; they do not depend on the new resource dual. The primitive fallback and compact-model cost hierarchy supply a strict common fallback on every interior feasible budget. The new boundary theorem uses the common-plan product and open-call reserves, not uniform expected cost-tail integrability. Its bounded terminal tails yield performed-law uniform-TV compactness at a fixed actual dictionary. Finite-score separation characterizes the budget-feasible performed-law family; each tested policy still satisfies all model budgets. Common completion and counted mixing then give same-budget finite-label law density.

Fixed-dictionary convexity gives the exact resource-price dual. Compact continuous model data and strict prefix feasibility give active gradients and the finite spectral witness. Conic reduction gives at most k+1 total cost/risk atoms; the common backward recursion and its slack identity certify the finite optimum. Multiple resources require one jointly strict fallback, with one coercive call-cost coordinate. Optimized dictionary width is not convexified. The delayed-release application uses the full stopping law and a positive-cycle argument, not command incompatibility; only its unrestricted and one-label curves and attaining memory threshold are claimed exact.

The original fixed-law predictive quotient, nonintervening sensing, ordered measurement, reset communication, rank/discrimination and A1/A2 lines remain separately scoped. No new theorem silently supplies their missing uniform, implementation or complexity assumptions.
