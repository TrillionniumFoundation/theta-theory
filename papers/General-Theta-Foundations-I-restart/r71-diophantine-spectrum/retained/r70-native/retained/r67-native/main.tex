\begin{abstract}
We refine exact finite classical memory on the least-cost face of prepared causal experiments. For one-command instruments with state- and model-dependent reports, a normalized residual-budget criterion is necessary and sufficient for every boundary policy to commit at the first herald. It strictly extends cost-active support coverage: a word may be absent from every maximizing-cost model and still admit no rejection. On this larger rigid class, the robust optimal-face and positive stochastic-realization theorems remain applicable without unique Bayes actions. We realize a prescribed protected-channel probability response as the unique robust optimum of one common squared-loss audit, rather than a pair of model-dependent calibration losses. A finite rational set of physical models already forces each prescribed exact memory dimension, with an explicit positive risk gap for every smaller stochastic observer. The lower bound combines a nonzero finite response determinant with finitely many physical generating-function measurements; it is not an asymptotic sharpness claim. For compact semialgebraic instrument families, the optimal risk at every prescribed finite memory is an attained computable algebraic number, without a supplied optimal response or target value. The unrestricted value has certified finite-prefix approximations, so a strict finite-memory gap has an eventual finite certificate. The inherited damped-qubit realization phases, unique-decision prediction laws, complete performed-law theory, geometric transfer, spectral and communication developments retain their separate hypotheses and full proofs. Fixed dictionaries, stochastic realization dimension, protected physical dimension, and computational resources are not identified.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and continuing mathematical questions}\label{sec:r67-scope}
The boundary criterion concerns one globally legal command, finitely many reports, compact continuous model uncertainty, a fixed finite decoder dictionary, and primitive uniform transience. It permits non-scalar herald effects, noncommuting channels and model-dependent word laws. It does not classify boundary feedback with several commands. Where the normalized unused-budget condition fails, the theorem supplies an actual rejecting policy but does not replace its remaining control problem by a decision quotient.

Robust ties are classified by a finite positive stochastic realization of an optimal kernel, not by arbitrary selection from wordwise Bayes ties. The invariant-polytope criterion is complete within the stated rigid class, but is not a general algorithm. Exact risk at each prescribed algebraic memory bound is computable; deciding whether some finite memory attains the unrestricted optimum is a different question. The rational exact-memory hierarchy and rank-three counterexample are physically realized squared-prediction constructions, not claims to settle a separately recognized open quantum-control problem. The sharp general-$M$ noisy-qubit risk frontier and the intermediate phase-type timing frontier remain open here.

The geometric transfer still requires its own actual-history converse and common paid construction. Its full finite-width, rank, singular-range, regular, mixed and atomic consequences remain in the current supplement, together with nonintervening sensing ancestry and the fixed-law predictive quotient. The independent ordered-measurement, two-stage reset communication and rank-constrained discrimination sources remain preserved at their immutable identities; they are not inferred from the new protected-channel embedding. General unknown-model predictive realization and matching optimal program, precision, workspace and time bounds remain independent research targets.
