\begin{abstract}
A finite causal observer must implement its predictions and its acquisition policy through the same retained state. We prove a stationary acquired-geometry transfer theorem for prepared positive instruments with a finite executable predictive realization. Feedback-uniform overlap yields an average-cost Bellman bias; an invariant unnormalized-ensemble correspondence then constructs one globally calibrated, time-homogeneous finite-state controller, including zero-mass labels and tied optimal actions. An independently charged label erasure, distinct from a physical reset, gives convergence from every admissible preparation under a separate averaged-instrument minorization. Actual acquired density and Bellman curvature yield matching excess risk of order $M^{-2/d}$ against the full-history average predictive-control optimum, with quantitative finite-acquisition entrance bounds and an all-Borel-controller converse. Active Gaussian hidden-state observations and noncommuting quantum instruments verify the same theorem. Visible affine strata give a weighted singular profile. We prove stationary perturbation and finite-precision certificates, preserving the state budget and separating program, workspace, clock and acquisition resources. Protected random audit times are specified operationally; no internally clocked fixed-deadline result is extended by implication.
\end{abstract}\maketitle
