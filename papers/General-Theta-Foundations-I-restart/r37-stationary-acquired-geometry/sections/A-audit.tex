\section{Three compatibility checks}\label{sec:compatibility}
\begin{example}[A tie must remain a correspondence]\label{ex:tie}
If two actions have equal $K_ah(q)$ at a state $q$, perturbations of $q$ can select opposite strict minimizers. A rule choosing the first minimizer is measurable but need not be continuous. The invariant-ensemble map with such a rule need not have a closed graph. Lemma~\ref{lem:fixedpoint} instead allows the entire minimizing simplex; linearity in action probabilities preserves convex values. This is a genuine fixed-point hypothesis check, not a claim that every selected-action map has no fixed point.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Calibration is not attraction]\label{ex:attraction}
A Markov map with two absorbing components has many invariant ensembles. Preparing one of them makes its conditional labels calibrated, but another initial component need never approach it. Therefore a calibrated invariant law alone cannot establish an all-initial-law resource theorem. The separate minorization \eqref{eq:ensemble-minor} supplies the missing uniqueness and convergence. This elementary example does not satisfy that minorization and is used only to separate the logical statements.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[An external clock is not an internal state]\label{ex:clock}
A homogeneous finite observer with equivalent report supports cannot generally reuse the same phase label and also guarantee halting at two different prescribed exact future dates. A possible path from its later occurrence to halting would also be possible from an earlier occurrence. The protected task here instead hides a sampled external request and asks the observer only to read its current label when that request arrives. Its counter belongs to the auditor and is explicitly charged. The stationary theorem cannot be substituted into a total-state exact-deadline formula by treating this counter as free.
\end{example}

These checks complement, rather than remove, the retained local centroid incompatibility, acquired-law versus reachable-set and delayed-information witnesses. None is used as a reason to abandon the general theorem; each identifies the additional hypothesis or accounting step used in its proof.
