\section{Two raw controlled instruments}\label{sec:realizations}
The next verifications derive predictive coordinates from executable audits. They do not use a realization to prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Some local acquired-density calculations were established for finite controlled horizons in \cite{GTFControlled}; we supply them again and prove the additional averaged-cone minorization required for stationary realization.

\begin{theorem}[Active Gaussian hidden-state observations]\label{thm:gaussian}
Let the physical state have $q\ge2$ values. Fix an invertible column-stochastic matrix $P$ with $P_{ij}\ge a_0>0$. An acquisition action $a$ chooses one of finitely many Gaussian sensors. Conditional on pre-transition state $i$, its report has distribution $N(m_i^a,\Sigma_a)$ on $\R^{q-1}$, with $\Sigma_a$ positive definite and the $(q-1)\times(q-1)$ likelihood-difference matrix of full rank. After the report the physical state transitions by $P$. The audit reveals the current state, and the task is categorical squared prediction loss. For every fixed such experiment, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} holds with $d=q-1$. In particular it compares one homogeneous finite controller with all full-history adaptive sensor-selection strategies, not merely with a prescribed passive filter.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $u$ be the conditional probability vector of the physical state. A categorical audit is executable and separates $u$. Every legal future experiment has expectation linear in $u$, so equality of $u$ is exactly future-test equality. Mixture preparations give the full simplex; the coordinate image is Borel, and preparation of each probability vector gives a Borel section. On its affine hull the instrument is
\[
 L_a(y)u=P\diag(f_1^a(y),\ldots,f_q^a(y))u,
\]
where $f_i^a$ are Gaussian densities. This formula uses Lebesgue measure; dividing all densities by their positive finite mixture gives the reference probability required above, without changing the instrument. The likelihood is $\sum_i u_if_i^a(y)$ and the normalized update is $P$ times the usual Bayes vector. Thus affine test closure and integrable positive maps are obtained directly from the physical experiment. The audit Brier risk is $g(u)=1-\norm u^2$ in the simplex hyperplane, including its actual sampling noise.

Set $K=P\Delta_{q-1}$ and $D=\{u\in\Delta_{q-1}:u_i\ge a_0/2\}$. Invertibility makes $K$ full-dimensional in the hyperplane. It is compact with a positive buffer inside $D$, and every report sends $D$ into $K$. Likelihoods are positive. Report total variation is at most a fixed multiple of $\norm{u-v}$ by linearity; dominated integration gives weak continuity.

Before $P$, use log odds $z_i=\log(w_i/w_q)$ of the Bayes posterior $w$. They equal
\[
 z_i=\log(u_i/u_q)+(m_i^a-m_q^a)^T\Sigma_a^{-1}y
       -\tfrac12\bigl[(m_i^a)^T\Sigma_a^{-1}m_i^a
                        -(m_q^a)^T\Sigma_a^{-1}m_q^a\bigr].
\]
Under the actual mixture report law this is a finite mixture of nonsingular Gaussian distributions. Their means range over a compact set for $u\in D$, and their covariance matrices are fixed for each of finitely many sensors. Hence the log-odds density is at most $C\exp(-c\norm z^2)$, uniformly in $u,a$. The inverse Jacobian of the logistic chart is $(\prod_iw_i)^{-1}$, up to the fixed affine-volume normalization, and is at most $C\exp(C\norm z)$. Gaussian decay therefore bounds the posterior density throughout the simplex, including its approach to every face. The invertible affine map $P$ supplies a uniform actual row density on $K$. No tail has been discarded or conditioned away.

On a compact full-dimensional interior simplex $W_*\subset\Delta_{q-1}$ the same Gaussian mixture densities have a uniform positive lower bound. After $P$ this gives $\mu_{a,u}\ge\epsilon_*\zeta$ for the normalized volume law on $PW_*$, uniformly in $u,a$. Thus acquired overlap holds with $b=1$, and it holds for randomized actions as well. Ordinary hyperplane grids supply the global covers and zero boundary mass.

Finally $\bar L_a=P$, independently of the sensor. Let $v_*=P(q^{-1}\one)\in K$. Since $0<(v_*)_i\le1$ and $P_{ij}\ge a_0$, for every positive vector $z$,
\[
 Pz\ge a_0\,\Bigl(\sum_i z_i\Bigr)v_*.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:minorization} with $\eta_*=a_0$ and $z_*=v_*$. We deliberately choose a minorizing state in $P\Delta$; a uniform vector need not lie there for an arbitrary positive $P$. All hypotheses are verified without assuming contraction of the optimal sensor selector or a one-step Wasserstein contraction of the filter.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A fixed binary experiment]\label{ex:binary}
Take
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4\\1/4&3/4\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Y\mid I=i\sim N((-1)^i,\sigma_a^2),
\]
with any fixed finite nonempty set of positive variances. The Bayes output coordinate is an affine image of a logistic Gaussian, and $K=[1/4,3/4]$, $D=[1/8,7/8]$ in the scalar probability chart. Thus the single stationary observer has excess order $M^{-2}$ and the constructed entrance bound has order $M^2/n$. Here the raw minorization can be improved to $\eta_*=1/2$ with the uniform state. Scalar Euclidean distance and the categorical task norm differ by a fixed factor $\sqrt2$. The experiment is nontrivial even with one sensor; adding sensors enlarges the full policy class. We do not infer that the average-cost optimal sensor switches from a last-step switching example. The actual finite-horizon switching examples and their assumptions remain in the complete controlled companion.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[Noncommuting controlled quantum instruments]\label{thm:quantum}
Let physical dimension be $D_q\ge2$, $0<\beta<1$, $0<\lambda<1$, and let $U_a$ range over a finite nonempty family of unitary matrices. Let $H$ have normalized Lebesgue measure on the Hilbert--Schmidt unit ball in the traceless Hermitian matrices. The effect, instrument and post-report channel are
\[
 E_H=I+\beta H,\quad K_H=E_H^{1/2},\qquad
 L_a(H)\sigma=\lambda U_aK_H\sigma K_HU_a^*
       +(1-\lambda)\tr(E_H\sigma)I/D_q.
\]
The action is selected before $H$. Use a fixed informationally complete finite POVM as the protected audit with categorical squared loss. Then Theorem~\ref{thm:main} holds with $d=D_q^2-1$, in the induced affine probability chart, against all full-history adaptive choices of $a$. The observer is classical and has no free quantum memory.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $\int H\,d\lambda(H)=0$, the effects integrate to $I$. They are positive definite and bounded above and below. The displayed maps are completely positive and integrate to a trace-preserving map. The report density is $\ell(H,\sigma)=1+\beta\tr(H\sigma)$, genuinely dependent on the incoming state and bounded between $1-\beta$ and $1+\beta$. A fixed informationally complete POVM separates the density matrix, and all future instrument/test expectations are linear in it. This derives the predictive quotient and affine closure; it does not assume a preassigned statistical manifold. The injective affine audit-probability chart carries the actual Brier norm and its affine second moment. All auxiliary Hilbert--Schmidt estimates below transfer through that fixed chart.

Write $\tau=I/D_q$. The physical state base is the compact density body. Take
\[
 K=\{\sigma\ge(1-\lambda)\tau:\tr\sigma=1\},\quad
 D=\{\sigma\ge\tfrac12(1-\lambda)\tau:\tr\sigma=1\}.
\]
Every normalized update is in $K$, which has a positive buffer in $D$. The report continuity and integrable operator domination follow from compactness and linearity.

We verify full-dimensional actual density. For fixed interior $\rho$, the map from a positive trace-$D_q$ effect $E$ to
$\sigma=\sqrt E\rho\sqrt E/\tr(E\rho)$ is a smooth diffeomorphism onto the positive trace-one body. Its inverse is
\[
 B_{\rho,\sigma}=\rho^{-1/2}(\rho^{1/2}\sigma\rho^{1/2})^{1/2}\rho^{-1/2},
 \qquad E=\frac{D_q B_{\rho,\sigma}^{\,2}}{\tr B_{\rho,\sigma}^{\,2}}.
\]
Indeed the positive solution to $B\rho B=\sigma$ is unique; normalization then proves both inverse identities. On $\rho\in D$ and $\norm H_{\HS}\le1$, the effect and the pre-channel posterior have positive uniform spectral floors. The inverse derivative is bounded on this compact set, so the forward Jacobian is bounded away from zero. The final affine map $\sigma\mapsto\lambda U_a\sigma U_a^*+(1-\lambda)\tau$ is invertible on the trace-one hyperplane. With the bounded actual report density this proves a common upper density bound. Effects with different $H$ need not commute.

For acquired overlap, at $\rho=\tau$ the normalized update is
$\tau+(\lambda\beta/D_q)U_aHU_a^*$. Thus the image of a strictly interior effect ball contains a common neighborhood $B_*$ of $\tau$ for all actions. The inverse formula and compactness yield a radius $r_*>0$ and a common positive density on $B_*$ whenever $\norm{\rho-\tau}_{\HS}\le r_*$. For arbitrary incoming $\rho\in D$, reports with $\norm H_{\HS}\le s$ satisfy
\[
 \norm{F_a(H,\rho)-\tau}_{\HS}
 \le\lambda\norm{\rho-\tau}_{\HS}+C\lambda\beta s.
\]
To verify this estimate, use $\norm{\sqrt{I+\beta H}-I}_{\rm op}\le C\beta s$, the lower bound $\tr(E_H\rho)\ge1-\beta s$, and expand numerator and denominator. Choose $s>0$ and an integer $b$ so that $C\lambda\beta s/(1-\lambda)<r_*/2$ and $\lambda^{b-1}\sup_D\norm{\rho-\tau}_{\HS}<r_*/2$. Each quiet report has actual conditional probability at least $(1-\beta)s^d$, whatever the past actions and reports. After $b-1$ such reports, the final row has the common lower density on $B_*$. Multiplying these actual conditional probabilities proves \eqref{eq:overlap} uniformly over every feedback policy. All $b$ calls, including the quiet ones, are charged; no conditioning creates a free preparation.

For the different raw condition, integration gives
\[
 \bar L_a z=\lambda U_a\left(\int K_H zK_H\,d\lambda(H)\right)U_a^*
              +(1-\lambda)\tr(z)\tau
 \ge (1-\lambda)\tr(z)\tau.
\]
Thus $\eta_*=1-\lambda$, $z_*=\tau\in K$. Global affine grids and zero boundary mass complete the verification. The proof is finite-dimensional; no closure statement for unbounded operators is used.
\end{proof}
