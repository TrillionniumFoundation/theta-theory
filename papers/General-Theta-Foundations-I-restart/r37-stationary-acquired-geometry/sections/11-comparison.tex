\section{Comparison, inherited mechanisms and scope}\label{sec:comparison}
The near-optimality of finite-state controllers for average-cost POMDPs is already an established subject. Yu and Bertsekas \cite{YuBertsekas2008} prove initial-distribution-independent $\epsilon$-optimal finite controllers for finite state, observation and control spaces when the optimal lower average cost is constant. That existence principle is not a novelty claim of the present paper. Demirci, Kara and Y\"uksel \cite{DKY2024} derive average-cost optimality and finite-memory/quantized approximations from nonlinear-filter contraction conditions. Our common acquired-overlap condition gives a different sufficient route to a bounded concave bias; it is not a necessary condition for average-cost control.

Saldi, Y\"uksel and Linder \cite{SYL2016} establish finite approximations to Borel-state MDPs, including quantitative and order-optimal results. Our claim is not that finite approximations or rate-optimal control discretization originate here. The missing object addressed here is a homogeneous finite observer of raw reports, with no supplied exact belief at runtime. Its globally calibrated unnormalized ensemble solves all incoming rows and minimizing actions at once. That construction, the acquired-curvature rate, and the all-Borel hard-label converse are the particular combination proved above. Sequential vector quantization \cite{BMT2001}, value-directed belief compression \cite{PB2002}, and Bayes-risk quantization \cite{Varshney2011} are substantive antecedents, not merely contextual citations.

Recent finite-memory work also requires careful comparison of task quantifiers. Kim \cite{Kim2026} explicitly studies a fixed-policy replacement of belief-level costs along the same closed-loop execution. Here $J_*(n)$ and $\rho$ optimize the complete legal acquisition policy, while the constructed controller chooses actions from its own finite labels. Conversely, our finite affine closure, positive actual-row mass and raw minorization are stronger structural restrictions than a general policy-conditional approximation statement. Neither result subsumes the other by a change of terminology.

The predictive realization, likelihood-weighted barycenter identity, acquired-mass curvature estimate and finite-horizon overlap method are inherited and explicitly reproved where used \cite{GTFBayes,GTFControlled}. Kakutani's fixed-point theorem, positive-cone contraction, Bellman span contraction and conditional projection are standard mathematical tools. The new result combines them to close the stationary calibration and all-initial-preparation problem, charges a genuine erasure label, derives an implementable precision region, and verifies two different raw instruments plus a weighted changing-rank extension. It does not infer a universal stationary resource law from finite physical dimension alone.

Several limits are material. The theorem does not characterize all causally compressible experiments. Necessity of acquired overlap, matching optimal transient costs below the entrance region, unknown-kernel learning, arbitrary nondominated or infinite-dimensional realizations, and a complete joint optimum for program, workspace, acquisition time and simulator state are not proved. The protected audit uses an external stopping request; an arbitrary unbounded stopping time or internally generated exact deadline is not covered by that argument. Our stationary perturbation controls bounded task scores, not an infinite-path likelihood ratio or a large-deviation principle. The preserved finite-deadline and singular-boundary results remain valid only under their own stated interfaces and hypotheses.
