\begin{abstract}
We study the least marked feedback simulation error at a prescribed
persistent-state and randomization budget. The finite theory distinguishes
private, hidden-selector, and visible-selector deficiencies. Hidden
convexification has a testing dual with a priced finite realization;
it need not preserve the private zero set. For every fixed private or
finite-selector budget we give instead a complete system of local testing
certificates and executable upper witnesses. Their error bound depends on
the depth and row alphabets, not the number of histories, and they retain
the original state budget. The resulting spectra compose and transport
endogenously controlled common-encoder decisions. A regenerative theorem
treats nonsummable average risks with singular physical transitions.
For active hard-sphere dynamics we combine graph-core approximation with
finite Gram residual bounds to control the actual marked experiment before
consumer optimization. This gives explicit a posteriori risk bounds at
unchanged consumer widths, including a finite-tail average-risk bound.
\end{abstract}
