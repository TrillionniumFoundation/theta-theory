\begin{abstract}
We study finite-use discrimination of ordered quantum measurements
in a fixed quadratic tangent neighborhood.  For a prescribed
allocation of complete probe--reference groups of sizes $n_r$, put
$N=\sum_r n_r$ and $V=\sum_r n_r^2$.  The optimal trace separation
has orders $\min\{1,\sqrt N s\}$ on the covariance range,
$\min\{1,\sqrt V s\}$ on coherent tangents, and $\min\{1,Ns\}$
on first-order support openings.  The same orders hold with
adaptive control inside groups and classical feedback between them,
provided every fresh complete acquisition is conditionally independent
of all old receiver memory.  Receiver quantum storage is unrestricted
but cannot be coherently imported into a later acquisition.
For history-dependent allocations with hard path budgets
$\sum n_r\le N$ and $\sum n_r^2\le Q$, $N\le Q\le N^2$,
the coherent order is $\min\{1,\sqrt Q s\}$.
Constants and the scale interval depend on the fixed base, nonzero
tangent and remainder allowance, but are uniform in all allocations,
budgets and legal quadratic remainders.  A common unequal-signal
readout supplies the lower without knowing that remainder;
conditional fidelity supplies the feedback upper.  Both retain the
complete finite channel error.  We also give a certified-reset
perturbation bound and a strict tester-level comparison with
measure-and-reprepare classical adaptation.  The complete support
kernel, global covariance upper, local orbit and curve laws, and
balanced-interior description and learning results remain part of
the theory.  Local orders do not assert equality of exact distances,
and reset budgets do not measure quantum-memory dimension.
\end{abstract}
