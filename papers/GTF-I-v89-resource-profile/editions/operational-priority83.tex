\section{Tomography comparisons and resource distinctions}
\label{sec:priority83}

The learner above uses established tomography as a statistical
primitive.  Its contribution is the future-use loss, the coherent
converse under that loss, and the simultaneous description guarantee,
not the introduction of finite-outcome measurement tomography.

\subsection{One-use guarantees and balanced legalization}
Write $\epsilon$ for one-use estimation accuracy and $\eta$ for failure.
Mele--Bittel \cite[Theorem~III.3]{MB67} learn a channel with input
size $d$, output size $k$, and Kraus rank at most $r$ using
$O(dkr\epsilon^{-2})$ calls at constant confidence, in halved diamond
distance.  Their stated high-confidence theorem includes an additional
$O(d\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\eta))$ term and a lower admissible failure
threshold; arbitrary smaller failure can instead be obtained by
independent repetition and metric-majority selection.  For a
measurement channel one may take $r\le dk$.  Dephasing the estimated
output gives a legal POVM $B$.  If the halved diamond error is at most
$\epsilon$, then $r(E,B)\le\epsilon$: on every input the outcome
differences sum to zero, so the magnitude of any one difference is at
most their total variation.

The estimator need not belong to the balanced interior.  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:affinerepair83}, rather than silently using the interior
metric outside its domain, gives
\[
 r(E,L_\epsilon(B))\le4\epsilon,\qquad
 D_N(E,L_\epsilon(B))\le8k\sqrt N\epsilon.
\]
Consequently $\epsilon=\delta/(8k\sqrt N)$ supplies a sufficient
constant-confidence future-loss budget
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mbdirect83}
 O(d^2k^4N\delta^{-2}).
\end{equation}
This is a parameter substitution, not a lower bound or a claim that
this analysis is optimal for that estimator.  The component reduction
used in Theorem~\ref{thm:multioutcomelearning82} instead invokes
Mele--Bittel's binary operator-norm primitive
\cite[Corollary~III.9]{MB67}, together with the confidence argument in
Lemma~\ref{lem:binaryconfidenceupper80}.  It gives the stated $k^3$
upper factor and its joint confidence term.

For comparison, let $d_{\rm op}$ be the worst-input outcome total
variation without a retained reference.  Zambrano--Ramos-Calderer--Kueng
\cite[Theorem~2]{ZRK76} give a legal projected least-squares estimate
with $d_{\rm op}(E,B)\le\epsilon$ and failure at most $\eta$ provided
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zrkexact83}
 m\ge\frac{8[d^2+\epsilon(d^2+1)/6]}{\epsilon^2}
       \log\frac{2^{k+1}9^{2d}}{\eta}.
\end{equation}
Their protocol uses nonadaptive single-copy probes from a global
state $2$-design and classical reconstruction.  The subset-event
formula for $d_{\rm op}$ implies $r(E,B)\le d_{\rm op}(E,B)$.
The same affine repair and the same choice of $\epsilon$ therefore
supply the sufficient bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zrkfuture83}
 O\!\left(k^2N\delta^{-2}d^2[d+k+\log(1/\eta)]\right)
\end{equation}
for the balanced-interior future-use problem.  These probes differ
from independent Choi acquisitions followed by collective processing.
Neither the source's lower-bound class nor its one-use loss is
identified with the coherent adaptive future-use converse proved here.

\subsection{Geometry and the scope of the new principle}
Horizontal purification and cancellation of cross-slot terms have
antecedents in channel geometry and channel-extension metrology
\cite{FI76,DKG67,KGAD76,YF76,SD76}.  Orthogonal projection, quadratic
energy minimization, and inverse-order comparison are also standard
methods.  What is established in Section~\ref{sec:covariance83} is
the particular normalized effect-tuple covariance, its exact binary
restriction, and its global regularized adaptive bound with an explicit
rank-stable midpoint formula.  The proof writes out these identities
rather than attributing them to a generic Fisher-information argument.

In particular, the covariance includes inter-outcome terms through
$S_E(K)$.  Its zero operator characterizes projection-valued
measurements, while uniform outcome noise supplies a positive floor.
The finite-angle crossover in Theorem~\ref{thm:noisecrossover83}
exhibits the change from linear to square-root accumulation.  The
classical-output, input-consuming interface is essential: a device
returning a residual quantum system is not covered by these theorems.

\subsection{Four distinct computational resources}
Unknown-device calls and the future-use horizon are different
quantities.  The common learner bounds the former on every record;
the latter appears only in the loss.  A public code charges the
fixed-length transmitted index, not a target-dependent dictionary.
All dictionary parameters and reconstruction rules are public and
fixed independently of the unknown device.

The covariance certificate and guarded affine repair have polynomial
bit complexity on Gaussian-rational input.  This does not make the
full public dictionary or an arbitrary collective learning readout
polynomial-time.  The finite-risk theorem concerns a fixed ideal
readout and a complete parameter net; the actual-control learning
theorem separately budgets errors of the implemented trusted controls.
An additional certified implementation-law error must be added when
transferring an ideal certificate.  A finite scalar replay illustrates
that theorem and checks the software contract; it is not execution of
a general matrix learner.

\subsection{Further questions}
The closed-body upper bound suggests a coupled boundary classification.
A matching nonadaptive lower certificate and the corresponding full
multi-outcome volume and entropy laws remain to be determined.  The
fixed-$k$ learning law also leaves a genuine growing-outcome problem:
its binary lower reduction does not match the explicit $k^3$ upper
factor.  For the complete binary body, the displayed $d^4$ learning
upper is not a growing-dimensional minimax classification.  Finally,
polynomial-time public dictionary construction and general readout
synthesis are distinct from the polynomial evaluator and legalizer
proved here.  These questions do not alter the ranges of the results
already established.
