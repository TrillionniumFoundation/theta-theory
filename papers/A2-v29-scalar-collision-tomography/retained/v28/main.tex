\begin{abstract}
We study recovery of periodic dispersing billiards from controlled
independent launches and localized first-impact positions in one fixed
laboratory aperture. A bound on some lattice presentation suffices to
determine the entire obstacle union and its full translation group,
even when obstacle shapes repeat, possess symmetries, and the presentation
is nonprimitive. A finite central-patch test distinguishes genuine periods
from translations which merely match one pair of bodies. No individual
shape separation, return network or free-area normalization is required
for exact determination. On $C^{6,\beta}$ classes with explicit geometric
bounds and a positive nonperiod patch-mismatch margin, the same experiment
recovers primitive multiplicity, exact rational period relations and a
$C^2$ finite presentation to error $O(\nu)$ using
$O(\nu^{-3/4}\log(C/(\nu\delta)))$ attempted launches with confidence
$1-\delta$ and calibrated position error $O(\nu^{3/2})$. The aperture and
horizon are fixed. Localization precision and deterministic computation
are separate resources; passive trajectory data are not claimed.
We retain the translation-separated fixed-aperture theorem, intrinsic
return-law inverse, completion and sequential certificates, and the
complete earlier moment and relative-law theory.
\end{abstract}
