\section{Physical indistinguishability in local count designs}
\label{sec:v7-lower}
The pairwise cubic inverse has a statistical counterpart in the actual
finite-offset count laws. We prove it within the specified Bernoulli
query experiment, including adaptive stopping. This result concerns
fixed bounded flight numbers; it is logically distinct from the
long-bridge experiment of Section~\ref{sec:v7-critical}.

Fix $R=1/4$ in the physical support family
\eqref{eq:g-isogap-family}, put $\alpha=0$, and let
$\theta=(\beta,\zeta)$ vary near zero. The common gap is $g=1/2$.
A query at $(j,d)$ consists of one fresh full-phase preparation and
the binary report $\one_{\{N_{jg+d}\ge j+1\}}$. We do not give the
experiment the lower values of $N_{jg+d}$ or a collision position.
Write its probability as $p_{j,d}(\theta)$.

\begin{lemma}[Cubic physical indistinguishability]
\label{lem:v7-cubic}
For each fixed $J<\infty$ there are $d_*,s_*,c,C>0$ such that, for
$1\le j\le J$, $0<d\le d_*$, and $|s|\le s_*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-cubic-p}
 |p_{j,d}(s,0)-p_{j,d}(-s,0)|\le Cd^2|s|^3,
 \qquad cd^2\le p_{j,d}(\pm s,0)\le Cd^2<\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
The matching distance between their unordered curvature triples is
bounded above and below by positive multiples of $|s|$ for $s\ne0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A rotation of the triangular lattice and the entire table by $\pi/3$
preserves the preparation measure and the unlabelled count. On
$(\beta,\zeta)$ it acts as a rotation through $2\pi/3$, up to reversal
of its sign. This follows directly by replacing $\vartheta$ by
$\vartheta-\pi/3$ in
$(1-\cos6\vartheta)(\beta\cos2\vartheta+\zeta\sin2\vartheta)$.
No nonzero linear functional is invariant under that rotation.
The gradient of $p_{j,d}(\theta)$ at zero must therefore vanish.

Theorem~\ref{thm:g-stability} and the equal-gap property give a smooth
extension of $d^{-2}p_{j,d}(\theta)$ to the closed collar, with bounded
third parameter derivatives uniformly for $j\le J$. Taylor expansion
along $(s,0)$ and $(-s,0)$ cancels the constant and quadratic terms;
the linear term is zero. This proves the first estimate for the full
physical probabilities, not merely their leading amplitudes. Their
positive continuous leading coefficients and a smaller common collar
prove the second estimates. The two radii multisets are
\[
 (R+36s,R-18s,R-18s),\qquad
 (R-36s,R+18s,R+18s).
\]
For $s>0$ sufficiently small, ordered matching of their reciprocals
has distance $36s/(R^2-324s^2)$, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-sharpness}. Replacing $s$ by $|s|$ proves the
last assertion.
\end{proof}

For a stopped adaptive design, a policy chooses the next query and
whether to stop using only the previous binary reports and independent
randomization. Its rules are the same under the two possible tables.
Suppose all its queries have $j\le J$ and $0\le d\le d_*$ on every
history. Define its weighted exposure by
\[
 \mathcal B=\max_{\theta\in\{(s,0),(-s,0)\}}
                  \E_\theta\sum_{i=1}^{T}d_i^2.
\]
Queries at $d=0$ are deterministic failures and contribute zero.
Bounds uniform over a fixed neighborhood mean the same exposure bound
holds in particular for each pair used below.

\begin{theorem}[A lower bound for count queries]\label{thm:v7-lower}
There is $c>0$ such that any predetermined or stopped adaptive design
as above with weighted exposure at most $B$ uniformly on the local
physical family has worst-case expected matching curvature error at
least $c(1+B)^{-1/6}$. In particular, if every queried offset satisfies
$d_i\le Lh$ on every history and
$\sup_\theta\E_\theta T\le N$, the lower bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-risk-lower}
 c(1+L^2h^2N)^{-1/6}.
\end{equation}
For each fixed confidence level $1-\eta_0>1/2$, accuracy $\varepsilon$
throughout this local family consequently requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-cost-lower}
 N\ge c_{L,\eta_0}h^{-2}\varepsilon^{-6}
\end{equation}
for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. The conclusion remains valid
when the exact common gap is supplied to the procedure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $0<p,q<1$, the inequality $\log x\le x-1$ gives
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(\operatorname{Ber}(p)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(q))
 \le \frac{(p-q)^2}{q(1-q)}.
\]
Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:v7-cubic} bounds the divergence of a single query
under the alternatives $s$ and $-s$ by $Cd^2s^6$. For a predetermined
schedule, the log likelihood is a sum and its expectation is at most
$Cs^6\sum_i d_i^2$.

For an adaptive policy, condition on each common observed history and
on its random seed. The next query, or stop decision, is then the same
in the two experiments. The conditional divergence is again at most
$Cd_i^2s^6$; a stop symbol contributes zero. Applying the chain rule
to the first $M$ steps, with the stopped process extended by stop
symbols, gives
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(\mathsf P_s^{[M]}\Vert\mathsf P_{-s}^{[M]})
     \le Cs^6\E_s\sum_{i\le\min(T,M)}d_i^2.
\]
The bound passes to the complete stopped transcript. Indeed relative
entropy is the supremum of its finite measurable-partition values;
conditioning these partitions on increasing finite histories yields
that supremum, while monotone convergence bounds the right side by
$Cs^6B$. Equivalently, this is the chain rule for a stopped sequence
obtained by truncation. A finite expected number of queries ensures
that the stop time is finite almost surely; if only weighted exposure
is bounded, the same finite-history argument applies to any measurable
estimator based on the infinite transcript. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-kl-exposure}
 D_{\rm KL}(\mathsf P_s\Vert\mathsf P_{-s})\le Cs^6B.
\end{equation}

We recall the testing step to fix constants and topology. Coarsening
to any event cannot increase relative entropy, by the log-sum
inequality. For Bernoulli laws the second derivative in the first
probability of relative entropy is $1/[x(1-x)]\ge4$, so Taylor's
formula at equality yields $D_{\rm KL}(\operatorname{Ber}(x)\Vert
\operatorname{Ber}(y))\ge2(x-y)^2$. Taking the supremum over events
therefore gives
$\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}\le\sqrt{D_{\rm KL}(P\Vert Q)/2}$.
Any test has sum of its two error probabilities at least
$1-\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}$, directly from the definition of total variation.

The curvature separation $\Delta_s$ is at least $c_0|s|$. Given an
estimator, classify it by its nearer alternative, with fixed tie
breaking. On a wrong classification its matching error is at least
$\Delta_s/2$, by the triangle inequality. Its maximum expected error
is consequently at least
$\Delta_s(1-\|\mathsf P_s-\mathsf P_{-s}\|_{\rm TV})/4$.
Choose $s=c_1(1+B)^{-1/6}$, with $c_1>0$ small enough to stay inside
the family and make the variation distance at most $1/2$ by
\eqref{eq:v7-kl-exposure}. This proves the expected-risk bound.
The bound $B\le L^2h^2N$ proves \eqref{eq:v7-risk-lower}.

For confidence accuracy choose $s=c_2\varepsilon$ so that
$\Delta_s>2\varepsilon$. Success with probability at least
$1-\eta_0$ under both alternatives would give a test whose error sum
is at most $2\eta_0$. Hence its variation distance is at least
$1-2\eta_0>0$. The entropy bound forces
$B\ge c_{\eta_0}\varepsilon^{-6}$, proving
\eqref{eq:v7-cost-lower}. The two alternatives have the same known
$g=1/2$, so supplying the gap does not change this test.
\end{proof}

For the fixed-order design of Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-count-only}, all
actual offsets are at most $L_mh$, including all pilot outcomes, and
$h\asymp\varepsilon^{3/m}$. Thus the power
$\varepsilon^{-(6+6/m)}$ in its preparation bound has a matching
obstruction within this shrinking-offset, bounded-flight-number binary
experiment, apart from confidence logarithms. This does not identify
a minimax exponent for unrestricted collision observations: nonshrinking
offsets, an unbounded choice of flight numbers, lower count values,
and position data are different experiments. All constants here are
at fixed $m,J,L_m$; sending $m$ to infinity is not justified.
