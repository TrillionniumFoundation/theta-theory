# Primary-source literature audit — A2 v30

This is a focused comparison of observation categories, not an exhaustive priority search. The new mathematical identities are proved in the primary. The accessible portions listed below support the attributions actually made; inaccessible texts are not described as fully reviewed.

## Capacity and mathematical morphology

G. Matheron, *Random sets theory and its applications to stereology*, Journal of Microscopy 95 (1972), 15–23. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2818.1972.tb03708.x

The publisher's abstract was read. It describes hit/containment probabilities for structuring figures and the capacity formulation. The primary does not attribute a new capacity theorem to the collision protocol. Proposition 8.1 instead proves the exact endpoint-exclusion formula P_a(f)=T_X([0,a])-T_X({0}) and the opposed cancellation under the particular all-attempt convention.

I. Molchanov, *Theory of Random Sets*, second edition, Springer London (2017). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4471-7349-6

The publisher's book record, edition and contents were read. Chapter 1 is *Random Closed Sets and Capacity Functionals* (pp. 1–223). Full access to that chapter was not obtained here; no specific theorem number or full-proof review is asserted. The reference is used for the established framework, not a claimed subsumption of the new inverse.

## Active geometric probing

H. Edelsbrunner and S. S. Skiena, *Probing Convex Polygons with X-rays*, SIAM Journal on Computing 17 (1988), 870–882. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1137/0217054

The publisher's abstract was read. The probe gives line/polygon intersection length; it reports 5n+O(1) probes sufficient for determination and separate verification bounds. The manuscript uses that stated determination bound to compare a finite-dimensional polygon target and real-length output with a localized Bernoulli smooth-class experiment. It does not claim to have reproduced the full algorithm proof or compare those counts as the same statistical resource.

P. Bose, J.-L. De Carufel, A. Shaikhet and M. Smid, *Probing Convex Polygons with a Wedge*, arXiv:1506.02572v3 (2016): https://arxiv.org/html/1506.02572v3

The primary arXiv HTML and Theorem 4.1 were read. For 0<omega<=pi/2 and a convex n-gon with all angles greater than omega, the stated algorithm uses at most 2n-2 probes. Its response includes wedge location/rays and contact points. The paper does not identify that response with a collision bit or claim that either experiment dominates the other.

## Retained comparisons and attribution limits

The v29 Galerne covariogram, Lagarias–Pleasants, Dolbilin–Garber–Schulte–Senechal and Herva–Kari comparisons remain byte-identical in the retained active chapter. They distinguish local period recognition under an assumed bounded presentation from proving crystallinity from local complexity. Their pinned historical versions are preserved; they are not represented as a newly exhaustive search.

The coarea/perimeter estimate, finite obstacle recursion, convex hull and compensated smoothing mechanisms are standard tools used with explicit proofs. The claimed additions concern their physical forcing, geometric stopping/tolerance conditions and finite reconstruction composition. No unsupported assertion of first priority, minimax optimality, conventional passive count rigidity or a stronger publication status is made.
