\section{Equivariance and two autonomous specifications}\label{sec:symmetry34}
An equivariant lift carries a group action on one extension, intertwining
with the action on the base. In the orthant case this is represented by
coordinate permutations after the usual normalization. Definitions 2.7--2.8
and Theorem 2.9 of the preprint of Gouveia--Parrilo--Thomas \cite{GPT}
relate symmetric cone lifts to symmetric slack factorizations. Symmetry
can change extension size substantially \cite{KPT}. In the regular-polygon
case, equivariant LP lifts of prime-power order require linear size,
whereas nonsymmetric LP lifts can have logarithmic size; the equivariant
PSD theory has different bounds \cite{FSP}, including logarithmic-size
lifts of regular $2^n$-gons.

Our stochastic diagrams do not impose a group representation, inverses,
one common section, or a permutation action on hidden coordinates. Their
maps may be noninvertible and epoch dependent. Hence the cited equivariant
lower bounds do not apply directly, and static small lifts do not provide
the stochastic maps needed here. The following elementary comparison makes
one exact point of contact. It is a consequence of classical stochastic
matrix spectral theory, not a new symmetric-lift bound.

\begin{proposition}[An exact cyclic stochastic lift]\label{prop:cyclic34}
Let $\xi=e^{2\pi i/m}$, $m\ge2$, and suppose stochastic rows
$E_0,\ldots,E_{m-1}\in\Delta_K$, a stochastic matrix $T$, and a complex
column $d$ satisfy
\[
 E_jT=E_{j+1\bmod m},\qquad E_jd=r\xi^j\quad(r\ne0).
\]
Then $K\ge m$. The bound is attained by the $m$-cycle permutation matrix.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The complex row $w=m^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\xi^{-j}E_j$ has $wd=r$,
so it is nonzero, and direct reindexing gives $wT=\xi w$.
Thus $\xi$ is an eigenvalue of $T$. Every unit-modulus eigenvalue of a
finite stochastic matrix belongs to a recurrent irreducible class and is
a root of unity of order dividing the period of that class. That period
is at most the number of its states. For completeness, transient blocks
have spectral radius less than one; in an irreducible recurrent block,
equality in the triangle inequality for an eigenvector at its maximal
modulus forces its phases to advance by the eigenvalue along every
positive edge. Along every directed cycle the accumulated phase is one,
so its order divides the greatest common divisor of the cycle lengths.
The primitive $m$th root therefore requires at least $m$ states.
The permutation construction realizes equality.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:cyclic34} assumes exact cyclic closure, including its
last-to-first transition. The finite irrational experiment has no such
closure. Its nonstationary lower bound comes instead from the finite
Fourier occupation theorem, which needs no eigenvalue shared by successive
matrices. This is why replacing the latter proof by a peripheral-spectrum
argument would be invalid.

\subsection{Fixed length is not an anytime query}
Define $A_N^{\rm fix}$ by requiring one common state set, two common command
matrices $T_0,T_1$, and one final decoder, but correctness only after
exactly $N$ commands. The entire machine may depend on $N$. Define
$A_N^{\rm any}$ with the same data required to answer after every command
length $0,1,\ldots,N$. A query is an atomic external input in both models.
Counts refer to internal labels before emission; charging two additional
absorbing answer labels increases the given upper bounds by at most two.
No internal test of input length is required by either interface.

\begin{corollary}[Bounds with the query-time quantifier fixed]
\label{cor:autonomy34}
For bounded-type $\alpha$,
\[
 \Omega_\alpha(N^{1/5})\le W_N(\alpha)\le A_N^{\rm fix}(\alpha)
                                      \le O_\alpha(N^{1/3}).
\]
For irrational $\alpha$, the stronger anytime specification satisfies
\[
                  N+1\le A_N^{\rm any}(\alpha)\le3(N+1).
\]
The anytime assertion is the inherited spectral comparison \cite{GTF33},
not a linear lower bound for $A_N^{\rm fix}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The unrestricted lower bound applies to stationary matrices in particular.
Proposition~\ref{prop:upper34} uses the same command matrices at all epochs,
so it gives the fixed-length autonomous upper bound without phase tags.
Its final radius and decoder depend on $N$, as permitted.

For the anytime lower bound fix seed $(1,1)$, use only active commands,
and combine the two decoders into $d$. If a machine with $K\le N$ states
were correct, then $E T_1^j d=c\zeta^j$ for $j=0,\ldots,K$, where
$c=(1+i)/10\ne0$. Cayley--Hamilton, applied to these $K+1$ identities,
forces the characteristic polynomial of $T_1$ to vanish at $\zeta$.
The unit-modulus eigenvalue property proved above contradicts irrationality.
For the upper bound keep the triangle label of the seed and the number
of active commands. The latter never exceeds $N$ on a permitted word.
Idle commands leave it unchanged; active commands increment it; the decoder
rotates the stored triangle vertex by that count. Arbitrary normalized
rows at count $N$ complete the matrix on unused transitions. This machine
answers correctly at every allowed length.
\end{proof}
