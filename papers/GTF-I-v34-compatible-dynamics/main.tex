\begin{abstract}
We prove a polynomial memory lower bound for a uniformly positive rotation
experiment whose separate positive minimum is three at every cut. The bound
allows arbitrary hidden states, unrelated transition matrices at different
epochs, and a new machine for each horizon. Its proof uses conditional
past-phase moments rather than the number of vertices of a projected
simplex section. A homogeneous angular inequality controls all Fourier
moments by one cumulative loss of conditional phase amplitude; positivity
of the Fej\'er kernel detects a register with few labels. For an angle
satisfying $\inf_{m\ge1}m\|m\alpha\|>0$, the unrestricted width is at
least a constant times $N^{1/5}$, improving the previous logarithmic lower
bound, while an exact $O_\alpha(N^{1/3})$ construction is retained.
The lower bound persists under any fixed sufficiently small uniform
output error. For the rational rotation $(3+4i)/5$ it gives a logarithmic,
rather than doubly logarithmic, lower bound. We formulate stochastic
extension as finite Blackwell comparison and transfer the new obstruction
to the accumulated deficiency of every proposed lift diagram. This rules
out logarithmic-coordinate low-defect lift chains for the bounded-type
family without imposing vector-state or equivariance restrictions. The
exponents $1/5$ and $1/3$ are not identified, and the fixed-length and
anytime autonomous specifications are kept distinct.
\end{abstract}
