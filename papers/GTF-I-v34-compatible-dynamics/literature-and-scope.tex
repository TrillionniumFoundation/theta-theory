\section{Relation to prior mechanisms and remaining questions}
Positive realization has long distinguished observable linear dimension
from positive-state dimension. Benvenuti--Farina \cite{BF99,BF04} give
fixed-degree examples requiring large positive order and explain
invariant-cone constructions. Heller \cite{Heller} and the realization
survey of Vidyasagar \cite{Vidyasagar} provide the stochastic-process
background. Accessible spaces, observable quotients and positive
intertwining are also explicit in Monr\`as--Winter \cite{MW}. None of these
mechanisms is renamed here as a new normal form. The specific assertion
proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:fourier34} is a finite-horizon lower bound for
unrelated stochastic updates, using a final correlation rather than one
repeated transition or a statewise predictive coordinate.

For comparison, the recent work of Lumbreras--Ma--Thompson--Gu
\cite{LMTG} studies irreducible finite-classical prediction and decision
errors in a stationary world-model setting. Its Section IV.B uses repeated
application of the same update along a Wait--Tick trajectory and the
peripheral behavior of that update. Here the matrices may change at every
epoch, and even the complete machine may change with the horizon. The
phase/Jensen/Fej\'er proof gives a quantitative finite budget under those
quantifiers. This distinction does not claim that all neighboring
approximate-realization results have been exhausted.

Static polyhedral lift theory identifies extension size through slack
factorizations \cite{GPT}. Regular polygons admit small nonsymmetric
extensions \cite{FRT,VGG}; respecting a group action can change this
substantially \cite{KPT,FSP}. Section~\ref{sec:symmetry34} states the exact
model distinction. Our converse does not assume equivariance or extract
an exposed face for each state. A hidden register instead produces at
most $K$ atoms in a measure of conditional \emph{past} phases, even when
its reachable simplex section has exponentially many projected vertices.
The common amplitude-loss estimate is what makes those atoms useful
through time.

The one-step equation $ET=F$, its decision inequalities, and one-sided
TV deficiency belong to classical comparison of experiments
\cite{Blackwell,Torgersen}; see also the modern formulation of the
Blackwell--Sherman--Stein theorem in \cite{FGPR}. Proposition~\ref{prop:blackwell34}
includes a proof to fix the direction and factor of two, not to claim a
new one-step duality. The additive composition of stochastic errors is
likewise standard. The main-family lower bound
\eqref{eq:diagram-lower34}, which applies to every proposed small-width
diagram, is a consequence of the new all-hidden-state budget.

\subsection{Conclusions}
For the fixed-alphabet uniformly positive rotation experiment, a hidden
register of bounded-type angle requires polynomial, not merely
logarithmic, width. The proof permits arbitrary hidden states and
arbitrary clocked updates. The same occupation theorem survives a fixed
uniform row error below $1/20$ and obstructs every small-width stochastic
lift diagram with vanishing total deficiency.

The exact hidden exponent is not determined: the present lower and upper
orders are $N^{1/5}$ and $N^{1/3}$. The vector-state order remains
$N^{1/3}$ under the inherited bounded-type hypotheses. Sharpening the
homogeneous angular estimate, the finite Fourier witness, or the exact
hidden construction are distinct possible routes to closing this gap.
No optimal global lift-search algorithm is asserted by the finite
comparison linear programs.

The model counts persistent classical labels, not construction time,
read-only program size, or fair-bit sampling workspace. Fixed-length
and anytime autonomous interfaces remain distinct. The historical
geometric, analytic and operator-theoretic manuscripts in the broader
program keep their independent proof obligations; no conclusion in this
article asserts their closure. Earlier results are preserved separately
without being used as evidence of priority or editorial significance.
