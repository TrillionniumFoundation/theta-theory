\begin{abstract}
We study the number of retained states needed to realize a specified
finite causal experiment. A criterion for simultaneous attainment of the
support-component rank is given by enclosing simplices and common positive
shift equations. Unlike a residual-vertex criterion, it allows all internal
continuations, including those outside the residual hull. For a binary-query
channel on $n$ acquired bits, we determine the exponential memory rate at
every fixed signal strength $0<\eta<1$. The checkpoint, fixed-order streaming,
and adaptive-acquisition minima all have rate
$1-h_2((1+\eta)/2)$. The upper construction realizes every conditional
probability exactly and has a fully charged sequential buffer; its excess
logarithmic space is $O_\eta(\sqrt{n\log n})$. At row total-variation tolerance
$\varepsilon$, the corresponding rate is obtained by replacing $\eta$ by
$(\eta-2\varepsilon)_+$. We also construct an exact $(t+1)$-state online
encoder for $\eta\le(2n-1)^{-1}$ and determine the sharp $n+1$-state threshold
$\eta=1/n$ whenever a Hadamard matrix of order $n+1$ exists. The entropy
exponent is classical in random-access coding; the conclusions here concern
exact conditional channel synthesis and the complete retained-state cost.
The relation to residual probabilistic automata is made explicit by a finite
language with exponentially many residual states and a polynomial-size
positive realization.
\end{abstract}
