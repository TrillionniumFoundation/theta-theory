\section{Residual automata and arbitrary positive states}
\label{sec:residual}
For a stochastic language $L$ on finite words and a prefix $u$ with
$L(u\Sigma^*)>0$, its normalized residual is
$L_u(v)=L(uv)/L(u\Sigma^*)$. A residual probabilistic automaton requires
every state's generated language to be one of these residuals. An
arbitrary probabilistic automaton need not satisfy that restriction.
The distinction is explicit in the probabilistic residual line initiated
by Esposito, Lemay, Denis and Dupont \cite{ELDD02}, and in
\cite[Section 2 and Propositions 16, 19]{DenisEsposito}. Canonical
minimality within a residual representation class is consequently not
a minimum over all positive generators.

The following construction makes the distinction within the precise
streaming family, without appealing to a conjectured difference between
models. It concerns the total number of states of a generative automaton;
its clock is explicitly incorporated in the states.

\begin{theorem}[A finite-language separation]\label{thm:prfa-gap}
Fix $n\ge2$ and rational $0<\eta\le(2n-1)^{-1}$. There is a finite
stochastic language $L_{n,\eta}$ such that every residual probabilistic
automaton generating it has at least $2^n$ states, whereas an unrestricted
rational probabilistic automaton has at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pfa-count}
               \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}2+n(n+1)+1
\end{equation}
states. The lower bound holds already for the states used immediately
before the query symbol.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use distinct symbols $u_-,u_+$ for acquired bits, $q_j$ for queries,
and $o_-,o_+$ for answers. Give mass
\begin{equation}\label{eq:language}
 L_{n,\eta}(u_{x_1}\cdots u_{x_n}q_j o_b)
             =\frac{2^{-n}}n\frac{1+b\eta x_j}{2}
\end{equation}
to those words and zero to every other word. The masses sum to one.
After a length-$n$ supported prefix, the residual has probabilities
$(1+b\eta x_j)/(2n)$ on $q_j o_b$. These $2^n$ residuals are an
injective affine image of the corners of an $n$-cube. In particular,
every one is an extreme point of their convex hull.

In a probabilistic realization, condition on such a prefix. The residual
is a convex combination of the state languages then occupied. Since it
is supported on words of length two of the displayed query--answer form,
nonnegativity forces every positively used state language to have that
same support restriction. In a residual automaton that state language
must itself be a residual of $L_{n,\eta}$. A residual after a supported
prefix of length $t$ is concentrated on length $n+2-t$; hence a residual
concentrated on length two must come from $t=n$. There are no other
supported prefix types at that length. Therefore the used state languages
belong to the $2^n$ cube-corner residuals above. Extremality forces each
corner to occur as a state language. They are distinct, proving the
lower bound.

For the upper bound, generate the $u$-symbols as independent fair bits.
Simultaneously run the encoder of Theorem~\ref{thm:reservoir}; a
transition labelled $u_x$ and updating to $s'$ has probability
$\frac12 T_{t,x}(s,s')$. Include the phase $t$ in the state. The
acquisition phases $t=0,\ldots,n$ have a total of
$\sum_{t=0}^n(t+1)=(n+1)(n+2)/2$ states. At phase $n$, emit $q_j$
uniformly and enter a state retaining $(s,j)$; there are at most
$n(n+1)$ such states. Emit $o_b$ with the exact decoder probability
and enter one common terminating state. Every transition and terminal
probability is nonnegative rational, and the generated language is
\eqref{eq:language}. This proves \eqref{eq:pfa-count} with its clock
and query buffer included.
\end{proof}
The theorem does not assert that exponential succinctness differences
between probabilistic models are new. Its role is to identify a concrete
instance in which a residual-only minimum cannot be substituted for the
all-machine minimum in this paper. In the present example the latter
has a genuinely online positive realization. The state languages of
that realization generally lie outside the residual hull.

One can equivalently treat the query as an external input, recovering
the checkpoint and streaming models of Section~\ref{sec:finite}.
Turning it instead into a generated symbol in \eqref{eq:language} is
what permits a direct comparison with stochastic-language automata.
The factor $1/n$ is essential: it normalizes the query choice rather
than appending an unnormalized family of conditional laws.
