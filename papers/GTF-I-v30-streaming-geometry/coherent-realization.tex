\section{Coherent positive realizations at the rank bound}
\label{sec:coherence}
\subsection{Finite causal arrays}
Fix a finite horizon and finite action and report alphabets. A complete
causal array $C(\mathbf a\mid\mathbf y)$ is nonnegative and normalized,
and its action-prefix marginal depends only on preceding reports. A
terminal output is an action with a dummy subsequent report. All storage
cuts required by the interface are included. In particular, an action
which must be remembered while awaiting a report has a charged buffer
cut; it is not available later as an uncharged input. Legal operation and
buffer-copy requirements can be specified as zeros and mandatory readouts
in the complete array. Elementary phases may be split to expose them.
The results below concern the resulting fixed, clocked interface.

For a controller prefix $h$ of positive conditional mass, let $R_h$ be
its normalized array of future actions, with future reports supplied
externally. Let $\cR_t$ be all distinct such arrays at cut $t$ and
$\cP_t$ the polytope of normalized causal arrays on its remaining
interface. Neither object is a runtime oracle. At zero-mass prefixes
no residual is defined; the complete array still specifies which
continuations have zero mass. Partial almost-sure specifications require
a choice, or an optimization, of complete extensions and are a different
input to the theorem.

For $B\in\cP_t$, let $p_B(a)$ be its next-action marginal, independent
of the unread report. Let $S_{a,y}B$ be the unnormalized restriction
obtained by fixing that action and the next report. If $p_B(a)>0$,
$S_{a,y}B/p_B(a)\in\cP_{t+1}$; at zero probability the restriction is
zero. For an actual residual, each positive normalized restriction is
again an actual residual. These maps are linear before normalization.

Write $F_t(R)$ for the minimal face of $\cP_t$ containing $R$.
Join two members of $\cR_t$ when their minimal faces intersect, and let
$\cR_{t,j}$, $1\le j\le c_t$, be the connected components. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support-rank}
 r_{t,j}=1+\dim\aff\cR_{t,j},\qquad s_t=\sum_{j=1}^{c_t}r_{t,j}.
\end{equation}
Normalization places each component in an affine hyperplane not through
zero, so $r_{t,j}$ is also its ordinary linear row rank.

\begin{lemma}[Separated-generator bound]\label{lem:separated}
Every stochastic realization has at least $s_t$ states at cut $t$.
If it has exactly $s_t$, the states used by component $j$ are precisely
$r_{t,j}$ affinely independent points of
$\cP_t\cap\aff\cR_{t,j}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Starting the machine from a state $s$ defines a normalized continuation
$B_s\in\cP_t$. Conditioning on a prefix gives
\[
 R_h=\sum_s E_h(s)B_s,\qquad E_h(s)\ge0,\quad\sum_sE_h(s)=1.
\]
Every positively used $B_s$ belongs to $F_t(R_h)$, by the face property.
Consequently the sets of states used by different support components
are disjoint. The convex hull of the states used by component $j$
contains $\cR_{t,j}$, and hence has at least $r_{t,j}$ generators.
This proves the lower bound. Equality in the sum leaves exactly that
many generators in each component and no additional used states.
Containment of an affine space of dimension $r_{t,j}-1$ in their
affine span forces independence and equality of the two affine spans.
No residual-state restriction has been imposed.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Coherent enclosing simplices]\label{def:coherent}
A coherent rank enclosure consists, at every cut and component, of
$r_{t,j}$ points $B_{t,j,k}\in\cP_t\cap\aff\cR_{t,j}$ whose convex
hull contains $\cR_{t,j}$, together with nonnegative numbers
$w_{t,j,k,a,y,u}$, where $u$ ranges over all vertices selected at the
next cut, satisfying
\begin{align}
 S_{a,y}B_{t,j,k}&=\sum_u w_{t,j,k,a,y,u}B_{t+1,u},
                                      \label{eq:coherent-shift}\\
 \sum_u w_{t,j,k,a,y,u}&=p_{B_{t,j,k}}(a).
                                      \label{eq:coherent-mass}
\end{align}
The initial array belongs to the initial selected hull. At the terminal
cut there is the single empty continuation. Action-buffer phases obey
the same equations on their explicitly split interface.
\end{definition}
The enclosure points are automatically affinely independent: they
contain a set of the full required affine dimension. In particular,
``simplex'' describes an enclosing object, not the residual hull.

\begin{theorem}[Rank saturation and causal compatibility]
\label{thm:coherence}
For a complete finite clocked causal array, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A stochastic realization has profile $K_t=s_t$ at every charged cut.
\item A coherent rank enclosure exists.
\end{enumerate}
If every component residual hull is a simplex, its own vertices give
such an enclosure. The equivalence itself places no simpliciality
condition on the residual hulls.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that a rank-saturating machine exists. Delete unused states.
Lemma~\ref{lem:separated} assigns its continuations to the indicated
component affine slices and proves their independence and enclosure
property. From a given state the actual next-action law is $p_B$.
Conditional on action $a$ and report $y$, the actual update probabilities
$q(u\mid B,a,y)$ are common to all histories giving that state.
Take $w_u=p_B(a)q(u\mid B,a,y)$. The law of the future program gives
\eqref{eq:coherent-shift} and \eqref{eq:coherent-mass}. Thus the
equations hold for one simultaneous collection, including buffer cuts.

Conversely, use the selected vertices as state labels. Initialize by
an enclosing convex decomposition of $C$. From state $B$, draw the
next action according to $p_B$. When $p_B(a)>0$, update after report
$y$ using $w_u/p_B(a)$. At zero $p_B(a)$ the row is unused and can
be assigned arbitrarily. Equation~\eqref{eq:coherent-mass} makes each
update stochastic, while $p_B$ is independent of the unread report.
Backward induction in \eqref{eq:coherent-shift} shows that the future
array from state $B$ is exactly $B$. The initialized law is therefore
$C$. The number of labels is $s_t$ at every cut.

For the final assertion, choose the vertices of each residual hull.
They are actual residuals because the residual set is finite. Their
positive normalized restrictions are residuals at the next cut and
have convex decompositions in that cut's chosen vertices. Multiplying
these decompositions by the action marginal supplies the required
weights. This is the residual construction, now a special case of
the equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Algebraic recognition]\label{cor:algebraic}
For a fully listed rational or real-algebraic array, simultaneous
attainment of $s_t$ is decidable by a finite existential system of
polynomial equalities and inequalities. A positive answer admits
real-algebraic stochastic rows. Here algebraic input numbers are given
by integer defining polynomials and isolating intervals.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The finite residuals, component graphs, affine slices, and ranks are
computed by exact linear algebra and linear feasibility. Write the
unknown enclosure vertices in these affine slices and impose the linear
causal-polytope constraints. Introduce convex coefficients for every
residual enclosure, and weights for \eqref{eq:coherent-shift}--
\eqref{eq:coherent-mass}. All unknown products have degree at most two.
Independence is forced by enclosure of the known full-dimensional
component, so no extra determinant condition is needed. Real quantifier
elimination, for example cylindrical algebraic decomposition
\cite{Collins,Davenport}, decides this finite system. A nonempty
semialgebraic set defined over the real algebraic numbers has a real
algebraic point, which the same sample-point procedure supplies.
The construction in the theorem then gives algebraic rows.
\end{proof}
This is not a complexity bound polynomial in the horizon or in a
succinct program. Nor does it replace equalities identifying a stochastic
row at different autonomous times. Those equalities are extra constraints.
The theorem characterizes rank saturation, not arbitrary minimum positive
rank. Its essential additional condition over a list of static enclosures
is the common shift equation.

\subsection{What static rank does and does not measure}
Let $A$ be a nonnegative row-stochastic matrix with one output alphabet.
Let $K(A)$ be the least number of normalized output rows whose convex
hull contains its rows. Let $K_{\rm aff}(A)$ impose the additional
condition that generators lie in the affine hull of the rows, and let
$K_{\rm res}(A)$ require generators to be actual rows.

\begin{proposition}[The normalization boundary]\label{prop:ranks}
For this single-output model,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ranks}
 \rank A\le\rank_+A=K(A)\le K_{\rm aff}(A)
 \le K_{\rm res}(A)=|\ext\conv\{A_i\}|.
\end{equation}
Writing $r=\rank A$, equality $K(A)=r$ holds if and only if an
$(r-1)$-simplex in the probability simplex contains every row and lies
in their affine hull. For a family of queried output laws, the same
convex characterization uses the product of the output simplices;
ordinary nonnegative rank of the concatenated matrix is only a lower
bound unless its factors satisfy all block-normalization equations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A stochastic encoder-decoder factorization is a nonnegative factorization.
Conversely, if $A=UV$ is nonnegative, discard zero rows of $V$ and put
$d_s=\sum_yV_{sy}>0$. Then $B_{sy}=V_{sy}/d_s$ is stochastic and
$E_{is}=U_{is}d_s$ has row sum one because $A$ does. This proves
$\rank_+A=K(A)$. The remaining inequalities follow by restricting the
generators. The extreme points of a finite hull are precisely the
necessary actual-row generators, proving the last equality. If $r$
generators enclose affine dimension $r-1$, their affine hull must be
the row affine hull and they are independent. This proves the simplex
criterion and its converse.

With a query $j$, a physical state has a separate normalized decoder
row for every $j$. Normalizing the total of a concatenated generator
only fixes the sum over all blocks; it does not make every block sum
one. The admissible ambient set is therefore the product of simplices,
which proves the stated distinction.
\end{proof}
The single-output affine-slice restriction is the transpose-normalized
form of restricted nonnegative rank \cite{GG}. At one cut, positive
generator complexity also decomposes additively over the support
components: a generator cannot serve two components by the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:separated}, and independent component decompositions can
be united. For one output alphabet these components are the disjoint
support blocks of the matrix. This static additivity is not asserted to
solve the compatibility problem between different cuts.

\begin{proposition}[A nonsimplicial equality and a strict inequality]
\label{prop:squares}
For the four binary-query mean rows $(\pm a,\pm a)$, ordinary normalized
row rank is three. If $0<a\le1/3$, their positive generator number is
three although their residual hull is a square. If $1/2<a<1$, their
positive generator number is four although all conditional probabilities
are strictly positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The rank assertion follows from affine dimension two. The triangle with
vertices $(-a,-a),(3a,-a),(-a,3a)$ lies in the outer square when
$a\le1/3$ and contains the inner square; its defining inequalities are
$z_i\ge-a$ and $z_1+z_2\le2a$. This proves the first assertion.
For the second, put $d_x(z)=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^2(1-x_i z_i)$ for
$x\in\{-1,1\}^2$. A convex representation of $a x$ uses a generator
with $d_x(z)<1/2$, because its average is $1-a<1/2$. Such strict caps
are disjoint for distinct $x$: a differing coordinate contributes one
to $d_x+d_{x'}$. Four distinct generators are required, and the four
rows themselves suffice. This proves a full-support rank gap without
assuming residual-state realizations.
\end{proof}
