\section{Exact finite streaming at the rank threshold}
\label{sec:finite}
\subsection{Checkpoint and streaming models}
For $n\ge1$ and $0\le\eta\le1$, the channel to be synthesized is
\eqref{eq:query-intro}. Every input coordinate is acquired exactly once
before the query. In a fixed-order encoder the order is $1,\ldots,n$.
In an adaptive encoder the next unread coordinate can depend on the
retained state and fresh randomness; any information required to make
that choice legal must be retained. There is no readable transcript or
unrecorded schedule. After acquisition, all input-dependent information
passes through a single finite state before the external query arrives.
One query is answered; a joint law for simultaneous answers to all queries
is not required.

The checkpoint minimum $K_n(\eta)$ imposes no bound on upstream memory.
The quantities $W_n^{\rm fo}(\eta)$ and $W_n^{\rm ad}(\eta)$ impose a
bound on every cut, including intermediate compression and retained
random selectors. The clock in the fixed-order convention is prescribed
externally. A terminal binary label counts as at most two states. The
query is a current atomic input; a model requiring its storage has to
charge that storage separately.

\begin{lemma}[Cube enclosure]\label{lem:cube}
For $\eta>0$, $K_n(\eta)$ is the smallest number of points
$z_s\in\cube^n$ whose convex hull contains $[-\eta,\eta]^n$.
In particular $K_n(\eta)\ge n+1$. This lower bound and every lower
bound obtained from such an enclosure also apply to adaptive acquisition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
From state $s$, let $z_{s,j}$ be the mean binary output under query $j$.
Conditioning on the complete input gives an encoder distribution $E_x$
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:query-factor}
                  \eta x=\sum_s E_x(s)z_s.
\end{equation}
Conversely, such a representation followed by a binary decoder with
mean $z_{s,j}$ realizes the prescribed channel. Upstream memory is
unrestricted for this converse construction. Convexity reduces enclosure
of the inner cube to its corners. Its affine dimension gives $n+1$.
For adaptive acquisition, conditioning on the complete input still gives
one distribution of the retained state; the subsequent decoder is common
to all inputs. This uses the acquisition barrier, not an assertion about
arbitrary interactive scheduling.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An online simplex with a linear signal window}
\begin{theorem}[An exact minimal acquisition profile]
\label{thm:reservoir}
For every $n\ge1$ and $0<\eta\le(2n-1)^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reservoir-peak}
       K_n(\eta)=W_n^{\rm ad}(\eta)=W_n^{\rm fo}(\eta)=n+1.
\end{equation}
One fixed-order encoder has exactly $t+1$ states after $t$ acquired
bits, $0\le t\le n$, and this profile is minimal at each acquisition
cut for that order. Its stochastic acquisition rows are rational and
independent of $\eta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $t\ge1$ use labels $0,1,\ldots,t$ with associated vectors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reservoir-vertices}
 v_{t,0}=-\eta\1,
 \qquad v_{t,i}=-\eta\1+2t\eta e_i\quad(1\le i\le t).
\end{equation}
Their convex hull is
\[
 P_t=\{z\in\R^t:z_i\ge-\eta,\ \sum_i z_i\le t\eta\}.
\]
Every vertex lies in the outer cube because $(2t-1)\eta\le1$.
Moreover $P_t\times\{-\eta,\eta\}\subset P_{t+1}$. The barycentric
coordinates of $w\in P_t$ are
\[
 \lambda_i(w)=\frac{w_i+\eta}{2t\eta},\qquad
 \lambda_0(w)=\frac{t\eta-\sum_iw_i}{2t\eta}.
\]
They are nonnegative and sum to one.

Here is an explicit update avoiding any stored real vector. After $t$
inputs, the state distribution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reservoir-law}
 \Prb(S_t=i\mid x_{1:t})=\frac{\1_{\{x_i=1\}}}{t},
 \qquad
 \Prb(S_t=0\mid x_{1:t})=\frac{|\{i\le t:x_i=-1\}|}{t}.
\end{equation}
To acquire bit $x_{t+1}$, keep the old label with probability $t/(t+1)$.
With probability $1/(t+1)$ replace it by $t+1$ when $x_{t+1}=1$ and
by zero when $x_{t+1}=-1$. For $t=0$ only the replacement is used.
Induction proves \eqref{eq:reservoir-law}. The mean of
$v_{t,S_t}$ is exactly $\eta x_{1:t}$. At the query barrier use the
binary decoder with mean $v_{n,S_n,j}$. This proves exact synthesis.
Only the label survives an update. The last raw bit and the replacement
coin do not persist in an auxiliary register.

The final lower bound follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:cube}. At an earlier
fixed-order cut, fix the unread bits identically for all prefixes.
The future response vectors for queries among the first $t$ coordinates
contain the $t$-dimensional cube of means $\eta x_{1:t}$. Factoring
these continuations through the current state requires at least $t+1$
labels. Thus the entire acquisition profile is minimal. The final binary
output uses no more labels than the peak $n+1$.
\end{proof}
For $t\ge2$ the actual prefix residuals have a cube, not a simplex,
as their hull. Equations~\eqref{eq:reservoir-vertices} and
\eqref{eq:reservoir-law} are therefore a growing nonsimplicial family
of coherent rank enclosures. Theorem~\ref{thm:coherence} applies without
requiring that an internal vertex be a residual.

\subsection{The sharp \texorpdfstring{$n+1$}{n+1}-state boundary in Hadamard dimensions}
\begin{lemma}[A simplex obstruction]\label{lem:trace}
If $K_n(\eta)=n+1$ with $\eta>0$, then $\eta\le1/n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The enclosing vertices $v_0,\ldots,v_n\in\cube^n$ are affinely
independent. Write their barycentric affine functions as
$\lambda_s(z)=a_s+b_s\cdot z$. Nonnegativity on the inner cube gives
$a_s\ge\eta\norm{b_s}_1$, and $\sum_sa_s=1$. The identity
$z=\sum_s\lambda_s(z)v_s$ gives
$\sum_s v_sb_s^T=I_n$. Taking its trace yields
\[
 n=\sum_s b_s\cdot v_s\le\sum_s\norm{b_s}_1\le\eta^{-1}.
\]
This proves the assertion for arbitrary enclosing vertices, not just
a chosen simplex construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A sharp threshold with an online realization]
\label{thm:hadamard}
Suppose that a Hadamard matrix of order $m=n+1$ exists. For $\eta>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hadamard-threshold}
 K_n(\eta)=W_n^{\rm ad}(\eta)=W_n^{\rm fo}(\eta)=n+1
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \eta\le1/n.
\end{equation}
This applies in particular to every $n=2^k-1$, $k\ge1$.
More generally, a Hadamard matrix of order $m\ge n+1$ gives an
$m$-state streaming realization whenever $0<\eta\le1/n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Normalize the first column of the Hadamard matrix to one, and select
$n$ other columns. Let $v_s\in\{-1,1\}^n$, $1\le s\le m$, be
their rows. Orthogonality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hadamard-orthogonal}
 \frac1m\sum_s v_{s,j}=0,
 \qquad \frac1m\sum_s v_{s,i}v_{s,j}=\1_{\{i=j\}}.
\end{equation}
For each index $i$ and observed bit $x_i$, define the probability law
\[
 q_{i,x_i}(s)=\frac{1+x_i v_{s,i}}m.
\]
Its mean vector is $x_i e_i$ by \eqref{eq:hadamard-orthogonal}.
At step $i$, retain the preceding state with probability $(i-1)/i$;
otherwise redraw a label from $q_{i,x_i}$. The first step always draws.
After $t$ bits its state law is $t^{-1}\sum_{i\le t}q_{i,x_i}$.
Consequently $\E[v_{S_n}\mid x]=x/n$. The final decoder with mean
$n\eta v_{S_n,j}$ is legal for $\eta\le1/n$ and gives mean $\eta x_j$.
This is an online machine with $m$ labels; the chosen index of a redraw
is not separately retained. All acquisition probabilities are rational.

For $m=n+1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:cube} proves optimality and
Lemma~\ref{lem:trace} excludes larger signals. The Sylvester construction
$H_1=(1)$ and
$H_{2m}=\begin{pmatrix}H_m&H_m\\H_m&-H_m\end{pmatrix}$ gives the
claimed infinite sequence directly.
\end{proof}
The equality in \eqref{eq:hadamard-threshold} concerns the peak, not
every intermediate width. The encoder in this proof may have $n+1$
possible labels already after its first bit. In contrast,
Theorem~\ref{thm:reservoir} attains the individual $t+1$ bounds in its
smaller signal interval. Choosing the next power of two for $m$ also
gives a less than $2(n+1)$-state upper bound at $\eta\le1/n$ for
arbitrary $n$; it does not decide the exact simplex threshold in every
dimension.
