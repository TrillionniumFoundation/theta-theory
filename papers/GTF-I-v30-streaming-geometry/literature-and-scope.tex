\section{Theorem boundaries and further questions}
\label{sec:literature}
\subsection{What is inherited from realization theory}
The probabilistic residual finite-state automata paper of
Esposito--Lemay--Denis--Dupont \cite{ELDD02} is an essential predecessor,
not a peripheral citation. Its announced characterization uses finite
generation by residual stochastic languages and canonical minimal forms.
The later full treatment \cite[Propositions 16, 19]{DenisEsposito}
provides a unique inclusion-minimal residual generating set and the
finite-generation characterization by residual probabilistic automata.
The relevant restriction is on the state languages. Our explicit
comparison in Theorem~\ref{thm:prfa-gap} uses that restriction rather
than inferring an all-machine minimum from the word ``canonical.''
The publisher record and abstract of \cite{ELDD02}, and the full cited
sections of \cite{DenisEsposito}, are the bases of this comparison;
we do not claim a completed line-by-line audit of the 2002 chapter.

Positive stochastic realization, including stable polyhedral sets of
continuations, is classical \cite{Heller,Vidyasagar}. The stationary
HMM existence question in \cite{Vidyasagar} is not the same input as a
fully listed finite controlled array. Nevertheless the principle that
positive generators must be closed under appropriate shifts is shared.
Theorem~\ref{thm:coherence} should therefore not be read as an invention
of positive realization. It makes rank saturation, affine-slice
enclosure, and the simultaneous finite shift constraints explicit.
Its use here is the non-residual streaming construction, the sharp
Hadamard-dimensional peak threshold, and the charged exact-synthesis
bounds, not an assertion that a finite normal form alone settles the
general realization problem.

Gillis--Glineur \cite[Definition 1 and Theorem 1]{GG} distinguish
restricted nonnegative rank from unrestricted nonnegative rank and
relate the former to nested polytopes. Proposition~\ref{prop:ranks}
is the normalized single-output specialization, with the transpose
convention made explicit. A queried law has block-normalization
constraints, and coherent realization has constraints between cuts;
these must not be removed when applying a static matrix theorem.

Compatible coverings for incompletely specified sequential machines
\cite{Reusch} concern a different form of missing information. Our
array theorem uses complete laws and allows support zeros in those
laws; it does not choose a canonical completion of a partial law.
Filtered-experiment comparison \cite{Norberg} is an adjacent comparison
framework, not a result replaced by the constructions here. No general
non-overlap assertion about that framework is made. The precise
remaining source-access limits are recorded with the revision response.

\subsection{What is inherited from random-access coding}
The classical lower and upper communication rates in
\cite[Theorems 2.1 and 2.2]{ANTVpre} are
$(1-h_2(p))n$ and $(1-h_2(p))n+O(\log n)$ for recovery success at
least $p$. Their upper construction uses a finite family of masks and
permutations to make the guarantee hold for every input and coordinate.
Thus the entropy function and the polynomial-overhead communication
scale are classical; neither is claimed as a new discovery here.

Our exact-channel proof uses a support-function enclosure of the entire
inner cube. It supplies exact prescribed conditional probabilities,
not only a success inequality. The block construction then prices all
retained raw data and selected codewords. The limiting exponent remains
the classical one, but the operational conclusion is about exact channel
synthesis at every row and the peak of the whole streaming schedule.
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:approx-rate} shows precisely how a uniform
row-error tolerance changes that exponent. It does not claim a result
for arbitrary sequentially repeated queries on one installation.

\subsection{Precision and implementation}
Atomic stochastic rows can contain a specified real parameter. A
rational row can be sampled exactly by a variable-length fair-bit
procedure, but that statement alone does not give a bounded-time or
bounded-workspace sampler. The present state counts concern atomic
rows. The finite codebooks and rows may be enormous as program objects;
program description length and computational time are not included in
$K_n$ or $W_n$. This is the same distinction as in the preceding
known-target physical statements. Acquiring an unknown target and
approximating its stochastic row requires calibration data, retained
counters, and precision-dependent workspace. Those results are retained
with their original costs in the mathematical companion \cite{GTF29}.

\subsection{Conclusions}
The proved exact class is now described by the coherent-enclosure
criterion, which is necessary and sufficient for the support-component
rank profile to be attained. Residual-hull simpliciality is one
sufficient subcase. The growing query family has nonsimplicial residual
hulls, an explicit minimal acquisition profile in a linear signal
window, and a sharp $n+1$-state threshold in Hadamard dimensions.

Unrestricted positive-state minimization, autonomous row sharing, and
joint optimization over all controlled policies remain different
problems. We have not supplied an example in which every static cut
attains its rank bound but no simultaneous rank profile exists; the
criterion states the exact compatibility test rather than claiming
such a separation without proof. The historical geometric and
unbounded-operator obligations of the larger program are likewise
not consequences of a finite-state coding theorem.

The former fixed-interior-signal gap in the query family is resolved
at the level of its exponential rate, both exactly and at a fixed
uniform row-error tolerance. Finite-size checkpoint versus streaming
gaps, optimal lower-order terms, the sharp simplex threshold in all
dimensions, and critical regimes with signal varying with $n$ are
not identified by these limits. The explicit enclosure and block bounds
provide quantitative statements in those regimes without replacing
them by an unsupported exact phase diagram.
