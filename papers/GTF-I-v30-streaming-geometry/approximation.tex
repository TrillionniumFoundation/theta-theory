\section{Uniformly approximate experiments and their exact rate}
\label{sec:approximation}
For two binary-output channels on the same input/query set, define
\[
 d_{\rm row}(P,\widetilde P)=
     \max_{x,j}\TV(P(\cdot\mid x,j),\widetilde P(\cdot\mid x,j)).
\]
For $\varepsilon\ge0$, this is a uniform comparison of experiments, not an error averaged over
a selected input prior. Let $K_n^\varepsilon(\eta)$,
$W_n^{{\rm ad},\varepsilon}(\eta)$ and
$W_n^{{\rm fo},\varepsilon}(\eta)$ denote the corresponding minima
allowing $d_{\rm row}\le\varepsilon$. Put
$\kappa=(\eta-2\varepsilon)_+$.

\begin{theorem}[A sharp row-error memory rate]\label{thm:approx-rate}
If $0<\kappa<1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx-sandwich}
 2^{nI(\kappa)}\le K_n^\varepsilon(\eta)
 \le K_n(\kappa),\qquad
 W_n^{{\rm fo},\varepsilon}(\eta)\le W_n^{\rm fo}(\kappa).
\end{equation}
In particular, for fixed $\eta\in(0,1]$ and fixed
$0\le\varepsilon<\eta/2$ with $\kappa<1$, all three normalized
logarithmic approximate complexities converge to $I(\kappa)$.
For $\varepsilon\ge\eta/2$, one query state and at most two terminal
states suffice, so the rate is zero. At $(\eta,\varepsilon)=(1,0)$
the exact state counts equal $2^n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $m_j(x)$ be the mean output of an admissible approximate channel.
Binary total variation is half the absolute difference of means; hence
$|m_j(x)-\eta x_j|\le2\varepsilon$. Factoring the approximate
channel through states gives $m(x)=\sum_sE_x(s)z_s$, with
$z_s\in\cube^n$. Therefore
\[
                  x\cdot m(x)\ge n(\eta-2\varepsilon)=n\kappa
\]
when $\kappa>0$. Some used generator has $x\cdot z_s\ge n\kappa$.
The caps in Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-cap} cover the input corners, giving
the lower bound, also for adaptive acquisition.

Conversely, exactly synthesize the channel with mean $\kappa x_j$.
Its distance from the required one is $(\eta-\kappa)/2=\varepsilon$.
This proves the upper bounds and allows Theorem~\ref{thm:rate} to be
applied at the fixed signal $\kappa$. If $\kappa=0$, the fair-output
channel is independent of input and query and has row defect $\eta/2$.
It needs one state before output. Finally, an exact noiseless response
to every query determines every input bit through the stored generator;
the cap argument at $u=1$, or disjoint supports, gives $2^n$ states,
matched by retention.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A statistical consumer: reusable binary sensors}
Consider a parameter $x\in\{-1,1\}^n$ and a preparation interface that
reveals its coordinates during acquisition. A later single coordinate
measurement has conditional mean $\eta x_j$. A simulator must be
installed before the measurement index is chosen. Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}
is an exact state-cost theorem for this simulator, not a theorem about
a prior chosen to fit a codebook. Theorem~\ref{thm:approx-rate} gives
its uniform error--memory exponent, with the same lower bound against
adaptive acquisition.

\begin{corollary}[Comparison under downstream experiments]
\label{cor:consumer}
A simulator with row defect $\varepsilon$ changes the expectation of
any common $[0,1]$-valued decision loss, after one queried report and
arbitrary parameter-independent postprocessing, by at most $\varepsilon$,
uniformly in $x$ and in a randomized query distribution independent of
the simulator's hidden state. If a sequence of fresh installations has
conditional row defect at most $\varepsilon$ at each round and is
coupled to the same downstream history-dependent controller, the
$T$-round path-law defect is at most $\min(1,T\varepsilon)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At one round, mixture over the common query and contraction under
postprocessing do not increase total variation; bounded losses then
have expectation difference at most $\varepsilon$. For repeated
installations, couple the two executions only while their public
histories agree. The downstream action laws then agree and the chance
of a first discrepant report in each active round is at most
$\varepsilon$. A union bound proves the path statement. The conditional
hypothesis is essential; it is ensured, for example, by fresh independent
reinstallation and fresh sampling randomness at every round.
\end{proof}
A single compressed label need not support a sequence of independent
queries with the product output law. Observing an answer may change the
conditional distribution of that label. Thus repeated use of the same
unrefreshed code is not justified by the one-query theorem. If all $n$
noisy answers are required jointly and independently, the specified
product channel has full matrix rank $2^n$ for every $\eta>0$, and
needs at least $2^n$ query-barrier states. Indeed its transition matrix
is the $n$-fold Kronecker power of the invertible binary symmetric
channel. The distinction between a later chosen measurement and a
joint vector of measurements is operational, not a change of notation.

The transport argument in the corollary is classical total-variation
contraction and coupling. Its quantitative memory input is the new
exact-synthesis law. It is a local statistical consumer, not a claim
that a Fourier, large-deviation, or nonlinear-semigroup theorem in a
different part of the foundations program has been proved by finite
channel synthesis.
