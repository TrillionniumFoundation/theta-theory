\begin{abstract}
We derive a nonlinear terminal semigroup from the same nonconjugate
moving-cut collision system used in the companion response and pressure
papers.  The essential new bridge is an exact innovation theorem: even when
the branch parameter is a predictable function of the previous branch
history and of a slow state, the current fast coordinate is conditionally
Lebesgue, the next branch has the prescribed parameter-dependent law, and the
image coordinate is again independent of the enlarged past.  Bounded branch
increments therefore form a genuine martingale triangular array generated by
the deterministic nonautonomous dynamics.

We prove first- and second-level martingale convergence, including predictable
covariance and area anomaly, and obtain nonautonomous rough homogenization.
For the actual moving-cut model the characteristics are explicit and the
full microscopic sequence converges; no response derivative is substituted
for an invariance principle.  A quantitative block theorem is also given.
Together with a sharp Gaussian benchmark it shows exactly how an enhanced-WIP
rate enters block freezing.

The macroscopic value is defined by a pointwise finite-branch entropic
recursion.  Its monotonicity, stability, and locally uniform consistency give
viscosity convergence to
\[
-u_t-b\cdot Du-\tfrac12\Sigma:D^2u
-\tfrac\vartheta2 Du^T\Sigma Du=0.
\]
The coefficients are globally defined and uniformly elliptic.  The Cole--Hopf
transform gives comparison, the exact Feynman--Kac formula, and a
time-consistent theta-semigroup.  No anisotropic-pairing-to-pointwise upgrade
is used anywhere in the proof.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Spectral response and long-time homogenization answer different questions.
Response differentiates frozen invariant data.  Homogenization requires a
functional limit theorem that remains valid when the frozen parameter follows
a slow, history-dependent path.  The present paper supplies that missing
arrow: the symbolic process of the moving-cut system is an exact predictable
innovation process.

The fast family is the full-cover map from the companion paper.  Its parameter
may depend on the slow variable, controls, or a frozen cotangent signal.  The
branch symbols are conditionally independent with the exact branch widths.
This converts the deterministic nonautonomous map into a martingale array
without replacing it by an unrelated Bernoulli shift.

Martingale approximation originates with Gordin \cite{Gordin1969};
deterministic diffusion limits and rough enhancements are developed in
\cite{MelbourneStuart2011,KellyMelbourne2016,KellyMelbourne2017,
ChevyrevFrizKorepanovMelbourne2020}.  We use martingale limit theory
\cite{HallHeyde1980,JacodShiryaev2003}, rough-path continuity
\cite{Lyons1998,FrizVictoir2010,FrizHairer2014}, and monotone-scheme viscosity
methods \cite{BarlesSouganidis1991,CrandallIshiiLions1992}.

\section{The deterministic moving-cut innovation process}\label{sec:innovation}

For $a\in I=[-1/40,1/40]$, let
\[
 T_a(x)=\frac{x-s_{i-1}(a)}{w_i(a)},
 \qquad x\in I_i(a)=[s_{i-1}(a),s_i(a)),
\]
where
\[
 (w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4)
 =(1/10+a,1/5+2a,3/10-a,2/5-2a).
\]
Each branch maps affinely onto $[0,1)$.  Distinct parameters are not $C^1$
conjugate, as proved in the companion response paper.

Let $X_0^{\rm f}$ be Lebesgue distributed.  Let $A_k\in I$ be measurable with
respect to
\[
 \F_k=\sigma(I_0,\ldots,I_{k-1}),
\]
where $I_k=i$ when $X_k^{\rm f}\in I_i(A_k)$, and set
\[
 X_{k+1}^{\rm f}=T_{A_k}(X_k^{\rm f}).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact predictable innovations]\label{thm:innovation}
For every $k\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item conditionally on $\F_k$, $X_k^{\rm f}$ is Lebesgue distributed;
\item
\[
 \Pp(I_k=i\mid\F_k)=w_i(A_k);
\]
\item conditionally on $\F_k\vee\sigma(I_k)$,
$X_{k+1}^{\rm f}$ is Lebesgue distributed and independent of that
sigma-field.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the branch symbols are exact conditionally independent innovations with
predictable probabilities $w(A_k)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The claim is true at $k=0$.  Assume conditional uniformity at time $k$.
Since $A_k$ is known given $\F_k$, the conditional probability of branch $i$
is its length $w_i(A_k)$.  Given that branch, the fast point is uniform on
$I_i(A_k)$.  The affine branch map sends normalized Lebesgue measure on that
interval to Lebesgue measure on $[0,1)$.  The resulting law is independent of
the branch and earlier history, proving the induction step.
\end{proof}

\section{Doob selection without feedback circularity}\label{sec:doob}

Let $c_i\in\R^d$ and let $\zeta$ be an external tilt.  The locally constant
twist has leading eigenvalue
\[
 Z(a,\zeta)=\sum_iw_i(a)e^{\zeta\cdot c_i}.
\]
The normalized Doob probabilities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-prob}
 p_i(a,\zeta)=
 \frac{w_i(a)e^{\zeta\cdot c_i}}{Z(a,\zeta)}.
\end{equation}
They are again positive branch widths.  Replacing the four intervals by widths
$p_i(a,\zeta)$ realizes the Doob kernel as another full-cover moving-cut map;
\cref{thm:innovation} applies to predictable $(a_k,\zeta_k)$.

\begin{definition}[Causal order]
The variables $(a,\zeta)$ and any cotangent signal used to choose them are
external frozen inputs.  The branch map, Doob probabilities, invariant law,
martingale characteristics, and homogenized generator are constructed before
a limiting value function is solved.  In the viscosity proof a test gradient
may be substituted only at a fixed contact point.
\end{definition}

\section{A nonautonomous martingale rough theorem}\label{sec:rough}

Let $(M_k^\varepsilon,\F_{k+1}^\varepsilon)$ be $\R^d$-valued martingale
differences.  Set
\begin{align}
 W^\varepsilon(t)&=\varepsilon
 \sum_{k<t/\varepsilon^2}M_k^\varepsilon,
 \label{eq:W}\
 \mathbb W^\varepsilon(t)&=\varepsilon^2
 \sum_{0\le i<j<t/\varepsilon^2}
 M_i^\varepsilon\otimes M_j^\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:WW}
\end{align}

\begin{assumption}[Predictable characteristics]\label{ass:chars}
On every finite horizon:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for some $q>4$ the conditional $q$th moments are uniformly bounded;
\item conditional Lindeberg holds;
\item uniformly in $t$,
\[
 \varepsilon^2\sum_{k<t/\varepsilon^2}
 \E[M_k^\varepsilon\otimes M_k^\varepsilon\mid
 \F_k^\varepsilon]
 \longrightarrow\int_0^t\Sigma_s\,ds
\]
in probability;
\item the predictable antisymmetric second-level compensator converges to
$\int_0^t\Gamma_sds$;
\item the continuous martingale problem with bracket
$\int_0^t\Sigma_sds$ is unique.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Nonautonomous enhanced martingale limit]\label{thm:rough}
Under \cref{ass:chars}, the canonical polygonal lifts converge in every
admissible $p$-variation topology to
\[
 \left(W,\mathbb W+\int_0^{\cdot}\Gamma_sds\right),
\]
where $W$ is the continuous Gaussian martingale with bracket
$\int_0^t\Sigma_sds$.  Uniformly Lipschitz rough differential recursions driven
by these lifts converge to the corresponding limiting RDE.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditional Lindeberg and bracket convergence give the martingale functional
central limit theorem.  The $q>4$ estimate and discrete
Burkholder--Davis--Gundy inequalities give tightness of the first level and the
iterated sums in $p$-variation.  Discrete integration by parts identifies the
symmetric second level, and the predictable antisymmetric compensator gives
$\Gamma$.  Martingale-problem uniqueness identifies all subsequential limits.
Continuity of the It\^o--Lyons map yields the RDE conclusion.
\end{proof}

\section{Actual nonautonomous homogenization on the same map}\label{sec:actual}

Let $\Theta:\R^m\to I$ be globally Lipschitz.  For each $a$, choose bounded
branch vectors $c_i(a)\in\R^m$ and set
\[
 \bar c(a)=\sum_iw_i(a)c_i(a),
 \qquad m_i(a)=c_i(a)-\bar c(a).
\]
Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ellipticity}
 \Sigma(a):=\sum_iw_i(a)m_i(a)\otimes m_i(a)
 \ge\lambda I_m.
\end{equation}
Let $b$ be globally Lipschitz and define, on the slow time
$t_k=k\varepsilon^2$,
\begin{align}
 A_k&=\Theta(X_k^\varepsilon),\label{eq:Ak}\\
 X_{k+1}^\varepsilon&=X_k^\varepsilon
 +\varepsilon m_{I_k}(A_k)+\varepsilon^2b(X_k^\varepsilon),
 \label{eq:slow-recursion}\\
 X_{k+1}^{\rm f}&=T_{A_k}(X_k^{\rm f}).
\end{align}
The parameter $A_k$ is predictable with respect to the branch history.

\begin{theorem}[Endogenous moving-cut diffusion limit]\label{thm:homogenization}
The polygonal interpolation of \eqref{eq:slow-recursion} converges in law to
the unique weak solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SDE}
 dX_t=b(X_t)dt+\sigma(\Theta(X_t))dW_t,
 \qquad \sigma(a)\sigma(a)^T=\Sigma(a).
\end{equation}
The enhanced martingale driver converges jointly, with zero antisymmetric area
anomaly.  More generally, smooth vector-field couplings converge to the
corresponding RDE/Stratonovich system with the canonical bracket correction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:innovation},
$M_k=m_{I_k}(A_k)$ is a martingale difference and
\[
 \E[M_k\otimes M_k\mid\F_k]=\Sigma(A_k).
\]
The increments are bounded, so all conditional moment and Lindeberg conditions
hold.  Tightness of the slow paths and continuity of $\Theta,\Sigma$ turn the
predictable bracket into Riemann sums for
$\int_0^t\Sigma(\Theta(X_s))ds$.  One-step innovations have zero predictable
antisymmetric area.  Apply \cref{thm:rough}; global Lipschitz coefficients and
\eqref{eq:ellipticity} give uniqueness of \eqref{eq:SDE}.
\end{proof}

This theorem is the required response-to-homogenization bridge on one actual
system: response supplies smooth coefficient dependence, while exact
innovations independently supply the long-time limit.

\section{Quantitative block replacement}\label{sec:block}

Suppose a frozen enhanced WIP is known with a uniform law-distance rate
\[
 \eta(N)\le CN^{-\delta},\qquad\delta>0.
\]
A block of $m_\varepsilon$ fast steps has duration
$h_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^2m_\varepsilon$ and there are
$O(h_\varepsilon^{-1})$ blocks.

\begin{proposition}[Strict block window]\label{prop:block}
If
\[
 m_\varepsilon\to\infty,\qquad
 h_\varepsilon\to0,\qquad
 \varepsilon^{-2}m_\varepsilon^{-(1+\delta)}\to0,
\]
and the accumulated freezing and switching errors vanish, then the block
replacement holds on the full sequence.  For
$m_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-\kappa}$ this is the nonempty window
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa-window}
 \frac{2}{1+\delta}<\kappa<2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The accumulated frozen-law error is
$h_\varepsilon^{-1}\eta(m_\varepsilon)
\le C\varepsilon^{-2}m_\varepsilon^{-(1+\delta)}$.
The second condition separates the block from the macroscopic scale.  The
power-law equivalence is an elementary exponent calculation.
\end{proof}

\section{A sharp enhanced Gaussian benchmark}\label{sec:rate}

Let $(\xi_k)$ be the coordinates of a deterministic Gaussian Bernoulli shift,
let $W_N$ be the polygonal normalized partial-sum path, and let
$\mathbf W_N$ be its step-two lift.  Let $\mathbf B$ be enhanced Brownian
motion.  Choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rough-window}
 p>6,\qquad \frac13<\eta-\frac1p,\qquad \eta<\frac12.
\end{equation}
Let $d_{\eta,p}$ combine $W^{\eta,p}$ at level one and
$W^{2\eta,p/2}$ at level two with the usual square-root scaling, and denote the
associated Wasserstein--one distance by $\mathcal W_1^{\eta,p}$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp rough Wasserstein rate]\label{thm:rate}
There are constants $0<c_{\eta,p}\le C_{\eta,p}<\infty$ such that
\[
 c_{\eta,p}N^{-(1/2-\eta)}
 \le\mathcal W_1^{\eta,p}
 \bigl(\mathcal L(\mathbf W_N),\mathcal L(\mathbf B)\bigr)
 \le C_{\eta,p}N^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple $\xi_k$ with the Brownian mesh increments.  Then $W_N=P_NB$ exactly.
The error $B-P_NB$ is a concatenation of independent Brownian bridges.
Scaling the fractional-Sobolev integral on each mesh interval and summing the
intervals gives
\[
 \E\norm{B-P_NB}_{W^{\eta,p}}^p
 \le C N^{-p(1/2-\eta)}.
\]
Cross-interval terms satisfy the same bound by the bridge endpoint zero and
Minkowski's inequality.

For level two, Chen's identity decomposes
$S_2(B)-S_2(P_NB)$ into local bridge L\'evy areas and bridge--coarse cross
integrals.  A local area has size $N^{-1}$; after the
$W^{2\eta,p/2}$ scaling and the square root in the rough metric, the sum is
$O(N^{-(1/2-\eta)})$.  The cross terms are controlled by the product of the
first-level bridge norm and the Brownian coarse norm.  This proves the upper
bound.

For the lower bound, let $V_N$ be the closed subspace of mesh-polygonal paths
and set
$F_N(\mathbf x)=\dist_{W^{\eta,p}}(x^1,V_N)$.  It is one-Lipschitz for the
rough metric and vanishes on $\mathbf W_N$.  The conditional Brownian bridge on
each interval is independent of the endpoints; a fractional Poincar\'e
inequality and scaling give
$\E F_N(\mathbf B)\ge cN^{-(1/2-\eta)}$.  Kantorovich duality proves the lower
bound.
\end{proof}

The result is sharp for the named full rough-law metric.

\section{The pointwise moving-cut entropic recursion}\label{sec:HJB}

Let $\Theta:\R^m\to I$, $b:\R^m\to\R^m$, and branch increments
$m_i(a)$ be globally Lipschitz and bounded.  Assume centering and uniform
ellipticity \eqref{eq:ellipticity}.  For $h>0$ and $\vartheta\ne0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Th}
 (\mathcal T_h\phi)(x)=\frac1\vartheta\log
 \sum_{i=1}^4w_i(\Theta(x))
 \exp\left\{\vartheta
 \phi\bigl(x+h b(x)+\sqrt h\,m_i(\Theta(x))\bigr)\right\}.
\end{equation}
For $T=Nh$, set
\[
 u_h(T,\cdot)=\phi,
 \qquad u_h(kh,\cdot)=\mathcal T_hu_h((k+1)h,\cdot).
\]
This is the prelimit value.  It is a pointwise function and is generated by the
same branch widths as the deterministic fast map.

\begin{lemma}[Monotonicity and consistency]\label{lem:consistency}
The operator $\mathcal T_h$ is monotone and cash additive.  For
$\phi\in C_b^3(\R^m)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consistency}
 \frac{\mathcal T_h\phi-\phi}{h}
 \longrightarrow b\cdot D\phi
 +\frac12\Sigma(\Theta(x)):D^2\phi
 +\frac\vartheta2D\phi^T\Sigma(\Theta(x))D\phi
\end{equation}
locally uniformly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity and cash additivity are immediate.  Taylor-expand at the four
increments.  The $\sqrt h$ term cancels by centering, the second moment gives
the Hessian term, and the second cumulant of the linear increment gives the
quadratic gradient term.  Bounded third moments and locally uniform derivative
bounds control the remainder.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Global actual HJB limit]\label{thm:HJB}
For every bounded uniformly continuous terminal payoff, $u_h$ converges
locally uniformly to the unique bounded viscosity solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HJB}
 -u_t-b(x)\cdot Du
 -\frac12\Sigma(\Theta(x)):D^2u
 -\frac\vartheta2Du^T\Sigma(\Theta(x))Du=0,
 \qquad u(T)=\phi.
\end{equation}
Let $X$ solve
\[
 dX_s=b(X_s)ds+\sigma(\Theta(X_s))dW_s,
 \qquad \sigma\sigma^T=\Sigma.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropic-formula}
 u(t,x)=\frac1\vartheta
 \log\E_{t,x}e^{\vartheta\phi(X_T)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The scheme is monotone, stable and consistent, so its upper and lower
half-relaxed limits are respectively viscosity sub- and supersolutions of
\eqref{eq:HJB}.  No paired residual enters this argument.  Put
$v=e^{\vartheta u}$.  The chain rule transforms \eqref{eq:HJB} into
\[
 -v_t-b\cdot Dv-\frac12\Sigma:D^2v=0,
 \qquad v(T)=e^{\vartheta\phi}.
\]
The uniformly elliptic linear equation has comparison and the Feynman--Kac
formula.  Hence the half-relaxed limits coincide, proving full convergence and
\eqref{eq:entropic-formula}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Theta-semigroup]
For $s\le t$ define
\[
 \cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi](x)
 =\frac1\vartheta\log\E_{s,x}e^{\vartheta\phi(X_t)}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Dynamic nonlinear evaluation]\label{cor:theta}
The theta-semigroup is monotone, cash additive, terminally stable, convex for
$\vartheta>0$, and time consistent:
\[
 \cE_{r,s}^{\vartheta}
 \bigl[\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]\bigr]
 =\cE_{r,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi].
\]
Its Hamiltonian vanishes at zero gradient.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the conditional log-moment-generating function and the tower property, or
comparison and concatenation of \eqref{eq:HJB}.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

The full chain is now carried by one nonconjugate deterministic fast family.
Exact predictable innovations supply the long-time martingale theorem;
spectral response supplies smooth physical coefficients; and a pointwise
finite-branch recursion supplies the viscosity limit.  The global Cole--Hopf
transform fixes comparison and the terminal sign without a compact-gradient
argument.
