\begin{abstract}
We derive nonlinear diffusion semigroups from deterministically generated fast
noise selected by a frozen Doob transform.  The microscopic selector is built
from an external state--cotangent--control signal before any Hamilton--Jacobi
solution is introduced.  A uniform martingale--rough package yields a frozen
enhanced invariance principle with covariance and area anomaly.  We then prove
a nonautonomous homogenization theorem by two routes: a block argument with an
explicit accumulated quantitative error, and a direct predictable-characteristics
argument for slowly varying martingale arrays.  Qualitative uniform weak
convergence alone is shown to imply only a cofinal diagonal result.

For a deterministic Gaussian Bernoulli shift we prove the sharp law rate in the
full Wasserstein distance associated with a step-two fractional-Sobolev rough
metric.  If $p>6$, $1/3<\eta-1/p$, and $\eta<1/2$, then the canonical lift of
the polygonal partial-sum process satisfies
\[
 W_1^{\eta,p}(\mathbf W_N,\mathbf B)
 \asymp N^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\]
The upper bound follows from an exact Brownian-grid coupling, including a
second-level bridge estimate; the lower bound is the expected distance of
Brownian motion from the mesh-polygonal subspace.

The homogenized characteristics generate monotone dynamic-programming
operators.  Stability, consistency, and comparison yield an HJB limit and a
time-consistent nonlinear semigroup, called the theta-expectation.  The
four-branch moving-seam model of the companion papers gives an actual
full-scale realization through direct martingale characteristics.  An explicit
nonconvex Hamiltonian shows that theta-expectations need not be subadditive.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Deterministic homogenization asks when a slow system driven by a rapidly
iterated chaotic map converges to a stochastic differential equation.  For a
fixed fast map, martingale approximation and rough-path enhancement now give a
robust route from weak invariance principles to stochastic limits; see
\cite{KellyMelbourne2016,KellyMelbourne2017,MelbourneStuart2011}.  The problem
addressed here is different in two respects.  First, the fast law is selected
by a parameter-dependent Doob transform.  Second, the parameter varies with
the slow state and an external cotangent/control signal.  Thus one needs a
uniform enhanced limit and a careful accumulation of frozen approximation,
switching, and initial-law errors.

The spectral coefficients used here are constructed in the companion paper
\cite{QiPressure}.  In particular, pressure response produces smooth physical
covariance, area, drift, and elliptic coefficient fields on a fixed common
operator space.  We do not feed an unknown value-function gradient back into
the microscopic operator.  The cotangent variable is external throughout the
selection and homogenization stages; only the viscosity consistency argument
later substitutes a test-function gradient.

Our first main theorem, \cref{thm:nonautonomous}, gives a full nonautonomous
rough limit under either a quantitative frozen modulus or direct predictable
characteristics.  The distinction is essential.  A compact-parameter
qualitative invariance principle permits a diagonal choice of block lengths,
but it gives no control between the selected lengths and therefore no theorem
for every microscopic scale.

Our second main theorem, \cref{thm:sharp-rate}, fixes a precise notion of
``optimal enhanced-WIP rate.''  The state space is the step-two geometric rough
path space endowed with a fractional-Sobolev metric, and the law distance is
its full Wasserstein--$1$ distance.  A Gaussian Bernoulli shift gives an exact
coupling to Brownian mesh interpolation.  Brownian bridges determine the
unavoidable exponent $1/2-\eta$ at both rough levels.  This benchmark supplies
an explicit admissible block window for nonautonomous homogenization.

Our third contribution is nonlinear.  The effective characteristics define a
family of monotone dynamic-programming operators.  The Barles--Souganidis
method \cite{BarlesSouganidis1991}, formulated in viscosity language
\cite{CrandallIshiiLions1992}, gives an HJB limit.  Its solution operators form a
time-consistent nonlinear semigroup.  We call this the theta-expectation to
emphasize that it need not be convex or sublinear.  A concrete trigonometric
Hamiltonian violates subadditivity.

The actual four-branch model in \cref{sec:actual} is inherited from the
moving-seam response papers, but its probabilistic proof is independent of a
quantitative mixing theorem: under the selected Bernoulli law, the increments
form a bounded martingale triangular array.  Predictable brackets and second
levels converge directly, yielding the full-scale limit.

\section{Frozen Doob selection and the no-feedback principle}\label{sec:doob}

Let $K$ be a compact state--cotangent--control window, and write
\[
 \theta=(x,p,\zeta)\in K.
\]
The companion pressure paper gives a fixed Banach space and a positive twisted
operator $L_\theta$ with simple leading eigenvalue $\lambda_\theta>0$, positive
right eigenfunction $h_\theta$, and positive left eigenfunctional
$\ell_\theta$, normalized by $\ell_\theta(h_\theta)=1$.

\begin{definition}[Doob operator]\label{def:doob}
For bounded $f$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob}
 \mathsf P_\theta f
 =\lambda_\theta^{-1}h_\theta^{-1}L_\theta(h_\theta f).
\end{equation}
Then $\mathsf P_\theta1=1$.  Its invariant probability is
\[
 \mu_\theta^D(f)=\ell_\theta(h_\theta f).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Parameter regularity]\label{prop:doob-regularity}
If the leading eigendata and the multiplier maps are $C^r$ on $K$, bounded away
from zero in the declared positive cone, then
$\theta\mapsto\mathsf P_\theta$ and $\theta\mapsto\mu_\theta^D$ are $C^r$ on
the corresponding operator and dual spaces.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:doob} is a finite product of the eigendata, multiplication,
and inversion on a set where $h_\theta$ is uniformly positive.  Differentiate
by the ordinary product rule.  The invariant law formula is treated similarly.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Causal order]\label{rem:no-feedback}
The variable $p$ in this section is an external signal.  Neither
$L_\theta$ nor $h_\theta$ is defined using an unknown HJB solution.  At the HJB
stage, a smooth test function supplies $p=D\phi(x)$ locally.  This order removes
a potential circularity between microscopic selection and macroscopic dynamic
programming.
\end{remark}

\section{A uniform martingale--rough packet}\label{sec:martingale}

Let $v_\theta$ be a centered $\R^d$-valued observable:
$\mu_\theta^D(v_\theta)=0$.

\begin{assumption}[Uniform martingale--rough hypotheses]\label{ass:UM}
Uniformly over $\theta\in K$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(UM\arabic*)}]
\item the collision/physical clock and parameter chambers are fixed;
\item for some $q>4$, $\sup_\theta\norm{v_\theta}_{L^q}<\infty$;
\item there is a Gordin decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordin}
 v_\theta=m_\theta+\chi_\theta\circ T_\theta-\chi_\theta,
\end{equation}
where $m_\theta$ is a reverse martingale difference;
\item predictable quadratic variations converge to $\Sigma_\theta$;
\item the predictable antisymmetric second level converges to
$\Gamma_\theta$;
\item the rescaled maximal coboundary in \eqref{eq:gordin} vanishes uniformly;
\item admissible initial laws are forgotten quantitatively;
\item $\Sigma_\theta,\Gamma_\theta,m_\theta,\chi_\theta$ have a common
parameter modulus;
\item either a frozen enhanced-law modulus $r(N)\to0$ is given, or the actual
slowly varying array satisfies direct martingale characteristics;
\item the switching error vanishes and the limiting martingale problem or RDE
is unique.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral sufficient conditions]\label{prop:spectral-UM}
Suppose $\mathsf P_\theta$ has a uniform spectral gap on a Banach algebra
$\mathcal B$ continuously embedded in $L^q$, and that $v_\theta$, its tensor
square, and the required antisymmetric area observable belong uniformly to
$\mathcal B$.  Suppose also that the common-space resolvent has a uniform
parameter modulus.  Then all parts of \cref{ass:UM} except the quantitative or
direct alternative in (UM9) and limiting uniqueness in (UM10) follow.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the centered space define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson}
 \chi_\theta=\sum_{n\ge1}\mathsf P_\theta^n v_\theta.
\end{equation}
The spectral gap gives convergence in $\mathcal B$, hence in $L^q$.  The usual
transfer identity shows that
$m_\theta=v_\theta+\chi_\theta-\chi_\theta\circ T_\theta$ has zero conditional
mean.  Doob and Burkholder maximal inequalities control the martingale and the
bounded Poisson endpoint, proving maximal coboundary negligibility.  Apply the
same Poisson construction to the centered matrix observable
$m_\theta\otimes m_\theta-\Sigma_\theta$ and to the antisymmetric iterated
observable to obtain the bracket and area compensator.  Exponential memory loss
transfers the statements to admissible initial laws.  Finally,
\[
 R_\theta-R_{\theta'}
 =R_\theta(\mathsf P_\theta-\mathsf P_{\theta'})R_{\theta'}
\]
gives the common parameter modulus for the Poisson solutions and derived
coefficients.
\end{proof}

\section{Frozen enhanced invariance principle}\label{sec:frozen}

Define the polygonal partial-sum process
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WN}
 W_N^\theta(t)=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j< Nt}v_\theta\circ T_\theta^j
\end{equation}
with linear interpolation, and its canonical step-two lift
$\mathbf W_N^\theta=(W_N^\theta,\mathbb W_N^\theta)$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform frozen enhanced WIP]\label{thm:frozen-WIP}
Under \cref{ass:UM}, the frozen lifts converge, uniformly in $\theta$, to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:enhanced-limit}
 \mathbf W_N^\theta\Longrightarrow
 \bigl(W^\theta,\mathbb W^\theta+t\Gamma_\theta\bigr)
\end{equation}
in every declared step-two rough topology below the available moment exponent.
The Brownian covariance is $\Sigma_\theta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Replace $v_\theta$ by $m_\theta$ using \eqref{eq:gordin}.  The first-level
coboundary is an endpoint difference and vanishes after $N^{-1/2}$ scaling by
(UM6).  Discrete integration by parts writes the second-level coboundary terms
as endpoint products plus an ergodic average.  The endpoints vanish and the
average converges to the deterministic anomaly $\Gamma_\theta$.  The martingale
functional CLT, together with the bracket hypothesis, gives the first level;
the martingale rough-path CLT gives the canonical symmetric second level and
identifies its antisymmetric compensator.  Uniform moments give tightness.
Initial-law transfer and the modulus in (UM9) make the convergence uniform.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Qualitative convergence gives only a diagonal theorem]\label{prop:diagonal}
Suppose the frozen convergence in \eqref{eq:enhanced-limit} is uniform on $K$
but no quantitative modulus is available.  Then one can choose a cofinal
sequence of microscopic scales for which a block homogenization proof closes.
In general this does not imply convergence along every $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose integers $M_k\uparrow\infty$ such that the uniform frozen-law error at
$M_k$ is at most $2^{-k}$.  Then choose $\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$ whose block
length is $M_k$ and whose physical block duration tends to zero.  This closes a
diagonal block proof.  Without a bound between $M_k$ and $M_{k+1}$, no estimate
controls arbitrary intermediate microscopic scales.  A full-scale statement
therefore requires a usable modulus or direct characteristics.
\end{proof}

\section{A sharp fractional-Sobolev rough-path rate}\label{sec:rate}

We now give an actual optimal benchmark in a standard full law metric.  Let
\[
 \Omega=(\R^d)^{\mathbb Z},
 \qquad T(\omega)_n=\omega_{n+1},
\]
with product Gaussian measure $N(0,\Sigma)^{\otimes\mathbb Z}$, and let
$\xi(\omega)=\omega_0$.  This is a deterministic measure-preserving Bernoulli
shift whose coordinate process is i.i.d. Gaussian.

Fix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-range}
 p>6,
 \qquad \frac13<\eta-\frac1p,
 \qquad \eta<\frac12.
\end{equation}
For a first-level path $x$, let $\norm{x}_{W^{\eta,p}}$ be the Slobodetsky
norm.  For a step-two geometric rough path $\mathbf x=(x,\mathbb x)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rough-metric}
 d_{\eta,p}(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)
 =\norm{x-y}_{W^{\eta,p}}
 +\norm{\mathbb x-\mathbb y}_{W^{2\eta,p/2}}^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Let $W_1^{\eta,p}$ be Wasserstein--$1$ for this metric.

Let $P_NB$ denote Brownian polygonal interpolation on the mesh $k/N$.  Under
the coupling
\[
 \xi_k=\sqrt N\bigl(B_{(k+1)/N}-B_{k/N}\bigr),
\]
the Gaussian partial-sum path has exactly the law of $P_NB$.

\begin{lemma}[First-level bridge estimate]\label{lem:first-bridge}
There are constants $0<c\le C<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-bridge}
 cN^{-(1/2-\eta)}
 \le \E\norm{B-P_NB}_{W^{\eta,p}}
 \le CN^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On each mesh interval of length $h=N^{-1}$, the error is an independent
Brownian bridge.  Brownian scaling gives local amplitude $h^{1/2}$; the
fractional derivative of order $\eta$ contributes $h^{-\eta}$.  Summing the
$p$th powers over $N$ intervals and taking the $p$th root gives
$h^{1/2-\eta}$.  Cross-interval terms are bounded by the same order using the
bridge endpoint cancellation.  For the lower bound, retain only pairs of
points lying in the central halves of the same mesh interval and use the
nondegenerate bridge variance.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Second-level bridge estimate]\label{lem:second-bridge}
Let $\mathbf B$ and $S_2(P_NB)$ denote the Stratonovich Brownian lift and the
canonical polygonal lift.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-bridge}
 \E\norm{\mathbb B-\mathbb{P_NB}}_{W^{2\eta,p/2}}^{1/2}
 \le CN^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $B=P_NB+\beta_N$, where $\beta_N$ is the collection of independent
Brownian bridges.  Chen's identity decomposes the second-level difference on
$[s,t]$ into the bridge iterated integral, the two cross integrals
$\int(P_NB-P_NB_s)\otimes d\beta_N$ and
$\int(\beta_N-\beta_N(s))\otimes d(P_NB)$, and endpoint concatenation terms.
On one mesh interval, the bridge area has $L^{p/2}$ size $h$ and each cross term
has size $h$ by Gaussian scaling.  The $W^{2\eta,p/2}$ kernel contributes
$h^{-2\eta}$; after the square root in \eqref{eq:rough-metric}, the local order
is $h^{1/2-\eta}$.  For increments crossing mesh boundaries, apply Chen's
identity once more: complete bridge pieces have zero first-level endpoint, and
the remaining coarse increments are controlled by discrete Burkholder and
Gaussian moment bounds.  Summation gives \eqref{eq:second-bridge}.
\end{proof}

For the lower bound, let $V_N$ be the closed subspace of paths affine on every
mesh interval and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-test}
 F_N(\mathbf x)=\dist_{W^{\eta,p}}(x,V_N).
\end{equation}
This is one-Lipschitz for $d_{\eta,p}$ and vanishes on every polygonal mesh
path.

\begin{lemma}[Distance from the polygonal subspace]\label{lem:lower-distance}
There is $c>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-lower}
 \E F_N(\mathbf B)\ge cN^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Restriction to each mesh interval and subtraction of the affine endpoint
interpolant define the Brownian bridge.  A fractional Poincar\'e inequality on
$[0,h]$ bounds the distance from affine functions below by the fractional norm
of the centered bridge on a fixed interior subinterval.  Brownian scaling gives
$h^{1/2-\eta}$ per interval in the global $W^{\eta,p}$ aggregation.  Independence
and a lower Khintchine bound prevent cancellation, yielding
\eqref{eq:distance-lower}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp rough Wasserstein rate]\label{thm:sharp-rate}
Let $\mathbf W_N$ be the canonical lift of the Gaussian polygonal partial-sum
path and $\mathbf B$ enhanced Brownian motion with covariance $\Sigma$.  Under
\eqref{eq:eta-range},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-rate}
 cN^{-(1/2-\eta)}
 \le W_1^{\eta,p}
 \bigl(\mathcal L(\mathbf W_N),\mathcal L(\mathbf B)\bigr)
 \le CN^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Brownian-grid coupling and
\cref{lem:first-bridge,lem:second-bridge} give the upper bound.  For the lower
bound, use the one-Lipschitz test \eqref{eq:distance-test} in Kantorovich
duality.  Its expectation under $\mathbf W_N$ is zero and under $\mathbf B$ is
bounded below by \cref{lem:lower-distance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The word ``sharp'' is tied to the full law metric $W_1^{\eta,p}$.  Endpoint,
H\"older, variation, and other path metrics may have different exponents.
\end{remark}

\section{Nonautonomous rough homogenization}\label{sec:nonautonomous}

Let the slow parameter be
\[
 \theta_t^\varepsilon
 =\Theta(X_t^\varepsilon,p_t^\varepsilon,\zeta_t^\varepsilon).
\]
Freeze it on blocks of $m_\varepsilon$ fast iterates.  The physical block
length and number of blocks on $[0,T]$ are
\[
 h_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^2m_\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 N_\varepsilon\asymp\frac{T}{h_\varepsilon}.
\]

\begin{assumption}[Accumulated block control]\label{ass:block}
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-basic}
 m_\varepsilon\to\infty,
 \qquad h_\varepsilon\to0,
\end{equation}
and, for the frozen law modulus $r$, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-rate}
 N_\varepsilon r(m_\varepsilon)\to0.
\end{equation}
The total parameter-freezing, switching, initial-law, and Taylor-remainder
errors over all blocks also vanish.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Doob-selected nonautonomous homogenization]\label{thm:nonautonomous}
Assume either \cref{ass:block} or the direct predictable-characteristics
alternative in (UM9).  Then the slow process converges to the unique solution
of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limit-SDE}
 dX_t=ar b(X_t,p_t,\zeta_t)\,dt
 +\sigma(X_t,p_t,\zeta_t)\,dW_t
 +b_\Gamma(X_t,p_t,\zeta_t)\,dt,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma\sigma^T=\Sigma$ and $b_\Gamma$ is the Lie-bracket drift obtained
by applying the antisymmetric anomaly $\Gamma$ to the slow vector fields.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the block route, replace each frozen deterministic enhanced driver by its
Brownian limit.  The sum of the law errors is bounded by
$N_\varepsilon r(m_\varepsilon)$.  The parameter modulus controls replacement
of the varying parameter by its block value; local rough Taylor estimates and
the slow modulus control freezing and switching.  Rough-path continuity then
transports driver convergence to the slow equation.  Tightness and uniqueness
identify the limit.

In the direct route, the slowly varying centered increments form a martingale
array.  Conditional Lindeberg, convergence of the predictable bracket to
$\int_0^t\Sigma(\theta_s)ds$, and convergence of the predictable antisymmetric
second level to $\int_0^t\Gamma(\theta_s)ds$ give the enhanced martingale FCLT.
The continuous It\^o/RDE map and uniqueness yield \eqref{eq:limit-SDE}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Explicit block window from the sharp rate]\label{cor:block-window}
Let $r(m)=O(m^{-\delta})$ with $\delta=1/2-\eta$, and put
$m_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-\kappa}$.  The frozen-law accumulation and
vanishing physical block conditions hold when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa-window}
 \frac{2}{1+\delta}<\kappa<2.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The accumulated error is
$O(\varepsilon^{-2}m_\varepsilon^{-(1+\delta)})$, which tends to zero exactly
when $\kappa(1+\delta)>2$.  The condition
$h_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{2-\kappa}\to0$ is $\kappa<2$.
\end{proof}

\section{The actual four-branch selected system}\label{sec:actual}

Let $w_i(a)$ be the four branch weights of the companion moving-seam model and
let $c_i\in\R^d$ be bounded branch increments.  For a compact tilt $\zeta$, the
Doob probabilities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-prob}
 p_i(a,\zeta)=
 \frac{w_i(a)e^{\zeta\cdot c_i}}
 {\sum_jw_j(a)e^{\zeta\cdot c_j}}.
\end{equation}
Under the selected law, branch symbols are independent with probabilities
\eqref{eq:doob-prob}.  Set
\[
 v_{a,\zeta}(i)=c_i-\sum_jp_j(a,\zeta)c_j.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Actual full-scale rough homogenization]\label{thm:actual-K2}
On compact $(a,\zeta)$ windows, the selected four-branch system satisfies the
uniform martingale--rough hypotheses.  For slowly varying predictable
parameters, the centered increments form a bounded martingale triangular
array, their predictable covariance is the Riemann sum of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-cov}
 \Sigma(a,\zeta)=\sum_ip_i(a,\zeta)
 v_{a,\zeta}(i)\otimes v_{a,\zeta}(i),
\end{equation}
and the antisymmetric area anomaly is zero.  Hence the full microscopic family,
not merely a subsequence, converges to \eqref{eq:limit-SDE}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All moments are uniformly bounded and the Gordin correction is zero.  Conditional
Lindeberg is automatic.  Smoothness of \eqref{eq:doob-prob} and slow variation
turn the predictable covariance sums into Riemann sums of
\eqref{eq:actual-cov}.  Conditional independence makes the predictable
antisymmetric area zero.  Apply the direct half of
\cref{thm:nonautonomous}.
\end{proof}

\section{Dynamic programming and the HJB limit}\label{sec:dpp}

Let $S_{t,s}^\varepsilon$ be prelimit dynamic-programming operators on bounded
uniformly continuous payoffs.  They are introduced only after the microscopic
selector and homogenized characteristics are fixed.

\begin{assumption}[DPP and comparison]\label{ass:DPP}
The operators are monotone, stable, constant preserving in the zero-cost
normalization, and dynamically consistent.  For every smooth test function
$\phi$ and every sequence on which the local microscopic scale vanishes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consistency}
 \frac{S_{t,t+h}^\varepsilon\phi(x)-\phi(x)}{h}
 \longrightarrow
 -\cH(x,D\phi(x),D^2\phi(x)).
\end{equation}
The limiting terminal-value equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HJB}
 \partial_tu+\cH(x,Du,D^2u)=0,
 \qquad u(T,\cdot)=\Phi,
\end{equation}
has comparison in the chosen growth class.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[HJB convergence]\label{thm:HJB}
Under \cref{thm:nonautonomous,ass:DPP}, the prelimit value functions converge
locally uniformly to the unique viscosity solution of \eqref{eq:HJB}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\overline u$ and $\underline u$ be the upper and lower half-relaxed
limits.  At a strict contact with a smooth test, monotonicity and the DPP reduce
the local value inequality to the one-step consistency formula
\eqref{eq:consistency}.  This gives the viscosity subsolution and supersolution
inequalities.  Stability passes the terminal data.  Comparison gives
$\overline u\le\underline u$; the reverse inequality is automatic.  Local
equicontinuity upgrades subsequential convergence to local uniform convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Filtering and two-player games are not hypotheses of \cref{thm:HJB}.  They are
parallel downstream branches treated in the next companion paper.
\end{remark}

\section{Theta-expectations and non-subadditivity}\label{sec:theta}

For $r\le s$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-eval}
 \cE_{r,s}^{\theta}[\Phi](x)=u(r,x),
\end{equation}
where $u$ solves \eqref{eq:HJB} on $[r,s]$ with terminal value $\Phi$.

\begin{theorem}[Theta-expectation semigroup]\label{thm:theta}
The evaluations \eqref{eq:theta-eval} are monotone, terminally stable, constant
preserving under the zero-cost normalization, and time consistent:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:time-consistency}
 \cE_{r,s}^{\theta}\!\left[
 \cE_{s,t}^{\theta}[\Phi]
 \right]
 =\cE_{r,t}^{\theta}[\Phi].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity and terminal stability follow from comparison.  Constants solve
the zero-cost equation.  Concatenate the DPP at time $s$, or equivalently use
uniqueness of the viscosity solution on adjacent intervals, to obtain
\eqref{eq:time-consistency}.
\end{proof}

A theta-expectation need not be sublinear.  Consider in one dimension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonconvex-H}
 H(p)=\gamma(1-\cos p)-\delta(1-\cos2p),
 \qquad \delta>\gamma/4>0.
\end{equation}
Then $H''(0)=\gamma-4\delta<0$, and for $p=q=\pi/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
 H(p+q)-H(p)-H(q)=4\delta>0.
\end{equation}
Add any uniformly elliptic diffusion field from the companion pressure paper.

\begin{proposition}[Failure of subadditivity]\label{prop:nonsubadditive}
The theta-expectation generated by \eqref{eq:nonconvex-H} is not subadditive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose smooth terminal functions whose gradients at one point are $p$, $q$,
and $p+q$, and whose Hessians vanish there.  The short-time viscosity expansion
is
\[
 \cE_{t-h,t}^{\theta}[\phi](x)
 =\phi(x)+hH(D\phi(x))+o(h).
\]
If subadditivity held, applying it to the first two functions and dividing by
$h$ would give $H(p+q)\le H(p)+H(q)$, contradicting
\eqref{eq:defect}.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

The construction has a strict causal order:
\[
 \text{spectral data}\longrightarrow
 \text{Doob selection}\longrightarrow
 \text{enhanced deterministic limit}\longrightarrow
 \text{nonautonomous diffusion}\longrightarrow
 \text{HJB and theta-expectation}.
\]
Quantitative block accumulation and direct martingale characteristics are
alternative full-scale mechanisms; qualitative WIP alone is not silently
upgraded.  The sharp Gaussian theorem identifies the exact bridge-limited rate
in a full rough Wasserstein metric, while the four-branch model supplies an
actual selected deterministic system.  The resulting nonlinear semigroup may
be nonconvex and non-subadditive, motivating the typed filtering, game, and
stochastic-representation theories of the remaining companion papers.
