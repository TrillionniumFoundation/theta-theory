\begin{abstract}
We derive a nonlinear terminal semigroup from the same nonconjugate moving
collision system used in the companion response and pressure papers.  The
central bridge is an exact innovation theorem: even when the collision
parameter is a predictable function of the slow state and previous branch
history, the current unstable coordinate is conditionally Lebesgue, the next
branch has the prescribed parameter-dependent law, and the image coordinate is
independent of the enlarged past.  Bounded collision increments therefore form
a genuine martingale triangular array generated by one deterministic
nonautonomous map.

We prove first- and second-level martingale convergence with the canonical
geometric diagonal, predictable covariance, and area anomaly.  The actual
moving-collision recursion converges on the full microscopic sequence to a
state-dependent diffusion or rough differential equation.  Response theory is
used only to differentiate frozen coefficients; it is not substituted for the
invariance principle.

The microscopic value problem is defined by a pointwise finite-branch
risk-sensitive recursion generated by the collision map.  Monotonicity,
stability, and locally uniform consistency yield convergence to the uniformly
parabolic entropic HJB equation with the covariance computed in the pressure
paper.  A controlled version produces a genuinely nonlinear risk-sensitive
HJB semigroup.  We introduce forward theta-independence and prove that the
exact collision innovations satisfy it.  The Cole--Hopf transform gives global
comparison and an exact Feynman--Kac formula for the uncontrolled branch.  No
anisotropic-pairing-to-pointwise or zero-measure contact argument occurs.

Finally, for a deterministic Gaussian Bernoulli shift we prove the matching
upper and lower rate $N^{-(1/2-\eta)}$ in Wasserstein distance associated with
a step-two fractional-Sobolev rough metric.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Frozen spectral response and long-time homogenization are different theorems.
Response differentiates invariant data with respect to a parameter.
Homogenization requires a functional limit theorem uniform along a parameter
path that is itself generated by the slow state.  The moving full-branch
collision family of the companion papers is sufficiently rigid to solve the
second problem exactly: its branch labels are predictable innovations.

The same rigidity also gives a pointwise microscopic value recursion.  At each
slow step, integration over the current fast collision coordinate equals a
finite branch sum with the exact moving widths.  Thus the prelimit is a
well-defined monotone operator on bounded continuous functions.  The viscosity
argument never evaluates an anisotropic distribution at a point and never
moves a contact away from a singular set.

Martingale approximation originates with Gordin \cite{Gordin1969}; deterministic
homogenization and rough enhancements are developed in
\cite{MelbourneStuart2011,KellyMelbourne2016,KellyMelbourne2017,
ChevyrevFrizKorepanovMelbourne2020}.  We use martingale convergence
\cite{HallHeyde1980,JacodShiryaev2003}, rough-path continuity
\cite{Lyons1998,FrizVictoir2010,FrizHairer2014}, and monotone-scheme viscosity
methods \cite{BarlesSouganidis1991,CrandallIshiiLions1992}.

\section{The exact nonautonomous innovation process}

For $a\in I=[-1/40,1/40]$, let
\[
 T_a(x)={x-s_{i-1}(a)\over w_i(a)},
 \qquad x\in I_i(a)=[s_{i-1}(a),s_i(a)),
\]
where
\[
 (w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4)
 =(1/10+a,1/5+2a,3/10-a,2/5-2a).
\]
Distinct parameters are not $C^1$ conjugate by the periodic-multiplier theorem
of the response paper \cite{QiResponseRound4}.

Let $X_0^{\rm f}$ be Lebesgue distributed.  Let $A_k\in I$ be measurable with
respect to
\[
 \F_k=\sigma(I_0,\ldots,I_{k-1}),
\]
where $I_k=i$ when $X_k^{\rm f}\in I_i(A_k)$, and set
\[
 X_{k+1}^{\rm f}=T_{A_k}(X_k^{\rm f}).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact predictable innovations]\label{thm:innovations}
For every $k\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item conditionally on $\F_k$, $X_k^{\rm f}$ is Lebesgue distributed;
\item
\[
 \Pp(I_k=i\mid\F_k)=w_i(A_k);
\]
\item conditionally on $\F_k\vee\sigma(I_k)$,
$X_{k+1}^{\rm f}$ is Lebesgue distributed and independent of that sigma-field.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the branch symbols are conditionally independent innovations with the
exact predictable probabilities $w(A_k)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The assertion holds at $k=0$.  Suppose it holds at time $k$.  Given $\F_k$,
the parameter $A_k$ is fixed and the current point is uniform.  Its probability
of lying in branch $i$ is the length $w_i(A_k)$.  Conditional on that branch,
the current point is uniform on its branch interval.  The affine full-cover map
sends normalized Lebesgue measure on that interval to Lebesgue measure on
$[0,1]$, independently of the branch.  This is the induction step.
\end{proof}

\section{Doob selection without feedback circularity}

Let $c_i(a)\in\R^d$ be smooth branch increments and let $\zeta$ be an external
tilt.  The locally constant twist has leading eigenvalue
\[
 Z(a,\zeta)=\sum_iw_i(a)e^{\zeta\cdot c_i(a)}.
\]
Its normalized Doob probabilities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob}
 p_i(a,\zeta)=
 {w_i(a)e^{\zeta\cdot c_i(a)}\over Z(a,\zeta)}.
\end{equation}
They are positive and sum to one.  Replacing the branch widths by
$p_i(a,\zeta)$ realizes the selected kernel as another full-cover collision
map, so \cref{thm:innovations} applies to predictable $(a_k,\zeta_k)$.

\begin{definition}[Causal order]
The slow state, the cotangent signal, and the tilt are external frozen inputs
when the collision kernel is constructed.  The Doob probabilities, invariant
law, martingale characteristics, and homogenized generator are obtained before
the limiting value function is solved.  In a viscosity proof a test gradient
may be substituted only after a smooth test has touched a relaxed limit.
\end{definition}

\section{A geometric martingale rough limit}

Let $(M_k^\varepsilon,\F_{k+1}^\varepsilon)$ be $\R^d$-valued martingale
differences.  Put
\begin{align}
 W^\varepsilon(t)&=\varepsilon
 \sum_{k<t/\varepsilon^2}M_k^\varepsilon,\label{eq:W}\\
 \mathbb W^\varepsilon(t)&=\varepsilon^2
 \left[
 \sum_{0\le i<j<t/\varepsilon^2}M_i^\varepsilon\otimes M_j^\varepsilon
 +{1\over2}\sum_{i<t/\varepsilon^2}
 M_i^\varepsilon\otimes M_i^\varepsilon
 \right],\label{eq:WW}
\end{align}
with the usual within-step interpolation.  The diagonal term makes this the
canonical geometric lift of the polygonal path.

\begin{assumption}[Predictable characteristics]\label{ass:chars}
On every finite horizon:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for some $q>4$, the conditional $q$th moments are uniformly bounded;
\item conditional Lindeberg holds;
\item uniformly in $t$,
\[
 \varepsilon^2\sum_{k<t/\varepsilon^2}
 \E[M_k^\varepsilon\otimes M_k^\varepsilon\mid\F_k^\varepsilon]
 \longrightarrow\int_0^t\Sigma_s\,ds
\]
in probability;
\item the predictable antisymmetric second-level compensator converges to
$\int_0^t\Gamma_sds$;
\item the continuous martingale problem with bracket
$\int_0^t\Sigma_sds$ is unique.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Nonautonomous enhanced martingale limit]\label{thm:rough}
Under \cref{ass:chars}, the canonical lifts
$(W^\varepsilon,\mathbb W^\varepsilon)$ converge in every admissible
$p$-variation topology to
\[
 \left(W,\mathbb W^{\rm Str}+\int_0^{\cdot}\Gamma_sds\right),
\]
where $W$ is the continuous Gaussian martingale with bracket
$\int_0^t\Sigma_sds$ and $\mathbb W^{\rm Str}$ is its Stratonovich lift.
Uniformly Lipschitz rough differential recursions driven by these lifts
converge to the corresponding limiting RDE.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditional Lindeberg and bracket convergence give the martingale functional
central limit theorem.  The $q>4$ estimate and discrete
Burkholder--Davis--Gundy inequalities give tightness of the first level and the
iterated sums in $p$-variation.  Discrete integration by parts gives
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}\mathbb W^\varepsilon_{s,t}
 ={1\over2}W^\varepsilon_{s,t}\otimes W^\varepsilon_{s,t},
\]
which identifies the geometric diagonal and the Stratonovich symmetric part.
The assumed predictable antisymmetric compensator gives $\Gamma$.  Uniqueness
of the martingale problem identifies every subsequential limit.  Continuity of
the It\^o--Lyons map proves the RDE assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{Endogenous diffusion from the same collision map}

Let $\Theta:\R^m\to I$ be globally Lipschitz.  For each $a$, let
$c_i(a)\in\R^m$ be bounded and smooth and put
\[
 \bar c(a)=\sum_iw_i(a)c_i(a),
 \qquad m_i(a)=c_i(a)-\bar c(a),
\]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sigma}
 \Sigma(a)=\sum_iw_i(a)m_i(a)\otimes m_i(a).
\end{equation}
Assume $\Sigma(a)\ge\lambda I_m$.  Let $b$ be globally Lipschitz and define
on the slow time $t_k=k\varepsilon^2$
\begin{align}
 A_k&=\Theta(X_k^\varepsilon),\label{eq:Ak}\\
 X_{k+1}^\varepsilon&=X_k^\varepsilon
 +\varepsilon m_{I_k}(A_k)+\varepsilon^2b(X_k^\varepsilon),
 \label{eq:slow}\\
 X_{k+1}^{\rm f}&=T_{A_k}(X_k^{\rm f}).
\end{align}
The parameter $A_k$ is predictable with respect to the branch history.

\begin{theorem}[Endogenous moving-collision homogenization]\label{thm:homogenization}
The polygonal interpolation of \eqref{eq:slow} converges in law to the unique
weak solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SDE}
 dX_t=b(X_t)dt+\sigma(\Theta(X_t))dW_t,
 \qquad \sigma(a)\sigma(a)^T=\Sigma(a).
\end{equation}
The enhanced martingale driver converges jointly with zero antisymmetric area
anomaly.  Smooth vector-field couplings converge to the corresponding
Stratonovich RDE with the canonical bracket correction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:innovations},
$M_k=m_{I_k}(A_k)$ is a bounded martingale difference and
\[
 \E[M_k\otimes M_k\mid\F_k]=\Sigma(A_k).
\]
All conditional moment and Lindeberg conditions are immediate.  Tightness of
the slow recursion and continuity of $\Theta,\Sigma$ turn the predictable
bracket into Riemann sums for
$\int_0^t\Sigma(\Theta(X_s))ds$.  A one-step innovation has no predictable
antisymmetric area.  Apply \cref{thm:rough}.  Global Lipschitz coefficients and
uniform ellipticity give uniqueness of \eqref{eq:SDE}.
\end{proof}

The covariance in \eqref{eq:Sigma} is exactly the pressure Hessian per collision
computed in the companion pressure paper \cite{QiPressureRound4}; division by
the mean roof gives the physical-time covariance.

\section{Quantitative block replacement}

Suppose a frozen enhanced WIP has a uniform law-distance rate
$\eta(N)\le CN^{-\delta}$, $\delta>0$.  A block of $m_\varepsilon$ fast steps
has duration $h_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^2m_\varepsilon$.

\begin{proposition}[Strict block window]\label{prop:block}
If
\[
 m_\varepsilon\to\infty,\qquad h_\varepsilon\to0,\qquad
 \varepsilon^{-2}m_\varepsilon^{-(1+\delta)}\to0,
\]
and the accumulated freezing and switching errors vanish, then frozen-block
replacement holds on the full sequence.  For
$m_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-\kappa}$ the window is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
 {2\over1+\delta}<\kappa<2.
\end{equation}
Qualitative uniform WIP without a rate gives only a cofinal diagonal sequence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
There are $O(h_\varepsilon^{-1})$ blocks, so the accumulated frozen-law error
is bounded by
\[
 h_\varepsilon^{-1}\eta(m_\varepsilon)
 \le C\varepsilon^{-2}m_\varepsilon^{-(1+\delta)}.
\]
The other two conditions separate fast equilibration from slow freezing.  The
power-law equivalence is an elementary exponent calculation.
\end{proof}

\section{A sharp enhanced Gaussian benchmark}

Let $\xi_k$ be the coordinate process of a deterministic Gaussian Bernoulli
shift and let $W_N$ be the polygonal interpolation of
$N^{-1/2}\sum_{k<Nt}\xi_k$, with canonical step-two lift $\mathbf W_N$.
Let $\mathbf B$ be enhanced Brownian motion.  Choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window}
 p>6,\qquad {1\over3}<\eta-{1\over p},\qquad \eta<{1\over2}.
\end{equation}
Let $d_{\eta,p}$ be the inhomogeneous rough metric formed from
$W^{\eta,p}$ at level one and $W^{2\eta,p/2}$ with square-root scaling at
level two.  Denote its Wasserstein-one distance by
$\mathcal W_1^{\eta,p}$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp fractional-Sobolev rough rate]\label{thm:rate}
There are constants $0<c_{\eta,p}\le C_{\eta,p}<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
 c_{\eta,p}N^{-(1/2-\eta)}
 \le\mathcal W_1^{\eta,p}
 \bigl(\mathcal L(\mathbf W_N),\mathcal L(\mathbf B)\bigr)
 \le C_{\eta,p}N^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple the Gaussian coordinates with Brownian mesh increments.  Then $W_N$ is
exactly the Brownian polygonal interpolation $P_NB$.  On every mesh interval,
$B-P_NB$ is a rescaled Brownian bridge.  Fractional-Sobolev scaling and the
independence of the bridges give
\[
 \E\norm{B-P_NB}_{W^{\eta,p}}
 \le CN^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\]
Chen's identity decomposes the second-level error into local bridge areas and
bridge--coarse cross terms.  Brownian scaling, the $p/2$ moment estimate, and
the square-root level-two metric give the same or a smaller order.  This proves
the upper bound.

For the lower bound, let $V_N$ be the closed subspace of mesh-polygonal paths
and define
\[
 F_N(\mathbf x)=\dist_{W^{\eta,p}}(x^1,V_N).
\]
It is one-Lipschitz for $d_{\eta,p}$ and vanishes on every $\mathbf W_N$.
The fractional Poincar\'e inequality on each mesh interval and Brownian-bridge
scaling give
$\E F_N(\mathbf B)\ge cN^{-(1/2-\eta)}$.  Kantorovich duality gives the lower
bound.
\end{proof}

\section{The microscopic entropic collision recursion}

Let $\Theta:\R^m\to I$, $b:\R^m\to\R^m$, and the centered branch increments
$m_i(a)$ be globally Lipschitz and bounded.  Fix $\vartheta\ne0$.  For $h>0$
define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Th}
 (\mathcal T_h\phi)(x)=
 {1\over\vartheta}\log
 \sum_{i=1}^4w_i(\Theta(x))
 \exp\left\{\vartheta
 \phi\bigl(x+hb(x)+\sqrt h\,m_i(\Theta(x))\bigr)\right\}.
\end{equation}
For $T=Nh$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recursion}
 u_h(T,\cdot)=\phi,\qquad
 u_h(kh,\cdot)=\mathcal T_hu_h((k+1)h,\cdot).
\end{equation}
This is the exact risk-sensitive evaluation of the deterministic collision
system under Lebesgue initial fast state.  The innovation theorem gives the
same conditional branch sum at every later step.

\begin{lemma}[Monotonicity, cash invariance, and consistency]\label{lem:consistency}
The operator $\mathcal T_h$ is monotone and
$\mathcal T_h(\phi+c)=\mathcal T_h\phi+c$.  For
$\phi\in C_b^3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consistency}
 {\mathcal T_h\phi-\phi\over h}
 \longrightarrow
 b\cdot D\phi+{1\over2}\Sigma(\Theta(x)):D^2\phi
 +{\vartheta\over2}D\phi^T\Sigma(\Theta(x))D\phi
\end{equation}
locally uniformly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity and cash invariance follow from the exponential and logarithm.
Taylor-expand the shifted test function to second order.  The branch mean of
the $\sqrt h$ term is zero.  The branch second moment gives
$\Sigma:D^2\phi/2$.  Expanding the logarithmic moment generating function adds
$\vartheta D\phi^T\Sigma D\phi/2$.  Uniform third moments control the
$O(h^{1/2})$ remainder after division by $h$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Microscopic construction of the theta-semigroup]\label{thm:HJB}
For bounded uniformly continuous terminal data, $u_h$ converges locally
uniformly to the unique bounded viscosity solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HJB}
 -u_t-b(x)\cdot Du-{1\over2}\Sigma(\Theta(x)):D^2u
 -{\vartheta\over2}Du^T\Sigma(\Theta(x))Du=0,
 \qquad u(T)=\phi.
\end{equation}
If $X$ solves
\[
 dX_s=b(X_s)ds+\sigma(\Theta(X_s))dW_s,
 \qquad \sigma\sigma^T=\Sigma,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropic}
 u(t,x)={1\over\vartheta}
 \log\E_{t,x}e^{\vartheta\phi(X_T)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The recursion is pointwise, monotone, stable, and consistent by
\cref{lem:consistency}.  The half-relaxed-limit argument therefore gives the
viscosity sub- and supersolution inequalities.  There is no distributional
residual and no singular contact selection.  Set $v=e^{\vartheta u}$.
Equation \eqref{eq:HJB} becomes the linear backward Kolmogorov equation
\[
 -v_t-b\cdot Dv-{1\over2}\Sigma(\Theta(x)):D^2v=0,
 \qquad v(T)=e^{\vartheta\phi}.
\]
Uniform ellipticity and global Lipschitz coefficients give comparison and the
Feynman--Kac formula, hence \eqref{eq:entropic} and convergence of the full
sequence.
\end{proof}

\section{Controlled theta-semigroups}

Let $U$ be compact.  For each control $\alpha\in U$, let
$a(x,\alpha)\in I$, $b(x,\alpha)$, branch increments $m_i(x,\alpha)$, and
running cost $\ell(x,\alpha)$ be globally bounded and Lipschitz, with covariance
$\Sigma(x,\alpha)\ge\lambda I$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:controlled-step}
 (\mathcal T_h^c\phi)(x)=
 \sup_{\alpha\in U}\left\{-h\ell(x,\alpha)
 +{1\over\vartheta}\log\sum_iw_i(a(x,\alpha))
 e^{\vartheta\phi(x+hb(x,\alpha)+\sqrt h\,m_i(x,\alpha))}
 \right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Controlled microscopic theta limit]\label{thm:controlled}
The terminal recursion generated by \eqref{eq:controlled-step} converges to the
unique bounded viscosity solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:controlled-HJB}
 -u_t-\sup_{\alpha\in U}\left\{
 b_\alpha\cdot Du+{1\over2}\Sigma_\alpha:D^2u
 +{\vartheta\over2}Du^T\Sigma_\alpha Du-\ell_\alpha
 \right\}=0.
\end{equation}
The limiting operators form a monotone, cash-additive, time-consistent
risk-sensitive control semigroup.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The supremum of monotone cash-invariant one-step operators has the same
properties.  Compactness of $U$ and global coefficient bounds make the Taylor
consistency uniform in the control.  Standard monotone-scheme convergence and
the global comparison theorem for uniformly elliptic HJB equations identify
the limit.  Concatenation of controls proves dynamic consistency.
\end{proof}

This controlled branch is genuinely nonlinear even before the two-player game
of the next paper.

\section{Forward theta-independence}

\begin{definition}[Forward theta-independence]\label{def:theta-independence}
Let $\mathcal G$ be a past sigma-field, let $X$ be $\mathcal G$-measurable, and
let $Y$ be a future variable.  We say that $Y$ is forward
$\vartheta$-independent of $\mathcal G$ if, for every bounded measurable
$f$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-independence}
 {1\over\vartheta}\log\E
 \left[e^{\vartheta f(X,Y)}\mid\mathcal G\right]
 =\left.
 {1\over\vartheta}\log\E e^{\vartheta f(x,Y)}
 \right|_{x=X}
\end{equation}
almost surely.
\end{definition}
The notion is directional, as is nonlinear conditional evaluation.

\begin{theorem}[Collision innovations are theta-independent]\label{thm:theta-independence}
For the predictable moving-collision process, the next branch label and the
next image coordinate are forward $\vartheta$-independent of the past in the
sense of \cref{def:theta-independence}, with the branch law determined by the
current predictable parameter.  Iterating this identity is exactly the tower
property of the microscopic recursion \eqref{eq:recursion} and of its
controlled version.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition on the past.  By \cref{thm:innovations}, the next image coordinate is
uniform and independent, and the branch probabilities are the deterministic
current widths.  Therefore the conditional exponential moment is the same
finite branch integral as if the past variable $X$ were frozen at its current
value.  Taking the logarithm gives \eqref{eq:theta-independence}.  Repeated
conditioning gives the dynamic tower identity.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

The complete path from microscopic dynamics to the HJB is now carried by one
actual nonconjugate collision system.  Its exact innovations prove the
nonautonomous martingale and rough limits; its finite branch integral defines a
pointwise prelimit; and its pressure covariance is the coefficient in the
limiting PDE.  The controlled branch and forward theta-independence give
structure beyond a renamed linear semigroup.  Every contact and comparison
argument occurs in a globally defined macroscopic state space.
