\begin{abstract}
We derive a nonlinear terminal semigroup from a deterministic product-shift
fast system in three independent steps.  First, a frozen external tilt selects
a normalized Doob kernel.  Second, a martingale rough-path theorem gives the
first and second iterated limits for predictably varying tilts; the actual
finite-branch model is verified by conditional characteristics rather than by
response differentiability.  Third, a pointwise monotone one-step evaluation
converges to a uniformly parabolic entropic Hamilton--Jacobi equation.  The
prelimit recursion is defined explicitly, so no passage from anisotropic
pairings to pointwise viscosity contacts is used.

We also prove a sharp benchmark for the enhanced invariance principle.  For a
deterministic Gaussian Bernoulli shift, polygonal partial sums converge to the
Brownian rough path at the matching upper and lower rate
$N^{-(1/2-\eta)}$ in Wasserstein--one distance associated with a step-two
fractional-Sobolev rough metric.  The upper bound uses the Brownian-grid
coupling and a separate second-level bridge estimate; the lower bound uses the
distance to the mesh-polygonal subspace.

The limiting terminal equation is written throughout in the convention
$-u_t-\mathcal Lu-H=0$.  For the actual entropic model, comparison and time
consistency follow from the Cole--Hopf transform.  The earlier claim of a
hand-planted nonconvex one-player port is removed; a genuinely game-generated
nonconvex branch is proved in the companion game paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Response of frozen spectral data and homogenization of long additive
functionals are different theorems.  The former controls changes of invariant
objects with a parameter; the latter requires an invariance principle,
second-level information, and uniformity along a varying parameter path.  We
therefore take the physical coefficients from the companion pressure paper but
prove the rough limit separately.

A second separation concerns topology.  The actual prelimit evaluation in this
paper is a scalar function of the slow state, defined by a monotone recursion.
Viscosity contact inequalities are consequently pointwise.  We do not infer
point evaluation from an anisotropic dual pairing, and we make no microscopic
a.e. identification claim.

The martingale approximation approach goes back to Gordin
\cite{Gordin1969}; deterministic diffusion limits and rough enhancements are
developed in \cite{MelbourneStuart2011,KellyMelbourne2016,KellyMelbourne2017,
ChevyrevFrizKorepanovMelbourne2020}.  We use standard martingale limit theory
\cite{HallHeyde1980,JacodShiryaev2003} and rough-path continuity
\cite{Lyons1998,FrizVictoir2010,FrizHairer2014}.  The pointwise scheme argument
is in the viscosity tradition of \cite{BarlesSouganidis1991,
CrandallIshiiLions1992}.

\section{Frozen Doob selection and the causal order}\label{sec:doob}

Let $\theta=(x,p,\zeta)$ be a frozen slow state, an external cotangent signal,
and an optional tilt.  Let $L_\theta$ be a positive transfer operator with a
simple leading eigenvalue $\lambda_\theta>0$ and positive eigenfunction
$h_\theta$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob}
 \mathsf P_\theta f
 =\lambda_\theta^{-1}h_\theta^{-1}
   L_\theta(h_\theta f).
\end{equation}
Then $\mathsf P_\theta1=1$.

\begin{definition}[No-feedback convention]\label{def:nofeedback}
The variables $(x,p,\zeta)$ in \eqref{eq:doob} are frozen external inputs.
The operator, eigenfunction, invariant law, martingale decomposition and rough
limit are constructed before any limiting value function is introduced.  In a
viscosity proof, $p$ may later be set equal to the gradient of a smooth test
function at one contact point; the unknown solution is never used to define
the microscopic dynamics.
\end{definition}

For the four-branch family with weights $w_i(a)$ and branch increments
$c_i\in\R^d$, the locally constant tilt gives the explicit probabilities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi}
 p_i(a,\zeta)=
 \frac{w_i(a)e^{\zeta\cdot c_i}}
      {\sum_jw_j(a)e^{\zeta\cdot c_j}}.
\end{equation}
This is a normalized Doob kernel, not a postulated random mechanism.

\section{A martingale rough-path theorem}\label{sec:martingale}

Let $\{\mathcal F_k^\varepsilon\}$ be a filtration and let
$M_k^\varepsilon\in\R^d$ be martingale differences.  Put
\begin{align}
 W^\varepsilon(t)&=\varepsilon
   \sum_{k<t/\varepsilon^2}M_k^\varepsilon,
 \label{eq:W}\
 \mathbb W^\varepsilon(t)&=\varepsilon^2
   \sum_{0\le i<j<t/\varepsilon^2}
   M_i^\varepsilon\otimes M_j^\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:WW}
\end{align}

\begin{assumption}[Predictable rough characteristics]\label{ass:chars}
For a finite horizon $T$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for some $q>4$,
$\sup_{\varepsilon,k}\E[\abs{M_k^\varepsilon}^q\mid
\mathcal F_{k-1}^\varepsilon]<\infty$;
\item the conditional Lindeberg condition holds;
\item uniformly in $t\le T$,
\[
 \varepsilon^2\sum_{k<t/\varepsilon^2}
 \E[M_k^\varepsilon\otimes M_k^\varepsilon\mid
 \mathcal F_{k-1}^\varepsilon]
 \longrightarrow\int_0^t\Sigma_s\,ds
\]
in probability;
\item the predictable antisymmetric correction converges to
$\int_0^t\Gamma_s\,ds$;
\item the limiting martingale problem with bracket $\int\Sigma_sds$ is
unique.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Nonautonomous martingale rough limit]\label{thm:rough}
Under \cref{ass:chars}, the step-two lifts
$(W^\varepsilon,\mathbb W^\varepsilon)$ converge in law, in every admissible
$p$-variation topology, to
\[
 (W,\mathbb W+\textstyle\int_0^{\cdot}\Gamma_sds),
\]
where $W$ is the continuous Gaussian martingale with bracket
$\int_0^t\Sigma_sds$.  If a slow recursion is a uniformly Lipschitz rough
differential equation driven by these lifts, its interpolation converges to
the corresponding limiting RDE.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The conditional Lindeberg condition and bracket convergence give the
martingale functional central limit theorem.  The $q>4$ moment bound gives
uniform Kolmogorov estimates for the first level and the discrete
Burkholder--Davis--Gundy estimate for the second level.  Hence the canonical
lifts are tight in $p$-variation.  Discrete integration by parts identifies
the symmetric second level with the It\^o/Stratonovich correction, while the
assumed predictable antisymmetric term yields $\Gamma$.  Uniqueness identifies
all subsequential limits.  Continuity of the It\^o--Lyons map gives the last
statement; see \cite{FrizVictoir2010,FrizHairer2014}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
No response derivative appears in \cref{ass:chars}.  Frozen spectral response
may prove continuity of $\Sigma$ and $\Gamma$, but it does not replace the
martingale characteristics.
\end{remark}

\section{Actual deterministic finite-branch realization}\label{sec:actualrough}

Let
\[
 \Omega=([0,1]\times\R^d)^{\mathbb Z}
\]
with product measure, and let $S:\Omega\to\Omega$ be the left shift.  This is a
deterministic measure-preserving dynamical system.  Write $U_k$ for the uniform
coordinate and $\xi_k$ for the standard Gaussian coordinate of $S^k\omega$.

Given a predictable parameter sequence $(a_k,\zeta_k)$, divide $[0,1]$ into
intervals of lengths \eqref{eq:pi}, and let $I_k$ be the interval containing
$U_k$.  Define
\[
 \bar c(a,\zeta)=\sum_ip_i(a,\zeta)c_i,
 \qquad
 M_k=c_{I_k}-\bar c(a_k,\zeta_k)+\sigma_0\xi_k,
\]
where $\sigma_0\sigma_0^T\ge\lambda_0I$.

\begin{proposition}[Direct verification of the rough packet]\label{prop:actualrough}
If $(a_k,\zeta_k)$ is predictable and its slow interpolation converges
uniformly, then $(M_k)$ satisfies \cref{ass:chars} with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cov}
 \Sigma(a,\zeta)=
 \sum_ip_i(a,\zeta)
 (c_i-\bar c)\otimes(c_i-\bar c)+\sigma_0\sigma_0^T
\end{equation}
and $\Gamma=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditionally on the past, $(U_k,\xi_k)$ has the product reference law, so
$M_k$ is a martingale difference.  Its moments are uniformly bounded on
compact $(a,\zeta)$ windows.  Conditional Lindeberg is immediate.  Its
conditional covariance is exactly \eqref{eq:cov}; continuity of
\eqref{eq:pi} turns the predictable covariance sums into Riemann sums.  The
independent increments have zero antisymmetric predictable area.  Uniform
ellipticity from $\sigma_0$ gives uniqueness of the limiting martingale
problem.
\end{proof}

Thus the actual full-scale nonautonomous limit follows directly from
\cref{thm:rough}; no unproved Berry--Esseen exponent or block accumulation is
used.

\section{Quantitative block use and its exact limitation}\label{sec:block}

For completeness, suppose a frozen enhanced WIP has a uniform law-distance
modulus $\eta(N)\to0$.  A block of $m_\varepsilon$ fast steps has physical
length $h_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^2m_\varepsilon$ and there are
$O(h_\varepsilon^{-1})$ blocks.

\begin{proposition}[Compatible block window]\label{prop:block}
The frozen block replacement is valid on the full sequence provided
\[
 m_\varepsilon\to\infty,
 \qquad h_\varepsilon\to0,
 \qquad h_\varepsilon^{-1}\eta(m_\varepsilon)\to0,
\]
and the summed freezing and switching errors vanish.  Qualitative uniform WIP
without a modulus yields only a cofinal diagonal subsequence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first two limits separate fast equilibration from slow freezing.  The third
is exactly the accumulated block-law error.  Without a modulus one may choose
successive block lengths for which the uniform error is small and then select
a compatible microscopic subsequence, but there is no estimate between those
chosen lengths.
\end{proof}

\section{A sharp Gaussian enhanced-WIP benchmark}\label{sec:rate}

Take the deterministic Gaussian Bernoulli shift and let
\[
 W_N(t)=N^{-1/2}\sum_{k<Nt}\xi_k
\]
with polygonal interpolation and canonical step-two lift $\mathbf W_N$.
Let $\mathbf B$ be enhanced Brownian motion.  Choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window}
 p>6,
 \qquad \frac13<\eta-\frac1p,
 \qquad \eta<\frac12.
\end{equation}
Let $d_{\eta,p}$ be the inhomogeneous step-two rough metric formed from
$W^{\eta,p}$ at level one and $W^{2\eta,p/2}$, with the square-root scaling, at
level two.  Denote its Wasserstein--one distance by $\mathcal W_1^{\eta,p}$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp fractional-Sobolev rough rate]\label{thm:rate}
There are constants $0<c_{\eta,p}\le C_{\eta,p}<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
 c_{\eta,p}N^{-(1/2-\eta)}
 \le
 \mathcal W_1^{\eta,p}
 \bigl(\mathcal L(\mathbf W_N),\mathcal L(\mathbf B)\bigr)
 \le
 C_{\eta,p}N^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple $\xi_k$ with Brownian mesh increments
$\sqrt N(B_{(k+1)/N}-B_{k/N})$.  Then $W_N$ is exactly the Brownian polygonal
interpolation $P_NB$.  On each mesh interval, $B-P_NB$ is a rescaled Brownian
bridge.  Fractional-Sobolev scaling and independence give
\[
 \E\norm{B-P_NB}_{W^{\eta,p}}
 \le C N^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\]
For the second level, Chen's identity decomposes the difference into local
bridge iterated integrals and bridge--coarse cross terms.  Brownian scaling,
the $p/2$ moment estimate, and the square-root level-two metric show that this
contribution has the same or a faster order.  This proves the upper bound.

For the lower bound, let $V_N$ be the closed subspace of mesh-polygonal paths
and define
\[
 F_N(\mathbf x)=\dist_{W^{\eta,p}}(x^1,V_N).
\]
It is one-Lipschitz for $d_{\eta,p}$ and vanishes on every $\mathbf W_N$.
A fractional Poincar\'e inequality on each mesh interval and Brownian-bridge
scaling give
\[
 \E F_N(\mathbf B)\ge cN^{-(1/2-\eta)}.
\]
Kantorovich duality gives the lower bound.
\end{proof}

The rate is sharp for the named law metric.  No metric-free optimality is
claimed.

\section{A pointwise deterministic prelimit evaluation}\label{sec:scheme}

Let $Y_k=c_{I_k}+\sigma_0\xi_k$ be i.i.d. centered increments with covariance
$\Sigma>0$, realized by the deterministic shift above.  Fix a bounded
Lipschitz drift $b$ and $\vartheta\ne0$.  For $h>0$, define the one-step
operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Th}
 (\mathcal T_h\phi)(x)
 =\frac1\vartheta\log\E\exp\left(
 \vartheta\phi(x+hb(x)+\sqrt h\,Y_0)\right).
\end{equation}
For terminal time $T=Nh$, define recursively
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recursion}
 u_h(T,\cdot)=\phi,
 \qquad
 u_h(kh,\cdot)=\mathcal T_hu_h((k+1)h,\cdot).
\end{equation}
This is the prelimit problem.  It acts on pointwise bounded continuous
functions; no microscopic distribution or dual density is part of its
solution concept.

\begin{lemma}[Monotonicity, cash invariance and consistency]\label{lem:scheme}
The operator $\mathcal T_h$ is monotone and satisfies
$\mathcal T_h(\phi+c)=\mathcal T_h\phi+c$.  For
$\phi\in C_b^3(\R^d)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consistency}
 \frac{\mathcal T_h\phi-\phi}{h}
 \longrightarrow
 b\cdot D\phi+rac12\tr(\Sigma D^2\phi)
 +\frac\vartheta2D\phi^T\Sigma D\phi
\end{equation}
locally uniformly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity and cash invariance follow directly from the exponential and the
logarithm.  Taylor-expand $\phi(x+hb+\sqrt hY)$ to order two.  The centered
first-order term has zero mean.  Expanding the exponential adds the quadratic
cumulant $\vartheta^2D\phi^T\Sigma D\phi/2$; division by $\vartheta$ gives
\eqref{eq:consistency}.  The third moment controls the locally uniform
remainder.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual HJB/theta limit]\label{thm:hjb}
For bounded uniformly continuous terminal data, $u_h$ converges locally
uniformly, as $h\to0$ through terminal meshes, to the unique bounded viscosity
solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hjb}
 -u_t-b\cdot Du-rac12\tr(\Sigma D^2u)
 -\frac\vartheta2Du^T\Sigma Du=0,
 \qquad u(T,\cdot)=\phi.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropic}
 u(t,x)=\frac1\vartheta
 \log\E_{t,x}\exp\bigl(\vartheta\phi(X_T)\bigr),
 \qquad
 dX_s=b(X_s)ds+\Sigma^{1/2}dW_s.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The recursion is the discrete entropic evaluation of the Euler chain.  Its
monotonicity, stability and consistency give the viscosity sub- and
supersolution inequalities by the standard half-relaxed-limit argument
\cite{BarlesSouganidis1991}.  No distributional residual is used.  For
comparison, set $v=e^{\vartheta u}$.  Equation \eqref{eq:hjb} becomes the
linear backward Kolmogorov equation
\[
 -v_t-b\cdot Dv-\tfrac12\tr(\Sigma D^2v)=0,
 \qquad v(T)=e^{\vartheta\phi}.
\]
Its Feynman--Kac formula gives \eqref{eq:entropic}, uniqueness, and therefore
full convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Entropic theta-semigroup]
For $s\le t$, set
\[
 \cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi](x)
 =\frac1\vartheta\log\E_{s,x}
 e^{\vartheta\phi(X_t)}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Semigroup properties]\label{cor:theta}
The family $\cE^{\vartheta}$ is monotone, cash additive, terminally stable,
convex for $\vartheta>0$, and time consistent:
\[
 \cE_{r,s}^{\vartheta}
 \bigl[\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]\bigr]
 =\cE_{r,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi].
\]
Its Hamiltonian vanishes at $p=0$ by construction.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
These statements follow from the conditional log-moment generating function
and the tower property.  Equivalently, they follow from comparison and
concatenation of \eqref{eq:hjb}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Nonconvexity]
The previous polynomial readout used to manufacture a nonconvex one-player
Hamiltonian is not part of this theorem.  A nonconvex Hamiltonian is derived in
the game paper by solving an explicit strong concave--convex saddle problem;
it is not inserted as a primitive port.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

The actual chain in this paper does not infer homogenization from spectral
response and does not infer pointwise viscosity inequalities from anisotropic
pairings.  The deterministic shift supplies an explicit nonautonomous
martingale array, while the scalar prelimit is a monotone pointwise recursion.
The sharp Gaussian benchmark records exactly which rough law metric is used.
The terminal sign, comparison theorem and semigroup orientation are therefore
fixed at the level of the prelimit itself.
