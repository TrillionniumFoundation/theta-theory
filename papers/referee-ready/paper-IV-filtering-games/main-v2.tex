\begin{abstract}
We separate and solve three distinct operations in a partially observed
multiscale game: filter stabilization, the order of the players' choices, and
pure versus mixed Isaacs structure.  A prediction contraction followed by a
Bayes update is contractive whenever the prediction performed between
observations dominates the Bayes expansion.  Independence of the limiting
value from the initial belief additionally requires a vanishing slow initial
layer; a bounded geometric sensitivity alone is insufficient.

Sequential lower and upper games retain their different optimization orders.
For simultaneous compact games, relaxed controls give a mixed Isaacs value.
We prove the exact compact pure-saddle criterion: a continuous game has a pure
saddle if and only if its max--min and min--max values agree.  For noncompact
controls we give a strong concave--convex theorem and a complete actual model.
The quadratic energy game
\[
 p(u+v)-\frac\mu2u^2+\frac\nu2v^2
\]
has the unique saddle $u=p/\mu$, $v=-p/\nu$ and Hamiltonian
$\frac12(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})p^2$.  When $\mu>\nu$ this Hamiltonian is concave,
so the nonconvex branch is generated by an actual two-player energy balance,
not inserted as a primitive readout.

The filtering and game limits are actualized on deterministic product shifts.
A noncompact Gaussian hidden signal is forgotten exactly after one prediction,
and a controlled Gaussian shift yields a monotone prelimit game whose terminal
values converge to the uniformly parabolic Isaacs equation.  In the actual
quadratic model comparison follows by a Cole--Hopf transform.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Partial observation and two-player order are logically independent.  A stable
filter does not imply an Isaacs equality, and a mixed Isaacs equality does not
imply a pure saddle.  The first goal of this paper is to state the three
branches without conflating them.

The second goal is actualization.  The hidden state and the game noise are
coordinates of deterministic product shifts; randomness is the invariant
reference law of those deterministic maps.  The noncompact filter forgets its
prior in one step, while the slow game has an explicit pointwise dynamic
programming recursion.  Thus no weighted Lyapunov statement is used as a
substitute for filter contraction, and no formal Hamiltonian optimization is
used as a substitute for a prelimit value theorem.

We use standard filtering ideas \cite{BainCrisan2009,AtarZeitouni1997,
vanHandel2009}, minimax theory \cite{Sion1958}, and viscosity methods for games
\cite{FlemingSouganidis1989,BuckdahnLi2008,FlemingSoner2006}.  The contribution
is the typed combination with deterministic fast dynamics, the exact
initial-layer condition, the maximal pure-saddle theorem, and the explicit
noncompact filtered game.

\section{Prediction, Bayes update, and the observation gap}\label{sec:filter}

Let $E$ be a measurable hidden-state space.  For slow state $x$ and controls
$(u,v)$, let $P_{x,u,v}$ be a prediction kernel.  An observation $y$ has
likelihood $g_y(z;x,u,v)>0$.  For a belief $\pi$, set
\begin{align}
 \mathsf{Pred}_{x,u,v}(\pi)&=\pi P_{x,u,v},\label{eq:pred}\
 \mathsf B_{y,x,u,v}(\pi)(dz)&=
 \frac{g_y(z;x,u,v)\pi(dz)}{\pi(g_y)}.
 \label{eq:Bayes}
\end{align}
Use
\[
 \norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}=
 \sup_{\abs f\le1}\abs{\mu(f)-\nu(f)}.
\]

\begin{assumption}[Uniform bounded filter packet]\label{ass:bounded-filter}
There are $0\le\rho<1$ and $0<g_-\le g_+<\infty$ such that, uniformly in all
slow states, controls and observations,
\begin{align}
 \norm{\mu P_{x,u,v}-\nu P_{x,u,v}}_{\TV}
 &\le\rho\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV},\label{eq:pred-contract}\
 g_-\le g_y(z;x,u,v)&\le g_+.
 \label{eq:likelihood-bounds}
\end{align}
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Bayes expansion]\label{lem:Bayes}
Under \eqref{eq:likelihood-bounds},
\[
 \norm{\mathsf B_g(\mu)-\mathsf B_g(\nu)}_{\TV}
 \le C_B\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV},
 \qquad C_B=2g_+/g_-.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\abs f\le1$, add and subtract $\nu(gf)/\mu(g)$:
\[
 \abs{\frac{\mu(gf)}{\mu(g)}-
       \frac{\nu(gf)}{\nu(g)}}
 \le\frac{\abs{(\mu-\nu)(gf)}}{\mu(g)}
 +\frac{\abs{\nu(gf)}\abs{(\mu-\nu)(g)}}
       {\mu(g)\nu(g)}.
\]
The two terms are bounded by $(g_+/g_-)\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Observation-gap contraction]\label{thm:filter}
Suppose $r_*$ prediction steps occur between observations and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 q=C_B\rho^{r_*}<1.
\end{equation}
Then one complete prediction--observation update contracts beliefs by $q$.
Along every fixed observation/control path,
\[
 \norm{\pi_n-\pi_n'}_{\TV}
 \le q^n\norm{\pi_0-\pi_0'}_{\TV}.
\]
The estimate is uniform over strategy trees.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Iterate \eqref{eq:pred-contract} $r_*$ times and apply
\cref{lem:Bayes}.  All constants are uniform in the choices made along the
tree.
\end{proof}

\section{The vanishing slow initial layer}\label{sec:initial}

A geometric series only gives finite sensitivity to the initial belief.  To
remove that belief from an order-one slow value, contraction must occur before
the first order-one reward is sampled.

\begin{assumption}[Initial layer]\label{ass:initial}
Before the first macroscopic reward, the filter performs
$\ell_\varepsilon$ complete updates and the corresponding slow duration is
$\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}$, where
\[
 \ell_\varepsilon\to\infty,
 \qquad
 \delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}\to0.
\]
The absolute running reward in that layer is at most
$C\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Value-level belief collapse]\label{thm:value-collapse}
Under \cref{thm:filter,ass:initial}, two values started from beliefs
$\pi,\pi'$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:value-collapse}
 \abs{V^\varepsilon(\pi)-V^\varepsilon(\pi')}
 \le C_Tq^{\ell_\varepsilon}
       \norm{\pi-\pi'}_{\TV}
 +C\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}
 +\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon$ is the accumulated slow-freezing
modulus.  If it tends to zero, the limiting value is independent of the initial
belief.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple both filters along the same observation and control tree.  At the first
macroscopic reward, \cref{thm:filter} gives the first term.  Only the initial
layer sees an uncontracted prior, and its reward gives the second term.
Thereafter, inserting at each update an intermediate filter with frozen slow
parameters yields the usual geometric convolution of the parameter moduli,
which is $\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

\section{A weighted noncompact filter}\label{sec:weighted}

For $W\ge1$, define
\[
 \norm{\mu-\nu}_W=\sup_{\abs f\le W}\abs{\mu(f)-\nu(f)}.
\]
A general weighted theorem requires a Harris contraction, preservation of a
common moment ball, a weighted Bayes bound, and a separate comparison theorem
for the limiting PDE.  These hypotheses are not consequences of one another.
We now give an actual noncompact model in which the dynamical part is exact.

Let
\[
 \Omega_H=\R^{\mathbb Z}
\]
with standard Gaussian product measure and left shift.  The hidden state at
step $n$ is $Y_n(\omega)=\omega_n$.  Its prediction kernel is
\[
 P(y,dy')=\gamma(dy'),
\]
independent of $y$, where $\gamma$ is standard Gaussian.  For observations
$o\in\{-1,1\}$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs}
 g_o(y)=\frac{1+o\epsilon_o\tanh y}{2},
 \qquad 0<\epsilon_o<1.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Actual exact noncompact filtering]\label{thm:gaussian-filter}
For $W(y)=1+y^2$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item prediction maps every prior to $\gamma$ in one step;
\item the posterior
\[
 \pi^o(dy)=\frac{g_o(y)\gamma(dy)}{\gamma(g_o)}
\]
is independent of the prior and has a uniformly bounded $W$-moment;
\item prior forgetting is exact after one prediction--observation update;
\item the slow duration of that one fast step tends to zero in the diffusive
scaling, so the initial layer is automatic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first statement follows from independence of successive product
coordinates.  Since
$(1-\epsilon_o)/2\le g_o\le(1+\epsilon_o)/2$, the posterior denominator is
uniformly positive and its Gaussian moments are uniformly finite.  The
posterior formula no longer contains the prior, proving exact forgetting.
\end{proof}

A Lyapunov estimate alone would not imply any of these conclusions.

\section{Sequential and simultaneous games}\label{sec:games}

Let player $U$ maximize and player $V$ minimize a continuous local payoff
$F(z;u,v)$, where $z$ denotes the state and jet variables.

In the sequential lower game,
\[
 H^-(z)=\sup_{u\in U}\inf_{v\in V}F(z;u,v),
\]
while the reverse order gives
\[
 H^+(z)=\inf_{v\in V}\sup_{u\in U}F(z;u,v).
\]
These are distinct equations unless an Isaacs condition is proved.

For compact $U,V$, extend $F$ bilinearly to relaxed controls:
\[
 \overline F(z;\mu,\nu)
 =\int_{U\times V}F(z;u,v)\,\mu(du)\nu(dv).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Mixed Isaacs equality]\label{thm:mixed}
For compact controls and continuous $F$,
\[
 \sup_{\mu\in\cP(U)}\inf_{\nu\in\cP(V)}\overline F
 =\inf_{\nu\in\cP(V)}\sup_{\mu\in\cP(U)}\overline F.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The relaxed-control spaces are compact convex and $\overline F$ is continuous,
concave in the maximizing variable and convex in the minimizing variable.
Apply Sion's theorem \cite{Sion1958}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Maximal compact pure-saddle criterion]\label{thm:pure-iff}
Let $U,V$ be compact and $F$ continuous.  A pure saddle exists if and only if
\[
 \max_u\min_vF(u,v)=\min_v\max_uF(u,v).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A pure saddle implies equality immediately.  Conversely, let $u_*$ attain the
max--min and $v_*$ the min--max, with common value $c$.  Then
\[
 F(u_*,v)\ge c\quad\text{for all }v,
 \qquad
 F(u,v_*)\le c\quad\text{for all }u.
\]
At $(u_*,v_*)$ the inequalities force equality, hence
$F(u,v_*)\le F(u_*,v_*)\le F(u_*,v)$.
\end{proof}

Matching pennies shows why mixed equality cannot be promoted to a universal
pure theorem.

\section{Noncompact strong concave--convex games}\label{sec:noncompact}

Let $U=\R^m$, $V=\R^n$, and assume $F(z;u,v)$ is $C^1$ in the controls,
$\mu$-strongly concave in $u$ and $\nu$-strongly convex in $v$.  Define the
saddle operator
\[
 G_z(u,v)=(-D_uF(z;u,v),D_vF(z;u,v)).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Unique noncompact pure saddle]\label{thm:strong-saddle}
Suppose $G_z$ is coercive and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-monotone}
 \langle G_z(w)-G_z(w'),w-w'\rangle
 \ge\lambda\abs{w-w'}^2
\end{equation}
for some $\lambda>0$, uniformly on the parameter set.  Then there is a unique
pure saddle $w_*(z)$, and it is continuous (Lipschitz when $G_z$ is Lipschitz
in $z$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Strong monotonicity and coercivity give a unique zero of $G_z$ by the standard
Minty argument.  Strong concavity and convexity turn that zero into the global
saddle inequalities.  Subtracting the two zero equations and using
\eqref{eq:strong-monotone} gives the parameter modulus.
\end{proof}

\section{An actual game-generated nonconvex Hamiltonian}\label{sec:quadratic}

In one dimension, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-F}
 F(p;u,v)=p(u+v)-\frac\mu2u^2+\frac\nu2v^2,
 \qquad \mu,\nu>0.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact saddle and Hamiltonian]\label{prop:quadratic}
The unique pure saddle is
\[
 u_*(p)=p/\mu,
 \qquad v_*(p)=-p/\nu,
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hquad}
 H(p)=\sup_u\inf_vF(p;u,v)
 =\frac12\left(\frac1\mu-\frac1\nu\right)p^2.
\end{equation}
The saddle operator is strongly monotone:
\[
 \langle G(w)-G(w'),w-w'\rangle
 =\mu\abs{u-u'}^2+\nu\abs{v-v'}^2.
\]
If $\mu>\nu$, then $H''<0$; the Hamiltonian is concave and therefore not
convex.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Minimizing in $v$ gives $v=-p/\nu$; maximizing in $u$ gives $u=p/\mu$.
Substitution yields \eqref{eq:Hquad}.  The monotonicity identity is direct.
\end{proof}

Thus nonconvexity is generated by the opposing quadratic energy costs and the
order of the two players.  It is not prescribed as an arbitrary polynomial in
the cotangent variable.

\section{A pointwise deterministic prelimit game}\label{sec:prelimit}

Let $\Omega_G=\R^{\mathbb Z}$ with Gaussian product measure and deterministic
left shift.  Let $\xi_k$ be its coordinates.  For $h>0$, define the one-step
operator on bounded continuous functions by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:game-scheme}
 (\mathcal S_h\phi)(x)=
 \sup_{u\in\R}\inf_{v\in\R}
 \left\{
 \E\phi(x+h(u+v)+\sqrt h\,\sigma\xi_0)
 +h\left(-\frac\mu2u^2+\frac\nu2v^2\right)
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The coercive quadratic terms make the optimizers finite.  Define the terminal
recursion $V_h(T)=\Phi$ and
$V_h(kh)=\mathcal S_hV_h((k+1)h)$.

\begin{theorem}[Actual pure-game limit]\label{thm:game-limit}
For bounded uniformly continuous terminal data, $V_h$ converges locally
uniformly to the unique bounded viscosity solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:game-PDE}
 -V_t-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}V_{xx}
 -c(V_x)^2=0,
 \qquad
 c=\frac12(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1}),
 \qquad V(T)=\Phi.
\end{equation}
The prelimit lower and upper values agree and are achieved by the pure feedback
saddle in \cref{prop:quadratic} at the consistency jet.  If $c\ne0$, comparison
follows from the transform $Z=e^{2cV/\sigma^2}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The operator \eqref{eq:game-scheme} is monotone and stable.  A second-order
Taylor expansion of a smooth test function gives
\[
 \frac{\mathcal S_h\phi-\phi}{h}
 \longrightarrow
 \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\phi_{xx}+H(\phi_x)
\]
with $H$ from \eqref{eq:Hquad}.  The half-relaxed-limit argument gives the
viscosity inequalities.  Since the local game has a pure saddle, lower and
upper consistency operators coincide.  The Cole--Hopf transform reduces
\eqref{eq:game-PDE} to the linear heat equation, giving comparison and hence
full convergence.
\end{proof}

Combining the product-shift game with the exact Gaussian filter of
\cref{thm:gaussian-filter} produces a partially observed noncompact model in
which the belief disappears before the first slow reward.  All strategy-tree
filter errors are zero after that update.

\section{Belief-state and path-state boundaries}\label{sec:belief}

If the strict contraction and initial-layer conditions do not remove the
belief, the Markov state is $(x,\pi)$ and the DPP must be written on the belief
space.  If no sufficient Markov statistic exists, the state is a path and the
limit is a PPDE.  We do not project either problem to a finite-dimensional HJB
without a proved sufficient statistic.

A general weighted theorem additionally requires comparison in its declared
growth class.  In the actual Gaussian model, comparison reduces to
\cref{thm:game-limit} because the belief is forgotten exactly.

\section{Conclusion}

Filtering, sequential order, relaxed minimax and pure saddles are four distinct
claims.  The observation-gap theorem and the vanishing initial layer control
the first; the DPP preserves the second; Sion's theorem controls the third; and
max--min equality or strong concave--convex structure controls the fourth.  The
actual product-shift model verifies both the noncompact filter and the pure
nonconvex game without hidden compactness or a hand-selected cotangent
polynomial.
