\begin{abstract}
We prove positive filtering and pure-strategy Isaacs theorems for partially
observed deterministic multiscale games.  The hidden state is a noncompact
Gaussian refresh--autoregression realized on a deterministic product shift.
The refresh component gives a strict total-variation contraction; the
autoregressive component gives a quadratic Lyapunov drift.  Bounded
nondegenerate observations preserve an explicit posterior moment ball.  After
finitely many predictions between observations, the Bayes expansion is
dominated and the filter contracts uniformly over all strategy trees.  A
vanishing slow initial layer removes the initial belief from the macroscopic
value.

For pure games we introduce a curvature-compensation theorem.  A general
$C^2$ mechanical payoff is supplemented by the actual quadratic energies of
the maximizing and minimizing actuators.  If these energy curvatures dominate
the adverse control Hessians, the saddle operator is strongly monotone; mixed
Hessian terms cancel.  On closed convex control sets the associated strongly
monotone variational inequality has a unique solution, which is exactly the
pure saddle and depends Lipschitz continuously on the state and jet.

The common nonconjugate moving-cut system supplies the predictable innovations
and diffusion tensor.  Lower and upper semi-Lagrangian recursions have the same
pure-saddle consistency Hamiltonian.  In the explicit quadratic energy game,
the Hamiltonian is
$\frac12(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})p^2$; it is concave when $\mu>\nu$, so the
nonconvex branch is generated by the two players' physical energies.  A
Cole--Hopf transform proves global comparison and convergence, and completion
of squares gives a continuous-time pure feedback saddle.  Combining this game
with the refresh filter yields a fully actual partially observed noncompact
model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Filter stability, player order, mixed minimax, and a pure saddle are different
claims.  A stable filter does not imply an Isaacs equality; mixed minimax does
not produce a pure feedback.  Here both the filtering and pure-game branches
are proved on explicit positive classes and then combined with the deterministic
moving-cut innovations of Papers I--III.

The filtering argument uses standard ideas from \cite{BainCrisan2009,
AtarZeitouni1997,vanHandel2009}.  The pure-game and viscosity arguments are in
the tradition of \cite{Sion1958,FlemingSouganidis1989,FlemingSoner2006,
BuckdahnLi2008}.  The new ingredients are the exact refresh--Bayes packet, the
strongly monotone curvature-compensation theorem, and their common actual
multiscale realization.

\section{Prediction, observation, and Bayes expansion}\label{sec:Bayes}
Let $E$ be a measurable hidden state space.  For slow state $x$ and controls
$(u,v)$, let $P_{x,u,v}$ be a prediction kernel and let the observation $o$ have
likelihood $g_o(y;x,u,v)>0$.  For a belief $\pi$, set
\begin{align}
 \mathsf{Pred}_{x,u,v}(\pi)&=\pi P_{x,u,v},\label{eq:pred}\\
 \mathsf B_{o,x,u,v}(\pi)(dy)&=
 {g_o(y;x,u,v)\pi(dy)\over\pi(g_o)}.
 \label{eq:Bayes}
\end{align}
Use
\[
 \norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}=
 \sup_{\abs f\le1}\abs{\mu(f)-\nu(f)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Bayes Lipschitz bound]\label{lem:Bayes}
If $0<g_-\le g\le g_+<\infty$, then
\[
 \norm{\mathsf B_g(\mu)-\mathsf B_g(\nu)}_{\TV}
 \le C_B\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV},
 \qquad C_B={2g_+\over g_-}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\abs f\le1$, insert $\nu(gf)/\mu(g)$ between the two normalized
expectations.  The numerator difference and the denominator difference are
each bounded by $g_+\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}$ and every denominator is at least
$g_-$.  Taking the supremum gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{A noncompact refresh--autoregressive filter}\label{sec:filter}
Let $\gamma=N(0,1)$, choose $0<\delta<1$ and $\abs r<1$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 P(y,\cdot)=\delta\gamma+(1-\delta)N(ry,1-r^2).
\end{equation}
The kernel is realized by a deterministic two-sided product shift carrying a
Bernoulli refresh coordinate and a Gaussian innovation coordinate.  Put
$W(y)=1+y^2$ and $\alpha=(1-\delta)r^2$.

\begin{lemma}[Strict prediction contraction and Lyapunov drift]\label{lem:drift}
For all probability laws $\mu,\nu$,
\begin{align}
 \norm{\mu P-\nu P}_{\TV}
 &\le(1-\delta)\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV},
 \label{eq:TV}\\
 PW(y)&=\alpha W(y)+2(1-\alpha).
 \label{eq:Lyapunov}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The common refresh law cancels in the signed difference; the remaining Markov
kernel is a total-variation contraction.  Direct calculation gives
$\E[Y_{n+1}^2\mid Y_n=y]=1-\alpha+\alpha y^2$, which is
\eqref{eq:Lyapunov}.
\end{proof}

For $o\in\{-1,1\}$ choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs}
 g_o(y)={1+o\epsilon_o\tanh y\over2},
 \qquad0<\epsilon_o<1.
\end{equation}
Thus $g_\pm=(1\pm\epsilon_o)/2$.

\begin{theorem}[Noncompact posterior contraction]\label{thm:filter}
Choose $r_*\in\N$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gates}
 q:=C_B(1-\delta)^{r_*}<1,
 \qquad
 \kappa:={g_+\over g_-}\alpha^{r_*}<1.
\end{equation}
Then one prediction--observation cycle contracts total variation by $q$ and
preserves the moment ball
\[
 \mathcal M_R=\{\pi:\pi(W)\le R\}
\]
for every
\[
 R\ge {2g_+/g_-\over1-\kappa}.
\]
For two filters driven by the same controls and observations,
\[
 \norm{\pi_n-\pi_n'}_{\TV}
 \le q^n\norm{\pi_0-\pi_0'}_{\TV}.
\]
The constants are uniform on compact coefficient families preserving the two
strict inequalities in \eqref{eq:gates}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After $r_*$ predictions, \eqref{eq:TV} gives the factor
$(1-\delta)^{r_*}$; apply \cref{lem:Bayes}.  Iterating
\eqref{eq:Lyapunov} gives $P^{r_*}W\le\alpha^{r_*}W+2$.  Bayes normalization
satisfies
$\mathsf B_g(\pi)(W)\le(g_+/g_-)\pi(W)$, so a cycle maps a moment $M$ to at
most $\kappa M+2g_+/g_-$.  This proves the invariant ball and the contraction
iteration.
\end{proof}

The model is genuinely noncompact and nontrivial: without a refresh, the
hidden state retains autoregressive memory, and without the prediction gap the
Bayes map need not contract.

\section{Slow initial layer and value collapse}\label{sec:initial}
Let $\ell_\varepsilon$ full filter cycles occur before the first macroscopic
reward, during slow time $\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}$.

\begin{theorem}[Strategy-tree value collapse]\label{thm:collapse}
Assume
\[
 \ell_\varepsilon\to\infty,
 \qquad\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}\to0,
\]
and that the absolute reward accumulated in the initial layer is at most
$C\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}$.  If the prediction and observation kernels
have a slow-parameter modulus whose geometric convolution is
$\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon\to0$, then
\[
 \abs{V^\varepsilon(\pi)-V^\varepsilon(\pi')}
 \le C_Tq^{\ell_\varepsilon}
 \norm{\pi-\pi'}_{\TV}
 +C\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}
 +\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon.
\]
The estimate holds uniformly over pure strategy trees.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple the two filters along the same tree.  At the first order-one reward their
distance is at most $q^{\ell_\varepsilon}$ times the initial distance.  Only
the vanishing initial layer sees an uncontracted prior.  Subsequent slow
perturbations satisfy an affine recursion with contraction factor $q$, whose
iteration is the declared geometric convolution.
\end{proof}

\section{Curvature compensation as a variational inequality}\label{sec:VI}
Let $U\subset\R^m$ and $V\subset\R^n$ be nonempty closed convex sets and
$K=U\times V$.  Let $F_0(z,u,v)$ be $C^2$ in the controls and assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hessian}
 D_{uu}^2F_0\le L_u I,
 \qquad
 D_{vv}^2F_0\ge-L_v I.
\end{equation}
Introduce actuator energies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 F(z,u,v)=F_0(z,u,v)-{\mu\over2}\abs u^2
 +{\nu\over2}\abs v^2,
 \qquad\mu>L_u,\quad\nu>L_v.
\end{equation}
Define
\[
 G_z(u,v)=(-D_uF(z,u,v),D_vF(z,u,v)).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Curvature-compensation pure-saddle theorem]\label{thm:VI}
The operator $G_z$ is strongly monotone on $K$ with constant
\[
 \lambda=\min\{\mu-L_u,\nu-L_v\}>0.
\]
If $G_z$ is continuous and coercive on $K$, there is a unique
$w_*(z)=(u_*,v_*)\in K$ satisfying the variational inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VI}
 \inner{G_z(w_*)}{w-w_*}\ge0
 \qquad\hbox{for all }w\in K.
\end{equation}
This $w_*$ is the unique pure saddle:
\[
 F(z,u,v_*)\le F(z,u_*,v_*)\le F(z,u_*,v)
 \qquad(u\in U,\ v\in V).
\]
If $G_z$ is Lipschitz in $z$, then
\[
 \abs{w_*(z)-w_*(z')}
 \le {1\over\lambda}
 \abs{G_z(w_*(z'))-G_{z'}(w_*(z'))}.
\]
Consequently the lower and upper Hamiltonians agree and have a unique pure
selector.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For an increment $(\dot u,\dot v)$, the symmetric quadratic form of $DG_z$ is
\[
 -\dot u^TD_{uu}^2F\dot u+
 \dot v^TD_{vv}^2F\dot v.
\]
The mixed Hessian terms cancel because
$D_{vu}^2F=(D_{uv}^2F)^T$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:hessian}--\eqref{eq:energy} give the lower bound
$\lambda(\abs{\dot u}^2+\abs{\dot v}^2)$, hence strong monotonicity.
Continuity, coercivity, and standard strongly monotone variational-inequality
theory give the unique solution of \eqref{eq:VI}.  Testing separately with
$(u,v_*)$ and $(u_*,v)$ gives the first-order optimality inequalities; strong
concavity in $u$ and strong convexity in $v$ upgrade them to the global saddle
inequalities.  Apply the two variational inequalities at $z,z'$ with the other
solution as test point, add them, and use strong monotonicity to obtain the
Lipschitz estimate.
\end{proof}

Thus actual actuator energies enforce a pure Isaacs structure for a broad
$C^2$ mechanical class.

\section{The explicit nonconvex energy game}\label{sec:quadratic}
In one dimension let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localgame}
 F(p;u,v)=p(u+v)-{\mu\over2}u^2+{\nu\over2}v^2,
 \qquad \mu,\nu>0.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact saddle]\label{prop:saddle}
The unique pure saddle is
\[
 u_*(p)=p/\mu,
 \qquad v_*(p)=-p/\nu,
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H(p)=\sup_u\inf_vF(p;u,v)
 ={1\over2}(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})p^2.
\end{equation}
If $\mu>\nu$, then $H$ is concave and therefore not convex.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first-order equations give the displayed controls; strict
concavity--convexity makes them the global saddle.  Substitution gives
\eqref{eq:H}.
\end{proof}

The nonconvexity is generated by the relative physical energy costs, not by a
prescribed cotangent polynomial.

\section{Moving-cut lower and upper schemes}\label{sec:schemes}
Let $m_i(a)$ be the centered branch increments of Paper III and assume
\[
 \Sigma(a)=\sum_iw_i(a)m_i(a)^2\ge\lambda_0>0.
\]
For globally Lipschitz $\Theta$, let $a=\Theta(x)$.  Define
\begin{align}
 (\mathcal S_h^-\phi)(x)&=
 \sup_{u\in\R}\inf_{v\in\R}\left\{
 \sum_iw_i(a)
 \phi(x+h(u+v)+\sqrt h\,m_i(a))
 +h\left(-{\mu\over2}u^2+{\nu\over2}v^2\right)
 \right\},\label{eq:lower}\\
 (\mathcal S_h^+\phi)(x)&=
 \inf_{v\in\R}\sup_{u\in\R}\left\{
 \sum_iw_i(a)
 \phi(x+h(u+v)+\sqrt h\,m_i(a))
 +h\left(-{\mu\over2}u^2+{\nu\over2}v^2\right)
 \right\}.
 \label{eq:upper}
\end{align}

\begin{lemma}[Common local consistency]\label{lem:consistency}
For $\phi\in C_b^3$, the optimizers remain uniformly bounded on compact jet
sets and
\[
 {\mathcal S_h^\pm\phi-\phi\over h}
 \longrightarrow {1\over2}\Sigma(\Theta(x))\phi_{xx}+H(\phi_x)
\]
locally uniformly, with $H$ from \eqref{eq:H}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The quadratic energy terms dominate the $O(h\abs u+h\abs v)$ shift in a
smooth test, localizing the optimizers.  Taylor expansion, branch centering,
and the covariance identity give the diffusion term.  The remaining control
optimization is exactly \eqref{eq:localgame}, whose lower and upper pure values
coincide.
\end{proof}

Let $V_h^\pm(T)=\Phi$ and iterate $\mathcal S_h^\pm$ backwards.

\begin{theorem}[Actual pure lower--upper limit]\label{thm:scheme}
For bounded uniformly continuous $\Phi$, both $V_h^-$ and $V_h^+$ converge
locally uniformly to the same unique bounded viscosity solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PDE}
 -V_t-{1\over2}\Sigma(\Theta(x))V_{xx}-c(V_x)^2=0,
 \qquad c={1\over2}(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1}),
 \qquad V(T)=\Phi.
\end{equation}
Thus the limit is a pure-strategy Isaacs value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both schemes are monotone and stable, and \cref{lem:consistency} gives the same
viscosity equation for their half-relaxed limits.  Let
\[
 A(x)=\Sigma(\Theta(x))/2.
\]
In the constant-covariance subfamily used below, the Cole--Hopf transform is
exact.  For general one-dimensional uniformly elliptic $A(x)$, the equation is
proper after the standard exponential-in-time change and its Hamiltonian is
locally Lipschitz with quadratic coercivity; the doubling-variable argument,
using bounded solutions and uniform ellipticity, gives comparison.  Hence the
half-relaxed limits coincide.  In particular, the fully explicit constant
$\Sigma$ branch has the independent proof in \cref{thm:feedback}.
\end{proof}

\section{Continuous-time pure feedback verification}\label{sec:feedback}
Fix a constant $\sigma>0$ and consider
\[
 dX_s=(u_s+v_s)ds+\sigma dW_s,
\]
with payoff
\[
 J_{t,x}(u,v)=\E_{t,x}\left[
 \Phi(X_T)+\int_t^T
 \left(-{\mu\over2}u_s^2+{\nu\over2}v_s^2\right)ds\right].
\]
Let $c=\frac12(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})$.

\begin{theorem}[Global value and pure feedback saddle]\label{thm:feedback}
For bounded Lipschitz $\Phi$,
\[
 V(t,x)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle {\sigma^2\over2c}
 \log\E_{t,x}\exp\left({2c\over\sigma^2}
 \Phi(x+\sigma(W_T-W_t))\right),&c\ne0,\\[2ex]
 \E_{t,x}\Phi(x+\sigma(W_T-W_t)),&c=0,
 \end{cases}
\]
is the unique bounded viscosity solution of
\[
 -V_t-{\sigma^2\over2}V_{xx}-cV_x^2=0.
\]
For $t<T$, the feedbacks
\[
 u_s^*=V_x(s,X_s)/\mu,
 \qquad v_s^*=-V_x(s,X_s)/\nu
\]
form a pure saddle:
\[
 J(u,v^*)\le J(u^*,v^*)\le J(u^*,v).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The transform $Z=e^{2cV/\sigma^2}$ reduces the equation to the heat equation,
which gives the formula, smoothness for $t<T$, and comparison.  It\^o's formula
and
\[
 -cp^2+p(u+v)-{\mu\over2}u^2+{\nu\over2}v^2
 =-{\mu\over2}(u-p/\mu)^2
 +{\nu\over2}(v+p/\nu)^2
\]
give the two saddle inequalities after integration.
\end{proof}

\section{End-to-end partially observed model}\label{sec:endtoend}
Take the product of the nonconjugate moving-cut fast system and the hidden
refresh--autoregression.  Let the hidden state enter the bounded running reward
through a bounded Lipschitz function $r(x,u,v,y)$ and let controls perturb the
filter coefficients inside a compact family preserving \eqref{eq:gates}.
Let the slow noise and state-dependent covariance be supplied by the exact
moving-cut innovations.

\begin{theorem}[Partially observed noncompact pure game]\label{thm:endtoend}
Under the strict filter gates, the initial-layer scaling, and the
curvature-compensation margins:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item posterior laws contract uniformly over every pure strategy tree and stay
in one quadratic moment ball;
\item the initial belief disappears from the limiting value;
\item the slow process has the control-uniform diffusion characteristics of
Paper III;
\item lower and upper prelimit values converge to the same pure-strategy
Isaacs solution;
\item in the quadratic constant-covariance branch, the limit and pure feedback
saddle are those of \cref{thm:feedback}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items (i)--(ii) are \cref{thm:filter,thm:collapse}.  Exact moving-cut
innovations give the predictable martingale characteristics uniformly over the
admissible control family.  The common pure local saddle and monotone schemes
give (iv), and \cref{thm:feedback} gives (v).  The bounded hidden running term
is stable under the posterior contraction and therefore does not alter the
initial-belief collapse.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}
Refresh produces strict noncompact filter stability without erasing hidden
memory.  Curvature compensation produces a unique pure saddle without
assuming minimax equality.  Combined with the exact moving-cut innovations,
these mechanisms give an actual partially observed deterministic multiscale
game with a pure Isaacs limit.
