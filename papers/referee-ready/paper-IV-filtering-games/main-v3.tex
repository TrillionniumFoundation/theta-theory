\begin{abstract}
We prove a positive pure-strategy and filtering theorem for partially observed
deterministic multiscale games.  The hidden state is a noncompact Gaussian
refresh--autoregression realized on a deterministic product shift.  Its
prediction kernel has a strict total-variation contraction supplied by the
refresh component and a quadratic Lyapunov drift supplied by the autoregressive
component.  Bounded nondegenerate observations preserve an explicit posterior
moment ball.  After finitely many predictions between observations, the Bayes
expansion is dominated and the nonlinear filter contracts uniformly over all
strategy trees.  A vanishing slow initial layer then removes the initial belief
from the macroscopic value.

For pure games we introduce a curvature-compensation principle.  Start from a
general $C^2$ mechanical payoff and add the actual quadratic energies of the
maximizing and minimizing actuators.  If the energy curvatures dominate the
unfavourable control Hessians, the saddle operator is strongly monotone; mixed
Hessian terms cancel exactly.  The game has a unique pure saddle and a
Lipschitz feedback selector.  Lower and upper semi-Lagrangian schemes have the
same consistency Hamiltonian and converge to the same Isaacs solution.

The common moving-cut fast system of the companion papers supplies the
predictable innovations and state-dependent covariance.  In the explicit
one-dimensional energy game the Hamiltonian is
$\frac12(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})p^2$; it is concave when $\mu>\nu$, so nonconvexity
is generated by the opposing actuator energies.  Cole--Hopf gives global
comparison, and a completion-of-squares verification gives an actual pure
feedback saddle for the continuous-time game.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Filtering, player order, mixed minimax, and pure saddles are distinct
operations.  This paper proves all four on explicit positive classes.  The
noncompact filter is not reduced to exact one-step forgetting: it has genuine
memory, a strict contraction rate, and a nontrivial observation update.  The
pure saddle is not assumed: it is produced by a verifiable mechanical energy
condition.

The fast innovations are the exact branch symbols of the nonconjugate
moving-cut map constructed in Paper I.  Paper III proves their nonautonomous
rough limit.  Thus the partially observed game uses the same deterministic
fast platform as the response, pressure, and HJB papers.

We use standard filtering methods \cite{BainCrisan2009,AtarZeitouni1997,
vanHandel2009}, minimax theory \cite{Sion1958}, and viscosity methods for
differential games \cite{FlemingSouganidis1989,FlemingSoner2006,
BuckdahnLi2008}.  The new ingredients are the explicit refresh--Bayes packet,
the curvature-compensation theorem, and their exact deterministic
actualization.

\section{Prediction and Bayes update}\label{sec:filter}

Let $E$ be a measurable hidden-state space.  For slow state $x$ and controls
$(u,v)$, let $P_{x,u,v}$ be a prediction kernel.  An observation $y$ has
likelihood $g_y(z;x,u,v)>0$.  For a belief $\pi$, define
\begin{align}
 \mathsf{Pred}_{x,u,v}(\pi)&=\pi P_{x,u,v},\label{eq:pred}\\
 \mathsf B_{y,x,u,v}(\pi)(dz)&=
 \frac{g_y(z;x,u,v)\pi(dz)}{\pi(g_y)}.
 \label{eq:Bayes}
\end{align}
Use
\[
 \norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}
 =\sup_{\abs f\le1}\abs{\mu(f)-\nu(f)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Bayes expansion]\label{lem:Bayes}
If $0<g_-\le g\le g_+<\infty$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bayes-Lip}
 \norm{\mathsf B_g(\mu)-\mathsf B_g(\nu)}_{\TV}
 \le C_B\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV},
 \qquad C_B=2g_+/g_-.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\abs f\le1$, add and subtract $\nu(gf)/\mu(g)$:
\[
 \abs{\frac{\mu(gf)}{\mu(g)}-
       \frac{\nu(gf)}{\nu(g)}}
 \le\frac{\abs{(\mu-\nu)(gf)}}{\mu(g)}
 +\frac{\abs{\nu(gf)}\abs{(\mu-\nu)(g)}}
       {\mu(g)\nu(g)}.
\]
Both terms are at most $(g_+/g_-)\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}$.
\end{proof}

\section{An actual noncompact refresh--autoregressive filter}\label{sec:refresh}

Let $\gamma=N(0,1)$, choose $0<\delta<1$ and $\abs r<1$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refresh-kernel}
 P(y,\cdot)=\delta\gamma
 +(1-\delta)N(ry,1-r^2).
\end{equation}
This kernel is realized by a deterministic product shift carrying a Bernoulli
refresh coordinate and a Gaussian innovation coordinate.  Let
$W(y)=1+y^2$.

\begin{lemma}[Prediction contraction and drift]\label{lem:refresh}
For all probability measures $\mu,\nu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TV-contract}
 \norm{\mu P-\nu P}_{\TV}
 \le(1-\delta)\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, with $\alpha=(1-\delta)r^2<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift}
 PW(y)=\alpha W(y)+2(1-\alpha).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The common refresh component cancels in the difference.  The remaining
Markov kernel is a contraction in total variation, proving
\eqref{eq:TV-contract}.  A direct Gaussian second-moment calculation gives
\[
 \E[Y_{n+1}^2\mid Y_n=y]
 =1-\alpha+\alpha y^2,
\]
which is equivalent to \eqref{eq:drift}.
\end{proof}

Use observations $o\in\{-1,1\}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:likelihood}
 g_o(y)=\frac{1+o\epsilon_o\tanh y}{2},
 \qquad0<\epsilon_o<1.
\end{equation}
Thus
\[
 g_-=(1-\epsilon_o)/2,
 \qquad g_+=(1+\epsilon_o)/2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Noncompact posterior contraction]\label{thm:noncompact-filter}
Choose an integer $r_*$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-gates}
 q:=C_B(1-\delta)^{r_*}<1,
 \qquad
 \kappa:=\frac{g_+}{g_-}\alpha^{r_*}<1.
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item one prediction--observation cycle contracts total variation by $q$;
\item there is an explicit $R<\infty$ such that the moment ball
$\{\pi:\pi(W)\le R\}$ is invariant under the cycle;
\item on every common observation/control path,
\[
 \norm{\pi_n-\pi_n'}_{\TV}
 \le q^n\norm{\pi_0-\pi_0'}_{\TV};
\]
\item all constants remain valid uniformly when $\delta,r,\epsilon_o$ and
bounded control-dependent perturbations range in a compact set preserving
\eqref{eq:two-gates}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After $r_*$ predictions, \eqref{eq:TV-contract} gives
$(1-\delta)^{r_*}$; apply \cref{lem:Bayes} to obtain $q$.
Iterating \eqref{eq:drift} gives
\[
 P^{r_*}W\le\alpha^{r_*}W+2.
\]
The Bayes update satisfies
\[
 \mathsf B_g(\pi)(W)
 \le\frac{g_+}{g_-}\pi(W).
\]
Hence one cycle maps a moment $M$ to at most $\kappa M+2g_+/g_-$.  Any
\[
 R\ge\frac{2g_+/g_-}{1-\kappa}
\]
is invariant.  Iterating the strict TV contraction proves (iii); compact
uniformity proves (iv).
\end{proof}

This is a genuine noncompact, nontrivial filter: absent a refresh, the state
retains autoregressive memory; absent the prediction gap, the Bayes map can
expand distances.

\section{The slow initial layer}\label{sec:initial}

Let $\ell_\varepsilon$ complete filter cycles occur before the first order-one
slow reward, during a slow time $\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}$.

\begin{theorem}[Value-level belief collapse]\label{thm:value-collapse}
Assume
\[
 \ell_\varepsilon\to\infty,
 \qquad\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}\to0,
\]
and that the running reward in the initial layer is bounded by
$C\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}$.  Then two strategy values started from
$\pi,\pi'$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:value-collapse}
 \abs{V^\varepsilon(\pi)-V^\varepsilon(\pi')}
 \le C_Tq^{\ell_\varepsilon}
 \norm{\pi-\pi'}_{\TV}
 +C\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}
 +\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon$ is the geometric convolution of the
slow-freezing moduli.  If that error vanishes, the limiting value is
independent of the initial belief.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple the filters along the same observation and strategy tree.  At the first
macroscopic reward the belief distance is at most
$q^{\ell_\varepsilon}\norm{\pi-\pi'}_{\TV}$.  The only reward that sees the
uncontracted prior lies in the initial layer.  Subsequent parameter
perturbations satisfy an affine recursion with contraction factor $q$; its
iteration gives the geometric convolution term.
\end{proof}

\section{Curvature compensation and pure saddles}\label{sec:curvature}

Let $U\subset\R^m$ and $V\subset\R^n$ be closed convex control sets.  Let
$F_0(z,u,v)$ be $C^2$ in the controls and suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hessian-bounds}
 D_{uu}^2F_0(z,u,v)\le L_uI,
 \qquad
 D_{vv}^2F_0(z,u,v)\ge-L_vI.
\end{equation}
Introduce the physical actuator energies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compensated-payoff}
 F(z,u,v)=F_0(z,u,v)-\frac\mu2\abs u^2+
 \frac\nu2\abs v^2,
 \qquad \mu>L_u,\quad\nu>L_v.
\end{equation}
Define the saddle operator
\[
 G_z(u,v)=(-D_uF(z,u,v),D_vF(z,u,v)).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Curvature-compensation pure Isaacs theorem]\label{thm:curvature}
Assume \eqref{eq:Hessian-bounds}, the strict inequalities in
\eqref{eq:compensated-payoff}, and coercivity of $G_z$ on $U\times V$.
Then $G_z$ is strongly monotone with constant
\[
 \lambda=\min\{\mu-L_u,\nu-L_v\}>0.
\]
Consequently the game has a unique pure saddle $(u_*(z),v_*(z))$.  If
$G_z$ is Lipschitz in $z$, the saddle selector is Lipschitz:
\[
 \abs{w_*(z)-w_*(z')}
 \le\lambda^{-1}
 \abs{G_z(w_*(z'))-G_{z'}(w_*(z'))}.
\]
The lower and upper Hamiltonians agree and equal
$F(z,u_*(z),v_*(z))$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $w=(u,v)$ and an increment $(\dot u,\dot v)$, the symmetric quadratic
form of $DG_z$ is
\[
 -\dot u^TD_{uu}^2F\dot u+
 \dot v^TD_{vv}^2F\dot v.
\]
The mixed terms cancel because
$D_{vu}^2F=(D_{uv}^2F)^T$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:Hessian-bounds}--\eqref{eq:compensated-payoff} therefore give the
lower bound $\lambda(\abs{\dot u}^2+\abs{\dot v}^2)$.  Integration along the
segment proves strong monotonicity.  Coercivity and the Minty argument give a
unique zero.  Strong concavity in $u$ and strong convexity in $v$ turn the zero
into the global saddle.  Comparing the two zero equations gives the Lipschitz
estimate.
\end{proof}

This theorem is a positive design principle: sufficiently strong, physically
specified actuator energies force a pure Isaacs structure for a broad $C^2$
mechanical game.

\section{An actual game-generated nonconvex Hamiltonian}\label{sec:quadratic}

In one dimension take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic}
 F(p;u,v)=p(u+v)-\frac\mu2u^2+
 \frac\nu2v^2,
 \qquad\mu,\nu>0.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact energy saddle]\label{prop:energy}
The unique saddle is
\[
 u_*(p)=p/\mu,
 \qquad v_*(p)=-p/\nu,
\]
and the Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H(p)=\frac12\left(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1}\right)p^2.
\end{equation}
If $\mu>\nu$, then $H''<0$: the Hamiltonian is concave and hence nonconvex as
a function required to be convex in a sublinear expectation theory.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first-order equations give the displayed controls.  Strong
concavity--convexity makes them the global saddle, and substitution gives
\eqref{eq:H}.
\end{proof}

The sign is generated by the relative physical energy curvatures of the two
players, not inserted as a polynomial readout.

\section{Lower and upper deterministic schemes}\label{sec:schemes}

Let $m_i(a)$ be the centered branch increments of the common moving-cut system,
with covariance $\Sigma(a)\ge\lambda_0I$.  Let
$a=\Theta(x)$ and let $f(x,u,v)$ and $\ell(x,u,v)$ satisfy the hypotheses of
\cref{thm:curvature}.  Define
\begin{align}
 (\mathcal S_h^-\phi)(x)&=
 \sup_u\inf_v\left\{
 \sum_iw_i(a)
 \phi(x+h f(x,u,v)+\sqrt h\,m_i(a))
 +h\ell(x,u,v)\right\},\label{eq:lower-scheme}\\
 (\mathcal S_h^+\phi)(x)&=
 \inf_v\sup_u\left\{
 \sum_iw_i(a)
 \phi(x+h f(x,u,v)+\sqrt h\,m_i(a))
 +h\ell(x,u,v)\right\}.
 \label{eq:upper-scheme}
\end{align}

\begin{lemma}[Common consistency Hamiltonian]\label{lem:scheme-consistency}
For every smooth test function with bounded first three derivatives, the
optimizers in \eqref{eq:lower-scheme}--\eqref{eq:upper-scheme} remain in a
compact set independent of small $h$, and
\[
 \frac{\mathcal S_h^\pm\phi-\phi}{h}
 \longrightarrow
 \frac12\Sigma(\Theta(x)):D^2\phi+
 H(x,D\phi),
\]
locally uniformly, where $H$ is the common pure-saddle Hamiltonian supplied by
\cref{thm:curvature}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The quadratic actuator energies dominate the $O(h\abs u+h\abs v)$ shift in a
smooth test and localize the optimizers.  Taylor expansion, branch centering,
and the covariance identity give the diffusion term.  The remaining
first-order control payoff is exactly the local game in
\cref{thm:curvature}; its lower and upper values agree.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual lower--upper Isaacs convergence]\label{thm:scheme-limit}
Let the terminal payoff be bounded and uniformly continuous.  Assume global
Lipschitz coefficients, uniform ellipticity, and the curvature-compensation
margins.  The lower and upper terminal recursions generated by
$\mathcal S_h^-$ and $\mathcal S_h^+$ converge locally uniformly to the same
unique viscosity solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Isaacs}
 -V_t-\frac12\Sigma(\Theta(x)):D^2V-H(x,DV)=0,
 \qquad V(T)=\Phi.
\end{equation}
The limit is therefore a pure-strategy Isaacs value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both schemes are monotone and stable.  By
\cref{lem:scheme-consistency}, their upper and lower half-relaxed limits solve
the same Isaacs equation.  Uniform ellipticity and the global structure give
comparison.  Hence every subsequential limit is the same, and the lower and
upper sequences converge to it.
\end{proof}

\section{Continuous-time verification of the pure saddle}\label{sec:verify}

For the quadratic game, consider
\[
 dX_s=(u_s+v_s)ds+\sigma dW_s
\]
and
\[
 J_{t,x}(u,v)=\E_{t,x}\left[
 \Phi(X_T)+\int_t^T
 \left(-\frac\mu2u_s^2+
 \frac\nu2v_s^2\right)ds\right].
\]
Let $c=\frac12(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})$.

\begin{theorem}[Pure feedback saddle and global comparison]\label{thm:feedback}
For bounded Lipschitz terminal $\Phi$, the function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ColeHopf-value}
 V(t,x)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{\sigma^2}{2c}
 \log\E_{t,x}\exp\left(\frac{2c}{\sigma^2}\Phi(x+\sigma(W_T-W_t))\right),
 &c\ne0,\\[2ex]
 \E_{t,x}\Phi(x+\sigma(W_T-W_t)),&c=0,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
is the unique bounded viscosity solution of
\[
 -V_t-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}V_{xx}-cV_x^2=0.
\]
For $t<T$ it is smooth and
\[
 u_s^*=V_x(s,X_s)/\mu,
 \qquad v_s^*=-V_x(s,X_s)/\nu
\]
form a pure feedback saddle:
\[
 J(u,v^*)\le J(u^*,v^*)\le J(u^*,v).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Cole--Hopf transform reduces the PDE to the linear heat equation, proving
\eqref{eq:ColeHopf-value}, regularity for $t<T$, and comparison.  Apply It\^o's
formula to $V(s,X_s)$ and use
\[
 -c p^2+p(u+v)-\frac\mu2u^2+
 \frac\nu2v^2
 =-\frac\mu2(u-p/\mu)^2+
 \frac\nu2(v+p/\nu)^2.
\]
With $v=v^*$ the second square vanishes and the first is nonpositive; with
$u=u^*$ the first vanishes and the second is nonnegative.  Integrating proves
the saddle inequalities.
\end{proof}

\section{Partially observed actual game}\label{sec:combined}

Take the product of the moving-cut innovation system and the hidden
refresh--autoregression.  Let controls enter the slow drift and the compact
parameter functions while preserving the strict filter and curvature margins.

\begin{theorem}[End-to-end partially observed pure game]\label{thm:endtoend}
Under the observation gap \eqref{eq:two-gates}, the initial-layer scaling, the
exact moving-cut innovations, and the curvature-compensation hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the noncompact filter contracts uniformly over all pure strategy trees;
\item the initial belief disappears from the macroscopic value;
\item the slow process has the control-uniform diffusion characteristics of
Paper III;
\item lower and upper prelimit values converge to the same pure-strategy
Isaacs solution \eqref{eq:Isaacs};
\item in the quadratic branch, the limiting pure feedback saddle is given by
\cref{thm:feedback}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items (i)--(ii) are \cref{thm:noncompact-filter,thm:value-collapse}.  Exact
moving-cut innovations give the control-uniform predictable martingale
characteristics, proving (iii).  Apply \cref{thm:scheme-limit} for (iv) and
\cref{thm:feedback} for (v).
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

The noncompact filter and pure Isaacs theorem are both positive and
quantitative.  Refresh produces strict prediction contraction without erasing
the noncompact dynamics; actuator energies produce a unique pure saddle
without assuming minimax equality.  Their combination with the exact
moving-cut innovation process closes the partially observed deterministic
multiscale game on one actual platform.
