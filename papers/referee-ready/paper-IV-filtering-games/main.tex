\begin{abstract}
We combine control-uniform deterministic rough homogenization with nonlinear
filtering and two-player dynamic programming.  The limiting theory splits into
four distinct branches.  Prediction contraction followed by a positive Bayes
update gives filter stability when the observation gap satisfies an explicit
strict inequality.  Independence of the macroscopic value from the initial
belief additionally requires a fast initial layer of vanishing slow duration.
The estimates are uniform along arbitrary strategy trees.  A weighted version
is developed for noncompact hidden states, with the PDE comparison condition
kept separate from Lyapunov and filter contraction.

Sequential games retain their order and converge to lower and upper HJB
limits.  Simultaneous games are convexified by relaxed controls and converge to
a mixed Isaacs equation.  We prove the exact compact-action criterion for a
pure value: a continuous game has a pure saddle if and only if its pure lower
and upper Hamiltonians agree.  Matching pennies rules out any unconditional
pure-saddle theorem.  For noncompact Euclidean controls, uniform strong
concavity in the maximizing variable and strong convexity in the minimizing
variable yield a unique pure saddle by strong monotonicity, with no smallness
assumption on the cross Hessian.

The four-branch deterministic model of the preceding papers supplies an actual
partially observed game: hidden-symbol prediction forgets the prior in one
step, and monotone lower, upper, mixed, and pure schemes converge to their
viscosity limits.  A deterministic Gaussian product shift gives an actual
weighted noncompact filter with exact one-step prior forgetting.  These results
provide the filtering and game inputs for path-dependent and stochastic
representations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Partially observed multiscale games combine three limiting operations that
must not be conflated: elimination or retention of the filter, homogenization
of the fast dynamics, and passage from discrete dynamic programming to an
Isaacs equation.  A fourth distinction concerns the order of play.  Sequential
lower and upper games, simultaneous mixed games, and pure-strategy games have
different Hamiltonians and require different structural hypotheses.

The preceding companion paper \cite{QiRough} constructs control-uniform
homogenized local characteristics from Doob-selected deterministic fast
dynamics.  We take those characteristics as the frozen input and address
filtering and game limits.  The main filtering estimate is elementary but its
correct use is subtle.  If prediction contracts at rate $\rho$ and the Bayes
map expands by at most $C_B$, then $r_*$ prediction steps between observations
give contraction only when
\[
 C_B\rho^{r_*}<1.
\]
This proves stability after the first update.  It does not by itself erase the
initial belief from a reward sampled before sufficient filtering.  We therefore
introduce an explicit fast initial layer whose slow duration vanishes.

The strategy-tree issue is equally important.  Filter constants must be
uniform in the controls selected by both players and in every observation
history.  Under this uniformity, the contraction estimate can be iterated path
by path before taking suprema and infima.

For noncompact hidden states, total variation is typically too strong or
unavailable.  We formulate a weighted bounded-Lipschitz/Harris packet.  A
Lyapunov drift and posterior moment ball make the Bayes normalization finite;
a strict prediction-versus-Bayes gap gives filter contraction.  The limiting
weighted HJB comparison theorem remains an independent PDE input.  The actual
Gaussian hidden model in \cref{sec:weighted-actual} has an even stronger
property: prediction maps every prior to one fixed Gaussian law in a single
step.

The game theory is organized by type.  The sequential Hamiltonians are
\[
 H^-=\sup_u\inf_vF(u,v),
 \qquad
 H^+=\inf_v\sup_uF(u,v).
\]
They need not agree.  Relaxed simultaneous controls yield mixed equality by
Sion's theorem \cite{Sion1958}.  In pure strategies, however, equality is not a
consequence of mixed minimax.  We prove the precise compact theorem
\[
 H^-=H^+\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{a pure saddle exists}.
\]
For Euclidean controls, a strong concave--convex theorem gives a robust actual
class with unique Lipschitz feedbacks.

Our viscosity arguments use the comparison and stability framework of
Crandall--Ishii--Lions \cite{CrandallIshiiLions1992} and the monotone-scheme
principle of Barles--Souganidis \cite{BarlesSouganidis1991}.  Classical
stochastic game results include Fleming--Souganidis
\cite{FlemingSouganidis1989} and Buckdahn--Li \cite{BuckdahnLi2008}.  For
filter stability and observability, see van Handel \cite{vanHandel2009} and the
monograph of Bain--Crisan \cite{BainCrisan2009}.

\section{Partially observed fast dynamics}\label{sec:setup}

Let $X_t^\varepsilon\in\R^m$ be the slow state and $Z_n^\varepsilon$ the hidden
fast state.  The maximizing player chooses $u\in U$ and the minimizing player
chooses $v\in V$.  For frozen $(x,u,v)$, let
\[
 P_{x,u,v}(z,dz')
\]
be the hidden prediction kernel.  Observations take values in a standard Borel
space $Y$ and have likelihood $g_y(z;x,u,v)>0$ relative to a reference measure.

A belief $\nu$ is updated by
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Pred}_{x,u,v}(\nu)&=\nu P_{x,u,v},
 \label{eq:prediction}\
 \operatorname{Bayes}_{y,x,u,v}(\nu)(dz)
 &=\frac{g_y(z;x,u,v)\nu(dz)}{\nu(g_y)}.
 \label{eq:bayes}
\end{align}
Controls may depend nonanticipatively on the available observation history.
All estimates below are required uniformly over the resulting strategy tree.

The companion homogenization paper supplies the following input.

\begin{assumption}[Control-uniform homogenized characteristics]\label{ass:K2}
For frozen $(x,u,v)$, the deterministic fast driver has drift $b$, covariance
$\Sigma$, and area correction $\Gamma$, continuous uniformly in controls.  The
slowly varying deterministic system converges, uniformly over bounded
Lipschitz feedbacks and admissible strategies, to the corresponding controlled
martingale problem.  Local dynamic-programming operators are monotone, stable,
and consistent with these characteristics.
\end{assumption}

\section{Bounded filtering and the observation gap}\label{sec:bounded-filter}

We use
\[
 \norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}=\sup_{\abs f\le1}\abs{\mu(f)-\nu(f)}.
\]

\begin{assumption}[Prediction contraction]\label{ass:pred-contract}
There is $0\le\rho<1$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pred-contract}
 \norm{\mu P_{x,u,v}-\nu P_{x,u,v}}_{\TV}
 \le\rho\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}
\end{equation}
uniformly in $x,u,v$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Positive observations]\label{ass:positive-observation}
There are constants $0<g_-\le g_+<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-bounds}
 g_-\le g_y(z;x,u,v)\le g_+
\end{equation}
for every admissible variable.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Bayes expansion constant]\label{lem:bayes}
Under \cref{ass:positive-observation},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bayes-lip}
 \norm{\operatorname{Bayes}_g(\mu)-
 \operatorname{Bayes}_g(\nu)}_{\TV}
 \le C_B\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV},
 \qquad C_B=\frac{2g_+}{g_-}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\abs f\le1$, add and subtract $\nu(gf)/\mu(g)$:
\begin{align*}
 \abs{\frac{\mu(gf)}{\mu(g)}-
 \frac{\nu(gf)}{\nu(g)}}
 &\le\frac{\abs{(\mu-\nu)(gf)}}{\mu(g)}
 +\frac{\abs{\nu(gf)}\abs{(\mu-\nu)(g)}}
 {\mu(g)\nu(g)}\\
 &\le\frac{g_+}{g_-}\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}
 +\frac{g_+}{g_-}\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}.
\end{align*}
Take the supremum over $f$.
\end{proof}

Suppose $r_*$ hidden prediction steps occur between observations.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform filter contraction]\label{thm:filter}
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filter-gap}
 q:=C_B\rho^{r_*}<1,
\end{equation}
then one complete prediction--observation cycle contracts beliefs by $q$.
Along any common observation and control path,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filter-contract}
 \norm{\pi_n^\mu-\pi_n^\nu}_{\TV}
 \le q^n\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}.
\end{equation}
The estimate is uniform over all admissible strategy trees.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Iterate \eqref{eq:pred-contract} $r_*$ times and apply
\cref{lem:bayes}.  The constants do not depend on the selected controls or
observations, so the pathwise inequality holds on every strategy branch.
\end{proof}

\section{Slow variation and the initial layer}\label{sec:initial-layer}

Let $\omega_P$ and $\omega_g$ be uniform moduli for the prediction kernel and
likelihood under slow/control perturbations.

\begin{proposition}[Geometric convolution estimate]\label{prop:convolution}
For two coupled filters with the same observation symbols and parameter
mismatch $\delta_k$ at the $k$th update,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convolution}
 \norm{\pi_n-\pi_n'}
 \le q^n\norm{\pi_0-\pi_0'}
 +C\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q^{n-1-k}
 \bigl(\omega_P(\delta_k)+\omega_g(\delta_k)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Insert an intermediate update that uses the first belief with the second
parameter.  The belief difference at fixed parameter contracts by $q$; the
parameter replacement is controlled by the moduli.  Iteration of the affine
recursion gives \eqref{eq:convolution}.
\end{proof}

A contraction after the first observation does not remove rewards earned
before that observation.

\begin{assumption}[Vanishing slow initial layer]\label{ass:initial}
Before the first order-one slow reward is sampled, the filter performs
$\ell_\varepsilon$ complete cycles, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-layer}
 \ell_\varepsilon\to\infty,
 \qquad
 \delta_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{init}}\to0.
\end{equation}
The second quantity is the corresponding slow physical duration, and the
running reward accumulated in the layer is at most
$C\delta_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{init}}$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Initial-belief collapse for values]\label{thm:initial-collapse}
Assume \cref{thm:filter,ass:initial}, the slow-variation estimate
\eqref{eq:convolution}, and uniform Lipschitz dependence of local rewards and
characteristics on the belief.  Then, uniformly over lower, upper, mixed, and
pure strategy trees,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:value-collapse}
 \abs{V_\varepsilon(t,x,\mu)-V_\varepsilon(t,x,\nu)}
 \le C_Tq^{\ell_\varepsilon}\norm{\mu-\nu}
 +C\delta_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{init}}
 +\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon\to0$ contains accumulated slow
perturbations and homogenization errors.  Hence the limiting value is
independent of the initial belief.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple the two games along the same controls and observations.  At the first
macroscopic reward time, \cref{thm:filter} gives the first term.  Rewards
before that time are confined to the initial layer and give the second term.
Thereafter, \eqref{eq:convolution} and the Lipschitz assumptions bound the
geometric sum of belief-dependent differences.  The estimate is pathwise and
uniform in strategies, so taking suprema and infima preserves it.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The finiteness of $\sum q^k$ would give only a finite Lipschitz constant in the
initial belief.  It does not imply the vanishing statement
\eqref{eq:value-collapse} without \cref{ass:initial}.
\end{remark}

\section{Weighted filtering on noncompact state spaces}\label{sec:weighted}

Let $W:E\to[1,\infty)$ be a Lyapunov function.  On a $W$-moment ball, use a
weighted bounded-Lipschitz distance
\[
 d_W(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{f\in\mathcal F_W}\abs{\mu(f)-\nu(f)},
\]
where $\abs f\le W$ and the weighted Lipschitz seminorm is at most one.

\begin{assumption}[Weighted filter packet]\label{ass:weighted}
There are $m_*\ge1$, $M<\infty$, and $0<\rho_W<1$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $P^{m_*}W\le\lambda W+b$ with $\lambda<1$;
\item the ball $\mathcal K_M=\{\mu:\mu(W)\le M\}$ is invariant under one
prediction--observation cycle;
\item prediction over $m_*$ steps contracts $d_W$ by at most $C_P\rho_W^{m_*}$;
\item Bayes normalization is $C_B(W,M)$-Lipschitz on $\mathcal K_M$;
\item $C_B(W,M)C_P\rho_W^{m_*}<1$;
\item the weighted initial-layer analogue of \cref{ass:initial} holds;
\item the limiting weighted HJB or Isaacs equation has comparison in the
declared growth class.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Weighted filter and game limit]\label{thm:weighted-filter}
Under \cref{ass:weighted}, the contraction, convolution, and initial-belief
collapse theorems hold in $d_W$ on finite slow horizons.  If the control-uniform
homogenized characteristics and DPP consistency hold, the values converge to
the unique weighted viscosity solution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The invariant moment ball bounds the Bayes denominator away from zero and
controls weighted numerators.  The strict product of the prediction and Bayes
constants gives geometric contraction.  The proofs of
\cref{prop:convolution,thm:initial-collapse} then apply verbatim in $d_W$.
Tightness and consistency yield weighted viscosity sub- and supersolutions;
the separately assumed comparison theorem identifies the limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
A Lyapunov coupling proves neither Bayes stability nor the final weighted PDE
comparison.  These are separate fields of \cref{ass:weighted}.
\end{remark}

\section{Sequential games}\label{sec:sequential}

Let $F(x,p,X;u,v)$ be the local Hamiltonian built from the homogenized
characteristics and running payoff.  The maximizing player is $u$ and the
minimizing player is $v$.  Define
\begin{align}
 H^-(x,p,X)&=\sup_{u\in U}\inf_{v\in V}F(x,p,X;u,v),
 \label{eq:Hminus}\
 H^+(x,p,X)&=\inf_{v\in V}\sup_{u\in U}F(x,p,X;u,v).
 \label{eq:Hplus}
\end{align}

\begin{assumption}[Sequential DPP packet]\label{ass:seq-DPP}
The prelimit lower and upper games have stable monotone DPPs.  Their one-step
operators are locally consistent, uniformly in the belief after
\cref{thm:initial-collapse}, with \eqref{eq:Hminus} and \eqref{eq:Hplus}.
The two limiting equations have comparison.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Sequential homogenized values]\label{thm:sequential}
Under \cref{ass:K2,thm:initial-collapse,ass:seq-DPP}, the lower and upper values
converge locally uniformly to the unique viscosity solutions of
\begin{align}
 \partial_tu^-+H^-(x,Du^-,D^2u^-)&=0,
 \label{eq:lower-HJB}\
 \partial_tu^++H^+(x,Du^+,D^2u^+)&=0.
 \label{eq:upper-HJB}
\end{align}
No Isaacs equality is needed for either conclusion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the half-relaxed-limit argument to each monotone DPP separately.  The
control-uniform local characteristics supply consistency, while
\cref{thm:initial-collapse} removes the initial-belief discrepancy uniformly
on strategy trees.  The order of optimization survives in the local test and
produces \eqref{eq:Hminus} or \eqref{eq:Hplus}.  Comparison gives convergence.
\end{proof}

\section{Simultaneous relaxed games}\label{sec:mixed}

Assume $U,V$ are compact metric spaces and $F$ is continuous.  For relaxed
controls $\mu\in\cP(U)$ and $\nu\in\cP(V)$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-F}
 \overline F(x,p,X;\mu,\nu)
 =\int_{U\times V}F(x,p,X;u,v)\,\mu(du)\nu(dv).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Mixed Isaacs equality]\label{thm:mixed-equality}
For every $(x,p,X)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-equality}
 \sup_{\mu\in\cP(U)}\inf_{\nu\in\cP(V)}\overline F
 =\inf_{\nu\in\cP(V)}\sup_{\mu\in\cP(U)}\overline F.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The relaxed-control spaces are compact convex in the weak topology, and
$\overline F$ is continuous and affine in each variable.  Sion's minimax
theorem applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous mixed limit]\label{thm:mixed-limit}
Suppose the simultaneous relaxed prelimit game has a stable monotone DPP
consistent with either side of \eqref{eq:mixed-equality}, and the limiting
mixed Isaacs equation has comparison.  Under the control-uniform K2 and filter
hypotheses, the mixed values converge locally uniformly to that unique
solution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof is the monotone half-relaxed-limit argument.  Compactness of relaxed
controls gives attainment along approximating subsequences; affine minimax
identifies the lower and upper local Hamiltonians.  Comparison completes the
argument.
\end{proof}

\section{Pure-strategy Isaacs theory}\label{sec:pure}

Mixed equality does not imply a pure saddle.  The exact compact theorem is the
following.

\begin{theorem}[Pure saddle if and only if pure Isaacs]\label{thm:pure-iff}
Let $U,V$ be compact and $F:U\times V\to\R$ continuous.  Then a pair
$(u_*,v_*)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:saddle}
 F(u,v_*)\le F(u_*,v_*)\le F(u_*,v)
 \qquad\forall u\in U,\ v\in V
\end{equation}
exists if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pure-isaacs}
 \max_u\min_vF(u,v)=\min_v\max_uF(u,v).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A saddle immediately gives equality.  Conversely, let the common value be $c$.
Choose a maximin optimizer $u_*$ and a minimax optimizer $v_*$; compactness and
continuity give both.  Then
\[
 F(u_*,v)\ge c\quad\forall v,
 \qquad
 F(u,v_*)\le c\quad\forall u.
\]
At $(u_*,v_*)$ the two inequalities force equality, and together they give
\eqref{eq:saddle}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Matching pennies]\label{ex:matching-pennies}
Let $U=V=\{-1,1\}$ and $F(u,v)=uv$.  Then
\[
 \max_u\min_vF=-1,
 \qquad
 \min_v\max_uF=1,
\]
while the mixed value is zero.  Thus compactness, continuity, a DPP, and mixed
minimax do not imply a pure saddle.
\end{example}

Let the state/jet variable be $z$ in a Polish space.  Define the saddle
correspondence $\cS(z)$ by \eqref{eq:saddle}.

\begin{proposition}[Measurable pure selector]\label{prop:selector}
If $F(z,u,v)$ is Borel in $z$, continuous in $(u,v)$, and $\cS(z)$ is nonempty
for every $z$, then $\cS$ admits a Borel measurable selector.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each value is compact.  Checking the saddle inequalities on countable dense
subsets of $U$ and $V$ shows that the graph of $\cS$ is Borel.  The
Kuratowski--Ryll-Nardzewski theorem gives a selector.
\end{proof}

We next give a noncompact positive class.  Let $U=\R^r$, $V=\R^s$.

\begin{assumption}[Strong concave--convex structure]\label{ass:strong}
The function $F(z,\cdot,\cdot)$ is $C^2$ and, uniformly in all variables,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-hessian}
 D_{uu}^2F\le-\mu I,
 \qquad
 D_{vv}^2F\ge\nu I,
 \qquad \mu,\nu>0.
\end{equation}
The first derivatives have at most linear growth and the saddle operator below
is coercive.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Unique noncompact pure saddle]\label{thm:strong-saddle}
Under \cref{ass:strong}, each $z$ has a unique pure saddle.  If the coefficients
are locally Lipschitz in $z$, so is the saddle selector on compact state sets.
No smallness assumption on $D_{uv}^2F$ is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define
\[
 G_z(u,v)=(-D_uF(z,u,v),D_vF(z,u,v)).
\]
For $w=(u,v)$ and $w'=(u',v')$, integrate the Jacobian on the segment.  The
mixed terms cancel because $D_{vu}^2F=(D_{uv}^2F)^T$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-monotone}
 \pair{G_z(w)-G_z(w')}{w-w'}
 \ge\mu\abs{u-u'}^2+\nu\abs{v-v'}^2.
\end{equation}
Continuity, coercivity, and strong monotonicity give a unique zero of $G_z$.
Strong concavity and convexity show that this zero satisfies the global saddle
inequalities.  Applying \eqref{eq:strong-monotone} at two parameter values and
using the coefficient modulus gives Lipschitz dependence.
\end{proof}

A useful explicit class is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-game}
 F(z,u,v)=f(z)+a(z)\cdot u+b(z)\cdot v
 -\frac\mu2\abs u^2+\frac\nu2\abs v^2+u^TK(z)v.
\end{equation}
In the scalar case $K=\gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-saddle}
 u_*(z)=\frac{\nu a(z)-\gamma b(z)}{\gamma^2+\mu\nu},
 \qquad
 v_*(z)=\frac{-\gamma a(z)-\mu b(z)}{\gamma^2+\mu\nu}.
\end{equation}

\section{Actual four-branch filtering and game limits}\label{sec:actual-four}

Let $I_n\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ be the branch symbol in the deterministic four-branch
system.  For slow state and controls, choose smooth probabilities
$p_j(x,u,v)\ge p_*>0$, $\sum_jp_j=1$, generated by compact Doob tilts, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-one-kernel}
 P_{ij}^{x,u,v}=p_j(x,u,v).
\end{equation}
Then every row is the same and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-step-forget}
 \mu P^{x,u,v}=p(x,u,v)
\end{equation}
for every prior $\mu$.

Let the observation likelihood satisfy \eqref{eq:g-bounds}.  Let bounded branch
increments $c_j(x,u,v)$ be centered under $p(x,u,v)$ and let the running payoff
be continuous.  For a slow step $h$, define the monotone one-step schemes
\begin{align}
 S_h^-\phi(x)
 &=\sup_u\inf_v\E\left[
 \phi\bigl(x+h b(x,u,v)+\sqrt h\,c_I(x,u,v)\bigr)
 +h\ell(x,u,v)\right],
 \label{eq:scheme-lower}\
 S_h^+\phi(x)
 &=\inf_v\sup_u\E\left[
 \phi\bigl(x+h b(x,u,v)+\sqrt h\,c_I(x,u,v)\bigr)
 +h\ell(x,u,v)\right].
 \label{eq:scheme-upper}
\end{align}
The mixed scheme replaces controls by product measures, and the pure scheme
uses \eqref{eq:quadratic-game} when that port is selected.

\begin{theorem}[Actual hidden-symbol game]\label{thm:actual-game}
The four-branch model has:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item exact one-step prior forgetting and a vanishing one-fast-step slow initial
layer;
\item control-uniform martingale rough homogenization;
\item monotone stable schemes \eqref{eq:scheme-lower}--\eqref{eq:scheme-upper}
consistent with the sequential Hamiltonians;
\item a simultaneous relaxed scheme consistent with the mixed Isaacs
Hamiltonian;
\item under the port \eqref{eq:quadratic-game}, a unique pure feedback saddle
and one pure Isaacs limit.
\end{enumerate}
All schemes converge to their unique viscosity solutions whenever the
corresponding comparison theorem holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:one-step-forget} proves the filter statement, and one fast
step has vanishing diffusive slow duration.  Conditional branch increments are
bounded and independent, so the predictable-characteristics theorem of the
companion paper gives uniform homogenization.  The schemes are monotone and
constant stable.  Taylor expansion of a smooth test gives the drift, covariance,
and running payoff, with the optimization order preserved.  The mixed scheme
uses \cref{thm:mixed-equality}; the pure scheme uses
\cref{thm:strong-saddle}.  Barles--Souganidis convergence and comparison give
the final statement.
\end{proof}

\section{An actual weighted noncompact filter}\label{sec:weighted-actual}

Let
\[
 \Omega=\R^{\mathbb Z}\times[0,1]^{\mathbb Z}
\]
with product Gaussian--uniform measure and deterministic left shift.  Define
the hidden state $Y_n$ to be the Gaussian coordinate at time $n$.  Thus the
transition kernel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-kernel}
 P(y,dy')=\gamma(dy'),
\end{equation}
independent of $y$, where $\gamma=N(0,1)$.  Let $W(y)=1+y^2$.

The observation $O_n\in\{-1,1\}$ is generated from the independent uniform
coordinate with likelihood
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-likelihood}
 g_o(y)=\frac{1+o\varepsilon_o\tanh y}{2},
 \qquad 0<\varepsilon_o<1.
\end{equation}
Then
\[
 \frac{1-\varepsilon_o}{2}\le g_o(y)
 \le\frac{1+\varepsilon_o}{2}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian weighted filter with exact forgetting]\label{thm:gaussian-filter}
For every prior probability $\mu$ with finite $W$ moment, one prediction gives
$\mu P=\gamma$.  After observing $o$, the posterior is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-posterior}
 \pi^o(dy)=\frac{g_o(y)\gamma(dy)}{\gamma(g_o)},
\end{equation}
independent of the original prior.  The family of posteriors has uniformly
bounded moments of every polynomial order.  Consequently this deterministic
noncompact hidden model has exact one-step filter stability, an invariant
weighted moment ball, and vanishing-initial-layer value collapse.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is immediate from \eqref{eq:gaussian-kernel}.  Bayes'
formula gives \eqref{eq:gaussian-posterior}.  The likelihood and its reciprocal
are bounded, so every posterior polynomial moment is bounded by a fixed
multiple of the corresponding Gaussian moment.  Since prediction erases the
prior exactly, the filter distance is zero after one cycle.  One fast cycle has
vanishing slow duration under diffusive scaling.
\end{proof}

\section{Belief-state and path-state limits}\label{sec:belief-path}

If the observation gap is not accelerated enough for
\cref{thm:initial-collapse}, the current filter remains part of the Markov
state.  The value is then $V(t,x,\pi)$ and its DPP yields an HJB or Isaacs
equation on $\R^m\times\cP(E)$.

\begin{theorem}[Belief-state limit]\label{thm:belief}
Assume tightness of the filter process, control-uniform homogenized
characteristics, local consistency on a separating smooth test class on the
belief space, and comparison in the declared topology and growth class.  Then
the partially observed values converge to the unique belief-state viscosity
solution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The filter is a sufficient statistic for the observation history.  Write the
DPP on $(x,\pi)$, take half-relaxed limits, and test with cylindrical functions
of finitely many moments of $\pi$.  Consistency gives the sub- and
supersolution inequalities.  Tightness passes the terminal condition and
comparison identifies the limit.
\end{proof}

If admissibility or the payoff depends on history not encoded by the current
filter, the state is genuinely path dependent.  No finite-dimensional
projection is made without a proved sufficient statistic.  The companion
representation paper constructs an actual noncompact path-state game and its
PPDE/BSDE representations.

\section{Conclusion}

Filtering, sequential play, mixed simultaneous play, and pure play are four
parallel mathematical branches.  The filter requires a prediction--Bayes rate
gap and, for value collapse, a vanishing slow initial layer.  Sequential lower
and upper limits remain distinct unless pure Isaacs equality is proved.  Mixed
minimax is obtained by relaxed controls but does not imply a pure saddle.  The
pure theory is closed by an exact compact necessary-and-sufficient theorem and
a strong concave--convex noncompact class.  The four-branch and Gaussian-shift
models show that the bounded, weighted, mixed, and pure conclusions are all
nonempty actual regimes.
