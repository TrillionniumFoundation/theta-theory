\begin{abstract}
We prove a positive filtering and pure-strategy Isaacs theorem for partially
observed deterministic multiscale games.  The hidden state is a noncompact
Gaussian refresh--autoregression realized on a deterministic product shift.  A
common refresh component gives a strict total-variation contraction, the
autoregressive component gives a quadratic Lyapunov drift, and bounded
nondegenerate observations preserve an explicit posterior moment ball.  After
finitely many prediction steps between observations, Bayes expansion is
dominated and the nonlinear filter contracts uniformly over all strategy
trees.  A vanishing slow initial layer then removes the initial belief from the
macroscopic value.

For pure games we introduce a curvature-compensation principle.  Starting from
a general $C^2$ mechanical payoff, we add the actual quadratic energies of the
maximizing and minimizing actuators.  If these energy curvatures dominate the
unfavourable control Hessians, the saddle variational inequality is strongly
monotone; mixed Hessian terms cancel exactly.  The game has a unique pure
saddle and a Lipschitz feedback selector on closed convex, possibly
noncompact, control sets.

Lower and upper semi-Lagrangian schemes generated by the same nonconjugate
moving collision system have the same consistency Hamiltonian and converge to
the same uniformly parabolic Isaacs equation.  The explicit one-dimensional
energy game has Hamiltonian
$\frac12(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})p^2$, which is concave when $\mu>\nu$; nonconvexity
is therefore generated by opposing actuator energies rather than inserted as a
cotangent polynomial.  A completion-of-squares argument gives an actual pure
feedback saddle for the continuous-time value.  Taking the product with the
refresh--autoregressive hidden system yields a fully specified partially
observed deterministic model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Filtering, optimization order, mixed minimax, and pure saddles are distinct
operations.  A stable filter does not imply an Isaacs equality, and an Isaacs
equality does not select a pure feedback.  This paper gives independent
positive mechanisms for both steps and then combines them on one deterministic
multiscale platform.

The fast collision coordinate is the exact-innovation moving-cut system of the
companion papers.  The hidden coordinate is a deterministic product shift that
realizes a refresh--autoregressive Markov kernel.  The product transformation
is deterministic; probability enters through its invariant reference law.
The response and rough-homogenization papers supply the state-dependent
collision covariance \cite{QiRoughRound4}.

We use standard filtering ideas \cite{BainCrisan2009,AtarZeitouni1997,
vanHandel2009}, monotone operator theory, and viscosity methods for stochastic
games \cite{FlemingSouganidis1989,FlemingSoner2006,BuckdahnLi2008}.  The new
ingredients are the explicit refresh--Bayes contraction packet, the
curvature-compensation theorem on noncompact controls, and their end-to-end
actualization by deterministic collision innovations.

\section{Prediction and Bayes update}

Let $E$ be a measurable hidden-state space.  For slow state $x$ and controls
$(u,v)$ let $P_{x,u,v}$ be a prediction kernel.  An observation $o$ has
likelihood $g_o(z;x,u,v)>0$.  For a belief $\pi$, define
\begin{align}
 \mathsf{Pred}_{x,u,v}(\pi)&=\pi P_{x,u,v},\label{eq:pred}\\
 \mathsf B_{o,x,u,v}(\pi)(dz)&=
 {g_o(z;x,u,v)\pi(dz)\over\pi(g_o)}.\label{eq:Bayes}
\end{align}
Use
\[
 \norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}=
 \sup_{\abs f\le1}\abs{\mu(f)-\nu(f)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Bayes expansion]\label{lem:Bayes}
If $0<g_-\le g\le g_+<\infty$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bayes-Lip}
 \norm{\mathsf B_g(\mu)-\mathsf B_g(\nu)}_{\TV}
 \le C_B\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV},
 \qquad C_B={2g_+\over g_-}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\abs f\le1$, add and subtract $\nu(gf)/\mu(g)$:
\[
 \abs{{\mu(gf)\over\mu(g)}-{\nu(gf)\over\nu(g)}}
 \le {\abs{(\mu-\nu)(gf)}\over\mu(g)}
 +{\abs{\nu(gf)}\abs{(\mu-\nu)(g)}
   \over\mu(g)\nu(g)}.
\]
Each term is bounded by
$(g_+/g_-)\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}$.
\end{proof}

\section{An actual noncompact refresh--autoregressive filter}

Let $\gamma=N(0,1)$, choose $0<\delta<1$ and $\abs r<1$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 P(y,\cdot)=\delta\gamma+(1-\delta)N(ry,1-r^2).
\end{equation}
This kernel is realized by a deterministic product shift carrying a Bernoulli
refresh coordinate and a Gaussian innovation coordinate.  Let
$W(y)=1+y^2$.

\begin{lemma}[Prediction contraction and Lyapunov drift]\label{lem:refresh}
For all probability measures $\mu,\nu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TV-contract}
 \norm{\mu P-\nu P}_{\TV}
 \le(1-\delta)\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\TV}.
\end{equation}
With $\alpha=(1-\delta)r^2<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift}
 PW(y)=\alpha W(y)+2(1-\alpha).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The common refresh component cancels in the difference, while the remaining
Markov kernel is a contraction in total variation.  Moreover,
\[
 \E[Y_{n+1}^2\mid Y_n=y]
 =1-\alpha+\alpha y^2,
\]
which is equivalent to \eqref{eq:drift}.
\end{proof}

Use observations $o\in\{-1,1\}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:likelihood}
 g_o(y)={1+o\epsilon_o\tanh y\over2},
 \qquad0<\epsilon_o<1.
\end{equation}
Then
$g_-=(1-\epsilon_o)/2$ and $g_+=(1+\epsilon_o)/2$.

\begin{theorem}[Noncompact posterior contraction]\label{thm:filter}
Choose an integer $r_*$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gates}
 q:=C_B(1-\delta)^{r_*}<1,
 \qquad
 \kappa:={g_+\over g_-}\alpha^{r_*}<1.
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item one prediction--observation cycle contracts total variation by $q$;
\item the moment ball $\{\pi:\pi(W)\le R\}$ is invariant for every
\[
 R\ge {2g_+/g_-\over1-\kappa};
\]
\item along every common observation and strategy path,
\[
 \norm{\pi_n-\pi_n'}_{\TV}
 \le q^n\norm{\pi_0-\pi_0'}_{\TV};
\]
\item all constants remain uniform under compact control-dependent
perturbations preserving \eqref{eq:gates}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After $r_*$ predictions, \eqref{eq:TV-contract} gives the factor
$(1-\delta)^{r_*}$; apply \cref{lem:Bayes}.  Iterating
\eqref{eq:drift} gives
$P^{r_*}W\le\alpha^{r_*}W+2$.  The Bayes update satisfies
\[
 \mathsf B_g(\pi)(W)\le{g_+\over g_-}\pi(W).
\]
Thus one cycle maps a moment $M$ to at most
$\kappa M+2g_+/g_-$.  The displayed $R$ is invariant.  Iteration gives the
strategy-tree estimate.
\end{proof}

\section{The slow initial layer}

Let $\ell_\varepsilon$ complete filter cycles occur before the first order-one
slow reward, during a slow time $\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}$.

\begin{theorem}[Value-level belief collapse]\label{thm:collapse}
Assume
\[
 \ell_\varepsilon\to\infty,
 \qquad \delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}\to0,
\]
and that the absolute running reward in the initial layer is at most
$C\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}$.  Then two strategy values started from
$\pi,\pi'$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse}
 \abs{V^\varepsilon(\pi)-V^\varepsilon(\pi')}
 \le C_Tq^{\ell_\varepsilon}\norm{\pi-\pi'}_{\TV}
 +C\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}
 +\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon$ is the geometric convolution of the
slow-freezing moduli.  If it vanishes, the limiting value is independent of the
initial belief.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple the two filters along the same observation and strategy tree.  At the
first macroscopic reward the belief distance is bounded by
$q^{\ell_\varepsilon}\norm{\pi-\pi'}_{\TV}$.  Only the initial layer sees the
uncontracted prior, giving the second term.  Thereafter the slow parameter
perturbation obeys an affine recursion with contraction factor $q$; its
iteration is the geometric convolution term.
\end{proof}

\section{Curvature compensation on noncompact controls}

Let $U\subset\R^m$ and $V\subset\R^n$ be closed convex sets.  Let
$F_0(z,u,v)$ be $C^2$ in the controls and suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hessian}
 D_{uu}^2F_0(z,u,v)\le L_uI,
 \qquad
 D_{vv}^2F_0(z,u,v)\ge-L_vI.
\end{equation}
Introduce the actuator energies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:payoff}
 F(z,u,v)=F_0(z,u,v)-{\mu\over2}\abs u^2
 +{\nu\over2}\abs v^2,
 \qquad \mu>L_u,\quad\nu>L_v.
\end{equation}
Define the saddle operator
\[
 G_z(u,v)=(-D_uF(z,u,v),D_vF(z,u,v)).
\]
For constrained controls, the saddle equation is the variational inclusion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VI}
 0\in G_z(w)+N_{U\times V}(w),
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the normal cone.

\begin{theorem}[Curvature-compensation pure-saddle theorem]\label{thm:curvature}
Assume \eqref{eq:Hessian}--\eqref{eq:payoff} and coercivity of
$G_z+N_{U\times V}$.  Then $G_z$ is strongly monotone with constant
\[
 \lambda=\min\{\mu-L_u,\nu-L_v\}>0.
\]
The variational inequality \eqref{eq:VI} has a unique solution
$w_*(z)=(u_*(z),v_*(z))$, and this solution is the unique global pure saddle:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:saddle}
 F(z,u,v_*(z))\le F(z,u_*(z),v_*(z))
 \le F(z,u_*(z),v).
\end{equation}
If $G_z$ is Lipschitz in $z$, the saddle selector is Lipschitz.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For an increment $(\dot u,\dot v)$, the symmetric quadratic form of $DG_z$
is
\[
 -\dot u^TD_{uu}^2F\dot u+
 \dot v^TD_{vv}^2F\dot v.
\]
The mixed Hessian terms cancel because
$D_{vu}^2F=(D_{uv}^2F)^T$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:Hessian}--\eqref{eq:payoff} give the lower bound
$\lambda(\abs{\dot u}^2+\abs{\dot v}^2)$.  Integrating along a segment gives
strong monotonicity.  The normal-cone operator is maximal monotone, so the sum
is strongly maximal monotone; coercivity gives existence and strong
monotonicity gives uniqueness.  The first-order variational inequalities plus
strong concavity in $u$ and strong convexity in $v$ give
\eqref{eq:saddle}.  Comparing the inclusions at two states gives the Lipschitz
estimate.
\end{proof}

This theorem is a positive design principle for general mechanical games: the
physical energy curvatures, not a minimax relaxation, force the pure Isaacs
structure.

\section{An actual game-generated nonconvex Hamiltonian}

In one dimension let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic}
 F(p;u,v)=p(u+v)-{\mu\over2}u^2+{\nu\over2}v^2,
 \qquad \mu,\nu>0.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact energy saddle]\label{prop:saddle}
The unique pure saddle is
\[
 u_*(p)=p/\mu,\qquad v_*(p)=-p/\nu,
\]
and the Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H(p)=\sup_u\inf_vF(p;u,v)
 ={1\over2}(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})p^2.
\end{equation}
The saddle operator satisfies
\[
 \langle G(w)-G(w'),w-w'\rangle
 =\mu\abs{u-u'}^2+\nu\abs{v-v'}^2.
\]
If $\mu>\nu$, then $H''<0$, so $H$ is concave and nonconvex.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first-order equations give $u=p/\mu$ and $v=-p/\nu$.  Substitution gives
\eqref{eq:H}.  The monotonicity identity is direct.
\end{proof}

\section{Lower and upper collision-game schemes}

Let the moving collision system have branch probabilities
$w_i(a(x,u,v))$, centered increments $m_i(x,u,v)$, drift $b(x,u,v)$, and
running payoff $\ell(x,u,v)$.  Assume all coefficients are globally bounded
and Lipschitz, the covariance
\[
 \Sigma(x,u,v)=\sum_iw_i(a)m_i\otimes m_i
\]
is uniformly elliptic, and $\ell$ satisfies the curvature-compensation
hypotheses uniformly.  Define
\begin{align}
 (\mathcal S_h^-\phi)(x)
 &=\sup_{u\in U}\inf_{v\in V}
 \left\{\sum_iw_i(a(x,u,v))
 \phi(x+hb(x,u,v)+\sqrt h\,m_i(x,u,v))
 +h\ell(x,u,v)\right\},\label{eq:lower-scheme}\\
 (\mathcal S_h^+\phi)(x)
 &=\inf_{v\in V}\sup_{u\in U}
 \left\{\sum_iw_i(a(x,u,v))
 \phi(x+hb(x,u,v)+\sqrt h\,m_i(x,u,v))
 +h\ell(x,u,v)\right\}.\label{eq:upper-scheme}
\end{align}
Quadratic coercivity localizes optimizers to a compact set depending only on
the global coefficient bounds.

\begin{lemma}[Common consistency Hamiltonian]\label{lem:consistency}
For every smooth test $\phi$,
\begin{align*}
 {\mathcal S_h^-\phi-\phi\over h}&\longrightarrow
 \sup_u\inf_v\left\{
 b\cdot D\phi+{1\over2}\Sigma:D^2\phi+\ell\right\},\\
 {\mathcal S_h^+\phi-\phi\over h}&\longrightarrow
 \inf_v\sup_u\left\{
 b\cdot D\phi+{1\over2}\Sigma:D^2\phi+\ell\right\}.
\end{align*}
Under \cref{thm:curvature}, both limits agree and are attained at the unique
pure saddle of the local jet game.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Taylor expansion gives the drift and covariance terms uniformly on the
coercively localized control set.  The local payoff is the sum of those terms
and $\ell$.  Its unfavourable control Hessians are bounded on bounded jet sets;
the actuator energies dominate them by hypothesis.  Apply
\cref{thm:curvature}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pure Isaacs limit]\label{thm:scheme}
Let $V_h^-$ and $V_h^+$ be the terminal recursions generated by
\eqref{eq:lower-scheme} and \eqref{eq:upper-scheme}.  For bounded uniformly
continuous terminal data, both converge locally uniformly to the same unique
bounded viscosity solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Isaacs}
 -V_t-\sup_u\inf_v\left\{
 b(x,u,v)\cdot DV+{1\over2}\Sigma(x,u,v):D^2V+\ell(x,u,v)
 \right\}=0.
\end{equation}
The Hamiltonian equals the reverse-order Hamiltonian and is realized by the
unique pure selector $(u_*,v_*)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both schemes are monotone and stable.  By \cref{lem:consistency} they are
consistent with the same continuous uniformly elliptic Isaacs operator.
Global coefficient bounds and the Lipschitz pure selector give comparison by
the standard doubling argument.  The Barles--Souganidis half-relaxed-limit
theorem identifies both limits with the same solution.
\end{proof}

No claim that the two discrete games have identical values at every mesh size
is needed; the theorem proves that their macroscopic values and pure feedback
limit coincide.

\section{Continuous-time pure feedback verification}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:controlled-SDE}
 dX_s=b(X_s,u_s,v_s)ds+\sigma(X_s,u_s,v_s)dW_s,
 \qquad \sigma\sigma^T=\Sigma,
\end{equation}
and let the payoff be the expected terminal reward plus the time integral of
$\ell$.  Assume the smooth verification window for the moment.

\begin{theorem}[Pure feedback value]\label{thm:feedback}
Let $V\in C^{1,2}$ solve \eqref{eq:Isaacs} and let
$(u^*,v^*)(t,x)$ be the Lipschitz saddle selector evaluated at
$(DV,D^2V)$.  Then for every admissible $u,v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verification}
 J(t,x;u,v^*)\le V(t,x)\le J(t,x;u^*,v).
\end{equation}
Consequently the lower and upper continuous-time values coincide and
$(u^*,v^*)$ is a pure feedback saddle.  The conclusion extends to the bounded
viscosity solution by uniformly parabolic smooth approximation and stability
of the strongly monotone selectors.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply It\^o's formula to $V(s,X_s)$ under an arbitrary control pair.  The PDE
and the pointwise saddle inequalities make the drift nonpositive under
$(u,v^*)$ and nonnegative under $(u^*,v)$.  Integration and expectation give
\eqref{eq:verification}.  Uniform ellipticity gives smooth approximations;
strong monotonicity gives uniform convergence of the feedback selectors, so
the inequalities pass to the viscosity limit.
\end{proof}

For the quadratic energy model the verification is visible algebraically:
with $c=(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:squares}
 -cp^2+p(u+v)-{\mu\over2}u^2+{\nu\over2}v^2
 =-{\mu\over2}(u-p/\mu)^2
 +{\nu\over2}(v+p/\nu)^2.
\end{equation}

\section{The fully actual partially observed platform}

Take the product of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the nonconjugate moving collision map, which supplies the exact branch
innovations and state-dependent covariance;
\item the deterministic Bernoulli--Gaussian shift realizing
\eqref{eq:kernel};
\item the observation map \eqref{eq:likelihood};
\item the pure energy game \eqref{eq:quadratic} or the general compensated
game \eqref{eq:payoff}.
\end{enumerate}
The product transformation is deterministic.  The invariant reference law is
Lebesgue on the collision coordinate times the product law of the hidden
coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[End-to-end filtered pure-game limit]\label{thm:endtoend}
Choose parameters satisfying \eqref{eq:gates} and a slow initial layer as in
\cref{thm:collapse}.  Let the slow recursion use the collision branch
increments and let the controls enter through a curvature-compensated payoff.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the nonlinear filter is noncompact, has a uniform posterior moment ball,
and contracts over every strategy tree;
\item the initial belief disappears from the macroscopic value;
\item the collision increments satisfy the nonautonomous enhanced WIP of the
companion paper;
\item lower and upper microscopic game values converge to the same uniformly
parabolic Isaacs solution;
\item the limiting value has a unique pure Lipschitz feedback saddle;
\item in the quadratic branch with $\mu>\nu$, the effective Hamiltonian is
strictly concave in the gradient.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items (i) and (ii) are \cref{thm:filter,thm:collapse}.  Exact collision
innovations and the rough homogenization theorem of \cite{QiRoughRound4} give
(iii).  The monotone lower and upper recursions and
\cref{thm:curvature,thm:scheme} give (iv) and (v).  Formula \eqref{eq:H} gives
(vi).
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

The filter and the game are closed by two independent positive mechanisms.  A
refresh plus Lyapunov drift produces a genuine noncompact posterior
contraction, while actuator-energy curvature produces a unique pure saddle on
noncompact controls.  The exact collision innovations turn both mechanisms
into an end-to-end deterministic multiscale theorem.  The nonconvex
Hamiltonian is computed from the saddle, not written into a primitive
cotangent readout.
