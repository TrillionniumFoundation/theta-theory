\begin{abstract}
We convert third-order collision-map response for systems with moving
singularities into physical-time spectral and probabilistic response.  A
finite Banach bundle is first stabilized inside a single direct-sum space by a
smooth quadratic partition of unity, making cross-parameter Riesz calculus
well defined even when the embedded fibre ranges move.  For the jointly twisted
operator
\[
 L_{a,q,s}h=L_a\bigl(e^{q\cdot\kappa_a-s\tau_a}h\bigr)
\]
we obtain a three-times differentiable pressure and reduced spectral data.  The
implicit pressure root in the roof variable gives physical drift, covariance,
and their parameter derivatives.  A renewal formula with distinct exit and
entry roof-cell operators transfers the result to the low-frequency suspension
resolvent.  We prove that the pressure Hessian, the continuous-time
Green--Kubo tensor, and the Gordin martingale bracket coincide.

The resulting parameter coefficients are lifted to smooth $(x,p)$ fields and
become uniformly elliptic under a quantitative noncoboundary and coupling-rank
condition.  The open moving-seam family of the companion response paper gives
an actual positive-diffusion example.  At high frequency we prove two
complementary family results: uniform Baladi--Demers--Liverani bounds on compact
families carrying strict finite-time witnesses, and exact all-frequency
parameter differentiation for similarity families.  A nonconjugate radial
Sinai family has uniform high-frequency bounds, while arbitrary
nonconjugate parameter derivatives remain correctly typed by a graded
generator-symbol packet.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Diffusion coefficients in deterministic systems may be described in several
apparently different ways: as pressure Hessians, Green--Kubo correlation
series, martingale brackets, or second derivatives of a physical-time
cumulant-generating function.  When the collision singularities move with a
parameter, proving that these descriptions remain differentiable requires more
than a fixed-space spectral gap.  The derivative sources are singular, the
collision and physical clocks differ, and the anisotropic spaces themselves
may form a nontrivial Banach bundle.

This paper starts from the third-order response theorem of the companion paper
\cite{QiResponse}.  We restate its output as an explicit hypothesis and derive
four consequences.

First, we solve the common-space problem.  Local trivializations of a Banach
bundle do not by themselves define the difference of two operators at distinct
parameters.  A smooth quadratic partition embeds every fibre as a uniformly
complemented subspace of one finite direct sum.  The construction is simple,
but it prevents a recurrent category error in moving-domain perturbation
theory.

Second, we analyze the collision operator twisted simultaneously by
translation and roof time.  Third-order Kato calculus yields a $C^3$ pressure
$\cP(a,q,s)$.  The physical-time exponent $\Lambda_a(q)$ is determined by
\[
 \cP(a,q,\Lambda_a(q))=0.
\]
Its Hessian gives the covariance per unit physical time.  The mixed derivative
$\partial_a\partial_{q_iq_j}\cP$ is of total order three, explaining exactly
why the response three-jet is needed.

Third, we derive the suspension resolvent by a renewal decomposition.  The
initial observable is integrated to the next collision with an exit weight,
whereas the terminal observable is integrated forward from a collision with an
entry weight.  Keeping these operators distinct removes a hidden orientation
error.  The long-return term is a collision twisted resolvent, while the
same-flight part is finite.

Fourth, we distinguish uniform high-frequency bounds from parameter
differentiability.  Strict local Dolgopyat/BDL witnesses are open; a finite
cover therefore uniformizes the fixed-table high-frequency estimates on a
compact family.  This does not create parameter derivatives on one ungraded
graph domain.  Exact derivatives are nevertheless available for similarity
families, where the generators are explicitly conjugate up to time scaling.
For the nonconjugate radial family from the companion paper, the finite-cover
argument gives actual uniform high-frequency bounds, but generic derivatives
still require a graded symbol packet.

The fixed-table high-frequency input is the anisotropic generator theory of
Baladi--Demers--Liverani \cite{BaladiDemersLiverani2018}.  The perturbative
spectral arguments are in the spirit of Kato and Keller--Liverani
\cite{Kato1995,KellerLiverani1999}.  The covariance identities go back to
Nagaev--Guivarc'h spectral methods and the Gordin martingale approximation; see
\cite{Gordin1969,Gouezel2005}.  Renewal methods for suspension flows and
Dolgopyat estimates are classical; see \cite{Dolgopyat1998,Melbourne2007}.

\section{Response input and a fixed operator space}\label{sec:common}

Let $\A$ be compact.  For each $a\in\A$, let $B_a$ be a Banach fibre carrying a
transfer operator $L_a$ with a simple isolated eigenvalue at one.  We assume the
following response output from the companion paper.

\begin{assumption}[Third-order collision response]\label{ass:response}
There is a finite graded fibre scale and a stable Riesz contour $\Gamma$ such
that the transported operator, its first three complete source derivatives,
and all contour-resolvent words of total order at most three are continuous in
$a$.  Finite difference quotients converge in the complete two-time response
topology.  The reduced resolvent preserves each declared ladder level.
\end{assumption}

The fibres need not have the same image in a pre-existing ambient space.

\begin{assumption}[Finite smooth Banach atlas]\label{ass:atlas}
There are finitely many open sets $U_\alpha$, Banach spaces $B_\alpha$, and
isomorphisms
\[
 G_{\alpha,a}:B_a\to B_\alpha,
 \qquad a\in U_\alpha,
\]
whose transition maps and inverses are uniformly bounded and $C^3$ on compact
subcharts.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Quadratic partition]\label{lem:quadratic}
There exist smooth functions $\psi_\alpha$ supported in $U_\alpha$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic}
 \sum_{\alpha=1}^N\psi_\alpha(a)^2=1.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a finite smooth subordinate family $\chi_\alpha$ with no common zero
and set
\[
 \psi_\alpha=\frac{\chi_\alpha}
 {\bigl(\sum_\beta\chi_\beta^2\bigr)^{1/2}}.
\]
The denominator is strictly positive and smooth.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-atlas stabilization]\label{thm:stabilization}
Let
\[
 B_*:=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^N B_\alpha
\]
and define
\begin{align}
 J_a h&=(\psi_\alpha(a)G_{\alpha,a}h)_\alpha,
 \label{eq:Ja}\
 R_a(v_\alpha)_\alpha
 &=\sum_\alpha\psi_\alpha(a)G_{\alpha,a}^{-1}v_\alpha.
 \label{eq:Ra}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RJ}
 R_aJ_a=\Id_{B_a}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $E_a=J_aB_a$ is uniformly complemented in $B_*$ by
$P_a=J_aR_a$.  Every fibre operator has the fixed-space realization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lhat}
 \widehat L_a=J_aL_aR_a,
\end{equation}
and $a\mapsto J_a,R_a,P_a,\widehat L_a$ has the regularity of the atlas and the
original family.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:RJ} follows from \eqref{eq:quadratic}.  Hence
$P_a^2=J_a(R_aJ_a)R_a=P_a$ and $\operatorname{ran}P_a=E_a$.  Uniform bounds and
parameter regularity follow from finiteness of the atlas and the ordinary
product rule.  The extra zero spectrum of $\widehat L_a$ on the complement of
$E_a$ is separated from the Riesz contour around one.
\end{proof}

\section{Joint collision twists and the pressure three-jet}\label{sec:pressure}

Let $\kappa_a:M\to\R^d$ be a displacement and $\tau_a:M\to(0,\infty)$ a roof
function.  Assume that multiplication by every derivative needed through total
order three is bounded on the response ladder.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twisted}
 L_{a,q,s}h=L_a\bigl(e^{q\cdot\kappa_a-s\tau_a}h\bigr),
\end{equation}
for $(q,s)$ near zero in $\C^{d+1}$.

\begin{assumption}[Uniform spectral window]\label{ass:window}
The stabilized operator $\widehat L_{a,q,s}$ has a simple eigenvalue
$\lambda(a,q,s)$ inside a fixed contour $\Gamma$, and no other spectrum meets
$\Gamma$.  At $(q,s)=(0,0)$, $\lambda=1$.
\end{assumption}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pressure}
 \cP(a,q,s)=\log\lambda(a,q,s),
 \qquad \cP(a,0,0)=0.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Pressure and spectral three-jet]\label{thm:pressure}
Under \cref{ass:response,ass:atlas,ass:window}, the maps
\[
 (a,q,s)\mapsto
 \lambda(a,q,s),\quad \Pi_{a,q,s},\quad R_{a,q,s}^{\perp},\quad
 \cP(a,q,s)
\]
are $C^3$ on the real parameter window.  Their derivatives are finite sums of
direct multiplier derivatives and ordered graded resolvent words.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The twisted multiplier does not alter the regularity-loss accounting, so
\cref{ass:response} gives the first three complete operator derivatives.
For $\cR(z)=(z-\widehat L)^{-1}$,
\[
 D\cR=\cR(D\widehat L)\cR.
\]
Repeated differentiation gives ordered words with coefficients $1,2,3,6$ as
in the noncommutative Leibniz formula.  The graded ladder makes every word
bounded.  Uniform contour separation permits differentiation under
\[
 \Pi_{a,q,s}=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma\cR_{a,q,s}(z)\,dz.
\]
A normalized left-right eigenpair gives $\lambda$, and the chosen logarithm
gives $\cP$.
\end{proof}

\section{The physical-time pressure root}\label{sec:physical}

Define $\Lambda_a(q)$ near zero by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root}
 \cP(a,q,\Lambda_a(q))=0.
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ps}
 \partial_s\cP(a,0,0)=-\int\tau_a\,d\mu_a=-\bar\tau_a<0,
\end{equation}
the implicit-function theorem applies uniformly on compact parameter sets.

\begin{theorem}[Physical drift, covariance, and response]\label{thm:physical}
The map $(a,q)\mapsto\Lambda_a(q)$ is $C^3$.  Its gradient at zero is the mean
displacement per unit physical time.  In the centered convention
$\cP_q(a,0,0)=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sigma}
 \Sigma_{ij}(a)=\partial_{q_iq_j}\Lambda_a(0)
 =\frac{\cP_{q_iq_j}(a,0,0)}{\bar\tau_a}.
\end{equation}
The physical diffusion matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dphys}
 D^{\mathrm{phys}}(a)=\frac12\Sigma(a),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sigma-response}
 \partial_a\Sigma_{ij}
 =\frac{\cP_{a q_iq_j}}{\bar\tau}
 -\frac{\cP_{q_iq_j}\,\partial_a\bar\tau}{\bar\tau^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:root}.  At $q=0$, the first derivative vanishes by
centering.  The second derivative gives
\[
 0=\cP_{q_iq_j}+\cP_s\Lambda_{q_iq_j},
\]
which together with \eqref{eq:Ps} gives \eqref{eq:Sigma}.  Differentiate the
quotient in $a$ to obtain \eqref{eq:Sigma-response}.  The mixed derivative has
total order three and is supplied by \cref{thm:pressure}.
\end{proof}

\section{Renewal decomposition for the suspension}\label{sec:suspension}

Let
\[
 M_a^\tau=\{(x,u):0\le u<\tau_a(x)\}/\sim
\]
be the suspension with flow $\Phi_a^t$.  For a starting observable $F$ and a
terminal observable $G$, define the distinct roof-cell operators
\begin{align}
 (\mathcal O_{a,z}F)(x)
 &=\int_0^{\tau_a(x)}
 e^{-z(\tau_a(x)-u)}F(x,u)\,du,
 \label{eq:exit}\
 (\mathcal I_{a,z}G)(x)
 &=\int_0^{\tau_a(x)}e^{-zv}G(x,v)\,dv.
 \label{eq:entry}
\end{align}
The first transports an observation to the next exit; the second inserts an
observation after the last entrance.  Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lz}
 L_{a,z}h=L_a(e^{-z\tau_a}h).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Renewal formula]\label{prop:renewal}
For roof-cell observables in the declared multiplier class, the Laplace
transform of the centered correlation has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal}
 \widehat C_{F,G}(a,z)
 =H_{a,z}^{\mathrm{short}}(F,G)
 +\ell_{a,z}^{F}
 (I-L_{a,z})^{-1}\mathcal I_{a,z}G,
\end{equation}
where $\ell_{a,z}^{F}$ is the bounded functional induced by
$\mathcal O_{a,z}F$, and $H^{\mathrm{short}}$ contains trajectories with no
completed return between the two observations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Partition every orbit segment by the number of completed returns to the
section.  The zero-return contribution is $H^{\mathrm{short}}$.  For $n\ge1$,
integration over the initial partial roof gives \eqref{eq:exit}; the $n-1$
complete intermediate roofs produce $L_{a,z}^{n-1}$; integration over the
final partial roof gives \eqref{eq:entry}.  Summing in $n$ gives the resolvent.
The two roof-cell operators are different because the Laplace time is measured
from opposite ends of the segment.
\end{proof}

Near zero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pole}
 (I-L_{a,z})^{-1}
 =\frac{\Pi_{a,z}}{1-\lambda(a,0,z)}+R_{a,z}^{\perp}.
\end{equation}
For centered correlations, the pole term cancels the product of means.

\begin{theorem}[Low-frequency suspension response]\label{thm:suspension}
Assume the roof-cell maps \eqref{eq:exit}--\eqref{eq:entry} and their derivatives
through total order three are bounded on the response ladder.  Then the
centered expression \eqref{eq:renewal}, the reduced resolvent in
\eqref{eq:pole}, and all derivatives of total order at most three in $(a,z)$
are continuous for $z$ near zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The short term contains finitely many roof integrations and is differentiated
by Leibniz and endpoint rules.  The long term is a collision twisted resolvent
covered by \cref{thm:pressure}.  Pole cancellation is uniform because
$\partial_z\lambda(a,0,0)=-\bar\tau_a$ is bounded away from zero.
\end{proof}

\section{Pressure, Green--Kubo, and martingale covariance}\label{sec:covariance}

Assume $\kappa_a$ is centered.  Define
\begin{align}\label{eq:GK}
 C_{ij}^{\mathrm{coll}}(a)
 &=\int\kappa_{a,i}\kappa_{a,j}\,d\mu_a\\
 &\quad+\sum_{n\ge1}\int
 \bigl(\kappa_{a,i}\,\kappa_{a,j}\circ T_a^n
 +\kappa_{a,j}\,\kappa_{a,i}\circ T_a^n\bigr)d\mu_a.
\end{align}
The series converges absolutely by the spectral gap and the response product
tail.

\begin{theorem}[Three covariance formulas]\label{thm:covariance}
One has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pressure-GK}
 \cP_{q_iq_j}(a,0,0)=C_{ij}^{\mathrm{coll}}(a).
\end{equation}
Let $\chi_a$ solve the Poisson equation on the centered space and set
\[
 m_a=\kappa_a+\chi_a-\chi_a\circ T_a.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket}
 C^{\mathrm{coll}}(a)=\int m_a\otimes m_a\,d\mu_a.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\Sigma=C^{\mathrm{coll}}/\bar\tau$ is at once the pressure
Hessian per unit physical time, the continuous-time Green--Kubo tensor, and
the limiting martingale bracket.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the eigenvalue equation twice in $q$.  Centering removes the
first derivative.  The second derivative consists of the instantaneous tensor
and two reduced-resolvent insertions.  Expanding the reduced resolvent as a
Neumann series gives \eqref{eq:GK}.  The Poisson equation is
$\chi=(I-Q)^{-1}Q\kappa$.  Expanding $m\otimes m$, using conditional mean zero,
and telescoping the coboundary terms yields the same correlation series.
Division by the mean roof changes collision number into physical time.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Coboundary kernel]\label{cor:coboundary}
The matrix $\Sigma(a)$ is positive semidefinite.  For $\xi\in\R^d$,
$\xi^T\Sigma(a)\xi=0$ if and only if $\xi\cdot\kappa_a$ is a coboundary in the
declared spectral class.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:bracket}, the quadratic form is the $L^2$ norm of the scalar
martingale difference.  It vanishes exactly when the martingale part is zero,
which is equivalent to the Poisson coboundary identity.
\end{proof}

\section{Coefficient fields and ellipticity}\label{sec:coefficients}

Let $K\subset\R^m\times\R^m$ be a compact slow/cotangent window and let
$\Theta:K\to\A$ be $C^{3,\alpha}$.  At this stage $p$ is an external cotangent
signal; the substitution $p=Du$ belongs to the dynamic-programming paper.  Let
$B(x,p)$ be a macroscopic coupling matrix and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Afield}
 A(x,p)=\frac12B(x,p)\Sigma(\Theta(x,p))B(x,p)^T.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Hölder coefficient lift]\label{thm:lift}
If the parameter coefficients obtained above and $\Theta,B$ are
$C^{3,\alpha}$, then the drift, covariance, area, roof-normalized response, and
$A(x,p)$ are $C^{3,\alpha}$ on $K$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After \cref{thm:stabilization}, all operator and finite-dimensional
coefficients live in fixed spaces.  The finite-dimensional chain rule and the
Hölder Nemytskii product estimates apply to composition with $\Theta$ and
multiplication by $B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ellipticity transfer]\label{thm:ellipticity}
If
\[
 \Sigma(a)\ge\lambda_\Sigma I_d,
 \qquad
 B(x,p)B(x,p)^T\ge\lambda_B^2I_m,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ellipticity}
 A(x,p)\ge\frac12\lambda_\Sigma\lambda_B^2I_m.
\end{equation}
A compact uniform noncoboundary margin and full coupling rank therefore imply
uniform ellipticity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $z\in\R^m$,
\[
 z^TAz=\tfrac12(B^Tz)^T\Sigma(B^Tz)
 \ge\tfrac12\lambda_\Sigma\abs{B^Tz}^2
 \ge\tfrac12\lambda_\Sigma\lambda_B^2\abs z^2.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{An actual moving-seam diffusion package}\label{sec:actual}

For the four-branch family of the companion paper, choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-data}
 \kappa(y)=\cos(2\pi y),
 \qquad
 \tau_a(y)=1+\delta\sin(2\pi y)+\delta_1a,
\end{equation}
with $\abs\delta+\abs{\delta_1}/40<1/2$.  The roof is uniformly positive and
all multiplier derivatives are bounded on the fixed-cut scale.

The point $y=0$ is a fixed point in the first branch and $\kappa(0)=1$.  A
coboundary identity $\kappa=\psi-\psi\circ T_a$ would force
$\kappa(0)=0$.  Thus the asymptotic variance is strictly positive for every
$a$, and compactness makes the lower bound uniform.

\begin{theorem}[Actual pressure and diffusion response]\label{thm:actual-four}
For the open moving-seam family with \eqref{eq:actual-data}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\cP(a,q,s)$ is $C^3$;
\item the centered low-frequency suspension resolvent has $C^3$ response;
\item the physical diffusion coefficient is positive and $C^1$ in $a$;
\item its derivative is \eqref{eq:Sigma-response};
\item every smooth selector into the parameter interval yields a smooth
uniformly elliptic field after full-rank coupling.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The companion paper verifies the complete third-response packet.  The data
\eqref{eq:actual-data} satisfy all multiplier hypotheses.  Apply
\cref{thm:pressure,thm:suspension,thm:physical,thm:ellipticity}; strict
positivity follows from the fixed-point obstruction to a coboundary.
\end{proof}

\section{High-frequency moving-family resolvents}\label{sec:highfrequency}

Uniform high-frequency bounds and parameter derivatives are distinct
statements.  We formulate them separately.

\begin{definition}[Strict BDL family witness]\label{def:bdl-witness}
A compact finite-horizon dispersing family carries a strict BDL witness if it
has uniform curvature, horizon, cone, distortion, complexity, flow-box,
compact-embedding, and Lasota--Yorke constants, together with finitely many
contact/nonintegrability witnesses whose temporal-distance derivatives are
bounded below by a positive margin.  A common resonance-free high-frequency
strip and polynomial exponent are included in the data.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform high-frequency BDL bound]\label{thm:uniform-bdl}
A compact family carrying \cref{def:bdl-witness} admits anisotropic fibre
spaces on which the fixed-table Baladi--Demers--Liverani resolvent estimates are
uniform.  After the quadratic stabilization of \cref{thm:stabilization}, there
are $C,b_0,\sigma_0,\nu>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hf-bound}
 \sup_{a\in\A}
 \norm{(z-\widehat A_a)^{-1}P_a}
 \le C(1+\abs{\Im z})^\nu
\end{equation}
for $\Re z\ge-\sigma_0$, $\abs{\Im z}\ge b_0$, outside the declared resonance
set.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every inequality in a strict finite-time witness is open in the $C^r$ table
topology.  Around each parameter, the fixed-table proof therefore persists
with half the positive margins.  A finite cover of the compact parameter set
uniformizes upper constants and positive lower margins.  The bounded maps
$J_a,R_a$ transfer the estimates to the fixed direct sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem gives uniform bounds.  It does not by itself differentiate
$a\mapsto A_a$ on a common graph domain.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Exact similarity families}
Let $Q_a=s(a)Q_0$ be a uniform geometric scaling of a fixed periodic
finite-horizon table, with $0<s_-\le s(a)\le s_+$.  Let $C_a$ be the induced
phase-space transport.  Unit-speed flows satisfy
\[
 \Phi_a^tC_a=C_a\Phi_0^{t/s(a)}.
\]
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generator-similarity}
 A_a=s(a)^{-1}C_aA_0C_a^{-1}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact all-frequency similarity response]\label{thm:similarity-bdl}
On the transported spaces $B_a=C_aB_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolvent-scaling}
 (z-A_a)^{-1}
 =s(a)C_a\bigl(s(a)z-A_0\bigr)^{-1}C_a^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Thus the complete fixed-table low- and high-frequency resolvent theory,
resonances, mixing bounds, and every parameter derivative allowed by $s$ and
$C_a$ are explicit and uniform on compact parameter intervals.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Factor
\[
 z-A_a=C_a\bigl(z-s(a)^{-1}A_0\bigr)C_a^{-1}
 =s(a)^{-1}C_a\bigl(s(a)z-A_0\bigr)C_a^{-1}
\]
and invert.  Differentiation is now the ordinary product rule applied to the
explicit identity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The nonconjugate radial family}

\begin{theorem}[Actual nonconjugate radial high-frequency family]\label{thm:radial-hf}
For a sufficiently small compact interval in the nonconjugate radial Sinai
family of the companion paper, the strict geometric and contact witnesses can
be chosen uniformly.  Hence \eqref{eq:hf-bound} holds uniformly on that family.
Generic table-parameter derivatives of the high-frequency resolvent are not
inferred; they require complete graded generator letters and symbol estimates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The radial interval preserves strict curvature, horizon, separation, and the
chosen finite contact witness.  Openness and compact finite-cover
uniformization give \cref{def:bdl-witness}.  Apply
\cref{thm:uniform-bdl}.  The final sentence is a typing statement: differentiating
the generator requires an independently defined common graph-domain letter.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No universal scalar grazing weight]
The natural collision flux weight $\abs{v\cdot n}$ is invariant across one
reflection, but the ratio of such weights at consecutive collisions is
unbounded near tangency.  One scalar pointwise conjugation therefore cannot
make every table-deformation letter bounded on one ungraded domain.  A graded
symbol packet or a branch-cocycle weight is essential.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

Third-order collision response controls the physical-time pressure root and,
through renewal, the suspension resolvent.  The pressure Hessian, Green--Kubo
series, and martingale bracket agree before any homogenization argument is
introduced.  Banach-bundle stabilization then permits smooth coefficient
composition, while a quantitative noncoboundary condition yields ellipticity.
At high frequency, strict witness uniformization gives robust family bounds,
and exact similarity gives a complete differentiable model.  These are the
frozen spectral inputs for the Doob-selected rough homogenization developed in
the next companion paper.
