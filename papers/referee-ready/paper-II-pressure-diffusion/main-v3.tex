\begin{abstract}
We convert the all-order moving-cut response of the companion paper into
physical-time pressure, diffusion, and suspension resolvents.  A finite Banach
bundle is stabilized inside one fixed direct-sum space.  Joint displacement
and roof twists then have a differentiable simple eigenvalue; the implicit
zero-pressure root gives physical drift and covariance.  An oriented renewal
formula uses distinct entry and exit roof-cell operators.

The diffusion tensor is defined by the symmetrized Green--Kubo series and is
proved equal to the pressure Hessian, the asymptotic variance, and the Gordin
martingale bracket.  This proves symmetry and positive semidefiniteness and
gives a quantitative square-root modulus under a positive spectral margin.

For the same nonconjugate four-branch moving-cut family, branchwise constant
displacements and roofs make the pressure explicit.  If the roof-difference
vector is Diophantine, the constant spectral block stays polynomially separated
from one at every nonzero frequency, while the quotient by constants contracts
uniformly.  We obtain a full all-frequency resolvent estimate and parameter
derivatives of every prescribed finite order.  This is an actual moving-family
high-frequency theorem, not a compactness inference.  We also correct the
finite-horizon geometry of the triangular periodic Lorentz gas: for nearest
neighbour distance one, circular scatterers have finite horizon throughout the
strict interval $\sqrt3/4<r<1/2$, and the property persists under a quantitative
$C^2$ normal-deformation margin.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A collision-map response theorem does not yet identify the coefficients of a
physical-time limit.  The pressure must be twisted simultaneously by spatial
displacement and roof time, the zero-pressure root must be differentiated, and
the suspension renewal formula must orient its first and last incomplete roof
segments correctly.  At high frequency, a family theorem needs a common
operator realization and a quantitative separation mechanism; fixed-table
estimates plus compactness do not supply parameter derivatives.

The companion paper proves all finite response orders and exact predictable
innovations for one nonconjugate four-branch collision system.  Here we use
that same system.  The low-frequency pressure and covariance are explicit.
The high-frequency theorem is driven by a Diophantine roof vector and a strict
quotient contraction.  The mechanism is elementary enough to expose every
constant and strong enough to produce resolvent derivatives.

The spectral perturbation ingredients are classical
\cite{Kato1995,KellerLiverani1999,ParryPollicott1990}.  Renewal and
Dolgopyat-type frequency separation belong to the suspension layer
\cite{Dolgopyat1998,ButterleyLiverani2007}.  The contribution is the complete
moving-family assembly, the physical-root formulas, the exact covariance
identification, and the positive all-frequency theorem.

\section{A common operator realization}\label{sec:common}

Let $a$ range in a compact parameter manifold $\A$.  Suppose $\B_a$ is a
Banach bundle with finitely many charts $U_\alpha$, model spaces $\B_\alpha$
and isomorphisms
\[
 G_{\alpha,a}:\B_a\longrightarrow\B_\alpha
\]
whose transition maps and inverses are uniformly bounded and $C^k$.
Choose smooth $\psi_\alpha$ subordinate to the atlas such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-partition}
 \sum_{\alpha=1}^N\psi_\alpha(a)^2=1.
\end{equation}
Set $\B_*=\bigoplus_\alpha\B_\alpha$ and
\begin{align}
 J_ah&=(\psi_\alpha(a)G_{\alpha,a}h)_\alpha,
 \label{eq:J}\\
 R_a(v_\alpha)_\alpha&=
 \sum_\alpha\psi_\alpha(a)G_{\alpha,a}^{-1}v_\alpha.
 \label{eq:R}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-atlas stabilization]\label{thm:stabilization}
One has $R_aJ_a=\Id_{\B_a}$.  Thus $P_a=J_aR_a$ is a uniformly bounded
projection onto $E_a=J_a\B_a\subset\B_*$, and
\[
 \widehat L_a=J_aL_aR_a\in\mathcal L(\B_*)
\]
has the same parameter regularity as the original fibre family.  Riesz
projections around an isolated eigenvalue away from zero agree with the fibre
projections after restriction to $E_a$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:quadratic-partition} gives
$R_aJ_ah=\sum_\alpha\psi_\alpha^2h=h$.  Hence $P_a^2=P_a$ and
$\operatorname{Ran}P_a=E_a$.  All estimates and derivatives are finite product
rules.  The stabilized operator is zero on the chosen complement, so its
additional spectrum at zero is separated from an eigenvalue near one.
\end{proof}

\section{Joint pressure and the physical-time root}\label{sec:pressure}

Let $\kappa_a:M\to\R^d$ be a displacement and
$\tau_a:M\to(0,\infty)$ a roof.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-twist}
 L_{a,q,s}h=L_a\left(e^{q\cdot\kappa_a-s\tau_a}h\right).
\end{equation}
Assume the graded derivatives through order $N$ supplied by the companion
response theorem, multiplier bounds of the same order, and a uniform contour
containing a simple eigenvalue $\lambda(a,q,s)$ near one.  Put
\[
 \mathscr P(a,q,s)=\log\lambda(a,q,s),
 \qquad \mathscr P(a,0,0)=0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Pressure jet]\label{thm:pressure}
The eigenvalue, Riesz projection, reduced resolvent and pressure are $C^N$ in
the real variables $(a,q,s)$.  Every derivative is a finite sum of direct
operator derivatives and graded reduced-resolvent words of total order at most
$N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On $\B_*$,
\[
 \Piop_{a,q,s}=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma
 (z-\widehat L_{a,q,s})^{-1}\,dz.
\]
Repeated use of
$D(z-L)^{-1}=(z-L)^{-1}(DL)(z-L)^{-1}$ produces the declared words.  The
companion grading makes each composition bounded.  Uniform contour separation
permits differentiation under the integral.  A normalized left--right
eigenpair gives $\lambda$ and its logarithm.
\end{proof}

Let $\Lambda_a(q)$ solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root}
 \mathscr P(a,q,\Lambda_a(q))=0.
\end{equation}
Since
\[
 \mathscr P_s(a,0,0)=-\bar\tau_a,
 \qquad \bar\tau_a=\int\tau_a\,d\mu_a>0,
\]
the implicit-function theorem applies.

\begin{theorem}[Physical drift and covariance response]\label{thm:physical}
The physical root is $C^N$.  Under centered displacement,
$D_q\Lambda_a(0)=0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sigma}
 \Sigma_{ij}(a)=\partial_{q_iq_j}\Lambda_a(0)
 =\frac{\mathscr P_{q_iq_j}(a,0,0)}{\bar\tau_a}.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sigma-a}
 \partial_a\Sigma_{ij}
 =\frac{\mathscr P_{aq_iq_j}}{\bar\tau}
 -\frac{\mathscr P_{q_iq_j}\partial_a\bar\tau}{\bar\tau^2}.
\end{equation}
The physical diffusion matrix is $D^{\rm phys}=\Sigma/2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:root}.  Centering removes first-order cross terms.
Twice differentiating in $q$ gives
$0=\mathscr P_{q_iq_j}+\mathscr P_s\Lambda_{q_iq_j}$, which yields
\eqref{eq:Sigma}; differentiating it in $a$ gives \eqref{eq:Sigma-a}.
\end{proof}

\section{Oriented suspension renewal}\label{sec:renewal}

For a flow observable $G$, define the entry operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entry}
 \mathcal B_{G,a,z}(x)=
 \int_0^{\tau_a(x)}e^{-z(\tau_a(x)-u)}G(x,u)\,du.
\end{equation}
For $F$, define the exit functional
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exit}
 \ell_{F,a,z}(h)=
 \int_M\int_0^{\tau_a(x)}e^{-zu}F(x,u)h(x)\,du\,d\mu_a(x).
\end{equation}
Let $L_{a,z}=L_a(e^{-z\tau_a}\cdot)$.

\begin{theorem}[Oriented renewal identity]\label{thm:renewal}
The Laplace transform of a centered suspension correlation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal}
 \widehat C_{F,G}(a,z)=H_{a,z}^{\rm local}(F,G)
 +\ell_{F,a,z}(I-L_{a,z})^{-1}\mathcal B_{G,a,z},
\end{equation}
where the local term contains paths with no complete return between the two
observations.  Near zero, the centered quantity has the same parameter
regularity as the pressure jet.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Decompose by the number of completed returns.  The first incomplete roof
segment is \eqref{eq:entry}, the last is \eqref{eq:exit}, and the complete
returns give the geometric resolvent.  Near zero split
\[
 (I-L_{a,z})^{-1}
 =\frac{\Piop_{a,z}}{1-\lambda(a,0,z)}+R_{a,z}^{\perp}.
\]
Centering cancels the pole; $\partial_z\lambda(a,0,0)=-\bar\tau_a$ is uniformly
nonzero.
\end{proof}

\section{Symmetric Green--Kubo covariance}\label{sec:GK}

For centered $\kappa_a$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GK}
 C_{ij}^{\rm coll}(a)=
 \int\kappa_{a,i}\kappa_{a,j}\,d\mu_a
 +\sum_{n\ge1}\int\left(
 \kappa_{a,i}\kappa_{a,j}\circ T_a^n+
 \kappa_{a,j}\kappa_{a,i}\circ T_a^n
 \right)d\mu_a.
\end{equation}
Assume absolute summability, as supplied by the response scale.

\begin{theorem}[Pressure--variance--bracket identity]\label{thm:GK}
One has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identities}
 \mathscr P_{q_iq_j}(a,0,0)=C_{ij}^{\rm coll}(a)
 =\int m_{a,i}m_{a,j}\,d\mu_a,
\end{equation}
where $m_a$ is the Gordin martingale increment.  Equivalently,
\[
 \xi^TC^{\rm coll}(a)\xi
 =\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \int\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
 \xi\cdot\kappa_a\circ T_a^k\right)^2d\mu_a\ge0.
\]
Thus $C^{\rm coll}$ and $\Sigma=C^{\rm coll}/\bar\tau$ are symmetric positive
semidefinite.  Their kernel is the coboundary subspace of the declared
spectral class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Twice differentiate the leading eigenvalue equation in $q$.  The direct term
is the instantaneous covariance and the two reduced-resolvent insertions
expand into the two ordered correlation series in \eqref{eq:GK}.  Expanding
the square of partial sums and using absolute summability yields the variance
limit.  Solving the Poisson equation and expanding the martingale square gives
the bracket formula.  A zero bracket is equivalent to a zero martingale part,
hence to a coboundary.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Quantitative square root]\label{cor:sqrt}
If $\Sigma(a)\ge\lambda I$ on a compact set, then its principal square root
satisfies
\[
 \norm{\Sigma(a)^{1/2}-\Sigma(a')^{1/2}}
 \le\frac1{2\sqrt\lambda}
 \norm{\Sigma(a)-\Sigma(a')}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The difference solves a Sylvester equation with both spectra bounded below by
$\sqrt\lambda$.
\end{proof}

The antisymmetric iterated-correlation tensor is recorded separately as an
area anomaly.

\section{The explicit moving-cut pressure model}\label{sec:explicit}

Use the four-branch family
\[
 (L_ah)(y)=\sum_{i=1}^4w_i(a)
 h(s_{i-1}(a)+w_i(a)y)
\]
from the companion paper.  Choose branch displacements $c_i\in\R^d$ and fixed
positive roof values $\tau_i$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-pressure}
 \mathscr P(a,q,s)=
 \log\left(\sum_{i=1}^4w_i(a)e^{q\cdot c_i-s\tau_i}\right).
\end{equation}
Let $\bar c(a)=\sum_iw_i(a)c_i$ and
$\widetilde c_i=c_i-\bar c(a)$.

\begin{theorem}[Actual physical coefficient package]\label{thm:actual-low}
For the nonconjugate moving-cut family:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the pressure and physical root are real analytic;
\item all positive-time branch correlations vanish and
\[
 C^{\rm coll}(a)=\sum_iw_i(a)
 \widetilde c_i(a)\otimes\widetilde c_i(a);
\]
\item $\Sigma(a)=C^{\rm coll}(a)/\bar\tau(a)$;
\item if the centered branch vectors span $\R^d$ for every $a$, compactness
gives $\Sigma(a)\ge\lambda I$;
\item the oriented low-frequency suspension response is obtained by direct
differentiation of \eqref{eq:explicit-pressure} and the roof-cell operators.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exact innovation theorem of the companion paper gives independent branch
symbols with probabilities $w_i(a)$ for a fixed parameter.  The constant
spectral block of the twisted operator is the sum in
\eqref{eq:explicit-pressure}; the quotient contracts uniformly.  Independence
makes all positive-time correlations vanish.  The remaining assertions are
finite-dimensional differentiation and compactness of the smallest covariance
eigenvalue.
\end{proof}

\section{Diophantine all-frequency response}\label{sec:highfreq}

Let
\[
 \omega=(\tau_2-\tau_1,\tau_3-\tau_1,\tau_4-\tau_1).
\]

\begin{assumption}[Diophantine roof]\label{ass:diophantine}
There are $\gamma>0$ and $\nu\ge0$ such that for all $\abs b\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dio}
 \max_{2\le j\le4}
 \dist\bigl(b(\tau_j-\tau_1),2\pi\mathbb Z\bigr)
 \ge\gamma(1+\abs b)^{-\nu}.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

Such vectors form a full-measure set; algebraic vectors satisfying the standard
linear-independence hypotheses give explicit examples through the Diophantine
approximation theorems.

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lb}
 (L_{a,b}h)(y)=
 \sum_iw_i(a)e^{-ib\tau_i}
 h(s_{i-1}(a)+w_i(a)y)
\end{equation}
and
\[
 \lambda_a(b)=\sum_iw_i(a)e^{-ib\tau_i}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Quantitative phase separation]\label{lem:phase}
Under \cref{ass:diophantine}, there is $c>0$, uniform in $a\in I$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda-separation}
 1-\abs{\lambda_a(b)}
 \ge c(1+\abs b)^{-2\nu},
 \qquad \abs b\ge1.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $p_*=\min_{a,i}w_i(a)>0$.  The identity
\[
 1-\abs{\lambda_a(b)}^2
 =2\sum_{i<j}w_i(a)w_j(a)
 \bigl[1-\cos(b(\tau_i-\tau_j))\bigr]
\]
shows that any phase separation $\delta\le\pi$ contributes at least
$c_0p_*^2\delta^2$.  Apply \eqref{eq:dio} to a difference from $\tau_1$ and
use $1-\abs\lambda\ge(1-\abs\lambda^2)/2$.
\end{proof}

Use the decomposition $W^{1,1}(\T)=\C1\oplus W^{1,1}_0(\T)$.  Constants are
invariant under $L_{a,b}$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_a(b)$.  The quotient by
constants contracts by at most $\rho=9/20$ in the derivative norm.

\begin{theorem}[Actual all-frequency moving-family resolvent]\label{thm:highfreq}
Under \cref{ass:diophantine}, for every finite $r\ge1$ there is $C_r$ such
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolvent-bound}
 \norm{(I-L_{a,b})^{-1}}_{W^{r,1}\to W^{r,1}}
 \le C_r(1+\abs b)^{2\nu},
 \qquad \abs b\ge1,\ a\in I.
\end{equation}
For every finite $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolvent-derivatives}
 \norm{\partial_a^k(I-L_{a,b})^{-1}}_{W^{r+k,1}\to W^{r,1}}
 \le C_{r,k}(1+\abs b)^{2\nu(k+1)}.
\end{equation}
The same bounds, with a larger constant, hold on the vertical line
$z=\sigma+ib$ for $\abs\sigma$ sufficiently small.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Relative to constants and the zero-mean quotient, $L_{a,b}$ is upper
triangular.  Its diagonal blocks are $\lambda_a(b)$ and a quotient operator
$B_{a,b}$ with spectral radius at most $\rho$.  The off-diagonal functional is
uniformly bounded.  The block inverse formula gives
\[
 \norm{(I-L_{a,b})^{-1}}
 \le C\left(1+\abs{1-\lambda_a(b)}^{-1}ight),
\]
which together with \cref{lem:phase} proves \eqref{eq:resolvent-bound}.
The roof is independent of $a$, so the exact parameter letters from the
companion paper are uniform in $b$.  Repeated differentiation of the inverse
produces finite sums
\[
 R_b(\partial_a^{j_1}L_b)R_b\cdots
 (\partial_a^{j_m}L_b)R_b,
 \qquad j_1+\cdots+j_m=k,
\]
with at most $k+1$ resolvents.  Apply \eqref{eq:resolvent-bound} and the graded
letter estimates to obtain \eqref{eq:resolvent-derivatives}.  A small real part
perturbs the two diagonal blocks continuously and preserves the strict
margins.
\end{proof}

This theorem supplies full high-frequency moving-family response on the same
nonconjugate system used for the low-frequency coefficients.

\section{Correct finite-horizon triangular Lorentz geometry}\label{sec:triangle}

Let $\Lambda$ be the triangular lattice generated by
$e_1=(1,0)$ and $e_2=(1/2,\sqrt3/2)$.  Put a circular scatterer of radius $r$
at every lattice point.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp strict finite-horizon interval]\label{thm:triangle}
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-window}
 \frac{\sqrt3}{4}<r<\frac12,
\end{equation}
then the periodic Lorentz gas has disjoint strictly convex scatterers and
finite horizon.  If a $C^2$ normal deformation contains the disk of radius
$r-\delta$, is contained in the disk of radius $r+\delta$, and
\[
 r-\delta>\frac{\sqrt3}{4},
 \qquad r+\delta<\frac12,
\]
with positive curvature, then the deformed periodic table also has finite
horizon.  Hence finite horizon holds on an explicit open deformation class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound in \eqref{eq:r-window} is exactly the nearest-neighbour
nonoverlap condition.  An infinite free line in a periodic configuration has
either irrational direction modulo the lattice, in which case its projection
is dense and it meets a scatterer, or is parallel to a primitive lattice vector
$v$.  Rows of lattice points parallel to $v$ are separated by
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{area}(\R^2/\Lambda)}{\abs v}
 =\frac{\sqrt3/2}{\abs v}
 \le\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\]
A corridor between two adjacent rows exists only if this spacing exceeds
$2r$.  The lower bound in \eqref{eq:r-window} excludes every primitive
direction.  Compactness of the unit tangent bundle modulo collisions upgrades
the absence of an infinite free line to a uniform bound on free flight.
For the deformation, the contained disks of radius $r-\delta$ still block all
corridors, while the containing disks of radius $r+\delta$ remain disjoint.
Positive curvature preserves dispersing collisions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Nonconjugate radial finite-horizon family]
Choose $r_0$ in the strict interval \eqref{eq:r-window} and
$r(a)=r_0+a$ on a sufficiently small nonzero interval.  The resulting specular
Sinai family remains finite horizon and nonoverlapping.  The multiplier of a
shortest normal two-collision orbit changes with $a$, so distinct parameters
are not locally $C^1$ conjugate.
\end{corollary}

\section{Coefficient fields}\label{sec:fields}

Let $\Theta(x,p)$ and a full-rank coupling matrix $B(x,p)$ be globally
$C^{N,\alpha}$ with bounded derivatives.  Define
\[
 A(x,p)=\frac12B(x,p)\Sigma(\Theta(x,p))B(x,p)^T.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Global coefficient and ellipticity lift]\label{thm:lift}
If the parameter coefficients are $C^{N,\alpha}$, then the drift, covariance,
area anomaly and $A$ are globally $C^{N,\alpha}$.  If
$\Sigma\ge\lambda_\Sigma I$ and
$BB^T\ge\lambda_B^2I$, then
\[
 A(x,p)\ge\frac12\lambda_\Sigma\lambda_B^2I.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the ordinary chain rule in the fixed parameter space.  For $z\in\R^m$,
\[
 z^TAz=\tfrac12(B^Tz)^T\Sigma(B^Tz)
 \ge\tfrac12\lambda_\Sigma\lambda_B^2\abs z^2.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

The nonconjugate moving-cut family now has both low- and high-frequency
physical response.  Diophantine separation supplies the high-frequency
mechanism, while quotient contraction prevents any hidden spectral branch
from approaching one.  The corrected triangular Lorentz theorem supplies the
proper finite-horizon geometry for the specular benchmark.  Symmetry,
positivity and square-root regularity of the diffusion tensor follow from
proved covariance identities rather than notation.
