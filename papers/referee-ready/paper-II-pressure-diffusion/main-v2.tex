\begin{abstract}
We convert third-order collision response into physical-time spectral and
probabilistic coefficients.  A finite Banach bundle is first stabilized inside
one fixed direct-sum space, so cross-parameter Riesz projections and reduced
resolvents are well defined.  Joint displacement--roof twists then yield a
three-times differentiable pressure.  Its implicit physical-time root gives
drift and covariance, and an oriented renewal formula with distinct entry and
exit roof-cell operators gives the low-frequency suspension resolvent.

The covariance is defined by a symmetrized Green--Kubo series.  We prove that it
is equal to the pressure Hessian, the limiting variance, and the Gordin
martingale bracket; symmetry and positive semidefiniteness therefore follow
rather than being assumed.  Uniform positivity gives a quantitative square-root
modulus and a uniformly elliptic macroscopic field.  The four-branch moving-cut
family of the companion paper admits an explicit branchwise displacement and
roof for which all pressure, diffusion, and parameter-response formulas are
closed.  Finally, we prove an all-frequency family theorem for exact geometric
similarities by an exact generator-resolvent identity.  We make no inference
from compact geometry alone to parameter differentiability of arbitrary
nonconjugate high-frequency billiard resolvents.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The collision map is the natural location for moving-singularity response,
whereas a slow equation evolves in physical time.  Passing from one to the
other requires more than dividing a discrete covariance by a mean roof.  One
must control a jointly twisted operator, identify the physical pressure root,
orient the entry and exit roof integrals correctly, and distinguish the
symmetric diffusion tensor from the antisymmetric area correction.

A second issue is functional-analytic.  Local trivializations of a Banach
bundle do not by themselves put all operators in one common operator space.
We give a finite stabilization that produces a genuine fixed ambient space.
This removes an ambiguity in parameter differences and Kato formulas.

The low-frequency theorem is sufficient for the diffusion and homogenization
results in the sequel.  High-frequency response is treated only where it is
actually proved: an exact similarity family.  A fixed-table Dolgopyat or BDL
bound plus compactness is not used to claim differentiability for an arbitrary
nonconjugate family.  This scope distinction is central.

The pressure construction is standard in spirit; see
\cite{Kato1995,KellerLiverani1999,ParryPollicott1990}.  Suspension renewal and
Dolgopyat-type estimates belong to a separate layer
\cite{Dolgopyat1998,ButterleyLiverani2007}.  Our contribution here is the
parameter-compatible assembly with the moving-cut response scale of the
companion paper, the physical-root derivative formulas, the exact covariance
identification, and the family-level scope theorem.

\section{A fixed ambient operator space}\label{sec:stabilization}

Let $a$ range in a compact parameter manifold $\A$.  Suppose $\B_a$ is a
Banach bundle with a finite atlas.  On a chart $U_\alpha$ let
\[
 G_{\alpha,a}:\B_a\longrightarrow\B_\alpha
\]
be a bounded isomorphism whose transition maps and inverses are uniformly
bounded and $C^3$ in $a$.

Choose smooth functions $\psi_\alpha$ subordinate to the atlas such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-partition}
 \sum_{\alpha=1}^N\psi_\alpha(a)^2=1.
\end{equation}
Such a family is obtained by normalizing a finite smooth subordinate family
with no common zero by the square root of the sum of its squares.

Set
\[
 \B_*:=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^N\B_\alpha
\]
and define
\begin{align}
 J_ah&=(\psi_\alpha(a)G_{\alpha,a}h)_\alpha,
 \label{eq:J}\
 R_a(v_\alpha)_\alpha&=
 \sum_{\alpha=1}^N\psi_\alpha(a)G_{\alpha,a}^{-1}v_\alpha.
 \label{eq:R}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-atlas stabilization]\label{thm:stabilization}
One has $R_aJ_a=\Id_{\B_a}$.  Hence
\[
 P_a=J_aR_a
\]
is a uniformly bounded projection onto $E_a=J_a\B_a\subset\B_*$.  If
$L_a:\B_a\to\B_a$, then
\[
 \widehat L_a=J_aL_aR_a\in\mathcal L(\B_*)
\]
has the same parameter regularity as the atlas and the fibre operator.  The
leading eigenvalue near one and its Riesz projection are unchanged after
restriction to $E_a$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:quadratic-partition},
\[
 R_aJ_ah=\sum_\alpha\psi_\alpha(a)^2h=h.
\]
Thus $P_a^2=P_a$ and its range is $E_a$.  Finiteness of the atlas gives the
uniform bounds and differentiability.  The stabilized operator vanishes on a
complement chosen through $P_a$, so the additional spectral point zero is
separated from the leading eigenvalue near one.
\end{proof}

\section{Jointly twisted pressure}\label{sec:pressure}

Let $L_a$ be the collision transfer operator.  Let
$\kappa_a:M\to\R^d$ be a displacement and $\tau_a:M\to(0,\infty)$ a roof.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twist}
 L_{a,q,s}h=L_a\left(e^{q\cdot\kappa_a-s\tau_a}h\right),
 \qquad (q,s)\in\C^d\times\C.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Three-jet spectral window]\label{ass:window}
On the graded common-space realization, the total derivatives of
$L_{a,q,s}$ through order three exist with the types required by the companion
third-jet theorem.  In a neighbourhood of $(q,s)=(0,0)$ there is a uniform
contour enclosing one simple eigenvalue $\lambda(a,q,s)$ and no other
spectrum.
\end{assumption}

Define
\[
 \mathscr P(a,q,s)=\log\lambda(a,q,s),
 \qquad \mathscr P(a,0,0)=0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Pressure three-jet]\label{thm:pressure}
Under \cref{ass:window}, the leading eigenvalue, its Riesz projection, the
reduced resolvent and $\mathscr P$ are $C^3$ in the real variables $(a,q,s)$.
Every derivative is a finite sum of a direct operator derivative and a
well-typed reduced-resolvent word.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the fixed space,
\[
 \Piop_{a,q,s}=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma
 (z-\widehat L_{a,q,s})^{-1}\,dz.
\]
Differentiate under the contour using
\[
 D(z-L)^{-1}=(z-L)^{-1}(DL)(z-L)^{-1}.
\]
At order at most three, the resulting words are precisely those controlled by
the graded third-jet hypothesis.  A normalized left--right eigenpair gives
$\lambda$, and the chosen logarithm gives $\mathscr P$.
\end{proof}

\section{The physical-time root}\label{sec:root}

Let $\Lambda_a(q)$ be the small root of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root}
 \mathscr P(a,q,\Lambda_a(q))=0.
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ps}
 \partial_s\mathscr P(a,0,0)
 =-\int\tau_a\,d\mu_a=-\bar\tau_a<0,
\end{equation}
the implicit-function theorem applies.

\begin{theorem}[Physical drift and covariance]\label{thm:physical}
The map $(a,q)\mapsto\Lambda_a(q)$ is $C^3$.  Its gradient at zero is the
physical mean velocity.  If the displacement is centered, then
$D_q\Lambda_a(0)=0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sigma}
 \Sigma_{ij}(a)
 =\partial_{q_iq_j}\Lambda_a(0)
 =\frac{\mathscr P_{q_iq_j}(a,0,0)}{\bar\tau_a}.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sigma-response}
 \partial_a\Sigma_{ij}
 =\frac{\mathscr P_{aq_iq_j}}{\bar\tau}
 -\frac{\mathscr P_{q_iq_j}\,\partial_a\bar\tau}
       {\bar\tau^2}.
\end{equation}
The physical diffusion matrix is $D^{\rm phys}=\Sigma/2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:root}.  Centering removes all first-gradient cross
terms at $q=0$.  Twice differentiating in $q$ gives
$0=\mathscr P_{q_iq_j}+\mathscr P_s\Lambda_{q_iq_j}$, so
\eqref{eq:Ps} yields \eqref{eq:Sigma}.  Differentiation in $a$ gives
\eqref{eq:Sigma-response}.  The mixed derivative has total order three.
\end{proof}

\section{Oriented suspension renewal}\label{sec:renewal}

Let
\[
 M_a^\tau=\{(x,u):0\le u<\tau_a(x)\}/\sim
\]
be the suspension.  Entry and exit roof operators are different.  For a flow
observable $G$, define the entry operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entry}
 \mathcal B_{G,a,z}(x)
 =\int_0^{\tau_a(x)}e^{-z(\tau_a(x)-u)}G(x,u)\,du.
\end{equation}
For $F$, define the exit functional
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exit}
 \ell_{F,a,z}(h)
 =\int_M\int_0^{\tau_a(x)}e^{-zu}F(x,u)h(x)\,du\,d\mu_a(x).
\end{equation}
Set $L_{a,z}=L_a(e^{-z\tau_a}\cdot)$.

\begin{proposition}[Oriented renewal formula]\label{prop:renewal}
For roof-cell observables for which the displayed integrals exist, the Laplace
transform of the centered correlation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal}
 \widehat C_{F,G}(a,z)
 =H_{a,z}^{\rm local}(F,G)
 +\ell_{F,a,z}(I-L_{a,z})^{-1}\mathcal B_{G,a,z}.
\end{equation}
The term $H^{\rm local}$ contains trajectories with no complete return between
the two observations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Decompose a trajectory according to the number of complete returns between
the observation times.  Zero returns give $H^{\rm local}$.  For $n\ge1$, the
first incomplete roof segment is encoded by \eqref{eq:entry}, the last by
\eqref{eq:exit}, and the $n-1$ complete returns by $L_{a,z}^{n-1}$.  Summing
this geometric series gives \eqref{eq:renewal}.
\end{proof}

Near zero,
\[
 (I-L_{a,z})^{-1}
 =\frac{\Piop_{a,z}}{1-\lambda(a,0,z)}+R_{a,z}^{\perp}.
\]
The pole cancels in centered correlations.

\begin{theorem}[Low-frequency suspension response]\label{thm:lowfreq}
Under \cref{ass:window} and $C^3$ roof-cell multiplier bounds, the centered
quantity in \eqref{eq:renewal} has continuous derivatives of total order at
most three in $(a,z)$ for $z$ near zero.  This theorem requires no
high-frequency family graph domain.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the finite local term by Leibniz' rule, including roof endpoints.
The long-return term is controlled by \cref{thm:pressure} and the reduced
resolvent decomposition.  Uniform nonvanishing of $\bar\tau_a$ controls the
canceled pole.
\end{proof}

\section{The symmetric Green--Kubo tensor}\label{sec:GK}

Let $\kappa_a$ be centered and assume absolute summability of its correlations.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GK}
 C_{ij}^{\rm coll}(a)
 =\int\kappa_{a,i}\kappa_{a,j}\,d\mu_a
 +\sum_{n\ge1}\int\left[
 \kappa_{a,i}\,\kappa_{a,j}\circ T_a^n
 +\kappa_{a,j}\,\kappa_{a,i}\circ T_a^n
 \right]d\mu_a.
\end{equation}
This is the symmetrized series; an unsymmetrized one-sided matrix is not called
the diffusion tensor.

\begin{theorem}[Pressure--variance--martingale identity]\label{thm:GK}
One has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pressure-GK}
 \mathscr P_{q_iq_j}(a,0,0)=C_{ij}^{\rm coll}(a).
\end{equation}
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance-limit}
 \xi^TC^{\rm coll}(a)\xi
 =\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
 \int\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}
 \xi\cdot\kappa_a\circ T_a^k\right)^2d\mu_a\ge0.
\end{equation}
If $m_a$ is the Gordin martingale increment, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket}
 C^{\rm coll}(a)=\int m_a\otimes m_a\,d\mu_a.
\end{equation}
Thus $C^{\rm coll}$ and $\Sigma=C^{\rm coll}/\bar\tau$ are symmetric positive
semidefinite.  Their kernel consists precisely of the declared coboundary
directions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating the leading eigenvalue equation twice in $q$ gives the
instantaneous covariance and the two reduced-resolvent insertions.  Expanding
the resolvents by their convergent Neumann series gives \eqref{eq:GK}.
Expanding the square in \eqref{eq:variance-limit}, dividing by $N$, and using
absolute summability gives the same series.  Solving the Poisson equation and
expanding the square of the martingale increment gives \eqref{eq:bracket}.
Nonnegativity follows from either of the last two formulas.  Vanishing bracket
is equivalent to a zero martingale part, hence to a coboundary in the spectral
class.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Square-root modulus]\label{cor:sqrt}
If $\Sigma(a)\ge\lambda I$ on a compact parameter set, then the principal
square root is Lipschitz:
\[
 \norm{\Sigma(a)^{1/2}-\Sigma(a')^{1/2}}
 \le\frac1{2\sqrt\lambda}
 \norm{\Sigma(a)-\Sigma(a')}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the integral representation of the square root, or the Sylvester equation
$S\Delta+\Delta S=E$ with $S\ge\sqrt\lambda I$.
\end{proof}

The antisymmetric part of the iterated correlation is recorded separately as
an area anomaly; it is not inserted into $\Sigma$.

\section{Coefficient fields and ellipticity}\label{sec:fields}

Let $\Theta:\R^m\times\R^m\to\A$ and $B(x,p)$ be globally $C^{3,\alpha}$
with bounded derivatives.  At this stage $p$ is an external cotangent signal.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Afield}
 A(x,p)=\frac12B(x,p)\Sigma(\Theta(x,p))B(x,p)^T.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Coefficient lift]\label{thm:lift}
If the parameter coefficients are $C^{3,\alpha}$, then the physical drift,
area anomaly, roof-normalized response and $A(x,p)$ are $C^{3,\alpha}$.  If
\[
 \Sigma(a)\ge\lambda_\Sigma I,
 \qquad B(x,p)B(x,p)^T\ge\lambda_B^2I,
\]
then
\[
 A(x,p)\ge\frac12\lambda_\Sigma\lambda_B^2I.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The stabilization puts all parameter maps in fixed spaces.  The ordinary
finite-dimensional chain rule and product estimates give the regularity.  For
$z\in\R^m$,
\[
 z^TAz=\tfrac12(B^Tz)^T\Sigma(B^Tz)
 \ge\tfrac12\lambda_\Sigma\lambda_B^2\abs z^2.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{An explicit moving-cut pressure and diffusion model}\label{sec:actual}

Use the four-branch map of the companion paper.  Its branch process under
Lebesgue probability is i.i.d. with probabilities $w_i(a)$.  Choose vectors
$c_i\in\R^d$ and positive roof values
\[
 \tau_i(a)=\tau_i^0+a\dot\tau_i,
\]
with a uniform positive lower bound.  Assume the additive subgroup generated
by the differences $\tau_i^0-\tau_j^0$ is dense in $\R$; this excludes a
nonzero lattice frequency near the origin.

For branchwise constant displacement and roof, the pressure is explicit:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-P}
 \mathscr P(a,q,s)
 =\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^4
 w_i(a)e^{q\cdot c_i-s\tau_i(a)}\right).
\end{equation}
Let
\[
 \bar c(a)=\sum_iw_i(a)c_i,
 \qquad \widetilde c_i(a)=c_i-\bar c(a).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Actual physical coefficient package]\label{thm:actual}
For the model above:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the joint pressure and physical root are real analytic;
\item the collision covariance is
\[
 C^{\rm coll}(a)=\sum_iw_i(a)
 \widetilde c_i(a)\otimes\widetilde c_i(a);
\]
all positive-time correlations vanish;
\item the physical covariance is
$\Sigma(a)=C^{\rm coll}(a)/\bar\tau(a)$;
\item if the centered vectors span $\R^d$ uniformly, then
$\Sigma(a)\ge\lambda I$ on the compact parameter interval;
\item the low-frequency suspension response and all derivatives required in
\cref{thm:lowfreq} are explicit derivatives of \eqref{eq:explicit-P} and the
oriented roof-cell formula.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Cylinder measures are products of branch widths, proving independence.  The
leading eigenvalue on the locally constant eigendirection is the sum inside
\eqref{eq:explicit-P}; the remaining spectrum is uniformly separated by the
companion contraction.  The covariance formula is the variance of one
centered symbol.  Compactness and the spanning assumption give the uniform
lower eigenvalue.  The remaining conclusions follow by direct differentiation.
\end{proof}

This is a complete moving-cut physical-time instance.  It is not a claim about
all specular scatterer deformations.

\section{An exact all-frequency similarity family}\label{sec:similarity}

Let a reference flow $\Phi_0^t$ have generator $\mathcal A_0$ on an
anisotropic space $\B_0$ and suppose its full-frequency BDL/resolvent theorem is
known.  Let $C_a:\B_0\to\B_a$ be a bounded geometric similarity and
$s(a)>0$ a $C^3$ time scale such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow-sim}
 \Phi_a^tC_a=C_a\Phi_0^{t/s(a)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact similarity resolvent]\label{thm:similarity}
The generators and resolvents satisfy
\begin{align}
 \mathcal A_a&=s(a)^{-1}C_a\mathcal A_0C_a^{-1},
 \label{eq:A-sim}\
 (z-\mathcal A_a)^{-1}
 &=s(a)C_a(s(a)z-\mathcal A_0)^{-1}C_a^{-1}.
 \label{eq:R-sim}
\end{align}
Consequently every full-frequency bound, resonance-free region and resonance
of the reference flow is transported with the exact scaling prescribed by
\eqref{eq:R-sim}.  If $s$ and $C_a$ are $C^k$ on transported spaces, the
parameter derivatives through order $k$ follow by differentiating the exact
identity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:flow-sim} at $t=0$ to obtain \eqref{eq:A-sim}.
Multiplication verifies that the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:R-sim} is the
inverse of $z-\mathcal A_a$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Nonconjugate high-frequency families]
A fixed-table BDL theorem plus compactness does not by itself prove
parameter differentiability on a common graph domain.  For an arbitrary
nonconjugate family, such a result requires an explicit graded generator-symbol
packet.  No stronger conclusion is used in this series.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

The physical diffusion tensor is the symmetric pressure Hessian per unit roof
time, not an unsymmetrized one-sided correlation matrix.  Entry and exit roof
operators have distinct orientations.  The four-branch model closes the
low-frequency moving-cut chain explicitly, while the exact similarity theorem
closes the all-frequency family problem at a scope where parameter derivatives
are identities rather than compactness guesses.
