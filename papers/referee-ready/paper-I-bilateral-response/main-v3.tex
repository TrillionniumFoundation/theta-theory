\begin{abstract}
We prove a bilateral response theorem for transfer operators whose physical
branch cuts move with a parameter.  A source-side recovery estimate and a
target-side recovery estimate are interpolated under a sharp crossed-rate
condition, producing an absolutely summable product tail in the two time
variables.  Difference quotients converge in the complete
$\ell^1(\N^2)$ response topology, and a joint-Cauchy theorem totalizes the
third source on a graded Banach scale.

The main result is an explicit, genuinely nonconjugate deterministic collision
system.  It is a four-branch full-cover pinball billiard with three moving
collision seams.  On periodic Sobolev--variation spaces the centered transfer
operator contracts uniformly, and every finite parameter derivative has an
exact formula losing precisely its order in regularity.  Consequently the
family has product response and spectral jets of every prescribed finite
order.  Distinct parameters are not $C^1$ conjugate because the multipliers of
three isolated fixed points vary in disjoint ranges.

A second theorem identifies the symbolic process of the same nonautonomous
system.  Even when the parameter at time $k$ is a predictable function of the
previous branch history, the current fibre coordinate is conditionally
Lebesgue, the next branch has the declared parameter-dependent probability,
and the image coordinate is again uniform and independent of the enlarged
past.  Thus the martingale arrays used in the companion pressure and
homogenization papers are exact innovations of this deterministic moving-cut
system, not an external random replacement.  We also prove a source-specific
all-order invariant-projector theorem for a nonconjugate radial family of
finite-horizon specular Sinai billiards.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

When a smooth operator family acts on one Banach space, isolated spectral data
are governed by classical perturbation theory \cite{Kato1995}.  A moving
singularity produces a different object: differentiating the branch partition
creates oriented currents on moving faces.  A propagated current remembers a
waiting time before insertion and a second waiting time after insertion.  A
one-time estimate, or an additive bound of the form $\rho^m+\rho^n$, does not
control the double response series.

The first goal of this paper is to isolate and prove the exact two-time
mechanism.  The second is stronger: we construct a single deterministic
collision system in which the mechanism is verified directly and remains
closed under every future insertion on a fixed graded scale.  The third is to
show that the symbolic martingale structure used downstream is generated by
that same nonautonomous map.  This removes a gap that often occurs when a
spectral example and a probabilistic homogenization example are presented on
different systems.

The functional-analytic setting is compatible with strong--weak perturbation
methods \cite{KellerLiverani1999}, higher response for piecewise expanding
systems \cite{BaladiSmania2008}, and anisotropic spaces for hyperbolic maps with
singularities \cite{DemersLiverani2008,BaladiDemersLiverani2018}.  Our actual
model is deliberately explicit: all constants are computed from four branch
widths.  The radial specular theorem in \cref{sec:specular} is geometrically
different and is stated with its exact source class.

The main abstract results are \cref{thm:bilateral,thm:fdq,thm:totalization}.
The actual all-order theorem and the exact innovation theorem are
\cref{thm:all-order,thm:innovations}.  Nonconjugacy is
\cref{thm:nonconjugacy}, and the collision realization is
\cref{prop:pinball}.

\section{Occurrencewise two-time response}\label{sec:abstract}

Let $a$ range in a compact interval $\A$.  Let $L_a$ be a Perron--Frobenius
operator with simple invariant projection $\Pi_a$ and centered operator
\[
 Q_a=L_a-\Pi_a.
\]
All fibres have been transported to a common measurable reference space.

\begin{definition}[Same-occurrence registry]\label{def:registry}
An occurrence label $\omega$ contains a physical face, incident side, owner
branch, oriented word, parameter chamber and refinement chart.  Labels are
identified only on a common refinement preserving all these data.  Orientation
reversal changes the sign but not the UID.
\end{definition}

The first parameter source is represented as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 \partial_aL_a=K_a^{\rm reg}+
 \sum_{\omega\in\Omega_a}J_{a,\omega}.
\end{equation}
For centered $g$ and an admissible test $f$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atom}
 A_{m,n}^{\omega}(a;g,f)
 =\pair{Q_a^nJ_{a,\omega}Q_a^m g}{f}.
\end{equation}
Distinct UIDs are not cancelled before an identity on a common refinement is
proved.

\begin{assumption}[Crossed envelopes]\label{ass:crossed}
There are $0<\alpha,\beta<1$, $\Gamma_+,\Gamma_-\ge1$, and occurrence
constants $C_\omega^\pm$ such that
\begin{align}
 \abs{A_{m,n}^{\omega}}
 &\le C_\omega^-\alpha^m\Gamma_+^n
 \norm g_{X_-}\norm f_Y,\label{eq:reverse}\\
 \abs{A_{m,n}^{\omega}}
 &\le C_\omega^+\Gamma_-^m\beta^n
 \norm g_{X_-}\norm f_Y.\label{eq:forward}
\end{align}
\end{assumption}

Set
\[
 A_0=\log(1/\alpha),\quad B_0=\log(1/\beta),\quad
 C_0=\log\Gamma_+,\quad D_0=\log\Gamma_-.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Sharp bilateral product tail]\label{thm:bilateral}
Assume \cref{ass:crossed} and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 A_0B_0>C_0D_0.
\end{equation}
Let
\[
 \lambda_*=
 \frac{B_0+D_0}{A_0+B_0+C_0+D_0},
 \qquad
 \log\rho_*=
 \frac{C_0D_0-A_0B_0}{A_0+B_0+C_0+D_0}.
\]
Then $0<\rho_*<1$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 \abs{A_{m,n}^{\omega}}
 \le
 (C_\omega^-)^{\lambda_*}(C_\omega^+)^{1-\lambda_*}
 \rho_*^{m+n}\norm g_{X_-}\norm f_Y.
\end{equation}
If the interpolated occurrence constants are summable, then
\[
 \sum_\omega\sum_{m,n\ge0}\abs{A_{m,n}^{\omega}}<\infty.
\]
Moreover, the strict window \eqref{eq:gap} is equivalent to the existence of
an interpolation parameter for which both time factors are strictly smaller
than one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Raise \eqref{eq:reverse} to the power $\lambda$ and
\eqref{eq:forward} to the power $1-\lambda$.  Geometric interpolation gives
\[
 \abs{A_{m,n}^{\omega}}
 \le (C_\omega^-)^\lambda(C_\omega^+)^{1-\lambda}
 (\alpha^\lambda\Gamma_-^{1-\lambda})^m
 (\Gamma_+^\lambda\beta^{1-\lambda})^n
 \norm g_{X_-}\norm f_Y.
\]
The logarithms of the two time factors agree at $\lambda_*$ and their common
value is $\log\rho_*$.  It is negative exactly when
$A_0B_0>C_0D_0$.  Summation of the two geometric series and then the
occurrence constants proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Invariant regularity ladder]\label{cor:ladder}
Suppose $Q_a$ contracts on the centered parts of $X_{r+k}$ and $X_r$, and
$K_a^{(k)}:X_{r+k}\to X_r$ is bounded and centered.  Then
\[
 \abs{\pair{Q_a^nK_a^{(k)}Q_a^m g}{f}}
 \le C_{r,k}\rho_{r}^{\,n}\rho_{r+k}^{\,m}
 \norm g_{X_{r+k}}\norm f_{X_r^*}.
\]
Thus every later insertion is a bounded composition on the same invariant
ladder; no assembly reset is required.
\end{corollary}

\section{Finite differences and totalized higher sources}\label{sec:higher}

For $h\ne0$, put $K_{a,h}=(L_{a+h}-L_a)/h$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete finite-DQ convergence]\label{thm:fdq}
Assume convergence of the finite-DQ response array uniformly on every finite
rectangle in $(m,n)$, and assume one summable product envelope independent of
small $h$ for the finite-DQ and limiting arrays.  Then
\[
 \sum_{m,n\ge0}
 \abs{\pair{Q_{a+h}^nK_{a,h}Q_a^m g
 -Q_a^nK_aQ_a^m g}{f}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a finite rectangle whose common envelope tail is below
$\varepsilon/3$.  Uniform convergence controls the finite head; the two tails
are controlled by the two copies of the envelope.  This is convergence in the
complete $\ell^1(\N^2)$ response topology.
\end{proof}

Let $d=(K,\varepsilon,\mathcal P)$ be a depth, intersection and refinement
cutoff, and write
\[
 G_{3,d}(a)=
 \Tot_{\mathfrak S_d}\{\eta_{a,\omega}:\omega\in I_d\}
 +S_d^{\rm seam}(a).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Joint-Cauchy totalization]\label{thm:totalization}
Assume each finite source is continuous in a complete graded source space,
\[
 \sup_a\norm{\eta_{a,\omega}}_{\rm CM2}\le b_\omega,
 \qquad\sum_\omega b_\omega<\infty,
\]
and the intersection and refinement defects are bounded by moduli tending to
zero.  Suppose common refinements preserve UIDs and cancel opposite
orientations, and reduced resolvents preserve every declared level.  Then
\[
 \norm{G_{3,d_1}-G_{3,d_2}}
 \le2\sum_{\omega\notin I_d}b_\omega
 +2\omega_{\rm vert}(\varepsilon)
 +2\omega_{\rm seam}(\mathcal P)
\]
for $d_1,d_2\succeq d$.  Consequently the third source and every graded
third-order Kato word converge uniformly in $a$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pass to a common refinement.  Common atoms cancel by UID and orientation.
Only the occurrence tail and the two declared defect sets remain.  The stated
bound follows, completeness gives the limit, and the graded mappings make the
resolvent words composable.
\end{proof}

\section{The nonconjugate four-branch family}\label{sec:family}

Let
\[
 I=[-1/40,1/40]
\]
and define
\begin{align*}
 w_1(a)&=1/10+a,&w_2(a)&=1/5+2a,\\
 w_3(a)&=3/10-a,&w_4(a)&=2/5-2a.
\end{align*}
Let $s_0=0$, $s_i=\sum_{j\le i}w_j$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
 T_a(x)=\frac{x-s_{i-1}(a)}{w_i(a)},
 \qquad x\in I_i(a)=[s_{i-1}(a),s_i(a)).
\end{equation}
Each branch maps bijectively onto $[0,1)$.  Its transfer operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 (L_ah)(y)=\sum_{i=1}^4w_i(a)
 h\bigl(s_{i-1}(a)+w_i(a)y\bigr).
\end{equation}
Lebesgue probability is invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Pinball collision realization]\label{prop:pinball}
There is a deterministic unit-speed collision flow on
$\T\times[0,1]$ whose bottom-to-bottom Poincar\'e map is \eqref{eq:T}.  The
three internal seams $s_1,s_2,s_3$ are discontinuity curves of the top
collision law and move with velocities $1,3,2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At the bottom wall launch vertically.  At the top wall, on the $i$th incoming
interval, choose the outgoing unit direction whose next bottom footpoint is
$T_a(x)$.  The required horizontal displacement is a bounded smooth function
on each open branch, and the branch endpoints are the declared collision
seams.  The bottom return map is therefore \eqref{eq:T}.  Differentiating the
partial sums of the widths gives the seam velocities.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Genuine nonconjugacy]\label{thm:nonconjugacy}
If $a,b\in I$ and $T_a,T_b$ are $C^1$ conjugate as piecewise expanding maps,
then $a=b$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $i=1,2,3$, the $i$th branch contains the interior fixed point
\[
 x_i(a)=\frac{s_{i-1}(a)}{1-w_i(a)}
\]
with multiplier $w_i(a)^{-1}$.  The parameter ranges of $w_1,w_2,w_3$ are
pairwise disjoint, so a conjugacy cannot permute these three multiplier
families.  Periodic multipliers are invariant under a $C^1$ conjugacy, hence
$w_i(a)=w_i(b)$ for $i=1,2,3$.  Since $w_1(a)=1/10+a$, one obtains $a=b$.
\end{proof}

\section{Uniform contraction and all-order response}\label{sec:allorder}

For $r\ge1$ let $X_r=W^{r,1}(\T)$ and $X_r^0$ be its zero-mean subspace.  Put
\[
 \rho=\sup_{a,i}w_i(a)=9/20.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Uniform centered spectral contraction]\label{lem:contraction}
For every finite $r$ there is $C_r$ such that
\[
 \norm{L_a^nh}_{W^{r,1}}
 \le C_r\rho^n\norm h_{W^{r,1}},
 \qquad h\in X_r^0,\ a\in I,\ n\ge0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $j\ge1$,
\[
 (L_ah)^{(j)}(y)=
 \sum_iw_i(a)^{j+1}h^{(j)}(s_{i-1}+w_i y).
\]
Changing variables on each branch gives
$\norm{(L_ah)^{(j)}}_1\le\rho^j\norm{h^{(j)}}_1$.
Iteration yields $\rho^{jn}$.  On zero-mean periodic functions,
$\norm h_1\le\norm{h'}_1$; apply this to $L_a^nh$ and sum the derivative
levels.
\end{proof}

Let $v=(1,2,-1,-2)$, $A_i=\sum_{j\le i}v_j$, and
\[
 \psi_i^a(y)=s_{i-1}(a)+w_i(a)y,
 \qquad r_i(y)=A_{i-1}+v_i y.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact parameter letters]\label{lem:letters}
For every $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:letterformula}
 \partial_a^kL_ah
 =\sum_{i=1}^4\left[
 k v_i r_i^{k-1}h^{(k-1)}(\psi_i^a)
 +w_i(a)r_i^kh^{(k)}(\psi_i^a)
 \right].
\end{equation}
For all $r,k\ge1$,
\[
 \norm{\partial_a^kL_ah}_{W^{r,1}}
 \le C_{r,k}\norm h_{W^{r+k,1}},
 \qquad \Piop\partial_a^kL_a=0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both $w_i$ and $\psi_i^a$ are affine in $a$.  In the $k$th Leibniz rule, either
no derivative or exactly one derivative can hit $w_i$; all remaining
derivatives hit the composition.  This gives \eqref{eq:letterformula}.  The
norm estimate is the one-dimensional product and affine-composition bound.
Differentiating $\int L_ah=\int h$ gives centering.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual all-order graded response]\label{thm:all-order}
Fix $N\ge1$.  On the finite ladder
\[
 W^{N+1,1}\hookrightarrow W^{N,1}\hookrightarrow\cdots
 \hookrightarrow W^{1,1},
\]
the nonconjugate family \eqref{eq:T} has:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a nonzero moving-cut source;
\item product response for every parameter letter of order at most $N+1$;
\item finite-DQ convergence in $\ell^1(\N^2)$ through order $N$;
\item a continuous spectral projection and reduced-resolvent jet through order
$N$;
\item closure of every future Kato insertion whose total derivative order is
at most $N$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular the complete $U3$ package holds with $N=3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Before inverse-branch assembly, differentiating the moving branch indicators
produces nonzero oriented endpoint currents.  Formula \eqref{eq:letterformula}
is their complete physical assembly and is not the zero operator, proving (i).
Combine \cref{lem:contraction,lem:letters} on the two sides of a letter to get
\[
 \abs{\pair{Q_a^n(\partial_a^kL_a)Q_a^m g}{f}}
 \le C_{r,k}\rho^{m+n}
 \norm g_{W^{r+k,1}}\norm f_{(W^{r,1})^*}.
\]
This proves (ii).  Taylor's formula with the order-$N+1$ integral remainder
and the same product envelope gives (iii) by \cref{thm:fdq}.  Riesz-contour
differentiation on the graded ladder gives (iv).  Every derivative word loses
the sum of its letter orders and every reduced resolvent preserves its level;
therefore all words of total order at most $N$ are bounded compositions,
proving (v).
\end{proof}

\section{Exact predictable innovations}\label{sec:innovations}

Let $X_0$ be Lebesgue distributed.  Recursively let
$A_k\in I$ be measurable with respect to
\[
 \F_k=\sigma(I_0,\ldots,I_{k-1}),
\]
where $I_k=i$ when $X_k\in I_i(A_k)$, and set
\[
 X_{k+1}=T_{A_k}(X_k).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact innovation theorem]\label{thm:innovations}
For every $k\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item conditionally on $\F_k$, $X_k$ is Lebesgue distributed;
\item $\Pp(I_k=i\mid\F_k)=w_i(A_k)$;
\item conditionally on $\F_k\vee\sigma(I_k)$, $X_{k+1}$ is Lebesgue
      distributed and independent of that sigma-field.
\end{enumerate}
Hence the symbols are exact conditionally independent innovations with the
predictable probability vector $w(A_k)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The assertion is true at time zero.  Assuming conditional uniformity at time
$k$, the branch probability is its interval length $w_i(A_k)$.  Given the
branch, $X_k$ is uniform on $I_i(A_k)$, and the affine bijection
$T_{A_k}|_{I_i(A_k)}$ sends normalized Lebesgue measure on that interval to
Lebesgue measure on $[0,1)$.  The resulting conditional law does not depend on
the branch or the earlier history, proving (iii) and closing the induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Deterministic martingale array]\label{cor:martingale}
Let $c_i(\theta_k)\in\R^d$ be bounded and predictable, and set
\[
 \bar c(\theta_k)=\sum_iw_i(A_k)c_i(\theta_k),
 \qquad M_k=c_{I_k}(\theta_k)-\bar c(\theta_k).
\]
Then
\[
 \mathbb E[M_k\mid\F_k]=0,
\qquad
 \mathbb E[M_k\otimes M_k\mid\F_k]
 =\sum_iw_i(A_k)(c_i-\bar c)\otimes(c_i-\bar c).
\]
All conditional moments are uniformly bounded.  Thus the same deterministic
moving-cut family supplies the exact triangular arrays used in the companion
rough-homogenization theorem.
\end{corollary}

\section{A nonconjugate radial specular channel}\label{sec:specular}

Let a finite-horizon periodic Sinai table contain two circular scatterers with
a unique shortest regular normal period-two orbit and a positive period gap.
Keep the centers fixed and set $R_1(a)=R_1+a$ on a sufficiently small interval
preserving strict convexity, nonoverlap and finite horizon.  If
$d(a)=d_0-a$, $\kappa_1(a)=(R_1+a)^{-1}$ and $\kappa_2=R_2^{-1}$, the Jacobi
return matrix has trace
\[
 \operatorname{tr}M(a)=2+4d(a)(\kappa_1(a)+\kappa_2)
 +4d(a)^2\kappa_1(a)\kappa_2,
\]
whose derivative at zero is strictly negative.  The isolated orbit therefore
excludes a local $C^1$ conjugacy.

In normalized arclength coordinates, if the component perimeters are
$\ell_i(a)$ and $L(a)=\sum_i\ell_i(a)$, the invariant density is
\[
 \rho_a(i,x,\varphi)=\frac{\ell_i(a)\cos\varphi}{2L(a)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[All-order invariant-projector response]\label{thm:specular}
For every finite $j$ for which the radial family is differentiated, the
completely assembled source satisfies
\[
 S_j=\sum_{k=1}^j{j\choose k}L_0^{[k]}
 \rho_0^{(j-k)}=(I-L_0)\rho_0^{(j)},
 \qquad R_0S_j=\rho_0^{(j)}.
\]
Moreover, for analytic observables $F_a,g_a$ and
$\psi_a=c(a)+g_a-g_a\circ T_a$, the susceptibility
\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}\int(F_a-\mu_aF_a)
 (\psi_a-c(a))\circ T_a^n\,d\mu_a
\]
equals
\[
 \int(F_a-\mu_aF_a)g_a\,d\mu_a
\]
and is analytic in $a$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate $L_a\rho_a=\rho_a$ and use the zero integral of every density
jet.  For the correlation claim, the partial sums telescope to
\[
 \int(F_a-\mu_aF_a)(g_a-g_a\circ T_a^N)\,d\mu_a,
\]
and fixed-table mixing removes the last term.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

The four-branch collision system simultaneously provides moving physical
currents, nonconjugacy, all finite graded response orders and exact predictable
innovations.  Thus neither the spectral theorem nor the downstream martingale
array is an uninstantiated interface.  Bilateral time decay, occurrence
assembly and regularity typing remain conceptually separate, but they are
proved together on one actual system.
