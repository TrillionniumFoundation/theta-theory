\section{Analytic obstacle coordinates}

Let $K_j(a)$, $1\le j\le J$, be a real-analytic no-eclipse family of strictly
convex obstacles.  Choose analytic boundary parametrizations
\[
 r_j:\mathcal A\times\T^{d-1}\longrightarrow\R^d
\]
with uniformly positive second fundamental forms.  The parameter interval is
compact and sufficiently small that obstacle separation, curvature, and
no-eclipse margins are uniform.

Let
\[
 \Sigma_A=\{\xi\in\{1,\ldots,J\}^{\Z}:\xi_k\ne\xi_{k+1}\}.
\]
For a fixed itinerary $\xi$, a collision sequence is described by boundary
coordinates $u=(u_k)_{k\in\Z}$ with
$q_k=r_{\xi_k}(a,u_k)$.  The specular equations are the Euler equations of the
length functional.  Write
\begin{align}
 \F_k(a,u;\xi)
 =D_{u_k}\Big(&\abs{r_{\xi_k}(a,u_k)-r_{\xi_{k-1}}(a,u_{k-1})}\\
 &+\abs{r_{\xi_{k+1}}(a,u_{k+1})-r_{\xi_k}(a,u_k)}\Big).
 \label{eq:reflection-equation}
\end{align}
Thus $\F(a,u;\xi)=0$ is exactly the reflection law at every collision.

\section{The Jacobi operator and exponential dichotomy}

The derivative $D_u\F$ is a bi-infinite block tridiagonal Jacobi operator.
Its diagonal blocks are the second variation of the two adjacent flight
lengths and its off-diagonal blocks couple neighbouring collision coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform Jacobi inverse]\label{lem:Jacobi}
There are $0<\theta<1$ and $C<\infty$, uniform in $a$ and $\xi$, such that the
Jacobi operator
\[
 \mathcal J_{a,\xi}=D_u\F(a,u(a,\xi);\xi)
\]
is invertible on $\ell^\infty(\Z;\R^{d-1})$ and its inverse has Green kernel
\[
 \norm{(\mathcal J_{a,\xi}^{-1})_{k\ell}}
 \le C\theta^{\abs{k-\ell}}.
 \label{eq:Green-decay}
\]
The same estimate holds on the weighted Hölder sequence spaces used below.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Linearization of the reflection equations is the discrete Jacobi equation for
transverse variations of the billiard ray.  Strict convexity makes every
reflection focusing operator positive, while the uniform positive flight
length and no-eclipse margins keep the free-flight blocks bounded and
invertible.  The standard unstable/stable cone calculation gives an exponential
dichotomy: a nonzero solution growing neither in the future nor in the past
would lie in both the stable and unstable cones, whose intersection is zero.
Uniform cone separation and expansion yield constants $C$ and $\theta$.

For a finitely supported forcing $f$, construct the bounded solution by
matching the forward stable solution to the backward unstable solution at each
forcing index.  The dichotomy gives
\[
 \abs{u_k}\le C\sum_\ell\theta^{\abs{k-\ell}}\abs{f_\ell}.
\]
This defines a bounded inverse with kernel satisfying
\eqref{eq:Green-decay}.  Density of finitely supported forcings and the same
Schur estimate extend the inverse to $\ell^\infty$ and to every weighted
sequence norm with weight exponent smaller than $-\log\theta$.
\end{proof}

\section{Analytic collision sequences}

Fix $\theta<\vartheta<1$ and let
\[
 \X_\vartheta=\left\{v=(v_k):
 \norm v_\vartheta=\sup_k\vartheta^{-\abs k}\abs{v_k}<\infty\right\}
\]
for perturbations anchored at the zeroth collision.  On a local boundary
coordinate chart, the map
\[
 (a,u)\longmapsto\F(a,u;\xi)
\]
is real analytic from $\mathcal A\times\X_\vartheta$ to
$\X_\vartheta$.  Uniform obstacle separation prevents a denominator in the
flight-direction derivatives from vanishing.

\begin{theorem}[Analytic infinite collision sequence]\label{thm:analytic-sequence}
For every $\xi\in\Sigma_A$ there is a unique bounded collision sequence
$u(a,\xi)$ solving \eqref{eq:reflection-equation}.  The map
\[
 a\longmapsto u(a,\xi)
\]
is real analytic in $\X_\vartheta$, with derivative bounds uniform in
$\xi$.  If two itineraries agree for $\abs k\le n$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holder-coding}
 \abs{u_0(a,\xi)-u_0(a,\xi')}
 \le C\theta^n.
\end{equation}
The same estimate holds for every finite parameter derivative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Existence and uniqueness of the billiard ray for a no-eclipse itinerary follow
by minimizing finite length functionals and passing to a limit; strict
convexity and the hyperbolic dichotomy give uniqueness.  At a solution,
\cref{lem:Jacobi} gives a uniformly invertible derivative on
$\X_\vartheta$.  The analytic implicit-function theorem therefore yields
analytic parameter dependence.  Cauchy estimates on a uniform complex
parameter neighbourhood give derivative bounds independent of $\xi$.

For the Hölder estimate, truncate the two itineraries outside
$[-n,n]$.  Their reflection equations agree on this interval; the difference
there is forced only through the two boundary equations near $\pm n$.
Applying the Green-kernel estimate to this boundary forcing gives
\eqref{eq:holder-coding}.  Differentiating the equations in $a$ produces the
same Jacobi operator and forcing terms already satisfying exponential boundary
localization, so the argument iterates at every finite order.
\end{proof}

Define the collision coding map by
\[
 \pi_a(\xi)=
 \bigl(r_{\xi_0}(a,u_0(a,\xi)),v_0(a,\xi)\bigr),
\]
where $v_0$ is the outgoing unit vector to the next collision.

\begin{corollary}[Analytic Hölder coding]\label{cor:coding}
For every $0<\alpha<-\log\theta/\log\rho_\Sigma$, where $\rho_\Sigma$ is the
symbolic metric base, the map
\[
 a\longmapsto\pi_a
\]
is real analytic as a map into $C^\alpha(\Sigma_A,\mathcal M)$.  The roof,
unstable Jacobian, and every analytic physical observable pulled back by
$\pi_a$ are analytic $C^\alpha$ functions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Hölder estimate follows from \eqref{eq:holder-coding}.  Flight length,
outgoing direction, and unstable Jacobian are analytic functions of finitely
many boundary jets and of the collision sequence.  Analyticity in the Hölder
norm follows from the uniform derivative version of
\eqref{eq:holder-coding} and the analytic composition theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Analytic Ruelle operators}

On the one-sided shift define
\[
 (\Lop_{a,q,s}f)(\xi)=
 \sum_{\sigma\eta=\xi}
 e^{\phi_a(\eta)+q\cdot\kappa_a(\eta)-s\tau_a(\eta)}f(\eta).
\]
The preimage number is finite and independent of the parameter.

\begin{lemma}[Analytic operator family]\label{lem:analytic-operator}
The map
\[
 (a,q,s)\longmapsto\Lop_{a,q,s}
\]
is analytic from a complex neighbourhood of the real parameter set into
$\mathcal L(C^\alpha,C^\alpha)$.  For every multi-index $\gamma$,
\[
 \norm{\partial_{a,q,s}^\gamma\Lop_{a,q,s}}_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}
 \le C_\gamma
\]
on smaller compact neighbourhoods.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{cor:coding}, the exponent is analytic in the Banach algebra
$C^\alpha$.  The exponential Nemytskii map is entire on this Banach algebra.
Multiplication, composition with a fixed inverse shift branch, and the finite
preimage sum are bounded multilinear operations.  Their composition is
analytic, and Cauchy estimates give the derivative bounds.
\end{proof}

Let $\lambda_{a,q,s}$ be the simple leading eigenvalue, $\Pi_{a,q,s}$ its
Riesz projection, and $Q_{a,q,s}=\Lop_{a,q,s}-\lambda_{a,q,s}\Pi_{a,q,s}$.
Uniform mixing of the subshift and compactness give
\[
 \norm{Q_{a,q,s}^n}_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}\le C\rho^n
\]
near the real origin.

\begin{theorem}[Normal all-order susceptibility]\label{thm:normal-susceptibility}
Let $F_a,G_a\in C^\alpha$ be analytic and centered with respect to the
normalized equilibrium state $\mu_a$.  Then
\[
 \chi(a)=\sum_{n\ge0}\mu_a(F_aG_a\circ\sigma^n)
\]
is analytic.  For every $N$, its $N$th derivative is the normally convergent
sum of all ordered words with total derivative order $N$ formed from:

\begin{itemize}
\item derivatives of $F_a$ and $G_a$;
\item direct derivatives of $\Lop_a$;
\item the reduced resolvent $(I-Q_a)^{-1}$;
\item derivatives of the equilibrium projection.
\end{itemize}

No cohomological representation of $F_a$ or $G_a$ is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The centered susceptibility is
\[
 \chi(a)=\mu_a\left(G_a(I-Q_a)^{-1}F_a\right).
\]
The reduced resolvent has the uniformly convergent Neumann series
$\sum_{n\ge0}Q_a^n$.  Analytic Riesz calculus and
\cref{lem:analytic-operator} give analytic $Q_a$, $\mu_a$, and the reduced
resolvent.  Repeated use of
\[
 \partial_a(I-Q_a)^{-1}
 =(I-Q_a)^{-1}(\partial_aQ_a)(I-Q_a)^{-1}
\]
gives the ordered words.  Every resolvent has a uniform norm bound and every
direct derivative has a uniform Hölder norm bound; the finite number of
partitions of $N$ and the geometric Neumann envelope prove normal convergence.
\end{proof}

\section{A non-coboundary physical source}

\begin{proposition}[Periodic-orbit certificate]\label{prop:noncoboundary}
Let $F$ be a locally constant cylinder function on the symbolic collision
space.  If two periodic itineraries of periods $p$ and $q$ satisfy
\[
 {1\over p}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}F(\sigma^k\xi)
 \ne
 {1\over q}\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}F(\sigma^k\eta),
\]
then $F$ is not cohomologous to a constant.  Such cylinder functions exist for
any mixing collision subshift with at least two periodic orbits.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $F=c+H-H\circ\sigma$, every periodic sum equals the period times $c$.
The displayed inequality contradicts this identity.  Assigning different
values to cylinders visited with different frequencies by two periodic orbits
constructs the required $F$.
\end{proof}

Through $\pi_a^{-1}$, this gives a Hölder observable on the physical
nonwandering set.  It supplies an independently prescribed non-coboundary
source for \cref{thm:normal-susceptibility}.

\section{Conclusion}

The moving obstacle family is differentiated only after it has been pulled to
one fixed symbolic Banach space.  The infinite collision sequence, roof,
Jacobian, observables, Ruelle operator, equilibrium state, and arbitrary-source
susceptibility all depend analytically on the parameter.  The proof uses
uniform hyperbolic inversion rather than differentiated invariance.
