\begin{abstract}
We prove a response theorem for transfer operators whose branch cuts move with
the parameter.  The physical derivative is resolved into oriented
same-occurrence currents.  A source-side estimate and a target-side estimate
are retained separately; a strict crossed-rate inequality interpolates them
into an absolutely summable product tail in the two time variables.  We then
prove convergence of finite differences in the complete
$\ell^1(\N^2)$ response topology and a joint-Cauchy theorem for totalizing the
third source on a graded Banach scale.

The theorem is realized without an unproved dynamical packet.  We study an
open four-branch full-cover family with three moving cuts.  Its transfer
operator is calculated on periodic Sobolev--variation spaces, its centered
powers contract uniformly, and its first four parameter derivatives satisfy
an explicit regularity-loss formula.  These estimates yield product response,
finite-difference convergence and a continuous third spectral jet.  We also
record a genuinely nonconjugate radial family of finite-horizon specular Sinai
billiards.  In that family the invariant-projector source and a class of
cohomological correlation susceptibilities are analytic; no arbitrary-source
specular response is inferred.  A final maximality theorem identifies the
precise sequence-space topology in which finite local data cannot imply global
two-time summability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For a differentiable family of bounded operators acting on one Banach space,
Kato's perturbation theory turns an isolated spectral value into differentiable
spectral data.  Moving singularities create a different problem.  Before
assembly, differentiating a branch partition produces currents on moving
faces.  After propagation, a current carries both a past waiting time and a
future waiting time.  An estimate that decays in only one of them cannot be
summed over the response array.

The purpose of this paper is deliberately narrower than a universal response
theory for dispersing billiards.  We isolate a sufficient and sharp bilateral
mechanism, prove the associated higher-source calculus, and verify every
hypothesis in one moving-cut system.  The abstract statement is useful because
it separates three logically independent issues:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item recovery in both time directions;
\item absolute totalization over physical occurrences;
\item the regularity loss of successive parameter letters.
\end{enumerate}
The actual model shows that these requirements are simultaneously satisfiable
and that the source is not removed by exact conjugacy.

Our approach is compatible with spectral perturbation on strong--weak scales
\cite{KellerLiverani1999}, higher response for piecewise systems
\cite{BaladiSmania2008}, and anisotropic constructions for hyperbolic systems
with singularities \cite{DemersLiverani2008,BaladiDemersLiverani2018}.  It does
not claim that fixed-table estimates or finite-horizon geometry automatically
produce the bilateral packet.  Nonstationary billiard memory loss
\cite{StenlundYoungZhang2013} is an important source of one-sided estimates,
but the two-time current theorem still has to be checked for the source under
consideration.

The principal results are \cref{thm:bilateral,thm:fdq,thm:u3,thm:actual}.
The specular result, \cref{thm:specular}, is explicitly source-specific.  The
maximality statement \cref{thm:maximality} is formulated in a named product
topology and makes no genericity assertion in the full $C^r$ observable space.

\section{Same-occurrence currents and graded spaces}\label{sec:setting}

Let $\A$ be a compact parameter interval and let $T_a$ be a piecewise smooth,
nonsingular map.  All fibres are transported to a common measurable reference
space.  Denote the Perron--Frobenius operator by $L_a$, its invariant rank-one
projection by $\Pi_a$, and
\[
 Q_a=L_a-\Pi_a.
\]

\begin{definition}[Occurrence registry]\label{def:registry}
An occurrence label $\omega$ consists of a physical cut, an incident side, an
owner branch, an oriented branch word, and a parameter chamber.  Labels are
identified only after passage to a common refinement on which all these data
agree.  Reversing orientation changes the sign but not the UID.
\end{definition}

The physical derivative is represented formally as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 \partial_aL_a=K_a^{\mathrm{reg}}+
 \sum_{\omega\in\Omega_a}J_{a,\omega}.
\end{equation}
No cancellation between distinct UIDs is used in an estimate.  For centered
$g$ and an admissible test $f$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atom}
 A_{m,n}^{\omega}(a;g,f)
 =\pair{Q_a^nJ_{a,\omega}Q_a^m g}{f},
 \qquad m,n\ge0.
\end{equation}

We use a finite scale
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale}
 X_R\hookrightarrow X_{R-1}\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow X_0.
\end{equation}
The $j$th total parameter letter is required to have the declared type
\begin{equation}\label{eq:letter}
 G_a^{(j)}:X_r\longrightarrow X_{r-j},\qquad r\ge j,
\end{equation}
and each reduced resolvent preserves its level.  This typing is part of the
statement: a current controlled only in the weakest space is not silently
reinserted into a higher-order word.

\section{The bilateral product-tail theorem}\label{sec:bilateral}

\begin{assumption}[Bilateral crossed envelopes]\label{ass:crossed}
There are $0<\alpha,\beta<1$, $\Gamma_+,\Gamma_-\ge1$, and nonnegative
constants $C_\omega^-,C_\omega^+$ such that
\begin{align}
 \abs{A_{m,n}^{\omega}}
 &\le C_\omega^-\alpha^m\Gamma_+^n
       \norm{g}_{X_-}\norm{f}_{Y},\label{eq:rev}\
 \abs{A_{m,n}^{\omega}}
 &\le C_\omega^+\Gamma_-^m\beta^n
       \norm{g}_{X_-}\norm{f}_{Y}.
       \label{eq:fwd}
\end{align}
The first estimate is source-side recovery; the second is target-side
recovery.
\end{assumption}

Put
\[
 A_0=\log(1/\alpha),\quad B_0=\log(1/\beta),\quad
 C_0=\log\Gamma_+,\quad D_0=\log\Gamma_-.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Bilateral product tail]\label{thm:bilateral}
Assume \cref{ass:crossed} and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 A_0B_0>C_0D_0.
\end{equation}
Let
\[
 \lambda_*=
 \frac{B_0+D_0}{A_0+B_0+C_0+D_0},
 \qquad
 \log\rho_*=
 \frac{C_0D_0-A_0B_0}{A_0+B_0+C_0+D_0}.
\]
Then $0<\rho_*<1$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prod}
 \abs{A_{m,n}^{\omega}}
 \le (C_\omega^-)^{\lambda_*}
      (C_\omega^+)^{1-\lambda_*}
      \rho_*^{m+n}\norm{g}_{X_-}\norm{f}_{Y}.
\end{equation}
If
\[
 \sum_\omega (C_\omega^-)^{\lambda_*}
                    (C_\omega^+)^{1-\lambda_*}<\infty,
\]
then
\[
 \sum_\omega\sum_{m,n\ge0}
 \abs{A_{m,n}^{\omega}}<\infty
\]
uniformly on compact parameter chambers and bounded input sets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Raise \eqref{eq:rev} to the power $\lambda$ and \eqref{eq:fwd} to the
power $1-\lambda$.  Their geometric interpolation is
\[
 \abs{A_{m,n}^{\omega}}
 \le (C_\omega^-)^\lambda(C_\omega^+)^{1-\lambda}
 (\alpha^\lambda\Gamma_-^{1-\lambda})^m
 (\Gamma_+^\lambda\beta^{1-\lambda})^n
 \norm{g}_{X_-}\norm{f}_{Y}.
\]
At $\lambda=\lambda_*$ the logarithms of the two time factors agree and
both equal
\[
 \frac{C_0D_0-A_0B_0}{A_0+B_0+C_0+D_0}.
\]
The strict gap makes this number negative.  Summing the two geometric series
and then the occurrence constants proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Optimality of the crossed window]
There exists a $\lambda\in(0,1)$ for which both interpolated time factors are
strictly below one if and only if \eqref{eq:gap} holds.  Equality is critical:
the best common factor is one, so the two one-sided bounds do not force
absolute product summability.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Regularity-loss route]\label{cor:loss}
Suppose $K_a:X_{r+\ell}\to X_r$, $\Pi_aK_a=0$, and
\[
 \norm{Q_a^m}_{X_{r+\ell}^0\to X_{r+\ell}^0}
 \le C_+\rho_+^m,
 \qquad
 \norm{Q_a^n}_{X_r^0\to X_r^0}
 \le C_-\rho_-^n.
\]
Then
\[
 \abs{\pair{Q_a^nK_aQ_a^m g}{f}}
 \le C_-C_+\norm{K_a}\rho_-^n\rho_+^m
      \norm{g}_{X_{r+\ell}}\norm{f}_{X_r^*}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The source is centered by $\Pi_aK_a=0$.  Apply the two operator bounds on the
respective sides of $K_a$.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite differences in the complete response topology}\label{sec:fdq}

For $h\ne0$, define $K_{a,h}=(L_{a+h}-L_a)/h$.

\begin{theorem}[Head--tail finite-difference criterion]\label{thm:fdq}
Assume that for every $N$,
\[
 \max_{0\le m,n\le N}
 \abs{\pair{Q_{a+h}^nK_{a,h}Q_a^m g
       -Q_a^nK_aQ_a^m g}{f}}
 \longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly on the declared unit balls.  Assume also that both arrays are
bounded, independently of small $h$, by one summable product envelope.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fdq}
 \sum_{m,n\ge0}
 \abs{\pair{Q_{a+h}^nK_{a,h}Q_a^m g
       -Q_a^nK_aQ_a^m g}{f}}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a finite rectangle whose complement has envelope mass below
$\varepsilon/3$.  Uniform convergence on the rectangle controls its finite
sum.  The two tails are bounded by the two copies of the envelope tail.
\end{proof}

Pointwise convergence in $(m,n)$ is therefore never substituted for
\eqref{eq:fdq}.

\section{Third-source totalization}\label{sec:u3}

Let $d=(K,\varepsilon,\mathcal P)$ encode a depth cutoff, an intersection cap,
and a finite atlas refinement.  The finite third source is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G3d}
 G_{3,d}(a)=
 \Tot_{\mathfrak S_d}\{\eta_{a,\omega}:\omega\in I_d\}
 +S_d^{\mathrm{seam}}(a).
\end{equation}
For an atom define
\[
 \norm{\eta_{a,\omega}}_{\mathrm{CM2}}
 =\sup_{\norm{g}\le1,\norm{f}\le1}
   \sum_{m,n\ge0}
   \abs{\pair{Q_a^n\eta_{a,\omega}Q_a^m g}{f}}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[CM2-to-third-jet Cauchy theorem]\label{thm:u3}
Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item every $a\mapsto G_{3,d}(a)$ is continuous in a complete graded source
space;
\item the atoms satisfy the bilateral theorem on their declared levels;
\item $\sup_a\norm{\eta_{a,\omega}}_{\mathrm{CM2}}\le b_\omega$ and
$\sum_\omega b_\omega<\infty$;
\item the intersection and refinement errors are bounded by moduli
$\omega_{\mathrm{vert}}(\varepsilon)$ and
$\omega_{\mathrm{seam}}(\mathcal P)$ tending to zero;
\item common refinements preserve UIDs and cancel opposite orientations
exactly;
\item reduced resolvents preserve all levels used by the third-order words.
\end{enumerate}
Then for $d_1,d_2\succeq d$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cauchy}
 \norm{G_{3,d_1}-G_{3,d_2}}
 \le2\sum_{\omega\notin I_d}b_\omega
 +2\omega_{\mathrm{vert}}(\varepsilon)
 +2\omega_{\mathrm{seam}}(\mathcal P).
\end{equation}
Consequently $G_{3,d}$ converges uniformly in $a$ to a continuous source
$G_3$.  If $G_j:X_r\to X_{r-j}$, every third-order word obtained from
$G_3$, $G_2G_1$, $G_1G_2$, or $G_1^3$ with level-preserving reduced
resolvents is a continuous bounded operator between the indicated levels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pass to a common refinement.  Common atoms cancel by UID and orientation.
The remaining atoms lie beyond the old cutoff or in the two declared defect
sets, which gives \eqref{eq:Cauchy}.  Completeness and uniform convergence give
$G_3$ and its parameter continuity.  The graded types make each resolvent word
composable; finite products of continuous bounded maps are continuous.
\end{proof}

\section{An open moving-cut realization}\label{sec:actual}

Let
\[
 I=[-1/40,1/40]
\]
and define
\begin{align*}
 w_1(a)&=1/10+a,& w_2(a)&=1/5+2a,\\
 w_3(a)&=3/10-a,& w_4(a)&=2/5-2a.
\end{align*}
Put $s_0=0$ and $s_i=\sum_{j\le i}w_j$.  The full-cover map is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ta}
 T_a(x)=\frac{x-s_{i-1}(a)}{w_i(a)}\pmod1,
 \qquad x\in[s_{i-1}(a),s_i(a)).
\end{equation}
The three internal cuts move with nonzero velocities.  Its transfer operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:La}
 (L_ah)(y)=\sum_{i=1}^4 w_i(a)
 h\bigl(s_{i-1}(a)+w_i(a)y\bigr).
\end{equation}
Lebesgue probability is invariant because a change of variables on each
branch gives $\int L_ah=\int h$.

For $r\ge1$ let $X_r=W^{r,1}(\T)$, with the periodic representative, and let
$X_r^0$ be the zero-mean subspace.  Set
\[
 \rho=\sup_{a\in I,i}w_i(a)=9/20.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Uniform centered contraction]\label{lem:contract}
For every finite $r\ge1$ there is $C_r$ such that
\[
 \norm{L_a^nh}_{W^{r,1}}
 \le C_r\rho^n\norm{h}_{W^{r,1}},
 \qquad h\in X_r^0,\quad n\ge0,\quad a\in I.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $j\ge1$, differentiation of \eqref{eq:La} gives
\[
 (L_ah)^{(j)}(y)
 =\sum_i w_i(a)^{j+1}h^{(j)}(s_{i-1}+w_i y).
\]
After changing variables on each branch,
\[
 \norm{(L_ah)^{(j)}}_1
 \le\rho^j\norm{h^{(j)}}_1.
\]
The zero-mean periodic Poincar\'e inequality controls
$\norm{L_a^nh}_1$ by $\norm{(L_a^nh)'}_1$.  Summing the derivative bounds
proves the claim.
\end{proof}

Let $v=(1,2,-1,-2)$, $A_i=\sum_{j\le i}v_j$, and
\[
 \psi_i^a(y)=s_{i-1}(a)+w_i(a)y,
 \qquad r_i(y)=A_{i-1}+v_i y.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact parameter letters]\label{lem:letters}
For $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:letters}
 \partial_a^kL_ah
 =\sum_{i=1}^4\left[
 k v_i r_i^{k-1}h^{(k-1)}(\psi_i^a)
 +w_i(a)r_i^kh^{(k)}(\psi_i^a)
 \right].
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $r,k\ge1$,
\[
 \norm{\partial_a^kL_ah}_{W^{r,1}}
 \le C_{r,k}\norm{h}_{W^{r+k,1}},
 \qquad
 \Piop\partial_a^kL_a=0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both $w_i$ and $\psi_i^a$ are affine in $a$, with derivatives $v_i$ and
$r_i$.  Leibniz' rule leaves only the term in which no derivative hits $w_i$
and the $k$ terms in which exactly one does, giving \eqref{eq:letters}.
The norm estimate follows from one-dimensional composition and product
bounds.  Differentiating $\int L_ah=\int h$ gives the centering identity.
\end{proof}

Before inverse-branch assembly, differentiating
$\mathbf 1_{[s_{i-1}(a),s_i(a))}$ gives the oriented endpoint currents
\[
 -s_{i-1}'(a)\delta_{s_{i-1}(a)}
 +s_i'(a)\delta_{s_i(a)}.
\]
Thus the family has genuine moving-cut currents.  Formula
\eqref{eq:letters} is their complete physical assembly, not an exact-conjugacy
zero source.

\begin{theorem}[Actual product response and third jet]\label{thm:actual}
The family \eqref{eq:Ta} has the following properties, uniformly for
$a\in I$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item its three physical cuts move and its first parameter source is nonzero;
\item for $1\le k\le4$,
\[
 \abs{\pair{Q_a^n(\partial_a^kL_a)Q_a^m g}{f}}
 \le C_{r,k}\rho^{m+n}
 \norm{g}_{W^{r+k,1}}\norm{f}_{(W^{r,1})^*};
\]
\item finite differences through order three converge in the corresponding
$\ell^1(\N^2)$ response topology;
\item the spectral projection and reduced resolvent have a continuous third
jet on the ladder
\[
 W^{4,1}\to W^{3,1}\to W^{2,1}\to W^{1,1}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Nonzero cut velocities give (i), and a smooth function supported near one cut
shows that the assembled first derivative is not the zero operator.  Apply
\cref{lem:contract,lem:letters} on both sides of the inserted letter to obtain
(ii).  Taylor's theorem with the fourth derivative as integral remainder gives
uniform convergence on each finite $(m,n)$ rectangle; the product bound is a
common envelope, so \cref{thm:fdq} gives (iii).  The occurrence atlas is finite,
so the cap and refinement errors in \cref{thm:u3} vanish.  The displayed
ladder makes every third-order word composable, proving (iv).
\end{proof}

\section{A source-specific nonconjugate specular theorem}\label{sec:specular}

Consider a periodic finite-horizon Sinai table containing two circular
scatterers with a unique shortest regular normal period-two orbit and a
positive period gap.  Keep the centres fixed and set
\[
 R_1(a)=R_1+a
\]
for $a$ in a sufficiently small interval on which strict convexity,
nonoverlap and finite horizon persist.

For the normal orbit let $d(a)=d_0-a$,
$\kappa_1(a)=(R_1+a)^{-1}$ and $\kappa_2=R_2^{-1}$.  With
\[
 F(d)=\begin{pmatrix}1&d\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 S(\kappa)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2\kappa&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
the return matrix is
$M(a)=S(\kappa_1(a))F(d(a))S(\kappa_2)F(d(a))$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace}
 \operatorname{tr}M(a)
 =2+4d(a)(\kappa_1(a)+\kappa_2)
 +4d(a)^2\kappa_1(a)\kappa_2.
\end{equation}
Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-der}
 \frac d{da}\operatorname{tr}M(0)
 =-4(\kappa_1+\kappa_2)-4d_0\kappa_1^2
 -8d_0\kappa_1\kappa_2-4d_0^2\kappa_1^2\kappa_2<0.
\end{equation}
The isolated orbit cannot be permuted away, so the family is not locally
$C^1$ conjugate; its flow period $2d(a)$ also excludes a time-preserving flow
conjugacy.

Pull the $i$th collision component to normalized arclength $x\in\T$.  If its
perimeter is $\ell_i(a)$ and $L(a)=\sum_i\ell_i(a)$, the invariant collision
probability has density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 \rho_a(i,x,\varphi)=
 \frac{\ell_i(a)\cos\varphi}{2L(a)}.
\end{equation}
It is analytic in $a$.  Differentiating $L_a\rho_a=\rho_a$ through order $j$
gives the completely assembled source
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sj}
 S_j=\sum_{k=1}^j{j\choose k}L_0^{[k]}
 \rho_0^{(j-k)}=(I-L_0)\rho_0^{(j)}.
\end{equation}

There is also an independently prescribed cohomological correlation class.
Let $F_a,g_a$ be analytic families of bounded smooth observables in normalized
coordinates and set
\[
 \psi_a=c(a)+g_a-g_a\circ T_a.
\]
Define
\[
 \chi_a(F,g)=\sum_{n\ge0}
 \int(F_a-\mu_aF_a)(\psi_a-c(a))\circ T_a^n\,d\mu_a.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Specular projector and cohomological susceptibility]\label{thm:specular}
For the radial family above:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the assembled invariant-projector sources satisfy
$R_0S_j=\rho_0^{(j)}$ for every finite order for which the radial family is
differentiated;
\item whenever the fixed-table mixing limit applies to $F_a$ and $g_a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coho}
 \chi_a(F,g)=
 \int(F_a-\mu_aF_a)g_a\,d\mu_a;
\end{equation}
therefore $a\mapsto\chi_a(F,g)$ is analytic;
\item these conclusions do not assert arbitrary noncoboundary reduced-resolvent
response for a general radial perturbation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The derivatives of \eqref{eq:rho} have zero integral, so applying the centered
reduced resolvent to \eqref{eq:Sj} proves (i).  For (ii), the partial sum to
$N-1$ telescopes to
\[
 \int(F_a-\mu_aF_a)
       (g_a-g_a\circ T_a^N)\,d\mu_a.
\]
Mixing makes the second term tend to zero.  Formula \eqref{eq:coho} follows,
and its analyticity is immediate from normalized coordinates and
\eqref{eq:rho}.  Statement (iii) is a scope assertion: the proof has used the
invariance and coboundary identities, not a general moving-singularity packet.
\end{proof}

\section{Maximality in the exact topology}\label{sec:maximality}

Let $\mathscr S=\C^{\Omega\times\N^2}$ with the product topology and let
\[
 \mathscr S_{\mathrm{CM2}}=
 \left\{a:\sum_{\omega,m,n}\abs{a_{\omega,m,n}}<\infty\right\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[No finite-prefix criterion]\label{thm:maximality}
Both $\mathscr S_{\mathrm{CM2}}$ and its complement are dense in
$\mathscr S$.  Consequently no condition depending on finitely many
occurrences and finitely many time indices can characterize CM2.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every basic open set in the product topology fixes only finitely many
coordinates.  Complete those coordinates by zero to obtain an element of
$\mathscr S_{\mathrm{CM2}}$.  Alternatively, outside the fixed coordinates,
choose one occurrence at each diagonal time and assign values
$1/(k+1)$; the resulting absolute sum diverges.  Thus the same basic open set
meets the complement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem concerns the named source-array topology.  It makes no Baire or
codimension claim for all physical $C^r$ observables.  A broader genericity
statement requires a separate continuous representation from the physical
observable space into $\mathscr S$.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

Bilateral recovery, absolute occurrence totalization and graded regularity are
separate requirements.  The crossed-rate theorem solves the first, the
joint-Cauchy theorem the second, and the finite ladder the third.  The
four-branch family proves that the complete mechanism is nonempty and needs no
unproved long-time packet.  The radial Sinai theorem is useful but deliberately
restricted to invariant-projector and cohomological channels.  This division
prevents a source-specific identity from being promoted to a universal moving
scatterer response theorem.
