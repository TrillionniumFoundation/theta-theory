# Common deterministic moving-cut reference platform for Papers I--V

## 1. The family

Let

\[
I=[-1/40,1/40]
\]

and put

\[
\begin{aligned}
w_1(a)&=1/10+a, & w_2(a)&=1/5+2a,\\
w_3(a)&=3/10-a, & w_4(a)&=2/5-2a.
\end{aligned}
\]

Then `sum_i w_i(a)=1` and

\[
3/40\le w_i(a)\le9/20.
\]

Let `s_0(a)=0` and `s_i(a)=sum_{j<=i}w_j(a)`.  Define

\[
T_a(x)=\frac{x-s_{i-1}(a)}{w_i(a)},
\qquad
x\in I_i(a):=[s_{i-1}(a),s_i(a)).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Each branch maps bijectively and affinely onto `[0,1)`.  Its inverse branch is

\[
\phi_{a,i}(y)=s_{i-1}(a)+w_i(a)y.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The Perron operator with respect to Lebesgue probability is

\[
(L_ah)(y)=\sum_{i=1}^4w_i(a)h(\phi_{a,i}(y)).
\tag{1.3}
\]

## 2. Physical collision realization

Consider the cylinder

\[
\mathcal Q=\mathbb T\times[0,1].
\]

At a collision with the bottom wall, the particle is launched vertically
upward.  At the top wall, the deterministic collision law reads the incoming
footpoint `x`, identifies its branch `I_i(a)`, and chooses the outgoing unit
velocity whose next bottom footpoint is `T_a(x)`.  The speed is kept equal to
one; a smooth ceiling profile can be chosen so that the return time has any
prescribed positive branch value `tau_i` after adding a vertical holding
channel of the required length.

The bottom-to-bottom Poincare map is exactly (1.1).  The three internal seams
`s_1(a),s_2(a),s_3(a)` are physical discontinuity curves of the collision law
and have velocities

\[
s_1'=1,\qquad s_2'=3,\qquad s_3'=2.
\]

Thus the system is a deterministic billiard with genuinely moving collision
seams.  It is not a specular Sinai table; the separate radial-specular theorem
in Paper I addresses that geometry.

## 3. Nonconjugacy

For `i=1,2,3`, the `i`th branch contains the fixed point

\[
x_i(a)=\frac{s_{i-1}(a)}{1-w_i(a)}.
\]

Its multiplier is

\[
T_a'(x_i(a))=w_i(a)^{-1}.
\]

The ranges of `w_1,w_2,w_3` on `I` are pairwise disjoint.  Hence a `C^1`
conjugacy between `T_a` and `T_b` must match the `i`th fixed point to the `i`th
fixed point.  Periodic multipliers are invariant under `C^1` conjugacy, so
`w_i(a)=w_i(b)` for each `i`; already `w_1(a)=w_1(b)` gives `a=b`.

Therefore distinct parameters give genuinely nonconjugate maps.

## 4. Exact innovation theorem

The decisive property is valid for nonautonomous, history-dependent parameters.
Let `X_0` be Lebesgue distributed.  Recursively choose `A_k in I` measurable
with respect to

\[
\mathcal F_k=\sigma(I_0,\ldots,I_{k-1}),
\]

where `I_k=i` means `X_k in I_i(A_k)`, and set

\[
X_{k+1}=T_{A_k}(X_k).
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (exact predictable innovations)

For every `k>=0`:

1. conditionally on `F_k`, `X_k` is Lebesgue distributed;
2. conditionally on `F_k`,
   \[
   P(I_k=i\mid\mathcal F_k)=w_i(A_k);
   \tag{4.2}
   \]
3. conditionally on `F_k vee sigma(I_k)`, `X_{k+1}` is Lebesgue distributed
   and independent of that sigma-field.

Consequently the branch symbols are conditionally independent innovations with
the predictable probability vector `w(A_k)`.

### Proof

The assertion is true at `k=0`.  Assume it at time `k`.  Since `A_k` is
`F_k`-measurable and `X_k` is conditionally uniform,

\[
P(I_k=i\mid\mathcal F_k)=|I_i(A_k)|=w_i(A_k).
\]

Conditionally on `F_k` and `I_k=i`, `X_k` is uniform on `I_i(A_k)`.  The affine
map `T_{A_k}|_{I_i(A_k)}` sends normalized Lebesgue measure on that interval to
Lebesgue measure on `[0,1)`.  Therefore `X_{k+1}` is conditionally uniform and
its conditional law does not depend on `i` or the preceding history.  This is
exactly conditional independence from `F_k vee sigma(I_k)`, and induction
closes the proof.

## 5. Martingale arrays generated by the same deterministic map

Let `c_i(theta) in R^d` be bounded continuous branch increments, where
`theta_k` is predictable and determines `A_k`.  Set

\[
\bar c(\theta_k)=\sum_iw_i(A_k)c_i(\theta_k),
\qquad
M_k=c_{I_k}(\theta_k)-\bar c(\theta_k).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The exact innovation theorem gives

\[
E[M_k\mid\mathcal F_k]=0
\]

and

\[
E[M_k\otimes M_k\mid\mathcal F_k]
=\Sigma(\theta_k)
:=\sum_iw_i(A_k)
(c_i-\bar c)\otimes(c_i-\bar c).
\tag{5.2}
\]

All conditional moments are uniformly bounded.  If a slow interpolation of
`theta_k` converges, the predictable bracket is a Riemann sum for the integral
of `Sigma(theta_t)`.  The canonical antisymmetric predictable area is zero for
the one-step innovation array.  This provides the actual nonautonomous rough
input of Paper III without an external random process.

## 6. Twists, pressure, and suspension

For branchwise displacement `c_i` and positive roof `tau_i`, the constant
subspace is invariant for

\[
L_{a,q,s}h
=\sum_iw_i(a)e^{q\cdot c_i-s\tau_i}h\circ\phi_{a,i}.
\]

Its eigenvalue is

\[
\lambda(a,q,s)
=\sum_iw_i(a)e^{q\cdot c_i-s\tau_i},
\qquad
\mathscr P=\log\lambda.
\tag{6.1}
\]

On the quotient by constants, the derivative seminorm contracts by at most
`rho=9/20`, uniformly in `a,q,s` when the real parts of `(q,s)` lie in a fixed
small window.  Thus the eigenvalue in (6.1) is the only spectral point outside
a fixed disk and all pressure derivatives are explicit.

For a Diophantine roof-difference vector, Paper II proves a polynomial
all-frequency lower bound on `|1-lambda(a,0,ib)|`, and hence a full
high-frequency resolvent bound with parameter derivatives.

## 7. Pointwise risk-sensitive and game recursions

The same branch innovations define the one-step risk-sensitive operator

\[
(\mathcal T_h\phi)(x)
=\frac1\vartheta\log
\sum_iw_i(A(x))
\exp\{\vartheta\phi(x+h b(x)+\sqrt h\,c_i)\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

No anisotropic distribution is an unknown of this recursion.  Its consistency
operator is

\[
b\cdot D\phi+rac12\Sigma:D^2\phi
+\frac\vartheta2D\phi^T\Sigma D\phi.
\]

For a two-player drift game, replace (7.1) by lower and upper
semi-Lagrangian operators with physical actuator energies
`-mu|u|^2/2+nu|v|^2/2`.  The curvature-compensation theorem of Paper IV gives a
unique pure saddle and a common Isaacs Hamiltonian.

## 8. Noncompact hidden component

To actualize weighted filtering without disconnecting it from the deterministic
platform, take the product of the moving-cut map with a two-sided product shift
carrying Bernoulli refresh variables and Gaussian innovations.  The hidden
coordinate has kernel

\[
P(y,\cdot)=\delta\gamma
 +(1-\delta)N(ry,1-r^2),
\qquad
0<\delta<1,\quad |r|<1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

It is a deterministic skew product on the underlying sequence space.  The
refresh component yields the exact total-variation contraction

\[
\|\mu P-\nu P\|_{TV}
\le(1-\delta)\|\mu-\nu\|_{TV},
\tag{8.2}
\]

while the Gaussian autoregression gives a quadratic Lyapunov drift.  Bounded
nondegenerate `tanh` observations then satisfy the strict observation-gap
criterion after finitely many prediction steps.

## 9. End-to-end status of the platform

The common platform supplies, on one deterministic construction:

```text
moving physical seams
+ nonzero parameter source
+ C1 nonconjugacy
+ explicit CM2 and U3
+ pressure and physical covariance
+ Diophantine all-frequency suspension bounds
+ predictable rough homogenization
+ pointwise entropic HJB/theta evaluation
+ weighted noncompact filtering
+ pure-strategy Isaacs game
+ BSDE/PPDE/path-evaluation branches.
```

Each paper repeats the portion of the construction needed for its own theorem,
so no paper relies on this note as an unpublished proof substitute.
