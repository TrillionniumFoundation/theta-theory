\begin{abstract}
We develop a representation theory for nonlinear theta-expectations after their
HJB, Isaacs, belief-state, or path-dependent dynamic programming equations have
been derived.  A single payoff-independent classical Markov law is shown to
represent the full semigroup only in the linear case.  For a sufficiently
regular individual payoff, the solution jet instead determines an exact
payoff-calibrated linear parabolic equation and Feynman--Kac formula.  We then
separate the available stochastic representations by equation type: Markov
semilinear equations yield FBSDEs; control equations yield controlled or
randomized BSDEs; game equations retain their lower, upper, mixed, or pure
strategy structure; convex second-order equations admit a nondominated 2BSDE
branch; nonconvex second-order Isaacs equations remain game or nonlinear
martingale problems; genuine path states lead to PPDEs and path-dependent
BSDEs or 2BSDEs.

Two complete actual path models are constructed from deterministic Gaussian
shifts.  In the first, a noncompact pure drift game with a genuinely
path-dependent terminal payoff has an entropic value.  Functional It\^o
calculus and completion of squares identify the pure saddle, the semilinear
PPDE, and a quadratic BSDE.  In the second, the controller selects volatility;
the deterministic prelimit converges to a nondominated family of continuous
martingale laws, the value solves a path-dependent $G$-heat equation, and the
minimal 2BSDE represents the same nonlinear evaluation.  Girsanov formulas are
used only after a law or calibrated diffusion has been fixed and never as an
upstream derivation of the theta-semigroup.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The preceding papers derive nonlinear semigroups from deterministic fast
systems by a sequence of spectral response, rough homogenization, filtering,
and dynamic programming limits.  Once the HJB or Isaacs equation is known, one
may ask for a stochastic representation.  This question is delicate because
several non-equivalent notions are often called a probabilistic
representation.

A classical Feynman--Kac formula uses one linear Markov law.  A control problem
uses a family of laws indexed by controls.  A zero-sum game uses two
nonanticipative strategy classes.  A semilinear BSDE uses one forward law but a
nonlinear backward driver.  A second-order BSDE uses a nondominated family of
volatility laws.  A path-dependent PDE lives on a canonical path space and
requires horizontal and vertical derivatives or an appropriate viscosity
framework.  These objects cannot be interchanged without structural
hypotheses.

Our first result is an obstruction: if one payoff-independent classical Markov
kernel represents every terminal payoff, then the semigroup is linear.  This
excludes a universal single-law representation of a genuinely nonlinear
theta-expectation.

Our second result is positive and payoff dependent.  If a solution is smooth
and the nonlinear generator is differentiable in its jet variables, its own
jet calibrates an exact linear operator with a residual source.  This gives a
Feynman--Kac formula for that payoff without reversing the logical dependency:
the calibrated coefficients are defined only after the nonlinear solution is
known.

The remaining theory is typed by the PDE.  Pardoux--Peng BSDEs
\cite{PardouxPeng1990} represent Markov semilinear equations; the general
financial/control framework is developed in El Karoui--Peng--Quenez
\cite{ElKarouiPengQuenez1997}.  Fully nonlinear path-dependent equations and
second-order BSDEs are treated in
\cite{SonerTouziZhang2012,EkrenTouziZhang2014I,EkrenTouziZhang2014II}, while
functional It\^o calculus is developed by Cont--Fourni\'e
\cite{ContFournie2013}.  We preserve the game types established in the
companion filtering/Isaacs paper \cite{QiGames}.

The actual entropic path game in \cref{sec:entropic} goes beyond a formal
pointwise Hamiltonian optimization.  Completion of squares inside functional
It\^o's formula proves both saddle inequalities.  Bounded uniformly continuous
path payoffs are obtained by cylindrical approximation and stability.  The
volatility-control model in \cref{sec:2bsde-actual} gives a separate actual
fully nonlinear branch and a nondominated 2BSDE.

\section{Upstream input and dependency guardrail}\label{sec:input}

The input to this paper is one of the following objects, already derived by a
DPP and a comparison theorem:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item a Markov HJB/theta semigroup;
\item sequential lower and upper Isaacs semigroups;
\item a simultaneous mixed or verified pure Isaacs semigroup;
\item a belief-state HJB semigroup;
\item a path-dependent value functional on a canonical path space.
\end{enumerate}
No CM2, spectral response, enhanced WIP, homogenization, filter stability, DPP,
or viscosity comparison is proved by a representation theorem below.

We use terminal-value generator orientation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orientation}
 \partial_tu+F(t,x,u,Du,D^2u)=0,
 \qquad u(T,\cdot)=\Phi.
\end{equation}
Where differentiable, $F_X$ is positive semidefinite.  The diffusion convention
is
\[
 \frac12\sigma\sigma^T:D^2u.
\]

\section{The single-law obstruction}\label{sec:nogo}

Let $\cE_{s,t}$ be a nonlinear evaluation on bounded continuous terminal
payoffs.

\begin{theorem}[No payoff-independent linear law]\label{thm:single-law}
Suppose that for every bounded continuous $\Phi$ there is one and the same
Markov transition kernel $P_{s,t}(x,dy)$, independent of $\Phi$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-law}
 \cE_{s,t}[\Phi](x)=\int\Phi(y)P_{s,t}(x,dy).
\end{equation}
Then $\cE_{s,t}$ is linear in $\Phi$.  In particular, a semigroup with a
genuinely nonlinear Hamiltonian or nonlinear Hessian dependence cannot satisfy
\eqref{eq:single-law}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The right side of \eqref{eq:single-law} preserves arbitrary finite linear
combinations.  Hence every transition operator is linear.  On a common core,
the infinitesimal generator is also linear, which is incompatible with a
genuinely nonlinear $F$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem does not exclude a payoff-calibrated law, a control-indexed family,
a game, a BSDE, or a nondominated family.  It excludes only one classical law
that is both payoff independent and valid for the whole nonlinear semigroup.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact jet calibration}\label{sec:calibration}

Assume $F$ in \eqref{eq:orientation} is $C^1$ in $(y,p,X)$ on a representation
window and $u$ is classical there.  Define along the solution
\begin{align}
 a^u(t,x)&=F_X(t,x,u,Du,D^2u),
 \label{eq:au}\
 b^u(t,x)&=F_p(t,x,u,Du,D^2u),
 \label{eq:bu}\
 c^u(t,x)&=F_y(t,x,u,Du,D^2u),
 \label{eq:cu}\
 r^u(t,x)&=F(t,x,u,Du,D^2u)
 -a^u:D^2u-b^u\cdot Du-c^uu.
 \label{eq:ru}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Exact calibrated linear equation]\label{thm:calibration}
The same function $u$ solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:calibrated-PDE}
 \partial_tu+a^u:D^2u+b^u\cdot Du+c^uu+r^u=0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \eqref{eq:ru} into \eqref{eq:calibrated-PDE}; the left side becomes
that of \eqref{eq:orientation}.
\end{proof}

Assume $a^u\ge\lambda I$ and choose $\sigma^u$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma-calibrated}
 \sigma^u(\sigma^u)^T=2a^u.
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:calibrated-SDE}
 dX_s=b^u(s,X_s)ds+\sigma^u(s,X_s)dW_s
\end{equation}
be well posed.

\begin{theorem}[Payoff-calibrated Feynman--Kac formula]\label{thm:FK}
If the exponential and source terms are integrable, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FK}
\begin{aligned}
 u(t,x)=\E_{t,x}\Big[&e^{\int_t^Tc^u(r,X_r)dr}\Phi(X_T)\\
 &+\int_t^Te^{\int_t^sc^u(r,X_r)dr}r^u(s,X_s)ds\Big].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply It\^o's formula to
$e^{\int_t^sc^u(r,X_r)dr}u(s,X_s)$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:calibrated-PDE} leaves drift
$-e^{\int_t^sc^u}r^u ds$.  Integrate, take expectations, and rearrange.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Direction of dependence]
The coefficients $a^u,b^u,c^u,r^u$ depend on the already constructed
solution.  Formula \eqref{eq:FK} represents that payoff.  It cannot be used as
an independent derivation of the upstream nonlinear semigroup.
\end{remark}

\section{Markov semilinear FBSDEs}\label{sec:markov-bsde}

Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semilinear-PDE}
 \partial_tu+\frac12\tr(\sigma\sigma^TD^2u)+b\cdot Du
 +f(t,x,u,\sigma^TDu)=0,
 \qquad u(T)=\Phi.
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forward}
 dX_s=b(s,X_s)ds+\sigma(s,X_s)dW_s
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BSDE}
 Y_s=\Phi(X_T)+\int_s^Tf(r,X_r,Y_r,Z_r)dr-
 \int_s^TZ_r\,dW_r.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Markov FBSDE representation]\label{thm:FBSDE}
Under the standard Lipschitz, growth, and forward well-posedness hypotheses,
\eqref{eq:BSDE} has a unique solution and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:YZ}
 Y_s=u(s,X_s),
 \qquad
 Z_s=\sigma(s,X_s)^TDu(s,X_s)
\end{equation}
whenever $u$ is classical.  The identity extends in the usual viscosity/BSDE
sense under the standard stability assumptions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply It\^o's formula to $u(s,X_s)$ and use
\eqref{eq:semilinear-PDE}.  The martingale coefficient is
$\sigma^TDu$.  BSDE uniqueness identifies the pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The HJB gradient $p=Du$ and the BSDE integrand $Z$ are not the same variable.
Their dimensions and probabilistic meanings differ; the relation is
\eqref{eq:YZ}.
\end{remark}

\section{Controlled equations and games}\label{sec:control-game}

For a control equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:control-HJB}
 \partial_tu+\sup_{\alpha\in A}\left\{
 \frac12a^\alpha:D^2u+b^\alpha\cdot Du
 +f^\alpha(t,x,u)\right\}=0,
\end{equation}
the representation is the value over admissible controlled diffusions.  If a
measurable optimal feedback exists, verification gives a feedback-selected
BSDE; without one, the control family or a randomized/constrained BSDE remains
part of the representation.

\begin{theorem}[Controlled representation]\label{thm:control}
Assume compactness or relaxed compactness, measurable selection, tightness,
DPP, and comparison for \eqref{eq:control-HJB}.  Then its viscosity solution is
the controlled diffusion value.  A classical fixed-law BSDE represents the
value only after an optimal feedback has been selected and verified.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The DPP and comparison identify the controlled value with the viscosity
solution.  For a smooth solution, It\^o's formula gives the Hamiltonian
inequality for every control and equality along an optimizer.  Approximation
and stability extend the verification identity.
\end{proof}

For a two-player equation, one retains the corresponding game type.

\begin{theorem}[Typed game representation]\label{thm:game-type}
Sequential lower and upper Isaacs equations are represented by their respective
nonanticipative games.  A simultaneous relaxed equation is represented by the
mixed game.  A pure feedback representation is valid only when a pure saddle
has been proved, for example by the pure-Isaacs or strong concave--convex
theorems of the companion game paper.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each statement is the verification/DPP theorem for the corresponding strategy
space.  The optimization order appears in the local Hamiltonian and cannot be
changed by a stochastic representation.  Mixed minimax convexifies the action
sets but does not create pure selectors.
\end{proof}

\section{Fully nonlinear second-order equations}\label{sec:second-order}

A convex volatility-uncertainty equation has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex-second}
 \partial_tu+\sup_{a\in\A(t,x)}
 \left\{\frac12a:D^2u+f(t,x,u,Du,a)\right\}=0.
\end{equation}
Under stability of the nondominated class, aggregation, comparison, and the
minimality condition, it admits a second-order BSDE representation
\cite{SonerTouziZhang2012}.

By contrast, a nonconvex Hessian dependence such as
\[
 \sup_u\inf_v\left\{\tfrac12a^{u,v}:D^2u+\cdots\right\}
\]
is a game unless an independent nonconvex second-order representation theorem
is proved.

\begin{theorem}[Second-order typing rule]\label{thm:second-order-rule}
A theta-equation enters the 2BSDE branch only when its Hessian generator is the
support function of a stable volatility family, or when an equivalent
second-order representation has been proved.  A generic nonconvex Isaacs
equation remains a game or nonlinear martingale problem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A 2BSDE value is a supremum, or more generally a time-consistent nonlinear
expectation, over a specified nondominated volatility family.  Its local
Hessian generator is therefore the corresponding support function.  Without
such a representation, the defining dynamic programming structure is the
original sup--inf game, which cannot be replaced by a convex volatility family
without changing the PDE.
\end{proof}

\section{Path-dependent equations}\label{sec:path}

Let $\Omega=C([0,T];\R^d)$ with canonical process $B$ and concatenation
$\omega\otimes_t\omega'$.  A nonanticipative functional is written
$U(t,\omega)$.  For sufficiently smooth functionals, $\partial_t^HU$ denotes
the horizontal derivative, while $\partial_\omega U$ and
$\partial_{\omega\omega}^2U$ are vertical derivatives.

A semilinear PPDE is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semilinear-PPDE}
 \partial_t^HU+rac12\tr(\sigma\sigma^T
 \partial_{\omega\omega}^2U)
 +b\cdot\partial_\omega U
 +f(t,\omega,U,\sigma^T\partial_\omega U)=0.
\end{equation}
Functional It\^o calculus gives a path-dependent BSDE representation for smooth
solutions, and viscosity stability extends the result under the comparison
framework of \cite{EkrenTouziZhang2014I,EkrenTouziZhang2014II}.

\begin{definition}[Path evaluation]
For a terminal path functional $\Phi$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-evaluation}
 \cE_{s,t}[\Phi](\omega_{[0,s]})=U(s,\omega_{[0,s]}).
\end{equation}
The path-space DPP gives time consistency under concatenation.
\end{definition}

A convex fully nonlinear PPDE may use a path-dependent 2BSDE.  A nonconvex game
PPDE remains a path game.  No finite-dimensional reduction is imposed without
a proved sufficient statistic.

\section{An actual entropic pure path game}\label{sec:entropic}

We now construct a complete path-dependent branch from deterministic fast
noise.  Let
\[
 \Omega_0=(\R)^{\mathbb Z}
\]
with Gaussian product measure and left shift.  Let $\xi_k$ be the coordinate at
integer time $k$.  For slow step $h=\varepsilon^2$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prelimit-drift-game}
 X_{k+1}^\varepsilon=X_k^\varepsilon
 +\varepsilon\xi_{k+1}
 +\varepsilon^2(u_k+v_k).
\end{equation}
Under bounded-energy progressively measurable controls, polygonal interpolation
converges to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift-game-SDE}
 dX_t=(u_t+v_t)dt+dW_t.
\end{equation}

The maximizing player chooses $u\in\R$ and the minimizing player chooses
$v\in\R$.  Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:running-game}
 \ell(u,v)=-\frac\mu2u^2+\frac\nu2v^2,
 \qquad \mu,\nu>0.
\end{equation}
For a path-gradient $p$, the Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-Hamiltonian}
 \sup_u\inf_v\left\{p(u+v)-\frac\mu2u^2+\frac\nu2v^2\right\}
 =c p^2,
 \qquad
 c=\frac1{2\mu}-\frac1{2\nu},
\end{equation}
with unique saddle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-saddle}
 u^*(p)=\frac p\mu,
 \qquad
 v^*(p)=-\frac p\nu.
\end{equation}

Choose a bounded uniformly continuous terminal functional that depends on the
entire final window, for example
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-payoff}
 \Phi(\omega)=\tanh\left(
 \int_{T-\delta}^T\omega_sds+
 \max_{T-\delta\le s\le T}\omega_s
 \right).
\end{equation}
It is not a function of $\omega_T$ alone.

Assume first $c\ne0$ and put $\theta=2c$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropic-value}
 U(t,\omega)=\frac1\theta\log\E\left[
 \exp\left(\theta\Phi\bigl(
 \omega\otimes_t(\omega_t+W_\cdot-W_t)
 \bigr)\right)\right].
\end{equation}
For $c=0$, use the linear conditional expectation.

\begin{theorem}[Entropic path PPDE and DPP]\label{thm:entropic-PPDE}
The functional \eqref{eq:entropic-value} is bounded, uniformly continuous, and
time consistent.  It is the unique bounded viscosity solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropic-PPDE}
 \partial_t^HU+rac12\partial_{\omega\omega}^2U
 +c\abs{\partial_\omega U}^2=0,
 \qquad U(T,\cdot)=\Phi.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The tower property of conditional expectation gives the path-space DPP.
For a smooth cylindrical terminal functional, set $V=e^{\theta U}$.  Functional
It\^o calculus shows that $V$ solves the linear path heat equation; the identity
$\theta=2c$ cancels the quadratic gradient term.  Hence $U$ solves
\eqref{eq:entropic-PPDE}.  Approximate \eqref{eq:path-payoff} uniformly by
bounded smooth cylindrical functionals.  Stability of the entropic formula and
of viscosity solutions gives existence for $\Phi$.  Comparison follows by the
same exponential transform and comparison for the linear path heat equation.
\end{proof}

The formal Hamiltonian saddle must still be verified at the game level.

\begin{theorem}[Pure path-game verification]\label{thm:path-game}
Let $J_{t,\omega}(u,v)$ be the payoff consisting of terminal value
\eqref{eq:path-payoff} plus the integral of \eqref{eq:running-game} along
\eqref{eq:drift-game-SDE}.  Then the feedbacks
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feedback-path}
 u_s^*=\frac{\partial_\omega U(s,X)}{\mu},
 \qquad
 v_s^*=-\frac{\partial_\omega U(s,X)}{\nu}
\end{equation}
form a pure saddle in the verification class, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:saddle-ineq}
 J_{t,\omega}(u,v^*)\le U(t,\omega)\le
 J_{t,\omega}(u^*,v)
\end{equation}
for every admissible $u,v$.  Approximation extends the value identity to the
bounded path payoff \eqref{eq:path-payoff}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a smooth cylindrical approximation, apply functional It\^o's formula to
$U(s,X)$ under arbitrary controls.  With $p=\partial_\omega U$, subtract the
PPDE drift and complete squares:
\begin{align}\label{eq:complete-squares}
 &-cp^2+p(u+v)-\frac\mu2u^2+\frac\nu2v^2\\
 &\qquad=-\frac\mu2\left(u-\frac p\mu\right)^2
 +\frac\nu2\left(v+\frac p\nu\right)^2.
\end{align}
Set $v=v^*$ to obtain a nonpositive residual and the left inequality in
\eqref{eq:saddle-ineq}; set $u=u^*$ to obtain a nonnegative residual and the
right inequality.  At both feedbacks the residual is zero.  Uniform
cylindrical approximation, boundedness of $\Phi$, and stability of the
controlled laws pass the saddle identity to \eqref{eq:path-payoff}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic BSDE representation]\label{thm:quadratic-BSDE}
The entropic value is represented by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-BSDE}
 Y_t=\Phi(X)+\int_t^Tc\abs{Z_s}^2ds-
 \int_t^TZ_s\,dW_s,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropic-BSDE-formula}
 Y_t=\frac1{2c}\log\E_t[e^{2c\Phi(X)}]
\end{equation}
when $c\ne0$.  For smooth data,
$Z_t=\partial_\omega U(t,X)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The process $e^{2cY_t}$ is a martingale: It\^o's formula cancels the drift in
\eqref{eq:quadratic-BSDE}.  Its terminal value is $e^{2c\Phi}$, giving
\eqref{eq:entropic-BSDE-formula}.  Conversely the martingale representation
theorem and logarithmic It\^o formula produce \eqref{eq:quadratic-BSDE}.
Functional It\^o calculus identifies $Z$ for smooth $U$.
\end{proof}

\section{An actual volatility-control PPDE and 2BSDE}\label{sec:2bsde-actual}

Use another deterministic Gaussian shift and let the controller choose
$\alpha_k\in[\underline\sigma,\overline\sigma]$ predictably.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prelimit-vol}
 X_{k+1}^\varepsilon=X_k^\varepsilon+
 \varepsilon\alpha_k\xi_{k+1}.
\end{equation}
The limit laws are those of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vol-SDE}
 X_t=x+\int_0^t\alpha_s\,dW_s,
 \qquad
 \underline\sigma\le\alpha_s\le\overline\sigma.
\end{equation}
Let $\cP_H$ denote the resulting stable nondominated family of continuous
martingale laws on canonical space.

For a bounded uniformly continuous path payoff $\Phi$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G-value}
 U(t,\omega)=\sup_{\Pp\in\cP_H(t,\omega)}
 \E^{\Pp}[\Phi(\omega\otimes_tB)].
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Actual path-dependent volatility value]\label{thm:G-PPDE}
The deterministic prelimit values associated with \eqref{eq:prelimit-vol}
converge to \eqref{eq:G-value}.  The value is the unique bounded viscosity
solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G-PPDE}
 \partial_t^HU+G(\partial_{\omega\omega}^2U)=0,
 \qquad
 G(\gamma)=\frac12\sup_{a\in
 [\underline\sigma^2,\overline\sigma^2]}a\gamma,
\end{equation}
with terminal condition $\Phi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditional Gaussian increments make \eqref{eq:prelimit-vol} a controlled
martingale array.  Predictable quadratic variations converge to
$\int\alpha_s^2ds$, and tightness plus the martingale problem identifies every
subsequential law in $\cP_H$.  Conversely, piecewise constant predictable
volatilities are approximated by prelimit controls, giving the lower bound for
values.  The DPP follows from concatenation of volatility controls.  Monotone
consistency yields \eqref{eq:G-PPDE}; path-dependent comparison identifies the
limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Minimal 2BSDE representation]\label{thm:2BSDE}
There exist processes $(Y,Z)$ and, for every $\Pp\in\cP_H$, an increasing
process $K^\Pp$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2BSDE}
 Y_t=\Phi-
 \int_t^TZ_s\,dB_s+K_T^\Pp-K_t^\Pp,
 \qquad \Pp\text{-a.s.},
\end{equation}
and the family $K^\Pp$ satisfies the standard minimality condition.  Moreover,
for every $\Pp\in\cP_H$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:esssup-rep}
 Y_t=\esssup_{\Pp'\in\cP_H(t+,\Pp)}
 \E^{\Pp'}[\Phi\mid\mathcal F_t],
 \qquad \Pp\text{-a.s.}
\end{equation}
Thus $Y_t=U(t,B)$ and \eqref{eq:2BSDE} represents the actual path-dependent
volatility evaluation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The family \eqref{eq:G-value} is time consistent and stable under
concatenation.  The nonlinear Doob--Meyer decomposition under each
$\Pp\in\cP_H$, together with aggregation of the universal martingale part,
gives \eqref{eq:2BSDE}.  The minimality condition is equivalent to the dynamic
essential-supremum formula \eqref{eq:esssup-rep}.  Conversely,
\eqref{eq:esssup-rep} implies the DPP and identifies the PPDE value.  This is
the standard second-order BSDE representation applied to the explicit stable
volatility family generated by \eqref{eq:prelimit-vol}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This branch is convex in the Hessian variable.  It does not represent an
arbitrary nonconvex Isaacs PPDE.
\end{remark}

\section{Girsanov transformations after calibration}\label{sec:girsanov}

Let a calibrated or feedback-selected diffusion be fixed, and let $\vartheta$
be progressively measurable with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Novikov}
 \E\exp\left(\frac12\int_t^T\abs{\vartheta_s}^2ds\right)<\infty.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 \mathcal Z_T=
 \exp\left(\int_t^T\vartheta_s\,dW_s
 -\frac12\int_t^T\abs{\vartheta_s}^2ds\right).
\end{equation}
Under $d\Qp=\mathcal Z_Td\Pp$,
$W_s^\Qp=W_s-\int_t^s\vartheta_rdr$ is Brownian and the drift changes from
$b$ to $b+\sigma\vartheta$.

\begin{theorem}[Post-calibration Girsanov rule]\label{thm:girsanov}
Every formula obtained from \eqref{eq:density} represents the already selected
or calibrated payoff.  It neither proves the upstream nonlinear HJB/Isaacs
limit nor produces one payoff-independent law for the full theta-semigroup.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Girsanov's theorem changes measure for a fixed semimartingale law.  The density
contains the already chosen $\vartheta$ and $\sigma$.  The single-law
obstruction \cref{thm:single-law} prevents promoting this transformed law to a
universal representation of a genuinely nonlinear semigroup.
\end{proof}

\section{The representation hierarchy}\label{sec:hierarchy}

\begin{theorem}[Typed representation theorem]\label{thm:hierarchy}
Let a theta, HJB, Isaacs, belief-state, or path semigroup have been derived by
an upstream DPP and comparison theorem.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item every sufficiently smooth individual payoff admits the exact calibrated
linearization and Feynman--Kac formula of \cref{thm:calibration,thm:FK};
\item a Markov semilinear equation admits the FBSDE representation of
\cref{thm:FBSDE};
\item a control HJB admits a controlled, relaxed, or randomized representation,
with a fixed feedback BSDE only after verification;
\item a game equation retains its sequential, mixed, or pure type;
\item a convex volatility-uncertainty equation may use a 2BSDE, while a generic
nonconvex second-order Isaacs equation remains a game or nonlinear martingale
problem;
\item a genuine path-state equation admits a PPDE and, according to its type, a
path-dependent BSDE or 2BSDE;
\item Girsanov transformations apply only after a law has been fixed and the
exponential integrability condition holds.
\end{enumerate}
None of these conclusions is an upstream proof of homogenization or the DPP.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items (i)--(vii) are respectively
\cref{thm:calibration,thm:FK,thm:FBSDE,thm:control,thm:game-type,
thm:second-order-rule,thm:entropic-PPDE,thm:2BSDE,thm:girsanov}.  The dependency
statement follows from the definitions: every calibrated coefficient,
feedback, game value, or nondominated law family is constructed only after the
upstream equation and admissible strategy class have been fixed.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

A nonlinear theta-expectation has no universal classical law, but it has a
rich, precisely typed representation theory.  Jet calibration gives an exact
payoff-dependent linear law.  Semilinear equations give FBSDEs, control
problems give controlled families, games retain their strategy type, and
convex volatility uncertainty gives a 2BSDE.  The deterministic Gaussian
models show that the path-dependent pure-game, entropic PPDE, quadratic BSDE,
volatility PPDE, and nondominated 2BSDE branches are actual rather than formal.
The causal separation from the deterministic microscopic derivation is
maintained throughout.
