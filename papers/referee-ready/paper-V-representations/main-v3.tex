\begin{abstract}
We develop a positive representation theory for the theta-semigroups obtained
from deterministic moving-cut dynamics in the companion papers.  The first
result is a parabolic implicit-function theorem: under explicit uniform
parabolicity and H\"older hypotheses, the terminal-value solution map is
Fr\'echet differentiable, and its derivative is the unique solution of the
linear tangent equation.  Thus differentiability is proved rather than
assumed.

For the actual entropic theta-semigroup the result is global and sharper.  The
terminal map
\[
\phi\longmapsto\frac1\vartheta\log P_{t,T}e^{\vartheta\phi}
\]
is real analytic on bounded payoffs.  Its first derivative is expectation
under an explicit terminally tilted probability law and its second derivative
is the corresponding covariance.  The tangent kernels form a cocycle obtained
by differentiating nonlinear time consistency.

When the coefficients and payoff are smooth, the density process of the tilted
law is the stochastic exponential of
$\vartheta\sigma^TDu$.  A stated Novikov condition gives a genuine Girsanov
theorem, including the Radon--Nikodym derivative, the Brownian drift shift, and
the tangent diffusion with drift $b+\vartheta\sigma\sigma^TDu$.  No nonlinear
inversion of $Z=\sigma^TDu$ is used.  The same solution is represented by a
quadratic BSDE and its tangent by a linear BSDE under the tilted law.

We extend these results to bounded path payoffs, obtaining a time-consistent
entropic path evaluation, its quadratic BSDE, and the corresponding PPDE.  The
pure energy game of the companion game paper has an exact pathwise entropic
value and pure feedback saddle.  Finally, a stable volatility-control family
gives an actual path-dependent $G$-heat equation and its nondominated 2BSDE
representation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A nonlinear semigroup cannot in general be represented by one
payoff-independent linear Markov law.  It may, however, possess a family of
tangent laws, one for each terminal payoff.  For the entropic theta-semigroup
these laws are explicit exponential tilts and have a strong dynamic structure:
they compose in time and are related by an actual Girsanov change of measure.

This paper begins after the deterministic response, homogenization, and HJB
limits have been proved.  It does not use stochastic representation to derive
those limits.  The representation layer has three independent outputs:
terminal-map differentiability, probabilistic tangent laws, and typed
path/second-order branches.

Classical BSDE theory begins with \cite{PardouxPeng1990}; quadratic equations
are treated in \cite{Kobylanski2000}.  Functional It\^o calculus and PPDEs are
developed in \cite{Dupire2009,ContFournie2013,EkrenTouziZhang2014I,
EkrenTouziZhang2014II}.  Nondominated second-order BSDEs are treated in
\cite{CheriditoSonerTouziVictoir2007,SonerTouziZhang2012}.  Our contribution is
the differentiable tangent-law bundle attached to the theta-semigroup, its
cocycle and genuine Girsanov realization, and the exact connection to the
actual deterministic models of the companion series.

\section{A precise single-law obstruction}\label{sec:obstruction}

Let $E$ be a Polish state space.

\begin{theorem}[Payoff-independent law implies linearity]\label{thm:single-law}
Suppose that for fixed $s<t$ there is one probability kernel
$P_{s,t}(x,dy)$ such that for every $\phi\in C_b(E)$,
\[
 \cE_{s,t}[\phi](x)=\int_E\phi(y)P_{s,t}(x,dy).
\]
Then $\cE_{s,t}$ is linear, positive and constant preserving.  Consequently a
semigroup whose generator is genuinely nonlinear cannot have such a universal
payoff-independent classical law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Integration against a fixed probability kernel preserves arbitrary linear
combinations, order, and constants.
\end{proof}

The theorem does not exclude payoff-dependent tangent laws, controlled
families, games, or nondominated representations.

\section{A parabolic implicit-function theorem}\label{sec:IFT}

We first prove differentiability for a general uniformly parabolic terminal
problem.  Work on the torus $\T^d$.  Fix $0<\alpha<1$ and let
\[
 \mathbb X=C^{1+\alpha/2,2+\alpha}([0,T]\times\T^d),
 \qquad
 \mathbb Y=C^{\alpha/2,\alpha}([0,T]\times\T^d)
 \times C^{2+\alpha}(\T^d).
\]
Let
$F(t,x,r,p,X)$ be $C^k$, $k\ge2$, on a neighbourhood of the relevant jet
set.  Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-PDE}
 -u_t-F(t,x,u,Du,D^2u)=0,
 \qquad u(T)=\phi.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Uniform classical window]\label{ass:classical-window}
There are $0<\lambda\le\Lambda<\infty$ such that
\[
 \lambda I\le F_X(t,x,r,p,X)\le\Lambda I
\]
on the jet neighbourhood.  All derivatives of $F$ through order $k$ are
bounded and H\"older there.  At one pair $(u_0,\phi_0)$,
$u_0\in\mathbb X$ solves \eqref{eq:general-PDE}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Fr\'echet differentiability of the terminal map]\label{thm:IFT}
Under \cref{ass:classical-window}, there are neighbourhoods of $\phi_0$ in
$C^{2+\alpha}$ and of $u_0$ in $\mathbb X$ on which the solution map
$\phi\mapsto u[\phi]$ is $C^{k-1}$.  For a terminal direction $\eta$, the first
derivative $v=Du[\phi]\eta$ is the unique classical solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-general}
 -v_t-F_rv-F_p\cdot Dv-F_X:D^2v=0,
 \qquad v(T)=\eta,
\end{equation}
where the coefficients are evaluated along $u[\phi]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define
\[
 \mathfrak F(u,\phi)=
 \bigl(-u_t-F(t,x,u,Du,D^2u),\ u(T)-\phi\bigr)
 \in\mathbb Y.
\]
H\"older composition theorems make $\mathfrak F$ a $C^{k-1}$ map.  Its
$u$-derivative at a solution is
\[
 v\longmapsto
 \bigl(-v_t-F_rv-F_p\cdot Dv-F_X:D^2v,\ v(T)\bigr).
\]
Uniform parabolic Schauder theory makes this a bounded isomorphism from
$\mathbb X$ to $\mathbb Y$.  The Banach-space implicit-function theorem gives
the $C^{k-1}$ solution map.  Differentiating
$\mathfrak F(u[\phi],\phi)=0$ yields \eqref{eq:tangent-general}.
\end{proof}

This theorem supplies the missing tangent equation without assuming an
expansion of $u^{\delta}$.

\section{The actual entropic theta-semigroup}\label{sec:entropic}

Let $X^{t,x}$ solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forward}
 dX_s=b(s,X_s)ds+\sigma(s,X_s)dW_s,
 \qquad X_t=x,
\end{equation}
where $b,\sigma$ are bounded globally Lipschitz, and
$a=\sigma\sigma^T$ is uniformly elliptic.  Let $P_{t,T}$ be the transition
operator.  For $\vartheta\ne0$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-map}
 \cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi](x)
 =\frac1\vartheta\log P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi})(x).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Global analytic terminal map]\label{thm:analytic}
For fixed $(t,x)$, the map
$C_b(\R^d)\ni\phi\mapsto\cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi](x)$ is real analytic.
Its first two Fr\'echet derivatives are
\begin{align}
 D\cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi]\eta
 &=\frac{P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi}\eta)}
        {P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi})},
 \label{eq:first-derivative}\\
 D^2\cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi](\eta_1,\eta_2)
 &=\vartheta\left[
 \frac{P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi}\eta_1\eta_2)}
      {P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi})}
 -\prod_{j=1}^2
 \frac{P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi}\eta_j)}
      {P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi})}
 \right].
 \label{eq:second-derivative}
\end{align}
Higher derivatives are the corresponding tilted joint cumulants multiplied by
$\vartheta^{n-1}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On every bounded ball of $C_b$, the exponential Nemytskii map has an absolutely
convergent power series in the sup norm.  The Markov operator is bounded and
positive, while
\[
 P_{t,T}e^{\vartheta\phi}
 \ge e^{-\abs\vartheta\norm\phi_\infty}>0.
\]
The logarithm is therefore analytic on a neighbourhood of its range.
Differentiating the composition gives \eqref{eq:first-derivative} and
\eqref{eq:second-derivative}; the higher logarithmic derivatives are
cumulants.
\end{proof}

\section{Tangent laws and their cocycle}\label{sec:tangent-laws}

Define a probability law on terminal paths by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-law}
 \frac{d\Qp_{t,x}^{\phi}}{d\Pp_{t,x}}
 =\frac{e^{\vartheta\phi(X_T)}}
 {P_{t,T}e^{\vartheta\phi}(x)}.
\end{equation}
Then \eqref{eq:first-derivative} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-expectation}
 D\cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi]\eta(x)
 =\E^{\Qp_{t,x}^{\phi}}\eta(X_T).
\end{equation}
For an intermediate interval, define the tangent kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-kernel}
 K_{s,t}^{\psi}(x,dy)=
 \frac{e^{\vartheta\psi(y)}P_{s,t}(x,dy)}
 {P_{s,t}e^{\vartheta\psi}(x)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Tangent-kernel cocycle]\label{thm:cocycle}
The nonlinear semigroup is time consistent and its tangent kernels satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-cocycle}
 K_{r,t}^{\phi}
 =K_{r,s}^{\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]}
  K_{s,t}^{\phi},
 \qquad r\le s\le t.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derivative-chain}
 D\cE_{r,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]
 =D\cE_{r,s}^{\vartheta}
   [\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]]
 \circ D\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Markov property gives
\[
 P_{r,t}e^{\vartheta\phi}
 =P_{r,s}\exp\{\vartheta\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]\},
\]
which is nonlinear time consistency.  The Radon--Nikodym factors in
\eqref{eq:tangent-kernel} multiply and the intermediate normalizations cancel,
giving \eqref{eq:kernel-cocycle}.  Formula \eqref{eq:derivative-chain} follows
either from this identity or from the Banach-space chain rule.
\end{proof}

Thus the tangent laws form a dynamically consistent bundle even though the
nonlinear semigroup has no universal linear law.

\section{The tangent PDE}\label{sec:tangent-PDE}

Assume now that $b,\sigma$ and $\phi$ are smooth enough for a classical
solution.  Put
\[
 u(t,x)=\cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi](x).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropic-PDE}
 -u_t-b\cdot Du-\frac12a:D^2u
 -\frac\vartheta2\abs{\sigma^TDu}^2=0,
 \qquad u(T)=\phi.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Tangent equation and tangent diffusion]\label{thm:tangent-PDE}
For a terminal direction $\eta$, the derivative
$v=D\cE^{\vartheta}[\phi]\eta$ is the unique solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropic-tangent}
 -v_t-\frac12a:D^2v
 -\bigl(b+\vartheta aDu\bigr)\cdot Dv=0,
 \qquad v(T)=\eta.
\end{equation}
It is represented by the diffusion with volatility $\sigma$ and drift
$b+\vartheta aDu$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:IFT} to \eqref{eq:entropic-PDE}, or differentiate the exact
formula \eqref{eq:theta-map}.  The derivative of the quadratic Hamiltonian is
$\vartheta aDu\cdot Dv$.  Linear parabolic Feynman--Kac gives the diffusion
representation, which agrees with \eqref{eq:tangent-expectation}.
\end{proof}

\section{A genuine Girsanov theorem}\label{sec:Girsanov}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-process}
 \mathcal Z_s^{\phi}
 =\E^{\Pp_{t,x}}
 \left[\left.
 \frac{e^{\vartheta\phi(X_T)}}
 {P_{t,T}e^{\vartheta\phi}(x)}
 \right|\F_s\right]
 =\exp\{\vartheta u(s,X_s)-\vartheta u(t,x)\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Novikov window]\label{ass:Novikov}
The classical solution satisfies
\[
 \E_{t,x}\exp\left(
 \frac{\vartheta^2}{2}
 \int_t^T\abs{\sigma^TDu(s,X_s)}^2ds
 \right)<\infty.
\]
This holds, for example, when $\sigma^TDu$ is bounded on the finite horizon.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Terminal tilt equals a Girsanov change of measure]\label{thm:Girsanov}
Under \cref{ass:Novikov},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stochastic-exponential}
 \mathcal Z_s^{\phi}
 =\exp\left(
 \int_t^s\beta_r^{\phi}\,dW_r
 -\frac12\int_t^s\abs{\beta_r^{\phi}}^2dr
 \right),
 \qquad
 \beta_r^{\phi}=\vartheta\sigma^TDu(r,X_r).
\end{equation}
The measure defined by
$d\Qp_{t,x}^{\phi}=\mathcal Z_T^{\phi}d\Pp_{t,x}$ is exactly
\eqref{eq:tilted-law}.  Under $\Qp_{t,x}^{\phi}$,
\[
 W_s^{\phi}=W_s-\int_t^s\beta_r^{\phi}dr
\]
is Brownian and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:changed-SDE}
 dX_s=\bigl(b+\vartheta aDu\bigr)(s,X_s)ds
 +\sigma(s,X_s)dW_s^{\phi}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the first terminal derivative is expectation under the changed
diffusion \eqref{eq:changed-SDE}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply It\^o's formula to $e^{\vartheta u(s,X_s)}$.  The drift cancels exactly
by \eqref{eq:entropic-PDE}, leaving
\[
 d\mathcal Z_s^{\phi}
 =\mathcal Z_s^{\phi}\beta_s^{\phi}dW_s.
\]
Novikov makes the stochastic exponential a true martingale and gives
\eqref{eq:stochastic-exponential}.  Its terminal value is the density in
\eqref{eq:tilted-law}.  Girsanov's theorem gives the shifted Brownian motion
and \eqref{eq:changed-SDE}.  The last claim is
\eqref{eq:tangent-expectation}.
\end{proof}

This is a probability-measure theorem, not an algebraic shift of a Hamiltonian.

\section{Quadratic BSDE and tangent BSDE}\label{sec:BSDE}

Let $X$ solve \eqref{eq:forward}.  Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-BSDE}
 Y_s=\phi(X_T)+\int_s^T\frac\vartheta2\abs{Z_r}^2dr
 -\int_s^TZ_r\,dW_r.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic theta-BSDE]\label{thm:quadratic-BSDE}
For bounded terminal $\phi$, \eqref{eq:quadratic-BSDE} has the unique bounded
quadratic-BSDE solution and
\[
 Y_s=u(s,X_s),
 \qquad Z_s=\sigma(s,X_s)^TDu(s,X_s)
\]
whenever the classical derivatives exist.  No inversion of the map
$p\mapsto\sigma(x,p)^Tp$ is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exponential transform gives
$e^{\vartheta Y_s}=\E[e^{\vartheta\phi(X_T)}\mid\F_s]$, which proves existence
and uniqueness in the bounded quadratic class; see \cite{Kobylanski2000}.
It\^o's formula identifies the Markovian pair.
\end{proof}

Differentiate the BSDE in a terminal direction.  If
$(V,U)=D(Y,Z)[\eta]$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-BSDE}
 V_s=\eta(X_T)+\int_s^T\vartheta Z_r\cdot U_rdr
 -\int_s^TU_r\,dW_r.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Linear tangent under the tilted law]\label{cor:tangent-BSDE}
Under the Girsanov measure of \cref{thm:Girsanov},
\[
 V_s=\E^{\Qp^{\phi}}[\eta(X_T)\mid\F_s].
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $dW^{\phi}=dW-\vartheta Z\,ds$, equation
\eqref{eq:tangent-BSDE} becomes a martingale representation under
$\Qp^{\phi}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Path-dependent entropic evaluation}\label{sec:path}

Let $\Omega=C([0,T];\R^d)$ and suppose path-dependent coefficients
$b(t,\omega)$ and $\sigma(t,\omega)$ are bounded, progressively measurable,
Lipschitz in the stopped-path norm, and uniformly nondegenerate.  Let
$\Pp^{t,\omega}$ be the continuation law.  For a bounded uniformly continuous
terminal functional $\Phi$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-eval}
 U(t,\omega)=\frac1\vartheta
 \log\E^{\Pp^{t,\omega}}e^{\vartheta\Phi(X)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Path theta-evaluation and quadratic BSDE]\label{thm:path}
The functional \eqref{eq:path-eval} is time consistent under path
concatenation.  It is the first component of the quadratic BSDE
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-BSDE}
 Y_s=\Phi(X)+\int_s^T\frac\vartheta2\abs{Z_r}^2dr
 -\int_s^TZ_r\,dW_r.
\end{equation}
The map $\Phi\mapsto U(t,\omega)$ is real analytic on bounded path payoffs,
with first derivative equal to expectation under the terminally tilted path
law and second derivative equal to its covariance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditional exponential expectation and the tower property give time
consistency.  The exponential transform proves the BSDE representation.
The Banach-space proof of \cref{thm:analytic} applies verbatim on
$C_b(\Omega)$.
\end{proof}

If $U$ is Dupire smooth, functional It\^o calculus
\cite{Dupire2009,ContFournie2013} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PPDE}
 -\partial_t^HU-b\cdot\partial_\omega U
 -\frac12a:\partial_{\omega\omega}^2U
 -\frac\vartheta2\abs{\sigma^T\partial_\omega U}^2=0,
 \qquad U(T)=\Phi.
\end{equation}
For nonsmooth bounded path payoffs, \eqref{eq:path-eval} is the canonical
viscosity solution in the path-dependent framework
\cite{EkrenTouziZhang2014I,EkrenTouziZhang2014II}.

\section{The actual pure energy path game}\label{sec:path-game}

Let $\sigma>0$, $\mu,\nu>0$, and
$c=\frac12(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})$.  On a weak formulation consider
\[
 dX_s=(u_s+v_s)ds+\sigma dW_s
\]
with payoff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-game-payoff}
 J_{t,\omega}(u,v)=\E\left[
 \Phi(X)+\int_t^T
 \left(-\frac\mu2u_s^2+
 \frac\nu2v_s^2\right)ds\right].
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Entropic path value and pure saddle]\label{thm:path-game}
For bounded uniformly continuous $\Phi$, the lower and upper path values agree
and equal
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-game-value}
 U(t,\omega)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{\sigma^2}{2c}
 \log\E^{t,\omega}
 \exp\left(\frac{2c}{\sigma^2}\Phi(X)\right),&c\ne0,\\[2ex]
 \E^{t,\omega}\Phi(X),&c=0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For Dupire-smooth cylindrical $\Phi$, the feedback controls
\[
 u_s^*=\frac1\mu\partial_\omega U(s,X),
 \qquad
 v_s^*=-\frac1\nu\partial_\omega U(s,X)
\]
form a pure saddle.  For general bounded uniformly continuous $\Phi$, smooth
cylindrical approximation yields pure $\varepsilon$-saddles and the same exact
value \eqref{eq:path-game-value}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The functional in \eqref{eq:path-game-value} solves the path PPDE
\[
 -\partial_t^HU-rac{\sigma^2}{2}
 \partial_{\omega\omega}^2U-c\abs{\partial_\omega U}^2=0.
\]
For a smooth cylindrical payoff, apply functional It\^o calculus and the exact
identity
\[
 -cp^2+p(u+v)-\frac\mu2u^2+
 \frac\nu2v^2
 =-\frac\mu2(u-p/\mu)^2+
 \frac\nu2(v+p/\nu)^2.
\]
With $v=v^*$ the drift defect is nonpositive; with $u=u^*$ it is
nonnegative.  This proves the saddle inequalities.  Uniform approximation of
$\Phi$, stability of exponential expectations, and the energy estimates pass
the value and $\varepsilon$-saddles to the bounded uniformly continuous class.
\end{proof}

This is the path-dependent representation of the actual game-generated
Hamiltonian from Paper IV.

\section{Volatility control and a nondominated 2BSDE}\label{sec:2BSDE}

Let $0<\underline a<\overline a<\infty$ and let $\cA$ be the progressively
measurable volatility controls with
$\alpha_s^2\in[\underline a,\overline a]$.  For
\[
 X_s=\omega_t+\int_t^s\alpha_r\,dW_r
\]
let $\Pp^{t,\omega,\alpha}$ be the induced continuation law.  The family is
stable under conditioning and pasting.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gvalue}
 U^G(t,\omega)=\sup_{\alpha\in\cA}
 \E^{\Pp^{t,\omega,\alpha}}\Phi(X).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Actual path $G$-heat and 2BSDE branch]\label{thm:2BSDE}
For bounded uniformly continuous $\Phi$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the stable family has the pathwise dynamic programming property;
\item $U^G$ is the unique bounded viscosity solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GPPDE}
 -\partial_t^HU^G-G(\partial_{\omega\omega}^2U^G)=0,
 \qquad
 G(\Gamma)=\frac12\sup_{a\in[\underline a,\overline a]}a\Gamma;
\end{equation}
\item there is a nondominated 2BSDE family
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2BSDE}
 Y_t=\Phi-\int_t^TZ_s\,dB_s+K_T^{\Pp}-K_t^{\Pp},
 \qquad \Pp\text{-a.s. for every }\Pp\in\cP,
\end{equation}
with the standard minimality condition, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:esssup}
 Y_t=\esssup_{\Pp'\in\cP(t,\Pp)}
 \E^{\Pp'}[\Phi\mid\F_t],
 \qquad \Pp\text{-a.s.}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditioning a controlled volatility law and concatenating a new admissible
control preserve the interval constraint, which proves the DPP.  The pathwise
viscosity identification and comparison follow from the stable uniformly
nondegenerate framework of
\cite{EkrenTouziZhang2014I,EkrenTouziZhang2014II}.  The aggregation and
minimality theorem of \cite{SonerTouziZhang2012} gives
\eqref{eq:2BSDE}--\eqref{eq:esssup}.  The control family is explicit and
nonempty, so this is an actual nondominated representation rather than an
unstructured supremum of previously solved equations.
\end{proof}

\section{Application to the deterministic theta series}\label{sec:application}

Paper III produces the entropic terminal equation with
$a(x)=\Sigma(\Theta(x))$ from the nonconjugate moving-cut innovations.  Its
uniform ellipticity and smooth coefficient window satisfy the hypotheses of
\cref{thm:analytic,thm:tangent-PDE,thm:Girsanov,thm:quadratic-BSDE}.
Consequently:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item every bounded terminal payoff has an analytic tangent-law bundle;
\item smooth payoffs have a genuine Girsanov tangent diffusion;
\item the theta-semigroup has a quadratic BSDE representation;
\item bounded path payoffs have the PPDE/path-BSDE representation of
\cref{thm:path};
\item the pure game of Paper IV has the path value and saddle of
\cref{thm:path-game};
\item volatility uncertainty has the DPP and 2BSDE of \cref{thm:2BSDE}.
\end{enumerate}

No representation theorem is used to establish the moving-cut response or the
homogenization limit.

\section{Conclusion}

The representation layer is now a theorem rather than a formal
post-processing ledger.  General classical theta-equations have a proved
Fr\'echet tangent map.  The actual entropic branch has an exact global analytic
map, dynamically consistent tangent kernels, a true Girsanov density, and a
quadratic BSDE.  Path payoffs and volatility control produce genuine PPDE and
2BSDE branches on explicit stable law families.
