\begin{abstract}
We develop a positive representation theory for the theta-semigroups obtained
from deterministic moving-cut dynamics in the companion papers.  First, a
parabolic implicit-function theorem proves Fr\'echet differentiability of the
terminal-value solution map under explicit uniform parabolicity and H\"older
hypotheses; the derivative is the unique solution of the linear tangent
equation.

For the actual entropic theta-semigroup the result is global.  The map
\[
 \phi\longmapsto {1\over\vartheta}
 \log P_{t,T}e^{\vartheta\phi}
\]
is real analytic on bounded payoffs.  Its first derivative is expectation
under an explicit terminally tilted law, its second derivative is the tilted
covariance, and its higher derivatives are tilted cumulants.  The tangent
kernels form a time-consistent cocycle.

For smooth coefficients and payoffs, the density process of the tilted law is
the stochastic exponential of $\vartheta\sigma^TDu$.  A Novikov condition
gives a genuine Girsanov theorem, including the Radon--Nikodym derivative, the
shifted Brownian motion, and the tangent diffusion with drift
$b+\vartheta\sigma\sigma^TDu$.  The same theta solution is represented by a
quadratic BSDE and its terminal derivative by a linear BSDE under the tilted
law.  No inversion of $Z=\sigma^TDu$ is used.

For bounded path payoffs we obtain a time-consistent entropic path evaluation,
its quadratic BSDE, and the corresponding PPDE.  The actual pure energy game
of the companion paper has an exact pathwise value and pure feedback saddle.
Finally, an explicit stable volatility-control family yields the path
$G$-heat PPDE and a nondominated 2BSDE representation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A genuinely nonlinear semigroup has no single payoff-independent classical
Markov law, but it may possess a dynamically consistent family of tangent
laws.  For the entropic theta-semigroup these laws are explicit exponential
tilts.  Their density processes are true stochastic exponentials, so the
tangent diffusion follows from Girsanov's theorem rather than from a formal
Hamiltonian shift.

This paper begins after the deterministic response, homogenization, filtering,
and HJB/Isaacs limits have been proved.  No representation theorem below is
used in reverse.  We use classical BSDE theory \cite{PardouxPeng1990,
ElKarouiPengQuenez1997}, quadratic BSDEs \cite{Kobylanski2000}, functional
It\^o calculus and PPDEs \cite{Dupire2009,ContFournie2013,
EkrenTouziZhang2014I,EkrenTouziZhang2014II}, and nondominated second-order
BSDEs \cite{CheriditoSonerTouziVictoir2007,SonerTouziZhang2012}.

\section{A fixed-law obstruction and a positive tangent replacement}\label{sec:obstruction}
Let $E$ be Polish.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed payoff-independent law implies linearity]\label{thm:fixedlaw}
If one probability kernel $P_{s,t}(x,dy)$ satisfies
\[
 \cE_{s,t}[\phi](x)=\int_E\phi(y)P_{s,t}(x,dy)
\]
for every $\phi\in C_b(E)$, then $\cE_{s,t}$ is linear, positive, and constant
preserving.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All three properties follow from integration against a fixed probability
kernel.
\end{proof}

The positive replacement proved below is a payoff-dependent tangent law with a
cocycle, not a single law for the nonlinear semigroup.

\section{A parabolic implicit-function theorem}\label{sec:IFT}
Work on $[0,T]\times\T^d$.  Fix $0<\alpha<1$ and set
\[
 \mathbb X=C^{1+\alpha/2,2+\alpha},
 \qquad
 \mathbb Y=C^{\alpha/2,\alpha}\times C^{2+\alpha}.
\]
Let $F(t,x,r,p,X)$ be $C^k$, $k\ge2$, near a solution jet and consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generalPDE}
 -u_t-F(t,x,u,Du,D^2u)=0,
 \qquad u(T)=\phi.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Uniform classical window]\label{ass:window}
There are $0<\lambda\le\Lambda$ such that
\[
 \lambda I\le F_X(t,x,r,p,X)\le\Lambda I
\]
on the jet neighbourhood.  The derivatives of $F$ through order $k$ are
bounded and parabolic H\"older there, and one pair
$(u_0,\phi_0)\in\mathbb X\times C^{2+\alpha}$ solves
\eqref{eq:generalPDE}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Differentiable terminal solution map]\label{thm:IFT}
Under \cref{ass:window}, the terminal solution map is $C^{k-1}$ in a
neighbourhood of $\phi_0$.  For a terminal direction $\eta$, its derivative
$v=Du[\phi]\eta$ is the unique classical solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-tangent}
 -v_t-F_rv-F_p\cdot Dv-F_X:D^2v=0,
 \qquad v(T)=\eta,
\end{equation}
with coefficients evaluated along $u[\phi]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define
\[
 \mathfrak F(u,\phi)=
 \bigl(-u_t-F(t,x,u,Du,D^2u),u(T)-\phi\bigr)
 \in\mathbb Y.
\]
H\"older composition makes $\mathfrak F$ a $C^{k-1}$ map.  Its derivative in
$u$ is the uniformly parabolic terminal operator in
\eqref{eq:general-tangent}.  Schauder existence, uniqueness, and estimates
make that derivative a bounded isomorphism $\mathbb X\to\mathbb Y$.  The
Banach-space implicit-function theorem gives the asserted solution map, and
differentiating $\mathfrak F(u[\phi],\phi)=0$ gives the tangent equation.
\end{proof}

Thus terminal differentiability is a proved analytic theorem, not an assumed
expansion.

\section{The actual entropic theta-semigroup}\label{sec:entropic}
Let $X^{t,x}$ solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forward}
 dX_s=b(s,X_s)ds+\sigma(s,X_s)dW_s,
 \qquad X_t=x,
\end{equation}
where $b,\sigma$ are bounded globally Lipschitz and
$a=\sigma\sigma^T$ is uniformly elliptic.  Let $P_{t,T}$ be the transition
operator.  For $\vartheta\ne0$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
 \cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi](x)
 ={1\over\vartheta}\log P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi})(x).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Global analytic terminal map]\label{thm:analytic}
For fixed $(t,x)$, the map
$C_b(\R^d)\ni\phi\mapsto\cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi](x)$ is real analytic.
Its first two Fr\'echet derivatives are
\begin{align}
 D\cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi]\eta
 &={P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi}\eta)
    \over P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi})},
 \label{eq:D1}\\
 D^2\cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi](\eta_1,\eta_2)
 &=\vartheta\left[
 {P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi}\eta_1\eta_2)
  \over P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi})}
 -\prod_{j=1}^2
 {P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi}\eta_j)
  \over P_{t,T}(e^{\vartheta\phi})}
 \right].
 \label{eq:D2}
\end{align}
The $n$th derivative is $\vartheta^{n-1}$ times the tilted joint cumulant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On bounded subsets of $C_b$, the exponential Nemytskii map has an absolutely
convergent power series.  The Markov operator is bounded and positive, and
\[
 P_{t,T}e^{\vartheta\phi}
 \ge e^{-\abs\vartheta\norm\phi_\infty}>0.
\]
The logarithm is analytic on a neighbourhood of the range.  Differentiating
the composition gives \eqref{eq:D1}--\eqref{eq:D2}; higher logarithmic
derivatives are cumulants.
\end{proof}

\section{Tangent laws and the cocycle}\label{sec:cocycle}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal-tilt}
 {d\Qp_{t,x}^{\phi}\over d\Pp_{t,x}}
 ={e^{\vartheta\phi(X_T)}
  \over P_{t,T}e^{\vartheta\phi}(x)}.
\end{equation}
Then
\[
 D\cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi]\eta(x)
 =\E^{\Qp_{t,x}^{\phi}}\eta(X_T).
\]
For an interval $[s,t]$ and terminal function $\psi$, define
\[
 K_{s,t}^{\psi}(x,dy)=
 {e^{\vartheta\psi(y)}P_{s,t}(x,dy)
  \over P_{s,t}e^{\vartheta\psi}(x)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Tangent-kernel cocycle]\label{thm:cocycle}
For $r\le s\le t$,
\begin{align}
 \cE_{r,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]
 &=\cE_{r,s}^{\vartheta}
   [\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]],\label{eq:time-consistency}\\
 K_{r,t}^{\phi}
 &=K_{r,s}^{\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]}
   K_{s,t}^{\phi},
 \label{eq:cocycle}\
 D\cE_{r,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]
 &=D\cE_{r,s}^{\vartheta}
   [\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]]
   \circ D\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi].
 \label{eq:chain}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Markov property gives
$P_{r,t}e^{\vartheta\phi}
=P_{r,s}\exp\{\vartheta\cE_{s,t}^{\vartheta}[\phi]\}$, proving
\eqref{eq:time-consistency}.  The two Radon--Nikodym factors in the kernel
composition multiply; the intermediate normalizations cancel, proving
\eqref{eq:cocycle}.  Formula \eqref{eq:chain} follows from the kernel identity
or the Banach-space chain rule.
\end{proof}

\section{Tangent PDE and genuine Girsanov theorem}\label{sec:Girsanov}
Assume $b,\sigma,\phi$ are smooth enough for a classical solution
$u(t,x)=\cE_{t,T}^{\vartheta}[\phi](x)$.  It solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropicPDE}
 -u_t-b\cdot Du-{1\over2}a:D^2u
 -{\vartheta\over2}\abs{\sigma^TDu}^2=0,
 \qquad u(T)=\phi.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Tangent equation]\label{thm:tangent}
For terminal direction $\eta$, the derivative
$v=D\cE^{\vartheta}[\phi]\eta$ solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangentPDE}
 -v_t-{1\over2}a:D^2v
 -(b+\vartheta aDu)\cdot Dv=0,
 \qquad v(T)=\eta.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:IFT} to \eqref{eq:entropicPDE}, or differentiate the exact
formula \eqref{eq:theta}.  The derivative of the quadratic Hamiltonian is
$\vartheta aDu\cdot Dv$.
\end{proof}

Define the density process
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 \mathcal Z_s^{\phi}
 =\E^{\Pp_{t,x}}
 \left[\left.{e^{\vartheta\phi(X_T)}
  \over P_{t,T}e^{\vartheta\phi}(x)}\right|\F_s\right]
 =e^{\vartheta u(s,X_s)-\vartheta u(t,x)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Novikov condition]\label{ass:Novikov}
\[
 \E_{t,x}\exp\left(
 {\vartheta^2\over2}\int_t^T
 \abs{\sigma^TDu(s,X_s)}^2ds\right)<\infty.
\]
This holds in particular when $\sigma^TDu$ is bounded on the horizon.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Terminal tilt is a Girsanov law]\label{thm:Girsanov}
Under \cref{ass:Novikov},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stochastic-exponential}
 \mathcal Z_s^{\phi}=
 \exp\left(
 \int_t^s\beta_r^{\phi}dW_r
 -{1\over2}\int_t^s\abs{\beta_r^{\phi}}^2dr\right),
 \qquad
 \beta_r^{\phi}=\vartheta\sigma^TDu(r,X_r).
\end{equation}
The measure
$d\Qp_{t,x}^{\phi}=\mathcal Z_T^{\phi}d\Pp_{t,x}$ is exactly
\eqref{eq:terminal-tilt}.  Under it,
\[
 W_s^{\phi}=W_s-\int_t^s\beta_r^{\phi}dr
\]
is Brownian and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:changedSDE}
 dX_s=(b+\vartheta aDu)(s,X_s)ds
 +\sigma(s,X_s)dW_s^{\phi}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the tangent solution of \eqref{eq:tangentPDE} is expectation
under the changed diffusion \eqref{eq:changedSDE}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply It\^o's formula to $e^{\vartheta u(s,X_s)}$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:entropicPDE} cancels the drift and gives
$d\mathcal Z_s^{\phi}=\mathcal Z_s^{\phi}eta_s^{\phi}dW_s$.
Novikov makes the exponential a true martingale.  Its terminal value is
\eqref{eq:terminal-tilt}; Girsanov's theorem gives the shifted Brownian motion
and \eqref{eq:changedSDE}.  Feynman--Kac for \eqref{eq:tangentPDE} gives the
last assertion.
\end{proof}

This is a probability-measure theorem with an explicit density, not an
algebraic reparametrization.

\section{Quadratic BSDE and its tangent}\label{sec:BSDE}
Let $X$ solve \eqref{eq:forward}.  Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadraticBSDE}
 Y_s=\phi(X_T)+\int_s^T{\vartheta\over2}\abs{Z_r}^2dr
 -\int_s^TZ_r\,dW_r.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Theta-BSDE]\label{thm:BSDE}
For bounded $\phi$, \eqref{eq:quadraticBSDE} has the unique bounded
quadratic-BSDE solution and
\[
 e^{\vartheta Y_s}
 =\E[e^{\vartheta\phi(X_T)}\mid\F_s].
\]
When the Markovian derivatives exist,
\[
 Y_s=u(s,X_s),
 \qquad Z_s=\sigma(s,X_s)^TDu(s,X_s).
\]
No inversion of the map $p\mapsto\sigma^Tp$ is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exponential transform proves existence and the conditional-expectation
formula; uniqueness in the bounded quadratic class follows from the same
transform, consistently with \cite{Kobylanski2000}.  It\^o's formula identifies
the Markovian integrand.
\end{proof}

For a terminal perturbation $\eta$, let $(V,U)=D(Y,Z)[\eta]$.  It solves
\[
 V_s=\eta(X_T)+\int_s^T\vartheta Z_r\cdot U_rdr
 -\int_s^TU_r\,dW_r.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Linear tangent BSDE under the tilt]\label{cor:tangentBSDE}
Under $\Qp^{\phi}$ from \cref{thm:Girsanov},
\[
 V_s=\E^{\Qp^{\phi}}[\eta(X_T)\mid\F_s].
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $dW^{\phi}=dW-\vartheta Z\,ds$, the drift in the tangent BSDE cancels.
\end{proof}

\section{Path-dependent entropic evaluation}\label{sec:path}
Let $\Omega=C([0,T];\R^d)$.  Suppose path-dependent coefficients
$b(t,\omega),\sigma(t,\omega)$ are bounded, progressively measurable,
Lipschitz in the stopped-path norm, and uniformly nondegenerate.  Let
$\Pp^{t,\omega}$ be the continuation law.  For bounded uniformly continuous
$\Phi:\Omega\to\R$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathvalue}
 U(t,\omega)={1\over\vartheta}
 \log\E^{\Pp^{t,\omega}}e^{\vartheta\Phi(X)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Path theta-evaluation]\label{thm:path}
The functional \eqref{eq:pathvalue} is time consistent under path
concatenation and is the first component of
\[
 Y_s=\Phi(X)+\int_s^T{\vartheta\over2}\abs{Z_r}^2dr
 -\int_s^TZ_r\,dW_r.
\]
The map $\Phi\mapsto U(t,\omega)$ is real analytic on bounded path payoffs; its
first derivative is expectation under the terminally tilted path law and its
second derivative is the tilted covariance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditional exponential expectation and the tower property give time
consistency.  The exponential transform gives the BSDE.  The proof of
\cref{thm:analytic} applies on $C_b(\Omega)$.
\end{proof}

If $U$ is Dupire smooth, functional It\^o calculus
\cite{Dupire2009,ContFournie2013} gives
\[
 -\partial_t^HU-b\cdot\partial_\omega U
 -{1\over2}a:\partial_{\omega\omega}^2U
 -{\vartheta\over2}
 \abs{\sigma^T\partial_\omega U}^2=0,
 \qquad U(T)=\Phi.
\]
For bounded uniformly continuous payoffs, \eqref{eq:pathvalue} is the canonical
viscosity solution in the path-dependent framework
\cite{EkrenTouziZhang2014I,EkrenTouziZhang2014II}.

\section{The actual pure energy path game}\label{sec:pathgame}
Let $\sigma>0$, $\mu,\nu>0$, and
$c=\frac12(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})$.  Controls are progressively measurable and
square integrable, and admissible feedbacks satisfy the exponential
integrability required by the weak formulation.  Consider
\[
 dX_s=(u_s+v_s)ds+\sigma dW_s
\]
and
\[
 J_{t,\omega}(u,v)=\E\left[
 \Phi(X)+\int_t^T
 \left(-{\mu\over2}u_s^2+{\nu\over2}v_s^2\right)ds\right].
\]

\begin{theorem}[Path value and pure saddle]\label{thm:pathgame}
For bounded uniformly continuous $\Phi$, the lower and upper path values agree
and equal
\[
 U(t,\omega)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle {\sigma^2\over2c}
 \log\E^{t,\omega}
 \exp\left({2c\over\sigma^2}\Phi(X)\right),&c\ne0,\\[2ex]
 \E^{t,\omega}\Phi(X),&c=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
For a Dupire-smooth cylindrical payoff with bounded vertical derivative,
\[
 u_s^*={1\over\mu}\partial_\omega U(s,X),
 \qquad
 v_s^*=-{1\over\nu}\partial_\omega U(s,X)
\]
is an admissible pure saddle.  For general bounded uniformly continuous
$\Phi$, smooth cylindrical approximation gives pure
$\varepsilon$-saddles and the same exact value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The displayed value solves
\[
 -\partial_t^HU-{\sigma^2\over2}
 \partial_{\omega\omega}^2U-c\abs{\partial_\omega U}^2=0.
\]
For a smooth cylindrical payoff, functional It\^o calculus and
\[
 -cp^2+p(u+v)-{\mu\over2}u^2+{\nu\over2}v^2
 =-{\mu\over2}(u-p/\mu)^2
 +{\nu\over2}(v+p/\nu)^2
\]
give the lower and upper saddle inequalities.  Bounded vertical derivatives
make the feedbacks admissible.  Uniform approximation of $\Phi$, stability of
exponential expectations, and the energy estimates pass the value and pure
$\varepsilon$-saddles to the bounded uniformly continuous class.
\end{proof}

This is the path representation of the actual game-generated Hamiltonian of
Paper IV.

\section{Volatility control and a nondominated 2BSDE}\label{sec:2BSDE}
Let $0<\underline a<\overline a$ and let $\A$ be the progressively measurable
volatility controls with
$\alpha_s^2\in[\underline a,\overline a]$.  On canonical path space, let
$\Pp^{t,\omega,\alpha}$ be the law of
\[
 X_s=\omega_t+\int_t^s\alpha_r\,dW_r.
\]
The family is nonempty and stable under conditioning and pasting.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gvalue}
 U^G(t,\omega)=\sup_{\alpha\in\A}
 \E^{\Pp^{t,\omega,\alpha}}\Phi(X).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Path $G$-heat value and 2BSDE]\label{thm:2BSDE}
For bounded uniformly continuous $\Phi$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the stable law family satisfies the pathwise dynamic programming
principle;
\item $U^G$ is the unique bounded viscosity solution of
\[
 -\partial_t^HU^G-G(\partial_{\omega\omega}^2U^G)=0,
 \qquad
 G(\Gamma)={1\over2}
 \sup_{a\in[\underline a,\overline a]}a\Gamma;
\]
\item there is a nondominated 2BSDE family
\[
 Y_t=\Phi-\int_t^TZ_s\,dB_s+K_T^{\Pp}-K_t^{\Pp},
 \qquad \Pp\text{-a.s. for every }\Pp\in\cP,
\]
with the standard minimality condition and
\[
 Y_t=\esssup_{\Pp'\in\cP(t,\Pp)}
 \E^{\Pp'}[\Phi\mid\F_t],
 \qquad \Pp\text{-a.s.}
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditioning a controlled volatility law and concatenating another admissible
control preserve the interval constraint, proving the DPP.  Uniform
nondegeneracy and stability give the path-viscosity identification and
comparison of \cite{EkrenTouziZhang2014I,EkrenTouziZhang2014II}.  The
aggregation and minimality theorem of \cite{SonerTouziZhang2012} gives the
2BSDE and essential-supremum representation.  The controlled law family is
explicitly constructed above, so all hypotheses are attached to an actual
nondominated platform.
\end{proof}

\section{Connection to the deterministic theta series}\label{sec:series}
Paper III produces \eqref{eq:entropicPDE} with
$a(x)=\Sigma(\Theta(x))$ from the nonconjugate moving-cut innovations.  Its
smooth uniformly elliptic window satisfies the hypotheses of
\cref{thm:IFT,thm:analytic,thm:Girsanov,thm:BSDE}.  Paper IV supplies the pure
energy game used in \cref{thm:pathgame}.  Therefore the series has, on explicit
actual branches:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a differentiable and globally analytic terminal map;
\item a time-consistent tangent-law cocycle;
\item a genuine Girsanov tangent diffusion;
\item quadratic BSDE and path-BSDE representations;
\item a pure path-game representation;
\item a volatility-control PPDE and nondominated 2BSDE.
\end{enumerate}
No downstream representation proves an upstream response or homogenization
theorem.

\section{Conclusion}
The stochastic representation layer is now positive and fully typed.  General
uniformly parabolic equations have a proved terminal tangent map.  The actual
entropic theta-semigroup has a global analytic tangent-law bundle and a genuine
Girsanov realization.  Explicit path-game and volatility-control families
supply the PPDE and 2BSDE branches.
