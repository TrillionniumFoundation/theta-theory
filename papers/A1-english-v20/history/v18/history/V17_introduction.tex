\section{Introduction}
An exact information state identifies histories that have the same
conditional laws for every future experiment. Its dimension does not,
however, determine the memory needed for prediction at a finite
resolution. A direction can cease to be identifiable on a singular
parameter stratum and remain arbitrarily weak near that stratum. Even
away from a singularity, a short past may excite only a proper part of
the space of future tests. We study the prediction geometry attained
by an actual past, including the probability with which its directions
are acquired.

For positive sparse monomial experiments we identify this geometry
uniformly through additive exponent collisions. The sharp finite-label
law is the full exterior-volume profile of the future spectrum,
truncated by the dimension of the actual product tangent after
normalization. Establishing this identification requires three matching
statements: a lower measure on attainable prediction directions, a
global cover with the same dimension-truncated scales, and a causal
realization that does not divide by a vanishing collision gap. In
particular, a spectral estimate for the future tests is not being
interpreted as a theorem about histories without an attainment argument.

The same distinction resolves a different degeneration for a circular
finite detector. There the acquired Fourier coefficients already shrink
with contrast, before the future experiment attenuates them again.
The resulting law identifies the joint acquisition--observation scales,
not merely the number of coefficients needed for an exact posterior
representation. In both models the comparison is two-sided, includes
unconditional lower bounds, and applies to a single causal memory at a
fixed horizon.

\subsection{The experiment and its resolution profile}
Let
\[
 A=\{0=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_{r-1}=D\},\qquad
 W_A=\operatorname{span}\{t^a:a\in A\},
\]
and let $mA$ denote sums of exactly $m$ members of $A$, with $0A=\{0\}$.
Write $h_A(m)=|mA|$. For the exact information statements let
$I=[l,u]$, where $0\le l<u<\infty$, and let the prior have full support
on $I$. The collision-uniform results below specialize to $I=[0,1]$.
A positive finite detector spans $W_A$. A command in
$[\eta,1-\eta]^J$, $0<\eta<1/2$, retains or rejects each detector
symbol; rejection is itself a report. All trials, including rejections,
are counted. Every report likelihood is at least a fixed $\kappa>0$.
The hidden parameter is shared by all trials. A continuation may use
new reports and commands but no discarded prefix or prefix-dependent
random seed. Section~\ref{sec:experiment} gives the precise model.

For the uniform results, the parameter interval is $[0,1]$, the horizon
$N\ge2$ is fixed, and the one-step exponents vary over a compact subset
$K$ of the strictly ordered positive chamber. A fixed full-column-rank
coefficient matrix specifies the positive detector on $K$. The prior
$\mu$ has full support, but need not have a density. Neither a
semialgebraic calibration set nor a lower bound on a nonzero additive
gap is assumed. Choose a fixed
$H\ge\max\{1,N\max_K a_{r-1}\}$. At future length $m$ retain all
nonconstant formal labels $\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^r$, $|\alpha|=m$,
even when their exponents coincide. Their number and normalized nodes are
\[
 q_m=\binom{m+r-1}{r-1}-1,\qquad x_\alpha=H^{-1}\alpha\cdot a.
\]
For $1\le\ell\le q_m$ put
\[
 \mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a)=\max_{|F|=\ell}
       \prod_{\{\alpha,\beta\}\subset F}|x_\alpha-x_\beta|,
 \qquad \mathcal V_{m,1}=1.
\]
A zero volume stays zero. Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 p_{n,m}&=\min\{n(r-1),q_m\},\\
 \Psi_{n,m}(M,a)&=\max_{1\le\ell\le p_{n,m}}
              (\mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a)/M)^{2/\ell},\\
 \Xi_N(M,a)&=\max_{1\le n<N}\Psi_{n,N-n}(M,a).
 \end{split}
\]
Endpoint profiles are zero. The checkpoint loss is the average squared
error in the conditional probabilities of a fixed finite spanning menu
of actual $m$-trial product queries. The query is selected independently
\emph{after} the memory index has been formed, and only the selected
query is executed. The unconditional criterion uses independent uniform
acquisition commands; the worst-history criterion takes a supremum over
admitted histories. A causal criterion minimizes the largest checkpoint
regret over one stage-compatible filter. The formal definitions appear
in Definition~\ref{def:finite-state}.

Theorem~\ref{thm:resolution-main} is the principal result. Its exact-real
counterpart, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, has dimension
\[
 d_A(n,m)=\min\{n(r-1),|mA|-1\}.
\]
The finite-resolution law contains strictly more information: it records
how a direction disappears, not just whether it is present. The exact
rank formula is proved in Section~\ref{sec:rank}; the uniform metric

Section~\ref{sec:collision-geometry}. When $r=1$ all likelihoods are

\subsection{Reconstructing the geometry from prediction}
The classification also admits an inverse formulation. For a checkpoint
curve $R(M)$, define
\[
 \mathcal I_\ell(R)=\inf_{M\in\N,\,M\ge1}M R(M)^{\ell/2}.
\]
Corollary~\ref{cor:monomial-operational-recovery} proves that
$\mathcal I_\ell(R)$ is uniformly comparable to
$\mathcal V_{m,\ell}$ through the attainable truncation and is zero
beyond it. Hence the products are necessary, as well as sufficient,
for uniform comparison of the whole checkpoint prediction laws.
For the circular experiment, Corollary~\ref{cor:circular-operational-recovery}
recovers the paired scales $\tau^{2j}$ from the same transform, including
the odd products that introduce no separate branch in the displayed
maximum. Dimension is recovered from the eventual logarithmic slope;
the transform recovers the additional finite-resolution geometry.

The elementary input is an envelope identity for ordered initial
products, with a factor of at most two for integer budgets. Its proof
and the comparison theorem are given in
Section~\ref{sec:operational-reconstruction}. This converse does not
supply the geometric hypotheses of the forward classification. Those
hypotheses are established by the actual-history, global-image, and
causal arguments described below. Nor does it infer a hidden parameter
or an experiment from finitely many measurements of a filter's error.

\subsection{Why the future spectrum is not enough}
The determinant volumes are classical spectral quantities, comparable to
products of singular values of a Vandermonde matrix. This comparison
alone neither puts a point in the posterior image nor assigns it
positive acquisition probability. Three additional assertions are needed.

First, actual interior failure factors have a product tangent supported
on $n(r-1)+1$ separated exponents. This separation survives collisions
among the future sums. Pairing this tangent with a complete Newton flag
of future tests gives full rank before normalization, including every
Hermite limit. Normalization removes exactly the constant direction.
The quantitative inverse theorem then produces a cube in every truncated
flag, together with an unconditional subprobability minorization under
one fixed exploration law. This is Lemma~\ref{lem:newton-attainment}.

Second, a local attained cube is not a global covering theorem. The
entire posterior image is rational in the command coordinates, with
positive evidence denominators and coefficient-independent bounded
semialgebraic format. Its dimension is at most $n(r-1)$ even when the
future test space is much larger. The real thin-rectangle covering
estimate in Lemma~\ref{lem:tame-rectangle} yields the same truncated
products as the local lower bound. Arbitrary prior integrals occur only
as real coefficients; no definability of the prior is required.

Third, checkpoint codebooks have to be compatible with causality.
Updating an actual representative produces another attainable state.
In raw moments, positive evidence gives a Lipschitz recurrence whose
constants do not involve inverse collision gaps. Quantizing that update
therefore realizes the maximum checkpoint profile with one finite-state
filter. The lower bounds use prefixes of the same exploration law and
hence apply to that same filter. These steps explain why an ambient
rank, a spectral estimate, or a collection of independently chosen
checkpoint codes would not settle the theorem.

\subsection{The attainment principle and a second application}
Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-attainment} isolates a sufficient condition for
attainment for every full-support prior. At an interior positive history,
its actual product tangent must contain a strict Chebyshev system of
order $p+1$, and the constant together with the $p$ selected future tests
must form another such system. Determinant integration makes their
pairing nonsingular. Proposition~\ref{prop:product-criterion} then removes
exactly the evidence direction and converts the submersion into a
subprobability minorization. Neither a likelihood span nor a future
spectrum alone meets these hypotheses. The statement is uniform over
compact continuous families and dominated prior envelopes.

There is also an application outside the ordered real-monomial setting.
On the circle with Haar prior, consider the positive cells
\[
 \tfrac14(1\pm\tau\cos\theta),\qquad
 \tfrac14(1\pm\tau\sin\theta).
\]
The command has four real lookup probabilities varying continuously
in $[\eta,1-\eta]^4$, and all failures are charged. Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-resolution} proves, for fixed $N$
and all sufficiently small known $\tau\ge0$, the checkpoint profile
\[
 H_{n,m}(M,\tau)=
 \max_{1\le j\le\min(n,m)}\tau^{2(j+1)}M^{-1/j}.
\]
With the contrast known to a read-only program, one causal $M$-label
filter attains the maximum over checkpoints. Its constants and
contrast interval are chosen for this fixed $N$, not uniformly in
unbounded horizon. The
exact dimension for positive contrast is $2\min(n,m)$, yet the $j$th
pair of physical directions has size $\tau^{2j}$. The acquired Fourier
coefficient has size $\tau^j$, and the actual future query contributes
another $\tau^j$. This double attenuation occurs within one experiment,
with one known prior and one query convention; it is not a comparison
between two information classes. The normalized acquisition map extends
to zero contrast through elementary symmetric polynomials. Their local
invertibility supplies the positive-history minorization, while an exact
Fourier menu and a recurrence with no inverse contrast supply the upper
bound. Consecutive harmonic phases meet at
$M=\tau^{-2j(j+1)}$.

The analytic comparisons can now be stated at theorem level.
Clustered Vandermonde estimates \cite{BatenkovEtAl}
concern singular values of evaluation matrices. They provide neither
admissible command histories nor a lower measure on a posterior image.
Lemmas 2.17--2.18 of \cite{ZhangKileel} give coefficient-independent
regularity of rational images and bounded-format semialgebraic sets.
The real variation inequality recalled in equations (4)--(5) of
\cite{ComteHalupczok} turns this regularity and thin side lengths into
an upper covering bound. Neither assertion is a positive-history lower
bound. Our additional obligations are the product-tangent attainment,
the match between its truncated scales and the global image, and one
causal realization. In the circular application, the query phase grid
is fixed and equally spaced; it is not a degenerating Fourier node set.
Thus recent nonuniform Fourier matrix bounds, including Theorem 1.2 of
\cite{ChenLinZhang}, address a different degeneration. We use discrete
orthogonality there, not an unproved transfer of clustered-node estimates.

\subsection{Unresolved prior information}
A further geometry distinguishes prior ambiguity from the past's
attainable image. Let $d_1\ge\cdots\ge d_{q_m}\ge0$ be the Leja
scales of the future nodes. Their initial products are comparable to
$\mathcal V_{m,\ell}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:ambiguity-ellipsoid}
shows that, after any fixed positive history, priors in a relative
$L^\infty$-neighborhood of radius $\varepsilon$ produce an ambiguity
body with all successive linear width orders
\[
 \varepsilon d_1,\ldots,\varepsilon d_{q_m},
\]
up to fixed comparison constants. Here the full future flag occurs;
there is no truncation at $n(r-1)$. The general bounded-dual principle of
Proposition~\ref{prop:general-bounded-dual}, applied to the complete
confluent test system, proves both inclusions in the asserted body.
This is a consequence of bounded finite-dimensional duality, not a
second physical-attainment mechanism.
The normalization of the perturbed prior is enforced explicitly.

With known calibration and a fixed history, after an exact prefix of
$k$ distinct raw future moments is supplied,
the squared local minimax radius is
$\asymp\varepsilon^2d_{k+1}^2$. For
$A_\theta=\{0,1,2+\theta\}$ at horizon three, the acquired-memory law
is $\Xi_3\asymp M^{-1}$ throughout the collision, whereas the
uncertainty remaining after four two-trial moments has squared radius
$\asymp\varepsilon^2\theta^2$. Thus a collision can be invisible to
the memory exponent at one horizon while remaining visible to unresolved
prior information. Section~\ref{sec:directional-geometry} states the
exact local-data convention and also gives a positive-probability
physical decision formulation. It does not identify these exact
conditional constraints with a noisy pre-acquisition moment oracle.

The principal common-description consequence is
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-common-moments}. For a finite absolute-error
moment name $y$ of accuracy $\delta$, one program must serve every
compatible calibration and prior in a fixed dominated family. At
interior names with fixed error slack, its sharp regret order is
\[
 \Xi_N(M,a)+\delta^2.
\]
The reference center describes the order; it is not supplied to the
program. The uncertainty converse uses genuine prior alternatives and
overlapping history laws. Since this class includes uncertainty in a
uniformly visible direction, the scalar $\delta^2$ floor does not
resolve the weaker directions just described. The result is joint
prior-and-calibration uncertainty, not identification of calibration
from moments with an exactly known prior.

\subsection{Finite realization and the organization of the paper}
The finite implementation statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:effective-main}.
Under its explicit numerical interface, integer transition tables and
dyadic outputs attain the profile with $M$ persistent labels. A
farthest-first covering certificate selects the numerical scale without
an equality test for exponents or a supplied value of $\Xi_N$.
Persistent state, numerical advice and read-only program length are
separate quantities: sharpness is asserted for the first, while the
bounds on the latter two are sufficient bounds. The intrinsic result
holds without effectiveness assumptions on the prior.

The exposition follows this hierarchy: the attainable geometric
classification, the common-name minimax consequence, and the finite
implementation under its stated interface. The main argument develops
the experiment, the standalone positive-history criterion, the normalized product
pairing, the collision-uniform classification, the circular contrast
classification, all local ambiguity-width orders and the common-name law.
The complete numerical construction, precision selection, stability
estimates, workspace bounds and request checks appear in
Appendices~\ref{sec:effective}--\ref{sec:request-conformance}.
The observation algebra, affine specializations, five- and seven-trial
calculations, decision theory and mechanical realization are also
retained with their full proofs in the appendices.

The analytic inputs are classical total positivity, confluent divided
differences and bounded-format real metric entropy
\cite{Pinkus,deBoor,YomdinComte,ZhangKileel,ComteHalupczok}.
Their roles are isolated in the proofs rather than counted as separate
novelty claims. A detailed comparison with finite control approximations,
approximate information states and finite-window methods is retained in
Appendix~\ref{sec:literature-comparison}. The contribution assessed here
is positive-history attainment and the two verified resolution
classifications with their stated consequences; no priority claim about finite-state approximation in
general is needed.
