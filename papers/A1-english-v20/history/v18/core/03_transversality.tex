\section{Attainable transversality and the exact memory profile}\label{sec:rank}
We first prove the positivity statement that prevents a prior-dependent
loss of rank. No density assumption on the prior is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Strict mixed-moment positivity]\label{lem:mixed-moment}
Let $\alpha_1<\cdots<\alpha_s$ and $\beta_1<\cdots<\beta_s$ be
nonnegative real numbers. For every full-support probability on $I$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-minor}
 \det\bigl[\mu(t^{\alpha_i+\beta_j})\bigr]_{i,j=1}^s>0.
\end{equation}
Consequently a rectangular moment matrix indexed by two increasing finite
sets of distinct nonnegative exponents has rank the smaller cardinality.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For increasing positive points $t_1<\cdots<t_s$, the generalized
Vandermonde determinant $\det[t_j^{\alpha_i}]$ is positive. Here is a
self-contained sign argument. Put $t_j=e^{x_j}$. A nonzero combination
of $s$ exponentials with distinct ordered exponents has at most $s-1$
distinct zeros: factor out the first exponential, differentiate, and use
Rolle's theorem inductively. Thus the determinant cannot vanish on the
connected region $x_1<\cdots<x_s$. When the $x_j$ coalesce to $x_0$
from ordered nearby points, its first nonzero Taylor term has the sign
of the Wronskian
\[
 e^{x_0\sum_i\alpha_i}\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_j-\alpha_i)
\]
times the ordinary positive Vandermonde in the $x_j$, divided by
positive factorials. Its sign is therefore positive everywhere in that
region. The same holds for the $\beta$ determinant.

Expanding determinants and applying Fubini gives the finite determinant
integration identity
\[
 \det[\mu(t^{\alpha_i+\beta_j})]
 =\frac1{s!}\int_{I^s}
       \det[t_j^{\alpha_i}]\det[t_j^{\beta_i}]
       \prod_{j=1}^s\mu(\dd t_j).
\]
The integrand is nonnegative, by permutation symmetry and continuity at
zero or repeated points. Choose $s$ disjoint ordered subintervals in
$(l,u)\cap(0,\infty)$. Each has positive prior mass by full support;
on their product the integrand is strictly positive. The integral is
strictly positive. Applying this to any square minor proves the last
assertion. This is the classical total-positivity mechanism
\cite{Pinkus}, with its needed form proved explicitly here.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A monomial tangent inside the attainable cube]\label{lem:binomial-tangent}
For every $n\ge1$, choose distinct, sufficiently small positive numbers
$c_1,\ldots,c_n$. The failure factors
$F_i=(1+c_it^D)/2$ are simultaneously attainable at interior commands.
At this tuple the differential image of the unnormalized product map
$W_A^n\to\spanop\{t^s:s\in nA\}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent}
 T_{A,n}=\spanop\{t^b:b\in B_{A,n}\},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-support}
 B_{A,n}=\{jD:0\le j\le n\}
 \cup\!\!\bigcup_{a\in A\setminus\{0,D\}}
          \{a+jD:0\le j<n\}.
\end{equation}
In particular $\dim T_{A,n}=n(r-1)+1$, and the product itself belongs to
this tangent space.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Attainability follows by applying the fixed right inverse in
Lemma~\ref{lem:interior} to $c_it^D/2$ and taking all $c_i$ small enough.
The factors and their implementing commands lie in an open subset of
the allowed cube. Constants $2^{-n}$ do not change a differential image,
so omit them in the remaining calculation. Put $z=t^D$,
$P(z)=\prod_i(1+c_iz)$ and $P_i(z)=P(z)/(1+c_iz)$. These $n$
polynomials form a basis of the polynomials in $z$ of degree at most
$n-1$. Indeed evaluation at $z=-1/c_j$ makes all $P_i$ except $P_j$
zero, and $P_j(-1/c_j)\ne0$.

The product differential is $\sum_i\dot F_i\prod_{j\ne i}F_j$.
Variations in the constant direction give all polynomials in $z$ through
degree $n-1$; variations in $t^D$ give their multiples by $z$.
Together they give all $z^j$, $0\le j\le n$. Variations in an interior
exponent $a$ give precisely $t^a z^j$, $0\le j<n$. The listed exponent
sets are disjoint: two numbers $a+jD$ and $a'+j'D$ with
$0<a,a'<D$ agree only if $a=a'$ and $j=j'$, and none is a multiple of
$D$. Their total cardinality is $(n+1)+n(r-2)=n(r-1)+1$.
Every summand of the differential has this form, proving equality rather
than only inclusion in \eqref{eq:tangent}. The product is obtained by
varying one factor along itself, so is in the tangent.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Generic normalized pairing rank]\label{thm:rank}
For fixed $n,m\ge1$, the map from $n$ all-failure commands to the moment
vector $(\mu(t^sP_h))_{s\in mA\setminus\{0\}}$ has generic rank
$d_A(n,m)$ in \eqref{eq:main-dimension}. This holds for every full-support
prior, including priors without a density. At an interior attainable tuple
it has a smooth $d_A(n,m)$-dimensional local image and a continuous local
section to histories. A projection onto $d_A(n,m)$ of the moment or
spanning-probe coordinates maps such a section onto an open Euclidean ball.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $P=\prod_iF_i$, $Z=\mu P>0$, and include the constant test in the
linear map
\[
 L:T_{A,n}\longrightarrow\R^{h_A(m)},\qquad
 LQ=(\mu(t^sQ))_{s\in mA}.
\]
At the tuple from Lemma~\ref{lem:binomial-tangent},
Lemma~\ref{lem:mixed-moment} gives
\[
 \rank L=\min\{n(r-1)+1,h_A(m)\}.
\]
Let $e_0$ extract the coordinate of exponent zero, and put $p=L(P)/Z$.
The derivative of the normalized prediction map on this tangent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-derivative}
 Q\longmapsto Z^{-1}(LQ-p\,e_0(LQ)).
\end{equation}
Now $e_0p=1$ and $p\in L(T_{A,n})$. The linear map
$v\mapsto v-p e_0v$, restricted to $L(T_{A,n})$, has kernel exactly
$\spanop\{p\}$ and image in the zero-constant-coordinate hyperplane.
It loses \emph{exactly} one rank. Deleting that identically zero coordinate
therefore gives rank $\min\{n(r-1),h_A(m)-1\}$.

At every tuple the normalized product map is invariant under independent
rescaling of its $n$ nonzero factors, so its rank is at most $n(r-1)$.
The number of nonconstant tests gives the other upper bound. Thus the
attained rank is globally maximal. The command-to-factor linear map is
surjective at every interior command; the same maximal rank is attained
in command coordinates. Its Jacobian entries are rational functions of
the commands with strictly positive polynomial denominator $Z^2$.
A maximal minor nonzero at the displayed tuple has a nonzero polynomial
numerator. Its zero set is a proper real algebraic set with empty interior
and zero Lebesgue measure. This proves genericity throughout the command
cube, not only along the binomial family.

Choose $d=d_A(n,m)$ independent output coordinates and $d$ command
coordinates at that tuple, and freeze the complementary command
coordinates. The inverse function theorem gives a local inverse for the
selected output projection; shrink to a ball with compact closure.
The full image of this section is a smooth local graph. A spanning list
of probes is an injective linear image of the moment coordinates, so the
same conclusion holds after choosing independent probe coordinates.
All section histories are feasible all-failure histories. Their probability
conditional on each parameter is at least $\eta^n$; no failure evidence
has been dropped.
\end{proof}

Notice that when $d<h_A(m)-1$ the attainable image can be curved.
The proof does not replace it by its affine hull. The lower-bound ball
is in a selected output projection, and its lift is a local graph, not
necessarily a flat ball in the ambient prediction space.

\begin{theorem}[Minimal continuous encoding and causal attainment]\label{thm:causal}
The minimal dimension stated in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is $d_A(n,m)$.
For a fixed $N$, there are compact exact state sets
$\cS_n\subset\R^{d_A(n,N-n)}$ and continuous-in-command causal updates
attaining this dimension at every $n$. Their state represents the
posterior-dependent future law, not an uncharged past-dependent task.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compose a sufficient continuous encoding with the section in
Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}. Different points in the projected ball have
different predictions, so this composition is injective. A continuous
injection of an open $d$-ball into $\R^k$, $k<d$, is impossible: embedding
$\R^k$ as a coordinate plane of $\R^d$ would contradict invariance of
domain. The decoder need not be continuous for this argument.

For the global upper bound, normalize each observed factor by its own
prior integral, $\widetilde f_i=f_i/\mu f_i$. Each normalized factor is
in the affine hyperplane $\mu f=1$ of $W_A$, with $r-1$ coordinates.
The chronologically ordered $n$ factors determine the posterior product
and use $n(r-1)$ real coordinates. This representation may distinguish
permutations; sufficiency does not forbid that. Alternatively, retain the
$h_A(m)-1$ nonconstant posterior moments in $mA$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:tests} proves this alternative sufficient on every
history. Use whichever representation is smaller. Both are continuous on
each fixed-report stratum. Finite unions of compact continuous images of
the command cube give compact state sets. The cases $n=0$ or $m=0$
have a single class and zero coordinates.

For causal implementation retain normalized factors while
$n(r-1)\le h_A(N-n)-1$. The left side increases and the right side
nonincreases, so there is at most one changeover. When the inequality first
fails, multiply the retained factors and compute the required moments.
Afterward, if $m=N-n$ and the new report factor is
$f(t)=\sum_{a\in A}f_at^a$, update by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-update}
 M'_s=\frac{\sum_{a\in A}f_aM_{s+a}}{\sum_{a\in A}f_aM_a},
 \qquad s\in(m-1)A\setminus\{0\},\qquad M_0=1.
\end{equation}
All indices $s+a$ belong to $mA$. The denominator is the report
probability and is at least $\kappa$. At the changeover the integrands
are products of $n$ factor monomials and a remaining-test monomial,
so their exponents are in $nA+(N-n)A=NA$. Normalizing each factor
requires only moments in $A\subset NA$. Thus a finite read-only table
$(\mu(t^s))_{s\in NA}$ suffices for all updates. Formula
\eqref{eq:sparse-update} then gives the exact shrinking state to time $N$.
\end{proof}

For arbitrary nonmonomial spaces $W$, the starting identity remains
\eqref{eq:normalized-derivative}, with the true product tangent and
$W^m$. What is special here is the explicit attainable tangent
\eqref{eq:tangent} and the strictly positive mixed pairing. No dimension
formula depending only on $\dim W$ is inferred outside this class.
