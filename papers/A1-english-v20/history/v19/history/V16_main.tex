\begin{abstract}
We determine the finite resolution of information acquired in positive
experiments. For sparse monomial likelihoods and any fixed full-support
prior, the sharp prediction error with $M$ persistent labels is the
exterior-volume profile of the future exponents, truncated by the
dimension attainable from the past. The comparison is uniform across
arbitrary additive collisions in a compact one-step exponent chamber.
Its converse uses actual histories with their evidence; its upper bound
uses the whole posterior image and is realized by one causal filter.
A product-tangent criterion isolates the positive-history attainment
underlying the converse. For a four-cell circular detector with Haar
prior, acquisition and future observation each contribute a factor
$\tau^j$ to the $j$th harmonic. For every fixed horizon $N$, sufficiently
small known contrast, and all $M\ge1$, the sharp causal squared loss is
$\max_{1\le j\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\tau^{2(j+1)}M^{-1/j}$;
it is zero at zero contrast. The causal constructions allow a read-only
program depending on the known experiment, but no uncharged history.
We also determine all width orders of the local monomial prior-ambiguity
body. Under dominated-family and interior-slack hypotheses, an imperfect
common moment name gives the monomial memory profile plus squared input
error. Complete finite numerical realizations and their resource bounds
are established separately for the monomial model.
\end{abstract}
