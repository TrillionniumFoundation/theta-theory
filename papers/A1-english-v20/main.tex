\begin{abstract}
We determine finite-memory prediction error in positive experiments
through the geometry jointly acquired by the past and observed by the
future. For sparse monomial experiments at a fixed horizon, maximal
Vandermonde products give the sharp resolution profile uniformly across
all additive exponent collisions. The profile is truncated by the
attainable past dimension and is realized by one causal finite-state
filter. The proof combines unconditional positive-history mass, a
whole-image anisotropic cover, and raw-moment updates without reciprocal
collision gaps. For command-polynomial likelihoods on an arbitrary
compact latent space, an augmented moment rank gives the checkpoint and
causal memory exponents for every prior. For one affine acquisition
step, the full profile is determined by the physical acquisition--query
covariance, uniformly through rank loss. We characterize exact kernels
of continuous moment pairings by a positivity condition and the rank of
a quotient multiplication pencil, with full-support witnesses obtained
by arbitrarily small bounded density perturbations. This separates exact
realization from the weaker feasibility of annihilation. A fixed positive
experiment realizes arbitrary small covariance matrices; classical
analytic orders then determine their joint memory--degeneration law.
Circular contrast, operational recovery of scale products, and prior
ambiguity give further consequences, with separate numerical resource
bounds for the monomial realization.
\end{abstract}
