\subsection{Witness size in the intrinsic behavior dimension}
\label{sec:v15-witness}
The local theorem can be complemented by a bound on how many different
tests are needed. This bound uses the dimension of attainable behaviors,
not the number of stochastic-row coordinates in a chosen realization.
It does not confuse the number of tests with the cost of verifying
that their violation regions cover a nonconvex set.

Let $\mathcal B_K=\{b(q):q\in\mathcal K_K\}$ be the private behavior
image from Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main}. Differences of behaviors have
zero mass in each parameter--feedback row. On their linear span put
\[
 \|b-b'\|_*=
       \max_{\theta,g}\frac12\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_g}
                    |b_{\theta,g}(\omega)-b'_{\theta,g}(\omega)|.
\]
This is a norm, $\diam\mathcal B_K\le1$, and every event discrepancy
$f_i(b)=Q_{\theta,g}(B)-b_{\theta,g}(B)$ is $1$-Lipschitz. Let
$a=\dim\aff\mathcal B_K$; redundant realization coordinates do not
change $a$.

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic-dimension testing witnesses]
\label{thm:v15-witness}
Suppose $\dpr_K(E,F)\ge L+\gamma$ with $\gamma>0$. There is a subfamily
$\mathcal I_0$ of the original marked feedback tests, of size at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-witness-count}
 |\mathcal I_0|\le \left\lceil(1+4/\gamma)^a\right\rceil,
\end{equation}
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-reduced-witness}
 \min_{q\in\mathcal K_K}\max_{i\in\mathcal I_0} f_i(b(q))
                                      \ge L+\gamma/2.
\end{equation}
Thus the same-budget private lower certificate can use this fixed
subfamily on every policy cell. With rational finite input, rational
$L,\gamma$, and classical real quantifier elimination, such a subfamily
can be extracted effectively. No convex mixture of simulators, state
increase or selector is introduced.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the $a$-dimensional affine behavior space choose a maximal set
$b_1,\ldots,b_s\in\mathcal B_K$ with pairwise distances strictly
greater than $r=\gamma/2$. It is an $r$-net by maximality. Balls of
radius $r/2$ about these points have disjoint interiors and lie in a
ball of radius $1+r/2$ about $b_1$. Lebesgue volume in this affine
space, and its homogeneity under dilation, give
$s\le(1+2/r)^a$. For $a=0$ one point suffices. At each center choose a
test $i_j$ attaining its maximal discrepancy. This discrepancy is at
least $L+\gamma$. Every behavior within $r$ of that center therefore
satisfies $f_{i_j}\ge L+\gamma/2$. Take the union of these tests and
remove repetitions. This proves both assertions.

For effective extraction use rational row tables as centers. Their
behavior images are dense, since rational points are dense in products
of simplexes and $b$ is continuous. Given finitely many centers, the
assertion that some feasible $q$ lies at distance strictly greater than
$r$ from all centers is a finite existential semialgebraic assertion:
absolute values and row maxima can be expressed by finitely many
polynomial inequalities and disjunctions. Real quantifier elimination
decides it \cite{Basu2014}. When true, rational row enumeration finds
such a point because the strict inequality defines a relatively open
nonempty subset of the row simplexes. When false, the chosen points
form an $r$-net. The volume bound forces termination. All discrepancies
at rational centers are rational and can be compared exactly.

Finally apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-local} to the reduced finite family.
Its maximal discrepancy has the strict gap in
\eqref{eq:v15-reduced-witness}; sufficiently fine policy cells certify
$L$ using only these tests. The extraction bound is on the number of
distinct tests, not on the number of cells or the complexity of real
quantifier elimination.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dimension dependence cannot be discarded]
\label{prop:v15-witness-lower}
For the delayed identity experiment on $n\ge2$ symbols at private
width one, $a=n-1$. If
\[
 0<\gamma<\frac1{n(n-1)},\qquad L=1-\frac1n-\gamma,
\]
any subfamily of original event tests that proves
$\min_q\max_i f_i(q)>L$ contains at least $n=a+1$ tests.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A private one-state behavior is a probability vector $p$ independent
of the parameter. A positive discrepancy in parameter row $i$ has
form $1-p(B)$ with $i\in B$, and is bounded above by $1-p_i$.
If fewer than $n$ tests are used, let $S$ be the parameter indices of
the positive-discrepancy tests. It has size at most $n-1$. When it is
nonempty choose $p$ uniform on $S$. Every chosen positive discrepancy
is at most $1-1/|S|\le1-1/(n-1)<L$; tests with target probability zero
are nonpositive. If $S$ is empty all tests are nonpositive. Hence no
such subfamily certifies the stated bound. The full behavior set is
the $(n-1)$-simplex, giving its dimension.
\end{proof}

The volumetric packing step and real-algebraic elimination are classical.
The conclusion here is a structural bound for the nonconvex original
resource image, invariant under the addition of unobservable row
coordinates. It supplies an alternative to retaining every possible
event at every policy cell. It is neither a polynomial-time algorithm
nor a claim that one constant convex separator characterizes the
private feasible set.
