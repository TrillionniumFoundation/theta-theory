\section{Enriched defect certificates and their convergence}
\label{sec:v15-enriched}
A small error of a Galerkin orbit need not force a chosen residual
majorant to be small. We replace that implication by an estimator
whose own convergence is proved. The approximation is allowed to be
a finite tree of trial observables rather than powers of one compressed
matrix. This enrichment changes the numerical presentation, not the
physical experiment or the charged consumer register.

Let $H$ be a separable real Hilbert space, $U(t)$ a strongly continuous
orthogonal group with generator $L$, and $C_a$ finitely many orthogonal
operators. Write $T_a=C_aU(\Delta)$, with fixed rational $\Delta>0$.
Let $\mathscr D$ be a countable rational vector space in $D(L)$ whose
real span is graph dense in $D(L)$, and suppose its rational span is
also graph dense. It suffices to start with a countable graph-core
family and all rational linear combinations.

\begin{definition}[Effective graph data]\label{def:v15-effective}
The data are effective if vectors of $\mathscr D$ can be enumerated
and rational intervals of any requested width can be computed for
inner products among finitely many vectors of the forms
\[
 v,\quad Lv,\quad C_av,\quad f_1,\ldots,f_T,
                 \qquad v\in\mathscr D.
\]
Only inner products actually used in the following norm expressions
are necessary. The initial observables have specified $H$ names.
For physical probability certification we require, in addition,
computable enclosures for \eqref{eq:v15-mass-integral} for finite trial
families selected by this procedure and certified preparation bounds.
These are finite analytic input operations; no deficiency optimizer or
exact controlled orbit is an input operation.
\end{definition}

For nodes $v_0,\ldots,v_l\in\mathscr D$ at rational times
$0=s_0<\cdots<s_l=\Delta$, let $z(s)$ be their piecewise affine
interpolant, and set
\begin{align}\label{eq:v15-enriched-residual}
 \mathcal D(z)&=\frac12\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}(s_{j+1}-s_j)
       \left(\|d_j-Lv_j\|+\|d_j-Lv_{j+1}\|\right),\\
 d_j&=(v_{j+1}-v_j)/(s_{j+1}-s_j).\nonumber
\end{align}
The interpolation lies in $D(L)$ at every time and is absolutely
continuous in its graph norm. For a trial input $g\in\mathscr D$
and trial output $v\in\mathscr D$, define the one-step certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-step-certificate}
 \mathcal E_a(g,z,v)=\|g-z(0)\|+\mathcal D(z)
                                    +\|C_az(\Delta)-v\|.
\end{equation}
No derivative of an intervened vector appears.

\begin{lemma}[Certified path defect]\label{lem:v15-path}
For every such triple,
\[
 \|T_ag-v\|\le\mathcal E_a(g,z,v).
\]
Given $g\in\mathscr D$, an action $a$, and $\eta>0$, there are rational
nodes with $z(0)=g$ and a rational-core output $v$ such that
$\mathcal E_a(g,z,v)<\eta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On each interpolation interval, variation of constants gives
\[
 U(\Delta)z(0)-z(\Delta)
       =\int_0^\Delta U(\Delta-s)(Lz(s)-z'(s))\,ds.
\]
The identity follows first on each graph-differentiable interval and
then by concatenation. The integrand's norm is bounded by the affine
interpolation of its endpoint norms, since $z'$ is constant on that
interval and $Lz$ is affine. Thus its integral is at most
$\mathcal D(z)$. Orthogonality of $C_a$ and the two endpoint errors
give \eqref{eq:v15-step-certificate}.

For existence put $u(s)=U(s)g$. It is continuously differentiable in
$H$, continuous in the graph norm, and $u'=Lu$. Let a uniform rational
mesh have size $h$. Its exact-node interpolant has derivative error
bounded by the modulus of continuity of $u'$ at scale $h$, and its
$L$-image differs from $Lu(s)$ by at most the modulus of continuity of
$Lu$ at that scale. Both moduli tend to zero. Approximate each noninitial
node by a member of $\mathscr D$ within $h^2$ in graph norm; keep the
initial node equal to $g$. The change in the interpolant derivative is
at most $2h$ and the change in its $L$-image at most $h^2$.
The endpoint norms in \eqref{eq:v15-enriched-residual} obey these same
modulus bounds, so $\mathcal D(z)\to0$, not only the orbit error.
Finally use density of $\mathscr D$ in $H$ to approximate
$C_az(\Delta)$ by $v$. No assertion that $C_az(\Delta)\in D(L)$ is
needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Effective enriched controlled approximation]
\label{thm:v15-enriched}
Under the effective graph-data hypothesis there is an algorithm which,
for a fixed finite horizon and rational tolerance $\eta>0$, returns
finite-core mean functions $\widehat m_{k,w}$ and rational upper bounds
$e_{k,w}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-enriched-word}
 \|T_{a_1}\cdots T_{a_k}f_k-\widehat m_{k,w}\|\le e_{k,w}<\eta,
             \qquad w=(a_1,\ldots,a_k),\quad 1\le k\le T.
\end{equation}
Each bound is a sum of an initial approximation error and certified
one-step path defects. The algorithm terminates; no rate or polynomial
complexity is asserted. Its certified physical marked error is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-enriched-physical}
 \alpha=1\wedge\frac{D}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
       \sum_{k=1}^T\frac1{\sigma_k}
          \left(\sum_{w\in A^k}e_{k,w}^{\,2}\right)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
and tends to zero along requested tolerances tending to zero.
The source and approximation are compared by stateless identity
report channels, including all independent exposed selectors.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate $\mathscr D$ and increasingly accurate rational norm
upper bounds until a trial vector for $f_k$ has error strictly below
a prescribed small tolerance. Density ensures termination. For each
step enumerate all finite rational time partitions and all rational
core nodes and outputs, with initial node the trial input. Dovetail
these candidates and interval precisions. Lemma~\ref{lem:v15-path}
provides a candidate whose true defect is strictly smaller than the
requested tolerance, so eventually its rational upper enclosure is
also smaller. This is a terminating semidecision search with a proved
strict witness. It does not test an undecidable equality of real
numbers.

For each final acquisition $k$, build a suffix tree starting at the
trial $f_k$. At the next level approximate $T_a$ applied to each
current trial vector using the preceding search. After $k$ levels,
the chronological word $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ corresponds to applying
$a_k$ first to the observable and $a_1$ last. This gives exactly
$T_{a_1}\cdots T_{a_k}f_k$, not its reversed physical dynamics.
Orthogonality propagates the accumulated error additively. Allocate
less than $\eta/(2(k+1))$ to the initial approximation and each step,
and refine finite norm enclosures until their rational sum is below
$\eta$. There are finitely many nodes and every search terminates.

All trial vectors used at finitely many nodes span a finite-dimensional
subspace of $H$. Initialize its evaluation vector jointly with the
actual mark and use the stored action-word table to read the appropriate
linear functional at each time. This is a causal probability model.
Its physical lookup table is not declared to be a finite consumer
register, and its coefficient matrices are not called Markov matrices.
The first-mismatch proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-residual}, with the
verified errors in \eqref{eq:v15-enriched-word}, gives
\eqref{eq:v15-enriched-physical}. Every term tends to zero at fixed
horizon. All numerical square roots and constants in an implementation
are replaced by rational upper enclosures; their extra tolerances may
also be made arbitrarily small.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Existence, effectiveness, and the old Galerkin estimator]
Graph-core density alone proves existence of arbitrarily small enriched
defects. Effective graph data make the search an algorithm. Neither
assertion says that the Galerkin majorants
\eqref{eq:v14-word-residual} converge along every graph-core sequence.
Those estimates remain valid for every selected space and are kept in
the article. The new estimator has a different convergence proof: its
piecewise affine residual itself is made small. Effective integration
of the resulting finite report probabilities is an additional operation,
explicit in Definition~\ref{def:v15-effective}, and is not inferred
from Hilbert-space density.
\end{remark}
