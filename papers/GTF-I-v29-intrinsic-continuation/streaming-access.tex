\section{A scalable full-support experiment with two exact memory laws}
\label{sec:access29}
We next keep the final answer alphabet binary while the preparation
length grows. This distinguishes intrinsic positive memory from
ordinary predictive dimension, and gives an exact causal construction
rather than a checkpoint factorization alone.

An input $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\{-1,1\}^n$ is fixed. Each coordinate
can be read once during acquisition. After acquisition an external
query $j\in[n]$ arrives as one atomic symbol, and a bit $B\in\{-1,1\}$
is emitted. For a specified $0\le\eta\le1$, the required experiment is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:access-law29}
             \Pr(B=b\mid x,j)=\frac{1+b\eta x_j}{2}.
\end{equation}
Acquisition may choose the next unread coordinate using the retained
state. At the query cut the entire acquisition transcript has been
discarded except for that state. Neither a public persistent seed nor
a transcript of the chosen order is given to the decoder. The external
round number is a declared clock; any data-dependent schedule variables
must be stored. The output is sampled in the update receiving $j$,
so $j$ need not be buffered after that update. Exact arbitrary stochastic
rows are atomic, as in the preceding realization theorems.

Let $W_n(\eta)$ be the least whole-schedule peak over these controllers.
Let $K_n(\eta)$ be the least number of states just before the query,
without an upstream cap. For $0<\eta<1$ every required answer probability
has full support. Equality with \eqref{eq:access-law29} is required for
every input and every query; an average success criterion is different.

\begin{theorem}[Linear and exponential exact memory regimes]
\label{thm:access29}
For every integer $n\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 K_n(\eta)=W_n(\eta)&=n+1,
       &&0<\eta\le n^{-2},\label{eq:access-weak29}\\
 K_n(\eta)=W_n(\eta)&=2^n,
       &&1-n^{-1}<\eta\le1.\label{eq:access-strong29}
\end{align}
In the weak-signal regime a fixed-order streaming controller has exactly
$t+1$ states after $t$ input bits, simultaneously for $0\le t\le n$.
For that fixed order these counts are minimal. Both peak lower bounds
hold for arbitrary adaptive acquisition schedules. The two displayed
regimes are not asserted to exhaust the intermediate parameter range.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The intrinsic checkpoint geometry}
For a query-state $s$, write $z_s(j)=\E[B\mid s,j]\in[-1,1]$.
An encoder gives \eqref{eq:access-law29} exactly if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cube-factor29}
                     \eta x=\sum_s E(s\mid x)z_s
              \quad\hbox{for every }x\in\{-1,1\}^n.
\end{equation}
Thus $K_n(\eta)$ equals the smallest number of points in $[-1,1]^n$
whose convex hull contains $[-\eta,\eta]^n$. Necessity is conditioning
on the state. Conversely any such containment supplies a stochastic
encoder and binary decoder. This is a normalized nested-polytope
formulation, not an identification with unrestricted matrix rank
\cite{GG}. It says nothing by itself about the memory needed to compute
the encoder online.

For $\eta>0$, the inner cube has affine dimension $n$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:access-rank29}
                          K_n(\eta)\ge n+1.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the matrix in \eqref{eq:access-law29}, with input words
as rows and pairs $(j,b)$ as columns, has ordinary rank $n+1$.
Adaptive acquisition cannot evade \eqref{eq:cube-factor29}: after the
barrier there is still only one retained state law for each complete
input, and the same decoder on every input.

\subsection{A compatible sequence of enclosing simplices}
We construct the encoder using only a label, never a stored real vector.
For $t\ge1$ put $b_t=t\eta$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:access-simplex29}
 P_t=\{z\in\mathbb R^t:z_i\ge-b_t,\ \sum_{i=1}^t z_i\le b_t\}.
\end{equation}
Its vertices are $v_{t,0}=(-b_t,\ldots,-b_t)$ and
$v_{t,i}=v_{t,0}+(t+1)b_t e_i$, $1\le i\le t$.
The distinguished coordinate of $v_{t,i}$ is $t^2\eta$ and its other
coordinates are $-t\eta$. Therefore $P_t\subset[-1,1]^t$ when
$\eta\le n^{-2}$ and $t\le n$. Set $P_0=\{()\}$.
The key causal inclusion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:access-nesting29}
                    P_t\times\{-\eta,\eta\}\subset P_{t+1}.
\end{equation}
Indeed old coordinates are at least $-t\eta$, the new coordinate is at
least $-\eta$, and the new sum is at most $t\eta+\eta=b_{t+1}$.
For $w\in P_t$ its barycentric coordinates are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:access-bary29}
 \lambda_0(w)=\frac{b_t-\sum_iw_i}{(t+1)b_t},\qquad
 \lambda_i(w)=\frac{w_i+b_t}{(t+1)b_t}\quad(1\le i\le t).
\end{equation}
They are nonnegative, sum to one, and reconstruct $w$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \eqref{eq:access-weak29}]
At time $t$ the register is an index $s\in\{0,\ldots,t\}$.
After bit $x_{t+1}$, replace it by index $s'$ sampled from the
barycentric coordinates of $(v_{t,s},\eta x_{t+1})$ in $P_{t+1}$.
These are legal rows by \eqref{eq:access-nesting29}. Induction gives
\[
 \sum_s\Pr(s\mid x_1,\ldots,x_t)v_{t,s}
                         =\eta(x_1,\ldots,x_t).
\]
Given the final state $s$ and query $j$, draw the answer with mean
$v_{n,s}(j)$. This is a valid binary row because $P_n\subset[-1,1]^n$.
The preceding identity proves \eqref{eq:access-law29} exactly.
It also proves the entire online implementation, not just existence
of a final stochastic matrix. The real vectors in the proof are
read-only row descriptions; the machine stores only their indices.
No past coin, real accumulator, or chosen-input transcript survives
outside the register. Formula~\eqref{eq:access-bary29} shows that all
acquisition rows are rational and independent of $\eta>0$, because
that common factor cancels. Only the final decoder depends on the
specified signal strength.

The peak is $n+1$; sampling and retaining the final binary answer uses
two states. Bound~\eqref{eq:access-rank29} gives the converse even with
adaptive acquisition. For a fixed acquisition order, after $t$ bits
fix all future input bits and consider queries among the first $t$
coordinates. The resulting continuation rows have affine dimension
$t$, forcing $t+1$ states at that cut. This proves simultaneous
minimality of the claimed streaming profile.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A geometry stronger than rank}
\begin{proof}[Proof of \eqref{eq:access-strong29}]
For a corner $x$ and a possible generator $z\in[-1,1]^n$, set
\[
                        d_x(z)=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n(1-x_i z_i).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:cube-factor29} implies that its average under the
encoder for $x$ is $n(1-\eta)/2<1/2$. Hence some used generator has
$d_x(z)<1/2$. For distinct corners $x,x'$, at a coordinate where they
differ the two corresponding summands add to one; all other summands
are nonnegative. Thus $d_x(z)+d_{x'}(z)\ge1$, and no generator can
serve both corners. All $2^n$ corners need distinct generators.
Retaining the entire input word and then applying the single noisy
query row gives the upper bound $2^n$. The factorization argument is
independent of the acquisition order or its internal randomization.
\end{proof}

For $n\ge2$, both exact regimes contain full-support laws. In the
strong regime the ordinary rank is only $n+1$, but the exact causal
memory is $2^n$. In the weak regime the nested simplices attain that
rank with a compatible streaming implementation. Thus neither support
alone nor linear dimension alone determines exact memory.
In persistent bits these two regimes give respectively
$\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ and $n$.
At $\eta=0$ the query-state complexity is one; the peak is two if the
emitted answer must be held for a charged output cut. Every nonzero
weak signal has query-state complexity $n+1$, despite full support
and uniform convergence to the fair-output channel as $\eta\downarrow0$.

\begin{proposition}[Known rows, approximation, and a strict rank margin]
\label{prop:access-approx29}
The input-independent fair-output channel has rowwise total-variation
error $\eta/2$ in approximating \eqref{eq:access-law29}.
For $n\ge2$, any approximation factoring through at most $n$ query
states has worst rowwise error at least $\eta/(2\sqrt n)$.
The exact state counts in Theorem~\ref{thm:access29} refer to known
$\eta$ and atomic stochastic rows, not to an unknown calibration or
a fixed-length finite fair-bit implementation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the binary total-variation formula. For the
second, form the $2^n$ by $2n$ probability matrix $A$. Its nonzero
singular values are $\sqrt{n2^{n-1}}$ and
$\eta\sqrt{2^{n-1}}$ with multiplicity $n$. This follows by using the
orthogonal row functions $1,x_1,\ldots,x_n$ and, in each query block,
the orthogonal column vectors $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$.
A matrix of rank at most $n$ differs from $A$ in Frobenius norm by at
least its smallest nonzero singular value. If every binary-query row
has total-variation error at most $\delta$, each pair contributes at
most $2\delta^2$, so the squared Frobenius error is at most
$2n2^n\delta^2$. The claimed bound follows. This is only an
approximation bound, not an assertion that the two sides match.
\end{proof}

Random-access coding is a classical communication task
\cite{ALMO,RAC2026,Liu2026}. Equation~\eqref{eq:access-law29}
fixes every conditional decoding probability, not merely a lower bound
on worst-case success or an average over inputs and queries. The new
causal assertion made here is the exact finite-horizon profile from
\eqref{eq:access-nesting29}, together with lower bounds that survive
adaptive acquisition and all persistent-randomness accounting. No
priority claim for the communication model or for convex coding is made.
