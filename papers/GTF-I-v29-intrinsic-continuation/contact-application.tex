\section{From saddle contact to intrinsic memory}
\label{sec:contact29}
The geometric theorems classify response laws. A statistical optimization
must first determine which laws are forced. We make that logical order
explicit, so that Bayes indifference is not mistaken for freedom to
choose the cheapest response.

Let $\mathcal C$ be the finite polytope of full-history causal response
arrays and $g_\theta(C)$ the continuous payoff against a fixed unknown
parameter in a compact space. Suppose $\mu_*$ is a least-favorable prior
with value $U$. The optimal set is
\[
 \mathcal S_*=
 \left\{C:\int g_\theta(C)\,d\mu_*=U\right\}
 \cap\bigcap_\theta\{C:g_\theta(C)\ge U\}.
\]
For every $C\in\mathcal S_*$, nonnegativity and zero prior integral give
$g_\theta(C)=U$ on $\supp\mu_*$. Differentiability at an interior
supporting parameter also gives $\nabla_\theta g_\theta(C)=0$.
These are the contact equations proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:saddle-realization}; they apply even when a response
coordinate has zero Bayes coefficient.

\begin{corollary}[A contact-rigid saddle classification]
\label{cor:contact-class29}
Suppose the saddle conditions determine a unique complete causal
response array $C_*$. If its normalized residual geometry is
componentwise simplicial at every charged cut, the minimum profile
attaining $U$ is exactly $(s_t(C_*))_t$, simultaneously. Every smaller
profile misses $U$ by a strictly positive amount in a fixed finite
interface. More generally, any finite family of common normalized
continuation rows forced for every $C\in\mathcal S_*$ gives the
support-component rank lower bound at its cut, even when those rows
are obtained by parameter-independent averaging.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Any value-attaining array belongs to $\mathcal S_*$, and hence equals
$C_*$ in the first assertion. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic29}.
The normalized finite-row realization space for a smaller fixed
profile is compact. If its maximal worst-case score were $U$, it
would contain an implementation of $C_*$, contrary to the lower bound.
For the last assertion, each forced row factors through the same
state continuations at that cut. Averaging encoder rows preserves
normalization and this factorization. The separated-component lower
argument therefore applies to the forced rows. This last lower bound
does not, without completeness and compatibility, assert an exact
whole-schedule profile.
\end{proof}

This corollary states a checkable implication after contact has been
solved; it does not assume that every saddle is response-rigid. The
following application deliberately uses only the common forced rows,
because the marked saddle does not determine every coupling of fresh
validation coins.

\subsection{The marked decision geometry as a complete instance}
The full-support saddle of Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-saddle}, proved
in the supporting development, concerns
\[
 (\gamma,\kappa)\in[6/25,13/50]\times[0,1/1000].
\]
Its contact equations force the following five averaged response rows
on the diagonal atoms $000,001,110,111$, with zero response at the four
other atoms:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:five-rows29}
 (t,0,1,1),\quad(1,0,1,1),\quad(1,1,1,1),\quad
 (1,1,0,1),\quad(1,1,0,t),\qquad 0<t<1.
\end{equation}
The inner three rows are individually forced by strict Bayes signs.
The outer rows are averages over the independent training marks;
contact and stationarity fix their formerly indifferent coordinates.
Every actual state encoder still factors these averages through its
same decoder. These facts are proved without a count-only restriction
on competitors in Theorems~\ref{thm:five-memory} and
\ref{thm:positive-saddle}.

\begin{proposition}[Canonical five-state decision capacity]
\label{prop:marked-csr29}
For the forced response rows \eqref{eq:five-rows29}, the support graph
has components of sizes $2,1,2$, with affine dimensions $1,0,1$.
Their hulls are simplices. Thus their intrinsic capacity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:five-rank29}
                            2+1+2=5.
\end{equation}
This recovers the exact decision count against all the original
stochastic encoders, training gates, stopping rules, and independent
calibration, not just against a chosen five-event compiler.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Convert each response coordinate $h(y)$ into the two probabilities
$(1-h(y),h(y))$. This affine embedding identifies the response cube
with its normalized binary-query channel polytope. The first two rows
have intersecting minimal faces: the edge of the first contains the
second. The last two rows have the reflected relation. Their faces
are disjoint from one another and from the singleton face of the
middle row. Hence these are exactly the three support components.
The first and last components each contain two distinct points;
the middle contains one. Corollary~\ref{cor:contact-class29} gives the
five-state lower bound. The retained optimal event selection attains
it and its validation implementation is proved in the supporting
physical development. No new physical hypothesis is inserted.
\end{proof}

For $m$ independent copies of this specified binary-query continuation,
the exact joint checkpoint capacity is $5^m$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:tensor29}. This requires the product output law for
the independent copies. It is not a claim that the minimax value of
an $m$-block audit is the product of $U_2$, or that marginal optimality
alone forces independent output randomization.

\subsection{Known-target physical exactness and acquisition}
The supporting proofs retain the earlier rational certificate and
uniform two-preparation construction, with constants
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constants}
 u_1=\frac{4035777}{10240000},\qquad
 m_2=\frac{12-3\sqrt3}{16},\qquad \beta_0=\frac1{300}.
\end{equation}
These are not substituted for the later exact saddle value $U_2$.

For every specified Brownian target $Q_{d,\sigma}$ in
$9/10\le d\le11/10$, $0<\sigma\le1/24$, the exact target-identification
proof gives
\[
 \gamma=\tfrac14-\tfrac12\E e,
 \qquad \kappa=\tfrac12\E[e(1-e)]>0.
\]
The conditional sign-error rate $e$ is shared through the physical
signal; replacing $\E e^2$ by $(\E e)^2$ is not justified.
The retained full-support saddle and schedule theorems therefore give
five decision states and minimum peak twelve over all externally
declared, order-preserving validation clocks. Exactly the serial
orders attain that minimum. The ideal reverse-order support limit
has peak ten. These are theorems about the specified target with its
exact stochastic row available, and their proofs remain in full below.

An acquired target is different. Its finite counter and finite-bit
construction in Proposition~\ref{prop:physical-acquisition} gives
approximate score recovery with its stated calibration error and
workspace. Neither that proposition nor Theorem~\ref{thm:access29}
identifies an unknown algebraic row exactly from finitely many samples.
The new intrinsic classifier does not change this distinction. Nor
does its declared-path corollary convert the physical fixed-clock
optimum into a lower bound for all report-dependent validation schedulers.
The adaptive-acquisition quantifier of Theorem~\ref{thm:access29} is
proved for that experiment by the barrier factorization, not imported
from the collision example.
