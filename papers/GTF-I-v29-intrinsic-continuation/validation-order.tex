\section{Optimal validation orders and a positive-noise memory jump}
\label{sec:validation-order}
We now optimize the order in the original two-tape, bit-level
validation interface. Each tape has slots $1,2,w$ in that order. Write
$X$ for the candidate tape and $Y$ for the target tape. There are twenty
declared-clock interleavings. A fixed clock is common to all executions;
its phase is not a report-dependent side channel. Gate choices, state
updates, training and frozen-event selection remain stochastic and
otherwise unrestricted as before.

Let $e_0,\ldots,e_4$ be the five events \eqref{eq:events}. A full raw
validation word has output $e_i(Y)-e_i(X)$. For the strict training
counts, contact forces $i=1,2,3$ respectively. At either extreme only
one raw-event coordinate is indifferent; Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-saddle}
fixes its mark-averaged probability to $t\in(1/10,1/4)$.

One precaution is needed. A freshly randomized gate need not couple
its two validation coins as one stored event mask would. When both raw
validation words equal the indifferent atom, their difference may be
random with mean zero. None of the lower certificates below uses that
coordinate as a forced deterministic zero. For every additional mixed
witness, the known prefix excludes at least one validation word from
the indifferent atom, so at least one of the two event indicators is
already deterministic. Its future output law is then fixed by the
remaining indicator's marginal. This covers both stored-mask and fresh
gate implementations.

\begin{theorem}[The minimum over all declared-clock validation orders]
\label{thm:all-clock-orders}
For every $(\gamma,\kappa)\in\mathcal R$ with $\kappa>0$, the least
whole-schedule peak attaining $U_2(b,Q_{\gamma,\kappa})$, minimized over
all twenty declared-clock interleavings, is twelve. The only minimizing
orders are the two serial orders
\[
                         XXXYYY,\qquad YYYXXX.
\]
Every nonserial declared order requires peak at least fourteen.
The lower bounds permit the full stochastic gate and event interface,
not merely an all-on deterministic compiler. These conclusions apply
to $Q_{d,\sigma}$ for every $9/10\le d\le11/10$ and
$0<\sigma\le1/24$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every raw validation prefix has positive probability at either
supporting candidate and at the target. Thus all cut witnesses may be
compared on a common full future alphabet. Generate all-on words to
simulate training gates as in Theorem~\ref{thm:five-memory}. The
post-training response is an unrestricted optimizer on the supporting
subfamily, hence has the contact-forced rows of
Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-saddle}. Stopping before the two training
preparations cannot occur with positive supporting probability: for
each retained first-word branch, two possible unused second words force
different strict vertices. A positive stopped component cannot belong
to both vertices. The witnesses below therefore concern actual
post-training validation cuts.

At a cut with two slots consumed from one tape and none from the other,
the eight deterministic vertices and four disjoint additional faces
from Theorem~\ref{thm:serial-twelve} give twelve. That argument required
only the four positive target diagonal words. It is still valid with
full target support. Exchanging the two raw tapes negates the output
and gives exactly the same channel-polytope certificate at the reversed
cut. Thus either serial order needs twelve.

We give explicit certificates at the two possible nonserial entrance
cuts. After one slot of each tape, write $x=X_1$, $y=Y_1$, and use
suffix coordinates $(u,w,v,z)=(X_2,X_w,Y_2,Y_w)$. Define
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 i&g_i^0(u,w)&g_i^1(u,w)\\\hline
 1&(1-u)(1-w)&u\\
 2&1-u&u\\
 3&1-u&uw
 \end{array}
\]
and let $d_i^{xy}=g_i^y(v,z)-g_i^x(u,w)$.
The twelve expressions with $i=1,2,3$ and $x,y\in\{0,1\}$ give
ten distinct deterministic vertices. The only repetitions are
$d_2^{11}=d_1^{11}$ and $d_3^{00}=d_2^{00}$, as is seen by substituting
$u,w,v,z\in\{0,1\}$ in the displayed functions.

There are four more required channels. Use count zero at prefixes
$(x,y)=(0,1),(1,0)$ and count four at these same two prefixes. Their
endpoints are respectively the continuations of $(e_0,e_1)$ and
$(e_4,e_3)$, with nontrivial weights $1-t,t$. Each excludes simultaneous
occurrence of the indifferent atom. The minimal face of each such
channel consists of distributions supported, at each suffix, on the
two endpoint output values. Among the ten mandatory vertices it contains
only $d_1^{01},d_1^{10},d_3^{01},d_3^{10}$, respectively.
These four faces are pairwise disjoint. In their listed order, suffixes
separating their six pairs are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
 \text{pair}&12&13&14&23&24&34\\\hline
 (u,w,v,z)&0110&0010&0000&0000&0001&0000
 \end{array}.
\]
At each listed suffix the two sets of permitted output integers are
disjoint; this follows directly from the displayed $g_i^x$ and the
masks $e_0,e_4$. None of the required mixed rows is the sole mandatory
vertex in its face, because $0<t<1$. Face packing gives $10+4=14$.

Suppose instead two slots of $X$ and one of $Y$ have been consumed.
A deterministic continuation is indexed by $(i,X_1X_2,Y_1)$.
The following twelve choices are distinct and include every strict-count
continuation:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|l}
 i& (X_1X_2,Y_1)\\\hline
 1&(00,0),(00,1),(01,0),(01,1),(11,0),(11,1)\\
 2&(00,0),(01,0)\\
 3&(00,1),(01,1),(11,0),(11,1)
 \end{array}.
\]
They are verified by the formula
$e_i(Y_1,Y_2,Y_w)-e_i(X_1,X_2,X_w)$ on the eight suffixes
$(X_w,Y_2,Y_w)$. At training count zero take additionally prefixes
$(01,0)$ and $(11,0)$. The corresponding two faces have endpoints
$(0,(1-Y_2)(1-Y_w))$ and
$(-1,-1+(1-Y_2)(1-Y_w))$, respectively. Each contains just its listed
$i=1$ vertex, and they are disjoint at any suffix with $Y_2=1$.
Both mixtures are nontrivial. Thus this cut also requires fourteen.
Swapping $X,Y$ proves the same result at the cut with one and two
slots consumed.

Any nonserial interleaving either reads one slot from each tape first,
or starts with two slots of one tape and then switches before its mark.
It therefore passes one of the preceding cuts and needs fourteen.
Every serial order has an upper realization of peak twelve: select the
same five-valued frozen event and use its future residual functions.
For full-support raw words the validation widths in either serial order
are
\[
                         (5,10,12,10,10,7,3).
\]
These counts follow by restriction of the five masks, exactly as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}; for the reverse order negate all output
residuals and exchange the tapes. Training and the charged stochastic
selection cut have width at most five. Therefore both serial orders
attain twelve, proving all assertions by
Theorem~\ref{thm:facial-schedule}. The physical specialization follows
from Theorem~\ref{thm:physical-noise-form}.
\end{proof}

This theorem optimizes the externally declared schedule; it does not
optimize a report-dependent scheduler that changes which tape is read
next. Such a scheduler has a different action interface, and its
protocol state cannot be supplied for free. The phrase ``all orders''
in Theorem~\ref{thm:all-clock-orders} refers to all twenty declared
interleavings, with this quantifier fixed explicitly.

\begin{theorem}[A singular support transition in reverse serial memory]
\label{thm:reverse-jump}
For every $\gamma\in[6/25,13/50]$, the exact minimum peak in the
reverse serial order $YYYXXX$ for the ideal target $Q_{\gamma,0}$ is
ten. For every $0<\kappa\le1/1000$ it is twelve. In particular the
reverse-serial exact memory of the physical collision experiment jumps
from ten at its zero-noise target to twelve at every positive
$\sigma\le1/24$, although its target laws converge in total variation
as $\sigma\downarrow0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the upper bound at the ideal target, ignore its first report bit;
its second bit equals the first almost surely, so this does not remove
any needed information. After its second bit and mark, retain the
residual $c-e_i(X)$, where $c=e_i(Y)$. Event $e_2$ has $c=1$ surely;
each other event permits two values of $c$. The compatible reverse
validation profile is
\[
                          (5,5,9,9,10,7,3).
\]
After the second target bit the nine functions are, with the unread
mark denoted by $z$,
\[
 -e_0,\ 1-e_0,\ 1-z-e_1,\ 1-e_1,\ 1-e_2,
 \ 1-e_3,\ z-e_3,\ 1-e_4,\ -e_4.
\]
Restricting after the target mark gives nine functions $c-e_i$.
After the first candidate bit they give ten distinct functions of
$(X_2,X_w)$; after its second bit they give the seven scalar mark
functions in Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}. Equality of residual functions
is preserved by restriction, so these counts describe one compatible
machine, not independent cutwise colorings. Training remains of peak
five and the terminal score is correct almost surely for every
candidate. On target words of zero probability, choose arbitrary legal
transitions.

For the converse examine the cut after all three target slots and the
first candidate bit. Write suffixes $(X_2,X_w)$ in the order
$00,01,10,11$. The three strict training events force the following
eight distinct deterministic output channels:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 (0,1,1,1),\quad(-1,0,0,0),\quad(1,1,0,0),\quad(0,0,-1,-1),\\
 (0,0,1,1),\quad(-1,-1,0,0),\quad(1,1,1,0),\quad(0,0,0,-1).
 \end{gathered}
\]
At training count zero take $X_1=0$ and target words $001$ and $110$.
They force the channels with output means
\[
                         (-t,0,0,0),\qquad(1-t,1,1,1).
\]
Every integer coordinate is a point mass, and the remaining coordinate
is the indicated mixture of neighboring output integers, because the
target event indicator is fixed on these words. Their faces each contain
exactly one of the eight mandatory vertices. They are disjoint at suffix
$01$, which requires output zero in the first and one in the second.
Thus two additional generators are required, giving ten. All these
prefixes have positive supporting probability. The full-support value
twelve follows from the preceding theorem. Formula
\eqref{eq:exact-physical-target} gives the physical assertion and
$\mu_1,\mu_2\to0$ by the uniform Gaussian bound.
\end{proof}

The discontinuity concerns exact attainment. It neither contradicts
continuity of the optimal score nor prevents a lower-memory device
from approximating that score. It also explains why a support-zero
compiler cannot be transferred to the noisy physical target by simply
keeping its state count unchanged.
