\section{An intrinsic continuation invariant}
\label{sec:intrinsic29}
We first give a finite realization theorem whose input is the complete
response law, rather than a selected collection of lower certificates.
The distinction between a law and an implementation is essential:
states in a competing implementation need not themselves be residuals
of the law. The lower bound below allows all such states.

\subsection{Normalized causal residuals}
Fix a finite horizon, finite alphabets, and a list of charged cuts.
At an elementary stage the machine chooses an action $a$, then receives
a report $y$. A terminal output is an action followed by a dummy report.
Let $C(\mathbf a\mid\mathbf y)$ be a nonnegative normalized array whose
action-prefix marginal depends only on the reports preceding that prefix.
Thus an action cannot read its own future report. Every necessary buffer
cut is an elementary cut. Time-dependent rows are allowed; a persistent
seed, data-dependent clock, or protocol register is not a free input.

These conventions describe exact realization of a \emph{specified}
causal array on the full declared alphabet. For a model imposing only
almost-sure requirements, one must first specify a legal completion on
unsupported words, or optimize over those completions. Changing the
completion can change the answer. Additional autonomous row-identification
constraints are not part of the clocked theorem below.

For a positive-mass controller prefix $h=(\mathbf a_{\le t},
\mathbf y_{\le t})$, let $R_h$ be the normalized array of future actions
conditional on that prefix and on externally supplied future reports.
Positivity of the prefix refers to its marginal in $C$, with past reports
fixed, and not to a prior on environmental parameters. Write $\mathscr R_t$
for the finite set of distinct such arrays. Duplicate arrays are removed.
Let $\mathscr P_t$ be the polytope of all normalized causal arrays on the
remaining interface. Its defining equations express normalization and
nonanticipation; its inequalities express nonnegativity. Legality
restrictions are included in the response array's zero pattern. A
mandatory retained output or buffer is included through its prescribed
later readout, so that deleting it changes the continuation task.

If $R\in\mathscr R_t$ and its next action has probability $p_R(a)>0$,
then, for every next report $y$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-shift29}
 R(a\mathbf a\mid y\mathbf y)
       =p_R(a)R^{a,y}(\mathbf a\mid\mathbf y),
 \qquad R^{a,y}\in\mathscr R_{t+1}.
\end{equation}
The scalar $p_R(a)$ is independent of $y$. If $p_R(a)=0$, the entire
left side is zero. These are normalized residuals of a controller, not
posteriors in its runtime register.

\begin{definition}[Support components and their rank]\label{def:csr29}
For $R\in\mathscr R_t$, denote its minimal face in $\mathscr P_t$ by
$F_t(R)$. Join two residuals when their minimal faces intersect. Let
$\mathscr R_{t,1},\ldots,\mathscr R_{t,c_t}$ be the connected components
of this graph, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:csr29}
 d_{t,j}=\dim\operatorname{aff}\mathscr R_{t,j},\qquad
 s_t(C)=\sum_{j=1}^{c_t}(d_{t,j}+1),\qquad
 e_t(C)=\sum_{j=1}^{c_t}
       \bigl|\operatorname{ext}\operatorname{conv}\mathscr R_{t,j}\bigr|.
\end{equation}
Call the residual geometry \emph{componentwise simplicial} at $t$ if
each $\operatorname{conv}\mathscr R_{t,j}$ is a simplex, including a
point as a zero-dimensional simplex.
\end{definition}

Everything in this definition is determined by $C$ and the charged
interface. For finite rational data, face intersection is a linear
feasibility problem with the two zero patterns imposed, affine dimension
is a matrix rank, and extreme-point membership is another linear
feasibility problem. No witness subfamily or trial controller enters the
definition. Since normalized residuals lie in an affine hyperplane not
containing zero, $d_{t,j}+1$ equals their ordinary row rank.
For terminal-output channels the ambient polytope is a product of
probability simplices; two minimal faces intersect precisely when their
allowed-output sets intersect at every future input.

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic simultaneous realization]\label{thm:intrinsic29}
Every stochastic clocked realization of $C$, with all persistent
variables included in its register, has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:csr-lower29}
                         K_t\ge s_t(C)
\end{equation}
at every charged cut. A single stochastic realization has profile
$K_t=e_t(C)$ simultaneously at all cuts. If the residual geometry is
componentwise simplicial at every cut, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:csr-exact29}
             K_t^{\min}=s_t(C)=e_t(C)\quad\hbox{simultaneously},
 \qquad W^{\min}(C)=\max_t s_t(C).
\end{equation}
A realizing machine is obtained directly from residual vertices and
barycentric one-step updates. Thus the statement determines an exact
profile, not just unrelated lower bounds at individual cuts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conditioning a realizing machine on a controller prefix gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:csr-factor29}
                 R_h=\sum_s E_h(s)B_{t,s},
 \qquad E_h(s)\ge0,\quad\sum_sE_h(s)=1,
 \quad B_{t,s}\in\mathscr P_t.
\end{equation}
Here $B_{t,s}$ is the future program starting in the actual state $s$.
It may lie outside the convex hull of the residuals. If $E_h(s)>0$,
the defining property of a face implies $B_{t,s}\in F_t(R_h)$.
A state used for residuals in two different components would therefore
belong to two intersecting minimal faces, which would join those
components. This is impossible. The states used by distinct components
are disjoint. Within component $j$, the affine span of its residuals
is contained in the affine span of its used state continuations, and
hence at least $d_{t,j}+1$ states are required. Summing proves
\eqref{eq:csr-lower29}. This argument does not replace stochastic
encoders by deterministic ones.

For the upper construction, at cut $t$ choose as states all extreme
points of the component convex hulls. Every such point is an actual
residual, since the residual set is finite. Each residual is a convex
combination of these chosen states. Given a state $R$, choose its next
action with probability $p_R(a)$. For positive $p_R(a)$, represent
$R^{a,y}$ from \eqref{eq:residual-shift29} as a convex combination of
the chosen states at cut $t+1$, and use those coefficients as the update
row after report $y$. At zero $p_R(a)$ choose an arbitrary update on
the unused row. The action row remains independent of the unread
report. Initialization is the decomposition of $C$ at the first cut;
the terminal continuation is realized by its prescribed output row.

Backward induction proves the entire future array from every chosen
state. In particular, the updates at different cuts implement the same
law; no separate factorization is being spliced without checking its
shifts. This gives the profile $e_t(C)$. In the simplicial case each
component has exactly $d_{t,j}+1$ vertices, so the upper and lower
profiles coincide. Within each component the barycentric coefficients
are unique, although arbitrary minimum-size realizations need not be
unique or have states equal to residuals.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A complete, finite test for the stated class]
\label{cor:decidable29}
For a complete finite rational response array, componentwise simpliciality
and the numbers in \eqref{eq:csr-exact29} can be decided from linear
systems, rational linear feasibility, and a finite graph. When the test
succeeds, rational transition tables realizing the exact profile can be
constructed. The same holds for real algebraic input using exact sign
and linear-feasibility decisions over the specified ordered field.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate the positive-prefix normalized residuals and their zero
patterns. Solve the pairwise face-intersection problems in the stated
causal polytope, find the connected components, and find the extreme
residuals of each component hull. A component hull is a simplex exactly
when its number of vertices equals its affine dimension plus one.
Solve the barycentric systems in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic29}.
All operations are finite and preserve the indicated field.
\end{proof}

The input table can be exponentially large in the horizon. The corollary
is a completeness and synthesis result for an explicit class, not a
polynomial algorithm in a succinct program length. It does not say that
ordinary rank solves unrestricted positive realization. Nor does it
claim that residual-state minimization, by itself, minimizes over all
probabilistic machines; that last conclusion uses the separated-component
lower bound. The distinction is also present in residual automata
\cite{DenisEsposito} and nested-polytope formulations \cite{GG}.

\subsection{Exact schedule fields without selected certificates}
Consider a terminal-output channel on finitely many tapes, internally
ordered on each tape, with a full product input alphabet. At a vector
$v$ of consumed-prefix lengths let $\mathscr R_v$ consist of the
channel rows for \emph{all} prefixes at $v$ as functions of the remaining
suffixes. The rows and their minimal faces are independent of the path
used to reach $v$. Define $s(v),e(v)$ by \eqref{eq:csr29}.

\begin{corollary}[Intrinsic declared-order minimization]
\label{cor:intrinsic-schedule29}
If every component hull at every schedule vertex is a simplex, the least
width on each declared schedule $\pi$ is
$\max_{v\in\pi}s(v)$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intrinsic-schedule29}
 W^{\min}_{\rm clk}
       =\min_{\pi}\max_{v\in\pi}s(v).
\end{equation}
The minimum is computed by the bottleneck recurrence on the finite
schedule graph. The same residual-vertex construction supplies the
updates for every allowed next-tape restriction.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Restriction of an actual prefix row by any next tape symbol is an actual
row at the corresponding child vertex. Apply the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic29} on the entire schedule graph, then on each
path. The last-edge decomposition of paths gives the bottleneck
recurrence. No lower certificate or upper residual family is an extra
input to this corollary.
\end{proof}

An observation-dependent choice of tape has to be included as a
controller action in $C$. Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic29} also classifies
such a specified causal array when its normalized residual geometry
passes the same test. Equation~\eqref{eq:intrinsic-schedule29} alone
optimizes declared paths, not all adaptive arrays. A scalable experiment
for which the peak optimum is unchanged even by adaptive acquisition
is solved in Section~\ref{sec:access29}.

\subsection{Why the completeness hypothesis has content}
The condition is neither universal nor a restatement of the answer.
Consider four binary-query channels with response vectors
$(\epsilon,\epsilon),(\epsilon,1-\epsilon),
(1-\epsilon,\epsilon),(1-\epsilon,1-\epsilon)$, where
$0<\epsilon<1/4$. All output probabilities are positive, so there is one
support component. Its affine dimension is two and $s=3$, but four
states are necessary. Indeed, for each corner of the square a convex
representation of its displayed row needs a generator with total
coordinate error at most $2\epsilon<1/2$. Two distinct corner-error
sums add to at least one, so no generator can serve two corners.
Four deterministic corner generators suffice. The nonsimplicial square
therefore has state count four rather than three. This is a strict
failure of the lower bound outside its completeness class, not an
unsupported claim that the class contains every experiment.
