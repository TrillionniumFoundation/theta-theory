\section{A full-support marked saddle}
\label{sec:positive-saddle}
The support restriction of the ideal target can be removed without
replacing the private simulator or changing the training experiment.
This section identifies an explicit region of positive targets on which
both the statistical saddle and its decision-memory number are exact.
The next section shows that the original Brownian sensor belongs to this
region throughout its previously stated noise range.

Put
\[
 \mathcal R=[6/25,13/50]\times[0,1/1000].
\]
For $(\gamma,\kappa)\in\mathcal R$, define $Q_{\gamma,\kappa}$ on
$\{0,1\}^3$ by the masses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leak-target}
 \begin{array}{c|cccc|c}
 \omega&000&001&110&111&\text{each other atom}\\\hline
 Q_{\gamma,\kappa}(\omega)&a&b&b&a&\kappa
 \end{array},\qquad
 a=\frac{1+\gamma}{4}-\kappa,\quad
 b=\frac{1-\gamma}{4}-\kappa.
\end{equation}
These masses sum to one. For $\kappa>0$ every atom has positive mass.
The candidate is still $b(p,q)=\Ber(p)\otimes\Ber(q)\otimes\Ber(1/2)$;
there is no additional candidate parameter.

\begin{theorem}[Full-support saddle and contact rigidity]
\label{thm:positive-saddle}
For every $(\gamma,\kappa)\in\mathcal R$, there is a unique
$r=r_{\gamma,\kappa}$ in $(33/100,7/20)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leak-cubic}
 f(r)=r^3+(13-2\gamma+8\kappa)r^2
             +(3-12\gamma+48\kappa)r-1-2\gamma+8\kappa=0.
\end{equation}
Set $D=f'(r)$ and
\begin{align}
 t&=\frac{2\{-3r^2+(14-8\gamma-16\kappa)r-3+48\kappa\}}{D},
                                                       \label{eq:leak-tie}\\
 v&=\frac{-r^3+(7-4\gamma-8\kappa)r^2
                     +(-3+48\kappa)r+13+4\gamma-104\kappa}{32}.
                                                       \label{eq:leak-value}
\end{align}
Then $8<D<10$, $1/10<t<1/4$, and
\[
                         U_2(b,Q_{\gamma,\kappa})=v.
\]
The optimal five response rows are \eqref{eq:rigid-five}, with this
value of $t$, and zero at all off-diagonal atoms. The equal prior on
$p=q=(1\pm\sqrt r)/2$ is least favorable. The same averaged rows are
forced for every full-word minimax response. At the three interior
counts the entire deterministic event is forced word by word.

The least post-training decision width attaining $v$ is exactly five,
including in the original gate interface with arbitrary stochastic
training, stopping and independently acquired calibration.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the indicated rectangle in $(\gamma,\kappa,r)$, direct monotonicity
gives $8<f'(r)<10$. The polynomial decreases with $\gamma$ and
increases with $\kappa$. Its two endpoint evaluations are bounded by
\[
 f_{6/25,1/1000}(33/100)=-81619/5000000<0,
 \qquad f_{13/50,0}(7/20)=387/40000>0.
\]
This proves existence and uniqueness in the stated interval. The
numerator of $t$ is
$-16\gamma r-32\kappa r+96\kappa-6r^2+28r-6$.
Bounding its negative and positive terms separately gives a lower
bound $10378/10000>1$ and an upper bound $19754/10000<2$.
Together with $8<D<10$ these prove the asserted interval for $t$.

Let $u=(p+q-1)^2$, $z=(p-q)^2$, and retain the polynomials
$H_\gamma,J_\gamma$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:two-family}. The probabilities
of the five training counts give
\begin{align*}
 E(u,z)&=\frac{(u-z)^2-6u+2z+13}{4},\\
 L(u,z)&=\frac{(u-z)^2+6u-2z+1}{8}.
\end{align*}
Here $E$ is the expected cardinality of the deterministic selected
four-atom event, and $L$ is the probability of an extreme training
count. The score of the proposed response is therefore
\[
 G=H_{\gamma,\kappa}+tJ_{\gamma,\kappa},\qquad
 H_{\gamma,\kappa}=H_\gamma-\kappa E,\quad
 J_{\gamma,\kappa}=J_\gamma-\kappa L.
\]
This is an identity in the actual training probabilities, not an
estimate. On the diagonal, $J_{\gamma,\kappa}(u,0)=-f(u)/64$ and
$H_{\gamma,\kappa}(r,0)=v$. Polynomial division, using $f(r)=0$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leak-square}
 G(u,0)-v=\frac{(u-r)^2 A(\gamma,\kappa,r,u)}{16D},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
 A={}&24\gamma^2+(3\gamma-10)r^2+(6\gamma-20)ru
       +12\gamma r+6\gamma u-51\gamma+30\\
    &-384\kappa^2-96\kappa r-48\kappa u-144\kappa.
\end{align*}
For $0\le u\le1$ the negative terms alone give $A>8$ throughout
$\mathcal R$. Moreover, on $0\le u,z\le1$ the bounds in the preceding
family theorem yield
\[
 \partial_zH_{\gamma,\kappa}\ge5/32-\kappa,\qquad
 \partial_zJ_{\gamma,\kappa}\ge-3/64.
\]
Indeed the additional derivatives are respectively
$\kappa(u-z-1)/2$ and $\kappa(u-z+1)/4$.
Since $t<1/4$, one has $\partial_zG>1/8$. Thus
\eqref{eq:leak-square} is a global lower certificate on the parameter
square, with equality at exactly the two proposed prior points.

For the Bayes upper bound, group the four report bits by their total
$S$; the independent training marks and arrangements conditional on $S$
are parameter independent under this prior. Put $L_r=r^2+6r+1$.
The first three rows of $256$ times the signed coefficient table on
$000,001,110,111$ are
\begin{align*}
 S=0:
 &\quad(0,-8\gamma L_r,32r(r+1)-8\gamma L_r,32r(r+1)),\\
 S=1:
 &\quad8(r-1)\bigl(r^2+4r-1-2\gamma(r+1)+8\kappa(r+1),\\
 &\hspace{21mm}r^2+4r-1+2\gamma(r+1)+8\kappa(r+1),\\
 &\hspace{21mm}r^2-4r-1+2\gamma(r+1)+8\kappa(r+1),\\
 &\hspace{21mm}r^2-4r-1-2\gamma(r+1)+8\kappa(r+1)\bigr),\\
 S=2:
 &\quad-12(r-1)^2(r-1-2\gamma+8\kappa,
              r-1+2\gamma+8\kappa,\\
 &\hspace{37mm}r-1+2\gamma+8\kappa,r-1-2\gamma+8\kappa).
\end{align*}
The other rows are their reversals. For example the potentially small
first parenthesis in row one is at most
\[
 (7/20)^2+4(7/20)-1-2(6/25)(133/100)
                         +8(1/1000)(27/20)<-1/10.
\]
The third parenthesis is negative by the same endpoint bounds; the
second is positive and the fourth negative. In row two,
$r-1+2\gamma+8\kappa\le-61/500<0$.
Row zero's third entry is positive since
$32(33/100)(133/100)-8(13/50)((7/20)^2+6(7/20)+1)>7$.
Consequently the signs are exactly those of the previous two-preparation
saddle. At each off-diagonal atom the candidate mass on both prior
points equals $(1-r)/8>13/160$, whereas $\kappa\le1/1000$.
Every off-diagonal coefficient is strictly negative. Coordinatewise
Bayes maximization therefore selects the proposed response, including
its two indifferent coordinates. Its equal score at the prior support
closes the minimax match against the full response class.

For completeness the contact argument persists, not just the sign
pattern. If $a_0,b_0$ are the averaged extreme response coordinates,
their extra diagonal contributions are
\[
 a_0(1-p)^4\{a-(1-p)^2/2\}
       +b_0p^4\{a-p^2/2\}.
\]
At either support point their sum with $a_0=b_0=1$ is zero by
\eqref{eq:leak-cubic}; the first term is positive at $p_+$.
Contact at both supports gives $a_0=b_0$. Interior stationarity gives
$a_0=b_0=64\partial_uH_{\gamma,\kappa}(r,0)/D=t$.
All strict coordinates are forced individually. Thus the three vertices
and two disjoint faces used in Theorem~\ref{thm:five-memory} are
unchanged. That theorem's positive-factorization argument gives five
states for every admissible stochastic encoder, while the same
five-event selection attains it. The common-garbling reduction is
unchanged because the candidate interface is unchanged.
\end{proof}

The rectangle $\mathcal R$ is an explicit stability region for the
\emph{saddle and its exact decision-memory number}. It is not a claim
that arbitrary nonsymmetric total-variation perturbations have the same
optimizer. At $\kappa=0$ every formula reduces to the retained ideal
theorem. Positive off-diagonal target masses do not destroy the strict
negative Bayes coefficients at those atoms: their magnitude relative
to the candidate, rather than their being zero, is what is needed.
