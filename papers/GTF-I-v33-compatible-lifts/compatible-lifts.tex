\section{Static extensions and stochastic compatibility}\label{sec:lifts}
A polyhedral extension is a polytope followed by an affine projection.
Its size is the number of inequalities, not the number of projected
vertices. The distinction is classical \cite{Yannakakis,FRT}. We first
record exactly what simplex normalization does, and does not, provide.

\begin{proposition}[Normalization of bounded extensions]\label{prop:slack}
Every positive-dimensional bounded polytope with $f$ facets is affinely
isomorphic to a section $H\cap\Delta_f$. Thus a bounded extension with
$f$ facets can be put in simplex-section form without increasing $f$.
An affine map between two such sections need not extend to a stochastic
map between their ambient simplices.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Work in the affine hull and write an irredundant facet description
$\ell_i(u)=b_i-a_i\cdot u\ge0$, $1\le i\le f$.
Boundedness implies that the normals positively span the dual space.
Consequently there are strictly positive $w_i$ with
$\sum_i w_i a_i=0$. For example, express each $-a_i$ as a nonnegative
combination of the normals and add these $f$ dependences.
The positive constant $c=\sum_iw_i b_i$ can be evaluated at a relative
interior point, where it is a sum of positive slacks. The affine map
\[
                     u\longmapsto (w_i\ell_i(u)/c)_{i=1}^f
\]
is injective, has coordinate sum one, and sends the polytope onto the
intersection of its affine image with $\Delta_f$. Injectivity follows
because the normals span. A point polytope uses $\Delta_1$.
The last assertion is proved quantitatively in
Proposition~\ref{prop:extension-gap} below.
\end{proof}

The projection of a normalized extension is defined on its affine
section. It need not map the \emph{whole} ambient simplex into the
observation domain. Nor does normalization produce transition kernels.
These two additional requirements are not part of static extension
complexity.

\begin{theorem}[Stochastic extension criterion]\label{thm:extension-dual}
Let $Q\subset\Delta_k$ be a polytope, let $q^1,\ldots,q^r$ affinely
span $Q$, and let $F:Q\to\Delta_l$ be affine. Put $E_{hi}=q^h_i$
and $B_{hj}=F(q^h)_j$. A row-stochastic $k$ by $l$ matrix $T$ satisfying
$eT=F(e)$ on $Q$ exists if and only if, for every real $r$ by $l$ matrix
$Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 \langle Z,B\rangle\le
             \sum_{i=1}^k\max_{1\le j\le l}(E^TZ)_{ij}.
\end{equation}
For fixed rational $E,B$, feasibility and a separating certificate are
finite linear-programming problems.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The desired set of image matrices is the compact convex polytope
$\mathcal B=\{ET:T\ge0,\ T\one=\one\}$. Its support function at $Z$
is the right side of \eqref{eq:dual}, since the rows of $T$ are
independent probability vectors. Finite-dimensional separation says
$B\in\mathcal B$ exactly when all the displayed inequalities hold.
Equality on the spanning rows extends to $Q$ by affinity. This is the
ordinary support-function dual of a stochastic post-processing problem;
it is not a new minimax interchange. The additional point is that the
same test can be applied to each proposed inter-epoch lift.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[An exact extension defect]\label{prop:extension-gap}
On
\[
 Q=\{e\in\Delta_4:e_1+e_2=e_3+e_4=1/2\}
\]
the affine map $F(e)=(2e_1,2e_2)$ takes values in $\Delta_2$, but has
no ambient stochastic extension. More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quarter}
 \min_{T\ge0,\ T\one=\one}\ \max_{e\in Q}\TV(eT,F(e))=\frac14.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $u_i=T_{i1}\in[0,1]$. At the four vertices
$e^{ij}=(e_i+e_j)/2$, $i\in\{1,2\}$, $j\in\{3,4\}$, the desired
first output probability is one for $i=1$ and zero for $i=2$.
For fixed $j$, the realized probabilities differ by
$(u_1-u_2)/2\le1/2$. Their two errors must therefore sum to at least
$1/2$, and one is at least $1/4$.
Taking $(u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4)=(1,0,1/2,1/2)$ attains error $1/4$
at the vertices. Convexity bounds the error everywhere else by the
vertex maximum. This also gives a finite strict witness for
\eqref{eq:dual}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Compatible stochastic lift]\label{def:dynamic}
For the mean dynamics in \eqref{eq:array}, a lift of profile $(k_t)$
consists of nonempty sections $Q_t=H_t\cap\Delta_{k_t}$, affine
observable maps $\pi_t:Q_t\to D$, seed lifts $E_x\in Q_0$ with
$\pi_0(E_x)=x/10$, and affine maps $F_{t,c}:Q_t\to Q_{t+1}$ with
\[
             \pi_{t+1}F_{t,c}=R_\alpha^c\pi_t.
\]
Each $F_{t,c}$ must extend to an ambient row-stochastic matrix.
For each final query $j$, the map
$e\mapsto(1+e_j^T\pi_N(e))/2$ must extend to a $[0,1]$-valued affine
map on the whole simplex, equivalently to a binary stochastic decoder.
The least possible peak coordinate count is denoted $\cw_N(\alpha)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Operational meaning of a compatible lift]
\label{thm:lift-equivalence}
The complete feasible coordinate profiles of compatible stochastic lifts
are exactly the feasible hidden-state profiles of \eqref{eq:array}.
In particular, $\cw_N(\alpha)=W_N(\alpha)$. For supplied finite
sections and supplied affine lift maps, all stochastic extension
requirements, including the final decoder requirements, can be tested
by Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-dual}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A machine supplies the sections and idle-suffix maps of
Lemma~\ref{lem:reachable}. Restrict its actual transition matrices to
these sections; their observable identity holds there. The final decoder
already extends to the whole simplex.
Conversely choose the stochastic extensions and start at the specified
seed lift. Induction keeps the running distribution in $Q_t$ and its
observable image equals the desired rotated seed. The final extended
decoder then gives exactly \eqref{eq:array}. The stochastic maps at
different cuts are used together, not selected afresh after seeing a
hidden parameter. Affinely spanning points suffice for each extension
test; convexity ensures legality throughout a section.
\end{proof}

The theorem defines the dynamic object and gives a local feasibility
criterion when its geometry is fixed. It is not an efficient algorithm
for searching over all lifts. Proposition~\ref{prop:extension-gap} does
not prove that every logarithmic extension of a regular polygon is
incompatible with rotation; it proves that compatibility cannot be
inferred merely from affine positivity on the section. In particular,
we neither assume nor exclude a logarithmic hidden-state construction
for \eqref{eq:array}. Static $O(\log m)$ extensions of regular $m$-gons
\cite{FRT,VGG} remain relevant candidates, but need the inter-epoch and
readout extension tests.
