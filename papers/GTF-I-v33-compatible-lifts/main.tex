\begin{abstract}
An exact finite-horizon stochastic transducer determines a sequence of
simplex-section extensions joined by stochastic maps. Static extension
size does not certify those maps. We formulate the compatibility condition,
give its linear dual for fixed extensions, and exhibit an affine map whose
best stochastic extension has uniform error exactly $1/4$.
For a fixed-alphabet rotation experiment of separate positive rank three,
a mean-support budget improves the all-hidden-state lower bound to
$\sum_{t<N}2^{-3K_t}\le320$ at the golden angle. A resonant polygon
construction improves the exact upper width from order $N^{1/2}$ to
order $N^{1/3}$. More generally, for every irrational angle with bounded
continued-fraction coefficients, the minimum width among vector-state
realizations is of order $N^{1/3}$; the lower bound covers all time-varying
polygon chains, not only regular polygons. For general hidden realizations
the resulting bounds are logarithmic and cube-root, and need not coincide.
An elementary denominator argument gives an explicit doubly logarithmic
lower bound for the rational rotation $(3+4i)/5$. Finally, requiring a single
clock-free decoder to answer at any stopping length up to $N$ gives linear
autonomous state complexity. These results distinguish static lifts,
compatible clocked lifts, vector-state realizations, and autonomous devices
without identifying their different optimization problems.
\end{abstract}
