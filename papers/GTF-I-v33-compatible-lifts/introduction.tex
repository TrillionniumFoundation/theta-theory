\section{Introduction}
The state count of a positive realization is not determined by its linear
rank. This is a classical fact. Benvenuti and Farina constructed transfer
functions of fixed McMillan degree three with arbitrarily large minimum
positive order \cite{BF99}; subsequent work obtained matching realizations
for their family \cite[Example 2]{CJM}. There is a second, different
classical fact: a polygon with many vertices may have an extension with
only logarithmically many facets \cite{Yannakakis,FRT,VGG}.
The simultaneous finite-horizon problem lies between these observations.
Its state register supplies an extension at each epoch, but also supplies
stochastic maps joining the extensions for every allowed command.
Neither independent positive factorizations nor independent small
extensions impose this compatibility.

We study the complete binary-output experiment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:array}
 C_{N,\alpha}(b\mid x,c_1,\ldots,c_N,j)
 =\frac{1+b\,e_j^T R_\alpha^{\,c_1+\cdots+c_N}x/10}{2},
\end{equation}
where $x\in\{-1,1\}^2$, $c_t\in\{0,1\}$, $j\in\{1,2\}$ and
$b\in\{-1,1\}$. Here $R_\alpha$ is rotation through $2\pi\alpha$.
All alphabets are fixed. All probabilities lie in the fixed interval
\[
 \left[\frac{1-\sqrt2/10}{2},\frac{1+\sqrt2/10}{2}\right].
\]
Equality is required for every word, not just in expectation over an
input prior. The separate positive minimum at every internal cut is three
(Proposition~\ref{prop:separate}). The question is the peak number of
labels needed by one compatible machine.

\subsection{The quantitative distinction}
Write $W_N(\alpha)$ for unrestricted clocked positive-realization width.
Write $V_N(\alpha)$ for its vector-state restriction, in which every state
has a predictive vector and each stochastic command row intertwines those
vectors, not merely their reachable affine combinations. These are defined
in Section~\ref{sec:model}. Let $A_N(\alpha)$ be the number of states in a
clock-free machine with one fixed decoder that answers a terminating query
at any command length from zero through $N$. The last task has a stronger
query-time specification, which is part of its definition.

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative width distinctions]\label{thm:main}
Let $\alpha$ be irrational, and suppose all its continued-fraction partial
quotients are at most an integer $A\ge1$. Let $q_N$ be the first convergent
denominator at least $\max\{5,(40N)^{1/3}\}$. For $N\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 \max\!\left\{3,\frac13\log_2\frac{N}{160(A+1)}\right\}
 &\le W_N(\alpha)\le V_N(\alpha)\le q_N,
                                                        \label{eq:main-hidden}\\
 \max\!\left\{3,\left(\frac{N}{160(A+1)}\right)^{1/3}\right\}
 &\le V_N(\alpha)<(A+1)\max\{5,(40N)^{1/3}\}.             \label{eq:main-vector}
\end{align}
Thus $V_N(\alpha)=\Theta_\alpha(N^{1/3})$. In addition, for every
infinite-order planar rotation, without the bounded-type assumption,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-autonomous}
             N+1\le A_N(\alpha)\le3(N+1)+2.
\end{equation}
At the golden angle $\alpha=(\sqrt5-1)/2$, every exact hidden-state
profile satisfies $\sum_{t<N}2^{-3K_t}\le320$; every vector-state
profile satisfies $\sum_{t<N}K_t^{-3}\le320$.
\end{theorem}

The hidden-state lower bound is not replaced by the stronger vector-state
bound. A section of a $K$-coordinate simplex can project to many more than
$K$ vertices. This distinction is indispensable: regular polygons have
small static extensions, and an unproved restriction to vector states
would discard precisely the possible hidden savings.
Theorem~\ref{thm:lift-equivalence} specifies the additional stochastic
compatibility required of a sequence of extensions. Theorem~\ref{thm:extension-dual}
checks it by a finite linear program when the extensions and the proposed
lifted affine maps have been supplied. It does not optimize over all
extensions efficiently.

For the rational rotation $R=(1/5)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&-4\\4&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:rational} gives the explicit necessary condition
\[
             N\le140\,2^{2W_N}\,5^{8\,2^{W_N}}.
\]
It requires neither quantifier elimination nor an unproved bounded-type
property of $\arg(3+4i)/(2\pi)$. Its growth rate is deliberately
separated from the sharper bounded-type statement.

\subsection{Mechanism and scope}
For an observable polygon $P$, use the mean support
$\mw(P)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int h_P$, rather than its area.
The one-step gain for $\conv(P\cup R_\alpha P)$ accumulates linearly
along a finite orbit. Comparing that orbit hull with the optimal perimeter
of an inscribed polygon gives a gain of order $M^{-3}$ for at most $M$
vertices and bounded-type angles. No squaring of a Hausdorff displacement
is needed. The matching vector-state upper bound aligns regular polygons
with convergents of the angle; their required radial expansion per step
is then of order $M^{-3}$ rather than $M^{-2}$.

All exact clocked counts use atomic stochastic rows, an external epoch,
and known read-only tables. They do not count arithmetic or table
construction. The new autonomous comparison removes the external epoch
and fixes the decoder, but still uses the same atomic-row convention.
The old rational fair-bit compiler remains a separately priced
implementation theorem \cite{GTF31}. No table-free bit-space claim is made.
