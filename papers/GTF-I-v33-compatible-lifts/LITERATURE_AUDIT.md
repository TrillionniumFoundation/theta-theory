## Direct positive-realization comparisons

**Benvenuti–Farina (1999).** *An example of how positivity may force realizations of “large” dimension*, Systems & Control Letters 36, 261–266, DOI 10.1016/S0167-6911(98)00098-X. Publication and attribution were verified. The original publisher full text was not obtained in this session. The detailed family and its lower bound were inspected in the authors' full 2003 survey (p. 223) and in the full matching-order paper below. This original result is now cited directly in the introduction; fixed degree three versus arbitrarily large positive order is not claimed new.

**Benvenuti–Farina (2003), full PDF inspected.** *Minimal positive realizations: A survey of recent results and open problems*, Kybernetika 39, 217–228. URL: https://www.kybernetika.cz/content/2003/2/217/paper.pdf . Section 3, Example 4 gives positive Hankel factorization without an equally small positive realization; Theorem 2 includes an intertwining matrix in the minimality criterion. These are stationary transfer-function statements. Their conceptual compatibility distinction is credited. The v33 finite-word profile inequalities permit cut-dependent stochastic rows and do not follow just by truncating a repeated-matrix order bound.

**Czaja–Jaming–Matolcsi, full arXiv PDF inspected.** *An efficient algorithm for positive realizations*, Systems & Control Letters 57 (2008), 436–441, DOI 10.1016/j.sysconle.2007.11.001; arXiv:math/0612551v2. Example 2, equation (3.1), states the degree-three family H_m and proves exact positive order m for m>=4. The paper's Lemma 2.1 and Section 4 distinguish constructive and lower-bound mechanisms. The present paper does not claim to improve that exact stationary result. Correct author names are Wojciech Czaja, Philippe Jaming and Mate Matolcsi.

**Benvenuti–Farina (2004), relevant full-text portions inspected.** *A tutorial on the positive realization problem*, IEEE TAC 49, 651–664. The invariant-cone and peripheral-spectrum principles are classical. A copy inspected was https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~sussmann/papers/res-farina-tutorial-positive-realization.pdf . They supply background for both the inherited reachable-section interpretation and the new finite anytime comparison, not a claimed new stationary existence principle.

## Extension complexity

**Yannakakis (1991).** *Expressing combinatorial optimization problems by linear programs*, JCSS 43, 441–466, DOI 10.1016/0022-0000(91)90024-Y. The slack-matrix/nonnegative-rank theorem is explicitly credited. The original paper was not claimed exhaustively re-proved or audited; its precise statement and role were checked in Theorem 1 and the surrounding argument of the full FRT paper below.

**Fiorini–Rothvoss–Tiwary (2012), full PDF inspected.** *Extended formulations for polygons*, DCG 48, 658–668, DOI 10.1007/s00454-012-9421-9; arXiv:1107.0371v2. Theorem 1 records slack-factorization equivalence; Theorem 2 gives O(log m) extensions for regular m-gons, tracing the result to Ben-Tal–Nemirovski (2001). The introductory face-count argument explains the classical 2^K estimate. Static facet count alone does not impose the ambient stochastic-extension constraints of v33.

**Vandaele–Gillis–Glineur (2017), full PDF inspected.** *On the linear extension complexity of regular n-gons*, LAA 521, 217–239; arXiv:1505.08031v2; DOI 10.1016/j.laa.2016.12.023. This supplies the requested subsequent regular-polygon slack-factorization comparison. We do not claim the direct vertex construction is optimal among hidden lifted realizations.

**Ben-Tal–Nemirovski (2001).** *On polyhedral approximations of the second-order cone*, Mathematics of Operations Research 26, 193–205. Its historical role is credited as documented in FRT, not presented as a separately completed full-text audit.

The new operational definition uses bounded simplex sections, seed lifts, stochastic extensions of the inter-epoch maps and a stochastic extension of the final binary decoder. The fixed-lift dual is an ordinary support-function separation argument. The explicit 1/4 defect demonstrates that positivity only on a section is insufficient; it does not rule out all possible small dynamic lifts of regular polygons.

## Precise contribution boundary

The proposed new quantitative content is the integrated mean-support accumulation bound, its continued-fraction profile law, the resonant exact cube-root construction, and their matching order for all vector-state chains. The rational arithmetic rate and finite anytime comparison are additional consequences. Ordinary extension complexity, static rank gaps, invariant cones, positive-Hankel compatibility, spectral root-of-unity restrictions and weighted sampling are explicitly classical.

The full hidden-state growth rate, a general search algorithm for compatible lifts, noisy-tag profile composition, and independent analytic pipeline closure are not claimed. Successful compilation and finite checks do not decide originality or journal significance.
