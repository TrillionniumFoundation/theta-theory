\section{Round-twelve positive closure: positive slice measures, evidence cones, and controlled belief limits}
\label{sec:r12-c1}

Oriented collision currents and positive posterior measures are separated.
Evidence is the mass of a positive disintegration measure, never the pairing of
a positive-dimensional current with the scalar $1$.  Zero evidence is handled
by a compact cone over a tight set of probability directions.

\subsection{Definable observation strata and positive slices}

Fix a finite decision horizon and a finite family of real-analytic, globally
subanalytic observation maps $O_k^u$.  Together with the hard-sphere collision
faces they generate a locally finite Whitney stratification
$\mathscr S_k$.  On each stratum, the predicted law has a positive coefficient
measure $\mu_S$ and a separate oriented normal current $J_S$ encoding the
transport/collision geometry.

\begin{theorem}[Positive coarea disintegration]
\label{thm:r12-c1-slice}
For every regular stratum map $O:S\to\mathbb R^d$ there are a positive evidence
measure $\zeta_O$ and positive conditional probability measures
$\nu_{O,y}$ such that
\[
 \int_SF\,d\mu_S
 =\int\left(\int_{O^{-1}(y)}F\,d\nu_{O,y}\right)d\zeta_O(y).
\]
The oriented slice $\langle J_S,O,y\rangle$ is retained as an additional
geometric coordinate but is not used as a posterior mass.  Critical strata
are disintegrated at their actual rank and inserted as lower-rank positive
components.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the coarea theorem to the positive rectifiable coefficient measure in
each constant-rank chart, including its Jacobian in $\zeta_O$.  Rokhlin
disintegration normalizes the fiber measures.  Federer slicing of the oriented
current supplies the independent geometric coordinate.  Global
subanalyticity gives a finite Whitney decomposition on each chronological
cell; Hardt triviality makes the refinements locally finite through the fixed
number of decisions.  Summing strata proves the formula.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The compact evidence cone}

Let
\[
 \mathcal K_M=\{\nu\in\mathcal P: \nu(W)\le M\}
\]
with the weighted weak topology; it is compact by the common Lyapunov moment
bound.  Define the cone
\[
 \operatorname{Cone}(\mathcal K_M)
 =([0,1]\times\mathcal K_M)/(\{0\}\times\mathcal K_M),
\]
where $r=Z/(1+Z)$ is the evidence radius.  To retain the approach direction at
zero, use the real oriented blow-up
$[0,1]\times\mathcal K_M$ and let the transition act on both coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Feller evidence-direction kernel]
\label{thm:r12-c1-feller}
Prediction followed by the positive disintegration of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r12-c1-slice} defines a Feller kernel on the compact blown-up
state
\[
 (r,\nu,(J_S)_S)\in[0,1]\times\mathcal K_M\times\mathcal J_M.
\]
At $r=0$ the next state is determined by the retained probability direction
$\nu$ and its predicted positive slice; if that slice again has zero mass, the
same blow-up rule is iterated.  The kernel is independent of the approximating
positive-evidence sequence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The B2 prediction semigroup preserves positivity and satisfies the common
weighted Lyapunov bound.  Integrated coarea disintegration is continuous for
bounded continuous tests after summing the finitely many rank strata; pointwise
continuity of an individual fiber is not asserted.  Normalize only when the
new evidence is positive.  If it tends to zero, divide before taking the limit
and retain the direction in the compact set $\mathcal K_M$.  Continuity of
prediction and integrated slices makes this limit unique.  Normal-current
compactness with fixed stratum dimension and the chronological local-finiteness
bound gives compactness of $\mathcal J_M$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Inserted observation coefficients}

The observation statistics used below are affine continuous functionals of the
particle path and actual-contact measure.  Their source is therefore a finite
additional coordinate of the B2 marked pressure.

\begin{theorem}[Source-uniform inserted coarea coefficient]
\label{thm:r12-c1-coefficient}
On every regular observation chart, the exact joint number--constraint--
observation coefficient has the B1 relative local-limit expansion, uniformly
with one bounded future insertion.  Numerator and denominator use their own
source-dependent saddles.  The denominator is zero-free on a common complex
neighborhood of the real regular chart, and their ratio converges to the
conditional prepared path law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Adjoin the observation coordinates to the full B2 pressure, not to independent
spatial blocks.  The affine-span condition is the nondegeneracy of the joint
pressure Hessian.  B1's compact-annulus and high-frequency pressure gaps apply
to this enlarged finite vector.  A bounded insertion is represented by a
central marked transfer derivative and has the same Fourier error estimates.
Separate saddle equations are solved for numerator and denominator.  The
positive real Gaussian coefficient has a uniform lower bound on the regular
chart; Rouch\'e's theorem and the relative error exclude nearby complex zeros.
Division gives the ratio theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reachable quantitative chaos and dynamic programming}

Fix a complex source radius $r_*>0$.  Let $\mathfrak R_\varepsilon(r_*,M)$ be
the set of belief laws whose factorial cumulant generating functional is
analytic on that radius, has norm at most $M$, and whose evidence direction
lies in $\mathcal K_M$.  This class is defined before any filtering limit.

\begin{lemma}[Invariant reachable class]
\label{lem:r12-c1-chaos}
For short time and the admissible observation/control family,
$\mathfrak R_\varepsilon(r_*,M)$ is invariant after increasing $M$ by a fixed
factor.  If $\kappa_{
m fin}$ denotes the finitely retained correlation
coordinates, then the full and reduced prediction--observation kernels admit a
coupling with weighted Wasserstein error $o(1)$ uniformly over the class and
over strategies.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
B2 normal source convergence and Theorem~\ref{thm:r12-c1-coefficient} preserve
a smaller common analytic radius under prediction and Bayes division.
Cauchy estimates give the factorial cumulant bounds.  Exponential moment
tightness makes the cumulants moment determining.  Couple laws by matching the
retained cumulants and use the analytic generating functional to bound the
remaining test discrepancy by the tail of its absolutely convergent series.
All constants are uniform on the compact control set.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Belief DPP and reduced game]
\label{thm:r12-c1-dpp}
The exact belief state has a Feller dynamic programming principle for one-time
preparation, reward control, and adaptive canonical actual-contact control.
On $\mathfrak R_\varepsilon(r_*,M)$ the reduced finite-correlation game value
converges uniformly to the full belief value.  The error after $N$ decision
stages is at most $C_N$ times the one-step Wasserstein error.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Ionescu--Tulcea constructs the law from the Feller belief kernel and the
nonanticipative strategies.  Conditioning at a decision time gives the exact
DPP.  Bounded Lipschitz rewards and transition kernels propagate a one-step
Wasserstein error by the Bellman contraction; induction gives the displayed
finite-horizon error.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An explicit posterior tangent}

Let $\vartheta\in\mathbb R^p$ parameterize one identifiable regular phase,
with a prior density continuous and positive near $\vartheta_0$.  Assume the
joint observation coefficient above has nonsingular information matrix
$\mathcal I(\vartheta_0)$ and the parameter stays a fixed distance from phase
boundaries.

\begin{theorem}[Controlled LAN and Bernstein--von Mises]
\label{thm:r12-c1-bvm}
Uniformly over admissible strategies and bounded local alternatives,
\[
 \log{d\mathbb P_{\vartheta_0+h/\sqrt\mu}\over
          d\mathbb P_{\vartheta_0}}
 =h^T\Delta_\varepsilon-	frac12h^T\mathcal Ih+o_{\mathbb P}(1),
 \qquad
 \Delta_\varepsilon\Rightarrow N(0,\mathcal I).
\]
The posterior of $\sqrt\mu(\vartheta-\widehat\vartheta_\varepsilon)$ converges
in total variation to $N(0,\mathcal I^{-1})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the exact inserted coefficient at the finite saddle; B3 supplies
the joint Gaussian score and its information matrix.  The common complex
radius gives a uniform cubic remainder.  Identifiability and the global
observation LDP make posterior mass outside a shrinking regular neighborhood
exponentially small.  On that neighborhood, change variables
$h=\sqrt\mu(\vartheta-\vartheta_0)$ and apply dominated Laplace convergence
using the positive continuous prior.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-twelve C1 closure]
\label{thm:r12-c1-main}
The controlled hard-sphere filter has a well-typed positive sliced-measure
state, compact zero-evidence blow-up, Feller DPP, inserted local coefficient,
quantitative reachable-chaos reduction, and the posterior tangent of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r12-c1-bvm}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r12-c1-slice},
\ref{thm:r12-c1-feller}, \ref{thm:r12-c1-coefficient},
\ref{thm:r12-c1-dpp}, and \ref{thm:r12-c1-bvm}.
\end{proof}
