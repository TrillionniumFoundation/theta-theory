\section{Round-four referee closure: three distinct games}
\label{sec:r3-c1}

This section replaces the single equation which mixed one-time preparation,
reward control, and adaptive law control.  Each game has its own timing,
strategy class, and dynamic equation.  Complete microscopic observation is
stated as a deterministic information reduction, not as a no-go principle.

\subsection{Fixed kinetic phases}

Let \(S_{s,t}^z\) be the B4 kinetic semigroup for a fixed prepared phase
\(z\).  The parameter may contain the source-dependent initial multiplier,
a B3 cotangent class, and a phase label.  The map \(z\mapsto S_{s,t}^z\Phi\)
is analytic on every regular source chart and continuous on its compact
closure.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform phase regularity]
\label{lem:r3-c1-phase}
For a smooth finite-dimensional phase manifold \(Z\) and a bounded terminal
cylinder \(\Phi\), the first two \(z\)-derivatives of
\(S_{s,t}^z\Phi\) exist locally uniformly.  Their fixed-phase Hessian is the
B3 pressure covariance, with the B1 preparation Schur complement in initial
directions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
B2 gives a normally convergent complex source expansion and B4 passes it to
the kinetic semigroup.  Cauchy estimates give uniform first and second phase
derivatives.  B3 identifies the second derivative with the correctly
normalized joint covariance, and B1 identifies the constrained initial block
with its Schur complement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Game I: one-time preparation}

Let \(U,V\) be compact action sets and let
\(z:U\times V\to Z\) be continuous.  Both actions are chosen at time zero.
Define
\[
 V_{s,t}^{\rm prep}\Phi(f)
 =\sup_{u\in U}\inf_{v\in V}
 S_{s,t}^{z(u,v)}\Phi(f).
\]

\begin{theorem}[One-time preparation game]
\label{thm:r3-c1-prep}
The displayed envelope is the complete dynamic value of the one-time game.
For \(r<s<t\), its correct composition is
\[
 \sup_u\inf_v
 S_{r,s}^{z(u,v)}S_{s,t}^{z(u,v)}\Phi;
\]
the same pair \((u,v)\) is retained across both factors.  In general
\(V_{r,s}^{\rm prep}V_{s,t}^{\rm prep}\ne V_{r,t}^{\rm prep}\), and no
pointwise Isaacs Hamiltonian represents this timing unless the phase is added
to the state and frozen.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After the time-zero choices, the path law is the fixed phase
\(z(u,v)\), so B4 gives the first expression and its fixed-phase tower.
Allowing a new optimization at time \(s\) enlarges the strategy class and is a
different game, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

For a finite action set the value is an upper--lower envelope of finitely many
smooth branches.  It is generally nonsmooth at branch crossings.  The smooth
saddle calculus below is not applied there.

\subsection{Game II: reward-only feedback}

Fix one phase \(z\).  Let compact controls \(u_t,v_t\) be predictable with
respect to the resolved history and let \(c(f,u,v)\) be a bounded continuous
running work.  Controls do not change \(A_f\), the elastic collision map, or
the prepared phase.

\begin{theorem}[Reward-only Isaacs equation]
\label{thm:r3-c1-reward}
The upper--lower value is the unique B4 viscosity solution of
\[
 \partial_tU+\mathbb H_z\left(f,\frac{\delta U}{\delta f},0\right)
 +\sup_u\inf_v c(f,u,v)=0,
 \qquad U(T,f)=\Phi(f).
\]
The Hamiltonian part is fixed; only the reward is optimized.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On a time partition, fixed-phase dynamic programming and measurable selection
give the discrete recursion.  The running work adds linearly to the B4 action.
Tightness and the B4 comparison theorem pass the mesh to zero.  Since the
transition action is independent of the controls, minimax acts only on
\(c\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Game III: exact block-normalized canonical law control}

An adaptive control changes the canonical weight of deterministic hard-sphere
paths; it does not postulate a point-process compensator.  Let
\(0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=T\), let \(G_{t_j}^\varepsilon\) be the complete
correlation state, and let \(\mathcal F_{t_j}^{\rm res}\) be the resolved
history available to the strategies.  For a bounded collision source \(\psi\)
define the exact conditional normalizer
\[
 K_{\varepsilon,j}(G_{t_j}^\varepsilon,\psi)
 =\frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\mathbb E\left[
 e^{\mu_\varepsilon\langle\psi,
 \Gamma_{(t_j,t_{j+1}]}^\varepsilon\rangle}
 \middle|G_{t_j}^\varepsilon\right].
\]
For a predictable compact family
\(\psi^{u,v}=\log q^{u,v}\),
\(0<q_-\le q^{u,v}\le q_+\), set
\[
 \frac{Z_{j+1}^\varepsilon}{Z_j^\varepsilon}
 =\exp\left\{
 \mu_\varepsilon\langle\psi^{u_j,v_j},
 \Gamma_{(t_j,t_{j+1}]}^\varepsilon\rangle
 -\mu_\varepsilon
 K_{\varepsilon,j}(G_{t_j}^\varepsilon,
 \psi^{u_j,v_j})\right\}.
\]
Then \(Z_j^\varepsilon\) is an exact mean-one martingale under every
nonanticipative strategy.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform conditional source chart]
\label{lem:r4-c1-conditional-chart}
On every compact regular correlation set and compact control family,
\[
 K_{\varepsilon,j}(G,\psi)
 \longrightarrow K_{\Delta_j}(G,\psi)
\]
locally uniformly with two derivatives in the resolved cylinder coordinates,
uniformly over all past strategies.  As \(\Delta_j\downarrow0\),
\[
 \frac1{\Delta_j}
 \left(K_{\Delta_j}(G,\psi+\Delta p)-K_{\Delta_j}(G,\psi)
 \right)
 \longrightarrow
 \langle f,v\cdot\nabla p\rangle
 +\int e^\psi(e^{\Delta p}-1)dA_f.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conditioning on the complete correlation state turns the next block into the
same local hierarchy problem used in B2, with an incoming norm bounded on the
compact regular set.  B2 bounded-source continuation gives a complex chart
whose radius and cluster majorant depend only on that bound and on
\(\sup|\psi|\), not on how the state was reached.  Therefore the convergence
and its derivatives are uniform over strategy histories.  The short-block
Duhamel expansion has one free-transport term and one actual-contact term;
after subtracting the block normalizer at \(p=0\), division by
\(\Delta_j\) gives the displayed Hamiltonian uniformly.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive canonical-law Isaacs game]
\label{thm:r3-c1-adaptive}
For Elliott--Kalton strategies on compact action sets satisfying the Isaacs
condition, first \(\varepsilon\to0\) and then mesh \(\to0\) gives the unique
bounded B4 viscosity solution of
\[
 \partial_tU+\sup_u\inf_v
 \left\{
 \langle f,v\cdot\nabla p\rangle
 +\int q^{u,v}(e^{\Delta p}-1)dA_f+c(f,u,v)
 \right\}=0,
 \qquad p=\frac{\delta U}{\delta f}.
\]
The controlled dynamic action is
\[
 \int\ell\left(
 \frac{d\Gamma}{q^{u,v}dA_f}
 \right)q^{u,v}dA_f.
\]
The microscopic sphere flow remains deterministic and elastic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exact conditional normalizers make each finite-mesh controlled law a
probability measure and give an exact discrete dynamic programming recursion
on the complete correlation state.  Lemma
\ref{lem:r4-c1-conditional-chart} proves consistency, uniformly over
nonanticipative strategies.  The conditional B2 joint LDP under the block tilt
has reference contact intensity \(q^{u,v}A_f\); exponential/entropy conjugacy
gives the action.  B4 energy compact containment and comparison give stability
of the upper and lower half-relaxed limits.  Isaacs equality identifies them.
No finite-volume compensator formula is used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Smooth saddle envelopes and discrete envelopes}

Let \(z=(u,v)\) lie in a smooth product manifold near a base phase.  Let
\[
 G(\phi,z)
 =\vartheta^{-1}\log\mathbb E_z e^{\vartheta\phi}+k(z),
\]
where \(k\) is strictly concave in the maximizing coordinates and strictly
convex in the minimizing coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[Model verification of saddle invertibility]
\label{lem:r3-c1-invertible}
If the signed curvature of \(k\) dominates the operator norm of the B3 phase
covariance on a regular source chart, then \(G_z=0\) has a unique local saddle
\(z^*(\phi)\), and the signed Hessian \(G_{zz}\) is invertible with a uniform
bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-c1-phase} bounds the pressure Hessian.  Adding the strictly
signed Hessian of \(k\) gives a uniformly negative block in maximizing
directions and a uniformly positive block in minimizing directions.  The
strong monotonicity estimate gives uniqueness, and the implicit-function
theorem gives the smooth saddle branch and inverse bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Verified optimizer-response Hessian]
\label{thm:r3-c1-schur}
On the smooth saddle branch of
Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-c1-invertible},
\[
 DV(\phi)[h]=\mathbb E_{Q_{\phi,z^*}}h
\]
and
\[
 D^2V(\phi)[h,k]
 =\vartheta\operatorname{Cov}_{Q_{\phi,z^*}}(h,k)
 -G_{\phi z}[h]G_{zz}^{-1}G_{z\phi}[k].
\]
For a finite preparation set, the correct derivative is the directional
envelope over active branches; the above Hessian applies only where one smooth
branch is uniquely active.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the verified equation
\(G_z(\phi,z^*(\phi))=0\) to obtain
\(Dz^*=-G_{zz}^{-1}G_{z\phi}\).  The first envelope derivative and one further
differentiation give the formula.  Finite envelopes follow from taking active
one-sided branch derivatives.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Information reduction, testing exponents, and local asymptotic normality}

Let \((\mathbb P_z^\varepsilon)_{z\in Z}\) be a compact regular family of
prepared path laws observed through the resolved filtration
\(\mathcal G_t^\varepsilon\).  Write
\(L_{z,z_0}^\varepsilon=d\mathbb P_z^\varepsilon/
 d\mathbb P_{z_0}^\varepsilon\) on \(\mathcal G_T^\varepsilon\).  The
posterior is \(\rho_T^\varepsilon(dz)\).

Define the path relative-entropy rate
\[
 \mathcal K_T(z_0\Vert z)
 =-\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}
 \frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \mathbb E_{z_0}\log L_{z,z_0}^\varepsilon
\]
and the Chernoff rate
\[
 \mathcal C_T(z_0,z)
 =-\inf_{0\le\alpha\le1}
 \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}
 \frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\mathbb E_{z_0}
 (L_{z,z_0}^\varepsilon)^\alpha.
\]
B2 real-source continuation makes both limits finite on regular compact phase
sets and strictly positive for separated phases.

\begin{theorem}[Almost-sure odds and expected posterior selection]
\label{thm:r3-c1-filter}
If \(z_0\) is the true phase, then for each fixed separated \(z\),
\[
 \frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\frac{\rho_T^\varepsilon(dz)}
 {\rho_T^\varepsilon(dz_0)}
 \longrightarrow-\mathcal K_T(z_0\Vert z)
\]
in \(\mathbb P_{z_0}\)-probability, and almost surely along every summable
subsequence of error probabilities.  For every neighborhood \(O\ni z_0\),
\[
 \limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
 \frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\mathbb E_{z_0}\rho_T^\varepsilon(O^c)
 \le-\inf_{z\notin O}\mathcal C_T(z_0,z)<0.
\]
Thus typical log odds have the KL rate, whereas expected posterior error has
the correct testing/Chernoff exponent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Bayes' formula writes posterior odds as the prior ratio times
\(L_{z,z_0}^\varepsilon\).  The B2 pressure law of large numbers for the log
likelihood gives the first limit; exponential concentration and
Borel--Cantelli give the subsequence statement.

For the expectation, use
\(x/(1+x)\le x^\alpha\) for \(0\le\alpha\le1\), integrate the posterior odds,
and optimize \(\alpha\).  A finite cover of \(O^c\) and compact-source
uniformity give the infimum of the Chernoff rates.  This argument controls the
rare likelihood events which prevent replacing the testing exponent by KL.
\end{proof}

For a smooth \(r\)-dimensional phase chart, put
\[
 m_{\varepsilon,z}=DQ_\varepsilon(z),
 \qquad
 \Delta_{\varepsilon,z}
 =\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}
 (\mathcal X_\varepsilon-m_{\varepsilon,z}).
\]

\begin{theorem}[LAN and Bernstein--von Mises tangent]
\label{thm:r4-c1-lan}
Uniformly for \(h\) in compact subsets of \(\mathbb R^r\),
\[
 \log L_{z_0+h/\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon},z_0}^\varepsilon
 =h\cdot\Delta_{\varepsilon,z_0}
 -\frac12h^T\mathcal I(z_0)h+o_{\mathbb P}(1),
\]
where \(\Delta_{\varepsilon,z_0}\Rightarrow
N(0,\mathcal I(z_0))\) by B3 and
\(\mathcal I(z_0)=D^2Q(z_0)\) on the identifiable quotient.  For a prior with
positive continuous density at \(z_0\), the posterior of
\(\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}(z-z_0)\) converges in total variation on compact
sets to the Gaussian with covariance \(\mathcal I(z_0)^{-1}\) and random mean
\(\mathcal I(z_0)^{-1}\Delta\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the exact finite-volume exponential likelihood and Taylor-expand
\(Q_\varepsilon\) at \(z_0\).  Centering at
\(DQ_\varepsilon(z_0)\), not at its limit, cancels the linear normalizing
term exactly.  Cauchy bounds on a larger complex chart make the cubic remainder
uniformly \(O(\mu_\varepsilon^{-1/2}|h|^3)\).  B3 gives the score CLT and
positive quotient information.  The LAN expansion, exponential tail control
from identifiability, and Laplace's method give the posterior Gaussian limit.
\end{proof}

For a finite phase family, the exact Markov information state is
\((\rho_t^\varepsilon,G_t^\varepsilon)\): posterior weights together with the
current resolved correlation/history state needed to evaluate future
conditional likelihoods.  The weight vector alone is sufficient only when the
observation kernel is itself Markov in those weights.  Under complete
microscopic observation, \(G_t^\varepsilon\) determines the future regular
trajectory and the continuation is deterministic.

\begin{theorem}[Round-four C1 closure]
\label{thm:r3-c1-main}
One-time preparation, reward-only feedback, and adaptive canonical-law control
have the distinct values and equations proved above.  Smooth and discrete
saddles are not conflated, and coarse phase information has an exponential
posterior contraction theorem.  Complete microscopic observation is handled
by deterministic continuation rather than by introducing artificial noise.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r3-c1-prep},
\ref{thm:r3-c1-reward}, \ref{thm:r3-c1-adaptive},
\ref{thm:r3-c1-schur}, and \ref{thm:r3-c1-filter}.
\end{proof}
