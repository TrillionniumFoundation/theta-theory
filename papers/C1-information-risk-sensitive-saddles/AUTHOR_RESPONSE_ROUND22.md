# Author response to Referee Round Twenty-Two — C1

C1 has been rebuilt around the fundamental distinction between deterministic hidden dynamics and dominated observations.

## C1.1 — no common domination of hidden transitions

The hidden kernel is exactly the Dirac push-forward `delta_{Phi_theta^a(x)}`.  It is not assigned a density and no fixed probability is claimed to dominate the family.  Only the observation kernel is dominated, stratum by stratum, by counting, surface, or Lebesgue reference measures.

## C1.2 — model-derived observation charts

The observation density is obtained by keeping the hidden entrance history as an insertion in the A2 Fourier calculation or the B1 exact-number block calculation.  Their integrable majorants and boundary-flat densities give `L^1` continuity and quadratic-mean derivatives uniformly on prepared compact shells.  An aggregate LLT is not used as a substitute for a conditional kernel theorem.

## C1.3 — small and zero evidence

The Bayes map is defined only where the evidence is positive and assigned an arbitrary value on the zero-evidence set, which has zero transition weight.  Filter continuity is proved after integration by the identity

`r_n |N_n/r_n - N/r| <= |N_n-N| + ||phi|| |r_n-r|`.

This works on infinite reference strata and does not invoke the invalid bound `delta nu(Y)`.

## C1.4 — finite coordinates

A finite bounded-Lipschitz coordinate map is paired with a partition-of-unity reconstruction taking convex combinations of finitely many beliefs.  The reconstruction always lands in the convex belief space `P(X)` and uniformly approximates the compact reachable set.  No retraction into an arbitrary nonconvex compact set is claimed.

## C1.5 — adaptive LAN/BvM

Uniformity is stated for the explicitly defined persistently excited policy class.  Conditional Fisher matrices have a uniform positive lower bound, scores satisfy a conditional Lindeberg condition, third derivatives have a common envelope, and fixed-distance alternatives are uniformly separated.  An uninformative policy is outside that class rather than being assigned positive information by compactness.

The positive Feller, risk-sensitive, finite-coordinate, LAN, and Bernstein--von Mises conclusions are retained with these corrected types and hypotheses.
