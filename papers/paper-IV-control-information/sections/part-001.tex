
\begin{abstract}
We extend the fixed-law theta-semigroup of Paper III to controlled and
partially observed values without confusing deterministic microscopic
predictability with coarse statistical uncertainty.  A complete Sinai
collision state determines the future orbit; a nontrivial dynamic program
arises only after the information state forgets stable/future coordinates or
introduces a declared hidden subsystem.  We formulate an exact-observation
no-go theorem and keep the canonical past-state kernel as the base sufficient
statistic.

For controls acting on the slow drift and work, but not on the fast billiard
geometry or current preparation, the limiting risk-sensitive Isaacs equation
retains the billiard-derived coefficients $D_{R,q}$ and $q$.  Quadratic actuator
energies yield a unique pure saddle on closed, possibly noncompact, convex
control sets.  A second-order saddle-envelope theorem shows that terminal
second variations contain both the fixed-policy covariance and an
optimizer-response Schur complement.  This closes the type gap between
fixed-law tangent theory and controlled theta-games.  A deterministic
refresh--autoregressive product extension supplies a separate soft-filter
branch; exact high-rate pre-action observation is classified by a no-go
result rather than silently collapsed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Information is part of the microscopic model}\label{sec:information}
Let $z\in M_R$ be the full billiard collision state.  Once $z$ is known, all
future collisions are determined by $T_R$.  The canonical quotient state
$\xi\in\Xi_R$ of Paper III remembers only the declared past/coarse coordinate;
its future conditional law is the Doob kernel $K_{R,q}$.

\begin{theorem}[Full-state observation no-go]\label{thm:full-no-go}
Suppose a controller observes the full collision state before choosing an
action and the one-step reward contains $u\,G(z)$ with
$u\in\{-1,1\}$.  Then
\[
 \int\sup_u uG(z)\,d\mu_{R,q}(z)
 =\int\abs{G(z)}\,d\mu_{R,q}(z)>0,
\]
whereas a controller restricted to a state independent of the current fast
variable obtains
\[
 \sup_u u\int G\,d\mu_{R,q}=0.
\]
Hence exact pre-action microstate observation cannot be replaced by the blind
or past-state Hamiltonian.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $u(z)=\operatorname{sign}G(z)$ in the fully observed problem.  The
coarse controller must choose one sign before seeing $G$ and $G$ is centred.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is not a defect of deterministic first principles.  It says that the
observation filtration is a first-principles input of the decision problem.
The base Papers I--III use the canonical current-prepared past-state
preparation.
\end{remark}

\section{The controlled microscopic operator}\label{sec:controlled}
Let $U\subset\R^m$ and $V\subset\R^n$ be closed convex action sets.  Controls
are chosen from the slow state and quotient information before the next
collision; they do not alter $T_R$, $K_{R,q}$, $J_R$, or $\tau_R$.  Let
$b^{uv},c^{uv}$ be bounded Lipschitz coefficients.  The exact lower operator is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (\mathcal T_\varepsilon^-F)(x,\xi)
 &=\sup_{u\in U}\inf_{v\in V}\frac1q\log\\
 &\quad\int K_{R,q}(\xi,d\xi')
 \exp\{q[\varepsilon^2\tau c^{uv}(x)\\
 &\hspace{39mm}+F(x+\varepsilon G+
 \varepsilon^2\tau b^{uv}(x),\xi')]\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The upper operator reverses $\sup$ and $\inf$.

\begin{proposition}[Controlled consistency]\label{prop:controlled-consistency}
If the local lower and upper Hamiltonians agree, both schemes are consistent
with
\[
 H_{R,q}(x,p,X)
 =\sup_u\inf_v\{c^{uv}(x)+b^{uv}(x)p\}
 +\frac{D_{R,q}}2X
 +\frac{qD_{R,q}}2p^2.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The fast correctors and pressure-root variance are independent of the actions.
Apply the fixed-policy calculation of Paper III inside the local optimization.
Uniformity follows from compact coefficient bounds or the coercivity below.
\end{proof}
