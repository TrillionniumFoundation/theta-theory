\section{Intrinsic causal deficiency}\label{sec:v13-deficiency}
\subsection{Experiments and executable signatures}
Fix a finite parameter set $\Theta$, a horizon $T$, and finite control
alphabets $A_0,\ldots,A_{T-1}$. An experiment has a report at checkpoints
$0,\ldots,T$ and an unobserved terminal mark $W$ in a common finite set.
It is specified by finite causal instruments: for each parameter and
control history they give the joint law of the report path and the
actual mark. Equivalently, a finite latent-state realization may be
expanded into its history tree. The parameter and mark are unavailable
to the encoder. The mark may depend on the physical controlled path.
We compare experiments with the same control interface and mark space.

Write $Y_t$ for the source report and $Z_t$ for the target report. A
private simulator of width $K$ has stochastic rows
\begin{align}
 q_0(z,m\mid y),&\qquad m\in[K_0],\label{eq:v13-rows}\\
 q_t(z,m\mid m',a,y),&\qquad
       m'\in[K_{t-1}],\ m\in[K_t],\quad 1\le t\le T.\nonumber
\end{align}
A row consumes the current source report, emits the target report, and
retains the new state. The previous target reports are not available to
that row unless encoded in $m'$. The clock and current control are part
of the declared interface. Fresh private randomness is permitted, but
no other persistent random variable is free. The product of all row
simplexes is denoted $\cK_K$.

A deterministic feedback tree $g$ chooses $a_t$ from $z_{0:t}$ and its
own previous controls. It is the same controller on both sides of a
comparison. Let $\Omega_g$ be the finite marked transcript space, including
reports, controls, and $W$. Put
\[
 P_{\theta,g}^q=\Law_\theta(E\xrightarrow{q}g),\qquad
 Q_{\theta,g}=\Law_\theta(F\xrightarrow{}g).
\]
Here $W$ on the left is the source's actual mark. The total variation
convention is $\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}=\sup_B|P(B)-Q(B)|$.
Private deficiency is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-private}
 \dpr_K(E,F)=\min_{q\in\cK_K}
       \max_{\theta,g}\|P_{\theta,g}^q-Q_{\theta,g}\|_{\rm TV}.
\end{equation}
The minimum will be justified below. Randomized feedback need not be
added to the maximum: conditioning on its independent random table
expresses its law as a mixture of deterministic feedback laws. The same
argument permits arbitrary independent side randomness in the tester.

A \emph{hidden selector} $U\in[r]$, independent of the source, chooses
one simulator $q^u$. It is not visible to the feedback controller or the
final test. A \emph{visible selector} is revealed before feedback begins.
The two costs are
\begin{align}
 \dhi_{K,r}(E,F)
 &=\min_{\lambda,q^1,\ldots,q^r}
   \max_{\theta,g}\left\|\sum_u\lambda_uP_{\theta,g}^{q^u}
                              -Q_{\theta,g}\right\|_{\rm TV},
                              \label{eq:v13-hidden}\\
 \dvi_{K,r}(E,F)
 &=\min_{\lambda,q^1,\ldots,q^r}
   \max_{\theta,(g_u)}
   \left\|\sum_u\lambda_u\delta_u\otimes P_{\theta,g_u}^{q^u}
             -\sum_u\lambda_u\delta_u\otimes Q_{\theta,g_u}
   \right\|_{\rm TV}.\label{eq:v13-visible}
\end{align}
In both expressions $\lambda\in\Delta_r$ and $q^u\in\cK_K$.
The second formula compares the joint seed--marked interface. Its target
seed has the same distribution and is independent of the target
experiment. A hidden selector can be stored privately by retaining
$(u,m)$, at width $rK_t$. A visible selector is separate side information
and is charged by its own alphabet size. Neither interpretation identifies
an arbitrary real-valued design with a free finite register.

\begin{definition}[Deficiency spectrum]\label{def:v13-spectrum}
The private resource spectrum of $E$ relative to $F$ is the upward-closed
set of pairs $(K,\varepsilon)$ satisfying $\dpr_K(E,F)\le\varepsilon$.
The hidden spectrum consists of triples $(K,r,\varepsilon)$ satisfying
$\dhi_{K,r}(E,F)\le\varepsilon$, and the visible spectrum uses
$\dvi_{K,r}$. Width profiles are ordered coordinatewise; in the seeded
spectra the seed bound and error are ordered as well. Unused states or
selector values may be adjoined. The hidden convexification is
$\dmix_K=\inf_{r\ge1}\dhi_{K,r}$.
\end{definition}
The spectrum, not a presumed unique coordinatewise minimum profile, is
the intrinsic object. For example one may minimize $\max_tK_t$, with a
specified seed signature and tolerance, over its feasible profiles.
Different checkpoints can have incomparable optimal tradeoffs.

\subsection{The finite comparison theorem}
Concatenate all probabilities $P_{\theta,g}^q(\omega)$ into $b(q)\in
\mathbb R^N$, retaining their row indices; put $b_F=(Q_{\theta,g}(\omega))$.
Let $\mathcal D_K$ be the finite set of deterministic choices of all
rows in \eqref{eq:v13-rows}. Empty or unreachable rows remain harmless
coordinates. Set
\[
 a_K=\dim\aff\{b(q):q\in\cK_K\},\qquad
 \cI=\{(\theta,g,B):B\subseteq\Omega_g\}.
\]
For $i=(\theta,g,B)$ and $d\in\mathcal D_K$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-matrix}
 A_{i,d}=Q_{\theta,g}(B)-P_{\theta,g}^{d}(B).
\end{equation}
Including both an event and its complement includes both signs.

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic deficiency, priced converse, and visibility]
\label{thm:v13-main}
For the finite marked causal experiments above the following assertions
hold.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(i)} Private deficiency and both fixed-$r$ deficiencies are
attained. The private problem has the exact polynomial formulation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-polynomial}
 \min\{\varepsilon: q\in\cK_K,\ 0\le\varepsilon\le1,
    Q_{\theta,g}(B)-P_{\theta,g}^q(B)\le\varepsilon
                         \text{ for all }(\theta,g,B)\in\cI\}.
\end{equation}
In particular its zero set is exactly the nonnegative causal
factorization of the target marked behavior by the rows
\eqref{eq:v13-rows}. At $K_t=1$ for every $t$, this is precisely the same
factorization with rows $q_t(z\mid a,y)$ and $q_0(z\mid y)$.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(ii)} Hidden convexification has the attained dual
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-dual}
 \dmix_K(E,F)=
 \min_{\lambda\in\Delta(\mathcal D_K)}\max_{i\in\cI}
                    \sum_d\lambda_d A_{i,d}
 =\max_{w\in\Delta(\cI)}\min_{d\in\mathcal D_K}
                    \sum_iw_i A_{i,d}.
\end{equation}
An optimal simulation uses at most $a_K+1$ selector values. Thus every
weighted causal test has optimal discrepancy at most $\varepsilon$ if
and only if a hidden mixture of $K$-state simulators has error at most
$\varepsilon$; it has a private realization of width at most $(a_K+1)K$.
More specifically,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-priced}
 \dpr_{rK}\le\dhi_{K,r},\qquad
 \dmix_K=\dhi_{K,a_K+1}\le\dpr_K.
\end{equation}

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(iii)} Put
$e_\theta(q)=\max_g\|P_{\theta,g}^q-Q_{\theta,g}\|_{\rm TV}$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-exposed}
 \dvi_{K,r}=\min_{\lambda,q^1,\ldots,q^r}
                  \max_\theta\sum_u\lambda_u e_\theta(q^u).
\end{equation}
Writing $a_K^{\rm v}=\dim\aff\{(e_\theta(q))_\theta:q\in\cK_K\}$,
the infimum over all finite visible selectors is attained using
$a_K^{\rm v}+1\le|\Theta|+1$ values and equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-visible-dual}
 \max_{v\in\Delta(\Theta)}\min_{q\in\cK_K}
                          \sum_\theta v_\theta e_\theta(q).
\end{equation}
For every $r\ge1$, and also after optimizing over all $r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-zeros}
 \dvi_{K,r}(E,F)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\dpr_K(E,F)=0.
\end{equation}
No corresponding equivalence with $\dmix_K=0$ holds in general.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(iv)} For compatible experiments $E,F,G$ and profiles $K,L$,
\begin{align}
 \dpr_{KL}(E,G)&\le\dpr_K(E,F)+\dpr_L(F,G),\label{eq:v13-triangle}\\
 \delta^{\rm s}_{KL,rs}(E,G)&\le
 \delta^{\rm s}_{K,r}(E,F)+\delta^{\rm s}_{L,s}(F,G),
                       \qquad {\rm s}\in\{{\rm h},{\rm v}\}.
                       \nonumber
\end{align}
Here $KL$ is the pointwise product, and the second line uses independent
selectors. If an $F$-consumer has widths $S_t$, composing it with a
private simulator gives widths $K_tS_t$ and changes every $[0,1]$ marked
risk by at most $\dpr_K(E,F)$. A hidden selector is stored at the
additional price $r$, whereas visible selector alphabets multiply.
The same bounds hold for optimized upper risk sets and their lower
support functions with the indicated costs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand a probability of a complete source path, simulator state path,
and transcript. It is a product of fixed experiment probabilities and
one chosen row entry per time. Summing latent and state paths gives a
polynomial in the finitely many row entries. No row belonging to a fixed
time can occur twice along one path. Consequently the polynomial is
multiaffine in the groups of stochastic rows. Continuity and compactness
of the product of closed finite simplexes prove attainment in
\eqref{eq:v13-private}; the same proof includes $\lambda$ and the $r$
policies in \eqref{eq:v13-hidden}. For the visible problem there are only
finitely many collections $(g_u)$ and transcript events, so its objective
is again continuous on a compact set. The finite-event formula for total
variation proves \eqref{eq:v13-polynomial}. Setting $\varepsilon=0$
forces equality of every transcript probability, in both directions of
each event inequality. This proves the factorization and stateless
claims, including their necessity.

For each stochastic row of $q$, sample its deterministic output once,
independently of all other rows, before the experiment. This constructs
a probability $\Lambda_q$ on $\mathcal D_K$. On any fixed path the row
variables encountered have different time indices, so the expectation
of their product is the product of the row probabilities. Summing paths
shows, simultaneously for every $\theta,g$, that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-purification}
 b(q)=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D_K}\Lambda_q(d)b(d).
\end{equation}
This is a behavior identity, not a same-width deterministic
purification of minimax error. It implies
$\conv b(\cK_K)=\conv\{b(d):d\in\mathcal D_K\}$.
The latter is a compact polytope. Minimizing the continuous convex
maximum of event discrepancies over this polytope gives the first
expression in \eqref{eq:v13-dual}. Finite-dimensional minimax gives the
second, with both optima attained. A point of this polytope is a convex
combination of at most $a_K+1$ of its generating points by
Carath\'eodory's theorem. For completeness, if a representation uses more
points, their affine dependence gives coefficients $c_d$ with
$\sum c_d=0$ and $\sum c_db(d)=0$. Subtract the largest permissible
multiple of $c$ from the positive mixing weights; a weight becomes zero
without changing the represented point. Repeating gives the stated
bound. Store its selector together with the machine state to obtain
\eqref{eq:v13-priced}. The dual inequality for every $w$ is equivalent
to the maximum over $w$ being at most $\varepsilon$, which proves the
priced testing converse.

For disjoint seed fibers, total variation is the sum of the fiber total
variations weighted by $\lambda_u$. For a fixed $\theta$, the tester can
choose a separate maximizing tree on each revealed fiber. Therefore
its maximum is $\sum_u\lambda_ue_\theta(q^u)$, proving
\eqref{eq:v13-exposed}. The map $q\mapsto e(q)$ is continuous, being a
finite maximum of continuous functions. Its image is compact. Convexifying
it in $\mathbb R^{|\Theta|}$ and applying the preceding finite-dimensional
separation and affine-dependence argument proves
\eqref{eq:v13-visible-dual} and its selector bound. Notice that the inner
minimum is over stochastic $q$, not over deterministic tables: the
nonlinear function $e_\theta$ does not commute with
\eqref{eq:v13-purification}. If the attained visible optimum is zero,
then for every $u$ of positive weight and every $\theta$ one has
$e_\theta(q^u)=0$, since every summand is nonnegative. Any such $q^u$
is a private exact simulator. The converse uses a selector of one value.
This also proves the assertion for the optimized visible selector,
whose support is finite by the bound just established.

Finally, cascade a $K$-state $E$-to-$F$ transducer and an $L$-state
$F$-to-$G$ transducer. Within a step, consume the intermediate report
before discarding it. The retained pair has at most $K_tL_t$ values.
For any terminal tester on $G$, the second transducer together with
that tester is an admissible randomized feedback system on $F$.
The first error bound applies to this system, and total variation
contracts under the remaining channel. The second error bound applies
to the genuine $F$ system. The triangle inequality gives
\eqref{eq:v13-triangle}. With selectors, condition on the independent
selectors in the appropriate places; in the visible case keep both
selectors in the compared transcript. The same argument is valid
because the target reference selectors are independent. Storing a
hidden selector or exposing both visible selectors gives the stated
costs. The minima established above may be used; equivalently one can
use nearly optimal simulators and let the extra tolerance decrease.

A consumer is another causal transducer with task-specific readout and,
where allowed, task-specific control, but with its required common
encoder. The simulator is task blind, so its product with that encoder
is still common. Condition on the consumer's independent random table
to apply the feedback bound. Integration of any $[0,1]$ marked loss
changes by at most the total variation error. Taking the same paired
consumer for every row proves the risk-set assertion, rather than a
separate choice of simulator for each row. Infima of nonnegative
weighted risks give the lower support inequalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the converse says]\label{rem:v13-converse}
The dual \eqref{eq:v13-dual} uses mixtures of indexed causal tests and
optimizes over the entire common transducer. It is not a task-by-task
choice of encoder. Its conclusion is an explicit hidden-selector
simulation, with the selector subsequently charged if private storage
is required. It does not replace the nonconvex private set by its
convex hull at unchanged resource. The next example shows why that
replacement would change the zero set.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A delayed channel and a strict signature separation}
Let the source reveal $i\in[n]$ at checkpoint zero and reveal only a
constant at checkpoint one. There is no control and the mark is
constant. The target reports a constant at checkpoint zero and at
checkpoint one draws $j\in[m]$ from a specified row-stochastic matrix
$V=(V_{ij})$. The unknown parameter is $i$. The first target alphabet
has one element: the simulator cannot use an early public output as
unpriced storage. Only the intermediate register of size $K$ matters.

\begin{proposition}[The nonnegative-rank slice]\label{prop:v13-rank}
For this experiment,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-factor}
 \dpr_K(E,F_V)=\min_{A,B}\max_i\frac12
                 \sum_j|V_{ij}-(AB)_{ij}|,
\end{equation}
where $A$ is an $n$-by-$K$ row-stochastic matrix and $B$ is a $K$-by-$m$
row-stochastic matrix. In particular
\[
 \dpr_K(E,F_V)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \rplus(V)\le K,
 \qquad
 \dpr_1(E,F_V)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ V\text{ has identical rows}.
\]
For $1\le K\le n$ the identity channel has the sharp laws
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-allidentity}
 \dpr_K(E,F_{I_n})=1-\frac1{\lceil n/K\rceil},\qquad
 \dmix_K(E,F_{I_n})=1-\frac Kn.
\end{equation}
Thus, at tolerance $0\le\varepsilon<1$, the least private width is
$\lceil n/\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\rfloor\rceil$, whereas the
least within-mode width with optimized hidden selection is
$\max\{1,\lceil n(1-\varepsilon)\rceil\}$. The latter still pays for
its selector. In particular, for $n=m=3$ and $K=2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-identity}
 \dpr_2(E,F_{I_3})=\tfrac12,
 \qquad \dmix_2(E,F_{I_3})=\tfrac13.
\end{equation}
For $V_*=(1/2)I_3+(1/6)\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-strictzero}
 \dhi_{2,3}(E,F_{V_*})=0,
 \qquad \dpr_2(E,F_{V_*})\ge\frac1{4\sqrt3}>0,
 \qquad \inf_r\dvi_{2,r}(E,F_{V_*})>0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The encoder maps the consumed $i$ into its retained label by $A$; the
terminal decoder maps that label into $j$ by $B$. This proves
\eqref{eq:v13-factor} including its converse implementation. If
$V=CD$ is any nonnegative factorization, let $s_k=\sum_jD_{kj}$.
Delete zero rows of $D$, define $B_{kj}=D_{kj}/s_k$ and
$A_{ik}=C_{ik}s_k$. Since $V$ is stochastic, the rows of $A$ sum to one.
Padding with unused states gives the claimed equivalence with
nonnegative rank. The one-state statement follows immediately.

For $V=I_n$, row error is $1-(AB)_{ii}$. If the least correct
probability is $c$, for each $i$ select a row $k(i)$ of $B$ with
$B_{k(i),i}\ge c$. Some row is selected at least $\lceil n/K\rceil$
times, so stochasticity gives $c\le1/\lceil n/K\rceil$. A partition
into $K$ groups of sizes at most $\lceil n/K\rceil$, each decoded
uniformly, attains that bound. Also $\sum_i(AB)_{ii}\le K$, by the
calculation below with $K$ in place of two. It remains valid under
mixing, giving hidden error at least $1-K/n$. Start with a deterministic
channel that reproduces $K$ input symbols exactly and sends all others
to those $K$ outputs. Average all simultaneous permutations of its
input and output labels. Every diagonal entry is then $K/n$, proving
the second formula. Inverting the two monotone formulas gives the
stated least widths.

For $V=I_3$, total variation in row $i$ is $1-(AB)_{ii}$.
If all three diagonal entries of $AB$ were greater than $1/2$, then
for each $i$ some row of $B$ would have its $i$th entry greater than
$1/2$. A stochastic row has at most one such entry, so two rows cannot
serve three indices. This proves the private lower bound $1/2$.
Merge two input symbols into a retained label that decodes uniformly
to that pair, and retain the third symbol exactly. This attains it.
For every stochastic factorization with two intermediate states,
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^3(AB)_{ii}
 =\sum_{k=1}^2\sum_{i=1}^3 A_{ik}B_{ki}
 \le\sum_{k=1}^2\sum_{i=1}^3B_{ki}=2.
\]
The same inequality holds under hidden mixtures, giving maximal row
error at least $1/3$. Mix uniformly the three preceding pair-merge
channels. The resulting diagonal entries are $2/3$ and the off-diagonal
entries are $1/6$. This is exactly $V_*$ and has error $1/3$ from $I_3$.
It proves the hidden claims in both displays.

The smallest singular value of $V_*$ is $1/2$. Any $AB$ with two
intermediate states has a unit vector $x$ in its right null space, so
$\|V_*-AB\|_{\rm F}\ge\|(V_*-AB)x\|_2\ge1/2$.
If the maximal row total variation error is $\varepsilon$, each row
has Euclidean error at most $2\varepsilon$, and hence the Frobenius
error is at most $2\sqrt3\varepsilon$. This proves the explicit private
bound. The optimized visible value is attained by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main}; if it were zero, its zero-set assertion
would contradict that bound. This proves its strict positivity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Optimal resource sets and decision certificates}
For a class $\mathcal C_{S,q}(F)$ of common consumers, with all other
information conventions fixed, let $\mathcal R_{S,q}(F)$ be its vector
of attainable $[0,1]$ risks. For a nonnegative row weight $w$ summing
to one, write
\[
 h_F(S,q;w)=\inf_{r\in\mathcal R_{S,q}(F)}\langle w,r\rangle.
\]
If $E$ privately simulates $F$ with cost $K$ and error $\varepsilon$,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-risktransport}
 \mathcal R_{S,q}(F)\subseteq
 \mathcal R_{KS,q}(E)+[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^J,
 \qquad
 h_E(KS,q;w)\le h_F(S,q;w)+\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
For nonfinite row sets the first expression means pointwise paired
risk functions within uniform distance $\varepsilon$; no finite
coordinate box is needed. Upper sets give the equivalent one-sided
order formulation. Hidden selectors multiply private width by $r$;
visible selectors multiply the separate public alphabet by $r$.
These are direct consequences of the final part of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main}, with the optimal intrinsic error in place
of an externally estimated simulator error.

When two stateless comparison directions have error at most
$\varepsilon$, paired risk sets and their support values at unchanged
width are within $\varepsilon$. If each is centered by a paired
unrestricted benchmark whose error is also at most $\varepsilon$, the
centered values are within $2\varepsilon$. Therefore a gap $g>0$
between zero excess and infeasible widths is stable under
$\varepsilon<g/4$: a value below $g/2$ in the approximation certifies
feasibility in the exact finite problem, and a value above $g/2$
certifies infeasibility. The benchmark and the class being optimized
must be specified; this assertion does not identify different
information signatures.

\begin{proposition}[Intrinsic finite-dimensional selector approximation]
\label{prop:v13-dimension}
Let $\mathcal R$ be a compact risk image in $C(J)$, where $J$ is compact.
Suppose a bounded linear operator $L:C(J)\to C(J)$ has rank at most $m$
and $\sup_{r\in\mathcal R}\|r-Lr\|_\infty\le\eta$. Every barycenter
$\bar r$ of a probability on $\mathcal R$ has a barycenter $r_*$ of
at most $m+1$ members of $\mathcal R$ with
$\|\bar r-r_*\|_\infty\le2\eta$. If $\mathcal R$ lies in a finite
affine space of dimension $m$, the error is zero with at most $m+1$
members. Thus the selector price can be controlled by the dimension
of attainable risks, rather than by a covering number of the row space.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The compact set $L\mathcal R$ lies in an $m$-dimensional space. Its
convex hull is compact, and $L\bar r$ belongs to it. The
finite-dimensional affine-dependence argument in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main} gives $r_1,\ldots,r_{m+1}\in\mathcal R$
and weights $\lambda$ such that
$L\bar r=\sum_i\lambda_iLr_i$. Put $r_*=\sum_i\lambda_ir_i$.
The two residual terms $\bar r-L\bar r$ and $r_*-Lr_*$ have norms
at most $\eta$, proving the estimate. Apply the same argument after
translation in the exact affine case. The implementation stores the
selected design index whenever it is not provided as visible side
information.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finite computation and general carriers]
For rational finite instruments, \eqref{eq:v13-polynomial} and the
fixed-$r$ formulations are finite polynomial feasibility problems;
\eqref{eq:v13-dual} is a finite linear program. These are exact
variational characterizations, not assertions of polynomial running
time in a succinct history description. The number of feedback trees,
events, or deterministic tables can be large. On standard Borel
carriers the same definitions use suprema over measurable feedback
and tests and infima over executable kernels. Their product-state
composition and decision transport remain valid by the same channel
argument. Attainment and the finite testing dual are asserted here
only for the stated finite class. The average-risk and physical results
below give separate nonfinite theorems with their own hypotheses.
\end{remark}
