\section{Joint residual budgets and growing families}
\label{sec:v21-joint}
We now put lower bounds on several cuts into one statistical budget.
Summing separate cut bounds without controlling their common words
would count the same regret more than once.

\begin{theorem}[A shared-capacity multicut obstruction]
\label{thm:v21-joint}
Let $f:\prod_{i=1}^L\Sigma_i\to\mathcal A$ and $\mu(w)\ge0$ be as
in Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-weighted}. At each chosen cut $t$, choose a
finite prefix graph with an edge $e=(u,v)$ carrying a distinguishing
suffix $s_e$, and a weight $\lambda_{t,e}\ge0$. Impose the single family
of constraints
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-capacity}
 \sum_{t,e:\ w=us_e\ \text{or}\ w=vs_e}\lambda_{t,e}\le\mu(w)
 \quad\text{for every complete word }w.
\end{equation}
For a graph at cut $t$, write
\[
 \Gamma_{t,K_t}(\lambda)=
 \min_{\phi:\,\text{prefixes}\to[K_t]}
       \sum_{e=(u,v):\,\phi(u)=\phi(v)}\lambda_{t,e}.
\]
Every stochastic machine of profile $\mathbf K$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-joint-bound}
 \sum_w\mu(w)\Pr(\widehat f(w)\ne f(w))
       \ge\sum_t\Gamma_{t,K_t}(\lambda).
\end{equation}
Consequently, with $R_w,S_\nu,\mu$ defined in
Corollary~\ref{cor:v20-bayes}, every audit of this profile and the fixed
training schedule has worst-case payoff at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-joint-bayes}
 S_\nu-\sup_{\lambda\text{ satisfying }\eqref{eq:v21-capacity}}
                    \sum_t\Gamma_{t,K_t}(\lambda).
\end{equation}
For fixed graphs the supremum is the value of a finite linear program.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each edge use the overlap inequality
\eqref{eq:v20-overlap}. Multiply it by $\lambda_{t,e}$ and sum over
all cuts and edges. The left side is bounded above by the left side of
\eqref{eq:v21-joint-bound}, by the shared capacity constraint. At each
cut the resulting overlap expression is concave in its prefix state
distributions, hence at least its minimum over deterministic labelings,
as proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-weighted}. Adding these bounds proves
\eqref{eq:v21-joint-bound}. Although prefix distributions at different
cuts are compatible, minimizing them separately only weakens the bound;
it does not assume independent realizability. The Bayes-regret argument
of Corollary~\ref{cor:v20-bayes} proves \eqref{eq:v21-joint-bayes}.

For the last assertion introduce variables $z_t$ and impose, for each
labeling $\phi$ of each finite graph,
\[
 z_t\le\sum_{e=(u,v):\phi(u)=\phi(v)}\lambda_{t,e}.
\]
Maximize $\sum_tz_t$ subject to these inequalities, nonnegative edge
weights, and \eqref{eq:v21-capacity}. There are finitely many constraints.
This is a computable obstruction, not a claim that every multicut
statistical optimum equals this relaxation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Actual physical word weights}
For $n$ all-on trials of the private marked collision subfamily, a complete
training word $w$ specifies the two report bits and the mark of each
trial. With $N_{ab}$ its report-pair counts,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-physical-weight}
 p_w(p,q)=2^{-n}
 p^{N_{10}+N_{11}}(1-p)^{N_{00}+N_{01}}
 q^{N_{01}+N_{11}}(1-q)^{N_{00}+N_{10}}.
\end{equation}
The formula has the continuous endpoint interpretation $0^0=1$.
For any finitely supported prior on actual pairs $(p,q)\in[0,1]^2$,
use this likelihood in $R_w(A)=\int p_w(p,q)(Q-b(p,q))\1_A\,d\nu$,
with all marked events $A\subseteq\Omega$ as actions. Randomized events
are included by linearity. Equations~\eqref{eq:v21-capacity} and
\eqref{eq:v21-joint-bayes} then give a finite all-algorithm obstruction
for that full training profile, with the true physical word probabilities.
Changing only $Q$ by total variation $\beta$ changes every audit's
payoff by at most $\beta$.

This specialization bounds all stochastic selectors on the fixed all-on
training schedule, not merely exact implementations of a majority rule.
It does not assert a new numerical lower bound above three for every
adaptive physical audit. Adaptive training has controller-dependent
word likelihoods and must instead be included in the full experiment
of \eqref{eq:v21-value}; using \eqref{eq:v21-physical-weight} unchanged
for such a likelihood would be incorrect. The feedback-uniform lower
bound used below remains Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-feedback}.

\subsection{A family with matching end-to-end growth}
\begin{theorem}[Point-source decision spectra]\label{thm:v21-point}
Let $d\ge2$, $B_d=\{\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_d\}$ on $[d]$, and let
$Q_d$ be uniform. For every $K\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-point}
 \mathfrak V_K(B_d,Q_d)=1-\frac{\lceil d/K\rceil}{d}.
\end{equation}
For $1-2/d<c<1-1/d$, the decision complexity is exactly $d$, and the
whole-schedule peak satisfies
\[
 d\le W_c(B_d,Q_d)\le W_c^{\rm rat}(B_d,Q_d)\le2d.
\]
The upper bound uses one training observation and two validations, with
no randomization. Thus the end-to-end width grows linearly in this
nontrivial sequence of compact behavior images.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assign each point source to a response that maximizes its score. Some
response is assigned a set $S$ of at least $\lceil d/K\rceil$ indices.
For $s=|S|<d$, the mean of its scores on $S$ equals
\[
 \frac{d-s}{d}\left(
 \frac1{d-s}\sum_{i\notin S}h_i-\frac1s\sum_{i\in S}h_i\right)
 \le1-\frac{s}{d}.
\]
For $s=d$ the mean is zero. At least one assigned source has score no
larger than this mean, proving the upper bound. Partition $[d]$ into
at most $K$ groups of size at most $\lceil d/K\rceil$ and use their
complements as events. This attains the lower bound.

In the stated interval the formula forces $K\ge d$. A single training
observation identifies the point source $i$ exactly. Keep $i$ and choose
the complement of $\{i\}$. During validation retain $i$ and the candidate
indicator, at most $2d$ states, then emit the target indicator minus it.
This computes the prescribed signed score on every input word, including
words that have zero probability under the point-source family.
Its expected score is $1-1/d$. The lower bound follows from the necessary
decision-cut width.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A sharp simultaneous storage profile}
A second class separates overlapping retention obligations. Independent
uniform labels $X_j\in[q_j]$ are revealed at scheduled epochs $a_j$ and
must be reported at epochs $b_j>a_j$. No later input reveals an active
label, and previous outputs cannot be read as external memory. Let
$A_t=\{j:a_j\le t<b_j\}$ and $D_t=\prod_{j\in A_t}q_j$.

\begin{theorem}[Overlapping retention intervals]\label{thm:v21-intervals}
Every stochastic machine whose probability of correctly reporting all
labels is at least $1-\varepsilon$ has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-intervals}
 K_t\ge(1-\varepsilon)D_t\quad\text{at every cut }t.
\end{equation}
The exact profile $K_t=D_t$ is attained simultaneously by a deterministic
machine. Thus the minimum peak number of bits lies between
$\log_2\max_t D_t+\log_2(1-\varepsilon)$ and
$\lceil\log_2\max_t D_t\rceil$ for $\varepsilon<1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a cut and give the decoder, as additional information, all labels
outside $A_t$, whether past or future. They are independent of the
uniform active vector. Conditional on them, every future prediction of
that vector factors through the $K_t$-valued retained state and fresh
randomness. If $a(m\mid x)$ is its conditional encoder and $d(x\mid m)$
the induced joint prediction law, its success probability is at most
\[
 \frac1{D_t}\sum_m\sum_xa(m\mid x)d(x\mid m)
 \le\frac1{D_t}\sum_m\sum_x d(x\mid m)
 =\frac{K_t}{D_t}.
\]
Correctness of all reports implies correctness of the active reports,
so averaging over the independent additional information proves
\eqref{eq:v21-intervals}. For the upper bound, keep precisely the vector
of active labels, append a label when revealed, and delete it when
reported. Every update is deterministic and respects the schedule.
The bit bounds follow by taking logarithms and rounding for an actual
binary register. The lower bound is a counting argument, not an
independence assumption about labels after conditioning on a compressed
state.
\end{proof}
