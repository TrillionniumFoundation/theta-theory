\section{Compatible occupation arrays}
\label{sec:v23-occupations}
A fixed terminal statistic and an optimal statistic under a sequence of
memory constraints are different objects. We begin with a characterization
of the second object. All the spaces in this section are finite. No
strict positivity assumption is imposed on a probability coordinate.

\subsection{The finite protocol and its observable rows}
Fix a finite schedule $\sigma$ and a register profile
$\mathbf K=(K_t)_{t\in\sigma}$. A cut is part of the specification of
$\sigma$: subdividing an atomic observation or a randomization operation
inserts additional register constraints. The clocked model permits a
different transition table at each scheduled operation. It does not
permit access to a discarded observation. We write
$\mathcal M^{\rm clk}_\sigma(\mathbf K)$ for this class. Initial
randomization, when allowed, is a transition of the same kind as all
other randomizations.

Let $\Theta$ be a finite set of environments. Under $\theta$, all
preparation and experimental kernels are specified. One may include
the complete physical and public past in a finite hidden index $i$.
This index is a variable in the analysis, not an observer register.
An elementary observer operation has an accessible row $r$ and chooses
$j$ from a nonempty finite set $J_r$. The row contains exactly the
current register and the current atomic input, if there is one. The
choice $j$ can specify a new register and a public action. A public
action that is subsequently needed by the observer must be retained in
that register. Every permissible transition has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-row}
 u(j\mid r),\qquad u(j\mid r)\ge0,\qquad
 \sum_{j\in J_r}u(j\mid r)=1.
\end{equation}
Neither $\theta$ nor the unobserved part of $i$ is an argument of $u$.

Known environment operations act linearly on conditional occupation
masses. For example, if $z(\theta,h,m)$ is the mass before an observation,
then its incoming mass at the observer is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-prediction}
 x(\theta,h',(m,y))=
       \sum_h z(\theta,h,m)L^\theta(h',y\mid h,m).
\end{equation}
Dependence on a previously issued action is included in $h$ or in the
retained part of $m$. After an observer choice, the public history is
updated and discarded indices are summed out, again by a known linear
map. Alternating these two kinds of operation represents a finite
controlled experiment, including feedback, calibration, validation,
and a terminal readout. The cut alphabets and the allowed rows are
fixed before optimization. In particular, this representation does not
silently grant the observer the hidden history used to define the
experimental kernels.

For each observer operation introduce outgoing masses
$f(\theta,i,r,j)$. Impose the conservation and compatibility equations
\begin{align}
 &f(\theta,i,r,j)\ge0,\qquad
   \sum_{j\in J_r}f(\theta,i,r,j)=x(\theta,i,r),
                                      \label{eq:v23-conservation}\\
 &f(\theta,i,r,j)x(\theta',i',r)
  =f(\theta',i',r,j)x(\theta,i,r)
       \quad(\theta,i,\theta',i',r,j). \label{eq:v23-minors}
\end{align}
All incoming masses are nonnegative and are linked to the preceding
operation by the known linear maps. Preparations are prescribed and
have conditional mass one for each $\theta$. We call the resulting
set of arrays $\mathcal O^{\rm clk}_\sigma(\mathbf K)$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact full-profile realization]
\label{thm:v23-occupation}
The occupation arrays of
$\mathcal M^{\rm clk}_\sigma(\mathbf K)$ are exactly
$\mathcal O^{\rm clk}_\sigma(\mathbf K)$. This set is compact and is
defined by linear equations, nonnegativity, and the quadratic equations
\eqref{eq:v23-minors}. Every feasible array determines an implementing
machine, with arbitrary legal rows only where the incoming mass vanishes
under every environment.

More generally, suppose specified occurrences of observable rows must
use the same transition distribution, with their choices identified.
For each such class impose \eqref{eq:v23-minors} also between different
occurrences. The resulting arrays are exactly those realized by machines
with these shared rows. In particular this gives an exact formulation
for an autonomous controller with a fixed state alphabet and a single
reused transition table.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A machine gives
$f(\theta,i,r,j)=x(\theta,i,r)u(j\mid r)$, so conservation and every
minor equation follow. Conversely, fix an occurrence of $r$. If some
$x(\theta_0,i_0,r)>0$, define
\[
 u(j\mid r)=\frac{f(\theta_0,i_0,r,j)}{x(\theta_0,i_0,r)}.
\]
Conservation makes this a stochastic row. The minor with
$(\theta_0,i_0)$ shows that the same formula reproduces $f$ at every
positive incoming mass. At a zero incoming mass, nonnegativity and
conservation force each outgoing mass to be zero. If all incoming
masses vanish, choose any legal stochastic row. Thus the recovered row
is independent of both the environment and the hidden index.

Perform this construction at every observer operation. Induction along
the schedule, using \eqref{eq:v23-prediction} and the corresponding
linear outgoing maps, proves that the recovered machine has exactly
the given occupation arrays. Its registers are the declared alphabets;
the proof has not introduced a retained selector. For a shared row class,
choose one positive incoming mass anywhere in that class and use the
cross-occurrence equations. The same argument reconstructs one common
row. A class with no positive incoming mass is treated arbitrarily.

Each coordinate is a conditional probability or a subprobability mass
and is therefore at most one. Adding these redundant upper bounds makes
boundedness explicit. The equations and inequalities are closed, and
there are finitely many coordinates. Compactness follows.
\end{proof}

The zero-mass provision is essential. Cancelling an occupation coordinate
without a case distinction would remove valid boundary machines. Nor
would separate low-rank descriptions at successive cuts imply
\eqref{eq:v23-prediction}--\eqref{eq:v23-minors}. The latter equations
refer to one execution, not to a list of independently selected encoders.
For an autonomous machine, the phase cannot be supplied through a
row name unless it is either an actual input or part of the charged
state. A clocked machine can be embedded autonomously by retaining
$(t,m)$; Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-occupation} also permits direct optimization
without making that possibly wasteful construction.

\subsection{A complete polynomial converse}
Let $G\in[-1,1]$ be a terminal payoff and let $L_\theta(z)$ denote its
expectation as a linear function of the complete occupation array $z$.
The terminal decision is included among the observer operations. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-value}
 V^{\rm clk}_{\sigma,\mathbf K}(\Theta,G)
 =\max_{A\in\mathcal M^{\rm clk}_\sigma(\mathbf K)}
                              \min_{\theta\in\Theta}\E_\theta^A G.
\end{equation}
The admissible row sets are assumed nonempty. Introduce $v\in[-1,1]$
and impose $L_\theta(z)-v\ge0$ for every $\theta$. Write $h_a=0$ for
all the linear and quadratic equalities and $g_b\ge0$ for the remaining
inequalities. Include the redundant ball inequality
\[
 R-\|(z,v)\|^2\ge0,
\]
where the positive integer $R$ is larger than the number of coordinates.
Let $\mathcal F$ be this basic closed feasible set. For $d\ge1$, define
$U_d$ as the infimum of $u\in\R$ for which the polynomial identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-sos}
 u-v=\sigma_0+\sum_b\sigma_b g_b+\sum_a q_a h_a
\end{equation}
holds, with each $\sigma_b$ a sum of squares, each $q_a$ a polynomial,
and every displayed summand of degree at most $2d$. An empty certificate
class has infimum $+\infty$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete full-profile certificates]
\label{thm:v23-dual}
For the finite protocol above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-complete-dual}
 V^{\rm clk}_{\sigma,\mathbf K}(\Theta,G)
 =\max_{(z,v)\in\mathcal F}v=\inf_{d\ge1}U_d,
 \qquad U_{d+1}\le U_d.
\end{equation}
A feasible occupation array is a constructive lower certificate. An
identity \eqref{eq:v23-sos} is an upper certificate for every stochastic
machine of the specified full profile. For rational experimental data,
every rational $u>V^{\rm clk}_{\sigma,\mathbf K}$ admits such a
certificate with rational coefficients. The same conclusions hold with
shared rows, including the autonomous case.

Let $\ell_r$ be the best worst-case payoff among the actual controller
tables whose entries are dyadic with denominator $2^r$. If transition
$t$ has at most $s_t$ choices per row, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-two-sided}
 \ell_r\le V^{\rm clk}_{\sigma,\mathbf K}
 \le \min\left\{U_d,\ell_r+
                2\sum_t(s_t-1)2^{-r}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Here repeated uses of a shared row are counted repeatedly in the sum.
Thus there are matching constructive and converse certificates to any
positive accuracy, without a minimax interchange or a convexification
of fixed-width machines.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first equality follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-occupation} and
the inequalities $v\le L_\theta(z)$. Compactness gives attainment.
Evaluating \eqref{eq:v23-sos} at any feasible point gives $v\le u$.
Increasing $d$ only enlarges the certificate class.

For completeness, take $u>\max_{\mathcal F}v$. The polynomial $u-v$
is strictly positive on $\mathcal F$. Apply Putinar's positivity theorem
\cite{Putinar1993} to the inequalities $g_b\ge0$ and the pairs
$h_a\ge0$, $-h_a\ge0$. The explicitly included ball makes the quadratic
module Archimedean. The resulting identity has finite degree. Collecting
the two multipliers of $h_a$ gives \eqref{eq:v23-sos}. Letting $u$
decrease to the maximum proves the second equality. For rational data
and rational $u$, the rational version with an explicit ball generator
\cite[Theorem 6]{Powers2009} gives rational sums of squares and rational
multipliers. This is an application of those positivity theorems, not a
new Positivstellensatz.

Finally round each stochastic row to the dyadic simplex, preserving its
causal zeros, at total-variation cost at most $(s_t-1)2^{-r}$. Round a
shared row once and use that rounded row at every occurrence. Coupling
the original and rounded executions until their first discrepancy bounds
the probability of a discrepancy by the sum in
\eqref{eq:v23-two-sided}, capped at one. The payoff range is at most
two. This estimate is uniform in $\theta$, so rounding a maximizing
machine proves the rightmost bound. The dyadic machines are actual
machines, which proves the left bound. Their finite enumeration is a
construction, even though its cost may be large.
\end{proof}

A numerical semidefinite solution is not by itself an identity
\eqref{eq:v23-sos}; coefficients and nonnegativity must be certified.
The theorem proves convergence, not a uniform degree bound, finite
termination at $u=V$, or an efficient algorithm. Its necessity statement
is nevertheless different from a resource upper construction: every
machine obeys every certified upper bound. Dropping compatibility
constraints produces a relaxation. It does not preserve the exact value
in general, as Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-bsc} will demonstrate explicitly.

\begin{corollary}[Compact families and finite experimental adapters]
\label{cor:v23-compact}
Suppose $B$ is a compact family of finite experimental kernels on a fixed
schedule with $N$ environment calls. Let $B_j\subset B$ be a finite
$\omega_j$-net in the maximum conditional total-variation metric, with
$\omega_j\to0$. Use the same bounded terminal payoff throughout. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-net-dual}
 \ell_r(B_j)-2N\omega_j
 \le V^{\rm clk}_{\sigma,\mathbf K}(B,G)
 \le U_d(B_j).
\end{equation}
As $j,r,d$ tend to infinity in a suitable order, the two sides have common
limit $V^{\rm clk}_{\sigma,\mathbf K}(B,G)$. A certified adapter changing
call $i$ by at most $\delta_i$ contributes
$2\sum_i\delta_i$ to a payoff comparison. A simulator with profile
$\mathbf R$ is charged by the product profile $(R_tK_t)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For any one controller, couple corresponding calls until the first
unequal outcome. Its expected payoff varies by at most $2N\omega_j$
between a kernel and its net representative. Hence
$V(B_j)-2N\omega_j\le V(B)\le V(B_j)$. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-dual} to $B_j$ and choose the dyadic and certificate
accuracies after choosing $j$. The same coupling with unequal
$\delta_i$ gives the adapter bound. Retaining the ordered pair of
simulator and controller states proves the product assertion.
\end{proof}

An effective net is additional data; abstract compactness is not an
algorithm for an arbitrary presentation. The finite-alphabet, compact
parameter collision experiments considered below have this interface.
Consequently their fixed-schedule, full-profile values admit the same
all-auditor certificates. This implication does not evaluate those
certificates numerically for every physical sample budget, nor does it
identify a minimal autonomous implementation.
