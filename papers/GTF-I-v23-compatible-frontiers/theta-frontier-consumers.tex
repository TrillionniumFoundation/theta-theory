\section{Consumers of the compatible frontier}
\label{sec:v23-consumers}
The preceding characterizations and the constructive continuation
results below have the same object of optimization: one experiment,
one schedule and one whole register profile. This section records the
connections before developing the underlying marked and physical
experiments in detail.

\subsection{Reset scores and physical protocols}
In the reset interface of Section~\ref{sec:v20-spectrum}, a fixed
candidate supplies the training and candidate-validation kernels. The
target supplies its own preparation and validation kernels. The final
score is a function of the two retained validation bits. Include the
response selection, all input-bit buffers, and all randomization cuts
in $\sigma$. This produces precisely the finite protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:v23-occupations}. The image of the candidate family
is not replaced by its convex hull.

\begin{corollary}[Common constructive and converse interface]
\label{cor:v23-reset-consumer}
At any fixed finite reset protocol and full profile, the best worst-case
score has the occupation characterization of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-occupation}; for a finite family it has the complete
dual of Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-dual}. For a compact family with effective
finite kernel nets it has the converging upper and lower certificates
of Corollary~\ref{cor:v23-compact}. The response-cover machines of
Theorems~\ref{thm:v21-endtoend} and \ref{thm:v22-tournament} supply
feasible lower certificates in these very classes. Any certified dual
bound applies to those machines and to every other stochastic machine
with the same protocol and profile.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Augment the hidden index by the fixed candidate parameter and the
finite public trial history. Selection and memory updates are
parameter-independent observer rows. Experimental draws are the known
linear environment operations. Terminal scoring is linear in the
terminal occupation array. Thus the realization theorem applies in
both directions. The constructive machines specify legal rows at
every cut and hence feasible occupation arrays. Compact-family and
dual assertions are exactly the corresponding general theorems.
\end{proof}

For the positive-noise marked collision experiment, the finite
row--report alphabet is fixed and the private row family is compact.
On compact subfamilies with the stated continuous kernel data, finite
kernel nets give the preceding certificates. The microscopic and
Gaussian comparison theorems below then transfer a certified payoff
with their stated common-preparation error. If training kernels differ,
their errors must also be charged; equality of the training source
cannot be inferred from equality of the terminal validation task.
The active-state estimate in Section~\ref{sec:v22-active} can replace
a worst-case telescoping estimate for a fixed retained execution, but
is not a bound on a full discarded transcript.

Thus the physical resource constructions $(399,40602)$,
$(320000,1604)$ and $(12800\cdot2^{400},12)$ are now accompanied by a
general all-auditor converse procedure on each fixed full profile.
They remain attainable resource points, not a list of proved Pareto
optima. In particular, the numerical statement
$3\le W_{2/5}^{\rm phys}\le12$ is not changed into an equality without
an additional certificate or construction.

\subsection{An exact confidence consumer}
Let a reset audit return $D\in\{-1,0,1\}$. Suppose its null mean is
zero and its alternative mean is at least $g$, where $0<g\le1$ is
known. On each independent repetition, use a fresh coin with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-consumer-coin}
 \Pp(Z=1\mid D)=\frac12+\frac{D-g/2}{4}.
\end{equation}
Under the null its success probability is $p=1/2-g/8$, and under the
alternative it is at least $1-p$. Consider the declared coin interface
consisting of the null Bernoulli law $p$ and all alternative Bernoulli
laws with parameter in $[1-p,1]$, independently repeated.

\begin{corollary}[Exact three-repetition confidence frontier]
\label{cor:v23-confidence}
At the atomic retained-coin profile $(2,2,2)$, the minimum possible
maximum of type-I and type-II error over this coin interface is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-confidence-exact}
 p-\frac13p(1-p)(1-2p).
\end{equation}
The rule \eqref{eq:v23-optimal-readout} attains it. Increasing only the
middle retained-coin alphabet to three changes this value to
$p-p(1-p)(1-2p)$. If one underlying audit uses $b$ candidate training
trials, two validation trials, and peak $W$, the three-repetition
construction uses $3(b+2)$ preparations. Its inner audit, coin sampler,
and outer state are charged jointly, rather than identifying an outer
two-state register with the entire physical register.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The alternative family contains the endpoint $1-p$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-bsc} gives the lower bound on the maximum error.
For the attaining test, its probability of deciding one, as a function
of the three input bits, is coordinatewise nondecreasing: the middle
state is AND, and the four final probabilities are $0,2/3,1,1$.
Couple Bernoulli inputs with common independent uniforms. The power
is then nondecreasing in their parameter, so the largest type-II error
occurs at the endpoint. This proves attainment for the whole declared
alternative interval. The majority test proves the assertion with
three middle states in the same way. Preparation counts follow by
repeating the base experiment independently three times. Product
registers implement the outer transitions together with each inner
repetition.
\end{proof}

If $g$ is dyadic, the coin in \eqref{eq:v23-consumer-coin} has a finite
dyadic sampler. At its internal cuts an explicit conservative bound
is $2\max(W,9)$ for the outer state and inner audit/coin sampler; the
additional $2/3$ terminal row is either an atomic rational operation or
is replaced by the finite-precision realization in
Remark~\ref{rem:v23-bsc-precision}. A fully fair-bit-serial realization
has the conservative peak $\max\{2\max(W,9),12\}$ and the indicated
$2^{-r}$ error allowance. This does not erase its clock or its bits
from the schedule.

The exact lower bound concerns the declared coin interface. A physical
subfamily inherits that lower bound only if it contains its endpoint
experiment (or an appropriate certified limiting experiment). A raw
physical auditor that uses more of $D$ or of the original report is
not restricted to this interface. For such auditors the general
occupation converse, with those richer inputs retained, is the relevant
statement. The confidence result is therefore a proved consumer, not a
relabeling of an expected-score lower bound as a hypothesis-testing
lower bound.

\subsection{The foundation and the longer program}
The initial objects remain positive experimental kernels, preparations,
causal interventions and executable continuations. Finite protocols
are a resource-controlled subcategory, not a replacement for the
standard Borel formulation or its limiting targets. The new
occupation and continuation results supply a precise G3-type finite
resource layer. The marked deficiency and Gaussian transport results
supply the comparison maps used above. The finite resource theorems
do not supply a missing kinetic generator domain, a compactness theorem
for collision measures, or an optional-projection theorem on a general
operator domain.

The full historical dependency graph is retained in
Appendix~\ref{sec:v20-program}. The geometric A2 chain has its own
premises. The B4 state-dependent control-transfer, range and compactness
targets, and the broader C2 operator and rigidity targets, retain their
original scope. Their relation to this paper is through explicit
experiment and simulator interfaces when those have been established,
not through a declaration that every manuscript is a consequence of
a finite-state comparison theorem.
