\section{Weighted continuation obstructions}
\label{sec:v20-weighted}
We next give a distribution-sensitive version of the wordwise obstruction.
It applies to finite-valued terminal decisions and is useful even when a
word of small probability should not be assigned the same weight as a
likely one.

Let $f:\Sigma_1\times\cdots\times\Sigma_L\to\mathcal A$ have finite
range. The residual of a prefix $x$ at cut $t$ is the function
$v\mapsto f(xv)$ on the suffix alphabet. Write $r_t(f)$ for the number
of distinct residuals. A stochastic machine has a charged register at
every cut; all persistent randomness is included in this register.
Its wordwise error is $e(w)=\Pr(\widehat f(w)\ne f(w))$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-valued realization and weighted cut bound]
\label{thm:v20-weighted}
Exact stochastic computation of $f$ requires $K_t\ge r_t(f)$ at every
cut. The whole residual profile is simultaneously attained by a
deterministic machine.

Fix a cut, prefixes $x_1,\ldots,x_r$, and a graph on $[r]$. For each
edge $ij$ choose a suffix $v_{ij}$ such that
$f(x_iv_{ij})\ne f(x_jv_{ij})$. Let $\mu(w)\ge0$ be arbitrary word
weights. Choose edge weights $\lambda_{ij}\ge0$ satisfying the word
capacity constraints
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-congestion}
 \sum_{ij:\,w=x_iv_{ij}\ \text{or}\ w=x_jv_{ij}}\lambda_{ij}
 \le\mu(w)\quad\hbox{for every word }w.
\end{equation}
Every machine with $K$ states at this cut satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-graph}
 \sum_w\mu(w)e(w)\ \ge\
 \min_{\phi:[r]\to[K]}
       \sum_{ij:\,\phi(i)=\phi(j)}\lambda_{ij}.
\end{equation}
The right side is a weighted monochromatic-edge obstruction. Maximizing
it over admissible witnesses and weights gives a finite,
distribution-sensitive lower bound. The bound can be an equality with
positive error.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $a_i(m)$ be the state distribution after prefix $x_i$. For a fixed
edge and a common state $m$, the future output distribution on suffix
$v_{ij}$ is the same for both prefixes. Since their correct outputs are
different, the sum of their two conditional error probabilities is at
least one. Multiplying by the smaller state mass and summing gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-overlap}
 e(x_iv_{ij})+e(x_jv_{ij})
 \ge \sum_{m=1}^K\min\{a_i(m),a_j(m)\}.
\end{equation}
Weight the inequalities by $\lambda_{ij}$ and use
\eqref{eq:v20-congestion}. The resulting lower bound is
\[
 F(a_1,\ldots,a_r)=\sum_{ij}\lambda_{ij}
                              \sum_m\min\{a_i(m),a_j(m)\}.
\]
This is a concave continuous function on $(\Delta_K)^r$. Every point
of that product is a convex combination of its deterministic vertices;
concavity implies that its value is at least the minimum over those
vertices. A vertex is a labeling $\phi:[r]\to[K]$, and its value is
exactly the right side of \eqref{eq:v20-graph}. This proves the bound
without treating stochastic encodings as deterministic encodings.

For zero error, choose one distinguishing suffix for every two different
residuals. Equation~\eqref{eq:v20-overlap} forces the corresponding state
supports to be disjoint. Hence at least $r_t(f)$ states are needed.
For the converse, keep the residual class of the observed prefix. Equal
residuals have equal shifts after every next input, so the next class is
well defined. At the last epoch the residual is the output label itself.
These quotient transitions realize all widths simultaneously.

For a positive-error equality, let the only relevant words be two
one-letter prefixes with different required labels, weighted $p$ and
$1-p$, where $0<p<1$. Put $K=1$ and
$\lambda_{12}=\min(p,1-p)$. The cut obstruction is $\min(p,1-p)$,
attained by always emitting the more likely label. Thus the weighted
constant is sometimes exact; universal sharpness for all graphs or
input distributions is not asserted. With unit weights on the same two
words the total-error lower bound is one, also attained by a constant
output; this is equality in the earlier $(r-K)/(r-1)$ bound at $r=2$,
$K=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A Bayes-regret obstruction for constrained audits]
\label{cor:v20-bayes}
Fix a finite action set $\mathcal A$, candidate class $B$, and a
probability measure $\nu$ on $B$. For a training word $w$, let $p_w(b)$
be its likelihood and let $f_a(b)$ be the expected validation payoff.
Put
\[
 R_w(a)=\int_Bp_w(b)f_a(b)\,d\nu(b),\qquad
 S_\nu=\sum_w\max_a R_w(a).
\]
Choose a maximizing label $f(w)$. Set
$\mu(w)=R_w(f(w))-\max_{a\ne f(w)}R_w(a)$, taking zero when a maximum
is tied. Let $\Gamma_{t,K}(f,\mu)$ be any lower bound supplied by
\eqref{eq:v20-graph}. Then every width-$K$ audit at cut $t$ has
worst-case expected payoff at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-bayes}
 S_\nu-\Gamma_{t,K}(f,\mu).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The conditional distribution of the auditor's output for a fixed word
is independent of the unknown candidate. Its Bayes payoff is therefore
$\sum_{w,a}R_w(a)\Pr(\widehat f(w)=a)$. Subtracting this from $S_\nu$
gives at least $\sum_w\mu(w)e(w)$. Apply the theorem, and use that a
minimum over candidates is at most the average under $\nu$.
\end{proof}

The corollary does not exchange a maximum and minimum over a nonconvex
fixed-width machine class. The prior is a lower-bound device, not a
randomization resource supplied to the candidate. With a unique maximizing
label and strictly positive margins at every word, equality with $S_\nu$
forces exact wordwise computation. This makes explicit the uniqueness
step used in the odd-sample binary theorem below. For even sample size,
the balanced-count words have zero likelihood gap. A rule can differ on
those ties without losing value; equality of values then does not force
one prescribed total Boolean rule, and the odd-sample width profile cannot
simply be asserted for that case.
