\section{Acquisition, clocks, and the resource order}
\label{sec:v23-acquisition}
A statistical bound is ambiguous unless the protocol and the resource
coordinates being compared are specified. The superscript ${\rm clk}$
in the preceding theorems is therefore part of their notation, not a
qualification confined to an implementation paragraph.

\subsection{Comparable resource points}
On one declared schedule, profiles are ordered coordinatewise.
Enlarging a register alphabet preserves every previously feasible
machine by padding unused states. Inserting observations is comparable
only through an explicit embedding that says which new inputs are
ignored and which registers survive the inserted cuts. Atomic and
bit-serial schedules are different schedules, even when they have the
same number of experimental preparations.

For a finite-precision implementation we record
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-resource-tuple}
 \mathfrak r=(N_B,N_Q,\mathbf K,r,D,\mathsf C).
\end{equation}
Here $N_B,N_Q$ count candidate and target preparations, $r$ bounds
fair bits per declared row sampler, $D$ bounds a specified read-only
description, and $\mathsf C$ specifies clocked or autonomous execution.
A description is measured in a fixed code, not by an unspecified
notion of compressibility. For example, with a known table layout,
store the first $s-1$ numerators of a $2^{-r}$ stochastic row in
$(s-1)(r+1)$ bits; its last numerator is the remainder. The layout,
response dictionary and any program used to expand repeated phases
have their own fixed-code costs. Sample-dependent calibration is not
part of that read-only description.

For the same code and schedule, increasing numerical resource caps
or register alphabets gives an inclusion of machine classes. Changing
$\mathsf C$ does not mean increasing one numerical coordinate. A
clocked execution with $T$ update epochs and profile $\mathbf K$ has an
autonomous realization on the disjoint union of its phase registers,
using at most $\sum_{t=0}^T K_t$ states. This is an attainable conversion,
not an equality of optimal frontiers. Shared-row optimization in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-occupation} can be strictly smaller than that
conversion.

\begin{proposition}[Joint finite resource optimization]
\label{prop:v23-resource-order}
Fix a finite experimental protocol and a finite table code. With finite
caps on description and precision, the feasible machines form a finite
set and their optimal worst-case payoff is exactly the maximum over
that set. If the precision restriction is removed on a fixed table
architecture, Theorems~\ref{thm:v23-occupation} and
\ref{thm:v23-dual} characterize its exact value. A finite collection
of permitted architectures is handled by taking the maximum of their
values. Calibration calls can be included as an initial part of the
same protocol, with all their retained information passed through the
same profile constraints.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
There are finitely many descriptions of bounded length and finitely
many allowed dyadic rows. Retain exactly the descriptions that decode
to legal tables of the specified finite schedule and budgets. Each
has a well-defined payoff. For unrestricted stochastic rows on a fixed
architecture, use its observable row sets in the occupation formulation.
Optimizing over a finite union is a maximum over its components. A
calibration prefix uses known experimental kernels at the unknown
environment in exactly the same way as any other prefix; its
sample-dependent state is an ordinary retained register. Thus it
introduces neither a parameter oracle nor a new exception to the
compatibility constraints.
\end{proof}

The proposition supplies a joint optimization model rather than merely
a post hoc resource ledger. It does not claim a short closed formula
for arbitrary description budgets. For compact continuous families the
net condition in Corollary~\ref{cor:v23-compact} is still required for
effective finite certificates.

\subsection{Acquiring a calibration rather than assuming it}
The next result gives a fully priced constructive calibration prefix.
Let $h_1,\ldots,h_K:\Omega\to[0,1]$ be a fixed known response list,
chosen independently of the calibration observations, and suppose its
values have denominator $2^r$. Write $d=|\Omega|$ and
$q_i=Qh_i$. The target $Q$ need not be given as a table: independent
preparations from $Q$ are available. Let $M=2^s$ and $0<\beta<1$.

\begin{theorem}[Sampled calibration with persistent-register pricing]
\label{thm:v23-calibration}
For every $\rho>0$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-calibration-samples}
 M\ge\frac{1}{2\rho^2}\log\frac{2K}{\beta},
\end{equation}
there is a clocked finite-precision calibration protocol using $M$
target preparations and $MKr$ fair bits. It returns
$\widehat q_i\in\{0,1/M,\ldots,1\}$ with
\[
 \Pp\left(\max_i|\widehat q_i-q_i|\le\rho\right)\ge1-\beta.
\]
The complete counter register has at most $C=(M+1)^K$ states; a word
buffer and the three-status dyadic sampler give peak at most $3dC$.
If a subsequent auditor has peak at most $W$ for any exogenously fixed
calibration vector, retaining the acquired calibration gives the bound
$C\max(3d,W)$ for the combined execution.

Let $v\in[-1,1]$ and $\epsilon\ge0$. Suppose, uniformly for calibration vectors $\widetilde q$, the auditor's
true expected score is at least
$v-\epsilon-2\|\widetilde q-q\|_\infty$, and its score is in $[-1,1]$.
With fresh training and validation after this prefix, the combined
expected score is at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-calibrated-score}
 v-\epsilon-2\rho-2\beta.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On target trial $j$, retain its word $Y_j$. For each $i$, generate a
Bernoulli variable $B_{ij}$ of parameter $h_i(Y_j)$ with an independent
$r$-bit dyadic sampler, and increment counter $i$. After all $K$ updates,
discard $Y_j$. For fixed $i$, the variables $B_{ij}$ over different
trials are independent, lie in $\{0,1\}$, and have mean $q_i$. Their
averages $\widehat q_i$ therefore satisfy
\[
 \Pp(|\widehat q_i-q_i|>\rho)\le2e^{-2M\rho^2}.
\]
This bounded-variable exponential estimate follows from the exponential
moment bound used in Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-spectrum}. A union bound
proves the probability assertion. Independence between different
counters is not required.

The $K$ counters each have $M+1$ possible values. The word buffer has
$d$ values, and the running comparison of a uniform $r$-bit word with
a fixed dyadic threshold has three comparison statuses. These yield
the stated state counts at every sampler cut. Trial, response and bit
indices are in the declared clocked schedule, not in an unmentioned
work tape. The persistent counter vector remains in the register of
the subsequent auditor and multiplies its width by at most $C$.
All its values are dyadic because $M=2^s$.

Conditional on the calibration vector, later observations have their
original independent laws. On the good calibration event the score
bound is $v-\epsilon-2\rho$. On the complementary event the score is
at least $-1$. Since $v\le1$ and $\epsilon,\rho\ge0$, a loss of at
most two times that event's probability suffices. This proves
\eqref{eq:v23-calibrated-score}.
\end{proof}

For the response-cover tournament of Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-tournament},
one may take
\[
 N_B=n(K-1),\quad W=3dK(2m+1),\quad
 \epsilon=4(K-1)\theta+2(K-1)
                         (e^{-4\theta m}+e^{-2n\theta^2})
\]
when $v$ is the envelope of the already dyadic response list. Starting
with an arbitrary list and rounding it to $r$ bits incurs the additional
$2^{1-r}$ term of that theorem. The new prefix prices target acquisition
and its persistent calibration state. It is an explicit construction,
not an optimal calibration-memory claim. Joint optimality at fixed
finite budgets is the distinct problem in
Proposition~\ref{prop:v23-resource-order}.
