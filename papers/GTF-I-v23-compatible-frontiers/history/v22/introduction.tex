\section{Introduction}
An observation can be informative and yet be unusable after a narrow
causal checkpoint. Conversely, a large empirical statistic may be
unnecessary when the observer is permitted a longer preparation
schedule. A resource theory of statistical experiments must distinguish
these effects. It must constrain the acquisition of a continuation,
the information carried through each intervening cut, and the execution
of its terminal decision. A bound on the last of these alone is not a
bound on the complete experiment.

We study this question with positive experimental kernels, explicit
preparations, and parameter-independent causal machines. Registers are
charged at every declared observation and randomization cut. The
schedule, calibration precision, read-only transition description and
number of preparations are separate resources. An external clock
supplies a predetermined transition, not a sample-dependent statistic;
an autonomous implementation must store that clock. The parameter
image is retained in its original form and need not be convex.

There are two complementary kinds of result. A realization theorem
turns a finite response cover into a bounded-state, finite-precision
training-and-validation machine. An exact risk theorem optimizes over
all machines satisfying a given multicut profile, rather than over
implementations of one chosen decision rule. Both can be transported
under a controlled change of the experiment, with the simulator's
register and the altered preparations priced explicitly.

\subsection{Statistical resource frontiers}
Let $B\subset\Delta(\Omega)$ be compact, $d=|\Omega|$, and let $Q$ be
a known target. A reset auditor trains on independent trials from one
fixed unknown $b\in B$, selects a validation event, and reports the
target-event indicator minus the candidate-event indicator on two
fresh validations. A randomized event has a mean response
$h\in[0,1]^\Omega$. Its $K$-response envelope is
\[
 \mathfrak V_K(B,Q)=\max_{h_1,\ldots,h_K\in[0,1]^\Omega}
                         \min_{b\in B}\max_{i\le K}(Q-b)h_i.
\]
Write $\kappa_c=\min\{K:\mathfrak V_K>c\}$. Let
$\mathsf V_{N,\boldsymbol K}$ be the best worst-case score of a whole
schedule with at most $N$ training trials and state profile
$\boldsymbol K$, and let $W_c$ minimize peak width over all finite
successful schedules. The distinction between $\kappa_c$ and
$\mathsf V_{N,\boldsymbol K}$ is the distinction between a decision
interface and its complete statistical realization.

\begin{theorem}[Continuation profiles and statistical resources]
\label{thm:v22-main}
The following statements hold in the declared clocked finite-state
model.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(i)} Every strict $K$-response cover of envelope $v$ has, for
integers $m,n,r\ge1$ and dyadic calibration error $\rho$, a rational
whole-schedule realization with
\[
 N=n(K-1),\qquad W\le3dK(2m+1),
\]
and worst-case score at least
\[
 v-2^{1-r}-2\rho-4(K-1)\theta
       -2(K-1)\bigl(e^{-4\theta m}+e^{-2n\theta^2}\bigr).
\]
Here $K\ge2$; one response needs no training. The previously available
constant-width realization gives, in the same model,
$\kappa_c\le W_c\le W_c^{\rm rat}\le12d\kappa_c$.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(ii)} Suppose a parameter in $\{1,\ldots,M\}$ has known ordered
prior $\pi$, and report $t$ independently reveals that parameter with
probability $q_t$ and otherwise reports $*$. Among all stochastic
machines having at most $K_t$ labels after each report, the exact
Bayesian success probability is
\[
 a\pi_1+\sum_{t=1}^n w_t\sum_{j=1}^{k_t}\pi_j,
 \quad
 k_t=\min(M,K_t,\ldots,K_n),
\]
where $a=\prod_t(1-q_t)$ and
$w_t=q_t\prod_{s>t}(1-q_s)$. One compatible deterministic machine
attains all the cut bounds simultaneously. The formula yields exact
sample--peak tradeoffs and exact losses under checkpoint insertion.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(iii)} If $P_t,Q_t$ are the induced retained-state transition
kernels of a fixed execution with common initial law, its terminal-law
change is bounded by
\[
 \min\left(1,\sum_t\E_P
           \|P_t(X_{t-1},\cdot)-Q_t(X_{t-1},\cdot)\|_{\TV}\right).
\]
For absorbed reset comparisons this prices active updates even when
training laws differ. It does not remove ignored preparations from
the schedule or from the full raw-data law.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(iv)} In the marked positive-noise collision experiment, a
$320000$-training-trial audit with peak width at most $1604$ has score
greater than $2/5$. The $399$-trial, $40602$-state audit and the
$12$-state long-clock audit are also retained. The exact row-indexed
decision width remains three. Separate finite-state amplifiers yield
specified type-I and type-II errors, with a polynomial-horizon growing
counter or a constant register and a longer clocked schedule.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Part (i) is Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-tournament} together with
Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-endtoend}. Part (ii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-revelation} and its corollaries. Part (iii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-active} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:v22-active-training}. Part (iv) follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-physical},
Corollary~\ref{cor:v22-physical-regimes}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-confidence}.
\end{proof}

The exact frontier in part (ii) is an all-algorithm statistical theorem
for a growing family with arbitrary priors and profiles. Its optimum
is Bayesian, whereas part (i) is uniform over the original candidate
image. These objectives are not interchanged. The attaining revelation
machine uses nested optimal label sets. Their compatibility is the
reason that the separate bottleneck inequalities are jointly sharp;
independently choosing an optimal dictionary at every cut would not
prove such a statement for general experiments.

The general realization bounds in part (i) remain constructive upper
bounds at fixed $N$. They do not purport to optimize every sample--memory
frontier. In the physical family, the exact optimum at the decision
cut and the full-schedule interval $3\le W^{\rm phys}_{2/5}\le12$
are different assertions. The three displayed physical implementations
exhibit distinct attainable resource regimes. An exact fixed-sample
physical optimum is not inferred from the exact residual profile of
its empirical selector.

\subsection{Comparison, realization, and earlier work}
The statistical comparison background is Blackwell--Le Cam theory and
its filtered versions~\cite{Blackwell1953,LeCam1964,Torgersen1991,
Norberg2002,Weisshaupt2002}. Weisshaupt's filtered-operator definition
and Lemma 1 on p.~14 and Theorem 5 on pp.~15--16 concern compact
convex classes of positive operators, with risk criteria implying a
filtered randomization. Here the constrained object is an executable
finite-state machine with a priced register, precision and clock.
Its behavior image need not be convex. A filtered existence theorem
does not by itself give the full-profile budgets proved below.
The original Norberg proof text has not been obtained for a definitive
theorem-level crosswalk; no absence or priority claim is made on that
basis.

Residual minimization, positive realization, and finite-state learning
are established subjects~\cite{Nerode1958,Fliess1975,Cover1969,
HellmanCover1970}. Cover's four-state time-dependent threshold learner
and explicit clock discussion are particularly relevant. The absorbing
walk and the bounded-variable concentration estimate used here are
also classical~\cite{Hoeffding1963}. The contribution is not a new name
for those mechanisms. It is the finite-horizon passage from a general
nonconvex response cover to a complete rational machine, the exact
profile-constrained statistical optimization in the revelation class,
and the compatible error and resource transfer to physical and
confidence-level consumers.

\subsection{Proof structure and the wider program}
The underlying marked deficiency, normalized continuations and decision
spectrum are followed by finite precision, compatible multicut
realization, and bounded-state training. Sections~\ref{sec:v22-frontier}
and \ref{sec:v22-revelation} give the preparation--memory statements;
Section~\ref{sec:v22-active} supplies their retained-law transport.
The weighted and joint residual obstructions, reset minimax results,
nonlinear and adaptive tests, and the entire microscopic and Gaussian
transport chain are included with their proofs. Their physical and
confidence consumers appear in Sections~\ref{sec:v22-physical} and
\ref{sec:v22-confidence}.

The bounded common-preparation Gaussian/entropic consumer is used only
under its stated positive-noise hypotheses. Appendix~\ref{sec:v20-program}
retains the namespaced eleven-component program and its real proof
obligations. The primary A2 geometric chain is independent; the kinetic
B4 and broader C2 operator targets are not replaced by fictitious
arrows from finite-state comparison. The resource layer supplies
preparation, memory, calibration and simulation bounds within that
program, while its model-specific and singular-limit theorems retain
their full mathematical targets.
