\section{An exact noisy frontier and the cost of hidden mixing}
\label{sec:v23-three}
Let $\theta\in\{0,1\}$, and let $Y_1,Y_2,Y_3$ be conditionally
independent binary reports with
\[
 \Pp_\theta(Y_t\ne\theta)=p,\qquad 0<p<1/2.
\]
Every report has overlapping support under the two hypotheses. A
clocked machine has at most $K_t$ labels after report $t$ and makes a
binary terminal decision. Updates are atomic stochastic operations;
additional random-bit cuts are discussed below. Let $B_{\mathbf K}(p)$
be its optimal success under the fair prior, and let
$V_{\mathbf K}(p)$ be its optimal minimum of the two conditional success
probabilities. Both optimize over all stochastic machines of this
profile. Put
\[
 d(p)=p(1-p)(1-2p)>0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Complete three-report profile frontier]
\label{thm:v23-bsc}
For every positive integer profile $(K_1,K_2,K_3)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-bsc-frontier}
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{profile condition}&B_{\mathbf K}(p)&V_{\mathbf K}(p)\\\hline
K_3=1&1/2&1/2\\
K_3\ge2,\ \min(K_1,K_2)=1&1-p&1-p\\
K_1,K_3\ge2,\ K_2=2&1-p+\tfrac12d(p)&1-p+\tfrac13d(p)\\
K_1,K_3\ge2,\ K_2\ge3&1-p+d(p)&1-p+d(p)
\end{array}
\end{equation}
In the third line a minimax optimal machine retains $Y_1$, then retains
$Y_1Y_2$ (the Boolean AND), and after the third report decides one with
probability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-optimal-readout}
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
(M_2,Y_3)&(0,0)&(0,1)&(1,0)&(1,1)\\\hline
\Pp(\widehat\theta=1\mid M_2,Y_3)&0&2/3&1&1.
\end{array}
\end{equation}
The two conditional success probabilities are equal. In particular,
for $p=1/4$ and profile $(2,2,2)$ the exact Bayesian and minimax values
are $51/64$ and $25/32$, whereas profile $(2,3,2)$ has value $27/32$
for both objectives.
\end{theorem}

We prove the minimax assertion without passing to a convex hull of
machines. The following elementary binary-channel domination is useful.

\begin{lemma}[Reduction of a two-report binary register]
\label{lem:v23-domination}
Every stochastic binary summary of $(Y_1,Y_2)$ is, up to exchanging its
two output labels, a stochastic postprocessing of a summary with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-edge-quantizer}
 \Pp(M=1\mid 00)=0,\qquad
 \Pp(M=1\mid 01)=\Pp(M=1\mid 10)=w,\qquad
 \Pp(M=1\mid 11)=1
\end{equation}
for some $w\in[0,1]$. This domination holds simultaneously under both
hypotheses and continues to hold after appending an independent third
report.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $u,v$ for the summary probabilities on $00,11$, and write $b$
for the average of its probabilities on $01,10$. Those two words
have the same probability under either hypothesis, so their average
contains all relevant information. Exchange output labels if necessary
to arrange $v\ge u$, and put $\alpha=v-u\in[0,1]$ and
$c=2p(1-p)\in(0,1)$. Its output-one probabilities satisfy
\[
 r_0=u+c(b-u)+p^2\alpha,
 \qquad r_1-r_0=(1-2p)\alpha.
\]
For the summary \eqref{eq:v23-edge-quantizer} these probabilities are
$s_0=p^2+cw$, $s_1=(1-p)^2+cw$. Seek a postprocessing that sends input
zero to one with probability $a$ and input one to one with probability
$a+\alpha$. It is stochastic precisely when $0\le a\le1-\alpha$,
and it reproduces $r_0,r_1$ if
\[
 a=u+c(b-u)-c\alpha w.
\]
As $w$ ranges over $[0,1]$, the right side ranges from
$u+c(b-v)$ to $u+c(b-u)$. The upper endpoint is nonnegative. The lower
endpoint is at most $1-\alpha$, since
\[
 1-\alpha-u-c(b-v)=(1-v)+c(v-b)
                           \ge(1-v)(1-c)\ge0.
\]
Consequently this interval meets $[0,1-\alpha]$. This proves the
claim, including $\alpha=0$. Applying the postprocessing independently
of the third report proves the last statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-bsc}]
If the last register has one state, its terminal decision is independent
of the reports. The fair-prior value is one half, and a fair terminal
randomization gives minimax value one half. If an earlier register has
one state, at most the last two reports remain informative. The
unrestricted optimal success from two equally reliable reports is
$1-p$ under either criterion: use their common value on agreement and
a fair decision on disagreement. One report already gives $1-p$.
These rules respect the stated profiles and prove the second line.

Suppose henceforth that $K_1,K_3\ge2$. There is no loss in retaining
$Y_1$ exactly. Indeed any stochastic first update followed by a second
update induces one binary channel from $(Y_1,Y_2)$ to $M_2$, and that
channel can be implemented directly when $Y_1$ is retained. Allowing
more than two first-stage labels gives no larger set of such channels.
At the last stage any randomized binary decision based on $(M_2,Y_3)$
can be placed directly in a two-label register. Thus, when $K_2=2$,
the unrestricted optimization is exactly over a binary summary of the
first two reports and a randomized readout of it together with $Y_3$.

For the Bayesian problem, Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-interval} reduces the
middle update to intervals of the three distinct likelihood-ratio
classes $00$, $\{01,10\}$, $11$. A nonconstant two-interval update
isolates $00$ or isolates $11$. These are the Boolean OR and AND,
up to labels. With AND, the Bayesian readout is
$\widehat\theta=Y_3\lor(Y_1\land Y_2)$. Its conditional successes are
\[
 c_0=1-p-p^2+p^3,\qquad c_1=1-2p^2+p^3.
\]
Their average is $1-p+d(p)/2$. The complementary rule interchanges
$c_0,c_1$. A constant middle update gives only $1-p$, proving the
Bayesian third line. This also covers updates that split the tied
middle words: the theorem permits assigning the entire tied class to
one maximizing suffix label without decreasing the Bayesian objective.

For the minimax problem, Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-domination} reduces the
search, without loss, to \eqref{eq:v23-edge-quantizer}. Complementing
all reports and output labels replaces $w$ by $1-w$ and interchanges
the hypotheses, so it suffices to take $0\le w\le1/2$. Write
\[
 r_0=p^2+2p(1-p)w,\qquad
 r_1=(1-p)^2+2p(1-p)w.
\]
In the experiment $(M_2,Y_3)$ the likelihood ratios are ordered as
$00,01,10,11$. Only the ordering of the middle two needs verification;
after multiplying by their positive denominators it is equivalent to
\[
 r_1(1-r_0)p^2-r_0(1-r_1)(1-p)^2
       =p^2(1-p)^2(1-2p)(1-2w)^2\ge0.
\]
Consider the test that accepts hypothesis one on $10,11$, rejects it
on $00$, and accepts it on $01$ with probability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-balanced-alpha}
 a(w)=\frac{1-r_0-r_1}
               {(1-r_0)p+(1-r_1)(1-p)}
     =\frac{2(1-2w)}{3-2w}.
\end{equation}
This number is in $[0,1]$. Its two conditional success probabilities
are equal, with common value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-Rw}
 R(w)=1-r_0-a(w)(1-r_0)p.
\end{equation}
It is a likelihood-ratio test, possibly randomized at one threshold.
Choose the prior odds equal to that threshold. The test is then
Bayesian optimal for those odds. Since its two successes are equal,
no test can have a larger minimum: such a test would also have a
larger payoff for this prior. Thus $R(w)$ is the exact minimax success
for this summary, not merely an equal-error attainable value.

Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-R-derivative}
 R'(w)=-\frac{2p(1-p)(1-2p)(5-2w)(1-2w)}{(3-2w)^2}\le0.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, an explicit nonnegative gap certificate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-gap-certificate}
 R(0)-R(w)=
 \frac{2p(1-p)(1-2p)w(5-6w)}{3(3-2w)}\ge0,
                  \qquad 0\le w\le1/2.
\end{equation}
The denominator is positive and every factor in the numerator is
nonnegative on the stated domain. Therefore the best $w$ is zero. Equations
\eqref{eq:v23-balanced-alpha}--\eqref{eq:v23-Rw} give
$a(0)=2/3$ and
\[
 R(0)=1-\frac23p-p^2+\frac23p^3
                         =1-p+\frac13d(p).
\]
The postprocessing in Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-domination} can be composed
with any final readout and does not need a retained selector. It follows
that this upper bound applies to every original stochastic machine.
The rule \eqref{eq:v23-optimal-readout} attains it.

Finally, if $K_2\ge3$, retain the number of ones in the first two
reports and output the majority of all three. Both successes are
$1-3p^2+2p^3=1-p+d(p)$. The likelihood-ratio rule for the full three
reports is majority under the fair prior, so no machine can have a
larger Bayesian value or a larger minimum. This proves the last line
and completes all cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A strict fixed-profile mixing gap]
\label{cor:v23-mixing-gap}
For profile $(2,2,2)$ a free initial selector that mixes the two
complementary Bayesian optimal machines would give worst-case success
$1-p+d(p)/2$. Without that uncharged selector the exact value is
$1-p+d(p)/3$. The excess $d(p)/6$ is strictly positive.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Mix the two payoff vectors $(c_0,c_1)$ and $(c_1,c_0)$ from the proof
with equal weights. Each coordinate becomes their average. Theorem
\ref{thm:v23-bsc} gives the smaller all-stochastic value at the original
profile. To implement the mixture literally, retain the selector
jointly with the machine state; a profile $(4,4,2)$ suffices. The
corollary does not claim that this product construction minimizes the
expanded profile.
\end{proof}

The gap identifies a specific failure of a convexified all-machine
converse. A terminal output law can be a convex combination of laws
while the machines producing those laws cannot be mixed within their
original memory profile. In contrast, the quadratic compatibility
constraints of Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-occupation} retain exactly the
required coupling. The noisy loss $2d(p)/3$ between two and three middle
states is also a strict full-schedule minimax loss; it is not an erasure
probability or the cost of remembering a label that was already known.

\begin{remark}[Atomic randomization and finite precision]
\label{rem:v23-bsc-precision}
The fraction $2/3$ in \eqref{eq:v23-optimal-readout} is an exact rational
row of the all-stochastic model. A fixed finite string of fair bits
cannot realize this fraction exactly. Replacing it by a dyadic number
within $2^{-r}$ gives a genuine finite-precision rule whose minimum
success is at least $V_{(2,2,2)}(p)-2^{-r}$. If each random-bit epoch is
a charged cut, retain $(M_2,Y_3)$ while executing the dyadic sampler;
the three-status comparison sampler of
Section~\ref{sec:v21-precision} gives a conservative peak of $12$ for
this refined execution. The theorem's exact two-state value refers to
its stated atomic-update profile, not to this finer profile. The latter
has its own exact occupation formulation. Neither an unbounded rejection
sampler nor a free fair-bit clock is hidden in the two-state assertion.
\end{remark}
