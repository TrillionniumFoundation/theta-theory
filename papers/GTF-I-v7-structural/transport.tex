\section{Causal morphisms and charged approximation}\label{sec:v7-transport}
A resource signature specifies ordered coordinates, for example
persistent cardinality, numerical precision, transient workspace,
program description, preparation count and physical time. A budget
transformer is a monotone map between two such signatures, accompanied
by an actual implementation. A coordinate may explicitly be unlimited.
The persistent-cardinality results below leave fixed programs, fixed
real constants, time and transient computation unlimited unless a more
specific implementation is given. There is no claim that these resources
are equivalent.

A causal simulation from an experiment $\mathcal E$ to $\mathcal F$
consists of a source controller, a causal transducer and its initialized
state. For every target policy the joint construction must produce the
target observation/cost history within a declared parameter-uniform
total-variation error. The transducer never sees the unknown parameter
or the unobserved physical state. Resource bounds are imposed on this
joint construction, not just on the terminal observation kernel. We
use $\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|$.

\begin{theorem}[Typed simulation and recursive approximation]
\label{thm:v7-transport}
The following statements hold.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(i)} Serial causal simulations of errors $\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2$
and budget transformers $\rho_1,\rho_2$ compose with error at most
$\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2$ and transformer $\rho_1\circ\rho_2$.
A simulator with $K$ persistent states and a target observer with $M$
states can be run with $KM$ persistent states. For losses in $[0,L]$,
the risk changes by at most $L(\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2)$. Other resource
coordinates are transformed according to the supplied implementations.
The identity simulation has zero error and the identity transformer.
Independent parallel simulations have additive TV error and the product
register, with preparations and other costs charged by their joint implementation.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(ii)} Suppose the true predictive state lies in a metric space
$(S,d)$ and updates by $s_t=F_t(s_{t-1},y_t,a_t)$. For all reports and
actions suppose $F_t$ is $\rho$-Lipschitz in its state, $\rho<1$.
Let an implementable approximate update $\widetilde F_t$ have local
error at most $\eta_t$, and let $Q_t:S\to C_t$ be a measurable finite
quantizer with radius $\epsilon_t$. The register
\begin{equation}
 \widehat s_t=Q_t\widetilde F_t(\widehat s_{t-1},y_t,a_t) 
                                                               \label{eq:v7-recursion}
\end{equation}
has at most $|C_t|$ states at time $t$ and, on every common observation
path,
\begin{equation}
 d(s_t,\widehat s_t)\le \rho^t d(s_0,\widehat s_0)
       +\sum_{j=1}^t\rho^{t-j}(\epsilon_j+\eta_j)=:b_t. 
                                                               \label{eq:v7-error}
\end{equation}
A data-dependent persistent numerical residue must be part of $C_t$;
it cannot be placed in an uncharged side register.
If the report kernels are $L_0$-Lipschitz in total variation as functions
of the predictive state, the length-$n$ simulated history differs by at
most $L_0\sum_{t<n}\E b_t$. The same statement holds up to a bounded
stopping time, with the stopped sum. If the true conditional mean is
$g(s_t)$ with $g$ $L_1$-Lipschitz into a Hilbert space, its implemented
prediction has Bayes excess at most $L_1^2\E b_t^2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compose the two transducers on their joint initialized state. Applying a
Markov kernel contracts total variation, so the triangle inequality gives
the sum of errors, uniformly over the policy and parameter for which
the two certificates were supplied. The joint register is the Cartesian
product. This proves the cardinality bound, and integration of a loss
with range $[0,L]$ proves the risk bound. The identity is the unchanged history. For independent parallel laws,
insert one product factor at a time and use TV contraction to obtain
the same sum bound. The composition order of the
budget maps follows from first realizing the target in the intermediate
experiment and then realizing that implementation in the source.

For (ii), insert $F_t(\widehat s_{t-1},y_t,a_t)$ between the two updates.
Contraction, the local defect and the quantization radius give
$d(s_t,\widehat s_t)\le\rho d(s_{t-1},\widehat s_{t-1})+
\eta_t+\epsilon_t$. Induction proves \eqref{eq:v7-error}.
For the law comparison, telescope the products of the history kernels,
replacing one kernel at a time. The prefix before the replaced kernel
is distributed according to the true experiment; its integrated local
TV error is at most $L_0\E b_t$. All suffix kernels are contractions in
TV. Summing proves the bound. An absorbing stopped state proves the
stopped version by the identical calculation. The policy is evaluated
on the same history in each local comparison: no continuity of a
possibly discontinuous policy is presumed. Finally apply conditional
orthogonality as in Proposition~\ref{prop:v7-bayes} and the Lipschitz
bound for $g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Resource transport of an attainable profile]
\label{cor:v7-resource-profile}
If an $M$-state target observer has risk $R_M$ and a $K$-state causal
simulation has error $\epsilon$ for its bounded decision problem, then
the source has a $KM$-state observer of risk at most $R_M+L\epsilon$.
Two simulations give the corresponding two directed inequalities; they
need not preserve the same cardinality. A $K$-valued decoder-side event
can always be absorbed into a register at a factor $K$, but a continuous
side observation admits no such finite-factor assertion in general.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the product construction. For the finite event store its current
value as the second register coordinate. For the last assertion, an
exact uniform real observation supplied directly to a decoder has zero
squared estimation error with one otherwise empty state. If it is
available only before an $M$-label encoding, scalar quantization has
error $1/(12M^2)>0$. Thus no finite factor simulates that side interface
for all accuracies.
\end{proof}

The theorem joins quotient approximation to experiment comparison.
Contractivity is a property to be verified on the true quotient, and
$Q_t$ is an executable representation of it. A finite-dimensional
formula for the conditional mean is sufficient for one application,
but is not a hypothesis of the theorem. In
Section~\ref{sec:v7-measure} the true state is an entire probability
measure. In the next section a different structure replaces metric
contraction by an exact finite-tree update.
