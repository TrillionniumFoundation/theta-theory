  -> recursively charged approximation
  -> verified nonlinear branch instrument and terminal test simulation

  -> arbitrary M-outcome experiment lower bound

  -> matching Gaussian M^(-2/r) experiment approximation

No historical Sinai, path LDP, particle LDP, nonlinear semigroup, common unbounded domain or phase theorem is a premise. The main article proves its causal converse instead of requiring the reader to import an older principal paper. The old pressure calculation is used only for the retained downstream exponent consequence, with its proof in the companion.

## Constants and errors

The tree constants include separation, image diameters, child floor, contraction, alphabet size and unary-chain bound. The deletion case also needs the displayed local readout-oscillation certificate; static Hilbert geometry alone is not silently sufficient to choose a consistent time decoder. The insertion model needs the true complete-history conditional mean represented by the declared past map.

The nonlinear-filter constant is explicit (`rho=6/25`). Its uniform finite-state radius is 3/(2n), and numerical defects are added before geometric summation. The W1 net is on all laws on a compact interval, not only atomic posteriors. The resulting logarithmic upper rate is not advertised as minimax sharp.

The Gaussian constants depend on the compact mean set, dimension and covariance. `H_2` is the displayed uniform Hessian operator bound after compactification, and `H_0` is the transformed marginal height bound. Both are finite suprema over known smooth Gaussian formulas; no budget or sample size enters them. The Poisson constant is deliberately loose but explicit. A2 uses fixed rank and conditioning; no constant is asserted uniform through degeneration.

The sample/register window follows from `M^(3/r)/sqrt(N) <= K^(3/r)`. The universal finite-output lower bound forces the N^(r/10) growth at target N^(-1/5). The sample endpoint N^(-1/3) is the rate of this construction in its balanced window, not a lower bound on all unquantized physical experiments.

## Preservation and checks

The complete development includes all v6/v5/v4/v3/v2 quantitative bodies and first-edition foundational sections. It preserves their hypotheses and labels; it does not claim that preservation independently re-proves them. The canonical core has the same mathematical label numbers in both documents.

`verify.py` checks finite exact tree closure and insertion, energy balance and counts, posterior noise arithmetic, empirical measure quantization, nonlinear contraction, positive reconstruction, and joint-budget powers. Numerical quadrature checks the second-order interpolation behavior and Poisson comparison scaling. Six deliberately incorrect variants are required to fail in ordinary and optimized Python. The mathematical theorems are proved in TeX, not established by these finite checks.
