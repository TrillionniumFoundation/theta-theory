The new introduction's comparison is confined to these checked statements. The graph-directed theorem concerns static distortion, while the present suffix theorem adds initialized causal execution in both time orientations. The average-cost theorem concerns control objectives and coding/policy approximations, whereas the present sharp hidden-shift result bounds the entire persistent register's Bayes excess. These distinctions do not erase overlap in the underlying quantization and contraction arguments.
