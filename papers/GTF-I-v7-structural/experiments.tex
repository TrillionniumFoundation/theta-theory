\section{Positive experiments and predictive quotients}\label{sec:v7-experiments}
\subsection{Actual histories}
All spaces of states, observations and actions are standard Borel.
For an unknown parameter $\lambda$, a controlled experiment has a
probability kernel
\begin{equation}
 K_t^\lambda(dx',dy,dc\mid x,a),                         \label{eq:v7-instrument}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is the cost of the actual step. Failure, rejection and stopping
are outcomes, not missing probability mass. A policy chooses $a_t$ from
the observed past and independent randomization. Preparation has a
specified law and cost. With an absorbing symbol after stopping, repeated
kernel integration constructs one probability law on the infinite
history space. Its finite marginals are consistent because every kernel
in \eqref{eq:v7-instrument} has mass one. This is the usual kernel
extension theorem \cite{Kallenberg2021}; conditioning on success would
define a different experiment unless its preparation and waiting costs
were separately charged.

For a fixed Bayesian preparation, let $\pi$ be a reachable belief state
on the hidden state, including the parameter when it is random. The
positive instrument associated with an action and observation event is
\[
 \mathsf T_{a,B}\pi(E)=\int K(E,B,dc\mid x,a)\,\pi(dx).
\]
After an observed event of positive probability, normalization gives the
updated belief. For nonatomic reports we use a specified disintegration,
with all equalities interpreted outside the appropriate null sets. A
formula for an event of probability zero is not an additional observation
channel.

\subsection{The future-test quotient}
A future test consists of a finite observation-based policy, a stopping
rule, and a bounded measurable terminal payoff. Its conditional
expectation at a belief $\pi$ is written $\pi(v)$, where $v$ is the
corresponding backward test function. Fix a family $\mathcal V$ containing
constants and closed under the one-step pullbacks of the allowed tests.
It may, for example, contain all bounded finite-horizon executable tests.
Define
\begin{equation}
 \pi\sim_{\mathcal V}\pi'
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \pi(v)=\pi'(v)\quad(v\in\mathcal V).                   \label{eq:v7-quotient}
\end{equation}
The set of classes is the predictive quotient. The choice of tests is
part of the experiment specification: identifying a hidden state is not
required if none of the designated tests distinguishes it.

\begin{proposition}[Minimality and descent]\label{prop:v7-quotient}
The quotient \eqref{eq:v7-quotient} is the coarsest set-valued statistic
that determines all designated predictions. If the test family is
one-step closed, normalized Bayesian updates descend to the quotient
where their normalization is positive. If, in addition, the reachable
belief domain $B$ is compact metrizable, a countable family of continuous
tests determines \eqref{eq:v7-quotient}, and each fixed-report update is
continuous on its positive-probability domain, the quotient has a
canonical metrizable realization as a subset of $\R^{\N}$, and each such
update descends continuously on compact subdomains on which its
denominator is bounded away from zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Map $\pi$ to the vector of all its test expectations. Its fibres are
exactly \eqref{eq:v7-quotient}. Any statistic determining the expectations
has fibres contained in these fibres, so the vector factors through that
statistic as a set map. For the update, the numerator of a future
prediction is a one-step pulled-back test and the denominator is the
pulled-back constant. Equivalent states have equal numerators and
denominators, hence equal normalized predictions.

In the countable continuous case use
$q(\pi)=(\pi(v_j))_{j\ge1}$. After scaling the tests to be bounded by
one, its range is a compact subset of $[-1,1]^{\N}$, with its product
metric. A continuous map from a compact space into a Hausdorff space is
a quotient map. The fibre-respecting continuous update therefore
factors continuously on the stated compact subdomain. No assertion of a
Borel cross-section, or of a smooth quotient for arbitrary Borel tests,
is used. The minimality statement in the first paragraph alone would
not imply either assertion.
\end{proof}

The proposition is relative to the prepared Bayesian experiment. An
identity for one prior is not a parameter-uniform comparison of the
family $\{K^\lambda\}_\lambda$. All experiment distances below explicitly
take the supremum over the parameter when that stronger meaning is
intended.

\subsection{Attainable Bayes geometry}
Let $U$ be square integrable in a separable Hilbert space $H$, let
$\mathcal H$ be the actual information, and put
$m=\E[U\mid\mathcal H]$. Define
\[
 e_M^2(m)=\inf_{z_1,\ldots,z_M\in H}
                \E\min_i\|m-z_i\|^2.
\]
Independent encoder/decoder randomization is permitted, but a retained
observation is not a free input after encoding.

\begin{proposition}[Checkpoint geometry and information loss]
\label{prop:v7-bayes}
The optimum squared loss of a single $M$-label checkpoint statistic is
\begin{equation}
 \E\|U-m\|^2+e_M^2(m).                                  \label{eq:v7-checkpoint}
\end{equation}
For any sub-$\sigma$-field $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal H$, posterior laws of $\lambda$
satisfy the barycentre identity
$\pi_{\mathcal C}=\E[\pi_{\mathcal H}\mid\mathcal C]$.
Consequently convex posterior functionals decrease under coarsening.
Whenever the quantities are finite,
\begin{equation}
 I(\lambda;\mathcal H)-I(\lambda;\mathcal C)
 =\E D_{\rm KL}(\pi_{\mathcal H}\Vert\pi_{\mathcal C}).   \label{eq:v7-information}
\end{equation}
In a differentiable dominated family, the score of a statistic $S$ is
$\E[s_\lambda\mid S]$, and the lost Fisher information matrix is
$\E\Cov(s_\lambda\mid S)$, provided differentiation and conditioning
are justified in $L^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional orthogonality gives
$\E\|U-z\|^2=\E\|U-m\|^2+\E\|m-z\|^2$ for every
$\mathcal H$-measurable output, and also after adding independent
randomness. Freeze that randomness. There are at most $M$ decoder
outputs, so nearest-centre assignment is no worse than the given
encoding. Conversely every finite codebook has a measurable nearest
assignment, with deterministic tie-breaking. Taking infima proves
\eqref{eq:v7-checkpoint}, without assuming attainment of an optimal
codebook. Conditional expectation against bounded parameter tests gives
the barycentre identity. Conditional Jensen proves the convex assertion.
The chain rule for the logarithm of posterior densities gives
\eqref{eq:v7-information}; finite partitions followed by monotone
approximation give the same assertion under the stated finiteness
condition. Differentiating the marginal density of $S$ gives the score
identity. The matrix law of total variance gives the Fisher identity.
These are the classical decision and information identities in
\cite{Blackwell1953,LeCamYang2000}; their role here is to specify the
geometry being implemented, not to certify recursive closure.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Normalized response and observation modules}
Let $b_\theta$ be a differentiable finite positive measure with
$b_\theta(1)>0$, and let $\pi_\theta=b_\theta/b_\theta(1)$.
Differentiating this actual normalization gives
\begin{equation}
 \partial_\theta\pi_\theta(f)=
 \frac{\partial_\theta b_\theta(f)
       -\pi_\theta(f)\partial_\theta b_\theta(1)}{b_\theta(1)}.
                                                               \label{eq:v7-response}
\end{equation}
If the derivative has score density $s$ relative to $b_\theta$, the
right side is $\Cov_{\pi_\theta}(f,s)$. Thus a response direction is
invisible exactly when its centered score annihilates the linear span
of the designated tests. If the observation protocol consists of
products from two declared linear test spaces $V,W$, its null space is
$(VW)^\perp$, with $VW=\operatorname{span}\{vw:v\in V,w\in W\}$.
This last statement follows immediately from linearity, but it requires
that those products really are the observed probes. It does not identify
an algebraic multiplicity with a physical noise variance. Formula
\eqref{eq:v7-response} and this annihilator are the normalization and
observation-module inputs used to type a contact experiment.
