\section{A sharp budget for the physical count-to-contact experiment}
\label{sec:v7-a2}
We now specify the actual local protocol, following the known-mark
experiment of A2 \cite{QiA2v118}. Fix $\lambda_0\in(0,\infty)^m$, a
matrix $B\in\R^{m\times d}$, a compact set $H\subset\R^d$, and a
full-row-rank matrix $C\in\R^{r\times m}$. Observe independent counts
\begin{equation}
 K_{N,j}\sim\operatorname{Pois}
       (N\lambda_{0,j}+\sqrt N(Bh)_j),\qquad h\in H.    \label{eq:v7-counts}
\end{equation}
Write $\Lambda=\diag\lambda_0$, $A=CB$, and $\Gamma=C\Lambda C^T$.
The contact score and limiting experiment are
\[
 S_N=C(K_N-N\lambda_0)/\sqrt N,\qquad
 \mathcal G=\{N(Ah,\Gamma):h\in H\}.
\]
The physical finite statistic is
\begin{equation}
          T_{N,J}=Q_J\bigl(\tau^{\otimes r}(S_N)\bigr),
               \qquad |T_{N,J}|\le J^r.               \label{eq:v7-physical-statistic}
\end{equation}
There is no physical jitter, no retained real score, and no omitted
overflow event. Every real score belongs to one of the $J^r$ cells of
the open compactified cube, with deterministic boundary tie-breaking.

\begin{theorem}[Joint sample and register law]
\label{thm:v7-a2}
For the fixed protocol \eqref{eq:v7-counts}, there are $N_0,C_*<\infty$
such that uniformly for $N\ge N_0$ and $J\ge2$,
\begin{equation}
 \Del(\mathcal E_{N,J},\mathcal G)
 \le C_*\left(N^{-1/2}+\frac{J}{\sqrt N}+J^{-2}\right), 
                                                               \label{eq:v7-a2-bound}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal E_{N,J}=\{\Law_h(T_{N,J}):h\in H\}$.
If $AH$ contains an $r$-dimensional ball, every experiment with at most
$M$ outputs has distance from $\mathcal G$ at least $c_*M^{-2/r}$.
Consequently the statistic with $J=\lfloor M^{1/r}\rfloor$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 c_*M^{-2/r}\le\Del(\mathcal E_{N,J},\mathcal G)
               \le C_{*,K}M^{-2/r},
 \quad 2^r\le M\le K N^{r/6},                          \label{eq:v7-a2-window}
\end{equation}
for every fixed $K<\infty$. In particular $M\asymp N^{r/6}$ gives
distance of order $N^{-1/3}$; achieving distance of order $N^{-1/5}$
requires and is achieved by a budget of order $N^{r/10}$.
All constants may depend on the fixed matrices, compact parameter set
and covariance conditioning. No uniformity at rank degeneration or
unknown marks is asserted.
\end{theorem}

We prove the uniform estimates in five steps, keeping comparison
randomization separate from \eqref{eq:v7-physical-statistic}.

\begin{lemma}[Jittered Poisson local normal comparison]
\label{lem:v7-poisson-normal}
There is an absolute $C_P<\infty$ such that for $\mu\ge1$, independent
$U\sim\operatorname{Unif}(-1/2,1/2)$, and $K\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)$,
\begin{equation}
          \|\Law(K+U)-N(\mu,\mu)\|_{\rm TV}
                                      \le C_P\mu^{-1/2}. 
                                                               \label{eq:v7-poisson-normal}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to consider $\mu\ge64$, increasing the constant for the
remaining range. The jittered density is $p_\mu(k)=e^{-\mu}\mu^k/k!$
on $[k-1/2,k+1/2)$, $k\ge0$. On the central cells
$|k-\mu|\le\mu^{2/3}/4$, put $t=k-\mu$, $x=k+u$, $|u|\le1/2$,
and $z=(x-\mu)/\sqrt\mu$. Stirling's formula with remainder
$0<R_k<1/(12k)$ gives
\[
 \log\frac{p_\mu(k)}{\phi_\mu(x)}
 =-\tfrac12\log(k/\mu)-R_k
  -\{k\log(k/\mu)-k+\mu\}+\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\mu},
\]
where $\phi_\mu$ is the $N(\mu,\mu)$ density. For $|t|\le\mu/2$,
Taylor's theorem applied to $(1+v)\log(1+v)-(1+v)+1$ gives remainder
at most $|t|^3/\mu^2$ after its quadratic term. Also
$|\log(1+t/\mu)|\le2|t|/\mu$ and
$|(x-\mu)^2-t^2|\le |t|+1/4$. Substitution therefore gives the
convenient bound
\begin{equation}
 \left|\log\frac{p_\mu(k)}{\phi_\mu(x)}\right|
 \le 32\mu^{-1/2}(1+|z|^3),\qquad
 \left|\log\frac{p_\mu(k)}{\phi_\mu(x)}\right|\le64.   \label{eq:v7-stirling-bound}
\end{equation}
The constants are deliberately loose. For example
$|z|\le\mu^{1/6}/4+1/(2\sqrt\mu)$ on these cells verifies the
second bound directly. The inequality $|e^v-1|\le e^{|v|}|v|$ now
bounds the integrated central density error by
$32e^{64}\mu^{-1/2}(1+\E|G|^3)$, $G\sim N(0,1)$.

For completeness the omitted tails are exponentially smaller. For
$|s|\le1$, the centered Poisson log moment generating function is
$\mu(e^s-1-s)\le\mu s^2$. Exponential Markov with
$s=\mu^{-1/3}/16$, and its negative, bounds the probability outside
the central cells, allowing the half-unit jitter margin, by
$2\exp(-\mu^{1/3}/128)$ after adjusting an absolute factor for
$64\le\mu$ in the bounded initial interval. The Gaussian bound follows
by its moment generating function. Thus an absolute choice such as
$C_P=10^8e^{64}$ dominates the central error, the tails and the finite
initial range. This proves a uniform $\mu^{-1/2}$ bound rather than
an unquantified local-limit assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Variance replacement and projected comparison]
\label{lem:v7-variance}
Let $U_j$ be independent uniform half-cell jitters, used only in this
proof, and set $S_N^o=S_N+CU/\sqrt N$. Uniformly for $h\in H$,
\begin{equation}
       \|\Law_h(S_N^o)-N(Ah,\Gamma)\|_{\rm TV}
                                  \le C_0N^{-1/2}.    \label{eq:v7-score-normal}
\end{equation}
One may choose $C_0$ as a function only of $m$, the minimum of
$\lambda_0$, $\max_{h\in H}\|Bh\|$, and fixed matrix norms.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $b_* =\max_{h\in H}\|Bh\|_\infty$ and
$\lambda_* =\min_j\lambda_{0,j}$. Choose
$N_0\ge\max(128/\lambda_*,4b_*^2/\lambda_*^2)$.
Then each count mean is at least $N\lambda_*/2$.
Product comparison using Lemma~\ref{lem:v7-poisson-normal}, followed
by centering and scaling, gives distance at most
$mC_P\sqrt{2/\lambda_*}\,N^{-1/2}$ from the normal vector with mean
$Bh$ and covariance $\Lambda+N^{-1/2}\diag(Bh)$.

For the replacement of this covariance by $\Lambda$, the relative
variance eigenvalues are $1+t_j$, with
$|t_j|\le b_*/(\lambda_*\sqrt N)\le1/2$.
The exact Gaussian relative entropy is
$\tfrac12\sum_j\{t_j-\log(1+t_j)\}\le\sum_jt_j^2$.
The elementary entropy--variation inequality
$\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}\le\sqrt{D_{\rm KL}(P\Vert Q)/2}$ gives a further
bound $\sqrt{m/2}\,b_*/(\lambda_*\sqrt N)$.
Taking
$C_0=mC_P\sqrt{2/\lambda_*}+\sqrt{m/2}\,b_*/\lambda_*$
is sufficient. Projection by $C$ contracts total variation and sends
the fixed Gaussian covariance to $\Gamma$, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Deterministic cell crossing]\label{lem:v7-boundary}
Let $H_0$ be the transformed marginal bound in
\eqref{eq:v7-Hconstants}, now for the means $AH$, and put
$d_N=\|C\|_{\infty,1}/(2\pi\sqrt N)$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{h\in H}\|\Law_h(T_{N,J})-
                   Q_J\tau^{\otimes r}N(Ah,\Gamma)\|_{\rm TV}
           \le 2C_0N^{-1/2}+2rH_0Jd_N.                \label{eq:v7-boundary}
\end{equation}
Here $\|C\|_{\infty,1}$ is the maximum absolute row sum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The coupled scores satisfy
$\|S_N-S_N^o\|_\infty\le\|C\|_{\infty,1}/(2\sqrt N)$.
Since $|\tau'|\le1/\pi$, their compactified coordinates differ by
at most $d_N$. Distinct grid labels require a coordinate of the jittered
score to be within $d_N$ of a grid boundary. On the torus there are $J$
boundaries per coordinate; under the transformed Gaussian their total
probability is at most $2rH_0Jd_N$, or its truncation by one. Replacing
the jittered law by that Gaussian adds at most $C_0N^{-1/2}$, by
\eqref{eq:v7-score-normal}. The coupling inequality then compares the
unjittered and jittered labels. One more data-processing comparison of
the jittered labels to the Gaussian labels adds the second
$C_0N^{-1/2}$. Boundary tie events are already in the strips. This
argument never asserts that the unjittered real score has a Gaussian
law in total variation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v7-a2}]
Use the deterministic Gaussian-to-cell kernel for the direction
$\mathcal G\to\mathcal E_{N,J}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:v7-boundary} bounds
its error by $b_{N,J}=2C_0N^{-1/2}+2rH_0Jd_N$.
For the reverse direction, use the positive kernel $H_J$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:v7-hat}. Kernel contraction and the triangle inequality
give
\[
 \sup_h\|H_J\Law_h(T_{N,J})-N(Ah,\Gamma)\|_{\rm TV}
           \le b_{N,J}+rH_2/(12J^2).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:v7-a2-bound}, with, for example,
$C_*=2C_0+rH_0\|C\|_{\infty,1}/\pi+rH_2/12$ increased to one.
Every kernel is parameter independent. Jitter was introduced only to
bound a difference of laws, not to modify the physical statistic.

If $AH$ contains a ball, restrict to an affine parameter section over
a smaller mean cube and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:v7-cardinality}. More
generally one may simply index the Gaussian family by its mean and
choose a Borel representative preimage for each mean in the cube
(the compact fibres of the continuous map on $H$ admit a measurable
section); the lower
comparison uses only that subfamily. For $J=\lfloor M^{1/r}\rfloor$
and $M\ge2^r$, $J^{-2}\le4M^{-2/r}$. If $M\le K N^{r/6}$, then
$M^{3/r}/\sqrt N\le K^{3/r}$; hence $J/\sqrt N$ and $N^{-1/2}$
are also at most fixed multiples of $M^{-2/r}$. This proves the
window. Taking $M$ respectively of order $N^{r/6}$ and $N^{r/10}$
gives the two rates. Conversely, an error at most $C N^{-1/5}$ forces
$M\ge c N^{r/10}$ by the universal cardinality lower bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The A2 matrices and the scope of the consequence}
In A2's known-mark local protocol, the moment derivative $D$ has
$\Sigma=D\Lambda D^T>0$, and its calibrated exposure direction is
$B=\Lambda D^T\Sigma^{-1}$, so $DB=I$. If $L$ maps to the contact
coordinates and $F$ is an orthonormal frame of its range, the matrices
above are
\[
 C=F^TLD,\qquad A=CB=F^TL,\qquad \Gamma=C\Lambda C^T>0.
\]
Here $A$ has full row rank $r$. A compact local parameter neighborhood
therefore has an image containing a mean ball. Both sides of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v7-a2} apply to that actual protocol. This invokes a
structural experiment-cardinality theorem verbatim, not just an analogy
between a root singularity and a contact observation.

The result is a matched budget theorem in the displayed joint window.
It is not a lower bound saying that $N^{-1/3}$ is the best sample-size
comparison achievable with arbitrarily many labels. It also does not
assign a stochastic noise law to a conductor scheme or establish
uniformity at a rank-changing physical stratum. The geometric source,
the exposure protocol, the finite statistic and the comparison kernels
are distinct typed objects throughout.
