\begin{abstract}
We recover a convex obstacle configuration and a single unknown
stationary launch density from the directional first-order germ of
short forward collision probabilities. The observations are one bit
per attempted preparation, with unobserved launch position. Angular
differentiation of the germ gives positive sums of translated copies
of the density at antipodal boundary points. Spatial separation
recovers the footprint, the normalized density and the boundary
locations. The entire observational ambiguity is a common translation
of the obstacles and launch law. Exact recovery requires the footprint
diameter to be smaller than the inter-obstacle separation and at least
one obstacle diameter; no periodicity, homothety relation or reciprocal
preparation is supplied. The germ determines every finite-length
collision response and the full translation period group.
For quantitative smooth classes with uniformly separated density
copies, we give a finite forward-only reconstruction of the footprint
and table in $C^2$. Its weak regularization, rational commands and
Bernstein sampling bounds require no density upper bound or continuity
modulus. Uniform finite period decisions retain a positive patch
margin; finite nonperiodic configurations use a complete protected
aperture. We also retain the sharp stationary minimax power
$(3s/2+1)/(s-2)$, $s=6+\beta$, for a fixed known uniform-disk law,
with one logarithmic factor between the bounds. All attempted bits
are charged, and nominal sites, command descriptions, numerical
arithmetic and physical positioning are distinguished.
\end{abstract}
