## 2. Physical flux and the Abel-coordinate programme

The v11 source `papers/A2-v11-abel-stable-boundary-profiles/sections/02_flux.tex`, blob `494231575f47a59546102d701b4bb3a11ab19a58`, derives a suspension/flux identity for the stationary billiard measure and complete collision records. It integrates over the first future impact state and residual flight time, with the invariant collision cross-section law. Its event counts hits near a minimal-flight threshold. Setting the record test equal to one gives a positive-part integral involving sums of successive roof times. The calculation retains both initial and terminal free-flight portions.

That argument supplies a methodological precedent: start from the physical event and its measure before applying an inverse transform. It does not give the present nominal-position-dependent short-flight germ for an arbitrary unknown launch density. The historical preparation law, threshold parameter, reflected multi-hit dynamics, and recorded datum differ.

The v11 files `article/21_abel_stability.tex`, blob `f7d11b05702d7a47599f54c2f75efde5844b7329`, and `article/26_abel_acquisition.tex`, blob `ba2f96ff0234b1770a9621c1fe9e58d590c71e26`, concern integrated threshold laws of normalized symmetrized energy profiles. Their weighted Abel/Volterra identity acts on an energy variable and uses derivatives of the integrated law. The stable inverse object is the energy profile in the specified flux topology. The acquisition analysis charges binary preparations and controls derivative errors.

The present revision retains that distinction between an exact derivative and its statistical acquisition. Its new operator acts on the command angle and its first variation acts on the command length. The current finite theorem transfers derivatives to known test functions and proves its own bias and sampling estimates. Neither the historical Abel transform nor the historical profile acquisition bound is imported as an inverse for the spatial launch density.

## 3. Intrinsic registration and lattice descent

The v17 sources are:

## 7. The present exact and finite additions

The current sources `core/19_single_law_rigidity.tex`, `core/19a_one_resolved_component.tex`, and `core/20_single_law_finite.tex` were read together with `main.tex`, `core/00h_single_law_overview.tex`, and `core/05_comparison.tex` for this audit.

The first source proves local \(L^1\) convergence of the short-flight means and a quantitative weak bias bound. Angular differentiation gives positive sums of translated launch-density copies at antipodal contact points. When every copy is separated, its support and mass recover the centered footprint and density; its Steiner point traces the obstacle boundary. The resulting fiber is exactly a common translation of the configuration and launch law, and the germ completes every finite-length forward response.

The second source broadens exact identification. It suffices that the footprint diameter be below the inter-obstacle separation and below the diameter of at least one obstacle. That obstacle is not supplied. A minimum-area angular support component recovers one pure density copy. The odd part of the germ then determines the gradient of occupation, whose zero-infimum normalization fixes the additive constant. Support cancellation recovers all remaining obstacles, even where their antipodal density copies overlap. This extension is exact and does not assert a uniform finite procedure for selecting an arbitrarily weakly separated minimum.

The finite source therefore keeps a uniform gap between every angular copy, quantitative smooth geometry, and a boundary-mass lower bound. Positive spatial/angular regularization, weak first variation, finite rational quadrature and Bernstein sampling require neither a density upper bound nor a continuity modulus. The theorem reconstructs the centered footprint and table in \(C^2\); it does not assert a strong-norm finite estimate of an unrestricted \(L^1\) density. Its stated sufficient exponent is \(Q_\gamma=(3\gamma+27/2)s/(s-2)\), and necessity is not claimed. Periodic and complete finite-cloud conclusions retain their separate acquisition assumptions.

These distinctions address the latest report within the existing scalar-collision topic. The present work uses the history as a checked derivation record while proving the new physical first variation, density-copy identification, exact extension, and finite sampling chain in the manuscript itself.
