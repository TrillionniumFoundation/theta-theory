## Huang–Meyer–Nuradha–Wilde and Yuan–Fung

Z. Huang, J. J. Meyer, T. Nuradha and M. M. Wilde, *Query complexities of quantum channel discrimination and estimation: A unified approach*, arXiv:2511.10832v2, is directly relevant. Its Theorem 17 treats n parallel channel uses, with isometric extensions and a contraction W on their environment spaces. Writing `M_W=V_0^*(W tensor I)V_1`, `a=||I-Re M_W||op` and `b=||I-M_W||op`, its squared channel-Bures upper is `n inf_W {2a+(n-1)b²}`. The source identifies the parallel inequality as an antecedent of Yuan–Fung (Eq. 23 in the cited source). Its Theorem 19 bounds common n-call adaptive experiments by `n inf_W {2a+(n-1)sqrt(2a)b}`.

The manuscript's selected canonical surrogate is a concrete, generally nonoptimal, aligned extension. From its displacement alpha*s one obtains `2a<=alpha²s²` and `b<=alpha*s`, so either generic bound supplies a sufficient alpha*n*s Bures estimate. Actual tube members additionally cost n*r*s² in diamond norm, hence sqrt(n*r)*s in Bures distance. That remainder is independent of environment-gauge optimization and cannot be omitted.

The new claim is not a new channel-Bures or tensor-fidelity principle. It is a local effect-tube theorem uniform in the unknown remainder, a matching support-coordinate lower construction, and its extension to conditional fresh adaptive blocks with receiver quantum memory, classical feedback and a pathwise quadratic budget. The generic upper by itself does not supply these matching and resource-sensitive statements. No absence-of-equivalent-theorem conclusion is inferred from this comparison.

## Ghosal–Halder–Patra–Sen

P. Ghosal, P. Halder, A. Patra and A. Sen, *Quantum hypothesis testing of non-mixed-unitarity: A multifaceted hierarchy of quantum channel discrimination*, arXiv:2609.40355v1, formalizes independent repeated blocks with arbitrary within-block correlations, with and without reference memory. This is a close resource antecedent and is acknowledged in the primary.

Their Theorem 1 concerns a closed convex composite null class and a fixed alternative channel, and identifies the fixed-block Stein exponent through a normalized optimized relative entropy. The task fixes the pair/class, allows asymptotically many identical blocks and fixes a type-I-error criterion. The block optimization is over a common probe with an infimum over the composite null, not a finite shrinking effect-tube trace-distance optimization. Their block-i.i.d. model is therefore not claimed new here. Our widths can vary, the budget is bounded on every record, final loss is finite unhalved trace separation, and the allowed second-order remainder is uniformly unknown. The reset theorem additionally allows classical feedback and fresh adaptive acquisition blocks subject to conditional independence from old receiver memory.

## Classical-feedback and metrology antecedents

Salek–Hayashi–Winter, *Usefulness of adaptive strategies in asymptotic quantum channel discrimination*, Physical Review A 105, 022419 (2022), arXiv:2011.06569, Theorem 9 and Remark 10, addresses fixed-pair asymptotic discrimination exponents and classical feedback under its stated input/reference convention. This is retained as an antecedent. The present result neither introduces that resource question nor identifies all versions of its resource model with the reset model. The finite pathwise-fidelity proof permits arbitrary receiver quantum memory while explicitly forbidding its coherent import into a new complete probe-reference state.

The Kraus-span criterion and error-correction metrological mechanism remain credited to Zhou–Jiang and Knill–Laflamme. GHZ amplification, entanglement-depth methods, PSD tangent geometry, Bures metrics and fidelity identities are established tools. The inherited tomography and covariant-learning comparisons remain active; their statistical estimators and gate constructions are not recast as new discrimination primitives.
