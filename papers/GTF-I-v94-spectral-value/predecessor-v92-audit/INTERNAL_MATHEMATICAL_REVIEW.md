# Internal mathematical review — Revision 92

This author-side review checks the new rank-resolved argument against the latest R60 objections and the intervening published v91 proof chain.

The potential gap with highest consequence is dimension-changing purification. A general dilation of an old receiver instrument or fresh mixed state can exceed k or ell. The new lemma instead records Kraus/spectral indices classically in the existing admissible record, with rectangular quantum outputs unchanged. Summing the refined branches recovers the original Born probabilities. The converse then realizes each finite Gram decomposition, so the change of representation does not narrow the optimized class.

The compact primal/dual argument is checked on the common first marginal, not on independently chosen marginals per label. Rank sets themselves are nonconvex, but their graph convexifications have closed homogeneous hypographs and strictly feasible common full-rank points. Finite branch elimination does not perturb the atom matrices. Hence the rank restrictions are preserved through both finite attainment and duality.

The centered-swap upper is exact at a common flat rank-r support. It uses the smaller overlap-rank bound, not a false common-support premise. Its equality derivation has an exception when d=2,r=1: the coefficient of fidelity is zero, and orthogonal pure factors can also attain the upper. The text and regression include that exception. No uniqueness of receiver dilations is asserted.

The stated construction consumes a d-dimensional first reference before its recorded compression; it is not an r-dimensional memory implementation at all times. After compression, both retained quantum registers have dimension r and every extra outcome is classical. The exact bottleneck min(k,ell) is proved for the shared-basis family, not all finite experiments. General experiments have the attained rank-envelope characterization and may have zero or flat increments.

The universal proofs are written in the manuscript. Exact tests exercise finite examples independently, including complex transposes and output normalization. Independent human priority review and the separate Theta analytic programme are not certified by this internal review.
