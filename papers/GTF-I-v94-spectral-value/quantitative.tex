\begin{abstract}
We study ordered quantum measurements with separate acquisition and
receiver resources.  A common-barycenter variational principle and
attained Hermitian-majorant dual describe every finite two-call reset
experiment, with rank restrictions resolving the retained receiver
registers.  On a finite shared-basis experiment, we evaluate the entire
rank-constrained optimum at every prescribed pure initial acquisition.
Its value is a concave spectral function: a least rank-constrained
majorant followed by one secular equation.  A recorded instrument with
at most $d$ outcomes attains it without changing the initial acquisition.
We determine exactly when communicating a receiver outcome to the next
preparation is strictly useful, and give its exact gain and the
initial-rank saturation threshold.  The proof combines a rank-sensitive
filtered-swap optimization with a spectral evaluation of concave
envelopes.  The equal-prior dimension hierarchy, complete initial
equality set and quantitative projection rigidity remain complementary
results on the same family.  The local theory classifies fixed quadratic
tangent tubes by the complete support-covariance trichotomy: a profile
with $N=\sum n_j$ and $V=\sum n_j^2$ has trace orders
$\min\{1,\sqrt Ns\}$, $\min\{1,\sqrt Vs\}$ or $\min\{1,Ns\}$;
a hard square budget $Q$ gives coherent order $\min\{1,\sqrt Qs\}$.
All finite remainder terms and the unknown-remainder independence of the
lower readouts are retained.  The general variational characterization,
solved finite family and fixed-tube local orders have distinct quantifiers.
\end{abstract}
