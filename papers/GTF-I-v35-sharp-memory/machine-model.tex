\section{The experiment and its realizing machines}\label{sec:model}
State distributions are row vectors and
$\Delta_k=\{e\in\R^k:e\ge0,\ e\one=1\}$.

\begin{definition}[Clocked stochastic realization]\label{def:machine}
For a specified $N$, a realizing machine has finite state sets $[K_t]$
for $0\le t\le N$, initialization rows $E_x\in\Delta_{K_0}$,
row-stochastic matrices $T_{t,c}\in\R_+^{K_t\times K_{t+1}}$ for
$t<N$, $c\in\{0,1\}$, and decoder means $d_j\in[-1,1]^{K_N}$.
Its conditional answer mean is
\[
 E_xT_{0,c_1}\cdots T_{N-1,c_N}d_j.
\]
Its error is one half the maximum absolute difference from
\eqref{eq:target}, over all seeds, command words and queries.
The profile counts \emph{available} labels, including padding. Its peak
is $\max_{0\le t\le N}K_t$, or the maximum of this number and two if
a binary answer is retained at an additional output cut.
$W_{N,\varepsilon}(\alpha)$ is the least such peak at error at most
$\varepsilon$.
\end{definition}

Only the updated register persists. An earlier random seed, a transcript,
an input-dependent clock or any retained protocol variable must be in
that register. The current input is consumed in its update; seed and
query are supplied just once. The epoch is external and the complete
row table may depend on $N$. All rows, including unused rows, are
normalized. Exact atomic stochastic rows and read-only tables are allowed;
row construction, arithmetic and table length are not part of this width.
A self-paced finite fair-bit compiler for rational rows has additional
workspace and is a different resource statement \cite{GTF31}.

The commands are externally specified inputs. No independence or fairness
is part of the simulation task. A lower bound may impose any distribution
on command words, including one chosen after the available profile is
known. That distribution is used in the proof and is not a persistent
seed supplied to the machine. When counting states under a chosen test
law, zero-probability labels may be removed; the conclusions in terms of
available labels remain valid.

\begin{proposition}[Separate positive minimum]\label{prop:separate}
At every cut after initialization the exact normalized residual rank
and the separate positive minimum of \eqref{eq:target} are three. The
latter permits arbitrary finite registers at the other cuts.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The predictive vectors are $R_\alpha^a x/10$. Idle prefixes from the
four seeds span a plane, and two coordinate queries after an idle suffix
separate it. Normalization adds the constant row, giving rank three.
The triangle with vertices $(1/2,0)$ and $(-1/2,\pm1/2)$ contains the
seed disk of radius $\sqrt2/10$, since its side distances from the
origin are $1/2$ and $1/(2\sqrt5)$. Its vertices have norm at most
$1/\sqrt2$, so all rotated continuations give valid binary laws.
At the chosen cut decompose the actual predictive vector in this
triangle. Before it retain the finite raw history; afterwards retain
the triangle label and the remaining active-command count. This is a
complete finite machine attaining three at that cut. Rank gives the
lower bound. This separate-cut construction is inherited from
\cite{GTF32,GTF33}.
\end{proof}

The statement does not require a hidden basis state to possess a
predictive vector. Indeed the converse below never projects a hidden
simplex, or bounds the number of vertices in such a projection.
