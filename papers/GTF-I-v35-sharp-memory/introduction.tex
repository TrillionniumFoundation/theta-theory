\section{Introduction}
A small sufficient state at each separate experimental cut need not be
compatible with small states at all cuts of one stochastic machine.
The rotation experiment below makes this discrepancy quantitative while
keeping the seed, command, query and answer alphabets fixed. Its separate
positive minimum is three at every cut. Its simultaneous minimum grows
with the number of commands, even when a fixed uniform approximation
error is allowed.

Write $R_\alpha$ for planar rotation through $2\pi\alpha$. A seed
$x\in\{-1,1\}^2$ is followed by $N$ binary commands and one coordinate
query $j\in\{1,2\}$. The answer $B\in\{-1,1\}$ must have mean
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \E[B\mid x,c_1,\ldots,c_N,j]
       =\frac1{10}e_j^T R_\alpha^{c_1+\cdots+c_N}x.
\end{equation}
All answer probabilities belong to
$[(1-\sqrt2/10)/2,(1+\sqrt2/10)/2]$, independently of $N$.
Let $W_{N,\varepsilon}(\alpha)$ denote the least peak number of
persistent labels achieving total-variation error at most $\varepsilon$
on every seed, word and query; Definition~\ref{def:machine} is precise.
Set
\[
 b_\alpha=\inf_{l\ge1}l\|l\alpha\|_{\R/\mathbb Z}.
\]
We call $\alpha$ \emph{badly approximable} when $b_\alpha>0$;
equivalently its continued-fraction partial quotients are bounded.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp hidden width]\label{thm:main}
Let $\alpha$ be badly approximable and $0\le\varepsilon<1/20$ be fixed.
In the clocked atomic stochastic-row model,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
                W_{N,\varepsilon}(\alpha)=\Theta_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(N^{1/3}).
\end{equation}
More explicitly, put $\kappa=1/10-2\varepsilon$. Every realization of
peak at most $K$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-main}
 N<(2K-1)+\frac{(2K-1)^3}{b_\alpha^2}\log\frac1\kappa.
\end{equation}
The upper bound is exact, uses two command matrices independent of the
epoch, and has a decoder depending on $N$. At the golden angle
$\alpha=(\sqrt5-1)/2$ and zero error,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:golden}
 \max\{3,(N/168)^{1/3}\}\le W_{N,0}(\alpha),
 \qquad
 W_{N,0}(\alpha)<2\max\{5,(40N)^{1/3}\}.
\end{equation}
In fact $W_{N,0}\ge3$ and $W_{N,0}>(N/168)^{1/3}$ separately.
\end{theorem}

None of the constants is optimized. The conclusion identifies the order
of width, not the exact integer optimum or its leading constant.
In persistent bits it gives $(\log_2N)/3+O_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(1)$.
This is not a uniform space bound charging program description or exact
real arithmetic.

The predecessor Fourier argument gives $\Omega(N^{1/5})$ for the same
unrestricted hidden-state class \cite{GTF34}. Its conditional phase
amplitude is the starting point here. The improvement does not come from
changing a trigonometric-kernel constant. Instead we choose correlated
commands \emph{inside} fresh independent packets. A packet of $2k-1$
commands produces $2k$ separated phase increments. Whatever hidden
mechanism is used inside it, a $k$-label endpoint loses a proportion of
amplitude of order $k^{-2}$. Since a wordwise simulator must pass this
input distribution, multiplication over disjoint packets gives the
cube-root converse. This also controls the number of low-width cuts
without bounding the remaining widths.

The underlying model is a nonuniform probabilistic ordered read-once
branching program, with two terminal affine readouts. Section~\ref{sec:comparison}
compares the result with the Fourier-growth theorems of
Reingold--Steinke--Vadhan, Steinke--Vadhan--Wan and Lee--Pyne--Vadhan
\cite{RSV,SVW,LPV}. Our packet argument is a hidden-compression estimate,
not a theorem about pseudorandom generators or Boolean Fourier growth.
The circle-walk literature \cite{BB} describes the uncompressed walk;
conditional averaging in a small register is an additional operation.

\paragraph{Contribution and provenance.}
The packet contraction, sharp unrestricted order, nonuniform packet
budget and compact-group applications are the new results. The
conditional-amplitude variable is inherited from \cite{GTF34}; the
resonant-polygon upper construction is inherited from \cite{GTF33} and
is proved again here. Jensen's inequality, sphere quantization,
continued fractions and finite Blackwell garbling are classical tools.
The earlier Fourier theorem is preserved, with its angular estimate
sharpened in Appendix~\ref{sec:angular}. No accumulated archive is needed
to verify the new proofs in this article.
