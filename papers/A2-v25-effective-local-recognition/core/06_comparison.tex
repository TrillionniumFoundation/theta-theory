\section{Information comparison and retained conclusions}
\label{sec:comparison}
The observation in this paper has three layers. A local probe certifies
nearby contacts and clearance. In the value-access version, first and
second derivatives and intrinsic arclength offsets are computed with
certified errors from local height values and numerical regularity bounds. A finite
fingerprint turns these measurements into record identity and coherent
orientation. Finally two endpoint densities, acquired by unconditional
histograms, determine geometry and absolute free area. These layers
should not be identified with one another.

\subsection{What has changed in the acquisition theorem}
The preceding geometric producer was given complete-body listing,
shortest-pair and blocker computations, and installation of a detector
recognizing every translated branch. The present local construction
never obtains a complete body map or a list of unknown bodies in a disk.
It works only at an actually encountered short trajectory, solves a local
normal problem, checks a bounded clearance tube and compares a fixed
finite descriptor with previously observed descriptors. Translation
invariance of the ideal recognition rule is proved, not supplied as an
oracle operation. Uniform positive arrival probability is derived from
protected return volume and independent bounded-time launches.

The replacement is not the removal of all geometric access. Certified
local boundary and distance measurements remain part of the theorem,
and its finite-jet separation constants depend on a compact analytic
prior. Analytic continuation enters the proof that finite local descriptors
distinguish coexisting translation classes, as well as the later global
inverse. The classifier is not claimed to discover branches from a passive
unmarked billiard trajectory. The old geometric-oracle theorem retains
its original scope; local probing is an additional acquisition theorem,
not a reinterpretation of that theorem's assumptions.

The quantitative version adds a further distinction. Its prior list
contains numerical analytic radii and bounds, curvature and separation
bounds, and shape and symmetry margins. It does not contain a separately
certified $(K_*,\chi)$. An explicit real-to-complex interpolation bound,
a chain of three-circles estimates and a geometric separation argument
compute such a pair. Uniform local value sampling also gives an alternative
fingerprint with no high-order derivatives. The finite-difference and
quadrature implementation reduces the apparatus to height and distance
measurements with chart coverage; it does not derive those measurements
from passive trajectories. All three versions retain the same endpoint-law
inverse and absolute-normalization completion mechanism.

\subsection{Nearest inverse-geometry comparisons}
Stefanov, Uhlmann and Vasy~\cite{SUV} study metric determination from
boundary distances and lens data, with local convexity and appropriate
global convexity conditions. Gurfinkel, Noakes and Stoyanov~\cite{GNS}
use exterior travelling times to compare nontrapping flows and recover
strictly convex obstacles in a nonpositively curved surface. Noakes and
Stoyanov~\cite{NS} establish exterior flow-conjugacy results from travelling
times and describe constructive recovery for two planar convex obstacles.
Their unknowns and observation sets are not the present periodic,
locally marked, volume-normalized record experiment.

We do not claim that elimination of an affine density factor is a new
abstract lens-rigidity principle. Equation~\eqref{eq:ratio} states exactly
what that elimination supplies: an absolute local action and the free
area. The present additional reduction concerns recognition of that
record without a complete-geometry producer or a periodic detector,
and the completion of its growing network using the nonnegative defect.
It does not replace the observation sets or hypotheses of the cited
metric and exterior-scattering theorems. Conditional analytic continuation
is likewise a classical mechanism; its severe dependence on analytic
bounds is part of the analysis, not a claim of a universal stability
exponent; compare \cite{Trefethen}.

Time-uniform soundness here is obtained by fresh conditional validation
and summable error spending. The hazard and coupon estimates and the
summability argument are standard probability. They are not presented
as new general concentration theorems. What requires the geometric work
is that the data to which they are applied are genuine periodic local
records, with an absolute normalization and registration error that have
both been accounted for.

\subsection{Preservation and logical boundaries}
The complete reviewed v24 manuscript is supplied unchanged as Supplement P
in \texttt{retained/v24}. Within it, Supplement Q is the exact reviewed
v23 manuscript, supplied with its
complete native source tree. It includes the previous fixed-list and
sequential theorems, geometric producer and local-margin exhaustion,
together with the retained v22, v21 and v18 companions. The original
smooth relative-law volume and its auxiliary document remain Supplement S.
All previous moment inverses, registered symmetric-table results, count
fibers, physical families and testing results remain supplied; the focused
primary reading order does not delete or weaken them.

The old moment-compressed regularization used increasing jet order and
order-dependent conditioning. The full-law histogram inverse instead
uses functions on an endpoint rectangle. The present finite $K_*$ has a
third role: class recognition within one table, not reconstruction of an
arbitrary analytic boundary. No comparison of these three roles turns
their error powers into minimax rates for a single experiment.

Finite and formal count-data fibers, smooth Taylor agreement, and equality
of exact analytic count germs remain different statements. Neither the
new acquisition theorem nor the completion defect proves exact analytic
count-only rigidity or nonrigidity. The theorem also does not resolve
unmarked passive-trajectory discovery or conventional marked-length
rigidity under the present priors. These are precise boundaries of the
proved assertions, not a change of topic or an editorial recommendation.
