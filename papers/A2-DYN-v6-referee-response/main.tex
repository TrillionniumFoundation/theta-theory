\begin{abstract}
We study the joint displacement, collision count and flight time of the
actual first returns in a triangular periodic Lorentz gas, uniformly for
disk radii in $[0.45,0.47]$. A convex optical action gives true periodic
orbits with controlled excess lengths. An adjacent-increment comparison
produces a uniform positive joint phase discrepancy using logarithmic-
length periods. Thickening a single one-step defect gives a quantitative
physical $L^p$ coercivity estimate for locally H\"older phases, with an
explicit phase-reconstruction criterion for operator resolvents.

A smooth killing weight on the collision space, combined with the
established perturbative spectral theory, proves uniform exponential
moments for the genuine return blocks. Exact marked visit intensity then
gives an exponential tail for their maximum over a growing stationary
observation interval. The largest unfinished block is logarithmic even
after conditioning on events of polynomially small probability, yielding
an explicit square-root path-comparison error. We also prove a common-
chart domination lemma for moving return domains and continuity of all
fixed-record moments. The physical edge coefficient, exact raw-density
subtraction, local Fourier inversion criterion, and geometric and
positive-denominator interfaces are retained. The original raw mixed-
density local limit is characterized by explicit induced-operator,
covariance and all-branch residual hypotheses; it is not inferred from
periodic phases or clock estimates alone.
\end{abstract}
