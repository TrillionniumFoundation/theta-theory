\begin{abstract}
Two fixed opposite forward collision fields determine a separated
configuration of strictly convex planar obstacles and its unknown
stationary launch probability, up to a common translation. The exact
data are two whole-plane spatial mean fields at one positive command
length, smaller than the obstacle separation minus the footprint
diameter. The compact launch law may be singular or atomic, with
lower-dimensional convex support. A finite prefix recovers occupation;
three matched support functions cancel the footprint and leave the
difference between the obstacle support and a contact-chord support.
The negative atoms of the signed surface-area measure recover the
chord and the geometry. Fourier uniqueness then recovers the complete
probability, every finite-length response, and the full period group.
No periodicity or boundary smoothness is required for this exact result.

For finitely sampled bits, quantitative smooth geometric classes admit
polynomial-cost geometric recovery and an exponential sufficient cost
for recovery of the law in $W_1$, using the same two commands. The
default moment acquisition uses shared signed two-bit records with
cost $C\tau^{-2}\log(C(n+1)/\delta)$ through degree $n$ at tolerance
$\tau$, conditional on a protected complete component. Fine geometry
and moment conditioning remain part of the joint cost. Finite response
prediction holds in local spatial $L^1$; a known $BV$ density bound
also gives density $L^1$ recovery and uniform response prediction on
bounded windows. Finite period decisions require a positive patch
margin; finite configurations require a complete protected aperture.
\end{abstract}
