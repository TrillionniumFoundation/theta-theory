\section{Backward residence and the height of the exit surface}
\label{sec:v84-residence}
A first-exit face carries more data than its roof coordinate. Its
backward lifetime records the length of the maximal regular segment
in the same exact event. Retaining that length yields an
essential-height estimate for the complete long-residence part of
the actual surface source.

Fix an exact event and its one-sided nongrazing outgoing faces
$\Gamma_{\rm ng}^+$. For $y$ on such a face put
\[
 b(y)=F(y),\qquad l(y)=\tau^-(y),\qquad a(y)=b(y)-l(y).
\]
Thus the maximal backward regular segment has roofs $(a(y),b(y))$.
The endpoint $a$ may be a true incoming face or a regular interior
critical point removed from the flow domain. It is not an auxiliary
chart seam. At fixed collision count the roof is bounded, so $l$ is
finite on every nonempty segment. All assertions below disregard only
the transverse null sets already excluded from the first-exit kernel.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v84-residence-kernel}
 k_r(t;y)=\left(1-\frac{b(y)-t}{r}\right)_+
                 \one_{\{a(y)<t<b(y)\}}.
\end{equation}
The original surface normalization is in $d\zeta(y)$; it is not
inserted again into $k_r$.

\begin{lemma}[The complete residence kernel]
\label{lem:v84-residence-kernel}
At every fixed count, as a jointly measurable nonnegative kernel,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v84-surface-kernel}
 \overline{\mathcal F}_r(t)=
           \int_{\Gamma_{\rm ng}^+}k_r(t;y)\,d\zeta(y).
\end{equation}
The same identity holds after any Borel restriction of the outgoing
face and its incident regular segment. No subsequent re-entry is
counted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed face put $s=b(y)-t$. The first-exit indicator is
$\one_{\{0<s\le h,\ s<l(y)\}}$. Its average over $0<h<r$ is
$(1-s/r)_+\one_{\{0<s<l(y)\}}$, which is
\eqref{eq:v84-residence-kernel}. Tonelli proves the assertion,
including the extended nonnegative case. The lifetime is taken in
the original exact event, so each face remains attached to its
incident physical component. The common flow-box kernels in
Appendix~\ref{app:v83-kernels} make these restrictions measurable.
\end{proof}

Split \eqref{eq:v84-surface-kernel} as $\mathcal L_r+\mathcal S_r$
by $l(y)>2r$ and $l(y)\le2r$, respectively. Both summands are
nonnegative. The letter $\mathcal L_r$ here denotes a surface
assignment, not the arithmetic reference $\mathcal L_{m,R}$.

\begin{theorem}[Long-residence essential height]
\label{thm:v84-long-height}
For almost every $t$ and every fixed sufficiently small $r>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v84-long-persistence}
 \mathcal L_r(t)\le\frac2r\int_{t-r}^{t}
                                    \overline{\mathcal F}_{2r}(v)\,dv.
\end{equation}
Consequently the original exact-label surface has the complete bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v84-long-height}
 \limsup_m\sup_{R,n,k}m^2\|\mathcal L_r\|_\infty
                              \le C_\eta r^{1/32}.
\end{equation}
This is an essential-height estimate, uniform over all actual words
with backward residence greater than $2r$. It is not an estimate of
only their total mass.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $k_r(t;y)>0$, put $s=b(y)-t\in(0,r)$ and assume $l(y)>2r$.
For $0<u<r$, one has $s+u<2r<l(y)$ and
\[
 k_{2r}(t-u;y)=1-\frac{s+u}{2r}
                       \ge\frac12\left(1-\frac sr\right)
                       =\frac12k_r(t;y).
\]
The comparison follows the same face and the same exact event.
Integrate first in $u$, then in $d\zeta(y)$. Removing the
long-residence restriction only from the upper comparison gives
\eqref{eq:v84-long-persistence}.

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-surface-local-mass} with surface width
$2r$ and interval length $r$. Division by $r$ bounds the ordered
right side by
$C r^{1/32}+C_\eta r^{(1+\eta)/2}\le C_\eta r^{1/32}$.
Every interval length is fixed before the collision limit. The
supremum over its location is already present in that theorem,
so \eqref{eq:v84-long-persistence} gives an essential supremum,
not merely an almost-everywhere pointwise limit.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathfrak S_M(r)$ denote the same ordered central essential
height as \eqref{eq:v83-surface-height}, with $\mathcal S_r$ in
place of $\overline{\mathcal F}_r$. Positivity and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v84-long-height} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v84-short-equivalence}
 \mathfrak S_M(r)\le\mathfrak F_M^{\rm all}(r)
       \le\mathfrak S_M(r)+C_\eta r^{1/32}.
\end{equation}
Thus the full surface height tends to zero if and only if the height
of its short maximal regular segments tends to zero. This exact
localization uses a proved height bound for the complementary source.
It does not assume that short segments have small height.
