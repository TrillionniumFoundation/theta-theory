\section{The signed angular current and the pointwise endpoint}
\label{sec:v77-current}
The angular source admits a finite signed roof derivative at each
fixed collision count. The differentiation is performed after the
complete exact-label sum, not separately with absolute values on
individual words.

\begin{theorem}[Finite variation before projection to the scalar roof]
\label{thm:v77-angular-bv}
For each fixed $m\ge2$ there is $C_m<\infty$, independent of
$R,\varepsilon,n,k$, such that for almost every $\theta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-angular-bv}
 A^\varepsilon(\,\cdot\,,\theta)\in BV(\R),\qquad
 \|D_t A^\varepsilon(\,\cdot\,,\theta)\|_{\TV}\le C_m.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\mathscr J^\varepsilon=D_tA^\varepsilon$ is a finite
signed measure on $\R\times\mathbb S^1$. It and its Jordan parts
disintegrate over $d\theta$ as the corresponding one-dimensional
roof derivatives and Jordan parts. Its projection to the roof is
$Db^\varepsilon$. No estimate for $m^2C_m$ is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We give the finite-count details because the original smooth guard
need not be definable. On the finite physical graph, enumerate the
$N=2m+1$ normalized incidence and clearance margins as $a_i$.
For $y\in[1,2]^N$ let
$W_y=1-\prod_{i=1}^N\one_{\{a_i>y_i\}}$ and let $A_y$ be its
angular density. By the nonzero Jacobian and the explicit finite
physical graph, the inverse fibers in \eqref{eq:v77-angular-sum}
are definable discrete sets. Uniform finiteness gives a bound $N_m$
for their size, uniform in $(R,n,k,t,e_\theta)$ at this fixed $m$.
The maps $B_m$, $K$, and $\chi$ on a regular sheet are definable:
their derivatives can be taken on the rational angular charts of
the physical graph, followed by the rational change from chart
derivatives to angular derivatives. Finite definable sums and
threshold tests show that $A_y$ has a definable representative.

The empty-set optical bound and $\chi\ge\lambda_\circ$ give
\[
 0\le A_y(t,\theta)\le
 H_m:=\frac{N_m R_+}{4\pi c_*\tau_-\lambda_\circ}
                    \frac{\sinh\eta}{\sinh(m\eta)}.
\]
Take the value zero on exceptional graph cells. On a fixed finite
atlas of the angle circle, the uniform monotonicity theorem for
definable families \cite{Coste} supplies $J_m<\infty$ monotonicity
intervals and point cells in the roof variable, with all remaining
variables treated as parameters. Extension by zero outside
$[m\tau_-,m\tau_+]$ therefore gives
$\|D_tA_y(\,\cdot\,,\theta)\|_{\TV}\le4(J_m+1)H_m$.
Both $N_m$ and $J_m$ are constants of a fixed finite formula, not
controlled word-pressure constants.

Put $\sigma=d\Theta$ and $V_\Theta=\|\sigma\|_{\TV}$.
The exact identity
\[
 W^\varepsilon=\int_{[1,2]^N}W_y\,d\sigma^{\otimes N}(y)
\]
uses $\sigma(\R)=1$ and is valid even when $\Theta$ is not
monotone. Coarea and finite signed Fubini give
$A^\varepsilon=\int A_y\,d\sigma^{\otimes N}$ almost everywhere.
The distributional characterization of $BV$ \cite{AFP} proves
\eqref{eq:v77-angular-bv} with
$C_m=4(J_m+1)H_m V_\Theta^N$.

Integrating the derivative kernels over the circle defines a finite
signed measure of variation at most $2\pi C_m$. Measurability follows
first for smooth test functions from Fubini, and then for the
variation and the Jordan kernels by the countable test-function
characterization of variation. This identifies the measure with
$D_tA^\varepsilon$ and its Jordan disintegrations. Testing with a
function independent of $\theta$ and using
\eqref{eq:v77-angular-marginal} proves the projection identity.
Jordan decomposition need not commute with this final projection.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The physical meaning of this current}
The finite-current argument just used is an application of the
inherited threshold method to the two-observable density. It is
not a new proof of collision-uniform weighted summability.
Its geometric computation can nevertheless be made entirely on
actual regular states. On a compact regular endpoint domain $D$
write $a=W/(4\pi c_*|B_m|)$ and $Y=X_q$. For a smooth test
$\psi(t,\theta)$, since $YF=1$ and $Y\vartheta=0$, the divergence
theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-angular-flux}
 D_t (F,\vartheta)_\#(a\one_D\,dq)
 = (F,\vartheta)_\#\left(
     \operatorname{div}(aY)\,dq|_D
       -(aY\cdot n)\,d\mathcal H^1|_{\partial D}\right).
\end{equation}
On the left, the derivative is that of the corresponding density.
For a locally Lipschitz weight the same identity follows by smooth
approximation on the compact domain. The bulk term is explicitly
\eqref{eq:v77-drift-formula}. At an artificial common boundary with
the same physical source, the two oriented traces cancel. A true
source jump leaves its jump flux. A limiting interior critical
boundary has the positive residue \eqref{eq:v77-angular-residue}.

Regular exhaustions converge to $\mathscr J^\varepsilon$ in
distributions: the omitted source mass tends to zero, and its action
is bounded by that mass times $\|\partial_t\psi\|_\infty$.
No convergence of the separately absolute-valued bulk and boundary
terms is asserted. The full finite measure is secured by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v77-angular-bv}, not by an unproved uniform bound
on an exhaustion. True singularities, first-hit switches, and
section decisions thus remain in the signed physical current.
Only actual cancellation at the same angle and exact label is
credited before taking its negative part.

Set
\begin{align}
 \Delta_r^\varepsilon(t)&=
       \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{0<h<r}\Delta_h^\varepsilon(t),
                                      \label{eq:v77-maximal-defect}\\
 V_r^\varepsilon(t)&=(\mathscr J^\varepsilon)^-
                             ([t,t+r]\times\mathbb S^1).
                                      \label{eq:v77-angular-strip}
\end{align}
The closed interval is harmless for the essential bounds and retains
all possible boundary atoms. All translated roofs remain on the
full roof line, not on a truncated central target.

\begin{theorem}[Complete angular persistence]
\label{thm:v77-persistence}
For every fixed $r>0$ and almost every $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-persistence}
 [b^\varepsilon(t)-2A_r b^\varepsilon(t)]_+
                 \le\Delta_r^\varepsilon(t)\le V_r^\varepsilon(t).
\end{equation}
The defect on the left comparison is no larger than the maximal
forward stopped-flow defect for the explicit field of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v77-field}. Positive interior critical entrances
are not charged as forward losses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a $BV$ function $f$,
$[f(t)-f(t+h)]_+\le(Df)^-([t,t+h])$ for almost every $(t,h)$.
Apply this to $A^\varepsilon(\,\cdot\,,\theta)$ and integrate in
$\theta$. The Jordan disintegration proves
$\Delta_h^\varepsilon(t)\le V_r^\varepsilon(t)$ when $0<h<r$,
and therefore the second inequality. The first follows by averaging
$b^\varepsilon(t)\le b^\varepsilon(t+h)+\Delta_r^\varepsilon(t)$
over $0<h<r$. Nonnegativity bounds this forward average by
$2A_r b^\varepsilon(t)$. The stopped-flow comparison follows
from \eqref{eq:v77-angular-drop}. The critical assertion follows
from its positive angular residue; unrelated true boundary terms
are still present in the signed sum.
\end{proof}

Define the ordered complete angular defect
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-ordered-defect}
 \mathcal A_{\varepsilon,M}(r)=
 \limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{R,n,k}m^2
       \esssup_{t\in\mathcal C_{m,R,n,k}(M)}\Delta_r^\varepsilon(t).
\end{equation}
There is no supremum over selected physical words in this definition;
their complete weighted sum has already been taken.

\begin{theorem}[Ordered implication for the unchanged pointwise law]
\label{thm:v77-closure}
Assume Input statement~\ref{input:v76-analytic}. For each sufficiently
large fixed $B$ and fixed $r>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-error}
 \mathcal E_M\le C'_M B^{-1/192}
                    +\mathcal A_{\varepsilon(B),M}(r).
\end{equation}
In particular the original target \eqref{eq:v76-target} follows if,
for some $A_M<\infty$, $q\ge0$, $\beta>0$ and fixed
$\varepsilon_0,r_0>0$, the complete source satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-remaining}
 \mathcal A_{\varepsilon,M}(r)
       \le A_M\varepsilon^{-q}r^\beta
       \quad(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0,\ 0<r<r_0).
\end{equation}
The same bound on the ordered norm of $V_r^\varepsilon$ is sufficient.
Estimate \eqref{eq:v77-remaining} is an additional dynamical estimate;
it is not proved by the fixed-count variation theorem or the
integrated identity \eqref{eq:v77-angular-total}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the positive remainder in \eqref{eq:v76-remainder} and
\eqref{eq:v77-persistence} to bound the pointwise error by
$2m^2A_r b^{\varepsilon(B)}+m^2\Delta_r^{\varepsilon(B)}+|e_B|$.
Fix $B$, $\varepsilon(B)$, $r$, and each auxiliary spectral band
before the collision limsup. The fixed-width complete-source bound
then gives
\[
 \mathcal E_M\le 2C\varepsilon(B)^{1/16}
                 +\mathcal A_{\varepsilon(B),M}(r)+C_MB^{-1/192}.
\]
Since $\varepsilon(B)=A_0B^{-1/12}$, this proves
\eqref{eq:v77-error}. Under \eqref{eq:v77-remaining}, take
$r(B)=c\varepsilon(B)^{(q+a)/\beta}$ with $a>0$ and a fixed
$c>0$ small enough that $r(B)<r_0$. This is fixed before the
collision limit. Enlarge the auxiliary spectral band only afterwards
and let $B\to\infty$ last. The two errors tend to zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The same scalar source in path normalization}
The retained path input uses a common finite positive path remainder
$\mathsf B_B(t)$ of mass $m^2b^{\varepsilon(B)}(t)$, and
\[
 \mathsf P_m(t)-\mathsf L_m(t)=\mathsf B_B(t)+\mathsf e_B(t),
 \qquad\limsup_m\sup_{R,n,k}\esssup_{\mathcal C(M)}
       \|\mathsf e_B(t)\|_{\mathrm{BL}^*}\le C_MB^{-1/192}.
\]
The bounded-Lipschitz class contains the constant one, so the norm of
the positive remainder is its mass. Under
\eqref{eq:v77-remaining}, the preceding proof makes this mass vanish
in the same ordered limits. Path-numerator convergence then follows.
Ratios may be formed only on central arithmetic classes with scalar
reference bounded below by a fixed positive constant. No arithmetic
zero class is divided by. Collision-to-return clock transfer and
forward likelihood retain their own inherited hypotheses. None of
these conclusions is used to establish the scalar estimate.
