\section{The complete central window in diffusive coordinates}
\label{sec:v89-global-window}
Uniformity over the location of a fixed-length interval does not
bound an unnormalized integral over a growing interval. It does,
however, give a global theorem in the natural diffusive roof measure.
We state that normalization explicitly.

For fixed $M>0$ let $H=[-M,M]$ and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v89-rescaled-error}
 \widetilde f_{m,R,n,k}(u)
 =f_{m,R,n,k}(m\bar\tau_R+\sqrt m\,u)
   \one_{\mathcal C_{m,R,n,k}(M)}(m\bar\tau_R+\sqrt m\,u).
\end{equation}
There is no integration over neighboring displacement or occupation
labels. The amplitude remains the original $m^2p-\mathcal L$.

\begin{lemma}[Gluing local norms with their exact scale factor]
\label{lem:v89-gluing}
Let a roof interval $K$ be partitioned into $N$ pieces, each contained
in an interval of length one. If the local $L^p$ norms of $f$ are
at most $d_m$, then
\[
             \int_K|f|^p\le N d_m^p.
\]
If its local critical measurable-set norms are at most $A_m$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v89-gluing}
              \mathcal N_Q(f;K)\le A_m N^{1-\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Under $t=t_0+\sqrt m\,u$, the critical norm is multiplied by
$m^{-(1-\gamma)/2}$ and the $p$th-power integral by $m^{-1/2}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is finite additivity. For $E\subset K$ write
$v_j$ for the measures of its intersections with the partition.
Then $\int_E|f|\le A_m\sum_jv_j^\gamma$.
H\"older on the finite counting measure gives
$\sum_jv_j^\gamma\le N^{1-\gamma}(\sum_jv_j)^\gamma$.
Divide by $|E|^\gamma$ and take the supremum. The scale factors
follow by change of variable, including the measure in the
measurable-set normalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A full diffusive-window raw limit]
\label{thm:v89-diffusive}
For every fixed $M>0$, every $1\le p<Q$ and every $\sigma>0$,
\begin{align}
 \sup_{R,n,k}\|\widetilde f_{m,R,n,k}\|_{L^p(H)}
                                      &\longrightarrow0,
                                     \label{eq:v89-global-lp}\\
 \sup_{R,n,k}\mathcal N_{Q,\sigma}
                (\widetilde f_{m,R,n,k};H)&\longrightarrow0,
                                     \label{eq:v89-global-log}\\
 \limsup_m\sup_{R,n,k}\mathcal N_Q
                (\widetilde f_{m,R,n,k};H)&\le C_M.
                                     \label{eq:v89-global-bound}
\end{align}
In original roof coordinates the first assertion is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v89-global-original}
 \sup_{R,n,k}\left\{\frac1{\sqrt m}
  \int_{\mathcal C_{m,R,n,k}(M)}
       |m^2p_{n,R}(k,m,t)-\mathcal L_{m,R}(k_1,k_2,t,n)|^p\,dt
                         \right\}^{1/p}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
No unnormalized growing-window norm or pointwise theorem is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The central roof interval is contained in
$[m\bar\tau_R-M\sqrt m,m\bar\tau_R+M\sqrt m]$.
Partition this interval into at most $2M\sqrt m+2$ pieces as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v89-gluing}. The inherited uniform local $L^p$
error $d_m$ tends to zero. After scaling, its $p$th-power integral
is at most $(2M+2/\sqrt m)d_m^p$. This proves
\eqref{eq:v89-global-lp} and its exact coordinate form.

The local critical norm is eventually bounded by a common constant
by Proposition~\ref{prop:v89-density-bound}. Its scaled glued bound
is at most $A_m(2M+2/\sqrt m)^{1-\gamma}$, proving
\eqref{eq:v89-global-bound}. Now use the global $L^1$ convergence
already proved and Lemma~\ref{lem:v89-log-interpolation} on the fixed
interval $H$. This proves \eqref{eq:v89-global-log}.
The number of intervals is handled in the displayed scale factor,
not treated as fixed before the collision limit.
\end{proof}

The residue concentration requirement for
\eqref{eq:v76-target} is unchanged. The new estimates keep every
measurable roof subset in the critical distribution norm before the
collision limit, but their logarithmic loss still allows sufficiently
narrow high peaks. Likewise $dt/\sqrt m$ in
\eqref{eq:v89-global-original} cannot be replaced by $dt$ without
an additional collision-rate estimate. Neither a same-roof path ratio
nor a forward likelihood follows merely from these global finite-norm
conclusions. Their scalar pointwise denominator and positive-arithmetic
restrictions remain the retained downstream hypotheses.
