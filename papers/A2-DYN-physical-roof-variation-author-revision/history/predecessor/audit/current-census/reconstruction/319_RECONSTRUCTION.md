## 6. Weighted overlay: all countable cases

Let two families of pairwise disjoint open intervals in a fixed bounded interval have nonnegative summable weights `a_I,b_J`. A nonempty overlap `I cap J` is itself an open interval. Charge `a_I b_J` to the interval with the later left endpoint, breaking a tie in favor of `I`.

If a charge is assigned to `I`, the other interval `J` starts no later than the left endpoint of `I` and extends strictly to its right. Two disjoint open `J` intervals cannot both do that. Hence a fixed `I` receives at most one charge, of size at most `a_I sup_J b_J`. The same argument applies to charges assigned to `J`. Touching intervals with empty open intersection produce no charge. Equal left endpoints, containment, zero weights and an empty family satisfy the same argument.

The finite-family inequality is

\[
\sum_{I\cap J\ne\varnothing}a_Ib_J
 \le (\sup_Jb_J)\sum_Ia_I+(\sup_Ia_I)\sum_Jb_J.
\]

Any finite set of overlap pairs is contained in finite subfamilies. Nonnegative monotone exhaustion therefore proves the countable inequality without an interchange of a conditional series. Summing over the two disjoint labelled angular circles is bounded by the combined sums and combined suprema. No minimum interval size, common endpoint momentum, word-connectedness assertion or comparison-cell abundance enters this step.
