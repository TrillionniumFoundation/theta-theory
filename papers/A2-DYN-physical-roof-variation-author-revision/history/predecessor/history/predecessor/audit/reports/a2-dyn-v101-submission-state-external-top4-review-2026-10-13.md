## 5. Global endpoint-momentum uniqueness

Module 238 fixes a lifted finite itinerary and two endpoint tangential momenta. For a contact configuration

\[
Q=(Q_0,\ldots,Q_m),
\]

it considers the polygonal length

\[
S(Q)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|Q_{j+1}-Q_j|.
\]

The function is convex in the Cartesian contact variables. For two regular physical trajectories with the same lifted itinerary, monotonicity of the gradient gives

\[
\sum_{j=0}^m
(\nabla_jS(Q)-\nabla_jS(\widehat Q))\cdot
(Q_j-\widehat Q_j)\ge0.
\]

At an interior contact the reflection law makes the gradient a strictly negative normal multiple. At the two endpoints, equality of the tangential momenta gives equality of the positive normal speeds. Since both contacts lie on the same circle,

\[
Q_j-\widehat Q_j=R(n_j-\widehat n_j),
\]

and the tangent-difference contribution vanishes against the normal difference. The monotonicity expression becomes the negative of a sum of strictly positive coefficients times

\[
|n_j-\widehat n_j|^2.
\]

It can be nonnegative only when every contact agrees.

This is a coherent global argument. It does not require convexity of the physical word domain, an interpolating path of physical trajectories, or a global angular lift. Repeated visits to one scatterer are separate Cartesian variables. I found no sign error in this proof.
