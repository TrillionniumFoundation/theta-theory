## 6. Audit of module 211: two-sided physical ends

### 6.1. The backward end is treated inside the same exact event

The new source uses maximal-segment coordinates

```text
(sigma,u),  a_sigma < u < b_sigma,
```

with forward and backward lifetimes `tau+` and `tau-`. The proof does not reverse the billiard record or relabel the occupation event. It examines the earlier endpoint of the same regular segment.

For a backward segment of length at most `s`, the proof separates:

- actual incidence, clearance, or section-decision failure; and
- a critical backward endpoint.

In the regular noncritical case, strict positivity of all physical margins would continue both the field and the exact word, contradicting maximality. In the critical case radial growth gives an endpoint-gradient bound of order `sqrt(s)`. These alternatives are compatible with the retained stopped-flow geometry, and chart seams are not counted as physical ends.

Subject to the inherited all-contact motion and endpoint continuation inputs, I find this classification coherent.

### 6.2. Physical cap and localized finite-count estimate

The claimed positive cap is

```text
bar-nu{tau+ <= s} + bar-nu{tau- <= s} <= C sqrt(s).
```

The proof pays incidence layers quadratically in incidence width and section/clearance layers linearly in geometric width. At depth-weighted widths of order `q_0^d sqrt(s)`, the complete geometric sum is `O(sqrt(s))`. The critical alternative is paid by a direct endpoint-gradient set.

The localized estimate has the form

```text
m^2 int_J e_s^+ + m^2 int_J e_s^-
 <= C s^(1/32)(|J|+B_*^(-1))
  + C_{B_*}(1+B_*|J|)m^3 min(theta_{B_*}^m,sqrt(s)).
```

The minimum is important. It is placed on a positive upper estimate rather than falsely assigned to an isolated signed spectral remainder. The elementary implication used to combine a spectral estimate with a physical cap is valid for nonnegative quantities.

I found no exponent inconsistency in this step.

### 6.3. Supremum over all finite collision counts

For fixed `0<theta<1`, the module proves

```text
sup_{m>=2} m^3 min(theta^m,sqrt(s))
 <= C_theta sqrt(s)(1+|log s|)^3.
```

Splitting at collision count comparable to `log(s^(-1/2))/(-log theta)` gives the stated logarithmic polynomial. Since `1/32<1/2`, the expression is bounded by a constant times `s^(1/32)` on `(0,1)` after changing the constant.

This is a genuine finite-count envelope. The auxiliary band is fixed before the supremum over `m`; no band is allowed to grow with collision count. That order directly addresses a central concern of the preceding report.

## 7. Audit of module 211: oscillation of the full original source

### 7.1. The lifted density and the guard derivative

On each maximal segment the new proof studies

```text
f_sigma^epsilon(u) = A_sigma(u) W^epsilon(Psi_sigma(u)),
```

extended by zero outside the segment. The absolute value is taken before summing over segments. Consequently the lifted oscillation dominates the translation difference of the projected source rather than relying on cancellation between different physical segments.

The retained angular-density derivative is used as

```text
|A'| <= C A I_m / z.
```

The original product guard gives

```text
|(W^epsilon)'| <= C epsilon^(-1)/z.
```

The resulting product derivative

```text
|(A W^epsilon)'|
 <= C A I_m/z + C epsilon^(-1) A/z
```

keeps the complete physical density. It does not set an inverse Jacobian equal to one or replace the source by a protected subword.

### 7.2. Payment of the inverse gradient

When the derivative is integrated over starting roofs, an intermediate roof point is counted for a set of starting positions of length at most

```text
min(h,u-a_sigma).
```

Radial growth gives `z(u)^2 >= c(u-a_sigma)`. The two load-bearing inequalities are

```text
min(h,u-a_sigma)/z(u)
 <= C h^(5/8) z(u)^(-1/4),

min(h,u-a_sigma)/z(u)
 <= C sqrt(h).
```

Both are correct by splitting into `u-a_sigma <= h` and `u-a_sigma >= h`. The first pays the optical derivative with the actual fractional source `I_m z^(-1/4)`; the second pays the guard derivative with the original density.

After the Borel segment bijection, the complete internal variation is bounded by

```text
C h^(5/8) int v^(1/4)
 + C epsilon^(-1) sqrt(h) int p.
```

No physical-word multiplicity appears.

### 7.3. Entry and exit mismatches

Zero extension of a segment creates terms where only one of `t` and `t+h` belongs to the segment. Revision 90 does not discard these terms. It charges them to the actual forward and backward end layers. This is essential: a common-survivor estimate alone would not control the original source.

Combining the internal variation with both end layers yields

```text
m^2 Xi_h^epsilon(J)
 <= K_{B_*,L}(h^(1/32)+epsilon^(-1)sqrt(h)).
```

For the unweighted density `p`, the guard-derivative term is absent.

I find the logic of this complete-source translation estimate internally consistent, subject to the inherited Borel parametrization and derivative inputs.

### 7.4. Exact scope

It is still an `L1` translation estimate. It does not control the starting-roof essential supremum of the source or of its translation difference.

## 8. Audit of module 212: arbitrary Borel prefixes

### 8.1. Why the prefix theorem is relevant

The original exceptional source uses the selector

```text
z<a or ell<delta.
```

On a maximal segment, total residence `ell=b-a` is constant, while radial growth makes `z` strictly increasing with roof coordinate. Thus a short-residence segment is selected completely; otherwise the set `z<a` is empty, full, or a single initial interval.

Accordingly, it is enough to prove a uniform estimate for every Borel prefix endpoint `c_sigma` without imposing angular regularity on the graph of that endpoint. This is the correct source class. It does not replace the original union by a common-survivor or transported approximation.

### 8.2. Crossing near the original entrance

If the prefix endpoint satisfies

```text
c_sigma-a_sigma < rho,
```

then every crossing starting roof has backward lifetime below `rho`. The crossing is therefore paid by the backward end layer. This includes prefix endpoints approaching a critical entrance and avoids assuming a trace on an irregular cutoff graph.

### 8.3. Interior anchor

If

```text
c_sigma-a_sigma >= rho,
```

an anchor interval is chosen a fixed fraction of `rho` before the prefix endpoint. Radial growth gives `z^2 >= c rho` throughout the comparison interval. Averaging the exact angular-density derivative over the anchor yields two positive sources:

```text
(h/rho) int p,

h rho^(-3/8) int v^(1/4).
```

Because there is at most one anchor interval per original maximal segment and the Borel parametrization is one-to-one, summing these intervals introduces no word-count factor. No angular derivative of `c_sigma` is used.

The crossing estimate is therefore

```text
C_{c,h}^epsilon(J)
 <= int_J e_rho^-
  + C(h/rho) int p
  + C h rho^(-3/8) int v^(1/4).
```

I find this estimate coherent at the stated source interfaces.

### 8.4. The exponent `1/33`

The complete prefix translation bound contains

```text
h^(1/32),
epsilon^(-1)sqrt(h),
rho^(1/32),
h/rho,
h rho^(-3/8).
```

Choosing

```text
rho = 4 h^(32/33)
```

gives

```text
rho^(1/32) = O(h^(1/33)),
h/rho       = O(h^(1/33)),
h rho^(-3/8)= O(h^(7/11)).
```

The last exponent is strictly larger than `1/33`, hence smaller at microscopic scales. The balance and the condition `h<=rho/4` are consistent for sufficiently small `h`.

Thus the uniform prefix estimate

```text
m^2 int_J |f_c^epsilon(t+h)-f_c^epsilon(t)|dt
 <= K_{B_*,L}(h^(1/33)+epsilon^(-1)sqrt(h))
```

has correct printed exponent arithmetic.

### 8.5. Exact representation of the original union

For `ell<delta`, the proof takes the full segment. On every remaining segment it takes the prefix where `z<a`. Strict radial monotonicity makes this prefix unique, and the endpoint can be chosen Borel from the existing segment parametrization.

This represents the union exactly. The intersection is not double counted, no exact label is pooled, and no positive gradient or residence cutoff is imposed on the final translation theorem.

### 8.6. Limitation

The supremum is over collision counts, labels, cutoffs, Borel prefix choices, and interval locations, but the quantity controlled remains

```text
int_J |f(t+h)-f(t)|dt.
```

The theorem does not provide

```text
esssup_t |f(t+h)-f(t)|
```

or a quantitative lower bound on the roof width occupied by a high spike.

## 9. Audit of module 213: the microscopic scale integral

### 9.1. Finite-count summation over every translation scale

For `0<s<1/33`, integrating the prefix translation envelope against `h^(-1-s)` gives

```text
int_0^r h^(-1-s) int_J |R(t+h)-R(t)|dt dh
 <= K [r^(1/33-s)/(1/33-s)
       +epsilon^(-1) r^(1/2-s)/(1/2-s)].
```

The proof first introduces a positive lower cutoff in `h`, applies the finite-count estimate, and then removes that cutoff by monotone convergence. The scale integral is therefore controlled before collision limits. It is not an infinite sum of collision limsups.

This is a genuine improvement over a collection of unrelated fixed-scale estimates.

### 9.2. Mass/translation interpolation

The interpolation lemma assumes

```text
int |f(t+h)-f(t)|dt <= A h^chi,
||f||_1 = mu,
0<s<chi<1.
```

The same difference is at most `2mu`. Splitting the Gagliardo integral where

```text
A h^chi = 2mu
```

gives

```text
||f||_{W^{s,1}}
 <= C [mu + A^(s/chi) mu^(1-s/chi)].
```

The exponent calculation is correct and the lemma is applied to each actual finite-count density before taking parameter suprema and collision limsups.

### 9.3. Cofinal original source

The source is kept exactly as in revision 89:

```text
R_r^epsilon = R_{r^(1/8),r^(1/4)}^epsilon.
```

The inherited interval-mass estimate is

```text
limsup m^2 int_J R_r^epsilon <= C_L r^(1/128).
```

Combining `chi=1/33`, the translation constant of size `1+epsilon^(-1)`, and the mass estimate yields

```text
limsup ||m^2 R_r^epsilon||_{W^{s,1}(J)}
 <= C_{L,s}(1+epsilon^(-1))^(33s)
          r^((1-33s)/128),
```

for every fixed `epsilon>0` and `0<s<1/33`.

The power `(1-33s)/128` follows directly from `1-s/chi=1-33s`. It is positive precisely in the stated range.

### 9.4. Regional fractional capacity

Truncating

```text
u = min(1,m^2 R_r^epsilon/lambda)
```

is one-Lipschitz and gives the stated regional `W^{s,1}` capacity estimate for the superlevel set. This measures more than Lebesgue mass: it controls the fractional variational cost of the large-value region.

The manuscript appropriately avoids claiming a pointwise capacity theorem on null sets or an essential-height estimate.

### 9.5. Outer-limit arithmetic

With the retained outer guard schedule `epsilon(B)` and a cofinal microscopic width

```text
r(B)=c epsilon(B)^N,
```

the explicit fractional bound vanishes when

```text
N > 4224 s/(1-33s).
```

Indeed the epsilon exponent is

```text
-33s + N(1-33s)/128,
```

which is positive under exactly that condition. No collision-dependent auxiliary band is introduced by this schedule.

This proves a legal cofinal fractional-variation conclusion. It does not prove the essential-height charge zero.

## 10. Why the new regularity still does not close the pointwise theorem

The manuscript itself gives the correct model obstruction. On a fixed roof interval, let

```text
f_d = 1_(0,d).
```

Then

```text
||f_d||_infinity = 1,
||f_d||_1 = d,
[f_d]_{W^{s,1}} = O(d^(1-s))
```

for every `s<1`. Thus mass and fractional variation can both vanish while essential height remains one.

This example is not asserted to be a physical counterexample. Its role is logical: it shows that the topology proved in revision 90 cannot by itself imply the target topology.

More conceptually, in one dimension a generic `W^{s,1}` estimate controls point values only beyond the threshold `s>1`, while revision 90 proves `s<1/33`. The gap is not a missing epsilon in the printed exponent. It is a qualitative gap between integrated oscillation and pointwise persistence.

The regional capacity estimate has the same limitation. Sets supporting fixed-height pulses may have fractional capacity tending to zero as their widths collapse. Therefore capacity decay does not exclude a high spike unless one proves a separate physical theorem forcing any high value to persist on a set of quantitatively positive capacity or width.

## 14. What a successful next revision must prove

The next revision should not merely improve `1/33` to another exponent below a generic pointwise threshold. It needs a theorem using additional physical structure to exclude collapsing high spikes.

Several mathematically relevant routes are possible.

### 14.1. Quantitative spike persistence

Prove that if the normalized original exceptional source exceeds a level `lambda` at a Lebesgue point, then it remains above a fixed fraction of `lambda` on a roof interval whose length is bounded below by an explicit function of `lambda`, uniformly in collision count, exact labels, radius, and the cofinal cutoffs. The revision-89 mass decay would then force the essential height to vanish.

This must be a source-specific persistence theorem. Generic `L1` translation continuity does not give such a lower-width bound.

### 14.2. A pointwise maximal estimate

Establish a collision-uniform inequality controlling the source at almost every roof by local averages plus a remainder that vanishes in the legal outer limit. The constants must remain uniform over exact labels and the original cutoffs. A weak maximal estimate that only reproduces measure decay of superlevel sets will not suffice.

### 14.3. Stronger regularity with a vanishing norm

A Sobolev/Besov route would need regularity and integrability beyond the one-dimensional pointwise threshold, together with a norm that actually tends to zero. Merely obtaining a bounded `BV` norm or a bounded `W^{s,p}` norm below `sp=1` still permits narrow fixed-height spikes.

The relevant endpoint would be, for example, a vanishing norm in a space embedded into `L-infinity`, or an equivalent physical estimate on oscillation at every starting roof.

### 14.4. Direct control of physical superlevel geometry

Use the maximal-segment geometry, monotone radial coordinate, exact angular density, and end classification to show that high source values cannot be supported on arbitrarily thin roof prefixes. The new prefix formalism may be a useful starting point, but the current theorem controls the integral of crossings, not the width of a high-value component.

### 14.5. Preserve the exact limit order

Any such argument must retain:

- the complete finite-count source before collision limits;
- one fixed auxiliary band before the collision limit;
- both physical ends and critical entrances;
- the original guard and exact labels;
- the original union rather than a protected substitute;
- the reconstruction band as the outermost limit; and
- the distinction between arithmetic zero and positive reference classes.
