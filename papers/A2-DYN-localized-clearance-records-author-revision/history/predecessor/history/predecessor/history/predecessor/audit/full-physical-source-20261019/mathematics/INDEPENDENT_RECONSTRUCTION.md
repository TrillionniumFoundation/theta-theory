## 2. The full physical mixed singularity

### 2.1 Which kernel is actually used

Theorem mc-kernel is an angular fiber bound for the original full physical source at every fixed initial momentum \(a\), including every actual word. Integrating that statement against \(da/(4\pi)\) gives

\[
(p_0,p_\ell)_*\nu
\le\frac{C_{\mathrm{mom}}}{4\pi}\,da\,dv
\qquad(\ell\ge1).
\]

The kernel constant remains the finite uniform constant defined through the inherited physical curve and growth chain. There is no section factor in this unconditioned inequality. The conditional version in the inherited theorem has one such factor; reusing that conditional formula as an unconditioned one would have given an incorrect normalization. The new proof correctly starts from the angular fiber statement.

Take \(\ell=m\). A disk of momentum radius \(s\) has area at most \(\pi s^2\), also when it extends beyond the square \((-1,1)^2\). Hence for every \(s>0\),

\[
\nu\{r<s\}\le A_0s^2,\qquad A_0=C_{\mathrm{mom}}/4.
\]

For an actual interior index \(1\le i<m\), take instead the physical block \(\ell=i\). Since \(r<s\) implies \(|p_0|<s\), its source is bounded by the rectangle in the true joint coordinates \((p_0,p_i)\). The first coordinate length is at most \(2s\). The second coordinate set, defined by \(c_i<u\), has length

\[
2(1-\sqrt{1-u^2})\le2u^2\quad(0<u\le1).
\]

For \(u>1\), its length is 2, still at most \(2u^2\). Thus for all \(s,u>0\),

\[
\nu\{r<s,c_i<u\}\le A_1su^2,\qquad A_1=C_{\mathrm{mom}}/\pi.
\]

This is a joint-density upper bound for an actual shorter block, with later tests dropped only on the upper side of a positive inequality. It is not the product of two physical probabilities. In particular no terminal collision or internal grazing contact is hidden. The validity for large \(s\) and large \(u\) is needed in the next annular argument and is proved explicitly.

### 2.2 All-level dyadic estimate

For any \(b,t>0\), the part \(r<b\) has mass at most \(A_0b^2\). On the actual annulus \(2^jb\le r<2^{j+1}b\), the inequality \((rc_i)^{-1}>t\) implies \(c_i<(t2^jb)^{-1}\). The preceding joint bound therefore gives

\[
\nu\{2^jb\le r<2^{j+1}b,\ (rc_i)^{-1}>t\}
\le\frac{2A_1}{t^2b}\,2^{-j}.
\]

Summing every \(j\ge0\) costs \(4A_1/(t^2b)\). Annuli beyond the physical bound \(r\le\sqrt2\) are empty; retaining them only enlarges the positive bound. Choosing

\[
b=(A_1/A_0)^{1/3}t^{-2/3}
\]

gives

\[
\nu\{(rc_i)^{-1}>t\}
\le K_\times t^{-4/3},\qquad
K_\times=5A_0^{1/3}A_1^{2/3}.
\]

The choice gives a valid explicit constant; it is not claimed to be the stationary minimum over \(b\). There is no small-\(t\) or large-\(t\) exception, since both input inequalities hold for all positive arguments.

For \(a=\int h\,d\nu\), layer cake at the break point
\((K_\times/a)^{3/4}\) gives

\[

\le\int_0^\infty\min\{a,K_\times t^{-4/3}\}\,dt
=4K_\times^{3/4}a^{1/4}.
\]

The case \(a=0\) is handled by nonnegative truncation. This calculation establishes the finite first moment before it is used.
