\section{Exact composition of feasible profiles}
\label{sec:composition}
The previous bounds accumulate a compatibility cost through time.
There is also an exact composition operation across distinguished
experiments. Its hypothesis is operational: the distinguishing label
must be recovered at the end and is not externally available after its
initial preparation.

Take $m$ complete terminal arrays $C_i$, on a common horizon and common
future input alphabets. Their initial alphabets may be identified by
padding. Define the \emph{verifiably tagged sum} $C^\oplus$ by receiving
$(i,x)$ initially and requiring terminal output $(i,b)$ with law
$C_i(b\mid x,\mathbf y,j)$. A wrong tag has probability zero. The tag
is not repeated in later inputs, and previous outputs are not readable
as memory. All branches have the same full suffix alphabet.

For two sets of integer profiles their sum below denotes coordinatewise
Minkowski sum, including the already defined upward closure.

\begin{theorem}[Profile addition under a retained tag]
\label{thm:tagged-sum}
For the internal registers of the tagged sum,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile-sum}
                   \cF(C^\oplus)=\cF(C_1)+\cdots+\cF(C_m).
\end{equation}
The identity holds for arbitrary horizons and arbitrary stochastic
realizations. It is an identity of complete feasible-profile sets, not
just of separate minima or a scalar matrix rank.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a realization and a cut $t$. Let $S_{t,i}$ consist of states having
positive probability after some prefix from branch $i$. If one state
belonged to $S_{t,i}$ and $S_{t,k}$ with $i\ne k$, run the same fixed
future input word from that state. Exact tag recovery in branch $i$
forces this conditional continuation to emit tag $i$ with probability
one: a positive mixture weight could not otherwise give zero wrong-tag
probability. The same reasoning in branch $k$ forces tag $k$. This is
impossible. Thus the sets $S_{t,i}$ are disjoint.

For every allowed next input, a positive transition from $S_{t,i}$
leads to $S_{t+1,i}$, since its source is reachable after some branch-$i$
prefix and the full product suffix is allowed. Restricting the machine
to these state sets and stripping its terminal tag therefore gives a
complete realization of $C_i$. In particular its profile
$(|S_{t,i}|)_t$ is feasible. Their coordinatewise sum does not exceed
the original profile. Padding any one of the restricted realizations
absorbs each unused coordinate and gives membership in the right side
of \eqref{eq:profile-sum}.

Conversely, use disjoint copies of the branch registers and transition
rows. Initialization selects the declared branch, which is thereafter
stored by membership in its disjoint register. The final decoder emits
its tag and answer. This gives the sum of any feasible branch profiles
and proves the reverse inclusion. No independent shared seed or free
branch selector is supplied.
\end{proof}

If the final tag were dropped, this proof would fail: states serving two
branches could be identified. Likewise, branch-dependent legal suffixes
must not be substituted for the common full alphabet without a separate
argument. These are hypotheses of the operation, not conclusions about
unlabelled mixtures.

\begin{corollary}[An exact growing two-cut frontier]
\label{cor:pareto-sum}
Let every branch be the two-cut array \eqref{eq:primitive}, with a fixed
$9/10\le h<1$, and let $m\ge1$. Its tagged sum has exactly the feasible
internal profiles
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growing-frontier}
              K_1\ge3m,\qquad K_2\ge3m,\qquad K_1+K_2\ge7m.
\end{equation}
The Pareto-minimal pairs are $(3m+j,4m-j)$, $j=0,\ldots,m$.
Both separate minima are $3m$, and the minimum full peak, including the
$2m$-symbol terminal output, is $\lceil7m/2\rceil$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Appendix~\ref{sec:primitive} proves the primitive feasible set as the
upward closure of $(3,4),(4,3)$. Its $m$-fold Minkowski sum has the
listed minimal pairs. To check that \eqref{eq:growing-frontier} has no
additional holes, put $u=K_1-3m$, $v=K_2-3m$. If $u,v\ge0$ and
$u+v\ge m$, choose an integer $j$ with
$\max(0,m-v)\le j\le\min(m,u)$. Then
$(K_1,K_2)\ge(3m+j,4m-j)$. The peak is minimized by the nearest
integer balance of $j$ and $m-j$, giving $\lceil7m/2\rceil$.
The output register has $2m\le\lceil7m/2\rceil$ labels.
\end{proof}

The primitive answer probabilities have full support, but the tagged
sum has zeros at wrong tags. We do not use it as the full-support part
of Theorem~\ref{thm:headline}; that theorem already has a uniform
full-support interval and fixed alphabets. The two results answer
different questions: \eqref{eq:growing-frontier} is an exact finite
Pareto law, whereas \eqref{eq:profile-power} excludes bounded
simultaneous width while every separate positive minimum remains three.
