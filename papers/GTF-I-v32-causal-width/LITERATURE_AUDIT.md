# Theorem-level literature audit — revision 32

The record below distinguishes material actually inspected in this revision from background citations. It is not an independent priority certification. The principal comparison is between a stationary positive realization and a sequence of exact finite-horizon, cut-dependent realizations with bounded width.

## Directly inspected primary sources

### Benvenuti–Farina, A Tutorial on the Positive Realization Problem (2004)

Full 14-page author/preprint PDF inspected at https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~sussmann/papers/res-farina-tutorial-positive-realization.pdf . Theorems 2 and 3 give invariant-polyhedral-cone/shift-cone formulations of stationary positive realization. Example 4, printed page 5, uses irrational rotation to exclude finite positive realization. Section VII discusses the relation between a positive realization's order and extremal generators, including the need for care in MIMO reductions.

The rotation obstruction and invariant-cone mechanism are classical. The present article does not claim them. Our finite lower bound allows unrelated rows at every epoch and a different realizing machine for each horizon, while every whole finite array is realizable and every separate positive minimum is three. The new proof uses a cumulative volume budget; it does not invoke a repeated hidden matrix's peripheral spectrum.

### Monras–Winter, Quantum learning of classical stochastic processes (2016)

Full preprint https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.3634 inspected, particularly Theorem 6, Example 2/Lemma 7 and Section 4/Theorem 9. The positive realization theorem is credited there to Dharmadhikari. The reachable and unobservable quotient construction is credited there to Ito–Amari–Kobayashi. These sources allow nonminimal hidden realizations; they are not residual-state-only criteria.

The new proof follows that caution: its projected object is the image of `aff{reachable distributions} intersect Delta_K`, not the convex hull of falsely assigned minimal-coordinate images of all unit hidden states. The section can have more than K vertices. The `2^K` allowance and time-summed geometric defect are explicit new ingredients of the finite width argument, not a claim that quotient realizations are new.

### Gillis–Glineur, On the geometric interpretation of the nonnegative rank

Full preprint https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.0880 inspected, especially Definition 1 and Theorem 1. Restricted nonnegative rank imposes an affine/linear restriction on factors; unrestricted hidden generators are not automatically in that slice. Static normalized factorizations alone do not impose common sequential transitions.

All unit state continuations are allowed in our lower bound. A lifted section is therefore retained. We do not claim ordinary rank or the number of vertices in a minimal observable polygon equals arbitrary hidden-state count.

### Lumbreras–Ma–Thompson–Gu (August 2026)

Primary arXiv record https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19779 and full HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19779v1 inspected, particularly Section IV.2 and its linked finite-classical proofs. The paper studies infinite-horizon decision and value errors for world models and exact quantum realizations. Its displayed explanation repeats one conditional classical update and applies Perron–Frobenius limits against an irrational phase.

This is relevant recent literature and is cited. Our new theorem concerns exact finite classical arrays and permits time-varying hidden rows; it makes no quantum advantage or irreducible action-value error claim. No assertion of exhaustive non-overlap with every appendix of this recent work is made.

### Denis–Esposito and the probabilistic-residual tradition

Full https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0602093 inspected in the retained chain and re-used for Proposition 16's finite residual-generation statement. The 2002 Esposito–Lemay–Denis–Dupont chapter is cited with its actual DOI and bibliographic information. We have not obtained that original chapter in full and do not invent a theorem-level audit of unavailable pages.

A residual automaton restricts state languages; the rotation lower bound does not. A large residual count alone would not prove the new result, because the minimum separate normalized positive dictionary remains three. The earlier residual-vs-positive finite-language separation remains in the unchanged supporting article and is not counted as a new v32 theorem.

### Qi–Comon–Lim, Semialgebraic Geometry of Nonnegative Tensor Rank

Full preprint https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.05351 inspected at Section 4, Lemma 4.1. Scalar nonnegative direct-sum additivity is proved by positivity and zero-support constraints. That mechanism is credited.

The tagged theorem here operates on complete feasible profile *sets* and proves that all reachable state subsets restrict to common sequential machines. Its Minkowski sum retains incompatible cross-time choices. This should not be advertised as discovering scalar nonnegative-rank additivity.

### Pharr–Jakob–Humphreys, Physically Based Rendering, fourth edition

Author book Appendix A.2 at https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Sampling_Algorithms/Reservoir_Sampling inspected, including the weighted replacement update `weight/weightSum`. This is the sampling mechanism in the retained additive encoder proof. It is explicitly attributed as weighted reservoir sampling. The special finite-state consequences do not justify a priority claim for the sampling algorithm itself.

### Basu–Pollack–Roy, Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry, second edition

Publisher record and contents at https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/3-540-33099-2 verified, including chapters on cylindrical decomposition and quantifier elimination. The manuscript specifies a finite first-order polynomial optimization, algebraic-number input representation, and root isolation; it invokes the standard real-closed-field decision theorem. It does not claim to execute the enormous excluded-horizon optimization numerically or to give a polynomial-time algorithm.
