\begin{abstract}
We compare the least memory at individual cuts with the least peak memory
of one exact finite-horizon stochastic transducer. We construct a
uniformly full-support family on fixed alphabets whose separate minimum
is three at every internal cut, whereas its minimum simultaneous width
tends to infinity. The lower bound allows arbitrary time-dependent
stochastic rows and hidden continuations outside the observable affine
space. Its mechanism is a volume budget for projections of reachable
simplex sections: each command forces an enlargement, while bounded
state number bounds the number of projected vertices. For a golden-angle
rotation we obtain the explicit bound
$W_N\ge\frac16\log_2(N/24000000)$, alongside an exact
$4\lceil\sqrt{N+1}\rceil$-state construction. A rational rotation gives
the same unbounded incompatibility with effective algebraic horizon
thresholds and rational finite realizations. A separate composition
theorem identifies feasible profiles under a verifiable retained tag as
Minkowski sums; applied to the two-cut obstruction, it gives the exact
frontier $K_1,K_2\ge3m$, $K_1+K_2\ge7m$. The finite-horizon volume
inequality, rather than a stationary nonrealizability statement, is the
central relation between local positive rank and causal memory.
\end{abstract}
