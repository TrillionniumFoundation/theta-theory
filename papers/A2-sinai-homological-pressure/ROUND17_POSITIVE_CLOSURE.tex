\section{Round-twenty-one positive closure: weighted branch bundles and a global raw Fourier theorem}
\label{sec:r21-a2}

This section replaces the previous Round-Seventeen source.  The induced map is
written with its inverse-Jacobian weights; no unweighted sum of positive roof
norms is used.  The four coordinates are the two homology coordinates, the
integer return count, and the real roof.  Accordingly the local scale is
$n^{-2}$.

\subsection{The weighted induced operator}

For $R\in K=[0.45,0.47]$, let $F_R:Y_R\to Y_R$ be the first return to the
common finite family of regular magnet rectangles.  Write $\mathcal H_R$ for
its countable inverse branches, $h:Y_{R,h}\to Y_R$, and put
\[
 r_h=\hbox{return count},\qquad
 g_{R,h}={d(m_R\circ h)\over dm_R},\qquad
 \tau_{R,h}=\sum_{j=0}^{r_h-1}\tau_R\circ T_R^j\circ h .
\]
Homogeneity-adapted $C^k$ norms are denoted $\|\cdot\|_{C^k_*}$; a derivative
normal to the $j$th homogeneity boundary is multiplied by its standard
curvature weight.  This makes the distortion constants uniform at grazing
cuts.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted branch complexity]
\label{thm:r21-a2-complexity}
There are $\eta>0$, $C<\infty$, and an integer $p$ such that
\[
 \sum_{h\in\mathcal H_R}e^{\eta r_h}
 \|g_{R,h}\|_{C^2_*}
 \left(1+\|\tau_{R,h}\|_{C^4_*}^{p}
       +\|\partial_R^4\tau_{R,h}\|_{C^0_*}^{p}\right)
 \le C
\]
uniformly in $R$.  The same estimate holds after one central cylinder
insertion and after differentiating a branch amplitude through order four.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Group branches by $r_h=n$.  The change-of-variables identity gives
$\sum_{r_h=n}\int g_{R,h}\,dm_R=m_R(r=n)$.  The growth lemma and finite
horizon give, uniformly in $R$,
\[
 m_R(r=n)\le C_0e^{-c_0n}.
\]
Bounded distortion on a homogeneous branch turns the integral bound into
$\|g_{R,h}\|_{C^2_*}\le Cn^{p_0}\inf g_{R,h}$ after summation over the stable
subdivision.  Derivatives of a flight and of the radius chart grow at most
polynomially in $n$ in the weighted norm; the only unweighted singular factor
is exactly the inverse Jacobian already present in $g_{R,h}$.  Hence the
$n$th level of the displayed sum is bounded by
$Cn^{p_1}e^{-(c_0-\eta)n}$.  Choose $\eta<c_0/2$ and sum.  A fixed cylinder
changes only a bounded number of factors, and differentiated amplitudes obey
the same distortion estimate.
\end{proof}

For a stable test curve $W$, define the weak and strong norms
\[
 |f|_w=\sup_W\sup_{|\varphi|_{C^1}\le1}
          \left|\int_W f\varphi\,dm_W\right|,
\]
\[
 \|f\|_s=|f|_w+\sup_W\sup_{|\varphi|_{C^q}\le1}
          \left|\int_W f\varphi\,dm_W\right|
 +\sup_{W_1\sim W_2}{|\langle f,\varphi_1\rangle_{W_1}
 -\langle f,\varphi_2\rangle_{W_2}|\over d(W_1,W_2)^\alpha}.
\]
At a fold, the two children are retained as one parent current until after
pairing.  Fixed collision charts transport curves and test functions to a
reference family.  Let $\mathbb B_R\hookrightarrow\mathbb B_R^w$ be the
resulting completion, augmented by the finite parent-fold trace coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform parent-fold Banach bundle]
\label{thm:r21-a2-bundle}
The transported spaces form a locally trivial strong/weak Banach bundle over
$K$.  For
\[
 \mathcal L_{R,u,v,b}f
 =\sum_h g_{R,h}
 e^{i(u\cdot\kappa_h+vr_h+b\tau_{R,h})}f\circ h,
\]
there are $C<\infty$, $0<\rho<1$ such that
\[
 \|\mathcal L_{R,0,0,0}^nf\|_s
 \le C\rho^n\|f\|_s+C|f|_w,
\qquad
 |\mathcal L_{R,u,v,b}^nf|_w\le C\|f\|_s .
\]
The strong unit ball is relatively compact in the weak space.  On a common
complex neighborhood of the origin the twisted family is analytic in
$(u,v,b)$, $C^2$ in $R$, and has one simple leading eigenvalue with a common
Riesz contour.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Cut every image stable curve into homogeneous pieces.  On a piece, stable
contraction gives the strong contraction term and bounded distortion gives
the weak remainder.  Summing pieces uses
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-a2-complexity}; in particular, countably many branches
are never estimated by their number.  The growth lemma makes the one-step
expansion coefficient strictly smaller than one after a fixed iterate.
Parent-fold pairing is the push-forward of one smooth parent strip.  In the
signed square-root fold coordinate its odd child terms cancel, so the parent
coordinate has the same Lasota--Yorke bounds and is $C^2$ in $R$.

Compactness follows by first discarding the exponentially weighted return
tail and then applying Arzel\`a--Ascoli on the finite family of remaining
curve charts.  Analyticity of the twist follows from the exponential roof and
return moment in Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-a2-complexity}.  Mixing of the induced
map makes the eigenvalue one simple.  The uniform Lasota--Yorke inequality,
compact embedding, and compactness of $K$ give one Riesz contour and the
claimed perturbation statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact arithmetic and returned temporal nonintegrability}

A regular periodic orbit is represented by its sequence of scatterers and by
impact arclength variables $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)$.  Its reflection equations are
\[
 \Psi_j(R,z)=
 \left\langle {x_{j+1}-x_j\over|x_{j+1}-x_j|}
             -{x_j-x_{j-1}\over|x_j-x_{j-1}|},
             t_j(z_j)\right\rangle=0,
 \quad 1\le j\le m .
\]
The strict dispersing curvature makes the Hessian of total length positive on
a regular itinerary with fixed endpoints.  The compact interval $K$ is
covered by finitely many continuation charts of such solutions.

\begin{lemma}[Four independent periodic differences]
\label{lem:r21-a2-periodic}
There are five finite families of regular periodic itineraries on a finite
cover of $K$ such that the four difference vectors
\[
 d_j(R)=\bigl(\Delta\kappa_1,\Delta\kappa_2,
              \Delta r,\Delta\tau\bigr)_j,
 \qquad 1\le j\le4,
\]
have $|\det(d_1,\ldots,d_4)|\ge d_*>0$ on the corresponding chart.  The
orbits stay uniformly away from grazing and from every nonendpoint
scatterer.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a small regular rectangle in one triangular gap.  Two pairs of words
share entrance and exit connectors and differ by one clockwise circuit in the
two primitive torus directions; their differences give $(1,0,0,*)$ and
$(0,1,0,*)$.  A third pair replaces a regular three-collision neutral detour
by its regular two-collision shortcut and hence has nonzero return-count
difference while its homology is zero.  For the fourth pair, slide one impact
point on a dispersing arc and close both words with the same suffix.  The
first variation of total length is the difference of the two nonparallel
outgoing tangential momenta and is nonzero, whereas homology and return count
are fixed.  Elementary row operations therefore make the difference matrix
triangular with nonzero diagonal at the center of each construction.

The reflection equations above have invertible derivative because the length
Hessian is positive.  The implicit-function theorem continues all words in
$R$.  Incidence angle, clearance, and determinant are continuous and positive
on a neighborhood.  Compactness of $K$ supplies a finite subcover and a
positive minimum $d_*$.  Appending common connectors does not change any
difference and makes all words periodic in the chosen Markov family.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact coboundary cancellation and UNI]
\label{lem:r21-a2-uni}
On every chart there are inverse return branches $h_+,h_-$ with a common
image and a common terminal suffix such that the temporal distance
\[
 D_R(x)=\tau_R^{(n)}(h_+x)-\tau_R^{(n)}(h_-x)
\]
satisfies $|D_R'(x)|\ge c_{\rm U}>0$ on a common unstable interval.  If
\[
 e^{i(u\cdot\kappa+vr+b\tau)}
 =e^{ic}{g\over g\circ F_R}
\]
on the induced map, then $u=0$ in $\mathbb T^2$, $v=0$ in $\mathbb T$,
$b=0$, and $c=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the slid pair in the last part of the preceding proof.  Differentiating
the two finite branch compositions gives a first unequal tangential-momentum
term.  Because the suffix is literally identical, all suffix derivatives are
composed with the same terminal point and cancel except for a geometrically
contracted tail.  Lengthen the common suffix until that tail is less than half
the first term; compactness gives $c_{\rm U}$.

For the coboundary equation, sum its argument around each periodic word.  The
$g$ terms telescope exactly, including the constant phase times the period.
Subtract the base word equation from the other four.  Lemma
\ref{lem:r21-a2-periodic} forces the real lift of $(u_1,u_2,v,b)$ to vanish
modulo its lattice.  The UNI derivative then excludes $b\ne0$ without any
endpoint assumption on $g$.  The remaining base equation gives $c=0$.
\end{proof}

The same argument shows uniform positive definiteness of the four-dimensional
covariance: a zero variance direction is an $L^2$ coboundary by the spectral
Poisson equation, regularity upgrades it to a continuous coboundary on the
regular core, and Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-a2-uni} excludes it.

\subsection{A complete Fourier decomposition}

Let $\Phi_{n,R}^{\ell}(u,v,b)=
\ell_R(\mathcal L_{R,u,v,b}^nh_R)$, where $\ell_R$ is either the physical
integral or a fixed central cylinder insertion.  The following lemma is the
new high-frequency tool.  It uses a fixed number of derivatives and therefore
has no constants $c_M$ whose growth is chosen after the frequency split.

\begin{lemma}[Two-coordinate branchwise coarea bound]
\label{lem:r21-a2-coarea}
There are $B,c,C>0$ such that for $|b|>B$,
\[
 |\Phi_{n,R}^{\ell}(u,v,b)|
 \le Ce^{-cn}(1+|b|)^{-2}+Ce^{-cn}
       \mathbf1_{\{|b|\le e^{c n/4}\}} .
\]
Equivalently, on $|b|>e^{c n/4}$ the first term alone holds.  The estimate is
uniform in $(u,v)\in\mathbb T^3$, $R$, and the allowed insertion.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Call a return block regular when it contains the UNI pair of
Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-a2-uni}, has incidence angle at least $\delta$, and has
homogeneity index at most $L$.  The growth lemma and exponential return tail
show that words with fewer than two disjoint regular blocks have total
weighted amplitude at most $Ce^{-cn}$.  This is a statement about the sum of
inverse Jacobians and not about the number of words.

On a word with two such blocks, choose unstable coordinates $x_1,x_2$ in the
interiors of the two returned rectangles.  A smooth partition, equal to one
outside a collar of relative width $|b|^{-1/2}$, is chosen inside each
rectangle.  The collar contribution is put into the bad-family estimate by
the growth lemma.  On the interior the phase map
$(x_1,x_2)\mapsto(\tau^{(n)},x_2)$ has Jacobian at least $c_{\rm U}$ in the
first block, and after exchanging the blocks the analogous map has the same
bound.  Apply the operator
\[
 {1\over ibD_j}\partial_{x_j},
 \qquad D_j=\partial_{x_j}\tau^{(n)},
\]
once in each coordinate.  The cutoffs are compactly supported in the branch
interiors, so there are no homogeneity or singularity boundary terms.
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-a2-complexity} sums all differentiated inverse-Jacobian
amplitudes.  Two integrations give $C(1+|b|)^{-2}$, while ordinary
opposition cancellation on a fixed fraction of the remaining blocks gives
$e^{-cn}$.  The bad family contributes $Ce^{-cn}$; it is needed only below the
stated exponential threshold, where the medium-frequency estimate below is
stronger.  This proves the two compatible forms of the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Global Fourier-line estimate]
\label{thm:r21-a2-fourier}
There are $\delta,B,\kappa,c,C,A>0$ such that the following exhaustive bounds
hold uniformly in $R$, the real saddle, and a fixed insertion:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
|(u,v,b)|\le n^{-2/5}:&
 \Phi_n=e^{nP(i\omega)}(a_\ell+O(\rho^n)),\quad
 P(i\omega)=im\cdot\omega-\frac12\omega^T\Sigma\omega+O(|\omega|^3),\\[1mm]
n^{-2/5}<|\omega|\le\delta:&
 |\Phi_n|\le Ce^{-cn|\omega|^2},\\[1mm]
\delta<|\omega|,\ |b|\le B:&
 |\Phi_n|\le Ce^{-cn},\\[1mm]
B<|b|\le e^{\kappa\sqrt n}:&
 |\Phi_n|\le C|b|^Ae^{-cn/\log(2+|b|)},\\[1mm]
|b|>e^{\kappa\sqrt n}:&
 |\Phi_n|\le Ce^{-c\sqrt n}(1+|b|)^{-2}.
\end{array}
\]
The integral of every nonsmall range is $o(n^{-2})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first two lines follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-a2-bundle} and uniform
positive covariance.  Compactness of the third region and
Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-a2-uni} give a uniform spectral-radius gap.  For the fourth
region, subdivide unstable curves at scale $|b|^{-1}$ and return a fixed
fraction to the UNI pair every $C\log(2+|b|)$ iterates.  The near-opposition
inequality gives a fixed loss at each return; distortion contributes only the
polynomial $|b|^A$.  With $|b|\le e^{\kappa\sqrt n}$ and $\kappa$ small, its
integral is $o(n^{-2})$.

For the last range use Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-a2-coarea}.  In the overlap where
its bad-family term is present, the preceding Dolgopyat line is
$e^{-c\sqrt n}$.  Above the overlap the integrable coarea term remains.
Therefore the last integral is at most $Ce^{-c\sqrt n}$, and all five regions
cover $\mathbb T^3\times\mathbb R$ without a circular choice of derivative
order.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Absolute continuity of the roof coordinate]
\label{lem:r21-a2-density}
For every $n$ and every lattice value of $(K_n,N_n)$, the regular part of the
roof law has an $L^1$ density.  The singular branch boundaries have zero
physical measure, and the density is the coarea sum over homogeneous branch
interiors.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On every nonconstant branch, at least one free-flight endpoint coordinate has
nonzero length derivative away from a finite union of critical hypersurfaces.
Partition by which derivative is largest and apply the one-dimensional
coarea formula.  The critical hypersurfaces and singularity boundaries have
zero Liouville measure.  The weighted branch sum is integrable by
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-a2-complexity}.  Conditioning on the lattice marks merely
selects a subfamily of branches.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Raw local and conditional theorems}

Let $Y_n=(K_n,N_n,T_n)\in\mathbb Z^3\times\mathbb R$ and let
$m_{R,\theta}$ and $\Sigma_{R,\theta}$ be its mean and covariance under a real
saddle $\theta$ in a compact interior chart.

\begin{theorem}[Unsmoothed four-dimensional local limit theorem]
\label{thm:r21-a2-llt}
Uniformly for $y_n-nm_{R,\theta}=O(\sqrt n)$,
\[
 \mathbb P_{R,\theta}
 \{(K_n,N_n)=k_n,\ T_n\in dt\}
 ={\exp[-z_n^T\Sigma_{R,\theta}^{-1}z_n/(2n)]
   \over(2\pi n)^2\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{R,\theta}}}
 \bigl(a_0+o(1)\bigr)\,dt,
\]
where $z_n=y_n-nm_{R,\theta}$ and $a_0$ is the lattice-cell factor.  The same
formula holds in the numerator with any fixed regular cylinder insertion.
No smoothing of the roof variable is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use Fourier inversion on $\mathbb T^3\times\mathbb R$, justified by
Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-a2-density}.  On the small ball put
$\omega=z/\sqrt n$ and use the cubic expansion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-a2-fourier}; dominated convergence gives the
four-dimensional Gaussian and $n^{-2}$.  Every complementary integral is
$o(n^{-2})$ by the same theorem.  The insertion changes only the analytic
amplitude $a_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Local, central, and saturated conditioning]
\label{cor:r21-a2-conditioning}
Let $J_n$ be a roof interval.  If its center is in a compact interior Cram\'er
chart, then the conditional cylinder law is obtained by the ratio of the
inserted and uninserted local formula in each of the following regimes:
$|J_n|=O(n^{-1/2})$, $n^{-1/2}\ll|J_n|\ll1$, and
$|J_n|\ge J_0>0$.  In the last regime the interval is covered by finitely many
real-saddle charts and Laplace's method is used; the central formula is not
extrapolated outside its range.  The denominator is uniformly positive on
the declared compact chart.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Integrate Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-a2-llt} in the first two regimes.  For a fixed
width, exponentially tilt separately at the unique saddle associated with
each average roof value, cover the compact interval by finitely many charts,
and apply Laplace's method.  The same saddle and the same exponential factor
occur in numerator and denominator, so they cancel in the ratio.  Strict
convexity and compactness give the positive denominator bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-twenty-one A2 closure]
\label{thm:r21-a2-main}
The radius family has a Jacobian-weighted countable-branch bundle, an exact
periodic/UNI obstruction, a noncircular global Fourier majorant, an
unsmoothed four-dimensional density LLT, and the three conditioning regimes
above.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r21-a2-complexity},
\ref{thm:r21-a2-bundle}, \ref{thm:r21-a2-fourier}, and
\ref{thm:r21-a2-llt} with Lemmas~\ref{lem:r21-a2-periodic},
\ref{lem:r21-a2-uni}, and \ref{lem:r21-a2-density}.
\end{proof}
