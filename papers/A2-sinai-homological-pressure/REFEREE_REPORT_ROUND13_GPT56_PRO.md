## Executive assessment

Round 13 removes the nonintegrable high-frequency remainder from the preceding version, treats small frequencies by covariance damping rather than a fictitious fixed gap, and no longer uses impossible period-one winding collisions. These are substantive improvements.

The headline local limit theorem nevertheless has the wrong dimension. The joint variable consists of two homology coordinates, one lattice collision-count coordinate, and one continuous roof coordinate. Its Fourier inversion is four-dimensional, so the local lattice–lattice–density prefactor is of order `n^{-2}`, not `n^{-3/2}`. The displayed theorem therefore overestimates every joint local probability by a factor of order `sqrt(n)`. The saddle notation also simultaneously centers at the target and inserts a second Gaussian deviation, and the periodic obstruction argument does not establish span one in the return-count coordinate.

## Decisive objections

### 1. The LLT prefactor has the wrong power of `n`

The twisted observable is

\[
 (\kappa_1,\kappa_2,r,\tau)
 \in \mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R.
\]

Thus Fourier inversion uses three compact lattice angles `(u_1,u_2,v)` and one real roof frequency `b`. The small-frequency change of variables is four-dimensional. A nondegenerate Gaussian integral therefore contributes

\[
 (2\pi n)^{-4/2}=(2\pi n)^{-2}.
\]

The manuscript instead states

\[
 {1\over(2\pi n)^{3/2}\sqrt{\det\Sigma_R}}.
\]

That is the prefactor for only three scalar coordinates. It was appropriate to the earlier displacement-plus-roof problem, but not after adding the independent lattice return-count coordinate.

This is not a cosmetic normalization. Summing over one count value and two displacement values while retaining a roof density gives probabilities smaller by a factor `n^{-1/2}` than the theorem claims. Every window-conditioning and coefficient ratio using this joint theorem is consequently misnormalized.

### 2. The saddle and Gaussian-deviation conventions are inconsistent

The manuscript says that the real saddle is chosen so that its mean is exactly

\[
 (k_n/n,\ell_n/n,t_n/n).
\]

It then defines

\[
 z_n=n^{-1/2}
 (k_n-na_R,\ell_n-n\bar r_R,t_n-n\bar\tau_R).
\]

Under the announced saddle choice, this vector is zero, apart from unavoidable lattice rounding. Yet the density formula contains both the large-deviation factor

\[
 e^{-nI_R(k_n/n,\ell_n/n,t/n)}
\]

and a nontrivial Gaussian factor in `z_n`. Moreover the rate is evaluated at the running density variable `t`, whereas `z_n` uses the fixed center `t_n`.

A correct saddlepoint formula must choose one convention: either tilt to the exact target and retain only the local rounding/deviation under that tilted law, or fix a base saddle and put the full deviation into the Gaussian. The present formula mixes the two.

### 3. The four stated Fourier ranges do not cover all joint frequencies as written

Parts (iii) and (iv) are indexed only by the roof frequency `|b|`. Points with

\[
 |b|\le b_0,
 \qquad |(u,v,b)|>b_0
\]

are outside parts (i)–(ii) and are not included in the statements of parts (iii)–(iv). The proof mentions a compact annulus, but the theorem does not state its operator bound, its insertion stability, or its material-derivative version.

For a joint lattice–continuous LLT, every point of

\[
 \mathbb T^2\times\mathbb T\times\mathbb R
\]

must be assigned to a proved range. An argument alluded to only in prose cannot support the uniform Fourier inversion theorem.

### 4. “Two different return counts” does not prove span one

After forcing the roof coefficient to vanish, the proof says that two different return counts eliminate the lattice character `v`. If the available periodic return counts differ only by multiples of an integer `d>1`, the nontrivial character

\[
 v={2\pi\over d}
\]

survives. One must prove that the subgroup generated by all periodic count differences is exactly `Z`, not merely that two counts are unequal.

The same issue must be checked jointly with homology and with the chosen inducing period. No explicit span-one certificate or symbolic gcd argument is supplied.

### 5. The parent-bundle and returned-UNI theorems remain proof outlines

A uniform anisotropic bundle for a moving Sinai family requires explicit control of singularity preimages, homogeneity traces, holonomy distortion, compact embeddings, and source/material derivatives on a common scale. The proof compresses this into a paragraph invoking finite horizon and Arzelà–Ascoli.

Likewise, the returned-branch UNI proof identifies two mirror itineraries but gives no branch domains, collision coordinates, derivative calculation, grazing clearance, or distortion constants. The existence of a first unequal free leg does not by itself prove a uniform temporal-distance derivative after all endpoint motions and common tails are included.

These are the principal model-specific theorems, not routine lemmas that may be left at schematic level.

### 6. Downstream conditioning results therefore have no valid input

A3 and A4 use A2 for renewal coefficients, roof stopping, resolvent continuation, and high-frequency invertibility. The wrong local prefactor and incomplete arithmetic/functional-analytic proof propagate directly into those papers.

## Genuine improvements recognized

The following changes should be retained:

- physical parent branches replace symbolic Heaviside births;
- small frequencies are handled by covariance damping;
- moderate and very-high roof frequencies now have integrable-looking bounds;
- period-one winding orbits have been removed; and
- roof windows are integrated through an exact conditional Gaussian mass.

These repairs do not cure the four-dimensional normalization error or supply the missing model-specific proofs.

## Required reconstruction

A serious revision must first state and prove one complete theorem on a precisely constructed common anisotropic scale. The joint LLT should then be rewritten with a four-dimensional `n^{-2}` prefactor, one consistent saddle convention, a genuinely exhaustive frequency partition, and an explicit span-one periodic obstruction theorem.

## Recommendation

**Reject.** The paper contains the seeds of a substantial uniform billiard theorem, but its headline LLT is quantitatively false and its two load-bearing geometric/spectral inputs remain outlines rather than proofs.
