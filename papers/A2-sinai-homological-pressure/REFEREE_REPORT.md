# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** A2 — Sinai Homological Pressure  
**Recommendation:** **Reject**  
**Standard applied:** Annals / Acta / Inventiones / JAMS  
**Review target:** pinned eleven-paper clean-main tree, SHA-256 `566335121763abaa7ed8ed2280a3b4345877777e8bedbd80f4eef0a6b48745e2`.

## Overall assessment

This manuscript aims at an attractive package: a uniform vector displacement/roof spectral theory for a radius family of finite-horizon periodic Lorentz gases, a projective empirical-process LDP, an analytic physical pressure root, and a quantitative two-sided Gibbs-conditioning theorem. If proved, parts of this program could be significant.

They are not proved here. The load-bearing assertions are compressed into short “packets” whose hypotheses are precisely the hard billiard analysis. Several subsequent deductions also misuse local large-deviation information as though it were a full projective LDP, and the quantitative current/clock conditioning estimate requires a joint local-limit theorem not supplied by a Laplace principle.

## Major objections

### 1. The uniform homological Lorentz packet is an unproved main theorem

The proof says, in effect, that the untwisted operator has a uniform spectral gap, multiplication by the weight is bounded, and analytic perturbation applies. This does not establish the theorem.

For a parameter-dependent billiard family one must verify, uniformly in the radius:

- common anisotropic strong/weak spaces or canonical identifications between changing phase/singularity structures;
- uniform one-step expansion, complexity, distortion, grazing control, and Lasota–Yorke estimates;
- bounded multiplication by the discontinuous displacement and roof weights on the chosen graded spaces;
- analyticity for the full vector/roof twist, not merely continuity of untwisted spectral projectors;
- absence of peripheral lattice modes and the exact span/aperiodicity needed for the \(\mathbb Z^2\) displacement;
- compatibility of left/right eigenvectors and normalized Doob data.

Demers–Zhang provides a framework conditional on substantial uniform hypotheses; citing that framework is not verification of those hypotheses for this family and these weights. The manuscript itself calls this its “principal imported analytic packet.” A principal theorem may not be imported by a seven-line proof.

### 2. The claimed projective LDP does not follow from the stated finite-dimensional results

For each finite tuple the manuscript proves, at most, local Gärtner–Ellis bounds for sets whose closures lie inside the gradient image of a small simple-eigenvalue domain. Dawson–Gärtner requires compatible full LDPs for the finite projections, with good rate functions. Local exposed-domain bounds are not enough.

Compactness of the product of bounded coordinate ranges does not supply the missing lower bounds or turn local rates into globally compatible good rates. Consequently the displayed supremum over cylinder potentials is a convex functional, but it has not been shown to be the rate function of an empirical-process LDP—not even a standard projective-limit LDP.

The later uniqueness claim also assumes strict convexity modulo coboundaries for arbitrary higher-block potentials. That is neither stated with a precise quotient nor proved.

### 3. The variational continuation is not justified by the local pressure branch

The paper uses a lower-semicontinuous Legendre extension generated by a local pressure domain and then writes a global variational root over invariant measures. Equality between this continuation and the analytic pressure root requires a valid duality theorem on the chosen function/measure pair. The preceding local projective construction does not provide it.

A “candidate phase” language is acceptable outside the proved domain, but the paper still uses the continuation as if its normalization and denominator were canonically fixed by a microscopic rate. That point is unresolved.

### 4. Positive definiteness and local invertibility depend on missing non-coboundary results

The formal differentiation of the root equation is standard and the Schur-complement covariance formula is plausible. Positive definiteness of
\[
\kappa-v\tau
\]
in every nonzero homology direction, uniformly in geometry and under nearby tilts, is a separate theorem. It requires excluding vector/clock coboundaries and proving full-rank non-arithmetic behavior. Referring back to the unproved “local-limit packet” is not a proof.

### 5. The two-sided current/clock conditioning theorem is unsupported

The advertised estimate is far stronger than an LDP. Conditioning on an exact \(\mathbb Z^2\) displacement and a roof interval of width \(b_n=o(n)\) requires a ratio theorem based on a joint lattice/non-lattice local limit or Edgeworth expansion, with uniform control of Fourier modes, eigenprojectors, smoothing, and central cylinder insertions.

A Laplace principle controls exponential rates; it does not give local probabilities or ratios at submacroscopic resolution. The theorem allows \(b_n\to\infty\) arbitrarily slowly, including \(b_n\ll\sqrt n\), where a temporal local-limit input is unavoidable. The statement that no temporal local-limit assumption is needed is not supported.

The specific error
\[
O((n-m)^{-1/2}+b_n/n+b_n^{-1}+\text{mismatch})
\]
is simply announced. No Fourier decomposition, minor-arc estimate, smoothing norm estimate, or denominator lower bound is provided.

### 6. Higher-block twists are not generated by the two-observable packet

The empirical-process section invokes “corresponding higher-block twisted operators” for arbitrary dynamically Hölder cylinder functions. The earlier theorem covers only the displacement/roof weight. One must construct the block extensions, prove the required multiplier bounds across billiard singularities, and establish uniform spectral simplicity on each domain. This is another hidden analytic input.

## What would be required for a new submission

The manuscript must choose one genuinely new theorem and prove it completely. The natural candidate is the uniform vector displacement/roof spectral and local-limit theorem. Only after that should the authors state a quantitatively correct conditioning result. The projective empirical-process material should be removed unless full finite-projection LDPs and a valid Dawson–Gärtner argument are supplied.

## Editorial recommendation

**Reject.** The principal claims are either imported without verification or do not follow from the cited large-deviation tools.
