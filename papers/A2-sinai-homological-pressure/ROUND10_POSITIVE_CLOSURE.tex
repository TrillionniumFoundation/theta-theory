\section{Round-ten reconstruction: a verified billiard packet and a four-range Fourier theorem}
\label{sec:r10-a2}

We use the triangular lattice with basis
\(e_1=(1,0)\), \(e_2=(1/2,\sqrt3/2)\), one circular scatterer of radius
\(R\), and
\[
 K=[9/20,47/100].
\]
The finite-horizon collision map, its homological displacement
\(\kappa_R\in\mathbb Z^2\), and the roof \(\tau_R\) are those of the paper.
Every numerical inequality below is recorded in
\texttt{A2\_ROUND10\_CERTIFICATE.json} and checked by
\texttt{tools/verify\_a2\_round10\_certificate.py}.

\subsection{A birth bundle with no fictitious physical inverse}

At a simple branch birth write \(r=R-R_0\).  On the birth side the two branch
traces have the expansion
\[
 T_{r,\pm}=T_0\pm r^{1/2}T_1+rT_2+O(r^{3/2}).
\]
Introduce coefficient coordinates
\[
 U^{\rm ev}=\tfrac12(T_{r,+}+T_{r,-}),
 \qquad
 U^{\rm od}=\tfrac1{2r^{1/2}}(T_{r,+}-T_{r,-})
 \quad(r>0).
\]
For \(r\le0\), the coefficients are retained as virtual normal jets but their
newborn physical realization is zero.  The bundle norm is a coefficient norm,
not the norm of an inverse of the physical realization.

\begin{lemma}[Renormalized one-sided birth bundle]
\label{lem:r10-a2-birth}
There is a fixed coefficient space \(\mathbb B^{j}_{\rm bl}\) and bounded
realization maps \(E_R:\mathbb B^{j}_{\rm bl}\to\mathscr D'\) such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item on the birth side the two realized traces are
      \(U^{\rm ev}\pm r^{1/2}U^{\rm od}\);
\item on the no-branch side the newborn realization is zero;
\item in \(s=\operatorname{sgn}(r)|r|^{1/2}\), the realization is \(C^j\)
      from each strong coefficient level to the next weak physical level; and
\item the transfer operator preserves the closed graph
      \(\{(U,E_RU)\}\) and satisfies a uniform graph Lasota--Yorke estimate.
\end{enumerate}
No uniformly bounded inverse from a vanishing physical current to an
order-one coefficient is asserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The parameterized Morse lemma puts the tangency equation into fold form
\(\zeta^2=s^2\).  Taylor expansion in \(s\), not in \(r=s^2\), gives the even
and odd jets with uniform remainder.  Stable-curve trace estimates place the
jet coefficients one strong order below the bulk and the remainder in the
weak level.  The branch transfer formula is smooth in the blown-up variables;
on bulk plus jets it is a finite lower-triangular system.  Uniform finite
horizon, curvature, homogeneity complexity and one-step expansion give the
graph Lasota--Yorke inequality, while stable-curve and strip truncation give
compactness.  The virtual no-branch coefficient is never physically inverted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform vector--roof spectral graph]
\label{thm:r10-a2-spectral}
For a fixed finite jet order, the operators
\[
 \mathcal L_{R,z,b}f
 =\mathcal L_R\bigl(e^{z\cdot\kappa_R+ib\tau_R}f\bigr)
\]
act on the graph bundle of Lemma~\ref{lem:r10-a2-birth}.  On one common
complex neighborhood of \((z,b)=(0,0)\) they have a simple analytic leading
eigenvalue and a uniform spectral remainder.  Material derivatives in \(R\)
lose one strong level per derivative.  Fixed central cylinder insertions and
their material derivatives act on the same scale.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The vector factor is branchwise constant and the roof factor has the stable
oscillation and unstable holonomy bounds of the Jacobian.  In fold coordinates
the two newborn multipliers form an even/odd Taylor system and preserve the
physical graph.  The graph Lasota--Yorke estimate, compact strong-to-weak
embedding and mixing yield a uniform Riesz contour.  Analytic perturbation and
the material resolvent identity give the derivatives.  A central cylinder is
cut by its finite singularity preimages, so all of its one-sided traces are
among the declared jet coordinates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An explicit arithmetic packet}

There are two primitive winding one-collision orbits with homologies
\(e_1,e_2\).  Let \(w_0\) be the two-collision nearest-neighbour backtracking
orbit.  Let \(w_3\) circulate around one triangular gap and \(w_4\) circulate
around the rhombus with centers \(0,e_1,e_1+e_2,e_2\).  Their roof sums and
collision counts are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 w&\tau_R(w)&n(w)\\ \hline
 w_0&2(1-2R)&2\\
 w_3&3(1-\sqrt3R)&3\\
 w_4&4\sqrt{1-(1+\sqrt3)R+2R^2}&4.
 \end{array}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Verified four-coordinate periodic certificate]
\label{lem:r10-a2-arithmetic}
The five orbits \(w_0,\ldots,w_4\) are regular and unobstructed for every
\(R\in K\).  If
\(c_R(w)=(\kappa_R^{(1)},\kappa_R^{(2)},\tau_R,n)(w)\), then
\[
 \left|\det(c_R(w_j)-c_R(w_0))_{j=1}^4\right|
 =|2\tau_R(w_3)-\tau_R(w_4)-\tau_R(w_0)|\ge0.55.
\]
Consequently the joint lattice/roof cocycle has no nontrivial compact-frequency
character.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the triangular orbit the contact points lie on the inward centroid
bisectors and every leg has length \(1-\sqrt3R\).  For the rhombus the four
inward angle-bisector contacts give the displayed common leg length.  Direct
substitution verifies equal incident and reflected angles.  Distance-to-segment
minimization against the finite neighboring lattice list gives clearances at
least \(0.16\) and \(0.058\), respectively.  The first two rows of the
four-by-four difference matrix form the homology identity.  The remaining
scalar determinant is evaluated on subintervals of width \(10^{-4}\), with a
mean-value derivative enclosure; the certified absolute lower bound is
\(0.55\).  A compact character coboundary vanishes on every periodic sum and
is therefore zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A geometric UNI pair independent of arithmetic}

The clockwise and counterclockwise three-collision branches around the same
triangular gap return to one common collision rectangle.

\begin{theorem}[Verified returned-branch UNI]
\label{thm:r10-a2-uni}
There are a fixed unstable interval \(J\), a return time \(N\), and inverse
branches \(h_R^\pm:J\to J\), continuous in \(R\), such that
\[
 \inf_{R\in K}\inf_{x\in J}
 \left|\partial_x\bigl(\tau_N(h_R^+x)-\tau_N(h_R^-x)\bigr)\right|
 \ge 3/4.
\]
The branch words, interval endpoints, cone bounds and derivative intervals are
part of the certificate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The billiard generating function is the flight length, and its derivative in
the initial arclength is the tangential momentum.  At the symmetric triangular
orbit the clockwise and counterclockwise branches have momenta \(\pm1/2\), so
the temporal-distance derivative is one.  The explicit flight Hessians on
\(K\) bound its variation by \(1/8\) on the certified interval.  The common
return suffix changes it by at most \(1/8\), by inverse-branch cone
contraction.  The clearance bounds keep both branches away from grazing and
all other singularities.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Moderate and very-high frequency estimates}

\begin{theorem}[Four-range twisted estimate]
\label{thm:r10-a2-four-range}
For every integer \(M\ge6\) there are \(C,c,A,\delta>0\), uniform in
\(R\in K\), such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for \(|(u,b)|\le\delta\), the simple eigenvalue has the uniform cubic
      Gaussian expansion;
\item on compact nonzero frequencies the spectral radius is at most \(1-c\);
\item for \(2\le |b|\le e^{\delta n}\),
\[
 \|\mathcal L_{R,iu,ib}^n\|_{\rm strong\to weak}
 \le C|b|^Ae^{-cn/\log(2+|b|)};
\]
\item for \(|b|>e^{\delta n}\), after \(M\) roof integrations by parts,
\[
 \|\mathcal L_{R,iu,ib}^n\|_{\rm insertion}
 \le C_Me^{C_Mn}|b|^{-M}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
The last estimate also holds with a fixed central cylinder insertion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first two assertions follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r10-a2-spectral} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:r10-a2-arithmetic}.  The UNI pair, standard-family return and
bounded distortion yield Dolgopyat cancellation in the moderate range.  In
the very-high range decompose into homogeneous inverse branches and integrate
the oscillatory integral \(M\) times in the UNI coordinate.  Derivatives of
amplitudes and inverse roof derivatives are bounded by the finite jet scale;
the number of branches grows at most exponentially in \(n\).  Since
\(|b|>e^{\delta n}\), choosing \(M>C_M/\delta+3\) makes the tail integrable.
Thus the moderate estimate is never extrapolated to infinite frequency.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The corrected vector--roof local limit theorem}

Let \(K_n\in\mathbb Z^2\), \(T_n\in\mathbb R\), let \(\Sigma_R\) be their
joint covariance under a regular real tilt, \(\Sigma_{K,R}\) its lattice
block, and \(\sigma_{c,R}^2\) the conditional roof variance.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform density LLT and separated window regimes]
\label{thm:r10-a2-llt}
Uniformly for \(|k_n-n\bar\kappa_R|=O(\sqrt n)\):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item on every fixed compact \(u\)-set,
\[
 p_{n,R}(k_n,n\bar\tau_R+\sqrt n\,u)
 =\frac{e^{-\delta k_n^T\Sigma_{K,R}^{-1}\delta k_n/(2n)}}
 {(2\pi n)^{3/2}\sqrt{\det\Sigma_R}}
 e^{-u_c^2/(2\sigma_{c,R}^2)}(1+O(n^{-\eta}));
\]
\item if \(\Delta_n=o(\sqrt n)\), a roof-sum interval of half-width
      \(\Delta_n\) has the two-dimensional lattice prefactor \(n^{-1}\)
      multiplied by \(2\Delta_n/(\sqrt{2\pi n}\sigma_{c,R})\);
\item if \(\Delta_n/\sqrt n\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\), the lattice prefactor
      is multiplied by the corresponding conditional Gaussian interval mass;
\item if \(\Delta_n/\sqrt n\to\infty\) and \(\Delta_n=o(n)\), that mass tends
      to one;
\item a fixed interval in the roof average is governed by a saddle-varying
      Bahadur--Rao integral and is not obtained by integrating a central
      Gaussian outside its uniform range.
\end{enumerate}
All statements remain valid with a fixed central cylinder insertion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fourier inversion is split into the four ranges of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r10-a2-four-range}.  The small range gives the joint Gaussian
density; the compact and moderate ranges are negligible; the very-high range
is absolutely integrable after \(M\) integrations by parts.  Integrating over
subcentral roof intervals gives (b)--(d), with the \(n^{-1}\) lattice
coefficient separated from the conditional roof probability.  For a fixed
average interval, cover the range by real source charts, tilt at the local
saddle, and apply Laplace's method to the local density theorem.  The insertion
statement follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:r10-a2-spectral}.
\end{proof}
