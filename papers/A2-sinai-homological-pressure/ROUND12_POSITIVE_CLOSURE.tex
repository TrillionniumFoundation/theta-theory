\section{Round-twelve positive closure: parent-fold transfer bundles and complete Fourier coverage}
\label{sec:r12-a2}

The radius interval is $\mathcal I=[9/20,47/100]$.  A change in a symbolic
cut is treated as a change of coordinates inside one physical inverse branch,
not as the creation of physical mass.  The arithmetic and high-frequency
arguments are rebuilt without period-one winding orbits and without an
uncovered Fourier band.

\subsection{The parent-fold material graph}

Near a cut birth let $r=R-R_0$ and use the signed blow-up coordinate
$s$ defined by $r=s|s|$.  The physical inverse branch before cutting is a
single parent operator $\mathcal K_R$.  On the split side it is represented by
$\mathcal K_{R,+}+\mathcal K_{R,-}$; on the unsplit side it remains
$\mathcal K_R$.

\begin{lemma}[Fold push-forward identity]
\label{lem:r12-a2-fold}
There are material charts in which
\[
 \mathcal K_{R,+}+\mathcal K_{R,-}=\mathcal K_R
\]
as distributions on the common physical test space, and
$R\mapsto\mathcal K_R$ is $C^r$ across $R_0$.  The odd split coordinate is
$s\mathcal J_s$ with $\mathcal J_s$ uniformly bounded and is annihilated by
physical realization at $s=0$.  Thus every physical derivative is taken from
the parent operator; no Heaviside turn-on term is differentiated.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Straighten the moving singular curve and write the parent inverse integral as
an integral over a fixed interval.  On the split side partition that interval
at the two roots of the fold equation and change variables separately on the
two pieces.  Adding the two integrals gives the original fixed-domain
integral exactly.  Differentiation in $R$ is therefore ordinary
differentiation of the parent integrand and Jacobian.  Subtracting the two
piece integrals gives an odd function of $s$; division by $s$ has a bounded
Taylor integral representation.  This proves all assertions in the common
strong/weak distribution scale.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform moving-billiard bundle]
\label{thm:r12-a2-bundle}
The bulk space together with the parent-fold trace graph forms a finite
atlas of Banach pairs $\mathbb B_R\hookrightarrow\mathbb B_{R,w}$ on
$\mathcal I$.  The vector--roof operators
\[
 \mathcal L_{R,z,\zeta}h
 =\mathcal L_R(e^{z\cdot\kappa_R-\zeta\tau_R}h)
\]
have a common Lasota--Yorke inequality, compact strong-to-weak injection, and
a simple analytic eigenvalue on one complex neighborhood.  Each material
$R$-derivative loses one declared strong level; physical even combinations
are $C^r$ in $R$ through every cut birth.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finite horizon gives common flight, curvature, cone, distortion and
one-step-expansion constants.  Homogeneity strips make the roof multiplier
summable with the same constants.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r12-a2-fold} identifies all
birth charts with the unsplit physical branch, so no trace-only diagonal block
or peripheral mode is added.  The growth lemma yields the common
Lasota--Yorke estimate and compactness.  A common resolvent contour and the
material graph estimate give analytic source perturbation and the stated
regularity loss.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Homology, collision count, and temporal nonintegrability}

The normal two-cycle between neighboring disks has collision count two and
roof $2(1-2R)$.  The symmetric triangular orbit facing the center of an
equilateral lattice triangle has collision count three and roof
$3(1-\sqrt3R)$; throughout $\mathcal I$ it is regular and disjoint from every
other disk.  These two zero-homology orbits have distinct collision counts.

\begin{lemma}[Returned-branch UNI]
\label{lem:r12-a2-uni}
There are a common unstable interval $J$, two returned inverse branches
$h_R^\pm:J\to J$, and $c_0>0$ such that their collision counts and homologies
coincide while
\[
 \inf_{R\in\mathcal I}\inf_{x\in J}
 \left|\partial_x(S\tau_R\circ h_R^+ -S\tau_R\circ h_R^-)(x)\right|
 \ge c_0.
\]
The branches, their common connector, and all distortion constants vary
$C^2$ in $R$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the two mirror itineraries through the triangular gap, one visiting the
right inward arc before the left and the other in the opposite order.  The
strict blocking margin
$2R-\sqrt3/2\ge9/10-\sqrt3/2$ keeps both itineraries away from grazing.  At
the symmetry point the first unequal free legs have endpoint-momentum
derivatives differing by $2\sin\beta_R$, where elementary triangle geometry
gives
$\sin\beta_R\ge(2R-\sqrt3/2)/(2+2\tau_+)$.  Append the same number of common
return iterates to both branches.  Hyperbolicity bounds the derivative of the
common suffix by a geometric series; choose the number of iterates so this is
at most half the preceding lower bound.  Continuity gives one interval $J$
and one $c_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joint periodic obstruction theorem]
\label{thm:r12-a2-arithmetic}
If
$u\cdot\kappa_R+v r_R+b\tau_R$ is a measurable coboundary modulo $2\pi$,
then $(u,v,b)$ belongs to the trivial lattice character.  Uniformly in $R$,
the four coordinates $(\kappa_1,\kappa_2,r,\tau)$ have full periodic rank.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Topological mixing and the finite connector family give regular periodic
orbits with homologies $e_1$ and $e_2$; the closing construction stays in a
fixed homogeneous core, so it is uniform in $R$.  Close the two UNI branches
with the same connector.  Their homology and collision count agree, whereas
their roofs differ by Lemma~\ref{lem:r12-a2-uni}; subtracting the periodic
identities forces $b=0$.  The normal two-cycle and triangular three-cycle then
force the collision-count character to be trivial.  Finally the two winding
orbits force the two homology characters to be integral multiples of
$2\pi$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A frequency partition with no gap}

Set $L_n=\exp(\kappa\sqrt n)$, where
$0<\kappa<\sqrt{c/(4A)}$ for the constants in the Dolgopyat estimate.  Fourier
space is split into
\[
 |b|\le n^{-2/5},\quad
 n^{-2/5}<|b|\le b_0,\quad
 b_0<|b|\le L_n,\quad
 |b|>L_n.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Multi-block very-high-frequency estimate]
\label{lem:r12-a2-multiblock}
There are $\eta,c,C>0$ such that, for $|b|>L_n$,
\[
 \|\mathcal L_{R,iu,ib}^{n}\|_{\mathbb B_R\to\mathbb B_{R,w}}
 \le Ce^{-cn}+C\left({C\over |b|}\right)^{\eta n}.
\]
The estimate holds with every fixed central-cylinder insertion and uniformly
in $R$ and $u\in[-\pi,\pi]^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The growth lemma returns a fixed fraction of every standard family to the UNI
rectangle in a bounded number of iterates.  Except for a set of weight
$Ce^{-cn}$, an $n$-itinerary contains at least $\eta n$ disjoint returned UNI
blocks.  On each block use the branch coordinate from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r12-a2-uni} and integrate once by parts in the phase
$b(S\tau^+-S\tau^-)$.  Bounded distortion pays a uniform factor $C/|b|$.
Because the blocks are disjoint, the factors multiply.  Boundary terms cancel
between the paired branches; homogeneity endpoints are assigned to the
exponentially small bad family.  A fixed insertion removes only finitely many
blocks.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete Fourier estimate]
\label{thm:r12-a2-fourier}
The four ranges above satisfy, respectively:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the analytic Gaussian expansion with cubic remainder;
\item a uniform compact-frequency spectral gap;
\item
$C|b|^A\exp\{-cn/\log(2+|b|)\}\le Ce^{-c'\sqrt n}$;
\item the estimate of Lemma~\ref{lem:r12-a2-multiblock}.
\end{enumerate}
All nonsmall ranges are integrable $o(n^{-3/2})$ errors, with all required
source and insertion derivatives.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first range is analytic perturbation.  The second follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r12-a2-arithmetic} and compactness.  In the third range,
$\log|b|\le\kappa\sqrt n$ and the choice of $\kappa$ makes the negative
Dolgopyat exponent dominate $A\log|b|$.  The fourth range is the preceding
lemma.  Integrating the resulting exponential and power tails proves the last
claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Density and window local limits}

Let $(K_n,T_n)=(S_n\kappa_R,S_n\tau_R)$ and let $\Sigma_R$ be their joint
covariance.  Write $\sigma_c^2$ for the conditional roof variance given the
homology coordinate.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform vector--roof density LLT]
\label{thm:r12-a2-density}
Uniformly for $k-na=O(\sqrt n)$ and $t-n\bar\tau=O(\sqrt n)$,
\[
 \mathbb P_R(K_n=k,T_n\in dt)
 ={e^{-\frac12 z^T\Sigma_R^{-1}z}\over
   (2\pi n)^{3/2}\sqrt{\det\Sigma_R}}\,(1+O(n^{-\eta}))\,dt,
\]
with the corresponding central-cylinder insertion.  For a roof-sum interval
$[t_n-b_n,t_n+b_n]$ the Gaussian factor is the exact conditional interval
mass
\[
 \Phi\!\left({r_n+b_n\over\sqrt n\sigma_c}\right)
 -\Phi\!\left({r_n-b_n\over\sqrt n\sigma_c}\right).
\]
The formula is uniform in the local, central, and saturated
$b_n/\sqrt n$ regimes; fixed average-width windows are treated by a varying
real saddle and not by this central formula.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fourier inversion uses Theorem~\ref{thm:r12-a2-fourier}.  The small range gives
the joint Gaussian density, while the other three ranges are integrable
relative errors.  Integrating the density over the interval gives the exact
conditional Gaussian mass and hence all three central regimes.  When
$b_n$ is order $n$, cover the interval by compact real-saddle charts and use
Laplace's method with the analytic pressure.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-twelve A2 closure]
\label{thm:r12-a2-main}
The compact finite-horizon Lorentz family has a uniform parent-fold transfer
bundle, a complete homology--count--roof obstruction theorem, returned-branch
UNI, a gap-free Fourier decomposition, and the vector--roof density and window
local limits of Theorem~\ref{thm:r12-a2-density}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r12-a2-bundle},
\ref{thm:r12-a2-arithmetic}, \ref{thm:r12-a2-fourier}, and
\ref{thm:r12-a2-density}.
\end{proof}
