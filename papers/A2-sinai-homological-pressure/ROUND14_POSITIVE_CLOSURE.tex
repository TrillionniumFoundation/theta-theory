
\section{Round-fourteen positive closure: a four-coordinate vector--return--roof theorem}
\label{sec:r14-a2}

The local theorem concerns
\[
 Y_n=(K_n,N_n,T_n)
 \in\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R ,
\]
so its Gaussian dimension is four.  All Fourier estimates below are written
on \(\mathbb T^2\times\mathbb T\times\mathbb R\), and the local prefactor is
\(n^{-2}\).

\subsection{Parent-branch regularity at a fold}

Let \(R_0\) be a parameter at which two homogeneous inverse branches are born.
On the birth side use \(s=\sqrt{R-R_0}\ge0\).  The two children have
\[
 h_{\pm,s}=h_0\pm s h_1+s^2h_{2,\pm}(s).
\]
The physical operator is not continued by a Heaviside term.  Instead it is
the pushforward of one parent branch before the fold is resolved.

\begin{lemma}[Parent-fold identity]
\label{lem:r14-a2-fold}
Let \(P_R\) be the parent collision strip and let
\(\pi_R:P_R\to M_R\) be its physical projection.  For every physical test
distribution \(f\),
\[
 \mathcal L_R^{\rm fold}f
 =(\pi_R)_*\bigl(J_R f\circ\pi_R\bigr),
\]
where \(J_R\,d\mathrm{vol}_{P_R}\) is a \(C^q\) density in \(R\).
On the birth side, resolving the parent into its two children gives
\[
 (\pi_R)_*(J_Rf)
 =\mathcal L_{+,s}f+\mathcal L_{-,s}f .
\]
Consequently the physical sum is \(C^q\) in \(R\), even though the individual
children have odd powers of \(s\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose fold coordinates \((y,z)\) on the parent strip in which
\(\pi_R(y,z)=(y,z^2-(R-R_0))\).  The pushforward pairing is
\[
 \langle(\pi_R)_*(J_Rf),\varphi\rangle
 =\int J_R(y,z)f(y,z)\varphi(y,z^2-(R-R_0))\,dy\,dz .
\]
The domain and integrand are \(C^q\) in \(R\); differentiation under the
integral is therefore legitimate in the parent anisotropic norm.  On the
birth side the changes of variable \(z=\pm\sqrt{\zeta+R-R_0}\) split the same
integral into the two child branches.  Odd terms cancel because they are the
two sheets of one pushforward, not because a nonexistent branch is continued
through the no-branch side.
\end{proof}

Using this identity at every moving tangency, the usual stable-curve bulk
space is augmented by parent-fold trace currents.  The Lasota--Yorke
constants are uniform because the finite-horizon, curvature, homogeneity, and
one-step expansion constants are uniform on the compact radius interval.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform parent-bundle spectral theorem]
\label{thm:r14-a2-bundle}
There is a finite parent-fold Banach bundle
\(\mathbb B_R^{m+1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb B_R^m\) and constants
\(C,\rho<1\) such that the joint twist
\[
 \mathcal L_{R,\xi,\zeta,s}f
 =
 \mathcal L_R
 \left(e^{\langle\xi,\kappa_R\rangle+\zeta r_R-s\tau_R}f\right)
\]
is analytic in \((\xi,\zeta,s)\), has a simple leading eigenvalue on a common
complex neighborhood, and satisfies
\[
 \|\mathcal N_{R,\xi,\zeta,s}^n\|_{\mathbb B_R^{m+1}\to\mathbb B_R^m}
 \le C\rho^n .
\]
Material \(R\)-derivatives of order \(j\le m\) are bounded
\(\mathbb B_R^{m+j}\to\mathbb B_R^m\).  Cylinder insertions of fixed depth
obey the same estimates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Cut stable test curves by the homogeneous singularity partition, but retain
each fold pair as one parent distribution until after pairing.  The growth
lemma gives
\[
 \|\mathcal L_R^nf\|_{m}
 \le C\theta^n\|f\|_{m}+C\|f\|_{m-1}
\]
with \(\theta<1\) uniform in \(R\).  Parent-fold currents satisfy the same
bound because Lemma~\ref{lem:r14-a2-fold} removes the square-root loss.
Compact embedding of the strong unit ball into the weak space gives
quasicompactness.  Mixing makes the eigenvalue one simple.  The displacement
and return marks are branchwise bounded and the roof has the homogeneous
derivative weights, so multiplication by the joint exponential is analytic.
A common Riesz contour then gives the decomposition.  Differentiating the
parent pairing consumes one strong level per material derivative, which
proves the scale statement and the insertion estimate.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Arithmetic rank and temporal nonintegrability}

Let \(\mathcal G_R\) be the finite directed graph of a common Markov family.
A regular loop \(w\) has lattice mark
\((\kappa(w),r(w))\in\mathbb Z^3\).

\begin{lemma}[Full lattice span]
\label{lem:r14-a2-span}
For every \(R\) in the compact radius interval there is one chart of a finite
cover and six regular loops
\(w_j^\pm\) in that chart such that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\kappa,r)(w_1^+)-(\kappa,r)(w_1^-)&=(1,0,0),\\
 (\kappa,r)(w_2^+)-(\kappa,r)(w_2^-)&=(0,1,0),\\
 (\kappa,r)(w_3^+)-(\kappa,r)(w_3^-)&=(0,0,1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The loops stay a uniform distance from grazing and singularities.
Consequently the subgroup generated by periodic lattice marks is all
\(\mathbb Z^3\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take a small Markov rectangle in the central triangular gap.  Topological
mixing supplies two regular connector words between any two rectangle
components.  Insert, between the same entrance and exit connectors, first a
clockwise and then a counterclockwise circuit around each of the two primitive
lattice directions; their collision counts are equal and their homology
difference is \(e_1\) or \(e_2\).  For the third pair insert into one word the
regular neutral triangular detour visiting the three neighboring scatterers
and into the other the two-collision shortcut with the same endpoints.  A
one-step extension of the shortcut makes the mark difference exactly one.

Existence of each reflected segment is obtained by minimizing the strictly
convex total length on the product of the selected inward-facing boundary
arcs.  The minimizing point is interior because every endpoint normal makes a
strict angle with both adjacent center vectors.  The Hessian is positive by
strict dispersing curvature.  The implicit-function theorem continues the
six loops in \(R\).  Their clearance and incidence angles are positive on
each local chart; a finite subcover gives the uniform bound.  Subtracting
marks gives the three standard basis vectors.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Returned temporal UNI]
\label{lem:r14-a2-uni}
There are a Markov rectangle \(Y_R\), two inverse return branches
\(h_R^\pm:Y_R\to Y_R\), an unstable coordinate \(x\), and \(c_0>0\) such that
the temporal distance
\[
 D_R(x)=
 \sum_{j\ge0}
 \bigl(\tau_R(F_R^jh_R^+x)-\tau_R(F_R^jh_R^-x)\bigr)
\]
converges in \(C^2\) and satisfies
\[
 \inf_R\inf_{x\in Y_R}|D_R'(x)|\ge c_0 .
\]
Every standard family returns a fixed positive fraction of its mass to a
bounded distortion copy of this pair within \(C\log(2+|b|)\) iterates.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose the two reflected itineraries around opposite sides of one triangular
gap.  At the symmetric central orbit, differentiating free-flight length with
respect to the unstable boundary coordinate gives the difference of the two
outgoing tangential momenta.  The two momenta have opposite nonzero tangential
components; compactness of the radius interval gives a lower bound \(2c_1\).
Append a common return word of length \(L\).  Hyperbolicity bounds the
derivative of all later paired terms by \(C\Lambda^{-L}\); choose \(L\) so
that this is below \(c_1\).  Distortion then preserves a lower bound \(c_0\)
on a common subrectangle.

The growth lemma and finite primitivity of the Markov family imply that a
fixed fraction of every standard family reaches this subrectangle after a
bounded recovery time.  At frequency \(b\), subdivision into unstable pieces
of length \(|b|^{-1}\) costs \(O(\log(2+|b|))\) iterates.  The same growth
argument on the subdivided family proves the final statement.
\end{proof}

If a joint unit-modulus eigenfunction existed at
\((u,v,b)\in\mathbb T^2\times\mathbb T\times\mathbb R\), periodic orbit
identities and Lemma~\ref{lem:r14-a2-uni} would force \(b=0\), while
Lemma~\ref{lem:r14-a2-span} would force \((u,v)=0\).  Thus the joint twist is
aperiodic.

\subsection{An exhaustive and integrable Fourier decomposition}

Let \(\omega=(u,v,b)\) and let \(P_R(\theta+i\omega)\) denote the pressure at
the real saddle \(\theta\).  Its Hessian is the covariance
\(\Sigma_{R,\theta}\) of the four-vector
\((\kappa_1,\kappa_2,r,\tau)\).

\begin{theorem}[Complete Fourier-line estimate]
\label{thm:r14-a2-fourier}
On every compact real saddle chart there are
\(\delta,B,\kappa,c,C>0\) such that, uniformly in \(R\):
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
|\omega|\le n^{-2/5}:&
 P_R(\theta+i\omega)-P_R(\theta)
 =i m_\theta\cdot\omega
  -\tfrac12\omega^T\Sigma_{R,\theta}\omega+O(|\omega|^3),\\[1mm]
n^{-2/5}<|\omega|\le\delta:&
 \|\mathcal L_{\theta+i\omega}^n\|
 \le Ce^{-cn|\omega|^2},\\[1mm]
\delta<|\omega|,\ |b|\le B:&
 \|\mathcal L_{\theta+i\omega}^n\|\le Ce^{-cn},\\[1mm]
B<|b|\le e^{\kappa\sqrt n}:&
 \|\mathcal L_{\theta+i\omega}^n\|
 \le C|b|^A e^{-cn/\log(2+|b|)},\\[1mm]
|b|>e^{\kappa\sqrt n}:&
 \|\mathcal L_{\theta+i\omega}^n\|
 \le Ce^{-cn}(1+|b|)^{-3}.
\end{array}
\]
The same bounds hold with a fixed central cylinder insertion.  In particular
the integral over every nonsmall region is \(o(n^{-2})\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first two estimates follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r14-a2-bundle} and the uniform positive covariance.  The
compact annulus is separated from the origin; joint aperiodicity and upper
semicontinuity of the spectral radius give the third estimate.

For the fourth range, use Lemma~\ref{lem:r14-a2-uni} on successive return
blocks.  Paired branches lose a fixed fraction of their norm every
\(C\log|b|\) iterates.  The polynomial distortion factor is dominated when
\(\log|b|\le\kappa\sqrt n\), after choosing \(\kappa\) below the fixed
cancellation threshold.

For the last range, reserve one returned UNI block for three integrations by
parts in its unstable coordinate.  Its phase derivative is at least
\(c_0|b|\); boundary terms cancel between the paired branches after the
homogeneous cut, while three amplitude derivatives are bounded by a fixed
power of the block distortion.  On a fixed positive fraction of the remaining
returned blocks use only the phase opposition, obtaining \(e^{-cn}\).
Thus the result is \(Ce^{-cn}|b|^{-3}\), with no frequency-independent
remainder.  The five regions are disjoint and cover the whole Fourier
domain.  Integrability of \((1+|b|)^{-3}\), Gaussian integration in the low
annulus, and exponential bounds elsewhere give \(o(n^{-2})\).  A central
insertion changes only a bounded number of branch factors.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The four-dimensional local theorem}

Let \(y_n=(k_n,m_n,t_n)\) satisfy
\(y_n-nm_\theta=O(\sqrt n)\).  Let
\(\phi_{\Sigma}\) be the centered four-dimensional Gaussian density.

\begin{theorem}[Vector--return--roof density LLT]
\label{thm:r14-a2-llt}
Uniformly on compact saddle charts,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb P_\theta
 \{K_n=k_n,\ N_n=m_n,\ T_n\in dt\}\\
 &\quad=
 {1\over (2\pi n)^2\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{R,\theta}}}
 \exp\!\left[
  -{1\over2n}(y_n-nm_\theta)^T
   \Sigma_{R,\theta}^{-1}(y_n-nm_\theta)
 \right]dt\,(1+o(1)).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently, before tilting the right side is multiplied by the exact
Cramér factor \(e^{-nI_R(y_n/n)}\).  These are two alternative conventions;
the saddle displacement is never counted twice.  For any roof interval
\(J_n\), the local probability is obtained by integrating this density, which
gives the subcentral, central, and saturated Gaussian interval masses with the
same \(n^{-2}\) lattice prefactor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Fourier inversion on
\(\mathbb T^2\times\mathbb T\times\mathbb R\).  On the small ball, substitute
\(\omega=z/\sqrt n\) in the expansion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r14-a2-fourier}; dominated convergence gives the
four-dimensional Gaussian and hence the power \(n^{-4/2}=n^{-2}\).
Every complementary range is \(o(n^{-2})\) by the same theorem.  Exponential
change of measure gives the untilted Cramér form.  Integrating the density
over \(J_n\) gives the stated window regimes without replacing a Gaussian
interval mass by an invalid linear approximation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conditioned central-window law]
\label{cor:r14-a2-condition}
The ratio version of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r14-a2-llt}, with a central cylinder insertion, gives the
stationary saddle law as the conditional local limit under joint homology,
return-count, and roof conditioning.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the insertion estimate in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r14-a2-fourier} for the numerator and the same density theorem
with insertion one for the denominator.  The common four-dimensional
coefficient cancels.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-fourteen A2 closure]
\label{thm:r14-a2-main}
The moving Sinai family has a parent-fold differentiable joint transfer
bundle, full lattice rank, returned temporal UNI, an exhaustive integrable
Fourier estimate, and the correctly normalized four-coordinate local limit
and conditioning theorems.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r14-a2-bundle},
\ref{thm:r14-a2-fourier}, and \ref{thm:r14-a2-llt}, with
Lemmas~\ref{lem:r14-a2-span} and \ref{lem:r14-a2-uni}.
\end{proof}
