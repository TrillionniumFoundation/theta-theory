## Overall assessment

The manuscript proposes an attractive local theory for homological displacement and roof time in a compact finite-horizon periodic Lorentz gas: a parameter-uniform twisted transfer-operator packet, an analytic physical-pressure root, a local current/clock duality, a projective cylinder-level large-deviation construction, and a quantitative two-sided conditioning theorem.

The revision has improved the truth boundary around the projective construction: it now says that no global empirical-process LDP is inferred outside the local simple-spectral component. This is a meaningful correction. The central model-specific theorem, however, remains explicitly designated as an “imported analytic packet,” and the strongest conditioning estimate still does not follow from the stated inputs. The paper therefore remains a program conditional on a difficult billiard theorem rather than a proved top-journal result.

## Major objections

### 1. The uniform vector-displacement/roof spectral theorem is still the missing principal theorem

The proof of the “Uniform homological Lorentz packet” says that the untwisted family has a uniform spectral gap, the vector/roof weight is a bounded analytic multiplier, and perturbation theory applies. For a radius-dependent billiard family, these are precisely the nontrivial assertions that require proof.

A complete argument must verify uniformly in the radius:

- common anisotropic strong and weak spaces, or canonical identifications between spaces attached to changing singularity structures;
- one-step expansion, complexity, distortion, homogeneity-strip and grazing estimates;
- uniform Lasota–Yorke inequalities;
- multiplier bounds for the discontinuous lattice displacement and roof observables;
- analytic dependence of the full complex vector/roof twist;
- simplicity and isolation of the leading eigenvalue on a common neighborhood;
- compatibility of left/right eigendata and normalized Doob transforms; and
- the lattice span and aperiodicity needed to exclude peripheral modes.

The manuscript itself says that external reviewers must verify these points and calls the theorem its principal imported packet. Citing a functional-analytic framework and a local-limit paper is not verification for this specific geometry and these weights. At a top journal, this packet would be the main theorem, not an admissible black box.

### 2. The “projective LDP” terminology still overstates what is proved

For each finite tuple, the manuscript claims upper and lower bounds only for Borel sets whose closures lie compactly inside the gradient image of a local pressure branch. These are local finite-dimensional large-deviation bounds, not full finite-projection LDPs on the entire coordinate range.

Dawson–Gärtner applies to compatible full LDPs for all finite projections. Consistency under coordinate deletion and compactness of the product of bounded coordinate ranges do not create the missing lower bounds outside the local exposed domain. The supremum over the declared cylinder pressure domain defines a natural local convex candidate functional, but calling the result a canonical projective LDP risks suggesting a theorem stronger than the available finite-dimensional input.

The correct formulation is a compatible family of local exposed-point LDPs or a local projective large-deviation principle, with no claim that the resulting functional governs arbitrary cylinder-topology closed and open sets.

### 3. Higher-block spectral control is another unproved analytic input

The projective section invokes “corresponding higher-block twisted operators” for arbitrary dynamically Hölder cylinder functions. The preceding packet covers only the displacement and roof weights. Passing to block potentials requires construction of the block extension, boundedness of the relevant multipliers across billiard singularities, and a common simple-eigenvalue neighborhood for each finite tuple. None of this is proved.

Thus even the local finite-dimensional pressure family used in the projective construction is not generated by the stated two-observable theorem alone.

### 4. Positive definiteness and local invertibility require a non-coboundary theorem

The implicit differentiation of the pressure root is formal and standard. The strict positive definiteness of the asymptotic covariance of

\[
\kappa_R-v_R(\xi)\tau_R
\]

in every nonzero homological direction is a separate dynamical theorem. It requires full-rank non-arithmetic behavior and exclusion of vector/clock coboundaries, uniformly in geometry and under nearby tilts. The proof merely refers to the same unproved local-limit packet.

Without this input, the local analytic diffeomorphism between the homological field and physical velocity is not established.

### 5. The two-sided current/clock conditioning estimate is unsupported at the announced resolution

The theorem conditions simultaneously on an exact lattice displacement and a roof interval of width \(b_n=o(n)\), permits \(b_n	o\infty\) arbitrarily slowly, and supplies an explicit ratio error. This is much stronger than an LDP or a local Laplace principle.

A proof at this resolution requires a joint lattice/non-lattice local-limit or Edgeworth analysis, including:

- Fourier inversion in the displacement coordinate;
- complex spectral estimates on major and minor arcs;
- smoothing of the roof indicator with controlled Fourier/Laplace tails;
- a denominator lower bound at the same submacroscopic scale;
- uniform estimates with a central cylinder insertion; and
- treatment of \(b_n\ll \sqrt n\).

The manuscript instead uses Fourier inversion only for displacement and a “joint spectral Laplace principle” for the roof interval. Exponential-rate information does not determine local interval probabilities or their ratios. In particular, the stated \(b_n^{-1}\), \(b_n/n\), and \((n-m)^{-1/2}\) error terms are announced without the estimates that would produce them. The claim that no temporal local-limit input is needed is not credible.

### 6. The variational continuation is legitimate only as a candidate outside the local branch

The revised paper is more careful here and explicitly says that microscopic realization outside the simple spectral component requires a separate global LDP. That truth boundary should be maintained consistently. Phrases such as “canonical convex continuation” and “generalized canonical candidates” are definitions, not theorems about actual prepared billiard laws.

### 7. The paper is not self-contained at the level required by its claims

The only genuinely billiard-specific assertions—the common family of spaces, aperiodicity, vector/roof local limit, and conditioning ratio—are either imported or sketched. The remaining calculations are consequences of those assertions. A paper cannot receive top-journal credit primarily for organizing implications of an unproved theorem.

## Status of prior objections

The revision correctly weakens the global projective-LDP rhetoric and distinguishes the variational continuation from microscopic realization. Those are substantive presentational improvements. It does not close the uniform spectral/local-limit packet or the submacroscopic conditioning theorem, which remain the load-bearing objections.

## Required reconstruction

The strongest viable new submission would focus on a single theorem: a parameter-uniform vector-displacement/roof spectral and joint local-limit theorem for the specified Lorentz family, proved in full. A quantitatively correct conditioning corollary could follow. The projective empirical-process material should remain explicitly local unless full finite-projection LDPs are established.

## Editorial recommendation

**Reject.** The potentially important model-specific theorem is still assumed rather than proved, and the strongest conditioning statement does not follow from the stated spectral and large-deviation inputs.
