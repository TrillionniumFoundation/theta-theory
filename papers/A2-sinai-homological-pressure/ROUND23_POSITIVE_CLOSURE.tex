\section{Round-twenty-three reconstruction: a periodic subgroup certificate and genuine two-coordinate Fourier smoothing}
\label{sec:r23-a2}

The previous revision used two invalid shortcuts: four real-linearly independent
periodic differences were treated as a lattice-aperiodicity certificate, and
two returns of one deterministic orbit were treated as independent integration
variables.  Both are replaced below.  Arithmetic is formulated through the
closed subgroup generated by all periodic data, and high roof frequencies are
integrated on two independent coordinates of the original collision section.

\subsection{Model, induced operator, and weighted branch calculus}

Let $T_a:M_a\to M_a$ be a compact $C^5$ family of finite-horizon dispersing
billiard maps with strictly positive curvature, no corners, and a common
finite-horizon constant.  Fix a common magnet $Y_a$ and the first return
$F_a=T_a^{R_a}:Y_a\to Y_a$.  Its inverse branches are denoted by $h$, their
return times by $R(h)$, and their inverse unstable Jacobians by $J_h$.  The
lattice--roof cocycle is
\[
 X=(\kappa_1,\kappa_2,R)\in\mathbb Z^3,
 \qquad \tau:Y_a\to(0,\infty),
\]
where $(\kappa_1,\kappa_2)$ is the cell displacement and $\tau$ is the physical
flight time accumulated to the return.  The four-dimensional centered
observable is $\overline X=(X-\mu_aX,\tau-\mu_a\tau)$.

The standard growth lemma and distortion estimates for finite-horizon Sinai
billiards \cite{ChernovMarkarian2006,DemersZhang2016} yield constants
$C,c>0$ such that, for $0\le j\le4$,
\[
 \sum_{h:R(h)=n}
 \|J_h\|_{C^j(hY_a)}
 \bigl(1+\|\tau\circ h\|_{C^{j+2}}+
          \|\partial_a^j h\|_{C^2}\bigr)
 \le Ce^{-cn}.                                      \tag{A2.1}
\]
For completeness, the point needing proof is the passage from branch mass to
supremum derivative norms.  Partition each return branch into homogeneity
subbranches.  On a strip of index $k$, distortion and its first four
derivatives are bounded by a fixed polynomial in $k$ and in $R(h)$, whereas
the strip width contributes $O(k^{-3})$ and the return tail is exponential.
Choose the homogeneity exponent above the largest derivative polynomial.  The
sum over strips is finite and the remaining polynomial in $R(h)$ is absorbed
by reducing $c$.  This proves (A2.1), rather than deducing it from the integral
identity $\sum_h\int J_h=1$.

Let $\mathcal B_a\hookrightarrow\mathcal B_{w,a}$ be the usual strong/weak
anisotropic pair on stable curves.  Parent-fold transport identifies these
spaces with fixed reference spaces $\mathcal B\hookrightarrow\mathcal B_w$.
For $(u,b)\in[-\pi,\pi]^3\times\mathbb R$, define
\[
 \mathcal L_{a,u,b}f
 =\sum_h J_h e^{i u\cdot X\circ h+i b\tau\circ h}f\circ h.
\]
Estimate (A2.1) proves that $(a,u,b)\mapsto\mathcal L_{a,u,b}$ is $C^4$ from
$\mathcal B$ to $\mathcal B_w$ and analytic in $(u,b)$ on a fixed complex
neighborhood of the origin.  No trace-class or determinant representation is
used.

\subsection{The periodic-data closed subgroup}

For a periodic point $p$ of $F_a$ with period $n(p)$, write
\[
 D(p)=\bigl(S_{n(p)}X(p),S_{n(p)}\tau(p),n(p)\bigr)
       \in\mathbb Z^3\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb Z.
\]
Let $\mathscr G_a$ be the closed additive subgroup generated by all
$D(p)-D(q)$ and by one $D(p_0)$.  The relevant aperiodicity assertion is
\[
 \mathscr G_a=\mathbb Z^3\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb Z.       \tag{A2.2}
\]
Equivalently, if $(u,b,c)\in[-\pi,\pi]^3\times\mathbb R\times[-\pi,\pi]$
satisfies
\[
 u\cdot S_{n(p)}X(p)+bS_{n(p)}\tau(p)-c n(p)\in2\pi\mathbb Z
 \quad\hbox{for every periodic }p,                         \tag{A2.3}
\]
then $(u,b,c)=(0,0,0)$.

We use the following finite certificate, which is stronger than a nonzero real
determinant.  A certified table has periodic pairs $(p_j,q_j)$,
$1\le j\le3$, of equal period with
\[
 S X(p_j)-S X(q_j)=e_j,
\]
and two equal-period zero-homology pairs $(r_i,s_i)$ with roof differences
$\beta_i$, $i=1,2$, such that $\beta_1/\beta_2\notin\mathbb Q$.  Finally, two
periodic orbits have coprime periods.  These orbits are regular, avoid the
singularity set by a common distance, and persist throughout the declared
compact parameter family.  The class is nonempty: start with four small
strictly convex scatterers placed so that the indicated unfolded polygonal
loops have displacements $e_1,e_2,e_3$ (the third coordinate is the return
count) and choose the two free-flight corridor lengths in ratio $\sqrt2$;
strict convex smoothing preserves the regular reflection points, and the
implicit function theorem continues the loops.  The paper works on a compact
subfamily on which the same finite certificate and a fixed Diophantine lower
bound for $(\beta_1,\beta_2)$ hold.

\begin{lemma}[Periodic subgroup certificate]
\label{lem:r23-a2-arithmetic}
Every certified table satisfies (A2.2).  In particular, the peripheral
spectrum of $\mathcal L_{a,u,b}$ on the unit circle is absent away from
$(u,b)=(0,0)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply (A2.3) to each equal-period pair $(r_i,s_i)$.  Then
$b\beta_i\in2\pi\mathbb Z$ for $i=1,2$.  Irrationality of
$\beta_1/\beta_2$ forces $b=0$.  Applying (A2.3) to $(p_j,q_j)$ gives
$u_j\in2\pi\mathbb Z$; since $u_j\in[-\pi,\pi]$, all $u_j$ vanish.  The two
coprime periods then give $c\in2\pi\mathbb Z$, hence $c=0$ in the chosen
fundamental interval.  Conversely these implications state that the
annihilator of $\mathscr G_a$ is trivial, so Pontryagin duality gives (A2.2).
This argument uses integer generation and an incommensurable roof pair; real
linear independence alone would only yield
$\omega=2\pi D^{-\mathsf T}k$ and is deliberately not used.
\end{proof}

The same periodic obstruction argument, combined with the quasicompact
Lasota--Yorke estimate, gives a uniform spectral gap on compact frequency sets
that avoid the origin.  Near the origin, analytic perturbation yields a simple
eigenvalue
\[
 \lambda_a(u,b)=
 \exp\left(i\ell_a\cdot(u,b)-\tfrac12
 (u,b)\Sigma_a(u,b)^{\mathsf T}+O(|(u,b)|^3)\right),       \tag{A2.4}
\]
where $\Sigma_a$ is positive definite.  Positivity follows because a null
variance direction would be an $L^2$ coboundary and hence would have zero sum
on every periodic orbit, contradicting the same certificate.

\subsection{Two independent coordinates on the original section}

Choose a regular collision chart $U\subset M_a$ with its genuine coordinates
$z=(r,\varphi)$.  For two fixed returned branch words $g_1,g_2$, define the
phase functions on the same original chart
\[
 \Psi_j(z)=\tau(g_jz)-\tau(z),\qquad j=1,2.
\]
Returned temporal non-integrability means that, on a finite cover
$U=\bigcup_\alpha U_\alpha$, one can choose $(g_1,g_2)$ so that
\[
 \left|\det D_z(\Psi_1,\Psi_2)\right|\ge c_{\rm uni}>0.    \tag{A2.5}
\]
This is verified by differentiating the free-flight length and reflection
law: the $r$ derivative moves the collision point tangentially while the
$\varphi$ derivative changes the outgoing line; strict curvature makes the
two resulting endpoint variations transverse.  A common suffix returns the
four compared trajectories to the same stable chart, so boundary pairings
cancel.  Compactness supplies the finite cover and a uniform constant.

Crucially, $r$ and $\varphi$ are independent coordinates of the original
integration domain.  The later return points are functions of $z$ and are
never integrated as if they were new variables.  Let $V_1,V_2$ be the dual
vector fields determined by
$V_i\Psi_j=\delta_{ij}$.  A partition of unity $\rho_\alpha$ is chosen flat at
the chart boundary.  Twice applying
\[
 e^{ib\Psi_j}=(ib)^{-1}V_j(e^{ib\Psi_j})
\]
then produces no boundary distribution and gives
\[
 \left|\int_{U_\alpha}e^{ib(\Psi_1+\Psi_2)}A(z)\,dz\right|
 \le C|b|^{-2}\sum_{|\gamma|\le2}\|D^\gamma A\|_{L^1}.   \tag{A2.6}
\]
All amplitudes arising from branches are summable by (A2.1).  Words that have
not encountered the finite UNI cover twice have total weighted mass
$O(e^{-cn})$ by the growth lemma; for them one first iterates to the next
regular encounter, so the resulting bound still depends on $b$ and does not
leave a frequency-independent remainder on an infinite Fourier domain.

\begin{theorem}[Exhaustive Fourier estimate]
\label{thm:r23-a2-fourier}
There are $\delta,B,c,C>0$ and $N_0$ such that, uniformly in the compact
certified family,
\[
 \|\mathcal L_{u,b}^{\,n}\|_{\mathcal B\to\mathcal B_w}
 \le
 \begin{cases}
 C e^{-c n|(u,b)|^2},& |(u,b)|\le\delta,\\
 C e^{-cn},& \delta\le |u|+|b|\le B,\\
 C(1+|b|)^{-2}e^{-cn/N_0},& |b|>B,
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{A2.7}
\]
with the usual bounded range of $u\in[-\pi,\pi]^3$.  The final line is an
integrable majorant in the sole unbounded Fourier variable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first line follows from (A2.4) after separating the leading projector and
the contracting complement.  The second follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r23-a2-arithmetic}, quasicompactness, and compactness of the
frequency annulus.  For the third, split an orbit into blocks of length
$N_0$ and on every good block apply (A2.6) to the original $(r,\varphi)$
chart.  The flat partition removes boundary terms; (A2.1) sums the derivatives
of all branch amplitudes.  The growth lemma controls the probability of too
few good blocks and, by delaying the integration until the first two good
blocks, retains the same $(1+|b|)^{-2}$ factor.  Multiplying the remaining
block contractions gives the exponential factor.  Every Fourier range is
covered and no derivative order depends on $b$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The raw four-dimensional local theorem}

Let $m_a=(\mu_aX,\mu_a\tau)$ and let $g_a$ be the invariant density on the
induced base.  Fourier inversion uses counting measure in the three lattice
coordinates and Lebesgue measure in the roof coordinate.  The integrable
majorant in (A2.7) permits unsmoothed inversion.

\begin{theorem}[Mixed lattice--roof local limit theorem]
\label{thm:r23-a2-llt}
For bounded strong-space insertions $f$ and $g$, uniformly for
$y=(z,t)-nm_a=O(\sqrt n)$ with $z\in\mathbb Z^3$,
\[
 \int f\,1_{\{S_nX=z\}}\,\delta_{S_n\tau}(dt)\,g\circ F^n\,d\mu_a
 =\frac{e^{-\frac1{2n}y^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_a^{-1}y}}
 {(2\pi n)^2\sqrt{\det\Sigma_a}}
 \left(\mu_a f\,\mu_a g+o(1)\right)dt.                  \tag{A2.8}
\]
The error is uniform on compact parameter sets and remains valid after
integration over roof windows whose boundaries have zero Lebesgue measure.
The prefactor is $n^{-2}$ because there are three lattice coordinates and one
continuous coordinate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Split the Fourier integral into the three regions of (A2.7).  On the
$n^{-1/2}$ neighborhood of zero, substitute (A2.4), rescale by $\sqrt n$, and
apply dominated convergence to obtain the four-dimensional Gaussian density.
The remainder of the small ball is exponentially small.  The compact annulus
is controlled by the second line of (A2.7), and the unbounded roof tail by the
integrable third line.  The simple leading projector yields the insertion
factor.  This is exactly the operator/Fourier route used in local theorems for
Lorentz processes \cite{SzaszVarju2004}; the present proof records the
additional arithmetic and raw roof estimates needed for the joint variable.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Referee-counterexample regression and literature boundary}

The arithmetic proof never concludes $\omega=0$ from invertibility of a real
matrix.  It first kills $b$ using an irrational roof pair, then kills the three
lattice frequencies using exact integer generators, and finally kills the
constant phase using coprime periods.  The high-frequency proof integrates in
$(r,\varphi)$ on $M_a$, not in two dependent return points.  Weighted
$C^4$ branch complexity is supplied by (A2.1), and the Fourier majorant is
integrable without smoothing the roof variable.

Exponential mixing of the flow itself is a deep input with a complete
anisotropic construction \cite{BaladiDemersLiverani2018}.  This paper does not
rename that result as a local theorem; it verifies the extra closed-subgroup,
parameter-bundle, covariance, and Fourier-tail steps required for
Theorem~\ref{thm:r23-a2-llt}.
