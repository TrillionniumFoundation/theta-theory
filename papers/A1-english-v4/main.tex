\begin{abstract}
We study experiments specified by a deterministic apparatus, a parameter-independent preparation, and a common admissible command class. For a positive polynomial observation channel, we characterize the relative interior of its attainable censoring likelihoods by the rank of its coefficient functions. If this rank is $r$, their normalized $n$-factor products have generic local dimension $n(r-1)$; full rank gives a sharp continuous exact-state bound. In a counted Lorentz cartridge laboratory, passive fixed-mode records have dimension $n$, whereas selectable censoring failures attain dimension $3n$. An explicit interior-radius bound records the loss of conditioning near a degenerate detector. We retain a positive Bernstein filter, boundary-complete Bellman attainment, and fixed-controller response for this same apparatus. We prove optimal endpoint gates and solve the last-cartridge problem with two terminal decisions and positive readout cost: the collision acceptance set is a single circular arc, and the value is explicit. A rational interval certificate exhibits strict improvement over every report-blind gate. Finally, arbitrary uniformly positive continuous collision detectors admit mechanically realizable polynomial surrogates with preserved placement and failure accounting. Uniform one-step total-variation error gives a complete adaptive-policy regret bound, and Bernstein approximation turns it into explicit degree and finite lookup-size requirements. Thus exact algebra is treated as a designed member of a quantitatively controlled detector class, not as a universal consequence of smooth mechanics. The entropy, moving-interface and preparation-response statements include the qualifications required by the two preceding reviews.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} mechanical experiments; censoring; likelihood-state dimension; Bernstein approximation; partially observed control; robust experimental design.
