\section{Operational setting and the main structural program}\label{sec:framework}
An experiment consists of a deterministic measurable program, a preparation measure on the system and apparatus, a report map, and an admissible family of commands. Its law is the push-forward of its preparation. Commands cannot receive the unknown parameter as an input. Reweighting a law does not, by itself, specify a realizable command. All resource charges and all observations available to a controller belong to the specification.

The central result of this revision is a class-level connection between attainable likelihood geometry, exact inference, gate structure, and robust approximation. The Lorentz apparatus is a verified realization of that connection. It is not asserted that its intentionally chosen cubic readout is forced by mechanics. The general approximation theorem explains how to retain controlled decision performance when that algebra is changed.

\begin{theorem}[Structural and robust operational synthesis]\label{thm:main}
For the counted apparatus below and its full common comparator class:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The one-cartridge law is the strictly positive cubic kernel in Proposition~\ref{prop:raw}. Every non-null length-$n$ history has an exact positive Bernstein likelihood with $3n+1$ coefficients, including both placement failure and censoring.
\item For a fixed nondegenerate mode, selectable all-censoring histories contain a $3n$-dimensional local likelihood manifold, whereas passive fixed-mode histories have maximal dimension $n$. The former is a sharp lower bound for continuous exact states. More generally, coefficient rank $r$ gives generic dimension $n(r-1)$, with an explicit attainable interior and a polynomial-class perturbation criterion.
\item The finite-budget observable-reward problem has an attained Borel feedback solution, including gates in $[0,1]$ and zero-evidence branches. An optimal controller uses endpoint gates. With one cartridge remaining, two terminal decisions and a positive constant readout cost, an optimal collision gate is a single arc; its value and a strict report-dependent advantage are explicitly calculated.
\item For a positive continuous collision detector with a uniform parameter total-variation Lipschitz constant $L_t$, there are physical polynomial detector surrogates of degree at most $m+1$. Their optimal policies have true-apparatus regret at most
\[
 \Delta_N N p_* L_t/\sqrt m,
\]
where $p_*$ is a uniform hit-probability bound and $\Delta_N$ bounds the range of the complete payoff. A second-derivative assumption improves the degree dependence to $m^{-1}$. A finite cell implementation and its separate error are also explicit.
\item For the specified cubic detector and every fixed parameter-independent adaptive controller, path laws have strong variation response of every finite order, a degree-$3N$ derivative cutoff, common-support quadratic-mean differentiability and the conditional Fisher-information sum.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Propositions~\ref{prop:raw}--\ref{prop:realize} derive the apparatus law and legal adaptive pasting. Theorems~\ref{thm:filter}, \ref{thm:rank} and \ref{thm:amplification} prove the inference and dimension assertions. Theorems~\ref{thm:bellman}, \ref{thm:endpoint} and \ref{thm:arc}, with Proposition~\ref{prop:example}, prove control and performance. Theorems~\ref{thm:model-error} and \ref{thm:hierarchy} supply the uniform policy comparison and constructive degree bounds. Theorem~\ref{thm:response} proves response directly for the same observed kernel. Each input is proved below.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The counted mechanical preparation}
Let $\Lambda$ have generators $(1,0)$ and $(1/2,\sqrt3/2)$, and write
\[
 a_0=\sqrt3/2,\quad I=[l,u]=[9/20,47/100],\quad
 A(R)=a_0-\pi R^2,\quad Q_R=\T^2_\Lambda\setminus\overline B(0,R).
\]
The fixed unknown radius is $R$. A placed particle moves at unit speed with specular disk reflections. Distinct lifted disks are separated by at least $1-2u=3/50$. An outgoing ray cannot return immediately to its own convex disk. Consequently any physical observation window shorter than $3/50$ contains at most one collision.

Each of $N$ independent cartridges is prepared with
\begin{equation}
 q\sim dq/a_0\text{ on }\T^2_\Lambda,\qquad
 \vartheta\sim d\vartheta/(2\pi),\qquad U,W\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1].
 \label{eq:preparation}
\end{equation}
The command $a=(T,\epsilon,\theta)$ is chosen before exposing these variables, in
\[
 \cA=[T_-,T_+]\times[0,E]\times\mathbb S^1,
 \quad 0<T_-\le T_+<3/50,\quad Eu^2<1.
\]
A hard-contact insertion guard releases the particle exactly when $q\in Q_R$. Otherwise it reports the placement-failure tag $\partial$. Vertical insertion over rigid cylinders, with a top-face failure collector and open release channels, is an ideal deterministic realization of this guard. It is a physical interaction, not a numerical query of the unknown radius. Boundary and tangent exceptions are null under the preparation.

\emph{Every attempt costs one cartridge}, including unsuccessful placement and subsequent censoring. The unused cartridge variables retain their initial product law conditional on the past. This is a finite intervention laboratory, not a sequence of independent collisions inferred from a single orbit. Neither mixing nor a free radius-dependent equilibrium reset is used. The budget is not a theorem about energy, quantum precision, or elapsed-time optimality; $T$ itself is an actual physical time window.

\subsection{An explicitly prepared detector}
On collision let $z$ be the impact vector relative to the local disk center. The center chart is unambiguous since $u<1/2$. Define
\[
 F_{c,\theta}(y)=\{y+c[\sin(y-\theta)+\sin\theta]\}/(2\pi),
 \quad 0\le y\le2\pi,\quad 0\le c<1.
\]
The collision report is $Y=F^{-1}_{\epsilon|z|^2,\theta}(U)$. On no collision it is $Y=2\pi U$. The inverse exists because $F'=(1+c\cos(y-\theta))/(2\pi)>0$. Firmware receives the measured coordinate $z$, not the unknown $R$; on impact $|z|=R$.

This is an intentionally designed information channel. In the ideal classical apparatus a smooth local readout $G$ can be coupled to a canonical pointer $(Q,P)$ with impulse Hamiltonian $PG$. On the prepared sheet $P=0$, Hamilton's equations give $\dot Q=G$ without moving the read variables; general pointer momenta would produce back-action and must not be suppressed. The hybrid guard, the seed and the comparator are specified parts of the instrument. No claim that an arbitrary Borel gate is a finite smooth Hamiltonian circuit is made. Finite lookup approximations are proved separately.

\subsection{Integration over the entire collision tube}
Write $n_\alpha=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)$ and $t_\alpha=(-\sin\alpha,\cos\alpha)$. A colliding initial condition has
\[
 v^-=-\cos\phi\,n_\alpha+\sin\phi\,t_\alpha,
 \qquad q_0=Rn_\alpha-sv^-,\quad 0<s<T,\quad |\phi|<\pi/2.
\]
Every regular collision has precisely these coordinates modulo the lattice and angular identifications. Conversely the backward segment of length $s$ cannot meet another disk, by separation, and lies outside its own disk by convexity. After reflection the remaining segment also has no collision. Thus these coordinates cover the full colliding preparation without overlaps or deletion of a positive-mass grazing neighborhood.

At fixed incoming angle $\vartheta=\alpha+\pi-\phi$, the position Jacobian has columns $Rt_\alpha$ and $-v^-$, with absolute determinant $R\cos\phi$. The angular change has determinant of absolute value one. Hence, for any bounded collision test $f$,
\begin{equation}
 \E_R[\one_{\mathrm{hit}}f]
 =\frac{R}{2\pi a_0}\int_0^T\int_0^{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}
 f(Rn_\alpha,v^-,s)\cos\phi\,d\phi\,d\alpha\,ds.
 \label{eq:tube}
\end{equation}
The ambient hit probability is $2TR/a_0$, placement failure has mass $\pi R^2/a_0$, and no-hit successful placement has mass $(A(R)-2TR)/a_0$. Conditional on successful placement the hit probability is $2TR/A(R)$. That conditional experiment has a different normalization and resource count.

Let $X=\{\partial\}\sqcup(\{0,1\}\times\mathbb S^1)$ and
$\nu=\delta_\partial+\sum_{h=0}^1\delta_h\,dy/(2\pi)$, of total mass three.
\begin{proposition}[Raw positive cubic channel]\label{prop:raw}
The raw density with respect to $\nu$ is $k_R^a(x)=H_a(R,x)/a_0$, with
\begin{align}
 H_a(R,\partial)&=\pi R^2,\notag\\
 H_a(R,0,y)&=a_0-\pi R^2-2TR,\notag\\
 H_a(R,1,y)&=2TR\{1+\epsilon R^2\cos(y-\theta)\}.
 \label{eq:raw}
\end{align}
It integrates to one and satisfies uniform positive upper and lower bounds. A numerator lower bound is
\[
 h_* =\min\{\pi l^2,\ a_0-\pi u^2-2T_+u,\ 2T_-l(1-Eu^2)\}>0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The tube integral gives the hit mass. Differentiating the detector CDF gives its conditional circle density. The other two masses follow from the insertion geometry. Their sum is $a_0/a_0$. The displayed bounds follow from $l\le R\le u$ and the mode restrictions. The middle lower bound is positive even with $T_+=3/50$, using $a_0>0.86$ and $\pi<22/7$. One upper bound is $a_0^{-1}\max\{\pi u^2,a_0,2T_+u(1+Eu^2)\}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact comparator family and chronological law}
For $0\le\eta<1/2$, a gate is a Borel function $g:X\to[\eta,1-\eta]$. The comparator accepts $x$ when $W\le g(x)$. The observed report is then $x$; otherwise it is $\dagger$. The raw rejected coordinate is not retained in controller memory. Put $Z=X\sqcup\{\dagger\}$ and $\mu=\nu+\delta_\dagger$.
\begin{proposition}[Common-gate realizability and adaptive pasting]\label{prop:realize}
The attainable kernels in this comparator class are exactly
\begin{equation}
 K_R^{a,g}(dx)=g(x)k_R^a(x)\nu(dx),\qquad
 K_R^{a,g}(\dagger)=f_R^{a,g}=\int(1-g)k_R^a\,d\nu.
 \label{eq:gated}
\end{equation}
A proposed accepted family $\ell_R$ is attainable precisely when there is one radius-independent gate $g$ such that $\ell_R=gk_R^a$ on a common $\nu$-full set for every $R$, with the complementary failure mass. Borel history-dependent commands compose for every finite budget using the prepared cartridges, with both failure tags charged.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditioning on $x$ and integrating the independent uniform $W$ proves the formula. Necessity follows because the comparator evaluates the same function at every unknown radius, and sufficiency is the same physical comparator. Positivity of the raw density makes all zero branches parameter independent. Conditional on a past history, unused cartridge coordinates still have their product preparation. The deterministic map from that history and the next cartridge to the next report is Borel. Induction gives the chronological product of the displayed conditional kernels, with no independence claim about a long billiard orbit.
\end{proof}

Set $b=2T$ and
\[
 v_a=(\one_\partial,\one_{h=0},\one_{h=1},\one_{h=1}\cos(y-\theta)),
 \qquad m=\int gv_a\,d\nu.
\]
The failure numerator is
\begin{equation}
 H_\dagger(R;m)=\pi R^2(1-m_\partial)
 +(a_0-\pi R^2-bR)(1-m_0)+bR(1-m_1)-b\epsilon R^3m_2.
 \label{eq:failure}
\end{equation}
Its four moments must be jointly feasible; individual interval bounds are insufficient. A failed comparison is informative and multiplies the likelihood by this polynomial.

A bounded source $V$ has a simultaneously attainable conditional tilt using $g=ce^V$. With $\eta>0$ this is possible exactly when
$\eta e^{-\inf V}\le c\le(1-\eta)e^{-\sup V}$; for $\eta=0$ take $c=e^{-\sup V}$. Repeating a fixed command to acceptance or a cap $B$ gives censoring mass $f_R^B$, expected count $(1-f_R^B)/(1-f_R)$, and the same conditional tilted accepted law, since its accepted sublaw is $\sum_{j=0}^{B-1}f_R^j gk_R^a\nu$. Placement failure can itself be an accepted report. Conditioning does not erase either resource charge.
