\section{Attained control and optimal endpoint gates}\label{sec:control}
\subsection{The common decision problem}
Fix $\lambda\in\R$. An accepted report $x$ earns $r_a(x)$ and censoring earns $r_a^\dagger$, jointly continuous on the compact component and mode domains, with absolute value at most $B$. Let $D_0$ be compact metric and $G(R,d)$ continuous, with $0<G_-\le G\le G_+$. We maximize
\begin{equation}
 \E_{\pi_0}^{\Pi}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^N r_i\right)G(R,d)\right]
 \label{eq:criterion}
\end{equation}
over all Borel history-dependent modes, common gates and terminal decisions. The gate has no extra nonlinear gate-dependent cost or coupling constraint; this qualification is essential for the endpoint theorem. Exactly $N$ attempts are scheduled. The capped protocol in Section~\ref{sec:framework} is a separate specified stopping experiment.

Let $n$ be the used count and $b$ the remaining count. At coefficient state $c\in\cS_n$, put
\[
 \bar k_c^a(x)=\int k_R^a(x)\pi_c(dR),\quad
 \bar f_c^a(m)=\int H_\dagger(R;m)/a_0\,\pi_c(dR).
\]
Let $c^{a,x}$ and $c^{a,m,\dagger}$ be the updates from Theorem~\ref{thm:filter}. The accepted posterior is independent of a positive gate scalar. On a zero-probability branch its chosen value is immaterial.

Given a continuous next value, define
\[
 w_{c,a}(x)=\bar k_c^a(x)e^{\lambda r_a(x)}V_{n+1,b-1}(c^{a,x}),\qquad
 F_{c,a}(x)=(v_a(x),w_{c,a}(x)).
\]
The exact feasible body is
\begin{equation}
 \cZ(c,a)=\left\{(m,z)=\int g(x)F_{c,a}(x)\nu(dx):\eta\le g\le1-\eta\right\}
 \subset\R^5.
 \label{eq:body}
\end{equation}
This is a five-coordinate representation, not a claim of intrinsic dimension five in every decision problem.

\begin{lemma}[Support and jointly measurable realizations]\label{lem:body}
The body is compact and convex and has support function
\begin{equation}
 h_{\cZ(c,a)}(v)=\eta\int v\cdot F_{c,a}\,d\nu
 +(1-2\eta)\int(v\cdot F_{c,a})_+\,d\nu.
 \label{eq:support}
\end{equation}
Every feasible point has a comparator realization. A jointly Borel representative of that gate can be selected as a function of $(c,a,m,z,x)$ on the feasible graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The order interval $\cG=\{\eta\le g\le1-\eta\}$ is weak-star compact in $L^\infty(\nu)$ and metrizable since $L^1(\nu)$ is separable. Integration against the five $L^1$ coordinates is continuous, so the image is compact and convex. Pointwise maximization of $g\,v\cdot F$ selects the upper endpoint where $v\cdot F>0$ and the lower endpoint where it is negative, proving the support formula.

An equivalence class cannot simply be evaluated at an observed point. Use nested dyadic partitions on the two circles, keeping $\partial$ as an atom. The cell averages $E_jg(x)$ are jointly Borel in $(g,x)$, since each average is a weak-star continuous coordinate. Clip their limsup to the gate interval. The resulting jointly Borel function equals the class almost everywhere by differentiation (equivalently, conditional-expectation convergence for these generating partitions).

The map $(c,a,g)\mapsto\int gF_{c,a}\,d\nu$ is continuous: changes in $(c,a)$ give $L^1$-continuous integrands, uniformly on compact sets. Fibers over its compact feasible graph are nonempty compact sets. To select one, cover the compact gate space by finitely many closed sets of progressively smaller diameter, refining at each step. Choose the first child meeting the fiber. Each such test is Borel because the projection of a closed subset of a compact graph is closed. Nested choices converge to a unique gate class. Compose this Borel selection with the representative construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Boundary-complete Bellman attainment]\label{thm:bellman}
The values of \eqref{eq:criterion} depend on the past only through $(n,c,b)$. They are continuous on the finite-budget compact strata, and
\begin{align}
 V_{n,0}(c)&=\max_{d\in D_0}\int G(R,d)\pi_c(dR),\notag\\
 V_{n,b}(c)&=\max_{a\in\cA,\,(m,z)\in\cZ(c,a)}
 \left[z+e^{\lambda r_a^\dagger}\bar f_c^a(m)V_{n+1,b-1}(c^{a,m,\dagger})\right].
 \label{eq:bellman}
\end{align}
The product involving $\bar f=0$ is defined to be zero. An optimal Borel feedback controller exists and is implemented by the same cartridges and comparator. Independent randomization of commands cannot improve the value. Finite interval gates and finite mode/decision menus approximate the optimum uniformly to any prescribed accuracy at each fixed budget.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The terminal value is continuous and attained by compactness, continuity of $G$ and posterior continuity. Suppose the assertion holds at the next resource level. Conditioning on the next observed report yields
\[
 Q(c,a,g)=\int gw_{c,a}\,d\nu+
 e^{\lambda r_a^\dagger}\bar f_c^a(m)V_{n+1,b-1}(c^{a,m,\dagger}).
\]
The accepted term is jointly continuous. Where $\bar f>0$, the failure coefficients and posterior are continuous. At zero evidence a posterior limit need not exist, but
\[
 0\le e^{\lambda r_a^\dagger}\bar f V_{n+1,b-1}
 \le \bar f\,G_+e^{b|\lambda|B}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the weighted term, not a fictitious null-branch posterior, extends continuously. The objective is continuous on the compact product $\cS_n\times\cA\times\cG$; its maximum is attained and continuous in $c$ by uniform continuity. Replacing $g$ by its five integrals gives exactly \eqref{eq:bellman}.

Choose a maximizing command by the same nested closed-cover argument: for a compact command subset $C$, the test $\max_C Q(c,\cdot)=V_{n,b}(c)$ is closed. The representative map in Lemma~\ref{lem:body} makes the selected comparator jointly Borel in state and raw report. Proposition~\ref{prop:realize} pastes these commands into the prepared finite laboratory. Conditional on the past, the parameter has posterior $\pi_c$, future cartridges are fresh, and the feasible continuation class and remaining count are unchanged. Past exponential rewards factor out as positive known scalars. This proves sufficiency against full-history policies by induction. A randomized command averages the same $Q$ values and cannot improve their maximum.

For finite implementation, replace any gate by $g_J=E_Jg$, keeping the placement atom unchanged. For each component $F_i$,
\[
 \left|\int(g-g_J)F_i\,d\nu\right|
 =\left|\int g(F_i-E_JF_i)\,d\nu\right|
 \le(1-\eta)\|F_i-E_JF_i\|_{L^1}.
\]
The compact family of continuous circle integrands is uniformly equicontinuous, so this tends to zero uniformly over states, modes and gates. Uniform continuity of the extended failure-weighted term gives uniform command-value convergence. This does not assert uniform $L^1$ convergence of arbitrary gates themselves. The Bellman operator is $e^{|\lambda|B}$-Lipschitz in its continuation value, because total predictive probability is one. Backward induction bounds error by a finite weighted sum of one-step errors. For fixed $J$, the gate values lie in a finite compact cube; finite grids in that cube, compact mode nets and terminal-decision nets approximate the continuous objective uniformly. Choosing the first maximizing menu entry is Borel, proving the claimed implementable approximation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Intrinsic dimension and the continuation coordinate]\label{rem:dimension}
For $\lambda=0$ and $G\equiv1$, every continuation value is one. With $M_j=\E_{\pi_c}R^j$, the fifth integrand is $w_{c,a}=A\cdot v_a$, where
\[
 A=a_0^{-1}(\pi M_2,\ a_0-\pi M_2-bM_1,\ bM_1,\ b\epsilon M_3).
\]
Hence every body point satisfies $z=A\cdot m$ and intrinsic dimension is at most four. In general it is at most five. The fifth coordinate depends on the current continuation value; neither it nor the four failure moments universally encodes the whole accepted instrument. A body point is realized by a gate before the next observation is generated. These facts are separate from the sharp $3n$ likelihood dimension.
\end{remark}

\subsection{An optimal policy uses only endpoint gates}
The next result develops, and explicitly credits, the endpoint argument in Section 7 of the English-v3 referee report \cite{RefereeV3}. Its value here is operational: it applies to the attained measurable feedback, not just to a finite discretization.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal Borel endpoint feedback]\label{thm:endpoint}
Under the reward and command assumptions of this section, an optimal feedback controller exists whose gates take values only in $\{\eta,1-\eta\}$. In particular the full comparator class admits an optimal deterministic accept/reject gate at every resource state.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a nonnegative finite parameter measure $\zeta$, let
\[
 \W_b(\zeta)=\sup_\Pi\int L_\Pi(R)\,\zeta(dR),
\]
where $L_\Pi$ is the bounded nonnegative payoff of a remaining-$b$ policy, including its final decision. The same policy class is used for every measure. Thus $\W_b$ is a supremum of linear functionals, is convex and positively homogeneous, and equals $\zeta(I)V_b(\zeta/\zeta(I))$ when the mass is positive, with $\W_b(0)=0$.

At a fixed state and mode, the accepted contribution to $Q(g)$ is linear in $g$. The failed parameter measure is $f_R^{a,g}\pi_c(dR)$, affine in $g$, and its contribution is $e^{\lambda r_a^\dagger}\W_{b-1}(f^{a,g}\pi_c)$. Hence $Q$ is convex. Its weak-star continuity, including zero evidence, was proved above.

Put $h=(g-\eta)/(1-2\eta)$ and $g_t=\eta+(1-2\eta)\one_{\{h>t\}}$ for $0\le t<1$. Layer-cake gives $g=\int_0^1g_t\,dt$ weak-star. Applying the supremum-of-linear-functionals formula for $\W$ gives $Q(g)\le\int_0^1Q(g_t)\,dt$. For an optimal gate $g^*$ with value $M$, every integrand is at most $M$, so equality forces $Q(g_t^*)=M$ almost everywhere. As $s\downarrow t$, the indicators $\one_{h>s}$ converge to $\one_{h>t}$; dominated convergence gives right continuity in the weak-star topology, and hence of $Q(g_t^*)$. A value below $M$ at any $t<1$ would persist on a right interval, contradicting the almost-everywhere equality. Every such threshold is therefore optimal.

Start with the jointly Borel representative of an optimal feedback gate supplied above and take threshold $t=1/2$. The result is the jointly Borel function
$\eta+(1-2\eta)\one_{\{g^*(c,x)>1/2\}}$. It preserves the selected mode's optimum at each state. Backward induction supplies an optimal endpoint feedback controller for the original experiment.
\end{proof}

This theorem does not claim a finite number of acceptance intervals for arbitrary horizons or rewards. The next section proves an exact boundary and value formula in a specified nontrivial terminal problem. The support-function, endpoint and Bellman results are not polynomial-time algorithms for a general continuous value function.
