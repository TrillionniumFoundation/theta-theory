\section{Selection, entropy, and conjugate fields}\label{sec:selection}
\subsection{An operational source rule}
\begin{theorem}[Positive continuous selection characters]\label{thm:character}
If $w:\R^d\to(0,\infty)$ is continuous, $w(0)=1$, and
$w(c+c')=w(c)w(c')$, then $w(c)=\exp\langle\xi,c\rangle$ for one $\xi\in\R^d$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $g=\log w$. Additivity yields $g(nc)=ng(c)$, $g(-c)=-g(c)$, and $g(c/n)=g(c)/n$, hence rational homogeneity. Continuity extends it to real homogeneity. Set $\xi_i=g(e_i)$ and expand $c$ in the coordinate basis. Uniqueness follows by evaluation at $e_i$.
\end{proof}

The multiplication rule describes an operation on additive quantities, not independence of time segments. Cross-segment selection interactions invalidate this particular character assumption unless retained in the source or in an enlarged state. Arbitrary bounded path sources are allowed below without claiming they are linear characters.

\subsection{The exact variational identity}
Fix a path probability $P$ and a bounded measurable real $V$. Write
\[
 Z_P(V)=Pe^V,\quad\Psi_P(V)=\log Z_P(V),\quad Q^V=e^VP/Z_P(V).
\]
Then $e^{\inf V}\le Z\le e^{\sup V}$.

\begin{theorem}[Gibbs identity with its exact gap]\label{thm:gibbs}
For $Q\ll P$,
\begin{equation}
 D(Q\Vert Q^V)=D(Q\Vert P)-QV+\log Pe^V.
 \label{eq:gibbs-gap}
\end{equation}
Consequently
$\log Pe^V=\sup_{Q\ll P}\{QV-D(Q\Vert P)\}$, uniquely attained by $Q^V$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The log density is $\log(dQ/dQ^V)=\log(dQ/dP)-V+\log Z$. Since $V$ is bounded, integration has no subtraction of two infinities; infinite entropy gives infinite entropy on both sides after the finite correction. Nonnegativity follows by integrating $t\log t-t+1\ge0$. Equality occurs only at density one with respect to $Q^V$, proving uniqueness and attainment.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[All finite source cumulants]\label{prop:cumulants}
For bounded $C_1,\ldots,C_d,B$ and $V_\xi=\xi\cdot C+B$, $Z(\xi)$ is entire and $\Psi$ is smooth on real parameters. At a real $\xi$,
\[
 \partial_i\Psi=Q^\xi C_i,\qquad \partial_{ij}\Psi=\Cov_{Q^\xi}(C_i,C_j).
\]
For directions $v_1,\ldots,v_r$,
\begin{equation}
 D^r\Psi[v_1,\ldots,v_r]
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak P_r}(-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!
 \prod_{A\in\pi}E_{Q^\xi}\prod_{j\in A}(v_j\cdot C).
 \label{eq:cumulants}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Boundedness supplies uniform exponential domination on complex compact sets, so the exponential series and every finite derivative can be integrated. Divide $Z(\xi+\sum t_jv_j)$ by $Z(\xi)$ and expand the logarithm near $t=0$. In the coefficient of $t_1\cdots t_r$, a partition with $k$ blocks is counted $k!$ times by ordered blocks and the logarithm contributes $(-1)^{k-1}/k$. This gives \eqref{eq:cumulants}. No global complex logarithm is asserted: an entire $Z$ can have complex zeros.
\end{proof}

The covariance kernel consists of directions $v$ for which $v\cdot C$ is almost surely constant. On a complement where the covariance is positive definite, $\xi\mapsto Q^\xi C$ has a local inverse with derivative $\Cov(C)^{-1}$. Positivity is a model property, not a consequence of the word ``noise.''

\begin{proposition}[Mean constraints with a retained bias, and event constraints]\label{prop:constraints}
Write explicitly $Q^{\xi,B}=e^{\xi\cdot C+B}P/Z$, where $C,B$ are bounded and $Z=Pe^{\xi\cdot C+B}$. If $Q^{\xi,B}C=c$, then for every $Q\ll P$ satisfying $QC=c$,
\[
 D(Q\Vert P)-QB=D(Q\Vert Q^{\xi,B})+\xi\cdot c-\log Z.
\]
Thus $Q^{\xi,B}$ uniquely minimizes $D(Q\Vert P)-QB$ on the mean-constraint class. In the explicitly bias-free case $B=0$, $Q^{\xi,0}$ uniquely minimizes bare entropy and
\[
 D(Q\Vert P)=D(Q\Vert Q^{\xi,0})+\xi\cdot c-\log Pe^{\xi\cdot C}.
\]
Bare-entropy minimization also follows when $QB$ is constant on the entire constraint class. If $P(A)>0$, then among laws supported on $A$, the minimizer is $P_A=P(\cdot\mid A)$ and
\[
 D(Q\Vert P)=D(Q\Vert P_A)-\log P(A).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:gibbs-gap} with $V=\xi\cdot C+B$, retain the term $QB$, and use $QC=c$. Boundedness prevents an undefined subtraction of infinities. Relative-entropy nonnegativity proves uniqueness for the biased objective; setting $B=0$ or keeping its expectation constant gives the stated bare-entropy cases. The event identity uses $dP_A/dP=\one_A/P(A)$ on the support of $Q$ and the same argument. A continuous exact-value constraint with probability zero requires a disintegration or a proved shrinking-window limit, not substitution of zero in the displayed formula.
\end{proof}

For example, $P=(1/2,1/2)$, $C=0$ and $B=(0,\log3)$ give $Q^{0,B}=(1/4,3/4)$, which minimizes the biased objective but not bare entropy. This explicitly resolves finding M1 without discarding the general biased-source family.

A mean constraint and an event constraint are different finite experiments. Their possible asymptotic equivalence requires a separate theorem \cite{ChetriteTouchette}. A scalar field is obtained only after a physical direction has been chosen. More generally, support fields of a rate function may be nonunique; Legendre duality alone does not establish equivalence with an unproved microscopic rate.

\subsection{Unbounded observables and gauge directions}
The definition of $Q^V$ extends whenever $0<Pe^V<\infty$. A sufficient local condition for $r$ derivatives of $\xi\mapsto Pe^{\xi\cdot C+B}$ is
\[
 E_P[(1+|C|^r)e^{\xi_0\cdot C+B+\delta|C|}]<\infty
\]
for some $\delta>0$. It dominates all derivatives near $\xi_0$ and justifies the same formulas. Infinite $QV$ and infinite entropy must not be subtracted without an extended-value convention; the bounded-source statements avoid this ambiguity.

If a current increment is $u(Tz)-u(z)$, its accumulated value is an endpoint difference. It may vanish after division by a long time under additional endpoint control, but it changes a finite-time weight. Likewise, collision invariants create source degeneracies. Quotienting such directions is legitimate only after their endpoint and constraint effects have been identified; finite-time data are not discarded on the basis of an asymptotic slogan.
