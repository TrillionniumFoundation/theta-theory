\section{A nonconjugate Lorentz family and a moving terminal event}\label{sec:lorentz-local}
\subsection{The actual mechanical geometry}
Let $e_1=(1,0)$, $e_2=(1/2,\sqrt3/2)$, $\Lambda=\Z e_1+\Z e_2$, and
$A_\Lambda=\sqrt3/2$. On $\T_\Lambda^2=\R^2/\Lambda$ remove the disk of radius
\[
 R\in I=[9/20,47/100].
\]
The lifted disks are $\overline B(\ell,R)$, $\ell\in\Lambda$. A unit-speed particle follows straight flights and Euclidean elastic reflections. This is the periodic family used in the earlier repository work \cite{RepoLorentz}; all geometric facts used here are rederived.

\begin{proposition}[Geometry, flux, and a strict collision gap]\label{prop:lorentz-geometry}
The family has a uniform finite horizon and disk separation at least
$d_*=1-2(47/100)=3/50$. Consecutive regular collisions are separated by at least $d_*$. The flow exists for all times outside a phase-volume null set, and the collision section in coordinates $\alpha\in\R/(2\pi\Z)$, $|\phi|<\pi/2$ has invariant probability
\[
 d\mu_{\rm coll}=\frac1{4\pi}\cos\phi\dd\alpha\dd\phi,
\]
independent of $R$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The shortest nonzero lattice vector has length one, so the disks are disjoint with the displayed separation. For a rational lattice direction with primitive vector $v$, parallel lattice rows are separated by $A_\Lambda/|v|\le\sqrt3/2$. Every line in that direction lies within $\sqrt3/4<9/20$ of a row and therefore intersects a disk interior. An irrational-direction line is dense on the torus and also enters the disk. If arbitrarily long collision-free rays existed at the smallest radius, compactness of initial torus position and direction would produce an infinite ray avoiding the open disk, a contradiction. Increasing $R$ cannot increase flight length, so the horizon is uniform.

An outgoing ray from a convex disk cannot return to the same lifted disk without another collision. Every next disk is at least $d_*$ away. Therefore at most $1+\lfloor T/d_*\rfloor$ collisions occur in a finite interval, excluding collision accumulation. The next-flight tangency condition has zero volume/flux measure: for a fixed position and a fixed candidate disk it selects finitely many tangent directions, and Fubini applies; section tangencies are handled by local regular collision charts. Finite horizon bounds the candidate labels for one step. Regular return branches preserve flux by Proposition~\ref{prop:reflection}, so finitely iterated singular preimages remain null. Countably many bounded time windows yield an all-time full-measure flow.

The unnormalized collision flux is $\cos\phi\dd s\dd\phi$. On the circle $\dd s=R\dd\alpha$ and total flux is $4\pi R$. Normalize to obtain the probability. This calculation does not assert a spectral theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Mean physical clock and marked nonconjugacy]\label{prop:roof}
With $A_R=A_\Lambda-\pi R^2$,
\[
 \bar\tau_R=\frac{A_R}{2R},\qquad
 \bar\tau_R'=-\frac{A_\Lambda}{2R^2}-\frac\pi2<0.
\]
The marked normal two-bounce periods between disks separated by $1$ and $\sqrt3$ are $2(1-2R)$ and $2(\sqrt3-2R)$. Their ratio changes strictly with $R$, so no one constant time rescaling can conjugate the family while preserving these orbit labels.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Decompose the unit-speed phase volume into outgoing collision flux and elapsed flight time. Its total mass is $2\pi A_R$, while section flux is $4\pi R$, yielding the mean roof. Differentiate directly. The nearest-disk orbit is unobstructed. The line joining centers separated by $e_1+e_2$ stays at distance $1/2>R$ from the intervening lattice centers, so that orbit is also unobstructed. The ratio $(\sqrt3-2R)/(1-2R)$ has derivative $2(\sqrt3-1)/(1-2R)^2>0$. This excludes the specified marked constant-time equivalence, not every possible unmarked topological conjugacy.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A radius-independent preparation crossing an event threshold}
Take initial position $(x,y)$ and velocity $(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)$ in
\[
 x\in(499/1000,501/1000),\quad |y|<1/1000,\quad|\phi|<1/1000.
\]
Prepare them with density $\rho_x(x)\rho_y(y)\rho_\phi(\phi)$, each factor smooth, flat at its support boundary, positive in the interval, and normalized. A rescaled $\exp[-1/(1-u^2)]\one_{|u|<1}$ supplies an explicit choice. This is positive phase volume on a fixed energy shell and does not depend on $R$.

Let
\[
 \ell=(1-x)\cos\phi-y\sin\phi,\qquad
 b=(1-x)\sin\phi+y\cos\phi.
\]
The first collision with the disk centered at $e_1$ has time
\begin{equation}
 \tau_R=\ell-\sqrt{R^2-b^2}.
 \label{eq:hit-time}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform prepared geometry]\label{lem:tube}
On the stated preparation and $R\in I$, the first collision is with the $e_1$ disk, $0.028<\tau_R<0.053$, $|b|\le0.001501$, and $\sqrt{R^2-b^2}>0.44999$. The absolute incident normal speed is greater than $0.99$. Before $T=1/25$, there can be at most this one collision.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The distances from the initial position to the origin and $e_1$ are at least $0.499>0.47$. Other lattice rows are vertically at least $\sqrt3/2-0.001>0.865$ away. Using $|\sin\phi|\le0.001$ and $\cos\phi\ge1-10^{-6}/2$ gives
$0.498998<\ell<0.501001$ and the displayed $b$ bound. Since
$0.45^2-0.001501^2>0.44999^2$, the square-root bound and the time bounds follow from \eqref{eq:hit-time}. Up to that first root the horizontal coordinate stays between $0.499$ and $0.554$ and the vertical coordinate has absolute value less than $0.0011$, excluding every other disk. At impact the normal speed is $\sqrt{R^2-b^2}/R>0.99$. The collision gap is $0.06>T=0.04$, so a second collision is impossible in the observation window.
\end{proof}

Restrict $R$ to $I_0=[919/2000,921/2000]$ and record
$C_R=\one_{\{\tau_R\le T\}}$. Solving the terminal intersection equation gives
\begin{equation}
 C_R=\one_{\{x\ge\chi_R(y,\phi)\}},\qquad
 \chi_R=1-T\cos\phi-\sqrt{R^2-(y+T\sin\phi)^2}.
 \label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
This uses the unique entry root; the actual trajectory is reflected after that root. Put $B_T=y+T\sin\phi$ and $s_T=\sqrt{R^2-B_T^2}$. Then
\[
 \chi_R'=-R/s_T,\quad\chi_R''=B_T^2/s_T^3,\quad
 \chi_R'''=-3B_T^2R/s_T^5.
\]
The bounds $|B_T|\le0.00104$, $R-s_T<1.18\times10^{-6}$, and
$T(1-\cos\phi)\le2\times10^{-8}$ place $\chi_R$ inside $(0.4994,0.5006)$, with $1\le R/s_T<1.001$.

\begin{theorem}[Unweighted terminal-collision response]\label{thm:local-hit}
The hit probability is
\begin{align}
 p(R)&=\int\rho_y\rho_\phi\int_{\chi_R}^{0.501}\rho_x(x)\dd x\dd y\dd\phi,\\
 p'(R)&=\int\rho_y\rho_\phi\rho_x(\chi_R)R/s_T\dd y\dd\phi>0.
 \label{eq:local-p}
\end{align}
It stays strictly between zero and one on $I_0$, and
$m_x\le p'(R)\le1.001\norm{\rho_x}_\infty$ for
$m_x=\min_{[0.4994,0.5006]}\rho_x>0$. For every finite $r$,
\begin{equation}
 p^{(r)}(R)=-\int\rho_y\rho_\phi
 \sum_{k=1}^rB_{r,k}(\chi_R',\ldots,\chi_R^{(r-k+1)})
 \rho_x^{(k-1)}(\chi_R)\dd y\dd\phi.
 \label{eq:local-all}
\end{equation}
The preparation-coordinate measure $\rho\one_{x\ge\chi_R}\dd x\dd y\dd\phi$ is strongly $C^r$ in $H^{-s}$ on a fixed compact embedding for $s>3/2+r$, without added depth weights.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Integrate first in $x$. Differentiation of the lower endpoint gives \eqref{eq:local-p} and its bounds. Positive mass to the right of $0.5006$ bounds $p$ away from zero; positive mass to the left of $0.4994$ bounds it away from one. The primitive $A(u)=\int_u^{0.501}\rho_x(x)dx$ has $A^{(k)}=-\rho_x^{(k-1)}$. Its composition with $\chi_R$ gives \eqref{eq:local-all} by the Bell formula. All derivatives are bounded on the compact support because the square root has a positive uniform margin.

Distributionally,
\begin{equation}
 \partial_R^r\one_{x\ge\chi_R}
 =\sum_{k=1}^r(-1)^kB_{r,k}(\chi_R',\ldots)\delta_x^{(k-1)}(x-\chi_R).
 \label{eq:local-current}
\end{equation}
Differentiate the first-order identity $-\chi_R'\delta(x-\chi_R)$ and use the Bell recursion to obtain every order; Appendix~\ref{app:jets} verifies the signs. Multiplication by smooth $\rho$ and integration over $y,\phi$ are Bochner operations on the moving Dirac derivatives. Lemma~\ref{lem:dirac} provides the strong norm derivatives. No probability factor is assigned to the physical interface.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Physical terminal state, not just the event label}
Before impact $z_T^-=(q+Tv,v)$. After impact,
\[
 n_R=(q+\tau_Rv-e_1)/R,\quad v_R^+=v-2(v\cdot n_R)n_R,
\]
\[
 q_T^+=q+\tau_Rv+(T-\tau_R)v_R^+.
\]
The radius derivatives include
\[
 \tau_R'=-R/\sqrt{R^2-b^2},\quad n_R'=(\tau_R'v-n_R)/R,
\]
\[
 (v_R^+)'=-2[(v\cdot n_R')n_R+(v\cdot n_R)n_R'].
\]
For a smooth terminal-state test $F$, differentiate its interior branch expression and add
\[
 \int\rho_y\rho_\phi\rho_x(\chi_R)\chi_R'
 [F(z_T^-)-F(z_T^+)]\dd y\dd\phi.
\]
At the cutoff, positions coincide but velocities have a reflection jump. A position test can have zero interface jump while a velocity test does not. The set $\tau_R=T$ has zero preparation measure, yet its motion produces the nonzero expectation derivative. This explains why differentiating an almost-everywhere constant event indicator pointwise would miss the response.
