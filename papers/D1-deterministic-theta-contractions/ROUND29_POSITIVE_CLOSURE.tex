\section{Round-twenty-nine reconstruction: positive-support phase asymptotics and epi-argmax control}
\label{sec:r29-d1}

Morse--Bott integration is used only for finite-dimensional mechanical state
variables that are actually integrated.  A policy is optimized, never
integrated, so no policy-manifold volume factor appears.  Zero prior phases
are excluded from every phase maximum, component rates are defined from a
single labelled rate, and near-optimal policies are handled by a uniform
second-order epi-development.

\subsection{Finite-dimensional mixed local input}

Fix a finite vector of upstream observables
\[
 \xi_N=(K_N,Y_N)\in\Z^{d_Z}\times\R^{d_R},                \tag{D1.1}
\]
chosen from coordinates for which A2 or B1 proves a source-uniform mixed local
theorem.  No unlisted infinite-dimensional path coordinate is integrated in
the coefficient below.  Under phase $j$, assume the labelled local density
has the $C^6$ expansion
\[
 P_N\{J_N=j,K_N=k,Y_N\in dy\}
 =e^{-N\mathcal I_j(k/N,y)}N^{-\lambda_j}
 [a_j+N^{-1/2}b_j+N^{-1}c_j+O(N^{-3/2})]d\varkappa_N,    \tag{D1.2}
\]
uniformly with four derivatives.  Here $d\varkappa_N$ is counting measure in
$k$ and Lebesgue measure in $y$, including the lattice covolume.

Let the continuous minimizer set $\mathcal M_j$ be a compact $C^5$ manifold
of dimension $r_j$, and suppose the normal Hessian $H_j(y)$ is uniformly
positive.  Lattice directions are treated by the A2/B1 lattice sum, while only
continuous normal directions are integrated in tubular coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Mixed lattice--Morse--Bott coefficient]
\label{thm:r29-d1-weight}
For a rate-separated phase cell,
\[
 P_N(\mathcal C_{j,N})
 =e^{-N\gamma_j}N^{-\kappa_j}
 [C_j+N^{-1/2}D_j+O(N^{-1})],                             \tag{D1.3}
\]
where
\[
 \kappa_j=\lambda_j+{d_R-r_j\over2}+{d_Z\over2},          \tag{D1.4}
\]
\[
 C_j=\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_j)(2\pi)^{(d_R-r_j+d_Z)/2}
 \int_{\mathcal M_j}{a_j(y,0)\over\sqrt{\det H_j(y)}}
 \,d\operatorname{vol}_{\mathcal M_j}(y)>0.              \tag{D1.5}
\]
The coefficient $D_j$ is a different symbol from every dimension and contains
the cubic/quartic normal form, tubular Jacobian, lattice Edgeworth term, and
$b_j$ contribution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First apply the mixed lattice local theorem to the $d_Z$ coordinates; its
Gaussian sum supplies the covolume and $N^{-d_Z/2}$.  In the continuous
variables, use a finite tubular cover of $\mathcal M_j$, put
$n=N^{-1/2}\eta$, and expand the rate through fourth order and the amplitude
through second order.  The $C^6$/four-derivative budget gives a uniform
integrable remainder $O(N^{-1})$ after the odd terms are combined with
$b_j$.  A partition of unity glues the charts, and the positive rate gap makes
the complement exponentially smaller.  No policy variable occurs in this
integration.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Noncircular labelled rates and prior support}

Let the upstream labelled LDP have one rate
$\mathcal I(j,z)$.  Define
\[
 \gamma_j=\inf_z\mathcal I(j,z),\qquad
 I_j(z)=\mathcal I(j,z)-\gamma_j,\qquad
 \gamma_*=\min_j\gamma_j.                                \tag{D1.6}
\]
Then contraction gives
\[
 I(z)=\min_j\{\gamma_j-\gamma_*+I_j(z)\}.                  \tag{D1.7}
\]
There is no circular definition of $I_j$ in terms of itself.

For a prior $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_J)$ put
\[
 J_+(w)=\{j:w_j>0\}.                                      \tag{D1.8}
\]
Only phases in $J_+(w)$ enter a finite-scale log-sum-exp.  If weights depend on
$N$ with
$-N^{-1}\log w_{j,N}\to\omega_j$, the shifted exponent is
$G_j-\omega_j$; a zero weight corresponds to $\omega_j=+\infty$.

\subsection{Compact common policies as strategic measures}

Let the augmented belief state be compact on the C1 invariant shell.  A
relaxed feedback policy induces a strategic measure on state--action--observation
paths.  Let $\mathfrak A$ be the set of strategic measures satisfying the
causal disintegration identities and the closed randomized-excitation
constraint of C1.

\begin{lemma}[Closed adapted policy compactness]
\label{lem:r29-d1-policy}
The set $\mathfrak A$ is sequentially compact in the stable topology.  Every
limit retains adaptedness and the common-action constraint.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Tightness follows from compact state and action spaces.  The causal identities
are equalities against bounded continuous functions of past coordinates and
are therefore closed under weak convergence.  The lower action-probability
bounds are closed inequalities.  Disintegration of a limiting strategic
measure gives a relaxed adapted policy.  Feller continuity is used to pass the
state-transition identity, not as a substitute for compactness or adaptedness.
\end{proof}

For $j\in J_+(w)$ and common policy $\alpha$, let
\[
 Z_{j,N}^\alpha
 =E_j^\alpha e^{NF_j(\xi_N)},\qquad
 G_j(\alpha)=\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-1}\log Z_{j,N}^\alpha.  \tag{D1.9}
\]
The finite-dimensional source family in (D1.1) and compactness of strategic
measures give uniform exponential tightness.  Assume the A2/B1 local charts
are uniform on the compact image of $\mathfrak A$ under these finitely many
sources.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform controlled finite-dimensional expansion]
\label{thm:r29-d1-uniform}
Uniformly in $\alpha\in\mathfrak A$ and $j\in J_+(w)$,
\[
 Z_{j,N}^\alpha
 =e^{NG_j(\alpha)}N^{-\kappa_j(\alpha)}
 [c_j(\alpha)+N^{-1/2}d_j^{(1)}(\alpha)+O(N^{-1})],        \tag{D1.10}
\]
where $G_j,\kappa_j,c_j$ are continuous on each finite stratification cell,
$c_j>0$, and the expansion is compatible on cell boundaries by taking the
minimum polynomial exponent and adding tied amplitudes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite family of source coordinates maps the compact strategic-measure
space into a compact parameter set of the upstream local theorem.  Uniform
exponential tightness removes its complement.  On each finite phase/source
stratum, Theorem~\ref{thm:r29-d1-weight} applies with uniform derivative
bounds.  A finite partition of unity and the exact lattice supports give
(D1.10).  This theorem does not claim a local expansion for unspecified path
coordinates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-scale common-policy value}

For one common policy define
\[
 Z_N^\alpha(w)=\sum_{j\in J_+(w)}w_j Z_{j,N}^\alpha,
 \qquad V_N(w)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak A}\log Z_N^\alpha(w).            \tag{D1.11}
\]
Put
\[
 G(\alpha)=\max_{j\in J_+(w)}G_j(\alpha),\qquad
 \mathfrak A_*=
 \operatorname*{argmax}_{\alpha\in\mathfrak A}G(\alpha).               \tag{D1.12}
\]
For a fixed policy, let
\[
 J_*(\alpha)=\{j\in J_+(w):G_j(\alpha)=G(\alpha)\},       \tag{D1.13}
\]
\[
 \kappa(\alpha)=\min_{j\in J_*(\alpha)}\kappa_j(\alpha),
 \quad
 C(\alpha,w)=\sum_{\substack{j\in J_*(\alpha)\\
                     \kappa_j(\alpha)=\kappa(\alpha)}}w_jc_j(\alpha).
                                                                    \tag{D1.14}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Phase-blind leading scalar]
\label{prop:r29-d1-leading}
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}{V_N(w)\over N}
 =\sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak A}G(\alpha)
 =\max_{j\in J_+(w)}\sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak A}G_j(\alpha).             \tag{D1.15}
\]
The equality is scalar and phase-blind; the common-policy constraint is kept
in (D1.11) and its subleading argmax, not falsely attributed to (D1.15).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:r29-d1-uniform} is uniform, and finite log-sum-exp gives
$G(\alpha)$.  Compactness permits the supremum to pass through the uniform
limit.  The last equality is the elementary identity
$\sup_\alpha\max_j=\max_j\sup_\alpha$ for a finite set of phases.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Epi-argmax rather than policy integration}

Assume the following uniform second-order epi-development:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(E\arabic*)]
\item if $N[G_*-G(\alpha_N)]=O(\log N)$, then
      $\operatorname{dist}(\alpha_N,\mathfrak A_*)\to0$;
\item $\kappa$ is lower semicontinuous near $\mathfrak A_*$ and
      $C(\cdot,w)$ is continuous and positive on the stratum where
      $\kappa$ is minimal;
\item the remainder in
\[
 \log Z_N^\alpha(w)=NG(\alpha)-\kappa(\alpha)\log N
                    +\log C(\alpha,w)+r_N(\alpha)          \tag{D1.16}
\]
      satisfies $\sup_{N[G_*-G(\alpha)]\le L\log N}|r_N(\alpha)|\to0$
      for every fixed $L$.
\end{enumerate}

These conditions are the policy analogue of a second epi-derivative.  They
control near maximizers without assigning them a fictitious integration
volume.

\begin{theorem}[Lexicographic common-policy selection]
\label{thm:r29-d1-subleading}
Let
\[
 \kappa^\dagger=
 \min_{\alpha\in\mathfrak A_*}\kappa(\alpha),\qquad
 C^\dagger(w)=
 \max_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathfrak A_*\\
                  \kappa(\alpha)=\kappa^\dagger}}C(\alpha,w).            \tag{D1.17}
\]
Then
\[
 V_N(w)=NG_*-\kappa^\dagger\log N+
        \log C^\dagger(w)+o(1).                          \tag{D1.18}
\]
There is no Morse--Bott factor from the policy set.  A smooth manifold of
maximizing policies only enlarges the deterministic argmax set in (D1.17).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Policies with $N(G_*-G)>L\log N$ are smaller than the candidate value by a
factor $N^{-L}$ and may be removed.  On the remaining set, (E1) moves every
sequence to $\mathfrak A_*$.  Lower semicontinuity in (E2) prevents a nearby
nonmaximizer from acquiring a smaller polynomial exponent than the minimum on
$\mathfrak A_*$.  Insert (D1.16), first minimize $\kappa$ and then maximize
$C$ on the surviving compact set.  Uniformity (E3) gives the matching upper
and lower bounds.  The proof uses a supremum throughout and performs no
integration over policies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform complex charts and labelled coexistence}

On the compact selected set in (D1.17), continuity and strict positivity give
\[
 \inf_{\alpha,j}c_j(\alpha,0)>0.                           \tag{D1.19}
\]
Uniform analytic derivative bounds then give one smaller complex chart on
which each active amplitude is zero-free.  On a strict phase region, Rouche's
theorem transfers zero-freeness to the finite phase sum; on coexistence
surfaces the finite analytic sum and its genuine zeros are retained.

Normalizing (D1.3) over $j\in J_+(w)$ gives the labelled Gaussian limit
\[
 \mathcal L\bigl(J_N,\sqrt N(\xi_N-m_{J_N})\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow
 \sum_{j\in J_+(w)}\rho_j\delta_j\otimes N(0,\Sigma_j),  \tag{D1.20}
\]
with zero-weight phases absent and exponentially varying priors incorporated
through their shifted rates.

The phase synthesis now rests on a finite-dimensional upstream local theorem,
a direct labelled rate, closed strategic measures, positive prior support,
and an epi-argmax expansion that respects the mathematical difference between
integration over mechanical variables and optimization over policies.
