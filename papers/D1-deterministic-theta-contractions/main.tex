\begin{abstract}
A phase is introduced as a genuine positive latent variable with
conditional microscopic path laws.  The joint labelled experiment has a good
large-deviation principle and contracts to the minimum of the phase costs and
component rates.  Restricted pressures use one consistent sign and the exact
finite-volume value is a log-sum-exp.  Dynamic consistency is restored by
carrying the adapted phase posterior in the state; the marginal log-sum alone
is not called a semigroup.  Quantitative complex dominance gives phase-local
response charts, and second-order component weights determine labelled
Gaussian mixtures at coexistence.
\end{abstract}
