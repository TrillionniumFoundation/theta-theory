\section{Round-six positive closure: projective steep pressures and deterministic commutation}
\label{sec:r6-d1}

The global rate is derived from full finite-dimensional pressure theorems on an
increasing separating source algebra.  No local analytic ball is evaluated
outside its domain, and no rate-dense exposed-point lower bound is assumed.
The finite-dimensional lower bounds follow from global differentiability and
steepness of each projected pressure; exponential tightness then supplies the
projective full LDP.  Every local likelihood is centred at the exact
finite-volume mean.

\subsection{A separating projective source system}

Let \(E\) be a Hausdorff path state space and let
\[
\pi_m:E\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{d_m},
\qquad m\ge1,
\]
be continuous maps such that \(\pi_m=R_{m,n}\pi_n\) for \(m<n\), and the
family separates points and generates the topology on compact sets.  Let
\(X_\varepsilon\) be \(E\)-valued and let
\(\mu_\varepsilon\to\infty\).  Define
\[
Q_{\varepsilon,m}(\theta)=
\frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}\log
\mathbb E
\exp\{\mu_\varepsilon\theta\cdot\pi_m(X_\varepsilon)\}.
\]

The hypotheses are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(P\arabic*)]
\item for every \(m\), \(Q_{\varepsilon,m}\to Q_m\) locally uniformly on
\(\mathbb C^{d_m}\), and all finite pressures are locally uniformly bounded
on complex compact sets;
\item \(Q_m\) is finite, differentiable on all of \(\mathbb R^{d_m}\), and
steep in the sense that
\(|\nabla Q_m(\theta_n)|\to\infty\) whenever
\(|\theta_n|\to\infty\) along a direction not annihilated by the support;
\item the pressures are projectively compatible:
\(Q_m(\theta)=Q_n(R_{m,n}^T\theta)\);
\item \((X_\varepsilon)\) is exponentially tight in \(E\).
\end{enumerate}
For a platform with conservation directions, the statements are read on the
affine support quotient, where steepness is required.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite-volume normalization]
\label{prop:r6-d1-normalization}
For directions \(h,k\in\mathbb R^{d_m}\),
\[
DQ_{\varepsilon,m}(\theta)h
=\mathbb E_{\theta,\varepsilon}[h\cdot\pi_m(X_\varepsilon)],
\]
\[
D^2Q_{\varepsilon,m}(\theta)[h,k]
=\mu_\varepsilon
\operatorname{Cov}_{\theta,\varepsilon}
(h\cdot\pi_m(X_\varepsilon),
 k\cdot\pi_m(X_\varepsilon)).
\]
All derivatives converge locally uniformly to those of \(Q_m\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the finite logarithmic moment generating function.  One factor
of \(\mu_\varepsilon\) remains in the second derivative after the outer
normalization.  Normal-family convergence and Cauchy's formula give convergence
of every derivative on smaller complex compact sets.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The projective full LDP without an exposed-density hypothesis}

Put
\[
I_m(y)=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb R^{d_m}}
\{\theta\cdot y-Q_m(\theta)+Q_m(0)\}
\]
and
\[
I(x)=\sup_m I_m(\pi_mx).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Projective steep-pressure LDP]
\label{thm:r6-d1-projective}
Under (P1)--(P4), \((X_\varepsilon)\) satisfies a full LDP on \(E\) with good
rate \(I\).  Equivalently, if
\(\mathcal D=\bigcup_m\pi_m^*(\mathbb R^{d_m})\) is the proved cylinder source
algebra, then
\[
I(x)=\sup_{F\in\mathcal D}
\{F(x)-Q(F)+Q(0)\}.
\]
The theorem does not use a pressure outside \(\mathcal D\) and does not assume
rate-density of exposed points.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(m\), the finite-dimensional Gärtner--Ellis theorem applies because
\(Q_m\) is finite, differentiable, and steep on the support quotient.  It
gives a full LDP for \(\pi_m(X_\varepsilon)\) with good rate \(I_m\), including
the lower bound at every point of its effective domain.  Projective
compatibility makes these finite-dimensional rates consistent.  The
Dawson--Gärtner theorem gives the projective-limit LDP with rate
\(\sup_mI_m\) in the initial projective topology.  Exponential tightness in
\(E\) upgrades the weak projective upper bound to the full topology and makes
the rate good.  Rewriting the supremum over \((m,\theta)\) gives the cylinder
source formula.
\end{proof}

For the Sinai chain, A2 supplies the global finite-cylinder twisted pressures
and A3 supplies exponential tightness.  For the hard-sphere chain, B2 source
sewing supplies every finite bounded real cylinder source, B3 supplies strict
quotient differentiability, and B2 compact containment supplies (P4).

\subsection{Typed contractions}

Let \(T:E\to F\) be continuous, or have an exponentially good sequence of
continuous approximants.  Let its finite coordinates factor through the
projective system.

\begin{theorem}[Pressure/rate contraction commutation]
\label{thm:r6-d1-contraction}
The contracted variables satisfy a full LDP with
\[
I_T(y)=\inf_{Tx=y}I(x).
\]
For every finite source \(\eta\) on \(F\),
\[
Q_T(\eta)=Q(T^*\eta).
\]
On a local analytic chart,
\[
DQ_T=T\,DQ,
\qquad
D^2Q_T=T(D^2Q)T^*.
\]
The same statements hold for exponentially good approximations by the
extended contraction theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The rate formula is the contraction principle applied to
Theorem~\ref{thm:r6-d1-projective}.  The pressure identity holds exactly at
finite volume because
\(\langle\eta,TX\rangle=\langle T^*\eta,X\rangle\).  Differentiate the local
analytic identity.  Exponentially good approximants have the same pressure and
rate limits by exponential equivalence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Source-dependent conditioning}

Let \(C:E\to\mathbb R^k\) be a continuous finite-coordinate constraint and
let \(a_\varepsilon\to a\).  Assume the B1-type exact coefficient property:
for every bounded cylinder source \(F\), there is a finite multiplier
\(\lambda_{F,\varepsilon}\) satisfying
\[
D_\lambda Q_\varepsilon(F+C^*\lambda_{F,\varepsilon})=a_\varepsilon
\]
and the corresponding shell has logarithmic probability
\(o(\mu_\varepsilon)\).  Assume the constraint Hessian is positive on the
support quotient.

Define
\[
Q^a(F)=
\inf_\lambda\{Q(F+C^*\lambda)-\lambda\cdot a\}
-\inf_\lambda\{Q(C^*\lambda)-\lambda\cdot a\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact conditioning commutation]
\label{thm:r6-d1-conditioning}
The microscopic conditional laws satisfy a full LDP with good rate
\[
I^a(x)=
\begin{cases}
I(x)-\inf_{Cy=a}I(y),&Cx=a,\\
+\infty,&Cx\ne a.
\end{cases}
\]
Their cylinder pressure is \(Q^a\).  On a regular source chart the unique
limiting multiplier is source dependent and
\[
D^2Q^a=
Q_{FF}-Q_{F\lambda}Q_{\lambda\lambda}^{-1}Q_{\lambda F}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Change measure at the exact finite-volume multiplier.  The coefficient
hypothesis makes the shell probability subexponential under that law, so the
conditional cylinder pressure is the displayed finite-dimensional infimum.
The finite projected conditional laws therefore satisfy Gärtner--Ellis with
that pressure.  Projective passage and conditional exponential tightness give
the full LDP.  Alternatively, the rate follows by restricting the good
unconditioned rate to the closed constraint surface and subtracting its
minimum.  Strict constraint convexity gives the unique multiplier.
Differentiating the saddle equation and then the envelope gives the Schur
complement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conditioning and contraction in either order}

Let \(T:E\to F\) and \(C:E\to\mathbb R^k\) be jointly continuous finite
coordinate maps.

\begin{theorem}[Closed joint-infimum interchange]
\label{thm:r6-d1-interchange}
The two routes
\[
E\xrightarrow{\text{condition }C=a}E_a\xrightarrow{T}F
\]
and
\[
E\xrightarrow{(T,C)}F\times\mathbb R^k
\xrightarrow{\text{condition second coordinate }a}F
\]
have the same good rate
\[
I_{T|a}(y)=
\inf_{Tx=y,\ Cx=a}I(x)-\inf_{Cx=a}I(x).
\]
On every regular finite-dimensional source chart this rate is the conjugate of
\(Q^a(T^*\eta)\).  No duality gap occurs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the contraction principle to the joint map \((T,C)\) and then restrict
to the closed slice with second coordinate \(a\).  Since \(I\) is good, every
finite joint infimum is attained and infimal projection is lower
semicontinuous on its effective domain.  This proves equality of the two
primal routes without a minimax interchange.  On a regular finite-dimensional
chart, the joint projected pressure is finite, differentiable, and steep; its
Legendre transform is the joint rate.  Eliminating the constraint multiplier
by the strictly convex saddle gives the conjugate of \(Q^a\), hence no gap.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact local likelihoods and Gaussian tangents}

Fix a projected source \(\theta\in\mathbb R^{d_m}\).  Put
\[
m_{\varepsilon,\theta}=DQ_{\varepsilon,m}(\theta),
\qquad
Z_{\varepsilon,\theta}=
\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}
(\pi_m(X_\varepsilon)-m_{\varepsilon,\theta}).
\]
For \(h\in\mathbb R^{d_m}\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_\varepsilon(h)=\exp\Bigl\{&
 h\cdot Z_{\varepsilon,\theta}\\
&-\mu_\varepsilon\bigl[
Q_{\varepsilon,m}(\theta+h/\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon})
-Q_{\varepsilon,m}(\theta)
-\mu_\varepsilon^{-1/2}DQ_{\varepsilon,m}(\theta)h
\bigr]
\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-centred likelihood and Gaussian shift]
\label{thm:r6-d1-lan}
For every finite \(\varepsilon\), \(L_\varepsilon(h)\) is exactly the
Radon--Nikodym density between the sources
\(\theta+h/\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}\) and \(\theta\); hence it has mean one.
Jointly,
\[
(Z_{\varepsilon,\theta},L_\varepsilon(h))
\Longrightarrow
\left(Z,
 e^{h\cdot Z-\frac12h^T\Sigma_\theta h}
\right),
\qquad
\Sigma_\theta=D^2Q_m(\theta).
\]
Under the tilted likelihood the limit mean is shifted by
\(\Sigma_\theta h\).  After conditioning, replace \(\Sigma_\theta\) by its
constraint Schur complement; after contraction, replace it by
\(T\Sigma_\theta T^*\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the definition of \(Z_{\varepsilon,\theta}\); the finite mean terms
cancel algebraically and leave
\[
\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}h\cdot\pi_m(X_\varepsilon)
-\mu_\varepsilon
[Q_{\varepsilon,m}(\theta+h/\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon})
-Q_{\varepsilon,m}(\theta)],
\]
the exact likelihood exponent.  Taylor expansion on a larger complex compact
and Proposition~\ref{prop:r6-d1-normalization} give the Gaussian
characteristic function and likelihood limit.  A slightly larger real source
ball gives an \(L^{1+\delta}\) bound for fixed \(h\), hence uniform
integrability.  Le Cam's third lemma gives the shift.  The conditioning and
contraction formulas follow from
Theorems~\ref{thm:r6-d1-conditioning} and
\ref{thm:r6-d1-contraction}.
\end{proof}

A process-valued Gaussian conclusion additionally requires tightness.  In the
hard-sphere platform it is supplied by the B3 localized-cumulant theorem; in
the Sinai platform it is supplied by the A4 enhanced conditional theorem.
The finite-dimensional analytic theorem above does not silently claim that
tightness.

\subsection{The dynamic rate--pressure--semigroup triangle}

Assume a path rate has the additive form
\[
I_{r,t}(x)=I_{r,s}(x|_{[r,s]})+I_{s,t}(x|_{[s,t]})
\]
on compatible concatenations and compact sublevels.  Define
\[
(S_{r,t}\Phi)(x_r)=
\sup_{x:x(r)=x_r}\{\Phi(x_t)-I_{r,t}(x)\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Dynamic commutation theorem]
\label{thm:r6-d1-dynamic}
The additive rate generates a nonlinear semigroup
\(S_{r,t}=S_{r,s}S_{s,t}\).  On every regular cylinder chart its infinitesimal
Hamiltonian is the pressure derivative dual to the local action.  Quadratic
expansion of the rate, pressure, and semigroup around an exposed phase gives
the same Gaussian covariance and the exponential-transform generator
\[
\mathfrak G^\vartheta F=
 b\cdot DF+\frac12A:D^2F+
\frac\vartheta2DF^TADF.
\]
Typed conditioning and contraction may be performed before or after this
quadratic tangent according to the Schur-complement and covariance-pushforward
rules above.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Split any admissible path at time \(s\).  Additivity gives one inequality in
the semigroup identity; concatenating near optimizers gives the reverse.
Fenchel duality on a short interval identifies the infinitesimal Hamiltonian.
Taylor expansion of the compatible finite-dimensional pressures yields the
Gaussian quadratic form, whose Legendre transform is the quadratic tangent of
the action.  Applying the logarithmic exponential transform to the Gaussian
generator adds the final quadratic-gradient term.  The finite-dimensional
matrix identities for conditioning and contraction commute with this
expansion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conditional calibration}

A smooth cash-additive and strongly time-consistent utility-generated certainty
equivalent is affine or entropic with a constant coefficient.  That coefficient
equals a mechanical preparation field only when the two normalized likelihood
cocycles are required to coincide in the same units.

\begin{theorem}[Round-six D1 closure]
\label{thm:r6-d1-main}
For the proved projective source systems of the Sinai and hard-sphere
platforms, full LDP formation commutes with typed contraction, exact
source-dependent conditioning, finite-dimensional pressure differentiation,
Gaussian likelihood formation, and additive dynamic programming.  The global
rate is derived from finite-dimensional steep pressures and exponential
tightness rather than an assumed exposed-density theorem, and every finite
likelihood is exactly centred at \(DQ_\varepsilon\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The global LDP is Theorem~\ref{thm:r6-d1-projective}.  Contraction,
conditioning, and their interchange are
Theorems~\ref{thm:r6-d1-contraction},
\ref{thm:r6-d1-conditioning}, and
\ref{thm:r6-d1-interchange}.  Exact likelihoods are
Theorem~\ref{thm:r6-d1-lan}, and dynamic commutation is
Theorem~\ref{thm:r6-d1-dynamic}.  The calibration qualification was stated in
the preceding subsection.
\end{proof}
