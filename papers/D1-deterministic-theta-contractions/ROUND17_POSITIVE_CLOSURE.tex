\section{Round-twenty-one positive closure: genuine finite-volume phases, shared-policy posterior control, and corrected coexistence limits}
\label{sec:r21-d1}

This section replaces the former Round-Seventeen source.  A phase is an actual
finite-volume latent label defined by a measurable partition of the original
microscopic experiment.  The sufficient information state contains the
phase-conditioned belief of every component.  A single observation-based
policy is optimized outside the phase mixture.  The Gaussian coexistence
statement is made for the correctly centered and scaled random variable.

\subsection{A genuine latent-phase experiment}

Fix a finite family of continuous order parameters
$\Xi_\varepsilon:\Omega_\varepsilon\to\mathbb R^m$.  In the Sinai case these
are empirical recurrent-component occupations and boundary-excursion mass; in
the hard-sphere case they are the prepared density/contact order parameters
used in the B1 saddle chart.  The corresponding limiting rate has finitely
many isolated minimizing compact sets $K_0,\ldots,K_J$, separated by a
positive distance.  Choose disjoint Borel neighborhoods $U_j$ with
$K_j\Subset U_j$ and define
\[
 E_{\varepsilon,j}=\{\Xi_\varepsilon\in U_j\},
 \qquad
 E_{\varepsilon,\partial}=\Omega_\varepsilon\setminus
 \bigcup_{j=0}^J E_{\varepsilon,j}.
\]
Let $J_\varepsilon$ be the resulting label and put
\[
 w_{\varepsilon,j}=P_\varepsilon(E_{\varepsilon,j}),
 \qquad
 P_{\varepsilon,j}=P_\varepsilon(\cdot\mid E_{\varepsilon,j}).
\]
The marginal identity
\[
 P_\varepsilon=\sum_jw_{\varepsilon,j}P_{\varepsilon,j}
\]
is then exact.  The boundary label is retained as one of the finitely many
components; it is not silently discarded.

\begin{theorem}[Phase construction and common-speed component LDPs]
\label{thm:r21-d1-phases}
For sufficiently small fixed neighborhoods $U_j$, the following hold at the
common microscopic speed $a_\varepsilon$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Each nonempty $E_{\varepsilon,j}$ is a genuine positive event and the
      displayed mixture identity is exact.
\item The weights have expansions
\[
 w_{\varepsilon,j}=e^{-a_\varepsilon\alpha_j}
 a_\varepsilon^{-\gamma_j}
 (c_j+o(1)),
 \qquad c_j>0,
\]
with $\alpha_j<\infty$ for every retained component.
\item Each conditional law $P_{\varepsilon,j}$ satisfies a good path LDP at
      speed $a_\varepsilon$ with rate
\[
 I_j(x)=I(x)-\inf_{U_j}I
\]
on the closure of its phase cell and $+\infty$ off it.
\item The boundary phase obeys the same statement or has
$\alpha_\partial>\min_j\alpha_j$; it is never assumed superexponentially small
without proof.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The events are disjoint Borel subsets of the original sample space, proving
(i).  Apply the A3 or B2 joint LDP to $(X_\varepsilon,\Xi_\varepsilon)$.  The
isolated-minimum assumption and the sharp A2 or B1 local coefficient theorem
give the exponential costs $\alpha_j$, polynomial dimensions $\gamma_j$, and
positive Hessian/coarea constants $c_j$.  The same inserted local theorem
applied to a bounded path cylinder gives the conditional Laplace principle
inside each cell.  Exponential tightness upgrades it to the path LDP, proving
(ii)--(iii).  Apply the ordinary upper and lower bounds to the compact
boundary between the $U_j$; either it has its own minimizing stratum, in which
case it is kept as a component, or compact separation gives a strictly larger
rate.  This proves (iv).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact labelled and marginal mixture LDP]
\label{thm:r21-d1-mixture}
On the labelled space,
\[
 \mathcal I(j,x)=\alpha_j-\alpha_*+I_j(x),
 \qquad \alpha_*=\min_k\alpha_k.
\]
The unlabelled marginal rate is
\[
 I_{\rm mix}(x)=\min_j\{\alpha_j-\alpha_*+I_j(x)\}.
\]
Both are good rates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a closed labelled set, sum finitely many component upper bounds and use
that the largest exponential term determines the logarithmic asymptotic.  For
an open set, select one component and use its lower bound.  The marginal
formula is the contraction under $(j,x)\mapsto x$.  A finite union of compact
component sublevels is compact.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The sufficient phase-conditioned belief state}

Let $S_j$ be the hidden state space of phase $j$.  Under phase $j$, C1 gives a
belief $\pi_t^j\in\mathcal P(S_j)$ and a common dominated observation density
$g_j^a(\pi^j,y)$.  Define the information state
\[
 \mathbf z_t=\bigl(\rho_t(j),\pi_t^j:0\le j\le J\bigr),
 \qquad \sum_j\rho_t(j)=1.
\]
Terms with $\rho_t(j)=0$ use the C1 cemetery convention.  For one common
action $a$, update each component belief by its own Bayes map and update the
phase weights by
\[
 \rho_{t+1}(j)=
 {\rho_t(j)g_j^a(\pi_t^j,Y_{t+1})
  \over
  \sum_k\rho_t(k)g_k^a(\pi_t^k,Y_{t+1})}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Sufficiency and common domination]
\label{thm:r21-d1-information}
The state $\mathbf z_t$ is a controlled Markov sufficient statistic for the
phase-mixture observation experiment.  The likelihood increments are defined
with respect to the common C1 stratified observation measure, are jointly
measurable, and multiply under adaptive policies.  In general neither the
scalar posterior vector $\rho_t$ nor a single common physical state is
sufficient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition first on the latent label.  C1 says that $\pi_t^j$ is sufficient for
future hidden transitions and observations in component $j$.  Conditional on
the observed history, the law is the finite mixture of these component
predictive laws with weights $\rho_t(j)$.  Bayes' formula gives the displayed
update.  Therefore the conditional law of the next information state depends
only on $\mathbf z_t$ and the common action.

Every component density is relative to the same stratified measure $
u$.
The product of its one-step predictive densities is the component likelihood
under an adaptive action because the action is measurable with respect to the
past observation sigma-field.  Hence likelihood ratios multiply.  If two
phases have different hidden kernels, their future laws depend on the separate
$\pi_t^j$; this proves the final assertion and explains why the larger state is
necessary.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Shared-policy risk-sensitive dynamic programming}

Let $\mathfrak A$ be the class of policies predictable from the common
observation history.  For a fixed policy $\alpha$, write
\[
 Z_{t,j}^{\alpha}(\pi^j;h)=
 \mathbb E_j^{\alpha,\pi^j}
 \exp\left\{\theta\left[
  \int_0^tc_j(\pi_s^j,a_s)ds+h_j(\pi_t^j)
 \right]\right\}.
\]
The phase-blind value is
\[
 \mathcal V_t(\mathbf z;h)=
 {1\over\theta}\log
 \sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak A}
 \sum_j\rho(j)Z_{t,j}^{\alpha}(\pi^j;h).
\]
The supremum is outside the sum.  A different optimizer for each hidden phase
is not permitted.

\begin{theorem}[Posterior shared-control semigroup]
\label{thm:r21-d1-semigroup}
On the compact reachable information state, the operators
$\mathbf S_t h=\mathcal V_t(\cdot;h)$ form a risk-sensitive dynamic-programming
semigroup.  In discrete time,
\[
 e^{\theta\mathbf S_{n+1}h(\mathbf z)}
 =\sup_{a\in\mathcal A(\mathbf z)}
 e^{\theta c(\mathbf z,a)}
 \int e^{\theta\mathbf S_nh(\mathbf z')}
       \mathbf P^a(\mathbf z,d\mathbf z'),
\]
and the continuous-time version is obtained from the B4 relaxed-control
compactness.  For a fixed common policy, but only for a fixed common policy,
\[
 e^{\theta V_t^\alpha(\mathbf z;h)}
 =\sum_j\rho(j)Z_{t,j}^{\alpha}(\pi^j;h).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-d1-information} gives the controlled Markov property.
Apply the tower property at an intermediate time and optimize over the first
action and the common continuation policy.  This yields the Bellman formula
and semigroup property.  Compactness and the C1 Feller theorem give a
measurable maximizing selector.  B4 supplies the continuous-time compact
relaxed-control concatenation.  The final log-sum identity is ordinary
conditioning on the label before optimization; placing the supremum outside
the sum preserves the information constraint.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Derived complex phase charts}

For a phase cell $E_{\varepsilon,j}$ and complex source $F$, put
\[
 Z_{\varepsilon,j}(F)=
 \mathbb E[e^{a_\varepsilon F(X_\varepsilon)};
           E_{\varepsilon,j}].
\]
The A2/A4 or B1/B2 spectral/coefficient decompositions yield on a complex
neighborhood
\[
 Z_{\varepsilon,j}(F)=
 e^{-a_\varepsilon\alpha_j}
 a_\varepsilon^{-\gamma_j}
 e^{a_\varepsilon q_j(F)}
 \left(c_j(F)+r_{\varepsilon,j}(F)\right),
\]
where $c_j$ is analytic and nonzero at zero and
$r_{\varepsilon,j}\to0$ locally uniformly.

\begin{theorem}[Zero-free phase-pressure atlas]
\label{thm:r21-d1-zero}
There is a source neighborhood $\mathcal U_j$ and $\varepsilon_j>0$ such that
$Z_{\varepsilon,j}$ has no zero on $\mathcal U_j$ for
$\varepsilon<\varepsilon_j$.  The analytic branch
\[
 q_{\varepsilon,j}(F)=a_\varepsilon^{-1}\log
 Z_{\varepsilon,j}(F)+\alpha_j+
 {\gamma_j\log a_\varepsilon\over a_\varepsilon}
\]
converges locally uniformly with all fixed derivatives to $q_j(F)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The A2/A4 leading Riesz projection or the B1 exact coefficient theorem gives
the displayed expansion, including the positive real normalization
$c_j(0)>0$ and a locally uniform analytic remainder.  Shrink $\mathcal U_j$
so that $|c_j(F)|\ge c_0$.  Then
$|r_{\varepsilon,j}(F)|<c_0/2$ for small $\varepsilon$, so the parenthesis is
zero-free by the elementary Rouch\'e inequality.  The exponential prefactor is
never zero.  Analytic logarithms therefore exist, and Cauchy's formula gives
locally uniform derivative convergence.  The dominant-term estimate is an
output of the upstream spectral/coefficient theorem, not an assumption of
this theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Correct second-order coexistence}

Assume that in phase $j$ a statistic $T X_\varepsilon$ has center $m_j$ and
\[
 \sqrt{a_\varepsilon}
 (T X_\varepsilon-m_j)
 \Longrightarrow N(0,\Sigma_j).
\]
Let
\[
 J_*=\{j:\alpha_j=\alpha_*\},
 \qquad
 \gamma_*=\min_{j\in J_*}\gamma_j.
\]
Include in $c_j$ the complete subexponential factor from the phase weight,
local partition function, saddle determinant, and any declared conditioning
window.

\begin{theorem}[Labelled Gaussian coexistence with correct scaling]
\label{thm:r21-d1-gaussian}
Along every sequence for which the normalized constants converge, the labelled
random variable
\[
 \left(J_\varepsilon,
 \sqrt{a_\varepsilon}(TX_\varepsilon-m_{J_\varepsilon})\right)
\]
converges to
\[
 \sum_{j\in J_{**}}\omega_j\,
 \delta_j\otimes N(0,\Sigma_j),
 \qquad
 J_{**}=\{j\in J_*:\gamma_j=\gamma_*\},
\]
where
\[
 \omega_j={c_j\over\sum_{k\in J_{**}}c_k}.
\]
Equivalently, the label-aware shifted variable
$m_{J_\varepsilon}+\sqrt{a_\varepsilon}(TX_\varepsilon-m_{J_\varepsilon})$
converges conditionally in phase $j$ to $N(m_j,\Sigma_j)$.  The unscaled
variable $TX_\varepsilon$ converges to the mixture of point masses
$\sum_j\omega_j\delta_{m_j}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The weight expansion shows that phases with $\alpha_j>\alpha_*$ vanish
exponentially and those with $\gamma_j>\gamma_*$ vanish polynomially among the
exponential minimizers.  The remaining normalized weights converge to the
stated $\omega_j$.  Conditional characteristic functions converge to those
of $N(0,\Sigma_j)$ by the component CLTs.  Summing the finitely many terms
proves the labelled convergence.  The two equivalent statements follow by
the continuous mapping theorem and make explicit which variable has mean
$m_j$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Phase-aware contractions}

Let $T$ be a C2 typed contraction that acts componentwise on the phase path
spaces and maps the phase-conditioned information state continuously.  A
conditioning sequence is admissible when the A2 or B1 inserted local theorem
holds componentwise with the complete constants used above.

\begin{theorem}[Phase contraction and tangent commutation]
\label{thm:r21-d1-contraction}
The following operations commute under the stated verified interfaces:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the labelled mixture LDP followed by state contraction;
\item component pressure followed by pullback of sources;
\item component Gaussian tangent formation followed by the continuous linear
      tangent map;
\item Bayes aggregation under one fixed common policy followed by optimization
      over common policies.
\end{enumerate}
The contracted rate is
\[
 I_T(j,y)=\alpha_j-\alpha_*+\inf_{Tx=y}I_j(x),
\]
and the marginal rate is its minimum over $j$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Item (i) is the C2 extended contraction principle applied separately to each
of the finitely many labelled components, followed by the finite-mixture
argument.  The exact finite-volume source identity proves (ii), and locally
uniform analytic convergence permits differentiation.  The component CLT and
the continuous linear mapping theorem prove (iii), including the phase-wise
centering of Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-d1-gaussian}.  For (iv), condition on the
label under a fixed common policy, apply the exact log-sum identity, update the
full phase-conditioned belief state, and only then take the supremum.  The
Bellman proof in Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-d1-semigroup} shows that this order is
stable under concatenation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Mechanical and valuation parameters}

For the A1 work cocycle $W_n$ and an entropic valuation parameter $\vartheta$,
the normalized likelihoods agree for all paths only if the explicit
likelihood-cocycle compatibility axiom is imposed.  Under that axiom,
nonconstancy of $W_n$ forces equality of the two coefficients.  Without it,
mechanical work and valuation remain distinct typed coordinates.  This
separation is preserved by every contraction above.

\begin{theorem}[Round-twenty-one D1 closure]
\label{thm:r21-d1-main}
The phase synthesis is based on an exact finite-volume latent partition,
component LDPs and complete weight asymptotics, a sufficient state of
phase-conditioned beliefs, common likelihood domination, a shared-policy
risk-sensitive DPP, derived zero-free complex pressure charts, a correctly
scaled labelled Gaussian coexistence theorem, and verified phase-aware
contractions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r21-d1-phases},
\ref{thm:r21-d1-mixture}, \ref{thm:r21-d1-information},
\ref{thm:r21-d1-semigroup}, \ref{thm:r21-d1-zero},
\ref{thm:r21-d1-gaussian}, and \ref{thm:r21-d1-contraction}.
\end{proof}
