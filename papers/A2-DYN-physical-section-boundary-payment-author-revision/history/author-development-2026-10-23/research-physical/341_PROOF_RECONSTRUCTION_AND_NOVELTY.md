## Why the new argument is stronger than the old section budget

Native324 controls every regular canonical side, but its general interval
overlay has a short-half loss. Summing it over shallow indices does not
give a vanishing normalized charge.

A constant physical roof enforces a stronger exact relation. For a canonical
source parameter \(v\), the reversed and forward endpoint graph slopes obey

\[
p_0'=-Rc_0V_-\alpha_0',\qquad
p_m'=Rc_mV_+\alpha_m',\qquad
\kappa_-\le V_\pm\le U.
\]

The true first variation therefore gives

\[
F_v=\frac{p_0p_0'}{c_0V_-}
       +\frac{p_mp_m'}{c_mV_+}.
\]

The plus sign in the backward term matters. It follows from the reversed
momentum \(-\varphi_0\), not from an assumption that the section is invariant
under reversal.

After fixed endpoint sign changes, both momenta increase on a canonical
overlap. Stationarity forces their signed values into an opposition
quadrant. There is at most one such quadrant interval, and the actual Gauss
angle is monotone on all of it. Consequently arbitrary disconnected
stationary subsets share one angular image: there is no component count.

Designating the inverse derivative of the short half by \(a\) and of the
long half by \(b\), the first variation gives

\[
\frac{|p_{\rm long}|}{|p_{\rm short}|}
  \le Q\,\frac ba,\qquad
b\le\epsilon=E\sigma^{L-1},\quad L=\max(i,m-i).
\]

Split pointwise at \(a\le\eta\).

For the small-\(a\) part, the interval weight is
\(\min(\sup_Ia,\eta)\), and the genuine weighted interval-overlay lemma gives

\[
\sum_{I\cap J\ne\varnothing}\min(a_I,\eta)b_J
    \le H(\epsilon+\eta).
\]

For the complementary part the selected angle lies in one wedge of width
at most \(Q\epsilon/\eta\). Its disconnected components are charged only
once because the angle is monotone on the full opposition interval. The
untruncated overlay sum is at most \(H(\epsilon+E)\).

Taking \(\eta=\sqrt{E\epsilon}\) pays both parts and gives the one-side bound

\[
F_{\rm cs}C_{\rm side}HE(\pi+2Q)\sigma^{(L-1)/2}.
\]

The parameter \(\eta\) is an internal split of a positive proof integral.
It neither removes trajectories nor changes a conditioning event, a guard,
a microscopic window, or an original limit.

## An explicitly rejected inference

Strict convexity of the optical action, even with separated half actions and
small mixed derivatives, does **not** by itself rule out a constant-roof arc
on a pulled-back middle-coordinate section.

An exact abstract example is

\[
S_-(x,t)=x^2-\delta t\sqrt{1-x^2}+2t^2,\qquad
S_+(t,z)=z^2+\delta tz+2t^2,\quad 0<\delta\le1.
\]

At \(t=0\), the stationarity equation
\(\partial_t(S_-+S_+)=0\) is \(z=\sqrt{1-x^2}\), and the total action equals
one along that arc. On \(2/5\le x\le3/5,\ |t|\le1/10\), both half Hessians
and the full three-variable Hessian are positive definite. Exact lower
bounds are

\[
S_{-,xx}\ge231/128,\quad
\det D^2S_-\ge213/32,\quad
\det D^2S_+\ge7,
\]

and the middle-coordinate Schur complement of the full Hessian is at least
\(1107/154\). The mixed derivatives are \(O(\delta)\), which can be made
arbitrarily small.

This is a counterexample only to the proposed abstract inference; it is not
asserted to be a trajectory of the Lorentz gas. The final proof pays actual
constant-roof arcs using their real source dynamics rather than claiming they
are absent.
