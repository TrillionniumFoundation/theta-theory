# Historical novelty audit: clearance-selected endpoint kernels

## Conclusion

The searched historical native sources do not already state or prove the complete positive-clearance-band endpoint root-kernel estimate

\[
 D_{m,R;j,\delta}(a,v)
 \le C_{\mathrm{end}}\{\delta+
       \theta_{\mathrm r}^{\max(j,m-1-j)}\}
 \qquad(0\le j<m),
\]

the selected first-image weighted-length budget

\[
 \sum_C\|\rho_C\|_\infty |C|\le M_{\mathrm{first}}\delta,
\]

or the resulting all-index positive-clearance critical-coefficient estimate

\[
 \sum_{\text{guard-selected one-copy critical germs}}
       \mathfrak J(x)\beta_y(x)
 \le C_{\mathrm{crit}}
       \left\{\frac{4\varepsilon(B)}{1-q_0^{1/4}}
                       +m\theta_{\mathrm r}^{(m-1)/2}\right\}.
\]

The novelty is narrow and must be described precisely. For interior flight indices \(1\le j\le m-2\), the proposed endpoint-kernel inequality is already a direct deduction from the inherited localized half-source theorem, applied in both genuine time directions. The new proof ingredient retains the selected horizontal-source length through the first actual collision. That completes the two endpoint indices \(j=0,m-1\), where the inherited half-source theorem does not permit selection before its first collision. The positive-width critical-coefficient conclusion then uses the inherited critical-sector comparison with this completed kernel.

The maximum-depth selection, the physical reversal identities, the exact first-image density, the length-sensitive renewal theorem, and the zero-clearance critical-fan payment are inherited. None should be presented as a newly discovered theorem here. The new first-image budget is an explicit proved consequence of that inherited geometry, not a new spectral or curve-growth input.

## Scope of the search

All 660 deduplicated native TeX objects in the parent census were searched, including historical main and supplement sources, preserved core modules, frontmatter, and mathematical-reconstruction drafts. Both ordinary line searches and whitespace-normalized multiline searches were used for endpoint kernels, selected length, first images, positive-clearance critical sources, guard coefficients, and the maximum-depth exponent. The closest mathematical statements and their proofs were read directly, including current modules 284–285, 316, 319–320, 322, and 327–329.

The associated machine-readable file, novelty_census_results.json, records all 660 object IDs, content SHA256 values, sizes, census paths and observation commits, and the search matches. The manifest against which this was run has SHA256:

## Recommended author wording

“For interior selected indices, the endpoint estimate follows from the inherited localized half-source theorem in the two physical time directions. The additional weighted first-image estimate covers a clearance witness selected before the first true collision and completes both endpoint indices. Combined with the inherited critical-sector comparison, this proves uniform tightness of the complete positive-clearance critical coefficient for the original all-depth guard.”

It is accurate to describe this as a new complete estimate and a proof-completeness advance in the original physical chain. It should not be described as a new zero-clearance fan theorem, a new renewal theorem, a new maximum-depth argument, a new raw roof-height result, or completion of the original local limit theorem.

## Unchanged limitation

The proposed leading coefficient has an \(O(\varepsilon(B))\) term before collision normalization. Multiplication by \(m^2\) at fixed \(B\) does not yield a vanishing estimate. The theorem also does not sum nonleading local coarea remainders on a collision-uniform roof neighborhood. The original same-pool positive-source shortage, its \(m^2L^\infty(dt)\) payment, and the required joint spectral/arithmetic/conditioning realization retain their existing obligations.
