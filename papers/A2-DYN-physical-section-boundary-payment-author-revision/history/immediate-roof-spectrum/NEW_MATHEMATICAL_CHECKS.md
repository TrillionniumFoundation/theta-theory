## 4. Native 337: Fourier identities, normalization and finite-band error

### 4.1 The derivative sign and full label torus

The convention is `fhat(s)=integral exp(ist) f(t) dt`. Distributional integration by parts gives

\[
\widehat{Df_\ell}(s)=-is\widehat f_\ell(s).
\]

Thus `-is F=A+G` and `sF-iA=iG`; the asymptotic sign in the theorem is correct. At `s=0`, the identity is the zero total mass of the compactly supported derivative. The compact support is the actual physical roof support, not one interval independent of collision count.

### 4.2 Roof coincidences and the exact sinc term

For an atomic derivative vector, direct integration gives

\[
\frac1{2S}\int_{-S}^S e^{is(u-v)}\,ds
=\begin{cases}
1,&u=v,\\
\sin(S(u-v))/(S(u-v)),&u\ne v.
\end{cases}
\]

The diagonal is the square of the already combined signed coefficient at each roof and label. The complete off-diagonal contribution is bounded by

\[
\mathcal C_S=\sum_\ell\sum_{u\ne v}
|a_\ell(u)a_\ell(v)|\min\{1,(S|u-v|)^{-1}\}.
\]

It is a finite sum at each fixed `m,R`; it tends to zero there, without a positive roof-gap assumption. A pair of roofs can approach one another as `R` or `m` changes. Their sinc term must remain in the finite-band inequality rather than being discarded by an unproved uniform separation.

### 4.3 Absolutely continuous part and mixed term

Approximation of an `L1` function by finite sums of bounded interval indicators proves its Fourier transform tends to zero. The finite exact-label sum then gives uniformity in the three torus frequencies at the fixed physical parameters. No uniformity in `m,R` is supplied by this approximation.

Writing `Q_S=(2S)^(-1) integral sum_l |dhat_l(s)|^2 ds`, the complete variation bound gives a uniform-in-frequency atomic vector norm at most `D`. Cauchy–Schwarz in the frequency average bounds the mixed part by `2D sqrt(Q_S)`; the continuous part contributes `Q_S`. Together with the exact atomic formula this proves

\[
|\mathcal W_S-\mathcal J_{m,R}|\le\mathcal C_S+2D\sqrt{\mathcal Q_S}+\mathcal Q_S.

At fixed `m,R`, `Q_S->0` by boundedness and the just-proved Fourier decay, while `C_S->0` by finiteness. Hence the limit is the complete signed jump energy and is at most `D^2`. This does not make the unaveraged differentiated transform tend to zero when jumps are present, nor does it make the bare transform absolutely integrable.
