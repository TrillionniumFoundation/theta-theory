\section{Endpoint section sides: isoroof payment and eventual strict convexity}
\label{sec:es-isoroof}

The regular constant-roof side terms in
\eqref{eq:sbv-atomic-coefficient} have not yet been estimated by their
geometric role.  We now pay the terms carried by the \emph{original
endpoint section sides}.  The result sums actual collision words and
exact labels before taking any supremum.  Its stronger conclusion is
that this entire regular endpoint contribution is absent for every
$m\ge114$.  Critical incoming sectors remain separate throughout.

Keep $R\in[R_-,R_+]$, $\sigma=47/59$, the physical roof $F=L_m$,
and the endpoint cap \eqref{eq:v109-endpoint-cap}.  On a positive-visited
regular word, or its positive-visited one-sided boundary trace, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:es-hessian}
 q=(q_0,q_1)=(\alpha_0,\alpha_m),\qquad
 G=\begin{pmatrix}a&e\\e&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \nabla F=R(-p_0,p_m).
\end{equation}
The symbol $e$ in this section is the mixed Hessian entry, not the
unit Gauss direction.  Thus $a,d\ge\lambda_\circ$,
$G\le\Lambda_\circ I$, and $|e|=R\omega$.  Define
\begin{align}
 E_0&=\frac{R_+^2}{\tau_-}=\frac{2209}{600},&
 E_1&=500R_+C_\omega,& Q&=\frac{\Lambda_\circ}{\lambda_\circ},
                                                        \label{eq:es-constants-a}\\
 \kappa_{\rm es}&=\frac{E_0}{\lambda_\circ},&
 K_{\rm es}&=\frac9{10}R_+Q
       \left(\frac{E_1}{\lambda_\circ}
                    +\frac{E_0C_F}{\lambda_\circ^2}\right).
                                                        \label{eq:es-constants-b}
\end{align}
Here $C_\omega$ and $C_F$ are exactly
\eqref{eq:el-omega-constant} and \eqref{eq:el-third-constant}.
All derivatives below are endpoint-angle derivatives at fixed $R$,
on the designated actual word.  No endpoint derivative is taken
across a newly appearing collision.

\begin{lemma}[Decay of the mixed endpoint derivative]
\label{lem:es-mixed}
For every endpoint direction $\eta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:es-mixed}
 |e|\le E_0\sigma^{m-1},\qquad
 |D_qe[\eta]|\le E_1\sigma^{(m-2)_+}\|\eta\|_\infty,
 \qquad |D_qa[\eta]|\le C_F\|\eta\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
The same bounds hold for positive-visited boundary traces in the
closure of the original endpoint tests.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows from $|e|=R\omega$ and
\eqref{eq:v109-optical-bounds}.  The optical cofactor is nonzero
and has a fixed sign on each regular word.  Consequently
$|D_qe[\eta]|=R\omega|D_q\log\omega[\eta]|$.
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:el-logomega} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:v109-marked}.  The final assertion is the
entrywise consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:el-third}, with the
other two directions equal to $(1,0)$.

These estimates use only positive visited incidences, the actual
flight lower bound, and the stated endpoint cap.  At a
positive-visited limit the interior stationary Hessian remains
invertible and the fixed-word action has its local smooth
continuation.  Passing the estimates from its physical side gives
the asserted traces, including limits where an unvisited obstacle
is tangent.  This passage uses the fixed-word action and does not
assign a collision-map derivative at a visited tangency.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The exact horizontal restriction]
\label{lem:es-restriction}
On an initial horizontal side $p_0=b$, use $y=q_1$ and write
$q_0=x(y)$ locally.  With $S(y)=F(x(y),y)$ one has
\begin{align}
 x'&=-e/a,&
 S'&=R(p_m+b e/a),
                                                   \label{eq:es-first}\\
 \frac{dp_m}{dy}&=\frac{d-e^2/a}{R},&
 S''&=d-e^2/a+Rb\frac{d}{dy}(e/a).
                                                   \label{eq:es-second}
\end{align}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:es-convexity}
 S''\ge\lambda_\circ-K_{\rm es}\sigma^{(m-2)_+}.
\end{equation}
If $S$ is constant on a regular arc, then on that arc
\begin{equation}\label{eq:es-isoroof-band}
 p_m=-b e/a,
 \qquad |p_m|\le\kappa_{\rm es}|b|\sigma^{m-1}.
\end{equation}
The analogous conclusions hold at a terminal horizontal section
side by physical time reversal, with the actual tests transported.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $F_{q_0}=-Rb$ and $a\ge\lambda_\circ$, the implicit function
theorem supplies the stated parametrization.  Differentiating this
identity gives $x'=-e/a$, and the chain rule gives the first formula
for $S'$.  Differentiating $F_{q_1}=Rp_m$ gives the first formula
in \eqref{eq:es-second}.  Since $x''=-(e/a)'$ and
$F_{q_0}=-Rb$, the second derivative is
\[
 S''=(x',1)G(x',1)^{\mathsf T}+F_{q_0}x''
     =d-e^2/a+Rb(e/a)'.
\]
The sign of the last term is therefore positive as displayed;
it cannot be omitted at a nonzero section level.

Put $v=(-e/a,1)$.  The Hessian bounds give
$\|v\|_\infty\le Q$ and $d-e^2/a=vGv^{\mathsf T}
\ge\lambda_\circ$.  By \eqref{eq:es-mixed},
\[
 |(e/a)'|\le Q\left(\frac{E_1}{\lambda_\circ}
              +\frac{E_0C_F}{\lambda_\circ^2}\right)
                    \sigma^{(m-2)_+}.
\]
We used $\sigma^{m-1}\le\sigma^{(m-2)_+}$, valid also for
$m=1$.  Multiplication by $R|b|\le(9/10)R_+$ proves
\eqref{eq:es-convexity}.  If $S'=0$, the first derivative identity
and $|e|/a\le\kappa_{\rm es}\sigma^{m-1}$ prove
\eqref{eq:es-isoroof-band}.  Time reversal changes the signs of
the endpoint momenta and exchanges the endpoints, preserving the
absolute bounds and the physical roof.  It does not require the
section or an observation to be invariant under reversal.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform eventual absence of regular endpoint atoms]
\label{thm:es-no-atoms}
On every original endpoint horizontal side, every positive-visited
regular word restriction of the physical roof satisfies
$S''>\lambda_\circ/2$ for $m\ge114$.  On an endpoint vertical
side the corresponding roof restriction is strictly convex for
every $m\ge1$.  These assertions hold for every radius in
$[R_-,R_+]$, including positive-visited one-sided boundary arcs.
Consequently no regular original endpoint section side contributes
a constant-roof atom to \eqref{eq:sbv-atomic-coefficient} when
$m\ge114$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Here is an entirely rational certificate for the constant in
\eqref{eq:es-convexity}.  Use
$\underline\lambda=9/50<\lambda_\circ$ and
$\underline c=2/5<c_\circ$.  The already displayed constants give
\[
 J_*=\frac{2845}{354},\quad
 C_F=\frac{16132182255644462065}{3378910095288},\quad
 C_\omega\le\overline C_\omega=\frac{18557935}{6372}.
\]
Replacing $\lambda_\circ,c_\circ$ by these lower bounds in the
denominators of \eqref{eq:es-constants-b} gives
\[
 K_{\rm es}\le\overline K
 =\frac{396358110646634302372735325}{39411607351439232},
 \qquad
 \overline K\left(\frac{47}{59}\right)^{112}
       <\frac9{100}=\frac{\underline\lambda}{2}.
\]
The last inequality is an exact integer comparison after multiplying
positive denominators; the supplied verification program performs
that comparison.  Thus for $m\ge114$,
$S''\ge\lambda_\circ-\underline\lambda/2
>\lambda_\circ/2$.  No numerical orbit or uniform chart-size
assumption is used.

On an initial vertical side $q_0$ is constant, so the restriction
in the other endpoint angle has second derivative $d\ge
\lambda_\circ$.  The physical optical twist makes that other
endpoint angle a valid local parameter.  At a terminal vertical
side the derivative is $a\ge\lambda_\circ$.  A strictly convex
function cannot be constant on a nonempty open interval.  Every
one-dimensional endpoint boundary arc has such a local parameter;
its isolated points have zero regular angular charge.  The
finite physical side decomposition of Lemma~\ref{lem:sbv-geometric-sides}
therefore excludes all the stated regular atoms.  Critical
incoming sectors at $\nabla F=0$ are not regular sides and have
not been removed by this argument.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An all-count physical payment, including singular side traces}

We make explicit a trace issue in applying a regular endpoint root
kernel to a section side.  A section side can meet an
unvisited physical boundary; the following safe trace bound does
not assume transversality. The regular-root assertion
\eqref{eq:mc-roots} is not a definition of the collision derivative
at that boundary. Lemma~\ref{lem:iso-transverse} below will
exclude open coincidences for the original canonical section
sources in this physical model.

\begin{lemma}[The two one-sided endpoint trace budgets]
\label{lem:es-trace-kernel}
Fix $b\in(-9/10,9/10)$.  Along all positive-visited one-sided
physical boundary arcs contained in $p_0=b$, at almost every
terminal momentum $v$ the sum of the distinct physical trace
weights $u=|\partial_{\alpha_0}p_m|^{-1}$ is at most
$2C_{\rm mom}$.  For arcs lying inside the regular physical
domain the sharper bound is $C_{\rm mom}$.  Artificial seams
and repeated descriptions of the same physical trace are counted
once.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At positive visited incidences the map from endpoint angles to
$(p_0,p_m)$ is locally invertible: its Jacobian has absolute value
$\det G/R^2>0$.  Apply the finite semialgebraic physical side
decomposition of Lemma~\ref{lem:sbv-geometric-sides} in these
coordinates.  Except at finitely many arc endpoints and
intersections, a physical sheet adjacent to the line $p_0=b$
has a one-sided collar $p_0>b$ or $p_0<b$.  More precisely, on
each relative-interior terminal-momentum subinterval and at each
fixed $v$ in it, all sufficiently small $h>0$ give a point of
that sheet at $(b+h,v)$ or $(b-h,v)$, respectively.  The allowed
width may depend on the sheet and on $v$; no uniform collar width
is asserted.  If both orientations are available,
choose one by a fixed rule.  Distinct positive-visited traces are
distinct limiting physical initial points; an additional visited
tangency belongs to its separate zero-trace stratum.

For a finite collection of traces with the same chosen collar
orientation, replace $b$ by $b+h$ or $b-h$, keep the same $v$,
and take $h>0$ sufficiently small for all their collars.  The
resulting roots are distinct actual regular physical roots.  Their
sum of weights is at most $C_{\rm mom}$ by
\eqref{eq:mc-roots}.  Smooth continuation of the designated
positive-visited words gives the limit of each weight.  Passing
to the limit and then taking the supremum over finite collections
proves the same bound for that orientation.  The two orientations
give $2C_{\rm mom}$.  The excluded finitely many momentum values
have zero angular measure when $b\ne0$; for $b=0$ the regular
angle is constant.  Interior regular roots use
\eqref{eq:mc-roots} directly.  This proof takes traces of an
already physical family and never declares the boundary collision
map differentiable.
\end{proof}

Let $N_h=20$, the number of horizontal side occurrences in the
five original rectangles at the two endpoint indices.  If a real
interface lies on several such sides, assign it to the first one
in a fixed ordering.  Let $a^{\rm ep,reg}_\ell(t)$ denote the
signed sum in \eqref{eq:sbv-atomic-coefficient} of the
constant-roof regular interfaces assigned to original endpoint
section sides.  Combine coincident contributions with the same
exact label and the same physical roof before taking absolute
values.  This definition selects terms of the original derivative;
it introduces no new conditioning event.

\begin{theorem}[All-label exponential endpoint-isoroof payment]
\label{thm:es-payment}
For the actual weights and observations of
\eqref{eq:wo-measures}, $A_V=a_0b_0$, and every $m\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:es-payment}
 \sum_{\ell,t}|a^{\rm ep,reg}_\ell(t)|
 \le 4N_h A_VF_{\rm cs}C_{\rm mom}
       \arctan(\kappa_{\rm es}\sigma^{m-1})
 \le 4N_h A_VF_{\rm cs}C_{\rm mom}
                 \kappa_{\rm es}\sigma^{m-1}.
\end{equation}
The left side is zero for $m\ge114$.  Both conclusions hold
uniformly in $R$ and retain every original exact record.  In
particular multiplying this endpoint contribution by any fixed
power of $m$ still gives a uniform vanishing quantity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Vertical endpoint arcs are never constant-roof by
Theorem~\ref{thm:es-no-atoms}.  On an initial horizontal side
$p_0=b\ne0$, the exact angle is $\theta=\arg(-b,p_m)$ and
\[
 |d\theta|=\frac{|b|}{b^2+v^2}\,|dv|,\qquad v=p_m.
\]
The identity used in Theorem~\ref{thm:cs-charge} gives
$A\le F_{\rm cs}u$, with the original $1/(4\pi c_*)$
normalization already in $F_{\rm cs}$.
On a constant-roof arc \eqref{eq:es-isoroof-band} restricts $v$
to the interval of radius $\kappa_{\rm es}|b|\sigma^{m-1}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:es-trace-kernel} therefore bounds the complete
positive angular charge of all those arcs by
\[
 2F_{\rm cs}C_{\rm mom}
 \int_{|v|\le\kappa_{\rm es}|b|\sigma^{m-1}}
          \frac{|b|}{b^2+v^2}\,dv
 =4F_{\rm cs}C_{\rm mom}
           \arctan(\kappa_{\rm es}\sigma^{m-1}).
\]
Original momentum caps, rectangle intervals, exact records and
other physical tests merely restrict this positive sum.  For
$b=0$, the angle is constant on each regular side of $v=0$,
so the regular angular charge is zero; the critical source at
$v=0$ remains separate.  Time reversal proves the terminal bound.

Across a genuine endpoint membership cut, the label vector on
the exterior side is zero and that on the retained side has at
most one nonzero exact record.  Its all-label jump has norm at
most one.  Coincident real constraints use their actual two
traces and are assigned only once.  Since $0\le w\le1$ and
$|V|\le A_V$, multiply by $A_V$ and sum the at most $N_h$
side occurrences.  The triangle inequality after exact
label-and-roof grouping proves \eqref{eq:es-payment}.  The
zero statement follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:es-no-atoms}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The precise advance and remaining sources]
\label{rem:es-scope}
The proof establishes a new geometric payment and eventual exact
exclusion of the regular endpoint-section atomic stratum.  It
does not assert that all regular boundary arcs are nonconstant
for small $m$.  Strict positivity of the endpoint Hessian alone
would not prove the horizontal assertion: its curvature has the
additional term in \eqref{eq:es-second}; the exponentially small
mixed endpoint derivative is essential.

The nonconstant endpoint sides still contribute to the absolutely
continuous derivative.  Interior section sides, unvisited
boundaries not assigned to an endpoint section side, and critical
germs strictly inside the section have not been paid by
\eqref{eq:es-payment}.  The original normalized raw pointwise
limit continues to require its remaining physical source estimate
in the original order of limits.  No normalized local-limit
conclusion is inferred from an unscaled variation bound.
\end{remark}
