## Native 338: section-critical incoming fans

### The source and one-copy assignment

The restriction to section-critical germs with positive visited incidences and strictly positive unvisited clearances is essential to the representative lemma. At fixed $R,m$, the actual finite word is then regular on a full neighborhood of its collision state. Its intersection with the *exact original* canonical source $p_i=b$ or $\alpha_i=a$ contains an open interval. Their exact section labels may vary near a cut. This is harmless for this particular upper bound: native 324 sums the full canonical physical source, while the coefficient being bounded keeps its original labels and sector fractions. No new conditional event is substituted into the target density.

An unvisited zero-clearance germ cannot be justified by this open-neighborhood argument. The revised draft correctly assigns every such germ first to native 322, including simultaneous section contacts and collapsed angular slices. Native 322 supplies its own positive-clearance representatives. The new representative lemma is then applied only to the remaining regular section germs. Visited-grazing traces are assigned before either family and use the proved zero coarea trace of native 323, rather than collision-area nullness.

For one fixed index and source level, the normalized state at that index determines its actual past and future and the unique translation recentering its initial disk. Two one-copy germs at the same source parameter are therefore identical. There is no word or lattice multiplicity. Artificial seams can be chosen away from any finite family before applying the source partition bounds.

### Why both half-sums are available

For an interior index $i$, a finite family of distinct limiting normal-endpoint germs has positive minimum separation in its source parameter. Both endpoint momenta of their representatives tend to zero. If two representatives occupied a common backward partition interval, native 324's monotonicity and derivative lower bound would imply

\[
 |p_0(x_r)-p_0(\widetilde x_r)|
 \ge E^{-1}\sigma^{-(i-1)}
       |v(x_r)-v(\widetilde x_r)|,
 \quad E=47/6,\quad \sigma=47/59.
\]

The left side tends to zero, while the right side has a positive lower limit. This contradiction applies independently to the forward partition, with $p_m$ and exponent $m-i-1$. Consequently representatives occupy distinct intervals in *each* partition. Taking finite sums of their reciprocal derivatives, passing to their strict-word limits, and then exhausting finite families gives both $H_h$ sums, or both $H_v$ sums. This is stronger than the interval-overlay estimate used for arbitrary regular side charges. It is justified here by the common normal endpoint values.

The local representative limit is taken at fixed $R,m$ and finite family. No uniform neighborhood size, exchange of limits, or parameter-dependent width is required.

## Native 339: endpoint isoroof atoms

$G=\begin{pmatrix}a&e\\e&d\end{pmatrix}$
and $\nabla F=R(-p_0,p_m)$.

\[

\]

The last term has the **plus sign** shown. It follows from
$F_{q_0}x''=(-Rb)(-(e/a)')$.
Positive definiteness of $G$ alone would not pay this term at a nonzero horizontal section level.
