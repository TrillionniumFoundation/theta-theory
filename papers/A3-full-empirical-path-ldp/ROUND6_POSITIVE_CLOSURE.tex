% ROUND6-REFEREE-POSITIVE-CLOSURE
\section{Round-six positive closure: recession-defect compactification and full path LDP}
\label{sec:r6-a3}

The collision clock is normalized before passing to the limit.  Long induced
blocks can therefore carry a positive fraction of collision time while their
block frequency tends to zero.  This mass is represented by an explicit
defect measure on a compact space of normalized excursion profiles; it is not
forced into an ordinary finite-moment probability on the countable alphabet.
All finite-state approximations use only admissible edges and connector words
of the billiard code.

\subsection{The normalized block and defect state}

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the finite-connector Young alphabet and let
\(r(a)\ge1\) be the collision return length.  Because the billiard has unit
speed and finite horizon, the graph-completed collision path space
\(\Omega_c\) is compact on every fixed local window.  To a block \(a\) assign
its normalized collision profile
\[
\chi(a)=\frac1{r(a)}\sum_{j=0}^{r(a)-1}
\delta_{\Theta^j\Gamma(a)}\in\mathcal P(\Omega_c).
\]
Let \(\mathcal Z_\infty\) be the compact closure of the profiles of sequences
\(a_n\) with \(r(a_n)\to\infty\), in the local-window weak topology.  A
collision-normalized block word \((a_0,\ldots,a_{N-1})\), with
\(R_N=\sum r(a_j)\), defines
\[
m_N=\frac1{R_N}\sum_{j<N}\delta_{\widehat\Theta^ja},
\qquad
\zeta_N=\frac1{R_N}
\sum_{j<N}r(a_j)1_{\{r(a_j)>L_N\}}\delta_{\chi(a_j)},
\]
where \(L_N\uparrow\infty\) after the finite-level projection.  The natural
limit state is a pair \((m,\zeta)\) satisfying
\[
 m\ge0,\quad m\circ\widehat\Theta^{-1}=m,\quad
 \int r\,dm+\zeta(1)=1,\quad
 \zeta\in\mathcal M_+(\mathcal Z_\infty).
\]
The collision path represented by the pair is
\[
\mathscr P_c(m,\zeta)
=\int r(a)\chi(a)\,dm(a)+\int z\,d\zeta(z).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Compact recession state space]
\label{lem:r6-a3-compact}
For every entropy/free-energy level \(M\), the set of invariant pairs
\((m,\zeta)\) satisfying the normalization above and rate at most \(M\) is
compact in the topology of finite-cylinder convergence of \(m\), weak
convergence of \(\zeta\), and convergence of \(\int(r\wedge L)dm\) for every
finite \(L\).  The map \(\mathscr P_c\) is continuous on these sublevels.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The total mass of \(m\) is at most one, and \(\mathcal Z_\infty\) is compact,
so both coordinates are weakly tight.  The normalization prevents loss of
collision clock: the missing limit of \(\int rdm\) is exactly recorded by
\(\zeta(1)\).  On a fixed path window, \(\chi(a)\) is continuous outside a
finite singularity shield; the shield cost is uniformly small on a rate
sublevel by the source estimate proved below.  Truncating at \(r\le L\) gives
a continuous finite-alphabet map.  Letting \(L\to\infty\), the remaining
collision mass is carried by the compact profile coordinate \(\zeta\).  This
proves compactness and continuity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Recurrent and recession free energies}

Let \(g(a,\widehat\omega)\) be the normalized induced Gibbs potential.  On
the recurrent coordinate define
\[
\mathcal J_{\rm rec}(m)=
\begin{cases}
 m(1)\bigl(P(g)-h_{\bar m}(\widehat\Theta)-\bar m(g)\bigr),
 &m(1)>0,\quad\bar m=m/m(1),\\
0,&m=0,
\end{cases}
\]
whenever the expression is finite.  For a recession profile
\(z\in\mathcal Z_\infty\), define
\[
\mathfrak c_\infty(z)=
\sup_{F\in C^{\rm cyl}(\Omega_c)}
\left\{z(F)-q_\infty(F)+q_\infty(0)\right\},
\]
where
\[
q_\infty(F)=\limsup_{r(a)\to\infty}
\frac1{r(a)}\log
\mathbb E_a\exp\left\{
\sum_{j<r(a)}F(\Theta^j\Gamma(a))
\right\}.
\]
Put
\[
\mathcal J(m,\zeta)=
\mathcal J_{\rm rec}(m)+
\int\mathfrak c_\infty(z)\,d\zeta(z).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Recurrent--recession variational principle]
\label{thm:r6-a3-variational}
The direct collision-time pressure satisfies
\[
Q_c(F)=\sup_{(m,\zeta)}
\left\{
\mathscr P_c(m,\zeta)(F)-\mathcal J(m,\zeta)
\right\}.
\]
The functional \(\mathcal J\) is good and lower semicontinuous.  On states
with \(\zeta=0\) it equals the induced free-energy cost divided by the mean
return length; on pure recession states it is the homogeneous excursion cost
\(\mathfrak c_\infty\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Decompose every block word at the threshold \(L\).  Short blocks contribute
the finite-alphabet pressure with collision normalization.  Each long block
contributes its length times its normalized profile, and its logarithmic
weight divided by length has the recession support function
\(q_\infty\).  The number of placements of long blocks is subexponential at
collision speed because their block frequency is at most \(1/L\).  Taking
first the collision-time limit and then \(L\to\infty\) gives the upper bound
and the displayed supremum.

For the lower bound, choose a finite recurrent Markov approximation and a
finite collection of long admissible excursions whose profiles and costs
approximate \(\zeta\).  Concatenate them with the fixed connector family.
Connector collision length is bounded per concatenation and its total
collision fraction tends to zero.  The Gibbs distortion of the concatenated
word is the sum of the recurrent free energy and the excursion costs up to
\(o(R_N)\).  This realizes every finite convex combination and then every
pair by approximation.  Compactness from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r6-a3-compact} and lower semicontinuity of entropy and support
functions prove goodness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Admissible entropy-preserving Markov approximation}

Let \(E\subset\mathcal A\times\mathcal A\) be the actual transition graph.
For a finite-rate invariant probability \(\nu\), let
\(p(a'\mid\omega^- )\) be a regular conditional transition kernel.  Select a
finite set \(A_L\) carrying all but \(o(1)\) of the free energy and information
function.  Every transition leaving \(A_L\) is represented by its first
return through an admissible connector path in the original graph; no zero
edge is assigned positive mass.

\begin{lemma}[Admissible rate-dense Markov phases]
\label{lem:r6-a3-markov}
For every invariant \(\nu\) with finite induced free energy there are mixing
finite-state Markov measures \(\nu_L\), supported on admissible finite
subgraphs of \(E\), such that
\[
\nu_L\Rightarrow\nu,\qquad
\nu_L(r)\to\nu(r),\qquad
h(\nu_L)\to h(\nu),\qquad
\nu_L(g)\to\nu(g).
\]
The same assertion holds inside any closed survivor component.  Each
\(\nu_L\) is the unique equilibrium state of a bounded finite-cylinder
perturbation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Finite free energy relative to the Gibbs reference implies uniform
integrability of the information function
\(-\log p(a_0\mid a_{-\infty}^{-1})\) and of \(r+|g|\).  Choose \(A_L\) so
their tail integrals are \(o(1)\).  Retain the conditional probabilities of
all admissible transitions inside \(A_L\).  If a transition exits, follow the
actual first-return word supplied by the finite connector property and record
its intermediate symbols as states; this creates no forbidden edge.  The
inserted connector frequency is bounded by the discarded transition mass and
tends to zero.

Approximate the conditional kernel by a finite-memory kernel on this finite
admissible graph.  The entropy difference is the integral of the conditional
information-function difference.  Uniform integrability and martingale
convergence of finite-past conditionals make it \(o(1)\).  The same tail
control gives convergence of \(r\) and \(g\).  Add an \(o(1)\) admissible
connector circulation inside the same component to make the chain mixing;
its entropy and potential cost vanish.  A finite mixing Markov chain is the
unique equilibrium state of the logarithm of its transition matrix, which is
a finite-cylinder potential.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Strong/weak survivor operators}

For a survivor component \(Y\), define
\[
\|h\|_{s,Y}=\sup_a e^{\alpha r(a)}
\bigl(\|h_a\|_{C^\eta}+\operatorname{var}_\eta(h_a)\bigr),
\qquad
\|h\|_{w,Y}=\sum_a e^{\alpha r(a)}
\|h_a\|_{L^1(m_a)}.
\]
The strong unit ball truncated to \(r\le L\) is compact in the weak norm, and
the Gibbs tail is \(O(e^{-cL})\).

\begin{theorem}[Survivor spectral theorem]
\label{thm:r6-a3-survivor}
Every irreducible survivor component with finite pressure has a strong/weak
Lasota--Yorke inequality, a simple leading eigenvalue after its period is
removed, and analytic bounded-cylinder perturbations.  The direct pressure is
the maximum of the recurrent renewal branch and the finitely or countably many
survivor branches, while the recession support function records accumulation
of branch pressures at infinity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Bounded distortion on a return branch gives contraction of variations by the
inverse expansion.  Summation with the exponential return weight gives
\[
\|\mathcal L^nh\|_s\le C\rho^n\|h\|_s+C\|h\|_w.
\]
Finite truncation plus the exponentially vanishing weak tail gives compact
embedding.  Quasicompact Perron--Frobenius theory applies on each irreducible
component; cyclic period is handled by an iterate.  Entrance, exit, and return
operators act on the same weak boundary space, so the renewal resolvent is
valid on its common half-plane.  If component pressures accumulate along
return lengths tending to infinity, their normalized limits are precisely
encoded by \(q_\infty\), rather than being asserted to form one simple
analytic branch.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The full lower bound}

\begin{theorem}[Collision-time empirical-path LDP]
\label{thm:r6-a3-collision-ldp}
The collision empirical path measures satisfy a full weak LDP with speed
\(n\) and good rate
\[
I_c(\rho)=\inf_{\mathscr P_c(m,\zeta)=\rho}
\mathcal J(m,\zeta).
\]
The lower bound holds at every finite-rate state, including states with
\(\zeta\ne0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The pressure variational principle and exponential tightness of finite path
windows give the closed-set upper bound.  For the lower bound, approximate the
recurrent part by Lemma~\ref{lem:r6-a3-markov}.  Approximate \(\zeta\) by
finitely many long admissible excursion profiles.  A typical recurrent Markov
word and the selected long excursions are concatenated using only the fixed
admissible connector words.  Choose multiplicities so their collision-length
fractions converge to the prescribed normalization.  Gibbs distortion and
the Markov type-counting formula give exponent
\(-n\mathcal J(m,\zeta)+o(n)\).  Modifying only a bounded connector and the
last incomplete block places the empirical path in any prescribed open
neighborhood.  Approximation and goodness complete the lower bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Singularity shielding and two clocks}

Let \(B_{q,\delta}\) indicate that a collision window of radius \(q\) lies
within \(\delta\) of a singularity.  The A2 ambient transfer estimate and the
survivor strong/weak theorem give, for every fixed \(s\ge0\),
\[
Q_c(sB_{q,\delta})-Q_c(0)
\le C(e^s-1)(e^{-c q}+q^C\delta^\alpha).
\]
Choose \(s=s(q,\delta)\uparrow\infty\) only after the bad-set coefficient has
been made small; Chernoff's inequality then yields an exponentially good
shield.

The physical duration \(\mathfrak t(a)\) obeys
\(\tau_-r(a)\le\mathfrak t(a)\le\tau_+r(a)\).  Add to each recession profile
its normalized physical-time occupation.  This enlarges
\(\mathcal Z_\infty\) by a compact continuous coordinate.

\begin{theorem}[Physical-time LDP and terminal surgery]
\label{thm:r6-a3-physical-ldp}
The physical empirical path measures satisfy a full LDP with speed \(T\) and
the contraction of \(\mathcal J\) by the physical profile map.  Both collision
and physical lower bounds can hit deterministic horizons: a terminal surgery
changes one connector and one final excursion segment, hence changes
\(o(n)\) collisions and \(o(T)\) physical time and has subexponential Gibbs
cost.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After the singularity shield, collision expansion and roof integration are
continuous on every finite window.  The finite-horizon inequalities make the
physical normalization continuous on the recurrent and recession coordinates.
Apply the extended contraction theorem.  For terminal surgery, use the finite
connector set to enter a fixed regular loop.  Repeat that loop until the
horizon is crossed and stop inside its final block.  The discrepancy is
bounded by the selected truncation length plus a connector; in the ordered
limit this is sublinear, and bounded distortion makes its logarithmic cost
sublinear.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Information projections and cotangents}

For a continuous finite-dimensional macro map \(C\), compactness of rate
sublevels gives minimizers.  Conditioning neighborhoods are required to be
LDP-regular: their interiors and closures have the same constrained infimum.
On the weighted continuous potential space, quotient constants and rational-
time coboundaries by their closed span.

\begin{theorem}[Round-six A3 closure]
\label{thm:r6-a3-main}
The Sinai billiard has full collision- and physical-time empirical-path LDPs
on a recession-completed state space.  Macroscopic long excursions are
retained by an explicit defect profile measure, entropy-dense approximants use
only admissible edges, and both random-clock lower bounds include terminal
surgery.  LDP-regular constraint neighborhoods yield Gibbs conditioning on
the compact minimizer set, and cotangents live in the closed weighted
coboundary quotient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r6-a3-variational},
\ref{thm:r6-a3-survivor}, \ref{thm:r6-a3-collision-ldp}, and
\ref{thm:r6-a3-physical-ldp}, together with
Lemma~\ref{lem:r6-a3-markov}.  Compactness gives information projections; the
standard rate-gap argument gives conditioning for LDP-regular neighborhoods.
Closedness of the coboundary span makes the cotangent quotient Hausdorff.
\end{proof}
