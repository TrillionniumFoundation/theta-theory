# Referee Report — Round 18 (Independent Harsh Review)

**Paper:** A3 — *Full Liouville Empirical-Path Large Deviations and Information Projections for Finite-Horizon Sinai Billiards*  
**Version reviewed:** `revision/round17-referee-positive-closure-11paper-2026-09-01@d13b48757f85d9ec1c6998f89c861845094891fa`  
**Controlling source:** `ROUND17_POSITIVE_CLOSURE.tex` (`A3_ENTROPY_CONTROLLED_STOPPED_LDP.tex`)  
**Date:** 1 September 2026  
**Editorial recommendation:** **REJECT**  
**Present mathematical status:** The asserted collision- and physical-clock path LDPs are not proved for the Sinai billiard.

## 1. Overall assessment

The revision addresses a legitimate earlier objection: the exact Gibbs entropy variational formula can indeed be applied to arbitrary predictable changes of measure without assigning those controlled laws the stationary transfer operator's spectral gap. The insistence that a complete terminal branch pay its complete selection entropy is also conceptually preferable to charging an arbitrary fraction of a branch cost.

The paper nevertheless fails at the first model-identification step. It replaces the deterministic induced Sinai process by a countable one-step Markov edge chain with branchwise constant marks, but does not prove that this chain represents the billiard law. The rest of the manuscript is consequently an abstract entropy-control sketch for a different process. The topology, compactness, recovery, terminal contraction, and clock-change arguments are also stated at a level far below what is needed for a full level-3 stopped-path LDP.

## 2. Decisive mathematical objections

### 2.1. [FATAL] The reference Sinai process is not shown to be the Markov chain used in `thm:r17-a3-entropy`

The manuscript introduces a countable graph `\mathcal G=(V,E)` and a transition kernel `p(e\mid v)`, then treats the induced edge sequence as a one-step Markov chain. No derivation of this kernel from the Sinai invariant/Gibbs measure is provided. For a general Young-tower or countable Markov coding, conditional probabilities depend on the full past through a `g`-function with summable variation; they are not automatically functions only of the current vertex.

This is not a speculative concern: Paper A4 in the same revision explicitly defines the next-edge probability as `g(e\mid x)` for a left-infinite history `x` and assumes summable variations. The A3 one-step kernel and the A4 history kernel cannot both be exact representations unless a nontrivial Markov reduction/lumpability theorem is proved. No such theorem appears.

Moreover, the roof, collision count, homology, singularity exposure, and complete excursion path are not generally constants of a coarse return branch. Position inside a branch remains continuous, and the roof varies with that position. To make an exact edge process, the state/edge would have to retain the relevant continuous coordinate or a conditional kernel on marked excursions. The paper instead assigns `r(e)`, `\tau(e)`, `\kappa(e)`, and `\gamma_e` to a countable edge as fixed marks.

Therefore Theorem `thm:r17-a3-entropy`, while a valid entropy identity for a specified discrete reference chain, has not been connected to the original billiard experiment. Every subsequent LDP conclusion is about an unconstructed surrogate.

### 2.2. [FATAL] The exponential Gibbs moment is assumed rather than proved in the required marked state

The paper assumes

\[
\sup_v\sum_{s(e)=v}p(e\mid v)e^{\eta(r(e)+\tau(e)+\mathfrak s(e))}<\infty.
\]

No definition of `\mathfrak s` is given that makes it both coercive for the proposed path topology and exponentially integrable under the actual Sinai return law. Exponential return tails do not automatically give exponential control of all singularity, oscillation, normalized-path, and recession-profile coordinates introduced later. Uniformity over source vertices is also nontrivial in a countable coding.

This single display is used to justify entropy compactness, exponential tightness, path compact containment, terminal control, and recovery. Those are distinct estimates and cannot be compressed into an undefined “singularity/excursion compactness cost.”

### 2.3. [FATAL] The state spaces and compact sets in `prop:r17-a3-space` are not mathematically specified

The proposed edge metric combines inverse return length, local Skorokhod distance of a normalized excursion, and source/target vertex metrics. The following essential points are missing:

1. the underlying path state and its one-sided singular germs;
2. the precise local Skorokhod topology and the behavior at variable lifetimes;
3. the metric on the countable vertex space;
4. the recession completion and which sequences have recession limits;
5. the compatibility equations linking empirical flow, expanded paths, clocks, and the pointed terminal edge;
6. a proof that the completed marked edge space is Polish.

The “weighted weak topology generated by continuous functions dominated by `1+r+\tau+\mathfrak s`” is not automatically Polish, and bounded first moment alone does not make its sublevels compact unless the weight is proper/coercive and uniformly integrable. The phrase “after taking their closed balance-compatible hull” does not define a compact set; closed hulls of relatively compact sets can be useful only after relative compactness and closedness of all compatibility relations are established.

The claimed continuity of `\pi_{\rm phys}` is likewise put into the topology by declaration. Forgetting a terminal completion is delicate precisely when the completion length diverges or when normalized paths have discontinuities at the stopping point.

### 2.4. [FATAL] Entropy expectation bounds do not prove the controlled compactness used in the Laplace principle

Lemma `lem:r17-a3-entropy-tight` is an elementary entropy inequality. From a normalized expected entropy bound it gives certain first-moment bounds if the corresponding exponential moment is already available. The proof of `thm:r17-a3-tight` then leaps to superlinear thresholds, exponential tightness at speed `N`, and compactness for entropy-controlled sequences.

Several conversions are missing:

- the number of return edges before collision time `N` is random;
- the entropy representation is first written for a fixed number `n` of edges;
- a stopped control can alter the distribution of long edges and hence the relation between `n` and `N`;
- expectation bounds under controls do not by themselves supply deterministic compact sets on which the controlled variables are tight in every path coordinate;
- an LDP-level exponential estimate under the reference law does not automatically produce the liminf compactness required for every nearly optimal controlled law.

A complete weak-convergence/Laplace-principle proof must formulate the controlled stopped process on one probability space, establish uniform integrability for all clocks and marks, and prove tightness of the occupation and terminal variables. None of that is done.

### 2.5. [FATAL] The recovery theorem is far stronger than its proof

Theorem `thm:r17-a3-relax` claims recovery for every balanced finite-cost edge flow, including prescribed recession profiles, by legal finite-memory Markov kernels and connector words. The proof merely truncates to a finite edge set, adds a small connector circulation, and invokes the ergodic theorem.

This argument requires, but does not establish:

1. quantitative strong connectivity of the legal return graph;
2. connector words whose total roof, collision count, singularity cost, and log likelihood are negligible on the chosen scale;
3. approximation of source and target marginals without losing absolute continuity with respect to the reference kernel;
4. convergence of unbounded marked coordinates and expanded paths, not merely bounded edge tests;
5. construction of actual legal edges realizing every claimed recession profile and scale;
6. control of the entropy of rare inserted long edges.

A recession atom in an abstract completion need not be the limit of real legal branches with the required probability asymptotic. Saying “choose an actual legal edge” assumes exactly the density and cost theorem that must be proved.

### 2.6. [FATAL] The terminal-prefix rate is not the stated negative logarithm in a continuous model

For a discrete edge chain, contracting the entropy of a selected edge onto a measurable prefix event can give `-\log P(prefix)`. In the billiard setting, however, an exact continuous path prefix typically has reference probability zero. Then

\[
-\log\sum_{a\in\mathcal C(v,\zeta)}p(a\mid v)
\]

is either undefined in the stated countable model or infinite for exact continuous data. The “analogous conditional integral when the completion set is uncountable” is not a definition of a large-deviation cost.

One needs a reference measure on prefix space, a regular conditional density or a neighborhood-based local LDP, and a proof of the entropy contraction under the exact topology. The current lemma conflates conditioning on a positive-probability discrete event with observing a point of a continuous path variable.

### 2.7. [FATAL] The rate function is not given and the Laplace-principle proof is circularly summarized

The collision-clock rate `\mathcal I_c^{\rm aug}` is defined only as “the lower-semicontinuous envelope of the sum” of several informal costs, subject to unspecified equations. This is not an explicit rate function. Its domain, normalization, recession terms, and terminal contribution are not stated as a formula from which lower semicontinuity and compact sublevels can be checked.

The proof of `thm:r17-a3-collision` then lists the desired ingredients—variational representation, compactness, liminf, recovery, goodness—and says they yield the theorem. Since the compactness, relaxation, and recovery results are themselves unproved, this paragraph merely restates the standard proof architecture.

The physical-clock theorem adds another nontrivial layer. Finite horizon compares individual flight times, but deterministic physical-time stopping in an induced process with a macroscopic terminal return requires an exact time-change map, exponential equivalence or extended contraction hypotheses, and a recovery that hits the physical horizon. These are asserted, not verified.

### 2.8. [MAJOR] Conditioning by the A2 LLT does not follow from the unconditioned LDP

Even if A2 were available, a local limit estimate for the denominator and an unconditioned joint LDP do not automatically imply a conditional LDP with the asserted rate. The numerator must be controlled on the same shrinking conditioning window, generally through a joint local LDP or exponentially accurate tilting argument. The paper says only that “the numerator is handled by the joint augmented LDP,” which is insufficient for exact lattice/continuous conditioning.

## 3. Dependency consequences

A3 depends on the unproved A2 LLT, but it also fails independently at the process-identification and recovery steps. Its outputs are used in A4's path/history platform and in later C2/D1 constructions. Those downstream uses cannot be credited.

## 4. Requirements before reconsideration

A future paper would have to:

1. construct an exact symbolic/history representation of the Sinai law, retaining all continuous marks needed for roof and path observables;
2. formulate the entropy variational representation on that exact state, not on an assumed one-step chain;
3. define a complete Polish stopped/recession state space and prove compactness of rate sublevels;
4. prove controlled tightness with random edge counts and both deterministic clocks;
5. establish a legal-flow approximation theorem with quantitative connector and entropy estimates;
6. replace the point-prefix formula by a rigorous conditional-density or local-LDP contraction;
7. state and prove an explicit good rate function and a conditional LDP theorem.

## 5. Recommendation

**Reject.** The revision identifies a sound entropy-control principle, but the process to which it is applied is not shown to be the Sinai billiard process. The main LDP proof consists of unproved topology, compactness, relaxation, and recovery assertions. The distance from a complete proof is foundational, not editorial.
