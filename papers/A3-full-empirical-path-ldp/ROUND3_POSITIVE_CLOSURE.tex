\section{Round-four referee closure: a shielded two-clock level-three theorem}
\label{sec:r3-a3}

This section proves the coding, exponential approximation, random-clock upper
and lower bounds, finite-rate support, and cotangent quotient which were only
listed in the first circulation.  The construction uses the uniform billiard
packet of Paper A2, but no level-three assertion is imported from it.

\subsection{A finite-connector induced code}

Let \(Y\) be a Young magnet with finitely many crossing components
\(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m\).  Cut each first-return branch by its complete finite
collision itinerary and record the tuple
\[
 a=(i,j,r,\mathfrak t,\zeta),
\]
where \(i,j\) are the entrance and exit components, \(r\) is the collision
length, \(\mathfrak t\) the physical duration, and \(\zeta\) the regular
finite orbit branch.  Denote the alphabet by \(\mathcal A\).

\begin{lemma}[Finite connectors and exponential marks]
\label{lem:r3-a3-connect}
The magnet and its refinement may be chosen so that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item there is a finite set \(\mathcal C\subset\mathcal A^*\) such that for
      every pair of symbols \(a,b\) one of the words in \(\mathcal C\)
      connects \(a\) to \(b\);
\item the induced Jacobian potential \(\widehat\phi\) has summable
      variations;
\item for some \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \int e^{\eta r+\eta\mathfrak t}\,d\widehat{\mathbb P}<\infty;
\]
\item the natural extension and suspension of the code push forward to the
      Liouville collision and physical path laws.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Mixing of the collision map gives, for each ordered pair
\((Y_i,Y_j)\), a regular orbit segment from a full unstable crossing of
\(Y_i\) to a full stable crossing of \(Y_j\).  Choose one segment for each of
the finitely many pairs and refine the return partition along these segments.
Their words form \(\mathcal C\), proving finite primitivity.  Homogeneous
bounded distortion gives
\[
 \operatorname{var}_n(\widehat\phi)
 \le C\rho^n,
\]
so the variations are summable.  The growth lemma gives an exponential tail
for the first return.  Finite horizon yields
\(\tau_-r\le\mathfrak t\le\tau_+r\), hence the joint exponential moment.
The tower projection follows every recorded collision and therefore agrees
with the billiard orbit outside the null singular set; suspension gives the
physical path law.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Defect-completed renewal and survivor pressure}

Let \(Y_R\) be the finite-connector magnet fixed in Paper A2 and let
\(T_Y\) be its first positive hit.  A macroscopic excursion with
\(T_Y\asymp n\) has ordinary exponential cost and must be retained.  We do so
by keeping, in addition to the induced renewal operator, the complete survivor
operator on paths which avoid \(Y_R\).

For a bounded dynamically H\"older collision cylinder \(F\), let
\(\widehat F(a)=\sum_{j=0}^{r(a)-1}F\circ\Theta^j\) on a return branch
\(a\), and define
\[
 \mathscr R_{R,F}(z)g
 =\sum_{a}e^{\widehat\phi_R(a)+\widehat F(a)-zr(a)}g\circ h_a.
\]
Its leading spectral radius is strictly decreasing in real \(z\); denote by
\(q_{Y,R}(F)\) the unique value at which it is one.

For integers \(m,K\), cut the collision space at singularity depth \(m\) and
homogeneity index \(K\), delete every component hitting \(Y_R\), and let
\(\mathscr E_{R,F}^{m,K}(n)\) be the positive transfer operator for surviving
\(n\) collisions.  Put
\[
 q_{\infty,R}^{m,K}(F)
 =\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \log\|\mathscr E_{R,F}^{m,K}(n)\mathbf1\|_\infty.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Survivor pressure limit]
\label{lem:r4-a3-survivor}
The limits above exist, are independent of the admissible norm, and satisfy
\[
 q_{\infty,R}^{m,K}(F)\uparrow q_{\infty,R}(F)
 \quad\text{as }m,K\to\infty.
\]
The limiting functional is convex, locally Lipschitz in the uniform norm, and
obeys
\[
 q_{\infty,R}(F+c)=q_{\infty,R}(F)+c.
\]
Moreover
\[
 q_{\infty,R}(F)
 =\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \log\mathbb E_R^c
 \left[e^{S_nF};T_Y>n\right].
\]

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \((m,K)\), the survivor dynamics is a finite union of uniformly
hyperbolic Markov components, so the positive matrix cocycle has a Perron
root.  Concatenating survivor words and using bounded distortion gives the
quasi-multiplicativity
\[
 C^{-1}Z_nZ_k\le Z_{n+k+\ell_*}\le CZ_nZ_k
\]
with one bounded connector length \(\ell_*\) whenever the two words lie in the
same survivor component; summing over the finitely many components gives the
existence of the logarithmic limit.  Enlarging \(m,K\) adds survivor words,
so the pressures increase.

Every finite regular survivor word occurs in some truncation.  Conversely a
word omitted at level \((m,K)\) enters a depth or homogeneity tail whose
partition weight is bounded by the uniform growth estimate of A2.  Taking
first \(n\to\infty\) and then \(m,K\to\infty\) identifies the monotone limit
with the microscopic no-hit partition function.  The uniform-norm Lipschitz
bound follows from
\(e^{-n\|F-G\|_\infty}Z_n(G)\le Z_n(F)\le
 e^{n\|F-G\|_\infty}Z_n(G)\).  Convexity is H\"older's inequality and the
cash identity is immediate.
\end{proof}

Let
\[
 Q_R^c(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \log\mathbb E_R^c e^{S_nF}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Defect-completed collision pressure]
\label{thm:r3-a3-block}
For every bounded dynamically H\"older collision cylinder,
\[
 \boxed{Q_R^c(F)=\max\{q_{Y,R}(F),q_{\infty,R}(F)\}.}
\]
If one branch is strictly larger, it is analytic on a neighborhood of \(F\)
and has a unique equilibrium path law.  On the induced branch the law has
positive magnet frequency and is the Palm lift of the induced Gibbs phase; on
the survivor branch it has zero magnet frequency.  At equality the
subdifferential is the convex hull of the two compact equilibrium sets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Decompose every word at its first and last complete visit to \(Y_R\).  The
operator generating function is
\[
 \mathscr G_F(z)=
 \mathscr E_F^{\rm in}(z)
 (I-\mathscr R_{R,F}(z))^{-1}
 \mathscr E_F^{\rm out}(z)
 +\mathscr G_{F,\infty}(z).
\]
All entrance and exit series are analytic to the left of both candidate
abscissae because the complete return tail is exponential.  The renewal term
loses boundedness exactly when the simple Perron eigenvalue of
\(\mathscr R_{R,F}(z)\) reaches one.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:r4-a3-survivor}, the second term loses boundedness at the
survivor abscissa.  Positivity prevents cancellation between the two terms,
so the scalar partition-function abscissa is their maximum.

A strict induced winner has the simple eigenvalue and spectral gap of the
weighted renewal operator.  A strict survivor winner is obtained as the
monotone limit of finite survivor Perron projectors; the strict gap from the
induced branch gives uniform tightness and excludes loss of mass to return
words.  Analytic perturbation and differentiation of the normalized Perron
root give the stated laws.  At equality, convex directional derivatives of a
maximum are precisely the convex hull of the active derivatives.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exposed phases and the full collision-path LDP}

Let \(\mathscr D_R\) be the algebra generated by finite collision cylinders,
finite-depth survivor indicators smoothed on the graph completion, and
singularity-shield functions.  It separates points and contains constants, so
Stone--Weierstrass makes it uniformly dense in
\(C(\Omega_R^c)\).

\begin{lemma}[Rate-dense exposed collision phases]
\label{lem:r3-a3-exposed}
Every invariant law \(\nu\) with finite pressure-dual cost admits unique
exposed phases \(\nu_j=DQ_R^c(F_j)\), \(F_j\in\mathscr D_R\), such that
\[
 \nu_j\Rightarrow\nu,
 \qquad
 (Q_R^c)^*(\nu_j)\to(Q_R^c)^*(\nu).
\]
This includes zero-magnet-frequency laws and coexistence mixtures.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(\nu(Y_R)>0\), induce on \(Y_R\), truncate return length and homogeneity,
and replace discarded entrance--exit components by the fixed finite connector
of the same type.  The discarded frequency and the normalized free-energy
cost tend to zero because \(\nu\) has finite direct dual cost.  A generic
finite-cylinder perturbation makes the resulting finite-state equilibrium
unique.

If \(\nu(Y_R)=0\), restrict it to the increasing finite survivor systems.
Ergodic decomposition and the closing lemma on each finite hyperbolic survivor
system approximate it by periodic orbit measures with convergence of entropy
plus potential cost.  Pinning potentials, separated by more than the bounded
connector length, make those periodic measures unique equilibrium phases.
Monotone convergence of survivor pressure gives convergence of the dual cost.
Long convex concatenations approximate nonergodic and coexistence laws while
a perturbation of size tending to zero selects a unique phase.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full collision empirical-path LDP]
\label{thm:r3-a3-collision-ldp}
The empirical collision-path measures
\[
 L_n^c=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\delta_{\Theta^j\omega}
\]
satisfy a full LDP on \(\mathcal P(\Omega_R^c)\), with speed \(n\) and good
rate
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal I_R^c(\nu)=
 \sup_{F\in C(\Omega_R^c)}
 \{\nu(F)-Q_R^c(F)+Q_R^c(0)\}.}
\]
It is infinite off invariant probabilities.  On positive-frequency phases it
reduces to the Abramov--Kac induced ratio; on zero-frequency phases it is the
survivor-pressure dual.  No macroscopic excursion is discarded.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The graph-completed collision path space is compact.  Exponential Chebyshev
and the pressure limit give the upper bound for every finite collection of
cylinders; compactness upgrades the projective bound to closed sets.  At a
unique exposed phase, the normalized Perron change of measure and its spectral
gap give the neighborhood lower bound with the dual cost.  The
rate-preserving approximation in Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-a3-exposed} extends the
lower bound to all finite-rate laws.  Invariance follows by the standard
endpoint estimate
\(|L_n^c(F-F\circ\Theta)|\le2\|F\|_\infty/n\).  The induced and survivor
identities follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a3-block}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Singularity exclusion and continuous graph factors}

Let \(\mathcal S_q\) be the finite union of singularity faces which can enter
a collision window of radius \(q\).  For a bounded continuous approximation
\(b_{q,\delta}\) of its \(\delta\)-neighborhood, homogeneous distortion gives
\[
 Q_R^c(sb_{q,\delta})-Q_R^c(0)
 \le C(e^s-1)e^{\kappa q}\delta^\alpha.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Infinite cost of singular-frequency mass]
\label{lem:r3-a3-frequency}
For every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \lim_{q\to\infty}
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0\atop e^{\kappa q}\delta^\alpha\downarrow0}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \log\mathbb P_R^c\left(
 L_n^c(N_\delta(\mathcal S_q))>\epsilon
 \right)=-\infty.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Chernoff's bound is
\[
 -s\epsilon+C(e^s-1)e^{\kappa q}\delta^\alpha.
\]
Choose
\(s=\frac12\log((e^{\kappa q}\delta^\alpha)^{-1})\) and take the ordered
limit.  Unlike the old return-length truncation, the bad observable is bounded,
so its source may tend to infinity without invoking an unbounded mark moment.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exponentially good graph-completed path coordinates]
\label{thm:r3-a3-factor}
Every bounded continuous collision or physical path cylinder has continuous
regular-component approximants whose discrepancy has arbitrarily large
collision-time exponential cost.  In particular the one-sided values at a
grazing or multiple-collision face are retained by the graph completion rather
than identified or deleted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed window, remove a \(\delta\)-neighborhood of \(\mathcal S_q\).
On the remaining finitely cut regular components, collision expansion and
physical suspension are uniformly continuous.  Extend each component value
to the graph completion and use a partition of unity.  Any discrepancy larger
than a fixed tolerance requires a positive empirical frequency in the removed
neighborhood, whose cost tends to infinity by
Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-a3-frequency}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Bounded-roof passage to physical time}

Finite horizon gives
\[
 0<\tau_-\le\tau_R\le\tau_+<\infty.
\]
For an invariant collision-path law \(\nu\), define its physical suspension
Palm law by
\[
 \int F\,d\mathscr S_p\nu
 =\frac1{\nu(\tau_R)}
 \int\int_0^{\tau_R(\omega)}
 F(\Theta_t\Gamma_R^p\omega)\,dt\,d\nu(\omega).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Bounded terminal clock surgery]
\label{lem:r3-a3-surgery}
For a deterministic physical horizon \(T\), the number of complete collision
flights differs from \(T/\nu(\tau_R)\) by the empirical clock fluctuation, and
the two terminal residual pieces have total duration at most \(2\tau_+\).
After division by \(T\), the residual contribution is uniformly \(o(1)\) and
has no separate exponential cost.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Stop at the last complete free flight before \(T\).  There is one initial and
one terminal partial flight, each bounded by \(\tau_+\).  The remaining clock
is the sum of the bounded roof observable, so its empirical mean is already a
continuous coordinate in the collision-path LDP.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full collision- and physical-time empirical-path LDP]
\label{thm:r3-a3-two-clock}
The collision LDP is Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a3-collision-ldp}.  The physical
empirical path measures satisfy a full LDP with speed \(T\) and good rate
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal I_R^p(\rho)=
 \inf_{\mathscr S_p\nu=\rho}
 \frac{\mathcal I_R^c(\nu)}{\nu(\tau_R)}.}
\]
Both rates are independent of the chosen finite-connector inducing magnet.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Jointly contract the collision empirical-path LDP with the bounded continuous
roof average and with the graph-completed suspension coordinates of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a3-factor}.  On a clock-mean slice
\(\nu(\tau_R)=a\), collision speed \(n\) and physical speed \(T\) satisfy
\(n/T\to1/a\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-a3-surgery} removes the bounded residuals.
The extended contraction upper and lower bounds give the displayed rate.
Because \(\tau_R\) is bounded away from zero and infinity, compact collision
rate sublevels have compact physical images, so the rate is good.  The direct
pressure and its dual are microscopic collision-path objects; changing the
inducing magnet changes only the renewal representation, proving
presentation independence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Presentation independence]
\label{prop:r3-a3-independent}
Two admissible inducing magnets yield the same direct pressures
\(Q_R^c\), collision rate \(\mathcal I_R^c\), and physical rate
\(\mathcal I_R^p\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both renewal decompositions equal the logarithmic moment limit of the same
collision empirical path law.  Equality of direct pressures on the dense
cylinder algebra, continuity, and convex duality give equality of the rates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-rate support and preparation}

Let \(\overline\Omega_{\rm det}\) be the graph completion which records the
incoming state and both one-sided continuations at a singular collision.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-rate deterministic support]
\label{prop:r3-a3-support}
If \(\mathcal I_R^c(\nu)<\infty\) or \(\mathcal I_R^p(\nu)<\infty\), then
\(\nu(\overline\Omega_{\rm det})=1\).  Every finite-rate law gives zero mass
to genuinely multivalued singular continuations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every shielded factor is supported on the closed graph completion.  The LDP
upper bound and exponential approximation make the rate infinite outside it.
If a law charged genuinely singular continuations, every sufficiently small
singularity neighborhood would have a fixed positive empirical frequency.
Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-a3-frequency} makes the cost of that event tend to infinity.
\end{proof}

Let \(C\) be a continuous affine finite-dimensional macro map on physical
path probabilities and let \(a\) be finite-rate feasible.

\begin{theorem}[Information projections and Gibbs conditioning]
\label{thm:r3-a3-projection}
The minimizer set
\[
 \mathcal M(a)=\operatorname*{argmin}_{C(\nu)=a}\mathcal I_R^p(\nu)
\]
is nonempty and compact.  If the minimizer is a unique exposed point, then
conditioning on shrinking continuity neighborhoods of \(a\) concentrates
exponentially at that law, including at a uniform interior time root.  Without
uniqueness, every subsequential conditioned limit is a probability mixture
supported on \(\mathcal M(a)\), and the phase label is retained.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A minimizing sequence lies in a compact rate sublevel; continuity of \(C\)
and lower semicontinuity give attainment.  Exposed uniqueness gives a positive
rate gap outside every neighborhood of the minimizer.  The LDP upper bound
controls the numerator and the lower bound at the minimizer controls the
conditioning denominator.  A uniform interior root changes only a vanishing
boundary fraction.  In the nonunique case the same rate-gap argument puts all
limit mass on the compact minimizer set; disintegration over that set gives
the phase mixture.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Weighted-strict cotangents and the exact coboundary kernel}

Let \(W\ge1\) be a continuous path weight with compact sublevels and with
\[
 \mathcal I_R^p(\rho)\ge c\rho(W)-C.
\]
Put
\[
 C_W(\Omega_R^p)
 =\{F\in C(\Omega_R^p):\|F/W\|_\infty<\infty\}
\]
and give it the weighted bounded-strict topology \(\beta_W\): bounded
\(W\)-norm and compact-uniform convergence after division by \(W\).  Its
continuous dual is the space \(\mathcal M_W\) of countably additive signed
Radon measures with finite \(W\)-moment.

Define
\[
 \mathscr N_W=
 \overline{\operatorname{span}}^{\beta_W}
 \left(
 \mathbb R\mathbf1+
 \{U-U\circ\Theta_t:
 U\in C_W,\ t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\}
 \right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Radon annihilator and Hausdorff cotangent]
\label{thm:r3-a3-cotangent}
The quotient \(C_W/\mathscr N_W\) is Hausdorff and complete.  A potential
\(F\) has the same integral under every invariant finite-weight probability
as a constant \(c\) if and only if
\(F-c\in\overline{\operatorname{span}}
\{U-U\circ\Theta_t\}^{\beta_W}\).  Consequently the pressure cotangent is
exactly the quotient by constants and flow coboundaries; no finitely additive
or distributional separator occurs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Closedness gives the Hausdorff quotient.  Every invariant Radon probability
annihilates every rational-time coboundary and hence its closure.

Conversely suppose \(F-c\) is not in that closed subspace.  Hahn--Banach in
the locally convex space \((C_W,\beta_W)\) gives a continuous separator
\(L\).  By the weighted Riesz representation theorem, \(L(G)=\int G\,d\mu\)
for a countably additive signed Radon measure \(\mu\in\mathcal M_W\).  The
separator annihilates every \(U-U\circ\Theta_t\), so
\((\Theta_t)_\#\mu=\mu\) for rational \(t\), and then for all \(t\) by
right-continuity and dominated convergence.  Its Jordan parts are invariant
because the flow acts by lattice isometries on signed measures.  Constants in
the annihilated subspace give \(\mu(\Omega)=0\); normalizing a nonzero positive
Jordan part and subtracting the other produces invariant probabilities which
do not give the same integral to \(F\), a contradiction.  Thus the stated
closure is exact.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Strict pressure subdifferential]
\label{cor:r4-a3-strict-tangent}
For every \(F\) in a bounded \(\beta_W\)-source set, the physical path
pressure is finite and its subdifferential is the nonempty compact set of
maximizing invariant finite-weight Radon probabilities.  At an exposed phase,
the pressure derivative is that phase law on the quotient
\(C_W/\mathscr N_W\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The coercive inequality confines the variational supremum to a compact rate
sublevel.  Weighted-strict continuity and the Radon dual identify subgradients
with countably additive maximizing measures.  The preceding theorem identifies
its kernel.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-four A3 closure]
\label{thm:r3-a3-main}
The periodic finite-horizon Sinai billiard satisfies full weak empirical-path
LDPs in collision and physical time with the good rates of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a3-two-clock}.  The rates are presentation independent,
finite only on deterministic graph-completed paths, support compact
information projections, and have cotangents in the Hausdorff quotient
\(\mathscr A_\beta/\mathscr N_\beta\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r3-a3-block},
\ref{thm:r3-a3-factor}, \ref{thm:r3-a3-two-clock},
\ref{thm:r3-a3-projection}, and \ref{thm:r3-a3-cotangent}, together with
Propositions~\ref{prop:r3-a3-independent} and
\ref{prop:r3-a3-support}.
\end{proof}
