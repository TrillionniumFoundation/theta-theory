\section{Round-seven reconstruction: canonical excursion compactification and an attainable recession rate}
\label{sec:r7-a3}

This section replaces the round-six controlling module.  There is no threshold
$L_N$.  Recurrent and escaping clock mass are coordinates of one compact
length-weighted marked empirical measure.  The recession rate is first defined
as a Gamma limit of actual excursion laws and is only afterwards identified
with a convex conjugate.  Periodic survivor components retain their period;
no forbidden edge is inserted to manufacture mixing.

\subsection{The intrinsic profile kernel}

Let $\mathcal A$ be the inducing alphabet, $r(a)$ the return length, and
$\mathcal Z$ the compact space of probability measures on a fixed finite-window
collision path space.  A branch $a$ contains a continuum of points.  Its
profile is therefore the probability kernel
\[
 K_a=\frac{1}{\mu_a(Y_a)}
       \int_{Y_a}\frac1{r(a)}\sum_{j=0}^{r(a)-1}
       \delta_{\Theta^j\Gamma(y)}\,d\mu_a(y)
 \in\mathcal Z,
\]
where $\mu_a$ is the normalized Gibbs conditional law on the branch.  Bounded
distortion implies that replacing $\mu_a$ by any conditional law with density
between $C^{-1}$ and $C$ changes every finite-window test by $O(r(a)^{-1})$.
Thus the boundary profile is intrinsic.

Let
\[
 \overline{\mathcal X}=
 \overline{\{(r(a)^{-1},K_a):a\in\mathcal A\}}
 \subset[0,1]\times\mathcal Z.
\]
Points with first coordinate zero are recession profiles.  This is a compact
space.

For an excursion sequence $a_0,\ldots,a_{N-1}$ put
$R_N=\sum_jr(a_j)$ and define the single length-weighted empirical measure
\[
 \Lambda_N=\frac1{R_N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}r(a_j)
 \delta_{(r(a_j)^{-1},K_{a_j})}
 \in\mathcal P(\overline{\mathcal X}).
\]
Its restriction to $x>0$ determines the recurrent occupation measure and its
restriction to $x=0$ is the defect profile.  No diagonal threshold enters.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical recurrent--defect decomposition]
\label{lem:r7-a3-compact}
The sequence $\Lambda_N$ is tight, and every limit $\Lambda$ decomposes
uniquely as
\[
 \Lambda=\Lambda_{\rm rec}+\Lambda_\infty,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{supp}\Lambda_\infty\subset\{0\}\times\mathcal Z.
\]
Writing
\[
 m(da)=x\,\Lambda_{\rm rec}(d(x,K))
 \quad\hbox{on }x=r(a)^{-1}
\]
and $\zeta$ for the projection of $\Lambda_\infty$ onto $\mathcal Z$, one has
\[
 \int r\,dm+\zeta(1)=1.
\]
The physical collision-path map
\[
 \mathscr P_c(\Lambda)=
 \int_{x>0}K\,d\Lambda(x,K)+
 \int_{x=0}K\,d\Lambda(x,K)
\]
is continuous.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Compactness of $\overline{\mathcal X}$ gives tightness.  The sets $x>0$ and
$x=0$ determine a unique Lebesgue decomposition.  The identity follows
already at finite $N$ because
\[
 \int_{x>0}x^{-1}x\,d\Lambda_N=1
\]
after the escaped mass is interpreted at the boundary; passing to the limit is
just weak convergence of the single marked measure, not a comparison of two
separately chosen thresholds.  The map $(x,K)\mapsto K$ is continuous in the
weak topology, so its barycenter is continuous.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Actual excursion laws and the recession Gamma limit}

For a finite family of path tests $F=(F_1,\ldots,F_d)$ let
$Z_a=(K_a(F_1),\ldots,K_a(F_d))$.  For $L<\infty$ define the constrained
actual excursion cost
\[
 c_L(z)=\inf\left\{-\frac1{r(a)}\log w(a):
 r(a)\ge L,\ |Z_a-z|\le L^{-1}\right\},
\]
with value $+\infty$ when the set is empty.  Here $w(a)$ is the exact induced
Gibbs weight, including distortion normalization.  Define
\[
 c_\infty(z)=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{L\to\infty}c_L(z),
\]
first on every finite projection and then by Dawson--Gartner on $\mathcal Z$.
This definition only credits profiles generated by actual admissible
excursions.

\begin{theorem}[Excursion-profile recovery]
\label{thm:r7-a3-excursion}
For every $z\in\mathcal Z$ with $c_\infty(z)<\infty$ there is a sequence of
actual admissible branches $a_n$ such that
\[
 r(a_n)\to\infty,
 \qquad K_{a_n}\to z,
 \qquad -r(a_n)^{-1}\log w(a_n)\to c_\infty(z).
\]
Conversely every such sequence has lower cost at least $c_\infty(z)$.  The
rate is good and equals
\[
 c_\infty(z)=\sup_{F\in C(\mathcal Z)}
 \{z(F)-q_\infty(F)+q_\infty(0)\},
\]
where the limit defining $q_\infty$ exists along the actual excursion net; no
limsup over source-dependent subsequences is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On a finite projection, the functions $c_L$ are equicoercive because
$\mathcal Z$ is compact.  The diagonal definition of the Gamma limit supplies
a recovery sequence of actual branches for every finite-rate point and the
liminf inequality for every convergent branch sequence.  To make one sequence
work for all tests, choose a countable convergence-determining family and use
the standard diagonal with projection dimension increasing more slowly than
$r(a_n)$.

The induced specification theorem concatenates two long branches with a
connector of uniformly bounded weight and length.  Hence the Gamma-limit rate
is convex and the actual excursion pressure is subadditive.  Fekete's lemma
therefore gives a genuine limit $q_\infty(F)$ for every continuous $F$.
The Laplace upper bound follows from the liminf property.  Exposed points of
the conjugate are recovered by the tilted maximizing branches; arbitrary
finite-rate points are barycentric limits of exposed points and are realized
by concatenating their recovery branches in rational proportions.  Connector
costs are $o(r)$.  This proves the reverse inequality and the dual formula.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cyclic Markov recovery and the survivor dichotomy}

A closed irreducible survivor graph has period $d\ge1$.  Let its vertices be
$V_0,\ldots,V_{d-1}$ with transitions $V_j\to V_{j+1}$.  A Markov measure on
the original one-step graph is $d$-cyclic; it is mixing only for the $d$th
iterate when $d>1$.

\begin{lemma}[Period-preserving finite-state approximation]
\label{lem:r7-a3-markov}
Every invariant finite-action measure supported on an irreducible survivor
component is approximated in entropy, energy, and all finite path tests by
finite-state irreducible Markov measures with the same period $d$.  Their
restrictions to each $V_j$ are mixing for $T^d$.  All transition edges are
edges of the original survivor graph.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a finite collection of cylinders carrying all but $o(1)$ mass and close
it under one full period.  Approximate the conditional transition kernels by
rational kernels on the existing edges and repair stationarity separately on
each cyclic class.  The flow equations are finite and have a positive solution
because the original invariant flow lies in their relative interior after a
small mixture with a periodic orbit of the same component.  No edge is added.
Entropy continuity for finite kernels and truncation of the action give the
claim.
\end{proof}

For a component $C$ and normalized potential $g$ define the first-return
partition function $Z_C(n)$.  Call $C$ strongly positive recurrent if there is
$\delta>0$ such that the pressure of loops avoiding a fixed finite core is at
most $P_C(g)-\delta$.

\begin{theorem}[SPR--recession dichotomy]
\label{thm:r7-a3-survivor}
Every irreducible survivor component of finite recurrent cost satisfies exactly
one of the following alternatives.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item It is strongly positive recurrent.  Then the Ruelle operator on the
physical quotient space has an isolated simple leading eigenvalue for $T^d$,
analytic perturbation on bounded source balls, and a cyclic family of $d$
equilibrium states for $T$.
\item There are loops escaping every finite core with pressure approaching
$P_C(g)$.  Their normalized profiles define a recession point with no greater
cost, and the component contributes through $c_\infty$, not through a claimed
recurrent spectral gap.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the renewal decomposition at a finite core.  If the avoiding-loop
pressure has a uniform gap, the first-return generating function is analytic
past its leading pole.  The compact core plus the exponential tail gives a
strong/weak Lasota--Yorke inequality and quasicompactness for $T^d$.
Otherwise choose avoiding loops with pressure gap tending to zero.  Their
lengths diverge, and compactness of $\mathcal Z$ gives a profile limit.  The
Gamma-liminf in Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-a3-excursion} bounds its cost by the
limiting loop cost.  The alternatives are mutually exclusive.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A direct singularity shield}

Let $B_{q,\delta}$ be the set of collision words entering the $\delta$-tube of
singularities at depth at least $q$.  The geometric growth estimate on
preimages and the A2 physical-quotient distortion theorem give directly
\[
 \mu\{N_n(B_{q,\delta})\ge\eta n\}
 \le \exp\{-n[\eta(cq+\alpha|\log\delta|)-C]\}.
\]
This estimate is obtained by selecting the bad times and summing cylinder
weights; no pressure source is sent to infinity.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform superexponential shield]
\label{lem:r7-a3-shield}
For every $M$ there are $q$ and $\delta$ such that
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \log\mu\{N_n(B_{q,\delta})\ge\eta n\}\le-M.
\]
The same estimate holds for every bounded tilted source in a fixed compact
chart.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The tilted Radon--Nikodym factor changes each selected cylinder weight by at
most $e^{Cn}$.  Choose $q$ and $|\log\delta|$ so that the geometric exponent
exceeds $M+C$.  This is uniform on the bounded chart.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The recurrent--defect variational principle}

For a limit $\Lambda$ write its recurrent component as $m$ and its recession
component as $\zeta$.  Define
\[
 \mathcal J(\Lambda)=\mathcal J_{\rm rec}(m)
 +\int_{\mathcal Z}c_\infty(z)\,d\zeta(z),
\]
subject to invariance, the normalization of
Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-a3-compact}, and the admissible endpoint constraints.

\begin{theorem}[Full collision-clock LDP]
\label{thm:r7-a3-collision}
The empirical measures $\Lambda_N$ satisfy a good full LDP with rate
$\mathcal J$.  For every finite-rate $\Lambda$, a recovery sequence is obtained
by alternating period-preserving recurrent Markov blocks from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-a3-markov} with actual long-excursion blocks from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-a3-excursion}.  Connector words are admissible and have
sublinear total cost.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finite projections follow from the A2 pressure theorem on SPR components and
from the actual excursion Laplace theorem on the boundary.  Exponential
tightness is automatic on the compact marked state, while
Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-a3-shield} removes singular cylinders.  Dawson--Gartner gives
the upper bound.

For the lower bound, approximate the recurrent part by a finite cyclic Markov
measure and the defect measure by a finite rational combination of excursion
profiles.  Use the recovery sequences from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-a3-excursion} and concatenate with the admissible
specification connectors.  The total connector length and logarithmic weight
are $o(R_N)$.  The single measure $\Lambda_N$ converges to the prescribed
$\Lambda$, and the costs add.  Lower semicontinuity and finite approximations
complete the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact deterministic-clock completion}

Choose two regular loops in the base component with coprime lengths $\ell_1$
and $\ell_2$.  The Frobenius lemma represents every sufficiently large integer
as $a\ell_1+b\ell_2$.  In the recovery construction reserve an $o(T)$ clock
budget before selecting the final block.  All macroscopic excursions are placed
first; the reserved remainder is filled by the two loops and a bounded
connector.  No macroscopic excursion is cut.

\begin{theorem}[Physical-time LDP]
\label{thm:r7-a3-physical}
The physical empirical collision path at deterministic time $T$ satisfies the
full good LDP obtained by contracting $\mathcal J$ through
$\mathscr P_c$.  The lower-bound recovery hits the deterministic clock exactly
up to a uniformly bounded suspension remainder, while every prescribed defect
profile is left unchanged.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The contraction upper bound follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-a3-compact}.  For the lower bound use the recovery sequence
of Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-a3-collision}, place all long excursions before the
reserved clock interval, and solve the remaining integer clock with the two
coprime loops.  Their total proportion tends to zero.  Roof interpolation
changes a uniformly bounded terminal segment only, hence has vanishing
physical empirical mass.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-seven A3 closure]
\label{thm:r7-a3-main}
The recession coordinate is a canonical boundary of one length-weighted marked
empirical measure; its cost is attainable by actual excursions; survivor
periods are preserved; non-SPR components are transferred to the recession
sector; and both collision-clock and deterministic-time lower bounds are
constructed without forbidden edges or macroscopic terminal surgery.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Lemmas~\ref{lem:r7-a3-compact},
\ref{lem:r7-a3-markov}, and
\ref{lem:r7-a3-shield} with
Theorems~\ref{thm:r7-a3-excursion},
\ref{thm:r7-a3-survivor},
\ref{thm:r7-a3-collision}, and
\ref{thm:r7-a3-physical}.
\end{proof}
