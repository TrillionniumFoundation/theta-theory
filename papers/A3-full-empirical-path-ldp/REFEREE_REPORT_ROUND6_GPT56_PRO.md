## Editorial summary

The introduction of a separate recession-defect coordinate is the first revision that addresses the one-big-excursion problem at the correct structural level. The manuscript also correctly forbids artificial positive mass on nonexistent graph edges. These are substantial conceptual improvements over the previous version.

The new state space, however, is not accompanied by the large-deviation theorem needed to justify it. The recession cost is defined by a limsup support function and then treated as if every finite-cost defect profile were generated by actual long excursions with matching exponential cost. That is precisely the missing lower-bound theorem. In addition, the claimed finite-state Markov approximation and survivor spectral theorem are false without irreducibility/periodicity and recurrence hypotheses that are absent here.

## Major mathematical objections

### 1. The recession support function does not imply an attainable excursion rate

The paper defines

\[
q_\infty(F)=\limsup_{r(a)\to\infty}\frac1{r(a)}
\log\mathbb E_a e^{\sum_{j<r(a)}F(\Theta^j\Gamma(a))}
\]

and then sets

\[
\mathfrak c_\infty(z)=
\sup_F\{z(F)-q_\infty(F)+q_\infty(0)\}.
\]

This convex conjugate gives, at most, an upper-bound candidate. It does not prove that for every `z` with finite `c_infty(z)` there is a sequence of actual admissible excursions whose normalized profiles converge to `z` and whose logarithmic weights converge to `-c_infty(z)`.

The proof of `thm:r6-a3-variational` simply says: “choose ... long admissible excursions whose profiles and costs approximate `zeta`.” No lemma establishes the existence of those excursions, no exposed-point density theorem is proved for the noncompact excursion family, and the limsup defining `q_infty` may be supported by different subsequences for different test functions. In general, the Fenchel conjugate of a limsup log-mgf is only the lower-semicontinuous convex envelope of possible costs; it may assign finite cost to profiles that no single sequence realizes.

This is not a technical omission. The same unjustified selection is the core of the lower bound in `thm:r6-a3-collision-ldp`. The manuscript has therefore encoded the desired recession lower bound into the definition of its rate and then assumed attainability in the proof.

### 2. The defect coordinate and normalization are not shown to arise as a unique projective limit

The empirical defect measure is defined using a threshold `L_N` that tends to infinity “after the finite-level projection.” Different diagonal choices can move collision-clock mass between `m_N` and `zeta_N`. The topology records only finite-cylinder convergence of `m`, truncated moments `int(r wedge L)dm`, and weak convergence of `zeta`; it does not identify a canonical joint Young measure of return length and profile.

The statement that the missing limit of `int r dm` is “exactly recorded” by `zeta(1)` requires a tightness/disintegration theorem for the length-weighted empirical measures. It does not follow from separate weak tightness of `m_N` and `zeta_N`. One must define a single compactification of the marked measure

\[
\frac1{R_N}\sum_j r(a_j)\,
\delta_{(a_j,\chi(a_j))}
\]

and prove that its boundary component is independent of the threshold diagonal. Without that construction, the normalization

\[
\int r\,dm+\zeta(1)=1
\]

is a desired identity, not a proved closed property of all subsequential limits.

The continuity of `P_c` is likewise asserted through a singularity shield that is proved only later and itself depends on A2. This is circular within the compactness/variational argument.

### 3. The branch profile `chi(a)` is not intrinsically defined

An inducing symbol or branch `a` contains a continuum of initial points. The formula

\[
\chi(a)=\frac1{r(a)}\sum_{j<r(a)}
\delta_{\Theta^j\Gamma(a)}
\]

uses `Gamma(a)` as if the symbol determined one collision orbit. The manuscript does not define a canonical representative, a conditional branch law, or a profile-valued kernel. Different points in one long branch can have different local-window empirical profiles, particularly near singularity cuts.

Replacing `chi(a)` by a representative can change the boundary compactification and `q_infty`; replacing it by a conditional law changes the state from a measure on profiles to a measure on profile laws. Bounded distortion alone does not identify the actual path profile in the topology used. This ambiguity must be removed before `Z_infty`, `zeta`, or `c_infty` are well-defined.

### 4. The mixing Markov approximation is false on periodic survivor components

`lem:r6-a3-markov` claims mixing finite-state Markov measures inside any closed survivor component. Consider an irreducible survivor component of period `d>1`. Every measure supported on the original one-step graph has the same cyclic decomposition. It cannot be mixing for the one-step shift.

The proof proposes adding an `o(1)` “admissible connector circulation” to make the chain mixing. If all admissible cycles have length divisible by `d`, no circulation using only the existing edges changes the period. Breaking the period requires adding a forbidden edge, leaving the component, or passing to the `d`th iterate. Each contradicts the statement as written.

The correct theorem must preserve the period and work with cyclic equilibrium states, or explicitly pass to an iterate. This matters because the manuscript later invokes unique mixing equilibrium phases and exact changes of law.

### 5. Finite pressure does not imply the survivor spectral theorem stated

The paper asserts that every irreducible countable survivor component with finite pressure has a strong/weak Lasota–Yorke inequality, a simple leading eigenvalue, and analytic perturbation theory. None of these follows from finite pressure.

Countable Markov shifts may be transient, null recurrent, or positive recurrent; even in the recurrent case, the Ruelle operator need not have an isolated eigenvalue on the proposed space. The displayed norm weights by `e^{alpha r(a)}`, while the proof simultaneously claims a Gibbs tail `O(e^{-cL})`; no model estimate shows that the survivor branches possess such an exponential return tail. Long survivors are the very phenomenon being retained by the recession coordinate.

A valid proof requires a componentwise positive-recurrence criterion, summable variations, a specific compact embedding, and control of the essential spectral radius. The one paragraph in `thm:r6-a3-survivor` does not supply these hypotheses or estimates.

### 6. The singularity shield does not justify sending the source to infinity

The bound

\[
Q_c(sB_{q,\delta})-Q_c(0)
\le C(e^s-1)(e^{-cq}+q^C\delta^\alpha)
\]

is stated for every fixed `s`. The proof then chooses `s=s(q,delta)` tending to infinity after making the bad-set coefficient small. To use this diagonal one needs a pressure estimate uniform on the growing source interval, including constants and operator domains. Neither A2 nor the survivor theorem supplies such a global source chart.

This repeats, in a different form, the prior mistake of deriving an infinite-cost shield from local pressure information. A direct cluster estimate with explicit `s` dependence, or a proved exhausted source domain, is required.

### 7. The physical-time lower bound is not obtained by the stated terminal surgery

Repeating a fixed regular loop until a deterministic horizon is crossed changes the path by an amount controlled by the loop length only if the preceding concatenated construction already ends within a uniformly bounded distance of the target horizon. In the recession regime the final selected excursion can have macroscopic length. Cutting it or replacing it by a loop can alter a nonvanishing fraction of the empirical profile.

The proof says the discrepancy is bounded by the “selected truncation length” and is sublinear in an ordered limit, but does not specify a diagonal compatible with an arbitrary prescribed defect profile. This is another manifestation of the missing excursion-recovery theorem.

## Dependency consequences

A4 uses the A3 singularity shield, history coupling, and compact path realization. C2 and D1 use the resulting path rate and conditional kernels. Until the recession state is canonically constructed and its lower bound is proved, those downstream interfaces are unavailable.

## Required reconstruction

A viable revision must:

1. define a canonical joint compactification of return length and normalized path profile;
2. replace the ambiguous branch representative `Gamma(a)` by an intrinsic profile kernel or prove branchwise concentration;
3. prove an excursion-level LDP or an exposed-profile recovery theorem establishing attainability of `c_infty`;
4. preserve cyclic periods in Markov approximation and state the equilibrium theorem componentwise;
5. establish positive recurrence/quasicompactness for every survivor component actually used; and
6. prove the singularity shield and deterministic-clock recovery with uniform, noncircular estimates.

## Recommendation

**Reject.** The recession-defect architecture is promising and materially better than the previous finite-moment state, but the manuscript has not proved the central recurrent–defect variational principle or the lower bound. It also contains a false mixing-approximation statement and an unsupported countable-component spectral theorem. The advertised full collision- and physical-time LDPs therefore remain open in the submitted text.
