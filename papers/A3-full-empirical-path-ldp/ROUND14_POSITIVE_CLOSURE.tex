
\section{Round-fourteen positive closure: graph-completed controlled renewal LDPs}
\label{sec:r14-a3}

The proof is formulated directly at a deterministic collision or physical
clock.  No cemetery Markovization is used, and singularity exposure is never
discarded uniformly over finite-entropy controls.  Completed excursions,
mesoscopic excursions, one-big-excursion profiles, and the pointed terminal
prefix are coordinates of one controlled renewal state and are charged by one
relative-entropy action.

\subsection{The graph-completed excursion kernel}

Let \(E\) be the graph completion of regular first-return excursions.  An
element
\[
 e=(y_-,a,y_+,r,\tau,\gamma,\varsigma)
\]
records entrance and exit Markov states, the complete inducing branch,
collision length \(r\), physical roof \(\tau\), the realized collision/flow
profile \(\gamma\), and the signed-distance profile \(\varsigma\) from every
singularity and homogeneity boundary met by the excursion.  The topology is
local Skorokhod convergence of \(\gamma\), convergence of finite branch
coordinates, and vague convergence of the exposure profile.  Add one compact
point at weighted infinity by the coercive function
\[
 W(e)=r+\tau+\int(1+|\log\varsigma|)\,d|\gamma|.
\]
Let \(K(y,de)\) be the Gibbs first-return kernel, including the next exit state.

\begin{lemma}[Polish kernel and exponential weight]
\label{lem:r14-a3-kernel}
The graph-completed space \(E\) is Polish, \(K\) is a Feller kernel, and for
some \(\eta>0\),
\[
 \sup_y\int e^{\eta W(e)}K(y,de)<\infty .
\]
Every finite profile cylinder is continuous.  Approaching a singular orbit
produces a boundary exposure coordinate rather than loss of continuity.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On each homogeneous branch, billiard flight and reflection maps are smooth
and the finite path profile depends continuously on the entrance state.
Graph completion adds the one-sided limits at every singular face.  The
countable disjoint union, metrized with the coercive weight, is Polish.
Bounded distortion makes the branch probability continuous on each completed
face and compatible at common boundaries.  The Young-return tail is
exponential; finite horizon gives \(\tau\le C r\), and the homogeneity-strip
estimate makes the logarithmic exposure moment exponential after reducing
\(\eta\).  Summation over branches proves the bound.
\end{proof}

A controlled trajectory uses kernels \(Q_j(\cdot\mid y_j)\ll K(y_j,\cdot)\).
Its cost is
\[
 \mathcal R_N(Q)
 ={1\over N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
 \mathbb E_Q D\!\left(Q_j(\cdot\mid y_j)\Vert K(y_j,\cdot)\right).
\]
This is the exact variational cost in the Gibbs change of measure.

\subsection{A deterministic collision-clock state}

Let \(S_m=\sum_{j<m}r(e_j)\) and
\(N(n)=\max\{m:S_m\le n\}\).  If \(a_n=n-S_{N(n)}\), let
\(\operatorname{pref}_{a_n}(e_{N(n)})\) be the pointed prefix of the next
excursion, including its branch and singularity exposure.  Define
\[
 \mathcal Z_n^c=
 \left(
 {1\over n}\sum_{j<N(n)}r(e_j)\delta_{e_j},
 {N(n)\over n},
 {1\over n}\operatorname{pref}_{a_n}(e_{N(n)})
 \right).
\]
The first coordinate is a clock-weighted excursion measure; it automatically
contains recurrent, mesoscopic, and macroscopic excursions.

\begin{theorem}[Controlled Laplace representation]
\label{thm:r14-a3-control}
For every bounded continuous \(\Phi\) on the state space of
\(\mathcal Z_n^c\),
\[
 -{1\over n}\log\mathbb E
 e^{-n\Phi(\mathcal Z_n^c)}
 =
 \inf_Q\mathbb E_Q
 \left[
  \Phi(\mathcal Z_n^c)
  +{1\over n}\sum_{j=0}^{N(n)}
   D(Q_j\Vert K)
 \right].
\]
The terminal kernel \(Q_{N(n)}\) is included once; hence every pointed
terminal residual is charged by the same likelihood as its completed
extension.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the entropy variational formula
\[
 -\log\int e^{-F}\,dP
 =\inf_{Q\ll P}\{\mathbb E_QF+D(Q\Vert P)\}
\]
to the finite path stopped when the accumulated integer clock first exceeds
\(n\).  The chain rule for relative entropy disintegrates the path entropy
into the displayed conditional terms.  The last excursion is sampled before
its prefix is observed, so its complete likelihood is present exactly once.
Monotone truncation of the stopping time is justified by the exponential
moment in Lemma~\ref{lem:r14-a3-kernel}.
\end{proof}

For a stationary controlled kernel \(\bar Q(y,de)\) with invariant entrance
law \(\pi\), set
\[
 \bar r=\int r\,d(\pi\bar Q),\qquad
 \mathfrak a_c(\pi,\bar Q)
 ={1\over\bar r}\int
 D(\bar Q(y,\cdot)\Vert K(y,\cdot))\,\pi(dy).
\]
A terminal prefix law \(\rho\) is admissible if it is the prefix contraction
of one final controlled kernel; its cost is the corresponding conditional
entropy divided by the deterministic clock.

\begin{theorem}[Collision-clock Laplace principle]
\label{thm:r14-a3-collision}
The variables \(\mathcal Z_n^c\) satisfy a full Laplace principle, hence a
good LDP with speed \(n\).  Its rate is
\[
 I^c(\lambda,\nu,\rho)
 =
 \inf\left\{
 {1\over\bar r}\int D(\bar Q\Vert K)\,d\pi
 +\mathfrak t(\rho\mid\pi,\bar Q):
 \mathscr C_c(\pi,\bar Q,\rho)=(\lambda,\nu,\rho)
 \right\},
\]
where \(\mathscr C_c\) is the exact clock-weighted reconstruction and
\(\mathfrak t\) is the entropy of the final excursion minimized over all
completions of the observed prefix.  The infimum ranges over ordinary and
defective invariant controlled kernels on the graph completion.  There is no
separate unnormalised recession speed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take controls with bounded cost in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r14-a3-control}.  The entropy inequality and the exponential
\(W\)-moment give, for \(L>0\),
\[
 \mathbb E_Q\left[{1\over n}
  \sum_{j\le N(n)}r(e_j)1_{\{W(e_j)>L\}}\right]
 \le {C+\mathcal R_n(Q)\over L};
\]
thus finite-cost controlled states are tight, including singular and
mesoscopic profiles.  No assertion that the bad set is exponentially small
under every control is made.

Any convergent controlled sequence has a limit occupation measure
\(\pi(dy)\bar Q(y,de)\).  The entrance and exit marginals agree because the
unmatched endpoints carry \(O(1/n)\) clock mass.  Lower semicontinuity of
relative entropy and the renewal identity give the lower bound, including
the last conditional entropy by disintegration.

For recovery, first take an ergodic finite-cost controlled kernel with bounded
\(W\).  Run its stationary chain until clock \(n\), and use the genuine last
sampled excursion for the terminal prefix.  The ergodic theorem gives the
state and entropy limits.  Finite convex combinations are concatenated using
regular connector excursions whose total clock and entropy are \(o(n)\).
Finally truncate \(W\) and approximate the kernel in relative entropy; the
entropy-moment estimate controls all ordinary, mesoscopic, and macroscopic
tails simultaneously.  This proves the Laplace upper bound and compactness of
rate sublevels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One-big-excursion and recession coordinates}

For \(L\ge1\), let
\[
 \zeta_L={1\over n}\sum_{j\le N(n)}
 r(e_j)1_{\{r(e_j)>L\}}\delta_{\gamma(e_j)/r(e_j)} .
\]
These are continuous after graph completion and form a projective family.

\begin{proposition}[Constructed recession epigraph]
\label{prop:r14-a3-recession}
The contraction of \(I^c\) to
\((\lambda,\zeta_L,\rho)_{L\ge1}\) has a projective good rate.  Its recession
part is the lower-semicontinuous epigraph
\[
 J_\infty(\zeta)
 =
 \sup_L\inf\left\{
 I^c(\lambda,\nu,\rho):
 \zeta_{L'}=\zeta_{L'}^{\,0},\ 1\le L'\le L
 \right\}.
\]
Every finite-rate \(\zeta\) is recovered by actual legal excursions and
connectors.  The formula includes all mesoscopic scales and the terminal
macroscopic prefix.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each finite collection is a continuous contraction of the already proved
good rate \(I^c\).  Their contraction rates are consistent, so the
Dawson--Gärtner projective theorem gives the displayed supremum.  Compactness
of \(I^c\)-sublevels supplies exponential tightness in the projective
topology.  The recovery sequence in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r14-a3-collision} consists of actual controlled excursions;
diagonal choice in \(L\) proves the final statement without inventing a
profile not realized by the kernel.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Physical-clock inversion}

Let \(U_m=\sum_{j<m}\tau(e_j)\),
\(N_p(T)=\max\{m:U_m\le T\}\), and retain the residual physical-flight prefix.
Define \(\mathcal Z_T^p\) exactly as above with \(r\) replaced by \(\tau\).

\begin{theorem}[Physical-clock LDP]
\label{thm:r14-a3-physical}
The variables \(\mathcal Z_T^p\) satisfy a good LDP with speed \(T\) and rate
\[
 I^p
 =
 \inf_{\mathscr C_p(\pi,\bar Q,\rho)=z}
 {1\over\bar\tau}
 \left[
 \int D(\bar Q\Vert K)\,d\pi
 +D_{\rm term}(\rho)
 \right],
 \qquad
 \bar\tau=\int\tau\,d(\pi\bar Q).
\]
The collision and physical rates are contractions of one joint
\((r,\tau)\)-controlled renewal action, not rates added at incompatible
speeds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Repeat the entropy representation with the stopping time \(N_p(T)\).
Finite horizon gives a positive lower roof and the exponential moment gives
upper-tail control, so \(N_p(T)/T\) is tight under every bounded-cost control.
The same lower-bound and stationary recovery argument yields the factor
\(1/\bar\tau\).  Because \(r,\tau\), the complete excursion profile, and the
terminal residual are present in the same kernel, clock inversion is an exact
measurable stopping operation and no uncharged residual remains.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Contraction to billiard paths}

Let \(\Gamma_c\) and \(\Gamma_p\) concatenate graph-completed excursions and
their terminal prefix into collision- and physical-time paths.

\begin{theorem}[Full empirical-path LDP]
\label{thm:r14-a3-main}
The empirical collision and physical path laws satisfy full good LDPs with
rates
\[
 \mathcal I^c(\nu)=\inf_{\Gamma_c z=\nu}I^c(z),
 \qquad
 \mathcal I^p(\nu)=\inf_{\Gamma_p z=\nu}I^p(z).
\]
The concatenation maps are continuous in the graph-completed local
Skorokhod topology, including at singular one-sided continuations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On each regular excursion, concatenation is continuous.  At a singular
boundary the exposure coordinate selects the one-sided graph limit, while the
pointed terminal coordinate records the unfinished path.  Thus convergence
in the state topology gives local Skorokhod convergence of the concatenation.
Apply the contraction principle to
Theorems~\ref{thm:r14-a3-collision} and
\ref{thm:r14-a3-physical}.
\end{proof}
