\section{The signed balance of the original source}
\label{sec:v93-balance}
The original exceptional selector has a useful sign. Along a maximal
segment the total residence is constant and $z$ is increasing. Thus
selection by small initial gradient can end along the forward flow,
but cannot begin at an interior roof.

Choose any Borel function $c_\sigma\in[a_\sigma,b_\sigma]$ and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v93-prefix-density}
 R_c^\varepsilon(t)=\int
   \mathbf1_{\{a_\sigma<t<c_\sigma\}}f_\sigma^\varepsilon(t)\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}
For the original union $z<\zeta$ or $\ell<\delta$, take
$c_\sigma=b_\sigma$ when $b_\sigma-a_\sigma<\delta$, and otherwise
let $c_\sigma$ be the endpoint of the initial interval $z<\zeta$.
This is Borel by strict radial monotonicity and the Borel segment map.
The empty and full intervals are allowed. Formula
\eqref{eq:v93-prefix-density} is exactly the original weighted union,
not the sum of its two overlapping restrictions. Taking every
$c_\sigma=b_\sigma$ gives the original bad-source density $b^\varepsilon$.

Define nonnegative roof measures, with the integrands zero when
$c_\sigma=a_\sigma$, by
\begin{align}
 \mathsf I_c^\varepsilon
   &=\int\mathbf1_{\{a_\sigma<c_\sigma\}}
             f_\sigma^\varepsilon(a_\sigma+)\delta_{a_\sigma}\,d\sigma,
                                                       \notag\\
 \mathsf D_c^\varepsilon
   &=\int\mathbf1_{\{a_\sigma<c_\sigma\}}
             f_\sigma^\varepsilon(c_\sigma-)\delta_{c_\sigma}\,d\sigma,
                                                       \notag\\
 \mathsf G_c^{\varepsilon,\pm}(H)
   &=\int\int_{(a_\sigma,c_\sigma)\cap H}
                   [\pm\partial_u f_\sigma^\varepsilon(u)]_+\,du\,d\sigma.
                                                       \label{eq:v93-measures}
\end{align}
Here $H$ is any Borel roof set and $\delta_v$ is a unit point mass.
All four measures are finite at fixed count by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v93-traces}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact balance with nonpositive artificial cuts]
\label{thm:v93-balance}
The distributional derivative of the original prefix source is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v93-balance}
 D_tR_c^\varepsilon
   =\mathsf I_c^\varepsilon+\mathsf G_c^{\varepsilon,+}
       -\mathsf D_c^\varepsilon-\mathsf G_c^{\varepsilon,-}.
\end{equation}
All interior prefix boundaries occur with the negative sign.
The positive entrance measure is supported on true backward physical
or section-decision ends and backward critical ends. Its grazing
entrance part is zero. The full-source measures
$\mathsf I^\varepsilon$ and $\mathsf G^{\varepsilon,+}$, obtained by
$c_\sigma=b_\sigma$, give the common domination
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v93-common-domination}
          D_tR_c^\varepsilon\le
                   \mathsf I^\varepsilon+\mathsf G^{\varepsilon,+}
\end{equation}
for every Borel prefix, without regularity of its transverse graph.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On one sheet, integration by parts on $[a_\sigma,c_\sigma]$ gives,
for $\phi\in C_c^1(\mathbb R)$,
\[
 -\int_{a_\sigma}^{c_\sigma}f_\sigma^\varepsilon\phi'
  =f_\sigma^\varepsilon(a_\sigma+)\phi(a_\sigma)
    -f_\sigma^\varepsilon(c_\sigma-)\phi(c_\sigma)
    +\int_{a_\sigma}^{c_\sigma}(f_\sigma^\varepsilon)'\phi.
\]
The endpoint values are justified by the absolute continuity and
integrable derivative proved above. The fixed-count finite variation
permits integration over $\sigma$. This proves the signed identity,
with the common original coarea kernels. Splitting the derivative
into its two signs before summation gives precisely
\eqref{eq:v93-balance}, not an identification of either summand with
the Jordan positive part of the projected derivative.

A prefix cut is the upper endpoint of an initial interval. Its
coefficient is therefore negative, including cuts coinciding with
physical forward ends. The lower endpoint $a_\sigma$ is an actual
maximal-segment end; chart assignments do not produce endpoints.
The trace at a grazing entrance vanishes by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v93-traces}. A critical directional trace is retained.
Finally the selected positive measures are submeasures of the full
positive measures. Discarding the two negative measures proves
\eqref{eq:v93-common-domination}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A comparison using only ancestors of the target fiber}
The common measure in \eqref{eq:v93-common-domination} is convenient,
but can charge segments which have already ended. The following
sharper comparison keeps only the ancestors of the selected target.
For a roof $t$, write
$\mathcal A_c(t)=\{\sigma:a_\sigma<t<c_\sigma\}$ and
$k_{r,t}(u)=(u-t+r)/r$ on $[t-r,t]$. Define
\begin{align}
 B_{c,r}^\varepsilon(t)
  &=\int_{\mathcal A_c(t)}\mathbf1_{\{a_\sigma>t-r\}}
       k_{r,t}(a_\sigma)f_\sigma^\varepsilon(a_\sigma+)\,d\sigma,
                                                        \notag\\
 G_{c,r}^{\varepsilon,+}(t)
  &=\int_{\mathcal A_c(t)}\int_{\max(a_\sigma,t-r)}^t
       k_{r,t}(u)[(f_\sigma^\varepsilon)'(u)]_+\,du\,d\sigma.
                                                        \label{eq:v93-ancestral-budget}
\end{align}
These are nonnegative Borel functions. In particular an entrance
is counted only when its same selected segment reaches the target
roof; there is no later re-entry or completed-prefix contribution.

\begin{theorem}[Prescribed-scale pointwise comparison]
\label{thm:v93-pointwise}
For every fixed $r>0$ and almost every roof,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v93-pointwise}
 R_c^\varepsilon(t)\le
 \frac1r\int_{t-r}^t R_c^\varepsilon(s)\,ds
                      +B_{c,r}^\varepsilon(t)+G_{c,r}^{\varepsilon,+}(t).
\end{equation}
No integral in the starting roof is taken on the left.
The radius is prescribed in advance and is independent of the
collision count. The inequality holds for the full original source
and the exact exceptional union at every pair of its cutoffs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a sheet in $\mathcal A_c(t)$. Integration of its zero extension
from the left endpoint, followed by averaging the earlier roof, gives
\[
 f_\sigma^\varepsilon(t)
 =\frac1r\int_{t-r}^t\mathbf1_{\{s>a_\sigma\}}
                                      f_\sigma^\varepsilon(s)\,ds
 +\mathbf1_{\{a_\sigma>t-r\}}k_{r,t}(a_\sigma)
                                      f_\sigma^\varepsilon(a_\sigma+)
 +\int_{\max(a_\sigma,t-r)}^t k_{r,t}(u)
                                      (f_\sigma^\varepsilon)'(u)\,du.
\]
The ramp is a Stieltjes averaging weight, not a physical Jacobian.
All roofs between the entrance and $t$ belong to the same selected
prefix. Sum this identity over the target's ancestors, replace the
last derivative by its positive part, and bound the earlier-fiber
submeasure by the full earlier $R_c^\varepsilon$. The lost earlier
prefixes are subtracted in the exact identity, not added as births.
Fubini and the common kernels justify the assertion at almost every
roof. No multiplicity bound enters the comparison.
\end{proof}

For later use let $B_{c,r}^{\varepsilon,\sharp}$ and
$G_{c,r}^{\varepsilon,+,\sharp}$ denote the same expressions with
the ramp replaced by one. The sheet identity between any earlier
roof and $t$ also proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v93-persistence}
 R_c^\varepsilon(s)\ge R_c^\varepsilon(t)
     -B_{c,r}^{\varepsilon,\sharp}(t)
     -G_{c,r}^{\varepsilon,+,\sharp}(t)
       \quad\text{for almost every }s\in(t-r,t).
\end{equation}
Thus a value at least $\lambda$ persists above $\lambda/2$ throughout
the prescribed backward interval unless its actual ancestral entrance
and positive-growth budget is at least $\lambda/2$. This is an exact
physical dichotomy. An estimate proving the latter alternative
uniformly small is still needed for unrestricted spike exclusion.
