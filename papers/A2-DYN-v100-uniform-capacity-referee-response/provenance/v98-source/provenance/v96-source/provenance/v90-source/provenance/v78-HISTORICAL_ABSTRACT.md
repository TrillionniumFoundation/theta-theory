We study the joint lattice displacement, collision count and flight time
at genuine returns to a moving section in a triangular periodic Lorentz
gas, uniformly for disk radii in $[0.45,0.47]$. A bounded collision
compensation gives Gaussian and functional laws, unsmoothed fourth
moments and quantitative convergence of the actual return covariances.
It also gives integrated Gaussian comparisons on a growing central
Fourier band, with a complex observable inserted at any actual return
and a separate variation cost. Measurable phase holonomy, physical
rotations and finite covers prove joint nondegeneracy and complete
collision-phase arithmetic. Quantitative bounded-function defects extend
from compact nonzero bands to a joint neighborhood of physical and
return spectral parameters. An exact tower lift carries the collision
estimates to the unbounded return record; orthogonal compressions give
local peripheral resolvents and a finite-time comparison with its
characteristic function.

For raw local inversion, we integrate the complete physical graph below
a finite collision-count cutoff. The component time densities are
constructible and admit an exact power--logarithm jet extraction with a
$W^{2,1}$ remainder. This includes singular itinerary boundaries as well
as regular critical words. Since the count is an observed coordinate,
a linear cutoff agrees exactly with the original raw density on central
windows. We obtain an inversion identity with a rapidly decaying
high-count convolution correction. Together with the central theorem,
this identity reduces the parameter-uniform raw mixed-density local
limit to explicit uniform residual-growth, complementary-integral and
local-edge estimates. The exact physical conditioning application retains
its weighted estimates and relative event-comparison requirements.
Keeping the finite binomial complexity bound yields exponential
variation growth and a single-logarithm return spectral estimate.
It also gives central Gaussian comparisons and actual moment limits
under the unchanged joint observation of logarithmically many returns.
Short-lag Gaussian control also bounds Gram matrices of the exact return
orbit. This yields uniform mean-square cancellation on arbitrarily late
windows throughout the small annulus, with arbitrary square-integrable
weights, and cyclic-vector estimates at every peripheral angle.
The rescaled spectral measure of the constant vector has a Cauchy limit.
These averaged conclusions do not replace the fixed-return raw estimates.
A separate finite-order cumulant argument bounds the derivatives of the
smoothed collision eigenvalue independently of the smoothing scale.
It gives raw-scale cancellation at each prescribed return count on an
inner annulus extending the central support from $2n^{-99/200}$ to
$2n^{-12/25}$, also with a bounded-variation mark at any return.
Keeping a cubic correction inside the damped collision word and using
higher visit-count moments extends this fixed-count support further to
$2n^{-19/42}$, with a quantitative marked estimate. Its sufficient
rescaled exponent range is $\varepsilon<1/20$.
The farther complementary integral and the long-time extracted-edge
terms remain explicit in the enlarged-cutoff inversion identity.

Multiscale stopping further yields all fixed marked Gaussian moments and
fourth-root comparison errors, preserving the original central rate on a
wider prescribed-count Fourier band.
An arbitrary fixed residual order permits separate widths in the four
frequency coordinates. In particular the fixed-count band reaches
flight-time frequencies of size $n^{-41/100}$ while retaining the
original central rate and the preceding isotropic support. The new
coordinatewise kernel has its own exact raw edge and residual budget.
