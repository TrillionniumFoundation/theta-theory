\section{Stopped transport of the physical level measure}
\label{sec:v76-transport}
Fix $m,R,n,k$. Remove the finite-time singularities, the preimages
of section boundaries, and the critical points of $L$ from the
regular exact event. Its open part is denoted by $\Omega$. These
removals have zero collision-source measure. At almost every roof
they have zero source in the coarea disintegration as well. For
critical points this follows, for example, from the finite
endpoint-cap cover and strict convexity: each word has finitely
many such points. The boundaries are not suppressed in a transport;
a trajectory reaching them is stopped.

Use the original cylinder area
$\dd\bar\nu_R=\dd\alpha\,\dd p/(4\pi c_*)$, restricted to
$\Omega$. It agrees with $\nu_R^*$ there. Give the cylinder its
flat metric. On $\{L=t\}\cap\Omega$ write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v76-coarea-fiber}
 \dd\mu_t(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi c_*|\nabla L(x)|}\dd\mathcal H^1(x),
 \qquad b_W(t)=\int W\dd\mu_t .
\end{equation}
We allow $0\le W\le1$ locally Lipschitz on $\Omega$; the complete
weight $W^\varepsilon$ has this property. The least clearance is a
minimum of finitely many smooth distance branches locally, and
therefore is locally Lipschitz even at a switch of minimizing disk.
The unweighted fibers are finite for almost every $t$ by coarea and
finiteness of the restricted cylinder area.

Choose a smooth vector field $X$ on $\Omega$ such that $XL=1$.
The normalized gradient is one choice. Other choices, including the
Morse-adapted choice below, are allowed. For a fixed geometric field,
coordinate changes do not change the construction. Let $\Phi_h$ be
its maximal flow, killed at its lifetime in $\Omega$. No continuation
through an excluded physical boundary is made. For $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$
and $h>0$ put
\[
 S^\sigma_h(t)=\{x\in\Omega:L(x)=t,\ \Phi_{\sigma s}x\in\Omega
                    \text{ and exists for every }0\le s\le h\}.
\]
On this set $L(\Phi_{\sigma s}x)=t+\sigma s$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v76-jacobian}
 J^\sigma_h(x)=\exp\left(\int_0^h
       \sigma\diver_{\bar\nu_R}X(\Phi_{\sigma s}x)\dd s\right).
\end{equation}
This is an area Jacobian relative to the original source, not a
Jacobian for a cap label.

\begin{lemma}[Physical coarea covariance]\label{lem:v76-covariance}
For every nonnegative Borel $a$ and almost every pair $(t,h)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v76-covariance}
 \int_{\Phi_{\sigma h}S^\sigma_h(t)}a(y)\dd\mu_{t+\sigma h}(y)
   =\int_{S^\sigma_h(t)}a(\Phi_{\sigma h}x)J^\sigma_h(x)\dd\mu_t(x).
\end{equation}
The formula includes the entire exact event and its original factor
$c_*^{-1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work first on a relatively compact regular flow box. The ordinary
change of variables for the smooth flow gives
$\Phi_{\sigma h}^*\bar\nu_R=J^\sigma_h\bar\nu_R$ by the
Liouville formula. Disintegrate both sides using $L$; its translation
has derivative one. This gives \eqref{eq:v76-covariance}, including
the coarea speed factor. Equivalently, contracting the oriented area
form with $X$ gives the level form, and the flow preserves $X$.
Uniqueness of solutions makes the flow injective wherever it exists.
A countable exhaustion by flow boxes, a disjoint measurable refinement,
and monotone convergence give the formula on the surviving domain.
Fubini supplies common almost-everywhere versions in $(t,h)$.
\end{proof}

The actual source mass lost under the resulting subcoupling is
\begin{align}
 E^\sigma_h(t)&=\int_{\{L=t\}\cap\Omega\setminus S^\sigma_h(t)}W\dd\mu_t,
 \notag\\
 D^\sigma_h(t)&=\int_{S^\sigma_h(t)}
       [W(x)-W(\Phi_{\sigma h}x)J^\sigma_h(x)]_+\dd\mu_t(x),
 \notag\\
 R^\sigma_h(t)&=E^\sigma_h(t)+D^\sigma_h(t).
 \label{eq:v76-defect}
\end{align}
Here $D$ denotes a transport defect, not differentiation or a
word-alphabet size. All physical words with this one exact label
are included in each integral.

\begin{theorem}[A label-preserving subcoupling without cap losses]
\label{thm:v76-subcoupling}
The measure on the graph $x\mapsto\Phi_{\sigma h}x$ with source
marginal
\[
 \one_{S^\sigma_h(t)}
 \min\{W(x),W(\Phi_{\sigma h}x)J^\sigma_h(x)\}\dd\mu_t(x)
\]
is dominated in its two marginals by the physical weighted level
measures at $t$ and $t+\sigma h$. Its unmatched initial mass is
exactly $R^\sigma_h(t)$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v76-drop}
 [b_W(t)-b_W(t+\sigma h)]_+\le R^\sigma_h(t)
 \qquad\text{for almost every }(t,h).
\end{equation}
Refining, changing, or removing an auxiliary endpoint-cap partition
changes neither this coupling nor its defect. The coupling never
changes an exact displacement, occupation, terminal test, return
index, or collision count.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first marginal domination is immediate. For the second, divide
by the positive Jacobian on the surviving graph and use
Lemma~\ref{lem:v76-covariance}; the transported density is at most
$W$ at its image. The image is a subset of the target exact event,
not necessarily the whole target. Subtract the coupled mass from
$b_W(t)$. On the nonsurvivors the result is $E^\sigma_h$; on the
survivors it is $D^\sigma_h$. Target marginal domination gives
\eqref{eq:v76-drop}. This proves the assertion without dividing by
$W$, which may vanish.

For an auxiliary measurable partition, disintegrate the very same
source measure on its pieces and then sum. A change of chart changes
both the displayed density and its Jacobian by inverse coordinate
factors; the measure and the graph are unchanged. In particular the
image of a point is assigned its target chart, not killed when it
ceases to have its starting chart. Thus no zero extension at a
first-admissible cap boundary enters \eqref{eq:v76-defect}. In contrast,
reaching a true boundary of $\Omega$ does enter $E^\sigma_h$.
All retained labels are locally constant in $\Omega$ and persist
throughout the stopped orbit. Different words cannot produce
multiplicity: the underlying physical initial states, and their
flow images by uniqueness, are disjoint.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An exact source for the distortion loss}
Weak derivatives are sufficient for $W$. Define the nonnegative
locally integrable function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v76-drift-source}
 g_\sigma=[-\sigma(XW+W\diver_{\bar\nu_R}X)]_+,
 \qquad G_\sigma(u)=\int g_\sigma\dd\mu_u.
\end{equation}
The latter may have infinite integral on a large roof interval;
no global finiteness is being assumed.

\begin{proposition}[Survivor distortion as a physical drift integral]
\label{prop:v76-drift}
For almost every $(t,h)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v76-drift-bound}
 D^\sigma_h(t)\le\int_0^hG_\sigma(t+\sigma s)\dd s.
\end{equation}
This is an extended nonnegative inequality. On compact regular
flow boxes both sides are finite.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Along an almost-everywhere flow line the locally Lipschitz function
$W$ obeys the chain rule. Differentiating its transported density gives
\[
 \frac{\dd}{\dd s}\bigl(W(\Phi_{\sigma s}x)J^\sigma_s(x)\bigr)
 =\sigma(XW+W\diver_{\bar\nu_R}X)(\Phi_{\sigma s}x)J^\sigma_s(x).
\]
The positive part of the net decrease is bounded by the integral of
the negative derivative. Integrate over $S^\sigma_h(t)$, use
$S^\sigma_h(t)\subset S^\sigma_s(t)$ for $s\le h$, and apply
\eqref{eq:v76-covariance}. The image at time $s$ is a subset of
the full fiber at $t+\sigma s$, so positivity gives the bound by
$G_\sigma$. Tonelli and compact exhaustions prove the extended
statement. No cardinality estimate for the word set is used.
\end{proof}
