\begin{abstract}
We study which directions of a future experiment can be excited by an
actual past. A product-tangent criterion, together with a quantitative
change of variables retaining the evidence, gives positive-history
attainment. For sparse monomial experiments this yields a sharp
finite-state prediction profile, uniformly across additive exponent
collisions: exterior Vandermonde volumes are truncated by the dimension
attainable from the past. The prior may be any full-support probability.
For a circular four-cell detector with Haar prior and contrast $\tau$,
we prove instead that the $j$th harmonic has physical scale $\tau^{2j}$,
one factor coming from acquisition and one from future observation.
Its sharp $N$-trial causal memory law is
$\max_{1\le j\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\tau^{2(j+1)}M^{-1/j}$
for $M$ labels and sufficiently small contrast, including zero contrast.
Both classifications have unconditional lower bounds and one causal
realization. A bounded-dual principle also gives all width orders of the
local monomial prior-ambiguity body; exact lower-moment information
exposes the vanishing directions. Under the stated dominated-family
and interior-slack hypotheses, the imperfect-common-name regret is the
monomial memory profile plus squared input error. Complete numerical
realizations of that profile, with their precision and resource bounds,
are given separately from the intrinsic results.
\end{abstract}
