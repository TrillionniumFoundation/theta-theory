\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct periodic dispersing tables from active reciprocal commands
returning one collision bit per attempted preparation. On bounded
$C^{6,\beta}$ classes, $s=6+\beta$, we prove joint recovery of the
obstacles and an unknown convex launch footprint from two fixed homothetic
noise scales. The stationary launch density is unknown and may be
asymmetric; only uniform footprint bounds, exact scale control and a
boundary-mass lower bound are prescribed. Nested positive components
identify the two-scale correspondence, and support addition separates
the footprint from the obstacles. The finite reconstruction retains the
calibrated one-scale attempted-bit upper power
$\nu^{-((2\gamma+3)s+1)/(s-2)}$ at $C^2$ accuracy $\nu$.
We also prove a lower power $\nu^{-(s+1)/(s-2)}$ for expected attempts
under one fixed known uniform-disk law, even with arbitrary-precision
adaptive short commands. A contracted binary-channel inequality makes
this a common-noise information bound, not a table-dependent calibration
ambiguity; a gap to the stationary upper power remains. The localized
reconstruction, matching noiseless binary powers, finite command
descriptions and worst-case calibration converse are retained. Random
spread, nominal positioning and physical calibration are distinct
resources. Every solid start and miss is charged. Uniform finite
primitive-period decisions use a bounded periodic presentation and a
known positive nonperiod-patch margin; real period vectors remain
estimates. No passive count or spectral invariant is asserted.
\end{abstract}
