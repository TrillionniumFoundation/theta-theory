\section{Polynomial testing of the original resource image}
\label{sec:v16-dual}
The private behavior image is usually nonconvex. Its testing criterion
can nevertheless be written as a limit of finite algebraic lower
certificates, without replacing that image by randomized designs.
The testing weights in this section depend on the candidate simulator.
Unlike Theorem~\ref{thm:v17-dual}, this identity is a certificate
transform, not an independent variational dual.
They are universal proof functions, not a hidden selector supplied to
an executed experiment.

Use any one of the finite signatures of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main}, with rational instrument data. Write its
objective as $F(q)=\max_{1\le i\le m}f_i(q)$, where $f_i$ are all its
marked event discrepancies, including the empty event $f_1=0$.
For visible selectors the event and feedback rule include their selector
fibers, and the target selector law remains in $f_i$. Thus $-1\le f_i\le1$
for all three signatures; the polynomials may be different.

Parameterize a row with $d$ outcomes by $d-1$ variables in $[0,1]$:
\[
 q_1=x_1,\qquad q_j=x_j\prod_{l<j}(1-x_l)\quad(1<j<d),
 \qquad q_d=\prod_{l<d}(1-x_l).
\]
This map is onto the row simplex, including its boundary. Indeed, divide
each desired probability by the remaining mass while that mass is
positive; when it is zero choose the remaining variables arbitrarily.
Taking the product of these maps, including a selector row when needed,
gives an onto polynomial map $q(x)$ from $[0,1]^D$ to the feasible
row product. Put $h_i(x)=f_i(q(x))$ and $g_i=(1+h_i)/2$.
All $h_i$ are rational polynomials, $0\le g_i\le1$, and $g_1=1/2$.
The case $D=0$ is a single feasible table and is interpreted directly.

\begin{theorem}[Strict polynomial lower-certificate hierarchy]
\label{thm:v16-dual}
For $p\ge1$ define
\[
 A_p(x)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^m g_i(x)^p h_i(x)}
                    {\sum_{i=1}^m g_i(x)^p},
 \qquad a_p=\min_{x\in[0,1]^D}A_p(x),
 \qquad \delta=\min_q F(q).
\]
Then the denominators are positive, $a_p$ is nondecreasing, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v16-powergap}
 \delta-2(1-m^{-1/(p+1)})\le a_p\le\delta,
 \qquad \delta=\sup_{p\ge1}a_p.
\end{equation}
For rational $L$ let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v16-positivepoly}
 P_{p,L}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^m g_i(x)^p(h_i(x)-L).
\end{equation}
A nonnegative tensor Bernstein expansion of $P_{p,L}$ on $[0,1]^D$
certifies $\delta\ge L$. If $L<\delta$, some $p$ and some finite
Bernstein degree have strictly positive coefficients. Conversely, a
strictly positive such expansion certifies $L<\delta$.
These are lower certificates at the original state and selector
budgets. No convexification of the feasible behavior image occurs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $x$ and put $b=\max_i g_i(x)\ge1/2$ and $r_i=g_i(x)/b$.
At least one $r_i$ is one. Write $S_j=\sum_i r_i^j$. H\"older's
inequality gives
\[
 S_p\le m^{1/(p+1)}S_{p+1}^{p/(p+1)},\qquad S_{p+1}\ge1.
\]
Consequently $S_{p+1}/S_p\ge m^{-1/(p+1)}$. Since
$A_p=2bS_{p+1}/S_p-1$ and $\max_i h_i=2b-1$,
\[
 0\le\max_i h_i-A_p\le2(1-m^{-1/(p+1)}).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives $S_{p+2}S_p\ge S_{p+1}^2$, so $A_p$ is
nondecreasing in $p$. Its denominator is at least $2^{-p}$ and it is
continuous on the cube. Taking minima proves
\eqref{eq:v16-powergap}. The bound is also at most $2\log m/(p+1)$,
by $1-e^{-u}\le u$.

Every tensor Bernstein basis function is nonnegative on the cube, and
the basis functions sum to one. Nonnegative coefficients therefore
imply $P_{p,L}\ge0$. Dividing by the positive denominator gives
$F(q(x))\ge A_p(x)\ge L$ for every $x$, which proves soundness.
For completeness choose $p$ so that the error in
\eqref{eq:v16-powergap} is less than $\delta-L$. Then
\[
 P_{p,L}(x)\ge2^{-p}\{\delta-L-2(1-m^{-1/(p+1)})\}>0.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:v16-bernstein} below gives a finite strictly positive
Bernstein expansion. Conversely strictly positive coefficients have a
positive minimum, and the denominator is at most $m$, so they give a
strict positive uniform gap above $L$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Positive Bernstein certificates and their degree elevation are
classical; see de Klerk--Laurent~\cite{deKlerkLaurent2010}. The next
elementary bound is retained for a self-contained exact verifier, not
as a new general hypercube positivity theorem.

\begin{lemma}[An explicit positivity degree]
\label{lem:v16-bernstein}
Let $P(x)=\sum_k c_kx^k$ be a rational polynomial in $D$ variables and
$n\ge\max_{k,j}k_j$. In its tensor Bernstein expansion of degree $n$
in each coordinate the coefficient at $\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,n\}^D$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v16-bernstein}
 b_{\alpha,n}=\sum_k c_k\prod_{j=1}^D
                          \frac{\binom{\alpha_j}{k_j}}{\binom n{k_j}},
 \qquad
 |b_{\alpha,n}-P(\alpha/n)|\le\frac{C(P)}n,
 \quad C(P)=\sum_k|c_k|\sum_j\binom{k_j}{2}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $\min P>0$ implies positivity of every coefficient
whenever $n>C(P)/\min P$ and the degree restriction holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a binomial random variable $B$ with parameters $(n,x)$,
$\mathbb E\binom Bk=\binom nk x^k$. Expanding this expectation proves
the univariate monomial formula, and products prove the displayed
coefficient identity. The convention is $\binom ak=0$ for $a<k$.
To compare $\binom ak/\binom nk$ with $(a/n)^k$, draw $k$ indices
uniformly from $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. Conditional on no repetition this is
sampling without replacement. The probability of a repetition is at
most $\binom k2/n$. Apply this total variation bound to the event that
all drawn indices lie in a prescribed $a$-element subset. Hence the
difference of the two factors is at most $\binom k2/n$. All factors
lie in $[0,1]$. A product telescoping bound followed by the triangle
inequality proves \eqref{eq:v16-bernstein}. The final assertion follows
by taking the minimum of $P$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Rational lower witnesses without policy cells]
\label{cor:v16-algorithm}
At every fixed finite rational signature, strict infeasibility above a
rational level $L$ is equivalent to the existence of the following
finite rational certificate: an integer $p$, an integer $n$, and a
strictly positive coefficient array for \eqref{eq:v16-positivepoly}
whose identity is checked by \eqref{eq:v16-bernstein}. Dovetailing these
certificates with rational original-budget upper witnesses produces
nested intervals converging to $\delta$. If a subfamily of the original
tests gives a strict gap, the same construction applies to it after
adjoining the empty test.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Soundness and completeness are Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-dual}. Each finite
candidate is checked by rational polynomial arithmetic. Enumerate all
$(p,n)$ and rational levels, and retain the largest verified lower
level so far, together with zero. Rational row tables are dense in the
feasible product, and their continuous objectives converge to the
minimum, so enumeration and retention of the best upper table converge
from above. This is a dovetailed enumeration, not a claim that any
fixed $(p,n)$ test search terminates. For a requested positive interval
width, convergence of both endpoints proves termination. The reduced
family conclusion uses only the same pointwise maximum argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Meaning and size of the strict certificate transform]
A constant distribution on tests generally detects only the convex hull
of the behavior image. The functions $g_i^p/\sum_jg_j^p$ are instead
functions of a proposed row table. Their universally quantified weighted
inequality is an algebraic certificate; the tester need not observe a
private state in an experiment. The displayed certificate identity is not an equality
obtained by exchanging a private minimum with a fixed mixed test.
Power means and Bernstein degree elevation are classical algebraic
approximation devices, proved here in the precise form needed. The
claim here is strict-level completeness for the original marked causal
resource image, including visible selectors, and its physical transfer
below. The coefficient count $(n+1)^D$, the number of original tests,
and the algebraic degrees may be large. No polynomial complexity or intrinsic-dimension bound on this
row-cube coefficient count is asserted. The independent adaptive
test has the separate behavior-size bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v17-degree}; its verification is a different cost.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A sharp two-time nonconvex example}
The source has singleton reports, the control alphabet and actual mark
are singleton, and the target emits $(0,0)$ or $(1,1)$ with probability
$1/2$ each. At width one, a private simulator emits two independent bits
with probabilities $p,q$. It cannot reread its first emitted bit.

\begin{proposition}[A gap invisible to constant testing mixtures]
\label{prop:v16-gap}
For this delayed experiment,
\[
 \dpr_{(1,1)}=\sqrt2-1,\qquad
 \dhi_{(1,1),2}=0,\qquad \dvi_{(1,1),r}=\sqrt2-1\quad(r\ge1).
\]
The private objective is the maximum of the four polynomials
\[
 0,\quad \tfrac12-pq,\quad
 \tfrac12-(1-p)(1-q),\quad p+q-2pq.
\]
Every constant mixture of these tests has minimum at most zero,
whereas Corollary~\ref{cor:v16-algorithm} certifies every rational
$L<\sqrt2-1$ at private width one.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $u=pq$ and $v=(1-p)(1-q)$. Direct total variation calculation gives
$F=1-\min(u,1/2)-\min(v,1/2)$ and the displayed maximum. By
Cauchy--Schwarz, $\sqrt u+\sqrt v\le1$. If $u\ge1/2$, then
$v\le(1-1/\sqrt2)^2$, so the sum of the two capped terms is at most
$2-\sqrt2$; the case $v\ge1/2$ is symmetric. Otherwise set
$a=\sqrt u$, $b=\sqrt v$. On
$0\le a,b\le1/\sqrt2$, $a+b\le1$, the maximum of $a^2+b^2$
is at a vertex of this polygon and equals $2-\sqrt2$.
Equality is attained at $p=q=1/\sqrt2$. A hidden fair bit selects the
two deterministic constant sequences and exactly reproduces the target.
If it is exposed, variation is averaged over the selector fibers;
each fiber has error at least the private minimum, which is itself
attainable without a selector. Finally the target lies in the convex
hull of the two private deterministic behaviors. The average discrepancy
of any fixed test mixture at those behaviors is zero, so at least one
has discrepancy at most zero. The strict polynomial certificates
therefore detect information lost by constant convex separation.
\end{proof}
