\section{Global transport of the backward equation}
\label{sec:v19-global}
We retain exactly the common-preparation, bounded-signal and positive-noise
hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:v18-transport}. In particular the canonical
joint laws $P_n,P$ are equivalent and have the same latent marginal $\nu$.
All comparisons on a single observation path use common measurable versions
of the conditional expectations. The following estimate avoids division by
a small observation density in the final bound.

\begin{lemma}[An integrated posterior comparison]
\label{lem:v19-posterior}
Let $P,Q$ be equivalent laws on a standard Borel product carrying a latent
variable $Z$ and an observation $Y$, and let
$\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}\le\epsilon$. If $|u(Z)|\le M$ and $\mathcal F$ is
an observation sub-$\sigma$-field, then, under either $S=P$ or $S=Q$,
\begin{align}
 \E_S|\E_Q[u\mid\mathcal F]-\E_P[u\mid\mathcal F]|
       &\le4M\epsilon,\label{eq:v19-posteriorL1}\\
 \E_S|\E_Q[u\mid\mathcal F]-\E_P[u\mid\mathcal F]|^2
       &\le8M^2\epsilon.\label{eq:v19-posteriorL2}
\end{align}
A common latent marginal is not needed for these two inequalities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use a dominating finite measure on $\mathcal F$. Write $p,q$ for the
densities of the restricted probability laws and $a,b$ for the densities
of the signed measures $\E_P[u\1_A]$, $\E_Q[u\1_A]$ on $\mathcal F$.
Their conditional means are $m_P=a/p$ and $m_Q=b/q$. Outside null sets,
$|m_P|,|m_Q|\le M$, and
\[
 p(m_Q-m_P)=m_Q(p-q)+(b-a).
\]
The integral of $|p-q|$ is at most $2\epsilon$, by contraction of total
variation. The integral of $|b-a|$ is at most $2M\epsilon$, since
multiplication by $u$ followed by restriction cannot increase signed
variation by more than $M$. Integrating the displayed identity proves
\eqref{eq:v19-posteriorL1} under $P$. Interchanging the two laws proves it
under $Q$. The squared bound follows from $|m_Q-m_P|\le2M$.
\end{proof}

Let $|f|\le1$, $\gamma>0$, and use the notation $\theta^n,U^n,Z^n,I^n$
of Proposition~\ref{prop:v18-innovation} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-entropic}. Here $Z^n$ denotes the scalar backward
integrand, whereas the unadorned latent argument of $H_n$ is the preparation
variable. Define the deterministic bounds
\begin{align}
 A_n&=\sigma^{-2}\{2\eta_n^2+16M^2T\varepsilon_n\},
       \label{eq:v19-An}\\
 C_n&=\frac{2(1+e^{4\gamma})}{\gamma^2}A_n,
       \label{eq:v19-Cn}
\end{align}
where $\varepsilon_n=\min\{1,\eta_n/(2\sigma)\}$ as before.

\begin{theorem}[Global innovation and entropic-integrand transport]
\label{thm:v19-global}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-gaussian}, under either
physical law $S=P$ or $S=P_n$ on the same canonical path,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-globalH2}
 \E_S\int_0^T|\theta_t^n-\theta_t|^2dt\le A_n,
 \qquad
 \E_S\int_0^T|Z_t^n-Z_t|^2dt\le C_n.
\end{equation}
No stopping at a density level is required. If $\eta_n\to0$, both
integrals also converge to zero in expectation under the reference law $R$.

On the single latent-preserving coupling of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-gaussian}, evaluate each process on its own observation
path, denoting these evaluations by a bar. Then
\begin{align}
 \E\int_0^T|\bar\theta_t^n-\bar\theta_t|^2dt
   &\le A_n+4M^2T\varepsilon_n/\sigma^2,
       \label{eq:v19-coupledtheta}\\
 \E\int_0^T|\bar Z_t^n-\bar Z_t|^2dt
   &\le C_n+16M^2T\varepsilon_n/(\gamma^2\sigma^2).
       \label{eq:v19-coupledZ}
\end{align}
In particular $\bar I^n\to\bar I$ uniformly in probability. Writing
$\bar J_t^n=\int_0^t\bar Z_s^n\,d\bar I_s^n$ for the stochastic
integral in its own observation filtration, and $\bar J$ for its limit,
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-coupledJ}
 \E\sup_{t\le T}|\bar J_t^n-\bar J_t|^2
 \le 2\E\sup_{t\le T}|\bar U_t^n-\bar U_t|^2
       +\frac{8M^2T}{\sigma^2}
                     \E\int_0^T|\bar Z_t^n-\bar Z_t|^2dt.
\end{equation}
The first term on the right has the explicit bound in
\eqref{eq:v18-entropiccoupling}. Thus the full bounded entropic backward
representation, including its martingale integral, is transported globally.
The coupling need not make either innovation Brownian in the joined
filtration; each integral is defined under its own marginal law.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each deterministic $t$, under $P$ split
\[
 \sigma(\theta_t^n-\theta_t)
 =\E_{P_n}[H_n(t,Z)\mid\mathcal F_t]
      -\E_P[H_n(t,Z)\mid\mathcal F_t]
      +\E_P[H_n(t,Z)-H(t,Z)\mid\mathcal F_t].
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:v19-posterior}, conditional Jensen and
$(x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2$ bound its expected square by
$16M^2\varepsilon_n+2\E_\nu|H_n(t,Z)-H(t,Z)|^2$.
Integration gives the first bound in \eqref{eq:v19-globalH2} under $P$.
Under $P_n$ use the same decomposition with the signal-difference term
conditioned under $P_n$ and the law-change term applied to $H$. The common
latent marginal gives the identical bound. Jointly measurable conditional
versions, and predictable versions equal for $dt$-almost every time, give
the same integrated statements.

Put $g=e^{\gamma f}$ and $c=\E_\nu g$. Tilt the two full joint laws by
$g(Z)/c$, writing $P^g,P_n^g$. They still have the same latent marginal
$\nu_g=g\nu/c$, and their observation signals remain $H,H_n$. Since
$e^{-\gamma}\le g,c\le e^\gamma$,
\[
 \|P_n^g-P^g\|_{\rm TV}\le e^{2\gamma}\varepsilon_n,
 \qquad \eta_{n,g}^2\le e^{2\gamma}\eta_n^2.
\]
Moreover, the reference martingale coefficient $a^{n,g}$ from the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-entropic} satisfies
\[
 \alpha_t^n:=\frac{a_t^{n,g}}{N_t^{n,g}}
       =\sigma^{-1}\E_{P_n^g}[H_n(t,Z)\mid\mathcal F_t].
\]
Apply the preceding innovation argument to these tilted laws. It bounds
the squared integral of $\alpha^n-\alpha$ by $e^{2\gamma}A_n$ under
either tilted physical law. For an observation-measurable nonnegative
random variable, passage back to the corresponding original physical law
costs at most $e^{2\gamma}$: the full density $c/g$ is bounded by this
constant, and so is its observation projection. Consequently
\[
 \E_S\int_0^T|\alpha_t^n-\alpha_t|^2dt\le e^{4\gamma}A_n.
\]
The identity $Z^n=(\alpha^n-\theta^n)/\gamma$ proves the second bound
in \eqref{eq:v19-globalH2}.

The pointwise bounds, valid for all models, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-uniformcoefficients}
 |\theta^n|,|\alpha^n|\le M/\sigma,
 \qquad |Z^n|\le2M/(\gamma\sigma).
\end{equation}
The squared time integrals are thus uniformly bounded random variables.
Convergence in $P$-probability, followed by absolute continuity of the fixed
$R$ with respect to $P$, gives convergence in $R$-probability. Boundedness
then gives convergence of their $R$-expectations. This argument establishes
global reference convergence without claiming a new explicit reference rate.

For the coupling, let $E_n=\{Y^n=Y\}$, so that
$\Pp(E_n^c)\le\varepsilon_n$. On $E_n$ both processes are functions of
the same observation path. The restricted expectation of their squared
difference is at most the corresponding unrestricted expectation under the
limiting marginal $P$. On $E_n^c$ apply
\eqref{eq:v19-uniformcoefficients} and integrate for time $T$. This proves
\eqref{eq:v19-coupledtheta}--\eqref{eq:v19-coupledZ}.

On $E_n$, the Brownian reference coordinates $Y^n/\sigma,Y/\sigma$
agree, so
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}|\bar I_t^n-\bar I_t|^2
       \le T\int_0^T|\bar\theta_t^n-\bar\theta_t|^2dt.
\]
Markov's inequality and $\Pp(E_n^c)\to0$ prove uniform convergence in
probability. This assertion does not require any moment estimate on the
observation difference on the exceptional event.

Finally, the backward equation already proved gives, under each marginal,
\[
 \bar J_t^n=\bar U_t^n-U_0
                   +\frac\gamma2\int_0^t|\bar Z_s^n|^2ds.
\]
The common initial value is $U_0=\gamma^{-1}\log\E_\nu g$.
By \eqref{eq:v19-uniformcoefficients},
\[
 \big||\bar Z_s^n|^2-|\bar Z_s|^2\big|
             \le\frac{4M}{\gamma\sigma}|\bar Z_s^n-\bar Z_s|.
\]
Subtract the two integral identities, use Cauchy--Schwarz in time and
$(x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2$, and take expectations. This gives
\eqref{eq:v19-coupledJ}. The integral identity also defines compatible
measurable versions on the coupling, without asserting a martingale
property for its joint filtration.
\end{proof}

The theorem strengthens the localized conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-entropic} without changing its terminal condition or
its driver. The terminal condition remains the observation-measurable
conditional certainty equivalent, not the unobserved latent payoff.
Positive fixed noise, bounded signals, and a common preparation are used
in the proof. A singular zero-noise limit, an unbounded terminal condition,
or a different nonlinear driver would require additional arguments.
