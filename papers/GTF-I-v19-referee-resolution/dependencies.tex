\section{The canonical proof chain}
\label{sec:v18-dependencies}
The diagram separates exact realization and testing complexity from the
physical verification of the common-preparation hypotheses. Both feed the
same final certification theorem. A dependency arrow means that the stated
proof uses the source result, not that the source is the only possible
method of proof.

The binary optimal-value theorem is the classification and lower-bound
branch of the main result; it is not needed to prove the two-count upper
bound. The latter uses the deterministic continuation realization and the
specific private behavior geometry directly. The finite-reset minimax and
its moment support bound describe the more general audit framework.
The exact nonlinear witness, revealed-behavior hierarchy and rational chart
retain their separate information conventions and full proofs.

The comparison theorem, positive continuation realization, erasure-cut law,
reset minimax and sampling obstruction, chart, nonlinear witness, marked
scattering gap, posterior estimate, moving-collision estimate and localized
entropic theorem are inherited inputs. The stochastic residual error
inequality, exact binary audit-width threshold, compressed certificate and
global backward-integrand bounds are the additions of this revision.
The preceding introduction states which proof mechanisms are classical.
None of the label, source, build or diagnostic checks accompanying this
article is an independent verification of the analytic proofs.

The actual mark is never exposed to the online simulator. The reset auditor
has a separate completed-trial interface. A revealed-behavior test sees an
exact law. The common-latent maximal coupling may use an entire observation
path and is not an executable causal simulator. These privileges are not
interchangeable.
