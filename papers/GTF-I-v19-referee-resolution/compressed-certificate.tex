\section{A finite-register certificate transported through the microscopic limit}
\label{sec:v19-certificate}
The general empirical audit in Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-audit} treats every
coordinate of a marked row as unknown. The private continuation constraint
in the collision experiment forces a smaller family. Exploiting this
constraint yields a much smaller executable auditor, without granting it
knowledge of the candidate law.

In the all-on row of Section~\ref{sec:v18-microscopic}, every private
width-one candidate emits two independent bits with parameters $p,q$,
independently of the fair actual mark $W$. Put
\[
 a=(1-p)(1-q),\qquad c=pq.
\]
The ideal target $Q_*$ is supported on $000,001,110,111$, with respective
masses $5/16,3/16,3/16,5/16$. The candidate masses on these atoms are
$a/2,a/2,c/2,c/2$. Every other atom has target mass zero and cannot improve
the maximizing event for $Q_*-b$.

\begin{lemma}[Two-count audit and its exact register implementation]
\label{lem:v19-counter}
In $n$ independent all-on candidate trials let $C_0,C_1$ count report
pairs $00,11$, respectively, ignoring the training mark. On the four
supported atoms use the empirical masses
$C_0/(2n),C_0/(2n),C_1/(2n),C_1/(2n)$, and select exactly those atoms
whose target mass is strictly greater than this empirical mass. Call the
resulting event $A_C$. For every $p,q\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-countregret}
 \E\{Q_*(A_C)-b(A_C)\}\ge\|Q_*-b\|_{\rm TV}-n^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
For $n=400$, the rule is implemented by the two truncated counts
$\min(C_0,250),\min(C_1,250)$, with comparisons to $150$ and $250$.
Including execution of complete transcripts and the two independent
validation trials, it has a deterministic streaming realization with at
most $504008$ retained states at any scheduled epoch. Thus a nineteen-bit
register suffices. The schedule clock is free; no other history store is
free. The table description and transition evaluation cost are not asserted
to be bounded by nineteen bits.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On any supported atom let $x=Q_*(w)-b(w)$ and let $\widehat x$ be the
empirical discrepancy. If the sign rule selects a different event from
an exact maximizing event, the lost contribution is at most
$|x-\widehat x|$. On unsupported atoms both rules can omit the atom.
Summing gives regret at most
\[
 |C_0/n-a|+|C_1/n-c|.
\]
The two counts need not be independent. Their marginal binomial variances
and Cauchy--Schwarz give expected regret at most
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{a(1-a)}+\sqrt{c(1-c)}}{\sqrt n}\le\frac1{\sqrt n}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:v19-countregret}.

For the $00$ pair, select both marked atoms when $C_0<150$, only the
atom of target mass $5/16$ when $150\le C_0<250$, and neither when
$C_0\ge250$. The same rule applies to $11$, with the other mark being
the atom of larger target mass. At equality the zero empirical discrepancy
is excluded. Increasing a count beyond $250$ cannot change the final
choice. Two registers with $251$ possibilities each therefore implement
the rule, with at most nine possible final event masks.

A buffer with eight possibilities is sufficient to execute and record a
completed two-bit-plus-mark transcript. During training the pair of
truncated counts and this buffer use at most $8\cdot251^2=504008$ states.
After training, discard the counts and retain the event mask. During
validation at most nine masks, one target-event indicator with two values,
and the same eight-state transcript buffer are needed, a total of $144$
states. The final signed score takes three values. Since the epoch is
known, labels can be reused between these phases; their state counts need
not be multiplied. The update maps and threshold comparisons are
parameter-independent deterministic maps. They are thus stochastic rows
with entries zero or one, or equivalently a compatible continuation cover
by Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-continuation}. Fresh sampling randomness belongs to
the specified reset experiments, not to an uncharged auditor register.
Finally $504008<2^{19}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-register microscopic certification and global transport]
\label{thm:v19-composition}
Consider the marked hard-sphere family of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-microscopic}, with $d\in[9/10,11/10]$ and
$0<\sigma\le1/24$. There is one rational streaming audit, independent of
$d,\sigma,p,q$, using $400$ candidate training resets and one independent
candidate and target validation trial, whose expected signed score is
strictly greater than $2/5$ against every private width-one candidate.
It uses at most $504008$ retained auditor states. The candidate remains
private width one; the auditor's register is a distinct, charged resource.
The physical deficiencies still satisfy
\[
 \dpr>9/20,\qquad \dvi_r>9/20\ (r\ge1),\qquad \dhi_2<13/100.
\]
For $d\le2/5$ all three finite-experiment deficiencies vanish.

Fix a positive $\sigma$ and a sequence $d_k\to d$ within the collision
interval. Put
\[
 \rho_k=|d_k-d|+2700|d_k-d|^2,\qquad
 \epsilon_k=\min\{1,\sqrt{\rho_k}/(2\sigma)\}.
\]
The same $400$-reset rule changes its expected score by at most
$\epsilon_k$ under this perturbation. At the same time the microscopic
paths, full and projected likelihoods, posteriors, and bounded entropic
values have the joint convergence of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-microscopic}. Their innovations, backward integrands
and backward martingale integrals additionally have all the global
conclusions of Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-global}, with $T=3$, $M=1$,
$\eta_k^2\le\rho_k$, and $\varepsilon_k\le\epsilon_k$.
On compact parameter sets bounded away from zero noise, the same audit
has the effective probability-enclosure and rational-verification
properties of Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-microscopic}(d).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The private width-one continuation restriction gives precisely the
candidate family used in Lemma~\ref{lem:v19-counter}; the actual mark is
not available to its transition rows. The exact finite separation in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v17-markedgap} gives
\[
 \min_{p,q}\|Q_*-b(p,q)\|_{\rm TV}
             =d_*:=\sqrt{5/2}-9/8.
\]
The scattering and observation estimates proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-microscopic} put the physical marked target within
$1/300$ of $Q_*$, uniformly in all stated parameters. Train the two-count
rule against the fixed rational $Q_*$, but use the actual physical target
for validation. Each selected event's target probability changes by less
than $1/300$. Hence its actual expected score is greater than
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-physicalscore}
 d_*-\frac1{20}-\frac1{300}>\frac25.
\end{equation}
The last strict inequality is exact: it is equivalent to
$\sqrt{5/2}>947/600$, whose squared margin is $3191/360000$.
The rule's executable memory bound was proved in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v19-counter}. Its total trial count is $402$, not $400$
once validation is included. The private, hidden and visible deficiency
bounds and the no-collision control are inherited from the same physical
theorem, rather than inferred from an auditor whose signature was changed.

For changing $d_k$, Lemma~\ref{lem:v18-movingcollision} supplies the
signal modulus $\rho_k$ and the pathwise $J_1$ bound
$100|d_k-d|$. Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-gaussian} gives the joint marked
variation bound $\epsilon_k$ and the likelihood/posterior convergence.
The candidate source is blind and its behavior does not depend on $d$.
Thus training laws and candidate validation laws are unchanged, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:v18-audittransport} has $\alpha=0$ and
$\beta=\epsilon_k$. There is no factor of $400$ in this transport cost.
The same preparation and signal bounds verify every hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-global}, proving the global backward assertions.
Finally the audit uses rational thresholds and finitely many deterministic
row maps. The positive-noise enclosure theorem therefore applies directly;
an additional target-presentation error $\zeta$ reduces its score by at
most $\zeta$.
\end{proof}

The two-count construction is specific to the private behavior family. A
hidden mixture can destroy the independence of its two reports, and its
mark relation must still be checked. The bound is therefore not asserted
against the hidden class. The visible lower bound has its separate
fiberwise proof; an arbitrarily large visible alphabet is not silently
stored in the nineteen-bit auditor. The certificate concerns expected
score, not a confidence statement about a single realized validation pair.
Repeating validation for statistical confidence would incur an additional
sample and storage budget.

The theorem uses a single mechanism: the continuation restriction fixes
the feasible candidate laws and permits the compressed audit; scattering
supplies its marked separation; common-preparation variation transports the
same executable test and the global conditional-law consumer through a
change in the microscopic trajectory. The collision transduces a prepared
impact variable into a signal. It does not create the prepared correlation
between that variable and the mark, and it is not a particle-number limit.
