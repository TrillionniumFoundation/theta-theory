\section{Introduction}
A causal reduction consumes observations, emits reports and retains a state.
The dimension of that state and the visibility of persistent randomization
are part of the mathematical problem. The same is true of a test intended
to distinguish a reduction from its target: a test given the complete
candidate law has a different information budget from a test which reads
finitely many trials through a finite register. We study these two sides
of causal comparison together, without convexifying an unpriced private
resource class.

The organizing object is a family of normalized continuation responses,
with one compatible list at each cut. Compatibility means that every
one-step input--output shift is a positive combination of the next list.
It characterizes realizability at a prescribed register profile. For
zero--one terminal decisions, the lists reduce to residual equivalence
classes. This allows an exact calculation of the memory needed to attain
a statistical testing value, as opposed to an upper bound for the size of
an unconstrained certificate. We then use a private continuation restriction
to construct a small streaming certificate for an interacting microscopic
experiment. The same certificate and its conditional-law consumers are
transported through a change in the microscopic trajectory.

\subsection{Main results}
The resource signature distinguishes fresh private randomness from an
independent persistent hidden selector and from a visible selector. An
actual terminal mark belongs to the experiment; the online simulator
cannot read it, but the terminal comparison can. A reset auditor is a
separate machine. It reads specified independent completed trials of a
candidate held fixed during the audit. Its schedule is known and its
retained register is charged. It has no access to the candidate law or to
the candidate's private state.

\begin{theorem}[Continuation complexity and stable causal certification]
\label{thm:v19-main}
With these information conventions the following statements hold.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(i)} A finite causal kernel is realizable at a width profile if
and only if it admits a compatible normalized continuation cover of that
profile. For a Boolean terminal test, the exact minimal profile is the
number of distinct continuation responses at each cut, even when the
implementing machine is stochastic. If a cut has $r\ge2$ distinct
responses and only $K$ retained states, its total wordwise error is at
least $(r-K)_+/(r-1)$.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(ii)} For $0<h<1/2$, consider $n=2m+1$ independent binary samples from one of
$\operatorname{Ber}(1/2-h)$ and $\operatorname{Ber}(1/2+h)$, and the
signed event-discrepancy audit against $\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)$. Its
unrestricted value is $h\|P_+-P_-\|_{\rm TV}$. The exact training widths
needed to attain it are
\[
 r_t=\min\{t+1,n-t+2\},\qquad 0\le t<n.
\]
A smaller width at any cut has the strictly positive value deficit
\eqref{eq:v19-memorydeficit}. Thus the continuation invariant controls
both an implementation and a statistical obstruction.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(iii)} In the prepared two-sphere experiment of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-composition}, one rational audit using $400$ candidate
training resets, two validation trials, and at most $504008$ retained
auditor states has expected discrepancy greater than $2/5$ against every
private width-one candidate. The same audit works for every contact
diameter $9/10\le d\le11/10$ and $0<\sigma\le1/24$. The private and
visible deficiencies exceed $9/20$, whereas a hidden two-valued selector
has error less than $13/100$. Removing the collision, by taking $d\le2/5$,
makes all three finite-experiment deficiencies zero.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(iv)} With positive noise fixed, changing $d$ changes both the
collision time and the microscopic path. If
$\rho=|d'-d|+2700|d'-d|^2$, the same audit's expected score changes by at
most $\min\{1,\sqrt\rho/(2\sigma)\}$. Along $d'\to d$, the microscopic
paths, full and projected likelihoods, and bounded posterior processes
converge jointly in the senses specified below. For the associated bounded
entropic backward equation, values converge in supremum $L^2$ and
integrands converge globally in time-integrated $L^2$, including on the
common-latent coupling. Innovations converge uniformly in probability,
and backward martingale integrals converge in supremum $L^2$.
\end{theorem}

Part (i) follows from Theorems~\ref{thm:v18-continuation} and
\ref{thm:v19-residual}; part (ii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-binarywidth}. Parts (iii)--(iv) are the single
composition theorem proved in Section~\ref{sec:v19-certificate}, using
the global transport theorem of Section~\ref{sec:v19-global}. The two
registers in part (iii) have different roles: the candidate remains private
width one, while the auditor's nineteen-bit implementation has its own
explicit budget. This is not a statement that the candidate can borrow
the auditor's memory.

The finite-data construction is materially tied to the resource geometry.
The private width-one candidate has independent reports and a fair
independent mark. Only two report-pair probabilities determine the four
relevant marked coordinates. Two truncated empirical counts therefore
replace a full multinomial table. A direct regret inequality gives the
$400$-sample rule, and its deterministic continuation states give the
register bound. The collision supplies the separating marked target. The
common-preparation variation estimate then transports the very same rule,
not a reoptimized rule, together with the global backward representation.

\subsection{The general comparison framework}
For a compact marked behavior image $B$ and fixed target rows $Q_j$, set
\[
 \delta(B,Q)=\min_{b\in B}\max_{j,A}\{Q_j(A)-b_j(A)\}.
\]
Complements supply both signs. Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main} supplies
compactness, exact factorization, priced composition and perturbation at
the declared signature. Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-audit} gives an attained
finite-reset moment dual on the original, possibly nonconvex $B$ and
$0\le\delta-\vaudit_n\le\sqrt{D/n}$. Its dual chooses one candidate for
the entire replicated experiment; its barycenter cannot replace that
candidate mixture. Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-auditlower} proves a matching
uniform sampling exponent. The new memory obstruction is different: it
identifies exactly when a streaming auditor attains the unconstrained
value, and gives a strict deficit otherwise. We do not exchange minimax
on the nonconvex set of fixed-width auditors.

The revealed-behavior representation and its intrinsic Bernstein hierarchy
remain separate tools, with their full proofs and exact nonlinear example.
Their evaluator sees an exact law, unlike the reset auditor. The formal
rational chart algorithm and physical probability enclosures retain this
distinction. Effective here means an exact terminating construction under
specified rational inputs and positive-noise hypotheses, not polynomial
bit complexity.

\subsection{Classical inputs and the contribution}
Statistical comparison and randomization have a classical foundation in
Blackwell and Le Cam \cite{Blackwell1953,LeCam1964,Torgersen1991}; filtered
comparison predates this work \cite{Norberg2002,Weisshaupt2006}. We do not
claim priority for introducing a filtration or a randomization criterion.
The original proof-level crosswalk with Norberg remains unverified; no
assertion about absence of the present results from that work is made.

Stable positive-cone realization is classical \cite{Fliess1975}, as is
residual-state minimization \cite{Nerode1958}. The continuation theorem is
a finite controlled normalization with a simultaneous width profile. The
zero--one residual characterization is proved here in a stochastic
implementation model, not claimed as a new deterministic automata theorem.
The added step is the overlap inequality \eqref{eq:v19-overlap}, which
converts insufficient continuation width into statistical regret and an
exact optimal-value threshold for the binary reset game. The quantitative
penalty is explicit, but not claimed to be a sharp joint asymptotic law in
sample size, memory and error.

The minimax theorem, moment support reduction, empirical second moments,
two-point testing, and polynomial positivity are classical tools
\cite{Sion1958,deKlerkLaurent2010,LaurentSlot2023,deKlerkVera2026}.
Likewise the scattering formula, Bayes' formula, Girsanov's theorem and
martingale inequalities are inputs, not discoveries. The global backward
estimate uses a denominator-free integrated posterior comparison and a
bounded terminal tilt. It improves the localized transport estimate while
retaining the same actual terminal condition and driver. The contribution
offered here is the information-budgeted theorem chain: compatible
continuations, constrained testing value, an executable microscopic
certificate, and global transport of its conditional-law consumer.

\subsection{Microscopic and programmatic scope}
The physical family has two labeled spheres and one nongrazing collision.
The correlation between the impact variable and the actual mark is
prepared; the collision creates an observable transverse signal of that
variable. The feedback is an acquisition gate, not a mechanical force.
Varying contact diameter is a genuinely changing microscopic path, not a
Boltzmann--Grad limit. The global transport theorem assumes a common
preparation, bounded signals and fixed positive noise. Its bounded scalar
entropic equation has an observation-measurable conditional certainty
equivalent at terminal time, not an unobserved latent terminal payoff.

This gives an explicit conditional-law consumer in the larger theta-theory
program, including changing paths, likelihoods, projected stochastic
exponentials and the complete bounded backward representation. It does not
manufacture a dependence of the independent A2 geometric chain, nor use an
unverified kinetic resolvent or rigidity theorem as an input. The wider
program and its historical targets are retained in full. Their source
assertions, current proof credit and independent-review status are recorded
separately in the accompanying statement ledger.
