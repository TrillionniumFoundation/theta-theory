# Focused primary-source comparison, A2 v25

This is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive priority search. On 3 October 2026 the official arXiv abstracts and bibliographic records below were checked; the planar lens-rigidity publisher abstract was also checked. The new geometric and effective-recognition proofs are given in full in the manuscript rather than attributed to an unseen theorem in these sources.

Trefethen, arXiv:1908.11097v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11097: bounded complex-domain priors make continuation conditionally possible but potentially severely ill-conditioned, especially along strips. This is the closest methodological comparison for Lemma 3.1. That lemma uses classical interpolation and three-circles, with an explicit deliberately nonoptimal constant; no new general continuation principle is claimed.

Stefanov–Uhlmann–Vasy, arXiv:1702.03638v3, https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03638: local boundary-distance determination of a metric near strictly convex boundary, and lens rigidity with convexity conditions. Those unknown metrics and boundary/lens observations differ from periodic obstacle records with a local value probe.

Gurfinkel–Noakes–Stoyanov, arXiv:2003.12261v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12261: travelling times for exterior obstacle trajectories on a Riemannian surface, flow conjugacy on nontrapping regions, and convex-obstacle uniqueness under nonpositive curvature. This is not a claim about recognition of periodic copies from bounded local chart samples.

Noakes–Stoyanov, arXiv:1404.4147, https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.4147: generic exterior travelling-time conjugacy and constructive recovery for two convex planar components. The direct abstract fetch was unavailable in one call; the official arXiv indexed abstract and publisher abstract corroborated this scope.

Noakes–Stoyanov, *Lens Rigidity in Scattering by Unions of Strictly Convex Bodies in R^2*, https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1270409: the publisher abstract states the planar union-of-bodies rigidity theorem and clarifies the separate planar proof relative to the earlier higher-dimensional paper. Section 8 now explicitly records this general planar result, rather than implying that known exterior rigidity stops at two obstacles. It does not share the present selected periodic data, active chart-value access or absolute-area completion mechanism.

The current work does not claim to supersede those results. Nor are standard differentiation, quadrature, error spending and coupon estimates presented as novel concentration or approximation theorems. The proposed advance is effective within-table bridge recognition and its value-access implementation, with stated quantitative priors and measurement costs.
