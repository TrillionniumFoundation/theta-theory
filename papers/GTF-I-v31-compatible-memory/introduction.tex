\section{Introduction}
A finite statistical response law may have a small positive factorization
at every cut and nevertheless require a larger state space when those
factorizations must be executed in succession. The difficulty is not
linear dimension alone: the generators selected at one cut determine
continuations which must be available at the next. Conversely, some
families have enough additive structure that a static factorization
already specifies a lossless online implementation. This paper proves
both statements in one precise model.

Our first main result is the family in Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction}.
An input with four possible values is followed by a five-valued command
and a binary query. Only the final binary answer is released. Each of
the two internal cuts separately has minimum state count three. No machine
has three states at both cuts, even though all prescribed answer
probabilities can be bounded between $1/20$ and $19/20$. The profiles
$(3,4)$ and $(4,3)$ are both realized explicitly. The proof excludes
arbitrary stochastic encoders and decoders, not merely residual-state
machines or a chosen architecture. It uses a forced affine slice at the
first cut and a quantitative triangle constraint at the second.

The contrast is sharp in cut rank. Proposition~\ref{prop:slice-shift}
shows that the intersection of the normalized causal polytope with the
affine span of the actual residuals is closed under normalized shifts.
If these canonical sections are simplices, their vertices provide a
simultaneously optimal machine (Theorem~\ref{thm:slice}). All nonempty
one-dimensional sections are intervals, so full-support cut ranks at most
two cannot exhibit the preceding failure. This criterion uses an intrinsic
polytope, not unknown transition coefficients. The invariant-cone and
residual-realization mechanisms have classical antecedents
\cite{Heller,Vidyasagar,DenisEsposito}; the explicit rank-three obstruction
is stated separately from them.

Our second main result is Theorem~\ref{thm:additive}. A stochastic encoder
on a product input alphabet which is additive in its input coordinates
can be computed by a finite-state replacement process using exactly the
same label alphabet. There is no retained coordinate selector or common
random seed. An enclosing simplex has affine barycentric coordinates,
so every static simplex encoder for the binary-query channel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:query}
 \Prb(B=b\mid x,j)=\frac{1+b\eta x_j}{2},\qquad
 x\in\{-1,1\}^n,\quad j\in[n],\quad b\in\{-1,1\},
\end{equation}
has an online realization of the same peak. In particular, in every
dimension and at every nonzero signal,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold-intro}
 K_n(\eta)=n+1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 W_n^{\rm fo}(\eta)=n+1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W_n^{\rm ad}(\eta)=n+1.
\end{equation}
The quantities on the right count all acquisition cuts; $K_n$ constrains
only the cut preceding the query. The proof of the first implication
works for every enclosing simplex, not only orthogonal or Hadamard ones.

The static critical inequality $\eta\le1/n$ is classical cube--simplex
geometry. We state its relationship to the absorption index explicitly.
Nevskii and Ukhalov's critical simplices in dimensions five and nine
\cite{NU} give, by the online theorem, six- and ten-state implementations
at $\eta=1/5$ and $\eta=1/9$. We display the rational vertices and the
replacement distributions, so this consequence does not rely on a
hypothetical static-to-dynamic transfer. Critical simplex matrices are
also closed under a normalized tensor construction, giving further
explicit online families without invoking unproved Hadamard existence.
The static inclusions are not claimed as new.

A further distinction can be settled exactly. The minimum checkpoint
alphabet for \eqref{eq:query} equals that for classical private-randomness
random access with worst-case success at least $(1+\eta)/2$. This follows
already from the geometric characterization and cube-containment argument
of Kondo et al.\ \cite[Theorem 14, Lemma 15]{Kondo2026}. We give the short
normalization and exactification proof in Section~\ref{sec:rac}. No extra
persistent symmetrization seed is required. Therefore exact conditional
rows do not themselves establish a different checkpoint exponent from
classical random-access coding \cite{ANTV}. The online implementation,
not that exponent, is the object of the present synthesis theorem.

A state count means the number of persistent labels. The primary model
has exact atomic stochastic rows and an externally declared clock.
Program descriptions and row-construction time are not part of that
count. Section~\ref{sec:implementation} gives a separate finite fair-bit
implementation with its additional phase and sampling registers. A query
is a current input, not a stored word; a model requiring a query buffer
must charge it. These conventions are used consistently in both upper
and lower bounds.
