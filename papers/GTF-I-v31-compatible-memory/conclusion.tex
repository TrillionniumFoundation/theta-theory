\section{Consequences and retained results}
The main distinction is now witnessed, rather than merely formulated.
At full-support cut rank three, independent minimum positive
factorizations need not fit one machine; the exact profile set
\eqref{eq:pareto} exhibits the cost of that incompatibility. At cut
rank at most two, canonical affine sections always yield compatible
minimum realizations. Additive encoders give a different structural
class with no online alphabet penalty, regardless of the size or shape
of the original residual hull.

For the binary-query problem, the minimal $n+1$ checkpoint alphabet
has an online realization whenever it exists. The critical geometric
threshold is therefore shared by checkpoint and full acquisition peak,
not only in Hadamard dimensions. The static absorption results are
classical; the transition formulas transfer each of them to an actual
online machine. At larger message alphabets a generic encoder need not
be additive, and the present theorem does not identify every finite
state count or the second-order streaming excess. No new complete
varying-signal phase diagram is inferred.

The full-support two-cut experiment has a fixed order of three atomic
inputs. It establishes a compatibility obstruction, not a theorem
optimizing all informative experimental actions or all report-dependent
collision-validation schedules. The earlier controlled minimax,
marked-saddle, positive-noise collision, fixed-clock scheduling,
revelation, entropy-rate and acquired-transport results remain in the
unchanged supporting article and cumulative mathematical volumes. Their
statistical experiments and resource conventions are not replaced by
the binary-query interface. Known-target exact stochastic rows and
acquired-target approximate guarantees remain distinct.

The repository's historical A2/A3/A4 and B2/B1/B3/B4/C1/C2/D1 derivations
have independent Fourier, large-deviation, operator-domain, semigroup
and optional-projection hypotheses. Preserving those derivations does
not make them consequences of a finite positive-realization theorem.
The new proof dependencies are internal and explicit: affine slices
locate the rank boundary; the forced-triangle obstruction proves a
nonattainable profile; additive synthesis transfers critical simplices
to online memory; exactification identifies the correct classical
checkpoint comparison. No historical analytic gate is marked complete
on the strength of that transfer.

The primary literature comparison also has precise limits. We use the
cube-absorption statements and explicit simplices of \cite{NU}, and
the full v3 worst-case coding characterization of \cite{Kondo2026}.
Positive realization and residual-state representations are older
frameworks \cite{Heller,Vidyasagar,ELDD02,DenisEsposito}; their general
existence language is not claimed as an invention. The support/affine
arguments here must be distinguished from arbitrary nonnegative-rank
minimization \cite{GG}, and complete arrays from incomplete machine
specifications \cite{Reusch}. General filtered-experiment comparison
\cite{Norberg} is not a priority claim of the paper. The exact
quantifiers and explicit constructions, rather than the size of the
preserved development, are the mathematical assertions submitted for
independent examination.
