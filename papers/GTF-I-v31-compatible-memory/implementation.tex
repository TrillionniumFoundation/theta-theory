\section{Finite coins and stochastic-language conventions}
\label{sec:implementation}
\subsection{A separate fair-bit register ledger}
The exact label counts above refer to atomic stochastic rows. They do
not include the internal computation of a real-probability sampler. For
the rational constructions a fully finite implementation is available,
with an additional cost which can be stated without hiding a counter
or the current input.

\begin{proposition}[Finite fair-bit implementation of rational rows]
\label{prop:finite-coins}
Consider $T$ input stages, an at-most-$m$-label transducer with $m\ge2$,
and current input alphabet size at most $d$. Suppose every row has a
common denominator for its entries of size at most $D\ge2$. Put
$b=\lceil\log_2D\rceil$. In a self-paced protocol which supplies the
next input only after the preceding update completes, the transducer
has an exact fair-bit implementation using at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coin-states}
                2(T+1)md\,2^b
\end{equation}
states, including phase, current input, old label, sampler prefix and
terminal output. Each stage uses fewer than $2b$ fair bits on average.
Thus its persistent bit cost is at most
\[
 \lceil\log_2(T+1)\rceil+\lceil\log_2m\rceil+
 \lceil\log_2d\rceil+b+2.
\]
This changes the implementation model and does not assert preservation
of the atomic optimum in total states.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a row of denominator $D_\rho\le D$, let
$b_\rho=\lceil\log_2D_\rho\rceil$. Draw $b_\rho$ fair bits to form
an integer $J$ between zero and $2^{b_\rho}-1$. If $J\ge D_\rho$,
repeat; otherwise map $J$ through the row's integer cumulative sums to
the new label. A denominator-one row is deterministic. The acceptance
probability is $D_\rho/2^{b_\rho}>1/2$, unless it is one, and the
accepted integer is uniform. This proves exactness and the expectation
bound.

Retain the phase, old label and current input throughout an attempted
draw. The proper binary prefixes of length less than $b_\rho$ form a
tree with $2^{b_\rho}-1\le2^b-1$ nodes. On the last bit, acceptance
can move directly to the ready state containing the new label;
rejection returns to the root. Hence a separate leaf or unbounded trial
counter is unnecessary. There are at most $(T+1)m$ ready states,
$Tmd(2^b-1)$ sampler states and two final states. Their sum is at most
\eqref{eq:coin-states}. The input provider waits at an explicit ready
boundary; the phase is stored, not supplied as a free data-dependent
clock.
\end{proof}

For \eqref{eq:U5} the distributions $q_i$ have denominators dividing
six, and for \eqref{eq:U9} they divide ten. Thus a row at acquisition
stage $t$ has denominator dividing $6t$ or $10t$, respectively. The
critical decoders are also rational. More generally the normalized
matrix tensors in Proposition~\ref{prop:critical-tensor} have rational
denominators bounded by products of fixed seed denominators; their
logarithms are $O(\log(n+1))$. The finite-coin implementation of those
families therefore uses $O(\log(n+1))$ persistent bits, with explicit
rational row data. This assertion concerns the critical polynomial-size
matrix families, not efficient construction of a fixed-interior-signal
exponential codebook.

\subsection{Internal normalization of probabilistic automata}
\begin{definition}[Proper stochastic-language automaton]
\label{def:automaton}
On a finite alphabet $\Sigma$, a proper probabilistic automaton consists
of a finite state set, an initial probability row $\alpha$, nonnegative
transition matrices $T_a$ for $a\in\Sigma$, and stopping probabilities
$\tau_s$, with
\[
                  \tau_s+\sum_{a,t}T_a(s,t)=1.
\]
Stopping emits no further symbol; a transition emits its label $a$.
Termination is almost sure from every reachable state. The generated
stochastic language and state languages are
\[
 L(w)=\alpha T_w\tau,\qquad L_s(w)=e_s^TT_w\tau.
\]
They are probability distributions on finite words. Unreachable states
are removed. The automaton is residual if every reachable state language
is $L_u(w)=L(uw)/L(u\Sigma^*)$ for some positive-mass prefix $u$.
\end{definition}

These are the conventions used when comparing with probabilistic
residual automata \cite{ELDD02,DenisEsposito}. If a nonnegative
representation initially has state continuation masses $h_s\in(0,\infty)$,
normalization replaces $T_a(s,t)$ by $T_a(s,t)h_t/h_s$, $\tau_s$ by
$\tau_s/h_s$, and $\alpha_s$ by $\alpha_sh_s$. The identity
$h_s=\tau_s+\sum_{a,t}T_a(s,t)h_t$ gives stochastic rows. States with
$h_s=0$ have no contribution to any generated word and are deleted.
Thus finite nonzero continuation masses can be normalized without
adding states. Infinite continuation mass is not a stochastic language
and is not included in this convention.

For completeness, we retain the operational separation with a proof in
these internal terms. It is a result of the preceding revision, not a
new succinctness-priority assertion.

\begin{proposition}[A normalized residual/positive separation]
\label{prop:language}
For $n\ge2$ and rational $0<\eta\le(2n-1)^{-1}$, define the finite
language
\begin{equation}\label{eq:language}
 L(u_{x_1}\cdots u_{x_n}q_j o_b)
              =\frac{2^{-n}}n\frac{1+b\eta x_j}{2},
\end{equation}
using disjoint input, query and output symbols, and give zero mass to
all other words. Every residual automaton has at least $2^n$ states.
A proper rational positive automaton has at most
$(n+1)(n+2)/2+n(n+1)+1$ states, including phase and query storage.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The query-phase residuals are the $2^n$ vertices of an affine cube,
with probabilities $(1+b\eta x_j)/(2n)$ on the two-symbol words
$q_j o_b$. Conditioning any proper automaton on a supported prefix
expresses that residual as a convex combination of normalized state
languages. Nonnegativity forces each used language to be supported on
those two-symbol words. A residual of \eqref{eq:language} after length
$t$ is concentrated on length $n+2-t$. Hence a residual state used here
must itself be one of the query-phase cube vertices. Extremality forces
a separate state for each of the $2^n$ vertices.

For the upper bound, generate the input bits independently and fairly.
At phase $t$ use labels $0,1,\ldots,t$. On the next input, retain the
old label with probability $t/(t+1)$; otherwise replace it by $t+1$
for a positive bit and zero for a negative bit. After $t$ inputs the
probability of label $i>0$ is $\1_{\{x_i=1\}}/t$ and the probability
of zero is the fraction of negative bits. At phase $n$, associate means
$v_0=-\eta\1$ and $v_i=-\eta\1+2n\eta e_i$. They are in the cube
when $\eta\le(2n-1)^{-1}$, and their mixture has mean $\eta x$.
Emit $q_j$ uniformly, store $(s,j)$, emit $o_b$ with mean $v_{s,j}$,
and terminate. Phase tags require $\sum_{t=0}^n(t+1)$ states, query
buffers require $n(n+1)$, and one state terminates. Every state language
is normalized and every row is rational. Multiplication of the three
stages yields \eqref{eq:language}.
\end{proof}
