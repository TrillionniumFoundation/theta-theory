\section{Compatible critical edges in guarded return packets}
\label{sec:v42-morse-packet}
Proposition~\ref{prop:general-edge} already classifies every regular
critical word and bounds its leading jump. The purpose here is different:
to sum a two-jet subtraction over all regular words below a collision
cutoff, with a quantitative derivative budget, without removing the
normal initial states. The conservative guard set contains grazing, first-hit singularities
and section-decision boundaries, as well as the auxiliary zeros in
\eqref{eq:explicit-collision-cutoff}. No separation between critical
roof values is required.

Throughout this section the table is circular, $R\in I$, and the source
law is the actual section probability $\nu_R^*$. Put
$c=\nu(Y_R^*)=c_*$ and $L_{m,R}=\sum_{j<m}\tau_R\circ T_R^j$.
The symbol $L$ without a subscript is an integer collision cutoff.
The total variation of a signed measure is its full variation mass.

\subsection{Quantitative coordinates near a regular critical point}
\begin{lemma}[Critical coordinates on a guarded branch]
\label{lem:v42-critical-coordinates}
On a regular $m$-collision branch write
$DT_R^m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\ C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The critical points of $L_{m,R}$ are precisely the states with
$p=p_m=0$. At such a point its Hessian in $(\alpha,p)$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-critical-hessian}
 \nabla^2L_{m,R}
   =R\begin{pmatrix}AC&BC\\BC&BD\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det\nabla^2L_{m,R}=R^2BC>0.
\end{equation}
In particular it is positive definite. There is at most one regular
critical point for a fixed ordered sequence of centers.

On a source region where the guards through time $m$ are bounded away
from their excluded sets at scale $\varepsilon$, the branch maps,
their first ten derivatives and the regularity neighborhoods have upper
and reciprocal lower bounds of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-regularity-budget}
 H=\exp\{C(L+1)\log(C/\varepsilon)\},\qquad m\le L.
\end{equation}
After increasing the absolute constant $C$, the following additional
bounds hold. A relevant critical point has a Morse coordinate chart
with $C^6$ coordinate and inverse bounds at most $H$, radius at least
$H^{-1}$, and
\[
 L_{m,R}(x)=t_z+|z|^2/2.
\]
Outside the smaller critical neighborhoods the roof gradient on the
source supports is at least $H^{-1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The action and differential identities in
Lemma~\ref{lem:self-contained-collision-differential} give
\[
 \partial_\alpha L_{m,R}=R(p_m A-p),\qquad
 \partial_p L_{m,R}=Rp_m B.
\]
Every entry of $(-1)^mDT_R^m$ is positive, by
\eqref{eq:positive-collision-matrix}; its determinant is one.
In particular $B\ne0$, which proves the critical-point characterization.
Differentiating at $p=p_m=0$ gives respectively $RAC$,
$R(AD-1)=RBC$ and $RBD$. This proves
\eqref{eq:v42-critical-hessian}. Its leading diagonal entry and
its determinant are positive. The uniqueness assertion is precisely
the convex endpoint-action argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:general-edge}; it concerns a physical word,
not a change of inducing section.

Here are the quantitative details used below. Use the explicit
one-collision guard of \eqref{eq:explicit-collision-cutoff}, with a
slightly enlarged regular region obtained by replacing its margins by
half their values. On that region each positive selected discriminant
and each incidence denominator has a power-law lower bound in
$\varepsilon$. The finite root formulas and reflection formulas may
therefore be differentiated through any fixed order. Through order
ten their bounds are $(C/\varepsilon)^C$. Repeated chain rules for
at most $L+1$ maps give \eqref{eq:v42-regularity-budget}. For clarity,
one can induct on the derivative order: the highest derivative of a
composition occurs linearly, and all products of lower derivatives
are bounded by a fixed power of the previous exponential bound.
Thus no exponent of the form $C^L$ is introduced into $\log H$.
The same argument, applied to the finitely many guard functions and
their margins, gives a regular coordinate ball of radius $H^{-1}$
about every point of the smaller guarded region. Constants in the
exponent may be increased a fixed number of times below.

Each entry of a positive one-flight matrix is at least
$v_0=(3/50)/(47/100)=6/47$. Hence each entry of its $m$-fold positive
product is at least $v_0^m$. This crude lower bound suffices; no
hyperbolic asymptotic is needed. The inverse of
\[
 (\alpha,p)\longmapsto(p,p_m),\qquad
 \det D(p,p_m)=-C,
\]
has norm at most a fixed power of $H$ on each regularity ball.
The quantitative inverse theorem follows here by applying the
contraction map to the linearized equation on a smaller ball: the
inverse derivative and second derivative are bounded by powers of
$H$, so its image contains a ball of radius $H^{-C_1}$.
Consequently a sufficiently small value of $|(p,p_m)|$ is within
$H^{-C_1}$ of its unique zero in that regular branch. The displayed
roof-gradient identities have an inverse coefficient matrix bounded
by a power of $H$. They give the asserted lower gradient bound outside
smaller critical neighborhoods after increasing $C$ in
\eqref{eq:v42-regularity-budget}. Apply this reasoning on the enlarged
guarded region, so a zero found near a source support is still regular.

At a critical point, \eqref{eq:v42-critical-hessian} has norm at most
a power of $H$ and smallest eigenvalue at least a reciprocal power
of $H$. An explicit Morse chart can be constructed as follows. With
$h=x-x_0$, set
\[
 Q(h)=2\int_0^1(1-s)\nabla^2L_{m,R}(x_0+sh)\,ds,
 \qquad z=Q(h)^{1/2}h.
\]
On a ball of reciprocal-power radius this matrix remains positive
definite and $L_{m,R}(x)-L_{m,R}(x_0)=|z|^2/2$.
Differentiating the positive matrix square root, or its defining
Sylvester equation, bounds its first six derivatives by powers of
$H$. Its derivative at zero is the invertible positive square root
of the Hessian. The same contraction-map inverse argument yields the
chart and inverse bounds claimed. Relabeling a fixed power of $H$
by increasing $C$ completes the proof. These estimates hold for each
regular branch and do not identify its boundary with a smooth branch.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two jets rather than a single jump}
\begin{lemma}[An invariant two-jet edge subtraction]
\label{lem:v42-two-jet}
Let $f$ have a nondegenerate minimum $x_0$ on a smooth two-dimensional
source chart, and let $b(x)\,dx$ be a smooth compactly supported source
in that chart. Choose a smooth source cutoff equal to one near $x_0$
and supported in a Morse chart $f=t_0+|z|^2/2$. Write the resulting
source density in that chart as $g(z)$, extended smoothly by zero.
Its roof density is, for $s>0$,
\[
 F(t_0+s)=\int_0^{2\pi}g(\sqrt{2s}\,n_\theta)\,d\theta,
 \qquad F(t_0+s)=0\quad(s<0).
\]
The right-hand value and derivative are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-edge-jets}
 a_0=2\pi g(0)=\frac{2\pi b(x_0)}{\sqrt{\det\nabla^2f(x_0)}},
 \qquad a_1=\pi\Delta g(0).
\end{equation}
They do not depend on the Morse chart or on a cutoff equal to one in
a neighborhood of $x_0$. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-rational-edge}
 e_{x_0}(t_0+s)
   =\one_{s\ge0}e^{-s}\{a_0+(a_0+a_1)s\},
\end{equation}
one has $F-e_{x_0}\in W^{2,1}(\R)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-rational-edge-transform}
 \widehat e_{x_0}(b_0)=e^{ib_0t_0}
       \left(\frac{a_0}{1-ib_0}
                    +\frac{a_0+a_1}{(1-ib_0)^2}\right).
\end{equation}
On charts with the bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:v42-critical-coordinates},
the coefficient sizes and the $W^{2,1}$ norm of the residual are bounded
by a fixed power of $H$, times the corresponding bounded source norm.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Polar coordinates, with $s=r^2/2$ and $r\,dr=ds$, give the density.
In the Taylor expansion of $g(rn_\theta)$ all odd angular monomials
integrate to zero, while
$\int n_\theta n_\theta^{\mathsf T}d\theta=\pi I_2$.
This gives \eqref{eq:v42-edge-jets}. The same expansion with integral
remainders through order four shows that the radial average has two
bounded right derivatives at zero. Away from zero, differentiating
under the angular integral is legitimate. Its $C^2$ bound is controlled
by the $C^4$ norm of $g$ and the reciprocal chart radius. The cutoff
makes it compactly supported in $s$. Changing a cutoff away from the
minimum changes no sufficiently small roof level in this chart.
Changing the chart changes no pushforward measure. Thus the two right
jets are intrinsic to the local source contribution.

The right value of \eqref{eq:v42-rational-edge} is $a_0$ and its right
derivative is $a_1$. The residual and its first derivative therefore
match their zero left values. Its second distributional derivative
has no atom, and its two smooth pieces have integrable second
derivatives. This proves $W^{2,1}$, including the exponential part
outside the original source chart. Integrating $e^{-(1-ib_0)s}$ and
$s e^{-(1-ib_0)s}$ gives \eqref{eq:v42-rational-edge-transform}.
All bounds follow from the displayed formulas and the chart bounds.
Subtracting only the leading exponential would instead leave, in
general, an atom in the second derivative; the second jet removes it.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The actual stopped source, with its normal states retained}
For a regular initial state with $N_{n,R}=m\le L$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-stopped-guard}
 G_{n,L,R,\varepsilon}(x)=\prod_{j=0}^{m}
                         \chi_{R,\varepsilon}(T_R^jx),
\end{equation}
and set it to zero on $N_{n,R}>L$ and on undefined itineraries.
Unlike \eqref{eq:whole-itinerary-cutoff}, this definition contains
\emph{no} factor cutting out $p=0$. The product also stops at the
actual terminal collision, rather than testing subsequent collisions.
Define
\[
 Q_{n,R}^{L,\varepsilon}=(J_{n,R})_*
                   (G_{n,L,R,\varepsilon}\nu_R^*),\qquad
 \mu_{n,R}=(J_{n,R})_*\nu_R^*.
\]
Both are measures on $\Z^3\times\R$. The label
$\ell=(k_1,k_2,m)$ contains the actual collision count.

\begin{theorem}[A compatible all-word regular critical packet]
\label{thm:v42-regular-critical-packet}
There is an exact decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-exact-density-packet}
 \mu_{n,R}=e_{n,R}^{L,\varepsilon}+q_{n,R}^{L,\varepsilon}
                                      +r_{n,R}^{L,\varepsilon},
\end{equation}
where the notation also denotes mixed densities. The last density is
nonnegative and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-removed-mass}
 \sum_\ell\|r_{n,R}^{L,\varepsilon}(\ell,\cdot)\|_1
   \le C\{e^{an-c_0L}+(L+1)\varepsilon^{1/16}\}=:\delta_{n,L,\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
Here $a,c_0>0$ are fixed cumulative-tail constants, and $n\le L$.
The edge density is the finite sum of
\eqref{eq:v42-rational-edge}, at the actual label and roof value of
\emph{every} regular critical point meeting the guarded source.
Its coefficients use the density
$(4\pi c)^{-1}G_{n,L,R,\varepsilon}$ in $(\alpha,p)$ on that source
piece. Each component of $q_{n,R}^{L,\varepsilon}$ belongs to
$W^{2,1}(\R)$, and for a fixed family constant $C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-summed-derivative-budget}
 \sum_z(|a_{0,z}|+|a_{1,z}|)
       +\sum_\ell\|q_{n,R}^{L,\varepsilon}(\ell,\cdot)\|_{W^{2,1}}
       \le \exp\{C(L+1)\log(C/\varepsilon)\}=:\mathcal A_{L,\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
There is no separate normal-strip removal, and coincident critical
roof values are allowed. Regular points on auxiliary guard zeros may
still lie in $r$; the conclusion concerns the whole guarded packet.
The edge density need not be nonnegative.

For a fixed regular critical return word with strictly positive margins
for every factor of \eqref{eq:explicit-collision-cutoff} through time $m$,
its two coefficients stabilize as soon as $L\ge m$ and
$\varepsilon$ is small enough. Its stabilized leading coefficient is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-physical-critical-jump}
 a_{0,z}=\frac1{2cR\sqrt{B_mC_m}}.
\end{equation}
The stabilized first jet is the intrinsic right roof derivative of
that same physical source contribution. This coefficient compatibility
is a statement about regular words; it does not assert convergence of
the infinite sum of their coefficients.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $0\le G\le1$, the difference $\mu-Q$ is positive. The cumulative
return tail bounds its part on $N_n>L$. On the rest,
\[
 1-\prod_{j=0}^{N_n}\chi\circ T_R^j
       \le\sum_{j=0}^{L}\one_{\{\chi\circ T_R^j\ne1\}}.
\]
Use $\nu_R^*\le c^{-1}\nu$, invariance of $\nu$, and
\eqref{eq:collision-cutoff-measure} to prove
\eqref{eq:v42-removed-mass}. Coarea absolute continuity applies to
$Y_R^*$ exactly as in Corollary~\ref{cor:raw-density}: the critical
set on each regular center word is at most a singleton, independent
of the choice of inducing section. Bounded source restriction and the
countable sum over $m$ preserve absolute continuity. Thus $\mu-Q$
has the stated nonnegative density.

Partition $\{N_n\le L\}$ by center words and section decisions.
All resulting source functions extend by zero smoothly: at the first
collision or decision where the word can change, its corresponding
guard vanishes on a two-sided neighborhood. This is the
first-decision proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:source-partition-zero-extension},
now through any fixed derivative order and with the product stopped
at $m$. No pole $1/p$ is used in this argument. The one-step list of
centers and the list of section decisions are finite. The number of
records through $L$ is consequently at most $C_1^{L+1}$; their open
sets may be disconnected without affecting the zero extension.
Each fixed center word has at most one regular critical point.

On each source piece apply Lemma~\ref{lem:v42-critical-coordinates}
in a slightly enlarged guarded region. If a critical point lies near
the support, use a source cutoff in its Morse chart equal to one on
a smaller ball. If the original source vanishes near that point its
two coefficients are zero. The remaining source is supported where
$|\nabla L_m|\ge H^{-1}$. Its vector field
\[
 V=\frac{\nabla L_m}{|\nabla L_m|^2}
\]
has the derivative bounds required for two integrations by parts.
For this part, writing $b_D$ for its zero-extended source density,
\begin{align*}
 D_t(L_m)_*(b_D\,dx)&=(L_m)_*(\operatorname{div}(b_DV)\,dx),\\
 D_t^2(L_m)_*(b_D\,dx)
  &=(L_m)_*(\operatorname{div}(V\operatorname{div}(b_DV))\,dx).
\end{align*}
The signs are positive: for a scalar test $\phi$, the distributional
first derivative is $-\int b_D\phi'(L_m)$, and source integration
by parts gives the first line. The second follows by iteration.
There are no boundary terms because of the zero extensions and the
cutoffs. The submersion coarea formula makes each right-hand measure
an $L^1$ roof density, whose norm is bounded by the absolute source
integral. These integrals and the undifferentiated mass are at most
a fixed power of $H$.

For each critical part apply Lemma~\ref{lem:v42-two-jet}. Its two-jet
edge is explicit and its residual has the same kind of bound. Sum the
identities over all source pieces, grouping by $\ell$. The factor
$C_1^{L+1}$ and each fixed power of $H$ are absorbed by increasing
$C$ in \eqref{eq:v42-summed-derivative-budget}. Equal roof values
require only addition of their jets, not division by their distance.
This proves the exact decomposition and the summed norm estimate.
The real-variable cutoffs used to construct a local Morse contribution
change none of its two jets.

Finally fix a regular critical word of length $m$ with the stated
guard margins. These include unselected candidate discriminants and
auxiliary angular or section-coordinate factors, not only the actual
first-hit discriminant. Every factor along this finite orbit is thus
a positive distance from its zero set.
For all small enough $\varepsilon$, each guard through time $m$ is
identically one on a neighborhood of the critical point. No condition
is imposed after time $m$. The local density is then the physical
constant $(4\pi c)^{-1}$, independent of $L\ge m$ and of smaller
$\varepsilon$. Formula \eqref{eq:v42-edge-jets} and
\eqref{eq:v42-critical-hessian} give
\eqref{eq:v42-physical-critical-jump} and stabilization of both jets.
At a true boundary or at a redundant guard zero the hypothesis fails,
and no such stabilization is asserted. Any contribution excluded there
remains in the exact nonnegative source $r$; it is not discarded from
the raw law.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Pointwise inversion of the extracted packet}
Write $q_\ell(t)$ for the residual in
\eqref{eq:v42-exact-density-packet}, and define, for $B\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-pointwise-packet-inverse}
 p^{L,\varepsilon,B}_{n,R}(\ell,t)
    =e_{n,R}^{L,\varepsilon}(\ell,t)
          +\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-B}^{B}e^{-ibt}\widehat q_\ell(b)\,db.
\end{equation}
Every rational edge is evaluated explicitly before the residual inverse.
For a jump value we choose the right trace, equivalently
$\one_{s\ge0}=1$ at $s=0$ in \eqref{eq:v42-rational-edge}.

\begin{corollary}[An explicit pointwise error and its remaining source]
\label{cor:v42-pointwise-packet}
The extracted positive source $Q_{n,R}^{L,\varepsilon}$ has the
right-trace density $e+q$, and, at every $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-pointwise-tail}
 |(e+q)(\ell,t)-p^{L,\varepsilon,B}_{n,R}(\ell,t)|
              \le\frac{\mathcal A_{L,\varepsilon}}{\pi B}.
\end{equation}
For the original raw density, at almost every $(\ell,t)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v42-raw-error-with-source}
 |p_{n,R}(\ell,t)-p^{L,\varepsilon,B}_{n,R}(\ell,t)|
      \le\frac{\mathcal A_{L,\varepsilon}}{\pi B}
                         +r_{n,R}^{L,\varepsilon}(\ell,t).
\end{equation}
For every $\lambda>0$, the counting--Lebesgue measure of the output set
where the left-hand error exceeds
$\mathcal A_{L,\varepsilon}/(\pi B)+\lambda$ is at most
$\delta_{n,L,\varepsilon}/\lambda$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Distributional integration by parts gives
$|\widehat q_\ell(b)|\le |b|^{-2}\|q_\ell''\|_1$ for $b\ne0$.
Together with the $L^1$ bound this makes the Fourier transform
integrable. Its inverse agrees with the continuous $W^{2,1}$
representative. The integral of $b^{-2}$ over $|b|>B$ is $2/B$,
which proves \eqref{eq:v42-pointwise-tail}. The exact density
identity gives \eqref{eq:v42-raw-error-with-source}. Markov's
inequality for the nonnegative output density $r$, with
\eqref{eq:v42-removed-mass}, gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The corollary is a pointwise reconstruction theorem for each regular
cutoff packet, not a density local limit for the full uncut law. In
particular small total mass of $r$ does not imply a pointwise bound
for $r$. Nor may the derivative budget
$\exp\{C(L+1)\log(C/\varepsilon)\}$ be replaced by a polynomial in
$n$ when $L$ is of order $n$. The remaining singular/decision boundary
source and its high-frequency contribution are precisely the terms
which must be controlled in a uniform raw local theorem. The normal
critical words themselves now have compatible two-jet coefficients
and a summed finite-cutoff residual estimate on the actual section.
