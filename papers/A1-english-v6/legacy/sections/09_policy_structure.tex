\section{Endpoint policies, explicit boundaries, and lookup complexity}\label{sec:policy}
\subsection{A homogeneous continuation functional}
For a nonnegative finite parameter measure $\zeta$ and $b$ remaining
cartridges, define
\[
 \mathcal W_b(\zeta)=\sup_\Pi\int L_\Pi(R)\,\zeta(dR),
 \qquad 0\le L_\Pi(R)\le M_b:=G_+e^{b|\lambda|B}.
\]
Here $\Pi$ ranges over the common admissible remaining policies and terminal
decisions, and $L_\Pi$ is their conditional expected multiplicative payoff.
The policy class is specified before choosing $\zeta$. Thus $\mathcal W_b$
is convex and positively homogeneous, equals zero at zero, and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 |\mathcal W_b(\zeta)-\mathcal W_b(\widetilde\zeta)|
 \le M_b\|\zeta-\widetilde\zeta\|_{\rm var}. \label{eq:homogeneous-Lipschitz}
\end{equation}
Indeed every fixed-policy integral has these bounds, and taking suprema
preserves the difference bound. At positive mass,
$\mathcal W_b(\zeta)=\zeta(I)V_b(\zeta/\zeta(I))$. This formula replaces a
normalized posterior precisely where its evidence might vanish.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal Borel endpoint-gate controllers]\label{thm:endpoint}
Under the reward and terminal-payoff hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:control},
an optimal finite-budget Borel feedback controller can be chosen with
$g(x)\in\{\eta,1-\eta\}$ everywhere. For $\eta=0$ its comparator is a
deterministic accept/reject decision. This conclusion does not add a
nonlinear cost depending on the gate value itself.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a fixed posterior, mode, and remaining budget, the command objective is
\[
 Q(g)=\int g(x)e^{\lambda r_a(x)}
               \mathcal W_{b-1}(k_R^a(x)\pi(dR))\,\nu(dx)
       +e^{\lambda r_a^\dagger}\mathcal W_{b-1}(f_R^{a,g}\pi(dR)).
\]
The first term is linear in $g$. The failure measure is affine in $g$, so
the second is convex. Theorems~\ref{thm:control} and \ref{thm:boundary}
prove weak-star continuity, including the null-failure boundary. Let $g^*$
be optimal for this fixed mode and put $h=(g^*-\eta)/(1-2\eta)$.
For $0\le t<1$ define $g_t=\eta+(1-2\eta)\one_{\{h>t\}}$.
The layer-cake identity gives $g^*=\int_0^1g_tdt$ weak-star. Convexity
implies $Q(g^*)\le\int_0^1Q(g_t)dt$. One can also see this without an
abstract integral Jensen theorem by applying the supremum-of-linear
representation of $\mathcal W$ to each term and interchanging bounded
integrals. Every $Q(g_t)$ is at most $Q(g^*)$, so equality holds almost
everywhere in $t$.

If $t_j\downarrow t$, dominated convergence of $\one_{\{h>t_j\}}$ against
every $L^1$ test gives $g_{t_j}\to g_t$ weak-star. Thus $Q(g_t)$ is
right-continuous. A value strictly below the maximum at any $t<1$ would
remain below it on a right interval of positive length, contradicting the
almost-everywhere equality. Every such threshold is therefore optimal.
In particular $t=1/2$ gives
$\eta+(1-2\eta)\one_{\{g^*>1/2\}}$.

The original compact selection theorem gives an optimal mode and a jointly
Borel representative $g^*(c,x)$. Thresholding this representative is jointly
Borel, respects the bounds on every point, and retains its fixed-mode
optimum. Backward induction gives an endpoint feedback controller at every
budget. Its pasting uses exactly the original prepared cartridges.
\end{proof}

The endpoint argument was proposed in Section 7 of the current referee
report and is included here with attribution, not as a claim of priority.
It does not give a finite boundary count at every budget. The next result
supplies an explicit boundary theorem for the last cartridge.

\begin{theorem}[Exact last-cartridge formula and at most two hit arcs]\label{thm:two-arcs}
Fix a posterior $\pi$, a mode $a$, and one remaining cartridge. Suppose the
accepted reward is constant on each raw tag $\partial,0,1$ (it may depend on
the mode); retain the compact decision space and continuous positive $G$.
Set
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A_d(x)&=e^{\lambda r_a(x)}\int G(R,d)k_R^a(x)\pi(dR),
 &A(x)&=\max_d A_d(x),\\
 C_d(x)&=e^{\lambda r_a^\dagger}\int G(R,d)k_R^a(x)\pi(dR).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Then the exact fixed-mode optimum is
\begin{equation}
 V_1(\pi,a)=\max_{d_\dagger\in D_0}\int_X
 \left[\eta\{A(x)+C_{d_\dagger}(x)\}
 +(1-2\eta)\max\{A(x),C_{d_\dagger}(x)\}\right]\nu(dx).
 \label{eq:last-step-explicit}
\end{equation}
For a maximizing $d_\dagger$, accept at level $1-\eta$ exactly where
$A>C_{d_\dagger}$ and at level $\eta$ otherwise. An accepted terminal
decision maximizes $A_d(x)$, while the censoring decision is $d_\dagger$.
The collision-component high-acceptance set can be chosen as the union of
at most two connected arcs of the circle. The no-collision gate is constant
in its angle, and the placement atom requires one binary choice.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed censoring decision, an accepted terminal decision can be
optimized separately at each $x$. Compactness and continuity give the
maximum and a Borel selector, by the same compact selection argument used
in Section~\ref{sec:control}. The total objective is
$\int\{gA+(1-g)C_{d_\dagger}\}d\nu$. Maximizing a linear function of
$g(x)\in[\eta,1-\eta]$ gives the integrand in
\eqref{eq:last-step-explicit} and the asserted gate. Maximizing over the
compact $D_0$ is legitimate because this integrated function is continuous
in $d_\dagger$. This proves the formula and its attainment.

On a collision tag, put $z=\cos(y-\theta)$. For every $d$,
\[
 A_d(1,y)=\frac{2T e^{\lambda r_a(1)}}{a_0}
       \left\{\int R G(R,d)\pi(dR)
                  +\epsilon z\int R^3G(R,d)\pi(dR)\right\}.
\]
This is affine in $z$, as is $C_{d_\dagger}(1,y)$. Consequently
$A(1,y)-C_{d_\dagger}(1,y)$, viewed as a function of $z$, is a supremum of
affine functions minus an affine function, hence continuous and convex on
$[-1,1]$. Its nonpositive set is an interval, possibly empty or the entire
interval. Its positive set is therefore at most two end intervals. The
inverse image of each end interval under the cosine map is a connected arc
of the circle, with empty and whole-circle cases allowed. Assigning equality
to the lower acceptance level gives the stated at-most-two-arc gate. On the
no-collision component all these functions are independent of $y$; the
placement component is an atom. The argument does not require a finite
terminal-decision space or differentiability of the convex envelope.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A quantitative finite endpoint lookup}
Assume, in addition, that on either circle component, uniformly in $R,a$,
\[
 |k_R^a(y)-k_R^a(z)|\le L_k d_{\mathbb S^1}(y,z),\qquad
 |r_a(y)-r_a(z)|\le L_r d_{\mathbb S^1}(y,z),\qquad k_R^a\le K.
\]
The explicit Lorentz kernel permits $L_k=2T_+Eu^3/a_0$; the no-collision
angular constant is zero. The extra reward regularity gives a rate; the
original continuity-only approximation theorem remains unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Finite endpoint lookup with an explicit value error]\label{thm:lookup-rate}
Divide each circle into $J$ equal arcs, leave the placement atom intact,
and restrict the gate on each cell to an endpoint. Keep the original compact
mode set and terminal decisions. Let $V_N^{(J)}$ be the optimum in this
restricted feedback class. With $c=|\lambda|B$ and $h=2\pi/J$,
\begin{equation}
 0\le V_N-V_N^{(J)}
 \le 2N G_+e^{Nc}(2L_k+K|\lambda|L_r)h. \label{eq:lookup-rate}
\end{equation}
This bound is uniform over the prior and reachable state. Each commanded
gate uses $2J+1$ endpoint choices. It does not assert that global policy
optimization takes polynomial time or that the posterior arithmetic uses
only a finite number of bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At $b$ remaining steps write the accepted integrand as
$w(x)=e^{\lambda r_a(x)}\mathcal W_{b-1}(k_R^a(x)\pi(dR))$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:homogeneous-Lipschitz} and the exponential mean-value
bound give the uniform angular Lipschitz constant
\[
 L_w\le e^c M_{b-1}(L_k+K|\lambda|L_r).
\]
For any gate let $g_J$ be its cell average. Conditional averaging is
self-adjoint under each uniform circle measure, so
$\int(g-g_J)w=\int g(w-E_Jw)$. The two circle components have total
reference mass two and the atom is unchanged. Hence the absolute accepted
error is at most $2L_wh$.

For the failure parameter measures the same cancellation, now applied to
$k_R^a$ at each $R$, gives
\[
 \|f_R^{a,g}\pi(dR)-f_R^{a,g_J}\pi(dR)\|_{\rm var}
 \le 2L_kh.
\]
Their continuation error is at most $2e^cM_{b-1}L_kh$ by
\eqref{eq:homogeneous-Lipschitz}. This remains true when one failure
measure is zero and does not assume a continuous null-branch posterior.
The combined one-step error is at most
$e_b=2G_+e^{bc}(2L_k+K|\lambda|L_r)h$.

For a fixed mode the objective is convex in the finitely many values of
$g_J$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint}'s homogeneous argument. Every point
of this finite cube is a convex combination of its vertices. At least one
vertex therefore has objective no smaller than that of $g_J$. Thus an
endpoint lookup gate loses at most $e_b$ against the ideal one-step choice.
The restricted finite-gate optimization with compact modes is attained and
has a Borel selector. Comparing the two Bellman recursions gives
$E_b\le e_b+e^cE_{b-1}$, $E_0=0$, since their predictive probabilities sum
to one. Summation gives \eqref{eq:lookup-rate}.
\end{proof}

For completeness a finite mode net also has a rate under explicit mode
moduli. Suppose $\int|k_R^a-k_R^{a'}|d\nu\le L_A d(a,a')$ uniformly in $R$
and all accepted and censored rewards differ by at most
$L_{r,A}d(a,a')$. Replacing the mode by a point of a $\rho$-net adds at most
\[
 N G_+e^{Nc}(2L_A+|\lambda|L_{r,A})\rho
\]
to \eqref{eq:lookup-rate}. To verify the one-step bound, apply
\eqref{eq:homogeneous-Lipschitz} to the accepted and failure measures;
their separate changes are each at most $L_A\rho$. For the reward change,
the accepted and failure predictive masses together are one, so the
exponential mean-value bound contributes only
$e^cM_{b-1}|\lambda|L_{r,A}\rho$. The same backward sum proves the formula.
The resulting menu contains at most $2^{2J+1}$ gates per mode. This explicit
exponential menu size is not hidden inside the filter's linear storage count.
