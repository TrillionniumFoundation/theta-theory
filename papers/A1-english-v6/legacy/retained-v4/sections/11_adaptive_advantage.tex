\section{A certified strict adaptive advantage in the full cubic laboratory}\label{sec:advantage}
The comparison in this section is between two specified policy classes on
the \emph{same} counted experiment. An adaptive controller may choose the
second command from the first observed report. A nonadaptive controller
chooses both commands before any report, possibly with a shared random
seed independent of the radius and cartridges. Both classes may make their
terminal decision from the entire observed history and known commands.
Both use arbitrary Borel gates in $[0,1]$, and both consume two cartridges.

Put $t=(R-l)/(u-l)$ and specify
\begin{equation}
 N=2,\quad T=1/20,\quad\lambda=1,\quad
 r_a(x)=\log(99/100)\ (x\in X),\quad r_a^\dagger=0,
 \label{eq:advantage-reward}
\end{equation}
\[
 D_0=\{-,+\},\qquad G(R,-)=2-t,\qquad G(R,+)=1+t.
\]
Thus an accepted report carries a small multiplicative cost, independent
of its tag, angle, and commanded mode; censoring retains information but
pays no acceptance cost. The terminal payoff is continuous, positive, and
nonconstant in the unknown radius. This is an instance of the original
observable-reward problem, not a hidden reward on a rejected coordinate.
Write $s=99/100$ and $\Omega=2-s^2=10199/10000$; every realized payoff lies
in $[s^2,2]$.

\begin{theorem}[Strict full-class adaptive separation]\label{thm:adaptive-advantage}
For \eqref{eq:advantage-reward}, the mode family
\[
 \cA=\{1/20\}\times[0,1/20]\times\mathbb S^1
\]
and the full-support prior
\begin{equation}
 \pi_\alpha=(1-\alpha)\left(\tfrac25\delta_l+\tfrac35\delta_u\right)
                 +\alpha\,\mathrm{Unif}(I),\qquad \alpha=1/10000,
 \label{eq:advantage-prior}
\end{equation}
the adaptive optimum exceeds the nonadaptive optimum by more than
$11/10000$. The nonadaptive comparison includes every precommitted pair
of Borel gates and modes, as well as their independent randomizations.
The mode family contains nonzero cubic readouts and the prior has full
support, so the same instance has the sharp $3n$ exact-state lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first solve a reference problem exactly, then transfer its strict gap
uniformly to the full cubic family and full-support prior.

\paragraph{1. Exact reduction of the entire nominal gate class.}
Use $\pi_*=(2/5)\delta_l+(3/5)\delta_u$ and $\epsilon=0$ for the moment.
The raw probabilities at $R_i\in\{l,u\}$ are
\[
 p_i=\left(\frac{\pi R_i^2}{a_0},
       \frac{a_0-\pi R_i^2-2TR_i}{a_0},\frac{2TR_i}{a_0}\right),
\]
with coordinates ordered as placement failure, no collision, and collision.
Conditional on either circular tag, the mark is uniform and independent
of the radius. For any Borel gate, let its three acceptance fractions be
$\beta=(g(\partial),\int g(0,y)dy/(2\pi),\int g(1,y)dy/(2\pi))$.
An accepted tag has likelihood $\beta_j p_{i,j}$; its conditional angle
has density proportional to $g(j,y)$, independent of $R_i$. Such an angle
can only randomize a subsequent command without changing its posterior.
Conditional averaging of that randomization cannot exceed the best command
for the same tag posterior. The censoring likelihood is
$\sum_j(1-\beta_j)p_{i,j}$. Thus the full nominal adaptive problem reduces
exactly to $\beta\in[0,1]^3$, not merely approximately to selected gates.

At the last step its objective is convex in $\beta$ by the homogeneous
continuation argument, so a vertex is optimal. At the first step the same
argument with the optimized continuation applies. For a precommitted pair,
the terminal Bayes payoff is a sum of maxima of linear functions of either
acceptance vector with the other held fixed; hence it is separately convex
on the two cubes. Replacing first one vector and then the other by a vertex
cannot reduce its value. Shared independent randomization averages values
of such deterministic pairs and cannot improve the supremum. Therefore all
nonadaptive policies are covered by the $8^2=64$ vertex pairs, and all
adaptive values by eight first gates with eight possible continuations for
each report. The absence of useful nominal angle information is essential
to this finite reduction; we do not apply it directly to nonzero amplitudes.

\paragraph{2. Finite exact formulas and the optimizing policy.}
For an unnormalized two-point parameter measure $z=(z_l,z_u)$ define
\[
 Z(z)=\max\{2z_l+z_u,z_l+2z_u\}.
\]
Let $\mathcal B=\{0,1\}^3$, and write coordinatewise multiplication as
$z\odot p_j$. For $g\in\mathcal B$, put
\[
 f_g=\sum_{j:g_j=0}p_j,\qquad
 B_g(z)=s\sum_{j:g_j=1}Z(z\odot p_j)+Z(z\odot f_g),
 \qquad B_*(z)=\max_{g\in\mathcal B}B_g(z).
\]
Zero-mass branches contribute zero. With $z_*=(2/5,3/5)$,
\begin{align}
 V_{\rm na,*}&=\max_{g,h\in\mathcal B}
 \left\{s\sum_{j:g_j=1}B_h(z_*\odot p_j)+B_h(z_*\odot f_g)\right\},
 \label{eq:nonadaptive-enumeration}\\
 V_{\rm ad,*}&=\max_{g\in\mathcal B}
 \left\{s\sum_{j:g_j=1}B_*(z_*\odot p_j)+B_*(z_*\odot f_g)\right\}.
 \label{eq:adaptive-enumeration}
\end{align}
These are finite expressions in rational numbers, $\pi$, and $\sqrt3$.
Their exact outward interval evaluation gives
\begin{align*}
 1.600432377967011&<V_{\rm na,*}<1.600432377967012,\\
 1.602586453549804&<V_{\rm ad,*}<1.602586453549805,\\
 0.002154075582792&<\Delta_*:=V_{\rm ad,*}-V_{\rm na,*}
                                      <0.002154075582793.
\end{align*}
The nonadaptive optimum uses $A=(0,1,0)$ twice: accept precisely no-collision
reports. The adaptive optimum first uses $A$; after an accepted no-collision
report it uses $A$ again, but after censoring it uses $O=(0,0,0)$.
The latter still consumes the second cartridge and reports censoring.
The final decision is minus exactly after two accepted no-collision reports,
and plus on the other positive-probability histories of these policies.

Here is both the certificate method and a complete upper-bound table for
the nonadaptive maximization. Use
$\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)$, the alternating power series with
40 and 10 terms respectively, and the signed next-term remainder. The
identity follows from the tangent addition formula and the angle range
$4\arctan(1/5)-\arctan(1/239)\in(0,\pi/2)$, whose tangent is one.
For $\sqrt3$ take the adjacent rationals of denominator $10^{40}$ obtained
by the integer square root of $3\cdot10^{80}$; their squared inequalities
are checked as integers. Outward rounding gives $\pi_-<\pi<\pi_+$ and $s_-<\sqrt3<s_+$ with
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \pi_-&=3.1415926535897932384626433832795028841971,\\
 \pi_+&=3.1415926535897932384626433832795028841972,\\
 s_-&=1.7320508075688772935274463415058723669428,\\
 s_+&=1.7320508075688772935274463415058723669429.
 \end{aligned}
\]

The maximizing first-gate comparison is also finite. In the order
$000$, $001$, $010$, $011$, $100$, $101$, $110$, $111$, certified upper bounds for the eight
adaptive first-gate values, on the same $10^{-9}$ scale relative to $8/5$, are
\[
1,\ -859097,\ 2586454,\ 1727357,\ -7730246,\ -8188276,\ -9967940,\ -10827037.
\]
Only the $010$ entry can attain the displayed lower bound for
$V_{\rm ad,*}$. At that first gate, the accepted no-collision branch has
weighted optimal continuation $C_A$, attained by $A$, and the censored
branch has weighted optimal continuation $C_O$, attained by $O$, where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0.261237365546032&<C_A<0.261237365546033,\\
 1.341349088003772&<C_O<1.341349088003773.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The certificate encloses all eight alternatives on each branch and checks
strict inequalities against the displayed choices.

The gap also has a useful closed form. Write $q_i=p_{i,0}$, where the
subscript $0$ now denotes the no-collision tag, and put
$z_l=2/5,z_u=3/5$. The posterior sign is minus only on two no-collision
reports: the four differences
$z_l q_l^j(1-q_l)^{2-j}-z_u q_u^j(1-q_u)^{2-j}$, with the mixed report
orders kept separately, have respectively the signs $-, -, -, +$ for
$00,01,10,11$, where 1 denotes no collision. Direct branch summation gives
\begin{align}
 V_{\rm na,*}
 &=z_l[1-(1-s)q_l]^2+2z_u[1-(1-s)q_u]^2
                  +s^2(z_lq_l^2-z_uq_u^2),\notag\\
 \Delta_*&=(1-s)\{z_lq_l(1-q_l)+2z_uq_u(1-q_u)\}>0.
 \label{eq:adaptive-closed-gap}
\end{align}
Thus adaptivity saves a positive acceptance cost specifically on the branch
where the second no-collision observation could not change the optimal
terminal decision. It does not obtain an uncharged physical reset.

\paragraph{3. Transfer to the full cubic comparator class and full support.}
Compare every mode $(1/20,\epsilon,\theta)$, $0\le\epsilon\le E=1/20$,
with its zero-amplitude version. Their one-step raw distance is
$(2TR/a_0)\epsilon R^2/\pi$, uniformly bounded by
\[
 e_0=\frac{2Tu\,Eu^2}{a_0\pi}
       <0.000190802066365,
 \qquad e_2=1-(1-e_0)^2<0.000381567727301.
\]
At zero amplitude the extra mode labels affect neither the observation
kernel nor its reward, so the just-solved nominal problem already optimizes
over those labels and all Borel gates. Common-policy model stability applies
to each of the full adaptive and nonadaptive classes, even though nonzero
amplitudes make the accepted angle informative. The two class values can
each move by at most $\Omega e_2$. Independently, replacing $\pi_*$ by
\eqref{eq:advantage-prior} changes each class value by at most $\Omega\alpha$,
since the priors have total variation at most $\alpha$ and the complete
policy payoff has oscillation at most $\Omega$. Consequently
\[
 V_{\rm ad}(\pi_\alpha)-V_{\rm na}(\pi_\alpha)
 \ge\Delta_*-2\Omega(e_2+\alpha)
 >0.001171773732645>\frac{11}{10000}.
\]
The last inequality uses the same exact interval arithmetic as the nominal
comparison and is recorded in the certificate. Since $E>0$, the mode class
contains the full-rank cubic mode of Theorem~\ref{thm:dimension}; since
$\alpha>0$, its prior has full support. This proves the claimed strict
advantage in the full cubic laboratory, not only in the finite reference
problem.
\end{proof}

The certificate's finite enumeration is logically downstream of the
all-Borel reduction and the uniform transfer theorem. Sampling a finite
menu in the nonzero-amplitude model would not prove this comparison.
Nor is a first-ever benefit of adaptive sensing claimed: the contribution
here is the explicit policy mechanism and quantitative separation within
the same mechanically derived, full-rank, failure-retaining experiment.
