\section{Attainable rank, intrinsic dimension, and perturbation margins}\label{sec:structural}
The algebraic representation, the rank of its physically available factors,
and a mechanical realization are three different assertions. This section
gives a criterion for the second assertion without choosing a particular
billiard or a cubic firmware. Work in $t\in[0,1]$, fix a mode, and write
\[
 H(t,x)=\sum_{j=0}^q h_j(x)t^j,\qquad
 h(x)=(h_0(x),\ldots,h_q(x))^\top.
\]
Assume $0<\nu(X)<\infty$, $h\in L^\infty(\nu;\R^{q+1})$,
$H\ge h_*>0$, and $\int H(t,x)\nu(dx)=D(t)>0$. The normalized density is
$H/D$. The common-gate comparator is the one in
Theorem~\ref{thm:attainable}; it is not a parameter-dependent coefficient
oracle. Fix $0\le\eta<1/2$, put $s_0=1/2-\eta$, and define
\[
 \Gamma=\int h(x)h(x)^\top\nu(dx),\qquad
 H_0=\mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_x|h(x)|_2,\qquad
 f(g)=\int(1-g(x))h(x)\nu(dx).
\]
The vector $f(g)$ contains the power coefficients of the failure numerator.

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative attainable-rank criterion]\label{thm:rank-criterion}
The following are equivalent: the $q+1$ coefficient functions are linearly
independent modulo $\nu$-null sets; $\Gamma$ is positive definite; and the
attainable set $\{f(g):\eta\le g\le1-\eta\}$ has nonempty interior in
$\R^{q+1}$. If $\gamma=\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma)>0$, that set contains the
open ball centered at $f(1/2)$ of radius $s_0\gamma/H_0$. Every increment
$z$ in this ball has the explicit common-gate realization
\begin{equation}
 g_z(x)=\tfrac12-h(x)^\top\Gamma^{-1}z,\qquad
 f(g_z)=f(1/2)+z. \label{eq:rank-right-inverse}
\end{equation}
Suppose a second degree-$q$ apparatus has coefficient vector $\widetilde h$
with $\|\widetilde h-h\|_{L^\infty(\ell^2)}\le\xi$, the same normalization
$D$, and the same comparator. If
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt{q+1}\xi<h_*,\qquad
 \Delta_\Gamma:=\nu(X)(2H_0\xi+\xi^2)<\gamma, \label{eq:rank-margin}
\end{equation}
then it is positive and full rank, and its attainable set contains a ball
of radius at least $s_0(\gamma-\Delta_\Gamma)/(H_0+\xi)$ around its own
center. These are sufficient, directly checkable perturbation margins.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $v\in\R^{q+1}$, $v^\top\Gamma v=\int(v\cdot h)^2d\nu$.
It vanishes exactly when $v\cdot h=0$ almost everywhere. Such a nonzero
$v$ annihilates every $f(g)$, confining the attainable set to a proper
linear subspace and ruling out interior. Conversely, if $\Gamma>0$, then
$|h(x)^\top\Gamma^{-1}z|\le H_0|z|/\gamma<s_0$. Thus
\eqref{eq:rank-right-inverse} is a permissible Borel representative, chosen
arbitrarily on a common null set. Integrating it gives
$f(g_z)=\frac12\int h+\Gamma\Gamma^{-1}z$, proving the ball and all
three equivalences. These gates depend on the known coefficient functions
of the apparatus and on the command $z$, never on the unknown $t$.

For the perturbation, $|(\widetilde h-h)\cdot(1,t,\ldots,t^q)|
\le\sqrt{q+1}\xi$, so $\widetilde H>0$. Expanding
$\widetilde h\widetilde h^\top-hh^\top$ and integrating bounds its operator
norm by $\Delta_\Gamma$. For every unit vector $v$,
$v^\top\widetilde\Gamma v\ge\gamma-\Delta_\Gamma>0$, while
$\|\widetilde h\|_\infty\le H_0+\xi$. Apply the ball construction to
$\widetilde h$. The normalization assumption ensures these polynomials
remain an experiment, rather than merely a set of positive functions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp intrinsic dimension for full-rank factors]\label{thm:q-dimension}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:rank-criterion} with $\Gamma>0$ and $q\ge1$, the
normalized likelihoods after $n$ censoring reports contain an open subset
of the normalized degree-$qn$ polynomial family, of dimension $qn$.
The likelihood is $P(t)/D(t)^n$; $n$ and the known denominator are retained.
Any exact Euclidean state continuously encoding the known command history
needs at least $qn$ coordinates at that budget. Normalized power
coefficients attain this intrinsic dimension. The same lower bound holds
for posterior-determining states under a fixed full-support prior.

A positive Bernstein implementation may require degree elevation from $q$
to $m>q$. Its $mn+1$ stored numerator coefficients are then redundant:
$mn$ is not asserted to be a sharp intrinsic lower bound. In the Lorentz
model the explicit choice $q=m=3$ realizes the sharp count without this
redundancy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The ball just constructed supplies an open family of positive failure
polynomials. It contains $n$ polynomials $F_i$ of exact degree $q$ which
are pairwise coprime. To justify this choice, the leading-coefficient-zero
condition and each pairwise resultant-zero condition are proper polynomial
zero sets in the coefficients. Their finite union has empty interior:
a nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on a ball, as repeated univariate
restriction shows. The resultant condition is proper, for example because
$t^q+1$ and $t^q+2$ have no common root. Thus a generic tuple in the ball
avoids all these conditions.

The differential of multiplication at this tuple sends $(\delta F_i)$ to
$\sum_i\delta F_i\prod_{j\ne i}F_j$. Reduction modulo $F_i$ shows, using
coprimality, that a zero differential requires $F_i\mid\delta F_i$.
The degree bound makes $\delta F_i=\lambda_i F_i$ and the remaining
condition is $\sum_i\lambda_i=0$. The kernel dimension is $n-1$, so the
rank is $n(q+1)-(n-1)=qn+1$. Normalize the product by its positive value
at one fixed $t_0\in[0,1]$. The normalization removes exactly one dimension.
The submersion theorem supplies an open normalized image and a continuous
local section back to gate histories via \eqref{eq:rank-right-inverse}.
All these finite all-failure records have positive probability, since the
gates used can be kept in a strict interior subinterval of $[0,1]$.

An exact continuous state composed with the local section is injective:
equal states determine equal normalized likelihoods. Padding an alleged
injection into fewer than $qn$ Euclidean coordinates with zeros contradicts
invariance of domain on an open subset of $\R^{qn}$. Normalized power
coefficients give a matching representation, though not necessarily a
positive arithmetic implementation. Equality of posteriors under a
full-support prior identifies their continuous positive likelihoods up to
a scalar, so the same argument applies. Division by the fixed positive
$D^n$ changes none of these injectivity statements. Degree elevation is an
injective linear re-encoding into a larger polynomial coefficient array,
not an increase of the underlying likelihood dimension.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Which model constraints supply full rank?}
For the Lorentz numerator written in powers of $R$, a linear annihilator
of the four coefficient functions must first annihilate the cosine on the
collision component. When $T\epsilon>0$ this forces its cubic component
to vanish. The placement atom then forces its quadratic component to
vanish, the collision constant forces its linear component to vanish,
and the no-collision constant forces its constant component to vanish.
Thus $\Gamma>0$. The affine change $R=l+(u-l)t$ is invertible on coefficient
space, so full rank also holds in the $t$ coordinates. At $\epsilon=0$
the cubic coefficient vanishes and the remaining three functions are
independent. The experiment is then full rank as a degree-two experiment,
not as a degree-three one. This is a model constraint on the available
failure factors, rather than a conclusion from their formal maximum degree.

More generally, the affine dimension of a moment body
$\{\int g Fd\nu:\eta\le g\le1-\eta\}$ equals the rank of
$\int FF^\top d\nu$. Apply the preceding annihilator argument on its range,
where its Gram matrix is invertible, to obtain relative interior. In
particular the five-coordinate body in Section~\ref{sec:control} need not
have dimension five. For $\lambda=0$ and $G=1$, continuation values equal
one and $w=\bar k$ belongs to the span of the four components of $v_a$.
Every body point then satisfies $u=A\cdot m$ for a fixed vector $A$,
so the intrinsic dimension is at most four (and is four for the
nondegenerate four-moment mode). This explains the current report's M2
without altering the sharp likelihood-state theorem.

The perturbation theorem above concerns \emph{same-degree} apparatus errors.
It does not claim that a smooth nonpolynomial perturbation remains cubic.
Section~\ref{sec:robust} treats precisely that larger physical class by a
normalized implementable approximation and a complete-policy error bound.
