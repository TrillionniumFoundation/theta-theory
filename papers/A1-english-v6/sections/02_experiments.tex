\section{Calibrated experiments and their operational quotient}\label{sec:experiment}
Let $k_1,\ldots,k_K\in W_A$ be strictly positive on $I$, satisfy
$\sum_jk_j=1$, and span $W_A$. On each trial a fresh raw symbol has these
probabilities conditional on the same fixed parameter $t$. The raw trials
are conditionally independent. A command
$g\in\cG=[\eta,1-\eta]^K$ accepts raw symbol $j$ with probability $g_j$,
using an independent prepared uniform variable. A rejected symbol is
reported as $\dagger$. Thus the report alphabet is
$\cX=\{1,\ldots,K,\dagger\}$ and its likelihood factors are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:channel}
 f_{g,j}=g_jk_j,\qquad
 f_{g,\dagger}=F_g=\sum_j(1-g_j)k_j.
\end{equation}
A single command is applied at every possible parameter value. Every
trial is counted, including every rejected report. There is a constant
$\kappa>0$ such that $\kappa\le f_{g,x}(t)\le1$ for all $g,x,t$.
Indeed one may take $\kappa=\eta\min_{j,t}k_j(t)$.

\begin{lemma}[Attainable interior and realizability]\label{lem:interior}
The failure family is precisely the image of the rejection cube under
$u\mapsto\sum_ju_jk_j$. It contains a neighborhood of the constant $1/2$
in $W_A$. Conversely, every finite exponent set $A$ as above has a positive
normalized $r$-cell realization with this property.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The displayed map is a surjective linear map onto $W_A$. Choose a linear
right inverse $L:W_A\to\R^K$. For every $v$ satisfying
$\norm{Lv}_\infty<1/2-\eta$, rejection vector
$u=\one/2+Lv$ realizes $1/2+v$ inside the cube. This proves both the
interior statement and that the same physical command implements it.
For existence, choose $\delta>0$ with
$\delta\sum_{a\in A\setminus\{0\}}u^a<1/r$ and set
\[
 k_a(t)=1/r+\delta t^a\quad(a\ne0),\qquad
 k_0(t)=1/r-\delta\sum_{a\ne0}t^a.
\]
They are positive and sum to one. Their span contains one and then each
$t^a=(k_a-1/r)/\delta$, so has dimension $r$. These are calibrated
categorical experiments. No mechanical origin is asserted for this general
realization; the separate Lorentz realization is derived in
Section~\ref{sec:common-risk}.
\end{proof}

A history $h=(g_1,x_1,\ldots,g_n,x_n)$ has posterior
\begin{equation}\label{eq:posterior}
 P_h(t)\mu(\dd t),\qquad
 P_h=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^nf_{g_i,x_i}}{\mu(\prod_{i=1}^nf_{g_i,x_i})}.
\end{equation}
All commands are known inputs when received. They cease to be available
once discarded by a memory-limited filter. A continuous history encoding
means continuity in $(g_1,\ldots,g_n)$ separately for each fixed report
word. It need not be injective on histories and need not identify all
histories with the same posterior.

\begin{definition}[Common future-only continuation]\label{def:future}
At a checkpoint a continuation has the same fixed task data in the two
experiments. Its commands are functions only of the reports and commands
\emph{since that checkpoint}, and any fresh independent random seed. Its
first command is the same in both experiments. It may not read the
original prefix, a prefix-dependent seed, or any other uncharged historical
tape. Two posteriors are $m$-equivalent when all such length-$m$
continuations have the same future report law.
\end{definition}

A separate payoff automaton referring to past observations is task memory,
not something removed by posterior equivalence. Losses that inspect $t$
directly are also not included without adding their test functions. This
quantifier matters: two different constant failure commands leave the
same prior, but a rule that reads and repeats the original command does
not meet Definition~\ref{def:future}.

\begin{proposition}[Exact future test space]\label{prop:tests}
The linear span of length-$m$ future likelihood tests is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:future-space}
 W_A^m=\spanop\{t^s:s\in mA\}.
\end{equation}
Two posteriors are $m$-equivalent if and only if their moments at the
exponents in $mA\setminus\{0\}$ agree. A finite list of actually
executable, nonadaptive all-failure probes spans the same space.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose finitely many attainable failures spanning $W_A$, using
Lemma~\ref{lem:interior}. All their $m$-fold products are executable
all-failure event probabilities. Multilinearity makes their span the
span of all products of $m$ members of $W_A$, which is exactly
\eqref{eq:future-space}. Independence is conditional on $t$; one does
not replace the shared parameter by fresh parameters between trials.

For any fixed future word a deterministic continuation chooses a known
sequence of commands along that word. Its likelihood is a product of
$m$ members of $W_A$, even when the chosen sequence differs on other
branches. Equality of the stated moments therefore implies equality of
all word probabilities. There are finitely many report words. Integrating
an independent continuation seed proves the randomized case. Conversely,
equivalence includes all the spanning all-failure probes; their predictions
determine all the moments in \eqref{eq:future-space}. The constant moment
is one. Only the selected probe is executed and all its $m$ trials are
consumed, regardless of earlier nonfailure reports.
\end{proof}

A fixed positive nonconstant denominator or an arbitrary nonmonomial
factor space is not silently substituted in \eqref{eq:future-space}.
Such models have their own product and test pairings. The theorem below
addresses every sparse monomial space $W_A$, not every finite-dimensional
space of continuous functions.
