\begin{abstract}
Let a fixed positive finite detector have probability functions spanning
$W_A=\spanop\{t^a:a\in A\}$ on a nondegenerate positive interval, where
$A$ is any finite set of nonnegative real exponents containing zero.
With a full-support prior and a bounded lookup comparator, we prove that
the generic dimension of the attainable quotient after $n$ observations
for $m$ future observations is
$\min\{n(|A|-1),|mA|-1\}$.
The same number is the minimal dimension of a continuous sufficient
encoding of admitted histories. A binomial factor family turns its product
tangent into an explicit monomial space; strict total positivity of mixed
moment matrices proves the required transversality for every such prior.
For integral exponents the future dimension is governed by the Hilbert
function of the observation algebra, not by one-step rank alone.
We construct a causal state attaining the resulting, generally asymmetric,
memory profile. Quantizing this state after every report gives genuinely
finite-state filters and matching fixed-horizon prediction exponents at
the profile's maximum; a separate bound transfers the construction to
bounded additive control tasks. In a counted two-cartridge mechanical
experiment we also determine the value of a collision bit at every finite
state budget by a common-risk partition formula. All error constants retain
their dependence on the horizon and calibration; exponentially rare probes
are not used to assert horizon-uniform memory requirements.
\end{abstract}
