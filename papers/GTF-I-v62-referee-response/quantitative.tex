\begin{abstract}
We study nonuniform stochastic realizations of compact group experiments
with legal convex-valued terminal outputs. A full Koopman action spectral
gap and a uniform orbit-cap estimate force a sharp occupation bound at
all cuts of any fixed width, with arbitrary intervening registers. The
proof uses a mass-weighted law of conditional-centroid directions,
compactly supported entropy smoothing, and transport paid by lost
centroid mass. Orthogonal covariance removes every return-word or idle
command assumption from this quantitative argument. Retaining the error
budget gives matching joint amplitude--accuracy laws whenever the least
unit-orbit dimension equals the target-orbit dimension. In particular,
vanishing pure-state error with subexponential inverse accuracy has
width of order $(N/\varepsilon_N)^{q/2}$. An explicit six-command rational
Bloch experiment has width $\Theta(N/\varepsilon_N)$ in this regime,
with numerical two-parameter bounds, whereas its exact profile is $5^t$
and remains necessary at sufficiently small exponentially shrinking error.
The numerical spectral constant is the classical Lubotzky--Phillips--Sarnak
constant. Sparse rational and dyadic realizations supply constructive
upper bounds. Label width does not charge the clock, real table
descriptions, exact arithmetic, or exact sampling.
\end{abstract}
