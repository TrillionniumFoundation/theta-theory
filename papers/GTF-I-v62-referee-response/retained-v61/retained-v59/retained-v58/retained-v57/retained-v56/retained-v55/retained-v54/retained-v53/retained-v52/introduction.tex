\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction51}
A finite stochastic register can implement a linear numerical response
without representing its physical state explicitly. Its reachable
probability distributions may lie on a proper section of the ambient
simplex, and the observation map may have nontrivial fibers. This
separates positive realization from ordinary rank and from static
nonnegative factorization. The distinction becomes especially sharp
when one permits a different clocked realization at each horizon.
The central question here is whether bounded label width under this
nonuniform convention can conceal an infinite orthogonal action.

We consider a finite alphabet of orthogonal maps on $\R^D$, including
the identity. The experiment starts at a signed coordinate vector,
executes a word, and returns a binary coordinate query with mean
$\rho e_j^TU_wx$, where $0<\rho<1$. The realization must approximate
these numerical probabilities for every seed, word and query; it is
not merely required to recognize a threshold language. Its stochastic
rows are common to all histories with the same current label, epoch
and command. Writing $U_w=U_{a_N}\cdots U_{a_1}$ for $w=a_1\cdots a_N$,
the prescribed law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \Pp(B=b\mid x,w,j)=\frac{1+b\rho e_j^TU_wx}{2},\qquad b\in\{-1,1\}.
\end{equation}
The detailed interface is given in Section~\ref{sec:model}.

The resource convention is material. Before interpreting width as
memory, we distinguish the following quantities.

\subsection{The central classification}
For every fixed $0\le\varepsilon<\rho/(2D)$, we prove
\begin{align*}
 \sup_N W_{N,\varepsilon}<\infty
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |\langle U_a:a\in\calA\rangle|<\infty\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{one exact stationary permutation realization exists}.
\end{align*}
All hypotheses of the numerical interface above are retained. The
result is Theorem~\ref{thm:noisy-finitegroup52}. It applies to arbitrary
hidden widths, arbitrary reachability ranks, and arbitrary
horizon-dependent real tables. Thus bounded approximate nonuniform
realizability forces exact uniform realizability in this error range.

The exact part has a geometric proof. Reachable probability sections
project to physical polytopes with a rank-sensitive vertex bound
(Theorem~\ref{thm:section51}). An invariant finite-vertex body carries
a faithful permutation action, while failure of invariance produces
volume growth across separated narrow cuts
(Theorems~\ref{thm:occupation51} and~\ref{thm:finitegroup51}).
This proof is retained because it yields geometric information that
the probabilistic proof does not replace.

For positive error, we use a different argument. Vectors with finite
orbits form a subspace on which the action is finite. On its orthogonal
complement every nonzero orbit is infinite. Compact orbit quantization
then supplies a finite executable word law with positive distortion
for every fixed endpoint width. Conditional-centroid contraction and
a moving-component calibration give an occupation budget
(Theorem~\ref{thm:noisy-budget52}). This combines the earlier word-profile
method with a finite-orbit reduction; it neither divides by a hidden
state probability nor assumes a well-conditioned observation map.

\subsection{Quantitative and finite consequences}
For any infinite-order rational planar rotation with denominator $c$,
we construct a finite word certificate yielding
$W_{N,\varepsilon}\ge(\log N-O(\log\log N))/(4\log c)$ on its
calibrated error range (Theorem~\ref{thm:height52}). The alphabet may
include noncommuting commands. This is an explicit height bound,
not an estimate whose constant requires minimizing over all polytopes.

The retained rank-tight exact occupation theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-budget50}
 c_D^{m-1}\le D!\rho^{-D},\qquad c_D=2^{-D}\binom{2D}{D}>1,
\end{equation}
when $I,-I\in\calA$, for $m$ selected rank-tight cuts with unrestricted
intervening widths. Its quantitative positive-error version remains
Theorem~\ref{thm:robust51}.

A stochastic trace inequality gives
$s_0(P)\ge D/(k-D)$ for a $D$-polytope with at most $k<2D$ vertices
or facets. It leads to an explicit one-surplus multiplier and, in
dimension three, $\beta_3(\rho)\ge1+\rho^3/1024$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:beta52}). We obtain both an unconditional computable
positive-error six-state interval and a separate explicit interval
under a quantified reachable-basis condition. These two assertions
have different effectiveness and conditioning content.

The finite projector equations receive complete scalar counts and a
global residual sum-of-squares hierarchy
(Proposition~\ref{prop:counts52} and Theorem~\ref{thm:sos52}). The
hierarchy is a classical algebraic certificate method applied to the
complete compatibility system, not a claim of polynomial-time
optimization. For rational finite-group data, the exact stationary
realization also admits a finite-description and random-bit accounting
(Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform52}).

\subsection{Organization and mathematical provenance}
The main text follows the chain from reachable sections to exact and
noisy rigidity, then to effective expansion, finite certificates and
uniform rational realization. The appendix retains the full simplex,
rank-tight stability, orthogonal circuit, two-state sign--magnitude,
word-profile, arithmetic and finite-bit developments. No earlier
theorem or proof is discarded in order to focus the main argument.
The extracted core reading copy is an excerpt of this complete
article, not a separate source of theorem credit.

Positive realization through invariant polyhedral cones and
nonnegative intertwiners is classical \cite{BF04,BF03}; static extension
complexity does not by itself supply stochastic transitions
\cite{Yannakakis}. Section~\ref{sec:priority52} compares individual
claims with published antecedents in realization, automata and
invariant-polytope theory. Internal revisions record derivation and
preservation, not independent priority. The finite-dimensional results
stand on their own; no unstated Fourier, limit-theorem, filtering or
operator-domain conclusion from the surrounding analytic series is
used as a premise or inferred as a consequence.
