\begin{abstract}
We classify bounded-width stochastic realizations of finite continuous numerical interfaces on compact groups with finitely many connected components. The output may be a categorical law or, more generally, a point of a compact convex set in a finite-dimensional normed space. The exact error threshold is the largest constrained Chebyshev radius of a component image. A stationary permutation realization attains the boundary; below it every fixed width has a horizon-independent occupation bound, even when all other registers are unrestricted. The theorem allows both clock-dependent rows and redesign at every horizon, and requires executable returns of all sufficiently large lengths rather than an identity letter. Finite representative functions and positive localization give an intrinsic proof without a faithful matrix embedding. For a planar alphabet of $s$ rotations whose angle vector is dual badly approximable and whose signal amplitude is strictly below one, the minimum width is $\Theta(N^{s/(2s+1)})$ at every fixed error below the sharp threshold; an exact polygon realization supplies the matching upper bound. For rational orthogonal commands and polynomial categorical interfaces, the threshold, a boundary machine, and subcritical occupation bounds are computable. The general continuous classification is an existence theorem, not an algorithm for unencoded real data.
\end{abstract}
