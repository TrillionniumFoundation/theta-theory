\begin{abstract}
We study finite stochastic registers whose transition rows may depend
freely on the clock and horizon, with numerical decoders constrained to
a compact convex output set. An entropy--transport estimate for
mass-weighted conditional-centroid directions removes the logarithmic
loss in the spectral-gap converse for alphabets with a physical identity
letter. If the least unit-orbit dimension is
$p_*$, every fixed subcritical error permits only $O(k^{2/p_*})$ cuts
of width at most $k$, regardless of intervening widths. A quadratic
orbit-hull construction gives the matching law when the target orbit
has dimension $p_*$. In particular, legal $d\times d$ density-matrix
outputs have width $\Theta(N^{d-1})$ at fixed positive subcritical
error. For three explicit rational Bloch rotations, a classical
Lubotzky--Phillips--Sarnak spectral bound makes the linear noisy law
fully effective, whereas the exact cut profile is $5^t$ and persists
under error at most $25^{-N}/16$. The earlier rational-unitary
experiment, rational sparse-table compiler and adaptive classical
observation construction are retained with their distinct hypotheses.
These are label-width laws, not uniform workspace bounds; spectral
constants and leading asymptotic constants are distinguished.
\end{abstract}
