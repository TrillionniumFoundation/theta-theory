\section{Future tests and predictive quotients}\label{sec:quotients}
\subsection{Operator words and adaptive protocols}
A finite future protocol starts from the present time, uses available
actions causally, and returns a bounded terminal readout. Its conditional
expected readout, as a function of the current hidden state, is a future
test. A terminal hidden-state probe is allowed only if an actual measurement
kernel producing it is part of the protocol. For finite report alphabets,
a deterministic depth-$m$ protocol is a tree of actions and terminal
readouts. Its test has the recursion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:test-tree}
 f=\sum_y\mathsf T_{t,a,y}f_y.
\end{equation}
Randomized protocols integrate such recursions. Let $\cV_t$ be the real
linear space generated by the designated future tests, including constants.
We require the one-step closure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closure}
 \mathsf T_{t,a,y}\cV_{t+1}\subset\cV_t
\end{equation}
for the continuations being retained. Where full future laws are intended,
the tests include indicator readouts from a determining event class and
its needed bounded-monotone closure.

A fixed finite menu used to assess prediction loss can be much smaller than
$\cV_t$. Such a menu does not automatically define a recursively sufficient
state. One must either prove its closure/completeness or keep a larger
predictive state for the online construction.

\begin{definition}[Predictive equivalence]\label{def:predictive-equivalence}
Two prepared beliefs at time $t$ are predictively equivalent when they have
the same continuation interface and
\[
 \pi\equiv_t\widetilde\pi
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \pi(f)=\widetilde\pi(f)\quad(f\in\cV_t).
\]
The continuation interface includes the legal future action tree, remaining
budget, and any public clock that affects future tests. The predictive
quotient is the set of these equivalence classes, or equivalently the image
of $\pi\mapsto(\pi(f))_{f\in\cV_t}$ together with that interface.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Minimal future prediction]\label{thm:quotient}
Under \eqref{eq:closure}, predictively equivalent beliefs give the same
laws for all designated future protocols. After an action and a report of
positive probability, their updated beliefs remain predictively equivalent.
Conversely, equality of those protocol laws implies equality of their
future tests. If a deterministic history statistic $r_t$ determines all
these predictions and the continuation interface, the predictive state
factors uniquely through $r_t$ on its attained image.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a one-step report, its probability is the value of
$\mathsf T_{t,a,y}1\in\cV_t$, and is therefore common. For a next-state
test $f$, both the numerator and denominator of \eqref{eq:bayes} have the
same values under equivalent beliefs. The conditional next predictions
are equal. Induct over the depth of a protocol tree using
\eqref{eq:test-tree}. Randomized action choices use the same conditional
randomization at corresponding visible histories and can be integrated.
Bounded monotone convergence extends equality from finite determining tests
to the designated generated events. Conversely, expectations of each
designated bounded readout are determined by its law, hence agree; equality
extends linearly to $\cV_t$.

If $r_t(h)=r_t(\tilde h)$, sufficiency of the statistic for these tasks
implies equality of all future predictions and interfaces. Thus each fibre
of $r_t$ is contained in a predictive-equivalence class. Define the factor
on the image of $r_t$ by the predictive class of any history in that fibre.
It is well defined and is the unique such map. This last assertion is a
set-theoretic universal property. Its measurable version requires an actual
measurable quotient realization, as specified below.
\end{proof}

The theorem does not give a normalized continuation at a zero-probability
report. One can extend an update arbitrarily there for a chosen measurable
implementation, but such an extension is not canonical information.
For worst-history statements, admissible histories and these extensions
must be explicitly specified.

\subsection{Safe measurable realizations}
The complete posterior on a standard Borel hidden space is a safe measurable
state under a fixed experiment and appropriate conditional versions. Its
predictive quotient may be simpler, but not every abstract measurable
quotient is standard Borel. We use the following concrete sufficient
condition.

\begin{theorem}[Compact continuous realization]\label{thm:compact-quotient}
Let $B_t$ be compact metric admissible belief domains and let
$(f_{t,j})_{j\ge1}$ be continuous bounded evaluation functions on $B_t$
that determine predictive equivalence. Put
\[
 e_t(b)=(f_{t,j}(b))_{j\ge1},\qquad S_t=e_t(B_t),
\]
after rescaling the functions into $[-1,1]$. Suppose action spaces are compact
metric, reports finite, and $F_t:B_t\times A_t\times Y_{t+1}\to B_{t+1}$
is continuous for each report and respects predictive equivalence. Then
$S_t$ is compact metrizable and the induced update
$\bar F_t:S_t\times A_t\times Y_{t+1}\to S_{t+1}$ is continuous for each
report. Every continuous evaluation constant on predictive fibres descends
to a continuous function on $S_t$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The countable product $[-1,1]^{\N}$ is compact metrizable, and $e_t$ is
continuous. Its image is compact, hence closed and metrizable. A continuous
surjection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a closed quotient
map. The same holds for $e_t\times\id_{A_t}$ because its domain is
compact and its target Hausdorff. For each report,
$e_{t+1}\circ F_t$ is continuous and constant on fibres of this quotient
map. The definition of quotient topology therefore gives a unique
continuous descended map. The final assertion is the same argument without
the action factor.
\end{proof}

If the hidden state space is compact and the belief domain is compact in
weak convergence, continuous hidden-state tests give the required
continuous evaluations. With continuous positive likelihoods bounded away
from zero, Bayes updating is continuous. In concrete finite models this is
verified directly. A countable dense set of actions can determine all
finite words when the corresponding evaluations are continuous; this must
be checked, not inferred from standard Borel structure alone.

For finite histories the quotient is finite and the measurable issue is
trivial. For noncompact or nondominated settings we state results on an
already supplied Borel predictive realization rather than claiming a
universal smooth quotient.

\subsection{Finite remaining-test coordinates}
The following elementary result is useful when a finite representation
exists, including representations with redundant coordinates.

\begin{proposition}[Positive finite-coordinate update]\label{prop:coordinate-update}
Let $\psi_t=(1,\psi_{t,1},\ldots,\psi_{t,q_t})$ be bounded tests. Suppose
\[
 \mathsf T_{t,a,y}1=b_{t,a,y}\psi_t,
 \qquad
 \mathsf T_{t,a,y}\psi_{t+1,i}=c_{t,a,y,i}\psi_t,
\]
with uniformly bounded coefficient rows and jointly Borel dependence on the
input. On a domain of beliefs where every relevant report probability is
at least $\kappa>0$, the coordinate vector
$v=(\pi\psi_{t,i})_{i\ge1}$ updates by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coordinate-update}
 v_i'=\frac{c_{t,a,y,i}(1,v)^{\mathsf T}}
 {b_{t,a,y}(1,v)^{\mathsf T}}.
\end{equation}
It is uniformly Lipschitz on segments joining such moment vectors when the
same report floor holds on their belief mixtures. Linear independence of
the tests is not required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:coordinate-update} follows from \eqref{eq:bayes}.
A segment between two belief moment vectors represents their convex
mixtures. The denominator is a report probability under that mixture and
is at least $\kappa$. The numerator, coefficient rows, and coordinate
vectors are bounded. Differentiating the quotient bounds its derivative
by a constant depending only on these bounds and $\kappa$. Integrate that
derivative along the segment. Redundant coefficient representations have
the same evaluations on actual moment vectors and their mixtures, so they
give the same update there.
\end{proof}

In the static latent-variable case the instruments are multiplication by
report likelihoods. In a dynamic model they are positive transfer
operators. In either case the update uses current coordinates, command,
and report, not a recovered history tape. An observer who stores the
entire vector $v$ at arbitrary real precision has not yet complied with a
finite-label budget; that is the problem of Sections~\ref{sec:checkpoint}
and \ref{sec:emulation}.

\subsection{Three different fibres}
It is useful to record the types before adding geometry. Predictive fibres
are fibres of $h_t\mapsto s_t(h_t)$. Statistical fibres are equivalence
classes of parameters with equal laws under the specified strategy class.
Feature fibres are fibres of a chosen map $\mathsf O:\Lambda\to Z$.
Equality of features can be strictly weaker than equality of laws, and
equality of a fixed-prior posterior prediction need not be parameter-uniform
sufficiency. Their derivatives, when defined, have different domains.
