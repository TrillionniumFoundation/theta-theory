\section{Attained prediction and checkpoint quantization}\label{sec:checkpoint}
\subsection{The physical loss}
Fix a preparation and an exploration law, independently of coding
randomness. At time $t$ choose a future query $J$ independently after the
label is formed, with probabilities $w_j>0$, $\sum_jw_j=1$. Conditional on
history and query, a bounded response $Z_j$ has mean $m_j(h)$. Squared loss
is used, and only the selected query is executed. Set
\[
 p(h)=(\sqrt{w_j}m_j(h))_{j=1}^q,
 \qquad \nu=\Law(p(H_t)).
\]
The weighted decoder vector is denoted by $c$. Subtracting the complete
history predictor's risk leaves $\norm{p(h)-c}^2$.

An $M$-label checkpoint encoder reads the complete prefix once and retains
one of $M$ symbols. Its decoder receives the label and the subsequent query,
not the discarded history or the acquisition commands. A public seed may
specify a codebook but is independent of the preparation. Private coins are
allowed and are either discarded or included in the retained label. We
assume a bounded closed convex prediction domain containing all $p(h)$;
projection into it can only improve squared error.

\begin{theorem}[Exact checkpoint identity]\label{thm:quantization}
The optimal mean checkpoint excess risk equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantization}
 R_M^{\rm chk}=e_M^2(\nu):=
 \inf_{c_1,\ldots,c_M}
 \int\min_{1\le j\le M}\norm{p-c_j}^2\,\nu(dp).
\end{equation}
Independent public or private coding randomization does not decrease this
infimum. An arbitrarily near-optimal deterministic nearest-centre encoder
is measurable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Condition on history, query, and the code randomness. The elementary
identity
\[
 \E[(Z_j-a)^2\mid h,J=j]
 =\Var(Z_j\mid h,J=j)+(m_j(h)-a)^2
\]
reduces the loss to the displayed Euclidean distortion. For each label,
replace a randomized decoder by its conditional mean over decoder coins;
convexity of squared norm does not increase the loss. Given fixed decoder
centres, random assignment by the encoder is no better than the nearest
centre. Hence every fixed public-seed codebook has risk at least the
right-hand side of \eqref{eq:quantization}; averaging over the independent
seed preserves this inequality.

Conversely choose centres within any prescribed positive tolerance of the
infimum. Encode by the first index attaining the least distance. Finite
comparisons of continuous distance functions make this rule Borel. The
resulting distortion is the integrand in \eqref{eq:quantization}. Let the
tolerance decrease to zero. A codebook indexed by an unknown true parameter
is not produced by this argument: $\nu$ is the one declared preparation law.
\end{proof}

For worst-history loss the analogous deterministic checkpoint quantity is
a covering radius, not the integral in \eqref{eq:quantization}. The mean
and worst-history laws have the same order only under additional geometric
and mass conditions.

\subsection{A small-ball lower bound}
\begin{lemma}[Mass obstruction]\label{lem:small-ball}
Suppose a probability law $\nu$ in $\R^d$ satisfies
$\nu(B(c,r))\le A r^d$ for every centre $c$ and radius $r>0$.
Then for every integer $M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-ball}
 e_M^2(\nu)\ge\frac12(2AM)^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
In particular, a Lebesgue density bounded by $B\epsilon^{-d}$ gives
$e_M^2(\nu)\ge c_{d,B}\epsilon^2M^{-2/d}$ for $\epsilon>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The union of $M$ balls of radius $r=(2AM)^{-1/d}$ has probability at most
one half. On its complement every centre is at least distance $r$ away.
Integrating the nearest-centre squared distance gives at least $r^2/2$.
For the density assertion use $A=Bv_d\epsilon^{-d}$, where $v_d$ is the
volume of the Euclidean unit ball.
\end{proof}

The lemma permits centres outside the support and therefore already
covers arbitrary decoders. It does not condition away a small probability
of reaching the difficult region. If the useful density lower geometry
instead occurs only on an event of probability $\beta$, the eventual risk
lower bound must carry that mass.

\begin{lemma}[A positive attained chart]\label{lem:attained-chart}
Suppose a coordinate projection of a prediction law dominates
$\beta$ times uniform probability on a $d$-dimensional cube of side $a$,
with $\beta,a>0$. If that projection has operator norm at most $L$, then
\[
 e_M^2(\nu)\ge c_d\beta a^2L^{-2}M^{-2/d}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any centres in the original prediction space, project the centres onto
the cube's coordinate space. Distances there are at most $L$ times the
original distances. The uniform cube law has density $a^{-d}$ and
satisfies Lemma~\ref{lem:small-ball}. Domination by $\beta$ and rescaling
prove the assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Whole-image covers and all integer budgets}
Let $D\subset\R^d$ be nonempty, compact, and contained in a cube of side
$2a$. Divide each coordinate interval into
$k=\lfloor M^{1/d}\rfloor$ pieces. There are at most $k^d\le M$ cells.
Choose one point of $D$ in each nonempty cell. Any point is within Euclidean
distance $2a\sqrt d/k$ of its representative. Since
$k\ge M^{1/d}/2$, we obtain a radius $4a\sqrt d M^{-1/d}$ for every
integer $M\ge1$. The corresponding $\ell^1$ bound is $4adM^{-1/d}$.
Neither a large-budget asymptotic nor an integer-budget relaxation is needed.

More generally, finitely many Lipschitz images of compact cubes have such
covers with exponent equal to the largest domain dimension. Allocate
centres between the finitely many charts, use the preceding grid, and enlarge
the constant for budgets smaller than the number of charts. This is a
sufficient global description, not a consequence of local rank alone.
A nonlinear or disconnected image cannot be replaced by a tangent plane
without checking its entire cover.

\subsection{Causal lower bounds use one exploration law}
Let $\mathcal C_{\boldsymbol M}$ be causal encoders with at most $M_t$ labels
at time $t$. Under the fixed exploration strategy, their label is also a
measurable, possibly randomized, encoding of the complete time-$t$ prefix.
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:causal-lower}
 \inf_{C\in\mathcal C_{\boldsymbol M}}
       \max_{1\le t\le N}\E\ell_t^C
 \ge \max_{1\le t\le N}e_{M_t}^2(\nu_t).
\end{equation}
Indeed the checkpoint lower inequality holds for every single $C$ and all
$t$ before taking the infimum. The same law of exploration supplies all
marginals $\nu_t$. Changing the exploration strategy with the encoder, or
with the checkpoint after the infimum, would define another problem.

\subsection{Calibrated anisotropy}
A useful model interface is provided by \cite{QiA1}. There, under whole-image
covering, positive attained mass for each initial scale flag, and uniformly
stable raw updates, the scale profile is
\[
 Q_t(M)=\max_{1\le\ell\le p_t}
   \left(\frac{s_{t,1}\cdots s_{t,\ell}}{M}\right)^{2/\ell}.
\]
The physical prediction metric is fixed; small singular values are not
removed by an unbounded coordinate whitening. The finite-horizon causal
bound follows by propagating the covering radii, as in the next section.
We cite this refined profile as a conditional model interface, not as an
unproved universal consequence of a list of singular values. The dynamic
model of Section~\ref{sec:refreshing} will establish its own isotropic
scale, density bound, and invariant cover directly from the experiment.
