\section{Nonreduced multigenerator schemes and fat-point transport}
\label{sec:nonreduced-multigenerator}
We next determine a multiplication failure scheme with arbitrarily
large embedding dimension and nilpotence order. It is not a diagonal
arrangement and its proof does not use Haiman's theorem.
Fix integers $e\ge2$ and $h\ge3$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fat-algebra}
 B_{e,h}=\C[z_1,\ldots,z_e]/(z_1,\ldots,z_e)^h,
 \qquad d=\binom{e+h-1}{e},\qquad N=\binom{e+h-1}{e+1}.
\end{equation}
Write $\mathfrak m$ for its augmentation ideal.
On $X=\Gr(e+1,B_{e,h})^\circ$ the augmentation of the universal
bundle $\mathcal A$ is surjective. Its kernel $K$ has rank $e$.
Let $\Delta\subset X$ be the determinant divisor of the canonical map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fat-tangent-map}
 K\longrightarrow(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\otimes\mathcal O_X.
\end{equation}
Thus this divisor is defined without a choice of unit or coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Exact primary law for truncated local algebras]
\label{thm:fat-primary}
For every $m\ge h-1$, on the entire generating Grassmannian $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fat-primary}
 \operatorname{Fitt}_0\operatorname{coker}(\Sym^m\mathcal A\to B_{e,h})
       =\mathcal I_\Delta^{\,N}.
\end{equation}
The divisor $\Delta$ is nonempty, integral, and Cartier. The displayed
ideal is primary, its exact nilradical index is $N$, and its generic
transverse length is $N$. For every $q\ge1$ its $q$th ordinary power
has the complete primary description $\mathcal I_\Delta^{Nq}$;
there are no embedded associated points. On a smooth unit-frame cover,
$\Delta$ is a generic $e$ by $e$ determinant, and its singular locus
is given by matrix rank at most $e-2$.

More precisely, choose a unit $a\in\mathcal A$ and write
$\mathcal A=\langle a,ax_1,\ldots,ax_e\rangle$, with $x_i\in\mathfrak m$.
After multiplying the target by $a^{-m}$, the cokernel in
\eqref{eq:fat-primary} is the cokernel of the square substitution map
\[
 T_x:B_{e,h}\longrightarrow B_{e,h},\qquad z_i\longmapsto x_i.
\]
If $M$ is the matrix of linear coefficients of the $x_i$, then
$\det T_x=(\det M)^N$. These presentation and ideal identities hold
after arbitrary base change. The primary assertions refer to the
universal parameter space $X$ over $\C$, not to every specialization.
For $m<h-1$ the zeroth Fitting ideal is zero, so the stated stable
threshold is sharp for this family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A unit exists locally because the augmentation of $\mathcal A$ is
onto; subtracting constants from the remaining normalized basis
vectors puts all $x_i$ in $\mathfrak m$. The normalized multiplication
matrix consists of the columns $x^\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\le m$.
Columns of total degree at least $h$ vanish. When $m\ge h-1$ the
remaining columns are exactly the $d$ columns of $T_x$; all other
columns are zero. This proves the cokernel assertion, over any
coefficient algebra.

The map $T_x$ preserves the $\mathfrak m$-adic filtration and its
block on $\mathfrak m^j/\mathfrak m^{j+1}$ is $\Sym^j M$. Hence
\[
 \det T_x=\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}\det(\Sym^j M)
          =(\det M)^{\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}\binom{e+j-1}{e}}
          =(\det M)^N.
\]
For completeness, the middle identity follows by taking $M$ diagonal:
among all degree-$j$ monomials each diagonal entry occurs with total
exponent $\frac je\binom{e+j-1}{e-1}=\binom{e+j-1}{e}$.
The identity holds for arbitrary matrices because both sides are
polynomial functions and diagonalizable matrices with distinct
eigenvalues form a dense open over $\C$. It is consequently a
polynomial identity and remains valid on nonreduced bases.
No condition on the coefficients of degree at least two was used.

The determinant of \eqref{eq:fat-tangent-map} differs from $\det M$
by a unit: the vectors $ax_i$ form a basis of $K$, and their linear
terms are the linear terms of $x_i$ multiplied by the nonzero
augmentation of $a$. Thus the ideal equality descends canonically.
To check its geometric structure, send $A$ to $K=A\cap\mathfrak m$.
This identifies $X$ with the total space of the quotient bundle
$\mathfrak m/K$ over $\Gr(e,\mathfrak m)$: a lift of the augmented
line has the unique form $1+v$ modulo $K$. On its frame cover the
entries of the linear-coefficient matrix $M$ and the higher
coefficients are independent variables, subject only to the open
condition that the full $d-1$ by $e$ matrix have rank $e$.
The generic determinant is irreducible. Its zero scheme remains
nonempty on this open, as witnessed by
\[
 x_1=z_1,\ldots,x_{e-1}=z_{e-1},\qquad x_e=z_1^2.
\]
It is therefore integral on the frame cover and descends to an
integral Cartier divisor on the smooth scheme $X$.

Locally its equation is a prime element in a regular local ring.
Every positive power is primary; alternatively, a hypersurface in
a regular scheme has no embedded associated points and its support
here has a single generic point. The radical of $(\delta^N)$ is
$(\delta)$, and $\delta^{N-1}$ is nonzero modulo $\delta^N$ at its
generic point. This proves the exact nilradical index and length,
and the same argument proves the assertion for all powers.
The partial derivatives of a determinant are its cofactors, giving
the stated singular locus.
Finally $\rank\Sym^m\mathcal A=\binom{e+m}{e}<d$ if $m<h-1$;
there are no maximal minors. For $A=\langle1,z_1,\ldots,z_e\rangle$
the stable map is surjective, so this threshold cannot be lowered.
\end{proof}

For $e=2$ this is a genuine three-plane theorem for every
$\C[x,y]/(x,y)^h$: the contact length is $\binom{h+1}{2}$ and the
failure multiplicity is $\binom{h+1}{3}$. Already $h=3$ gives a
length-six, embedding-dimension-two algebra whose failure divisor
has multiplicity four. These are schemes on a full generating
Grassmannian, not selected transverse slices. They are distinct from
the curvilinear hyperplane schemes and the reduced pair-diagonal ideal.

\subsection{A global conductor theorem in arbitrary projective dimension}
The preceding algebra is not the algebra of a divisor on a curve.
To apply its scheme calculation to global sections, we require a
separate conductor theorem. Its range depends on the order $h$ of
the fat point, not on its length $d$.

\begin{theorem}[Fat-point conductor transport]\label{thm:fat-conductor}
Let $e\ge1$, $h\ge2$, let $p\in\Pj^e$, and let $Z$ be the fat point
with ideal $\mathcal I_p^h$. Suppose $n\ge2h-1$. For any generating
$k$-plane $A\subset H^0(Z,\mathcal O_Z(n))$, let
\[
 W=H^0(\Pj^e,\mathcal I_p^h(n)),\qquad
 U=\operatorname{res}_Z^{-1}(A).
\]
For every $m\ge2$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fat-saturation}
 WU^{m-1}=H^0(\Pj^e,\mathcal I_p^h(mn)).
\end{equation}
Restriction induces an isomorphism of coherent cokernels on the
entire relative generating Grassmannian, also with $p$ varying,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fat-global-cokernel}
 \operatorname{coker}(\Sym^m\mathcal U\to H^0(\mathcal O(mn))\otimes\mathcal O)
 \simeq
 \operatorname{coker}(\Sym^m\mathcal A\to\pi_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z}(mn)).
\end{equation}
It and all its Fitting ideal equalities commute with arbitrary base
change. The bound is sharp uniformly over all generating dimensions
$k$ and all $m\ge2$; no rank-specific sharpness is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First fix $p=[1:0:\cdots:0]$ and work on its affine chart with
coordinates $z_1,\ldots,z_e$. A section of $\mathcal O(n)$ is a
polynomial of degree at most $n$, and $W$ has basis the monomials
of total degree between $h$ and $n$. Choose $u\in U$ with constant
coefficient $u_0$ a unit. This is possible over a local parameter
base because the augmentation of $A$ and restriction onto $A$ are
surjective.

For every integer $t$ with $h\le t\le mn$, one can choose
$1\le j\le m$ with $jh\le t\le jn$. Indeed the integral intervals
$[jh,jn]$ have no gaps: $jn+1\ge(j+1)h$ follows from $n\ge2h-1$.
Every monomial $z^\alpha$ of total degree $t$ can be factored into
$j$ monomials of degrees between $h$ and $n$. Their product,
multiplied by $u^{m-j}$, belongs to $WU^{m-1}$ and has the form
\[
 u_0^{m-j}z^\alpha+
             \text{monomials of total degree strictly greater than }t.
\]
Its degree is at most $mn$. Choosing this column for every monomial
of degree between $h$ and $mn$ gives a square triangular matrix
with units on the diagonal. It is invertible over the coefficient
ring. These columns are thus a basis of the whole space on the
right of \eqref{eq:fat-saturation}. The reverse containment is immediate.

Restriction in degrees $n$ and $mn$ is surjective, since those
degrees are at least $h-1$. Its kernels and targets are vector
bundles, with monomial bases on the current chart. In the locally
split sequence $0\to W\to U\to A\to0$, the kernel on symmetric
powers is the image of $W\otimes\Sym^{m-1}U$. Equation
\eqref{eq:fat-saturation} identifies its multiplication image with
the kernel of target restriction. Taking the quotient of this
restriction square proves \eqref{eq:fat-global-cokernel}.
The triangular proof works over every coefficient ring once $u_0$
is a unit. For moving $p$, choose projective coordinates smoothly
locally; restriction and the cokernel isomorphism are intrinsic, so
they descend. All the split restriction sequences and surjective
maps remain so after arbitrary pullback.

At the boundary $n=2h-2$, take $A=\langle1\rangle$. Then
$U=\langle1\rangle+W$, and its quadratic monomials have degrees in
$\{0\}\cup[h,2h-2]\cup[2h,4h-4]$. They omit $z_1^{2h-1}$,
although it lies in the conductor kernel in degree $2n$.
Its nonzero global cokernel class restricts to zero on $Z$.
Thus the cokernel comparison already fails in degree two.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Global noncurvilinear primary schemes]
\label{cor:global-fat-primary}
For $e\ge2$, $h\ge3$, and $n\ge2h-1$, consider all inverse-image
series $U$ of generating $(e+1)$-planes on $Z=(\mathcal I_p^h)$.
For every $m\ge h-1$, their global multiplication failure scheme
has the exact primary ideal $\mathcal I_\Delta^{\binom{e+h-1}{e+1}}$.
Every power is primary and there are no embedded associated points
on this fixed-$p$ generating parameter space. In degrees $2\le m<h-1$
the failure ideal is zero. The same primary assertions hold on the
relative parameter space with $p\in\Pj^e$ varying; the coherent
cokernel comparison itself holds after every base change.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorems~\ref{thm:fat-primary} and~\ref{thm:fat-conductor}.
For moving $p$, coordinate charts give the relative determinant
Schubert divisor with free higher-jet parameters. It is reduced
and Cartier, and its fibres over the integral base $\Pj^e$ are
nonempty integral divisors of constant dimension. These locally
trivial descriptions make the total divisor integral as well.
The total parameter space is smooth, so the same local primary-power
argument applies. No primary-decomposition assertion is inferred
for arbitrary nonreduced specializations of this parameter space.
\end{proof}
