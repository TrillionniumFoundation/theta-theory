\begin{abstract}
We identify the limiting nonuniform stochastic label width of a numerical experiment with its stationary minimum. The physical alphabet is assumed to approximate the identity at every sufficiently large length; no compatibility between physical returns and hidden transition rows is required. A finite Ramsey extraction shows that arbitrarily many cuts of width at most $k$ force a stationary $k$-label realization with the same uniform error, even when all intervening registers are unrestricted. This result applies to irreversible topological monoids and norm-compact convex Banach-valued outputs. For compact groups, a further purification gives a finite continuous group action without increasing width or error, while retaining randomized initialization. We obtain a complete finite-quotient criterion, including positive critical radii on groups with infinitely many components, and the exact limiting minimum in each return-length phase. Under separate quantitative hypotheses we retain matching planar and profinite width laws and prove spherical width bounds of order $N^{(d-1)/2}$ up to a logarithmic factor; an explicit rational noncommuting alphabet generates $\mathrm{SO}(3)$. For rational categorical data on explicitly given finite groups, stationary minimization is $\exists\R$-complete. Dyadic compilation yields horizon-independent description and random-bit budgets for stationary permutation realizations. Arbitrary real descriptions and exact sampling are uncharged only in the original label-width model.
\end{abstract}
