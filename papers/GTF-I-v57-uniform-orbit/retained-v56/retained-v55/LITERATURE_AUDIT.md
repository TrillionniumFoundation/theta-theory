# Theorem-level literature audit — Revision 55

This is a targeted author-side comparison with primary mathematical sources. It does not certify exhaustive novelty or an independent human priority review. The complete v54 audit is preserved in `retained-v54/LITERATURE_AUDIT.md`.

## Classical nonnegative matrix groups

Peter Flor, *On groups of non-negative matrices*, Compositio Mathematica 21(4) (1969), 376–382. Primary source: https://www.numdam.org/article/CM_1969__21_4_376_0.pdf . The paper and the rendered pages containing Theorems 2 and 3 were inspected. Flor treats nonnegative idempotent geometry and bounded nonnegative matrix groups; his introduction credits Brown's compact-group finiteness and Schwarz's stochastic-group structure. This structural ingredient is classical, not a novelty claim of v55. The v55 application uses the joint physical/stochastic closure, an idempotent over the physical identity, and closure of every seed/query error inequality to replace transitions without increasing stationary width. Initial randomization remains and is necessary in the five-versus-six-state example.

Flor's bibliography identifies Š. Schwarz, *On the structure of the semigroup of stochastic matrices*, Publ. Math. Inst. Hungar. Acad. Sci. Ser. A 9 (1964), 297–311, and D. R. Brown, *On clans of non-negative matrices*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 15 (1964), 671–674. Those antecedents are credited through the inspected primary Flor paper; this audit does not falsely claim a separate complete inspection of both earlier papers.

## Modern stochastic matrix semigroups

Frederik vom Ende and Fereshte Shahbeigi, *Generating Sets of Stochastic Matrices*, arXiv:2411.18946v1 (2024), https://arxiv.org/html/2411.18946v1 . The inspected abstract and introduction concern generating sets and divisibility of stochastic matrices. They are relevant to the matrix-semigroup setting but do not state the external compact-group uniform-error minimization problem considered here. No novelty claim is based merely on a different application vocabulary.

## Compact and profinite groups

Peter–Weyl approximation, Haar projection, finite-dimensional compact Lie quotients, the profinite component quotient and finite images of finite-dimensional representations of profinite groups are standard structural inputs. The main article cites Folland and uses the no-small-subgroups implication explicitly. The new strict-side clocked reduction preserves a legal compact-convex output through a finite-dimensional approximation; the exact boundary proof combines cofinite padding with finite Hankel rank. The explicit 2-adic example is analyzed directly: its discrete derivative becomes a circulant with a nonzero rational Fourier spectrum, supplying the linear exact-width lower bound. No general theorem about arbitrary profinite output complexity is inferred from that example.

## Numerical automata and weighted series

The retained bibliography and v54 audit compare Rabin/Paz numerical probabilistic automata, Berstel–Reutenauer finite-Hankel-rank representations, Brodsky–Pippenger group-language simulation, Steinberg topological language recognition and distribution-based bisimulation. The classical finite-rank argument is not claimed newly discovered. Its use here is an exact clocked-width implication under executable padding, followed by a finite-quotient conclusion on a profinite observable group. Language preservation and additive preservation of every numerical output remain distinct.

## Positive realization and arithmetic enclosures

The invariant-cone and positive-realization antecedents in Benvenuti–Farina remain credited. The exact polygon enclosure and Diophantine exponent are inherited from the earlier manuscript pipeline. The v55 unit-amplitude statement uses a positive-error amplitude budget and therefore does not solve the exact endpoint. The linear 2-adic width law is a different quantitative result proved by exact response-matrix rank.

## Effective closure and minimization

The closure algorithms of Derksen–Jeandel–Koiran and Nosan–Pouly–Schmitz–Shirmohammadi–Worrell, the compact orthogonal closure fact used by Blondel–Jeandel–Koiran–Portier, and Basu–Pollack–Roy real algebraic algorithms remain external premises. The new stationary minimization enumerates finite permutation tuples and computes augmented orthogonal closures before a finite existential–universal feasibility test. It supplies a terminating structural procedure, not a newly implemented closure engine or a tractable complexity classification.
