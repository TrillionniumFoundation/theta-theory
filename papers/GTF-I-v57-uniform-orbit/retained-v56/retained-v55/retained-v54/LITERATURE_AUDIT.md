## Source register and exact scope

- M. O. Rabin, *Probabilistic automata*, Information and Control 6 (1963), 230–245. Publisher record: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(63)90290-0. A. Paz, *Introduction to Probabilistic Automata*, Academic Press, 1971. These are classical language/probabilistic-automata references, not citations for our additive numerical threshold. No unverified theorem number is assigned to them.
- A. Brodsky and N. Pippenger, *Characterizations of 1-way quantum finite automata*, SIAM J. Comput. 31 (2002), 1456–1478; https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9903014. Theorem 3.3 concerns group languages recognized with bounded error; Theorem 3.6 concerns cutpoint-preserving probabilistic simulation with a possibly changed cutpoint. Neither is described here as uniform additive preservation of all acceptance probabilities or full categorical laws.
- V. D. Blondel, E. Jeandel, P. Koiran and N. Portier, *Decidable and undecidable problems about quantum automata*, SIAM J. Comput. 34 (2005), 1464–1473; https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0304082. The compact orthogonal closure argument and strict-threshold decision are relevant. Theorem 3.1's enumeration of polynomial identities, by itself, is not treated as a computably recognized finite stopping point for a full group description.
- H. Derksen, E. Jeandel and P. Koiran, *Quantum automata and algebraic groups*, J. Symbolic Comput. 39 (2005), 357–371; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2004.11.008. This supplies the established terminating group-closure algorithm, rather than a new algorithm claimed by this article.
- R. Nosan, A. Pouly, S. Schmitz, M. Shirmohammadi and J. Worrell, *On the computation of the Zariski closure of finitely generated groups of matrices*, arXiv:2106.01853v6 (3 March 2025); https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01853v6. The verified version gives effective closure and a complexity analysis. The present theorem imports termination and does not claim its full multi-stage stochastic classification has that same complexity bound.
- W. A. de Graaf, *Computing the Zariski closure of a finitely generated matrix group*, arXiv:2505.02577v2 (9 January 2026); https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02577v2. Its Lie-algebra and component-group computation is part of the current closure literature. Its algebraic-group component terminology is not substituted for real topological connected components; O(2) versus its complexification already requires care.
- S. Basu, R. Pollack and M.-F. Roy, *Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry*, second edition, Springer, 2006. Real quantifier elimination, algebraic sampling and effective semialgebraic components are classical algorithmic inputs. Our proof states explicitly where each is used.
- J. Berstel and C. Reutenauer, *Noncommutative Rational Series with Applications*, Cambridge University Press, 2011. The finite-Hankel-rank/linear-realizability characterization concerns weighted representations over a field. It does not enforce nonnegative common stochastic command rows. The author bibliographic record was checked; this audit does not pretend a fresh full-book theorem-by-theorem reading or assign an unchecked theorem number.
- L. Benvenuti and L. Farina, *A tutorial on the positive realization problem*, IEEE Trans. Automat. Control 49 (2004), 651–664. Author-hosted copy: https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~sussmann/papers/res-farina-tutorial-positive-realization.pdf. Invariant-cone/polytope positive realization is credited as classical. The article's novelty is not the general idea of storing barycentric probabilities.
- G. B. Folland, *A Course in Abstract Harmonic Analysis*, second edition, CRC Press, 2016. Haar averaging, representative functions and Peter–Weyl density are the intrinsic approximation ingredients. Approximation by linear matrix coefficients alone does not yield a positive stochastic machine, its sharp error threshold, or arbitrary-width occupation.
- B. Steinberg, *Topological dynamics and recognition of languages*, arXiv:1306.1468; https://arxiv.org/html/1306.1468v1. Theorems 2.2 and 2.6 give the minimal compact automaton and syntactic compact semitopological monoid; Proposition 2.8 treats clopen recognition by a compact topological monoid. Those hypotheses and conclusions are language/topology statements, not norm-approximation of continuous numerical laws.
- Y. Feng, L. Song and L. Zhang, *Distribution-based bisimulation and bisimulation metric in probabilistic automata*, arXiv:1512.05027 (2015); https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05027. The distribution-based equivalence and metric compare prescribed probabilistic automata. The source's abstract and bibliographic data were verified; no uninspected theorem number is claimed. We distinguish that task from minimizing over all machines, one independently chosen at each horizon.

## Remaining priority boundary

The current paper now contains conventional references and a theorem-level semantic comparison, rather than only a repository-internal audit. It does not certify exhaustive novelty in all weighted automata, controlled hidden-Markov, almost-periodic or compact-semigroup literatures. The precise proposed contribution is the combination of sharp constrained component radii, boundary attainment, arbitrary nonuniform stochastic quantifiers, general finite joint laws, full-subcritical matching arithmetic width, and effective rational specialization. Independent priority review remains appropriate.
