\section{The original sources and the inputs used here}
\label{sec:v102-inputs}
Fix $m,R,n,k$ until a uniform norm is explicitly taken. Write
$\bar\nu=\one_\Pi\nu_R^*$ and let $\mu_t$ be its original roof
kernels. The Borel maximal-segment parametrization gives
$\Psi(\theta,u)$ for $a(\theta)<u<b(\theta)$, with
\[
 A(u,\theta)=\frac{\omega(\Psi(\theta,u))}{4\pi c_*\chi(\Psi(\theta,u))},
 \quad\omega=|\partial_{p_0}\alpha_m|^{-1},\quad
 \chi=|\det D_q(F,\arg g)|,\quad g=\nabla_qF.
\]
Thus $d\bar\nu=A\,du\,d\theta$ after disjoint physical summation.
Let $z=|g|$ and $I_m=1+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}q_0^{d_i}/c_i$.
We use the following assertions with precisely their original scope.

\begin{inputstatement}[Physical geometry and trace comparison]
\label{input:v102-geometry}
The finite-count physical graph and section decisions are definable.
The endpoint Hessian has uniform positive lower and upper bounds;
for a fixed lifted itinerary the endpoint-momentum map is globally
injective. The maximal-segment Borel parametrization is one-to-one,
and its kernel identities retain incident components at coincident
physical faces. For $f^\varepsilon=AW^\varepsilon$ the one-sided
traces exist, and
\[
 |\partial_u f^\varepsilon|
       \le C(1+\varepsilon^{-1}) A I_m/z.
\]
The trace is zero at a genuinely grazing end, not necessarily at a
backward critical end. If a Borel target selector $H$ is supported
on $0<\ell=b-a\le\delta$, let
\[
 R_H^\varepsilon(t)=\int\one_H W^\varepsilon\,d\mu_t,
 \quad
 T_H^\varepsilon(t)=\sum\int
 \one_{\{a<t<b\}}\one_H(\Psi(\theta,t))
                  \min(f^\varepsilon(a+),f^\varepsilon(b-))\,d\theta.
\]
Then, on the original kernels and at every finite count,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v102-trace-input}
 |R_H^\varepsilon(t)-T_H^\varepsilon(t)|
 \le C(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\int_{t-\delta}^{t+\delta}v^0(u)\,du,
 \qquad v^0\,dt=F_\#(I_mz^{-1}\bar\nu).
\end{equation}
The selector is held at the target; no trace of an artificial selector
boundary is taken.
\end{inputstatement}
The Borel map is Supplement Theorem~\ref{supp-v85archive:thm:v85-segment-map}.
The physical momentum uniqueness and trace comparison are Supplement
Theorems~\ref{supp-v100archive:thm:v99-unique} and
\ref{supp-v100archive:thm:v98-paired-trace}. The first theorem's label
is recorded also in the dependency table below. No independent
recertification of the inherited continuum chain is implicit in its
restatement here.

\begin{inputstatement}[Uniform volume concentration and scalar reduction]
\label{input:v102-analytic}
For every previously fixed $r,\varepsilon>0$, with
$A_rh(t)=(2r)^{-1}\int_{t-r}^{t+r}h(u)\,du$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v102-volume-input}
 \limsup_m\sup_{R,n,k}m^2\|A_rb^\varepsilon\|_\infty
       \le C\varepsilon^{1/16},\qquad
 \limsup_m\sup_{R,n,k}m^2\|A_rv^0\|_\infty\le C.
\end{equation}
The constants are independent of those fixed widths. The actual
uniform weighted momentum capacity and its geometric-pressure proof
supply the second assertion without an additional capacity premise.
The original positive scalar remainder is
\[
 m^2p-\mathcal L=m^2b^{\varepsilon(B)}+e_B,
 \qquad\varepsilon(B)=A_0B^{-1/12},\qquad
 \limsup_m\sup_{R,n,k}\esssup_{\mathcal C(M)}|e_B|
                                      \le C_MB^{-1/192}.
\]
Use the fixed radial clock threshold $q=1/8$, radius
$r=c\varepsilon(B)^4$, gradient threshold $a=\sqrt{qr}$, and
short-residence threshold $\delta=6qr$. The retained low-clock
failure source has a disjoint short part $\mathsf S$ supported on
$\ell\le\delta$. With
$\mathcal H_M(h)=\limsup_m\sup_{R,n,k}m^2
\esssup_{t\in\mathcal C(M)}h(t)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v102-scalar-input}
 \mathcal E_M\le\mathcal H_M(T_{\mathsf S}^{\varepsilon(B)})
       +C_MB^{-1/192}+C(qr)^{257/256}
                           +C(1+\varepsilon(B)^{-1})qr.
\end{equation}
The original pointwise law is equivalent along this schedule to
$\mathcal H_M(T_{\mathsf S}^{\varepsilon(B)})\to0$.
\end{inputstatement}
The uncut volume estimate is Supplement
Theorem~\ref{supp-v100archive:thm:v100-uncut}; the scalar bound is
Supplement Theorem~\ref{supp-v100archive:thm:v100-endpoint} together
with its retained necessity argument. The original low-clock selector,
including the comparison collar and the original exact-event
lifetimes, is reproduced in that article; it is not differentiated or
modified in the new argument.

Every auxiliary band is fixed before the collision limit and enlarged
only afterwards, with $B$ outermost. At fixed $m,R,n,k$, common roof
kernels and joint roof/time kernels are used. Uniformity is
$\sup_R\esssup_t$, not a claim of one null set for all radii or all
uncountable selector families. The new finite-count comparisons are
measure identities before their uniform norms are taken.
