\subsection{A second inverse from the full endpoint law}
The preceding theorem uses the moment-compressed observation. Retaining
rather than integrating out the endpoint positions yields a different
inverse, which is the principal additional result of this paper.
It reconstructs functions on contact arcs before invoking analyticity.
In the following statement the two observations are density functions,
not two numbers, and their finite acquisition is part of the conclusion.

\begin{theorem}[Two-window reconstruction and finite acquisition]
\label{thm:full-law-main}
For an isolated normal channel with smooth strictly convex boundary
patches, two strictly active endpoint-arclength laws for one return
orientation determine the two boundary germs in their relative position,
the gap and the free area, up to a common Euclidean isometry. If $f_j$
is the endpoint density at absolute time $T_j$, $T_2>T_1$, their
stationary two-flight action on the active rectangle is
\[
 \mathcal W=\frac{T_1f_2-T_2f_1}{f_2-f_1}.
\]
Its diagonal gradient and Hessian give the explicit curvature formulas
\eqref{eq:tw-sourcecurv} and \eqref{eq:tw-othercurv}. On uniform physical
classes this inverse is Lipschitz from the two $C^2$ density functions
to the two intrinsic $C^2$ boundary arcs; there is no increasing jet
order and no analyticity assumption for this local assertion.

For the compact uniformly analytic, quantitatively asymmetric classes
of Theorem~\ref{thm:tw-global}, a finite onset pilot and two endpoint
histograms on each edge of a full-rank network determine the whole
labelled table with error
\[
 C\{h^\beta+\epsilon h^{-4}\}^{\theta},
 \qquad 0<\beta\le1,\quad 0<\theta<1,
\]
when each cell probability has error at most $\epsilon$. Taking
$h=\epsilon^{1/(\beta+4)}$ gives a fixed exponent
$\beta\theta/(\beta+4)$. The experiment supplies neither exact onsets,
contact registrations nor relative edge signs. Its discrete channel
and lattice-copy labels remain marks. All preparations, including
failures, are counted in \eqref{eq:tw-budget}.
\end{theorem}

Section~\ref{sec:two-window} proves this theorem directly. The
residual-time dependence of the physical density is affine above its
support onset. Eliminating its slope recovers the action, and a Schur
complement of the intermediate reflection variable gives the curvature
identities. The only analytic continuation in the global conclusion
is from actual reconstructed arcs to closed boundaries. The familiar
two-constants principle supplies its conditional loss; it is not being
claimed as a new theorem of complex analysis.

This full-law result and the moment-compressed theorem are deliberately
both retained. They identify the price of compression: coefficientwise
recovery from moment germs is distinct from a function inverse of two
endpoint laws. The finite-bin theorem makes the latter operational
under explicit analytic and symmetry margins. Neither theorem identifies
this intrinsic marked scattering law with a marked length spectrum, nor
turns formal count-data fibers into exact analytic nonrigidity.

For finite histograms all endpoint rectangles and square grids are chosen
invariant under $(s,t)\mapsto(-s,-t)$. Thus reversal permutes cell
outcomes in both windows, and the finite observation has precisely the
same coherent edgewise sign convention as its parent endpoint law.
