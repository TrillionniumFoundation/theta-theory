\section{Introduction}\label{sec:v17-introduction}

Comparison of experiments asks whether the information supplied by
one observation system can replace that supplied by another. A causal
comparison must also respect the order in which observations and
actions become available. A resource-constrained comparison asks a
further question: how much persistent state and shared randomness does
the replacement require? These questions differ even for two binary
reports. A simulator that cannot retain or reread its first emission
cannot in general reproduce a correlated second emission. Revealing a
persistent random selector to the tester is also different from hiding
it. The distinction concerns the joint laws being compared, not merely
the implementation of a convex combination.

We study marked experiments. A mark is an actual latent quantity of
the source or target system, not a fresh variable sampled after a
comparison. The comparison tests the joint law of the mark and the
controlled report history. Private simulation uses finite retained
registers and fresh private randomization. A hidden selector is an
additional priced persistent variable; a visible selector is exposed
and must occur with the same independent reference law on the target
side. With these conventions the attainable behavior image is compact
but usually nonconvex. The central issue is to compare this original
image rather than a convex relaxation with a different information
pattern.

\subsection{Main results}
For fixed finite instruments and a resource signature, let $B$ denote
the observable discrepancy image and $F(b)=\max_i f_i(b)$ its marked
feedback error. The $f_i$ are affine event tests, including zero.
The deficiency is $\delta=\min_B F$. An adaptive test is an independently
chosen function $\kappa:B\to\Delta_m$, scored at $b$ by
$\sum_i\kappa_i(b)f_i(b)$. It observes a proposed law for purposes of
verification; it is not an added physical information channel.

Theorems~\ref{thm:v17-dual} and~\ref{thm:v17-degree} give the
independent variational identity
\[
 \delta=\sup_\kappa\min_{b\in B}\sum_i\kappa_i(b)f_i(b)
       =\min_{\mu\in\cP(B)}\sup_\kappa
                  \int\sum_i\kappa_i(b)f_i(b)\,d\mu(b),
\]
and a quantitative polynomial hierarchy. In a bounded chart of the
observable affine space, of dimension $a$ and metric diameter $R$,
its attained finite-level values satisfy
\[
 0\le\delta-d_n\le4Ra/\sqrt n.
\]
An approximating test needs at most $(n+1)^a$ occupied Bernstein
coefficients, and the corresponding moment dual needs at most
$(n+2)^a$ support points. The dimension counts behavior, not row
variables. The nonlinear score prevents replacement of a lottery over
revealed candidate laws by its barycenter. At degree zero such a
replacement is possible, and the resulting convex relaxation can
miss a strictly positive private deficiency.

Proposition~\ref{prop:v17-smallcertificate} gives a concrete nonlinear
witness with three cubic behavior weights, nine nonnegative derivative
terms and seventeen positive rational coefficients. It certifies a
private lower bound above $2/5$, while constant tests give zero.

The independent game and its size theorem complement the strict
polynomial certificate hierarchy proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-dual}. The latter remains an exact verifier on a
chosen row cube; it is not itself called an independent dual. Strict
lower levels are complete, and their verification complexity is kept
separate from the behavior-coordinate degree bound. With uniformly
effective stateless finite presentations this produces certified
intervals at the original state and randomization budgets.

The second main result makes collision mechanics essential. We prepare
two spheres in full-dimensional incoming phase-space boxes and observe
a bounded function of the tagged transverse velocity after scattering.
An actual binary mark, correlated with the incoming impact orientation,
is retained but never revealed. A binary gate is chosen after the
first noisy report. Theorem~\ref{thm:v17-markedgap} first solves the
limiting finite experiment exactly:
\[
 \dpr=\dvi_r=\sqrt{5/2}-9/8,\qquad \dhi_2=1/8
 \quad\hbox{at width }(1,1).
\]
Uniform nongrazing collision estimates and Gaussian tails then give,
for contact distance $9/10\le d\le11/10$ and noise
$0<\sigma\le1/12$,
\[
 \dpr,\dvi_r>9/20,\qquad \dhi_2<13/100.
\]
The same detector and preparation have zero deficiency for
$0<d\le2/5$, when no collision reaches the detector during acquisition.
Thus conserved collective motion cannot account for the gap. The
estimates are uniform on an interval of contact distances and under
geometric similarity. Explicit collision charts also produce rational
marked presentations with certified errors tending to zero.

The third result strengthens the conditional-law content of marked
comparison. For equivalent joint laws with a common latent marginal,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v17-optional} constructs a latent-preserving coupling
under which the squared uniform distance between bounded posterior
martingales is at most $84$ times the joint total variation. The
coupling is a convergence device, not an executable simulator.
Gaussian observation of an invariant microscopic flow gives
\[
 \|P_n-P\|_{\rm TV}\le\frac{\sqrt{CT}}{2\sigma}
                  \|h_n-h\|_{L^2(\mu)},
\]
so graph-core approximation yields convergence of entire optional
projection processes. The maximum-over-time estimate, rather than a
finite-dimensional argument, supplies the changing-filtration input.
Finite-checkpoint risk transport still uses the stateless identity
comparison and keeps its original budget.

\subsection{Relation to classical theory}
Classical statistical comparison and randomization originate with
Blackwell and Le Cam~\cite{Blackwell1953,LeCam1964,Torgersen1991}.
Filtered comparison has important antecedents, including
Norberg~\cite{Norberg2002}. We do not claim priority for introducing
causal deficiency. In the adapted setting Weisshaupt's
Definition~5.1 and Theorems~5.1--5.2~\cite{Weisshaupt2006} use a compact
convex closure of adapted stochastic operators, allowing finitely
additive outputs. A fixed finite-state private image need not have
that convexity. The two-report examples identify the precise failed
step in inserting the state constraint into such a separation proof.
Our testing game retains the nonconvex image by changing the verifier's
strategy space, not by asserting a new unrestricted randomization
criterion. Its minimax ingredient is classical~\cite{Sion1958}.

Bernstein positivity is likewise not new here. Theorem~1.4 of
de Klerk--Laurent~\cite{deKlerkLaurent2010} gives constructive
hypercube degree and error bounds through Bernstein and Handelman
representations. Laurent--Slot~\cite{LaurentSlot2023}, Theorem~2 and
Corollary~3, obtain stronger Schm\"udgen degree bounds using Jackson
kernels. Theorem~9 and Proposition~2 of
de Klerk--Vera~\cite{deKlerkVera2026} connect Chebyshev coefficient
norms to effective hypercube certificates. Our bound concerns an
independent simplex-valued adaptive test on a nonconvex behavior
image; it is not an improvement to those general positivity bounds.
Pulling that test back to row coordinates still incurs a separate
positivity-verification cost.

The finite-state, common-information and automata antecedents
\cite{Witsenhausen1979,NayyarMahajanTeneketzis2013,PaullUnger1959,Kiefer2020}
remain relevant to the task and synthesis theory. The physical flow
and collision rule are classical~\cite{GallagherSaintRaymondTexier}.
The contribution of the scattering construction is the marked,
feedback-sensitive resource comparison with uniform quantitative
certificates in that flow, not a new mechanics of elastic collision.

\subsection{Organization and analytic scope}
After the exact comparison conventions, Sections~\ref{sec:v17-adaptive}
and~\ref{sec:v17-collision} prove the adaptive hierarchy and its
collision-sensitive realization. Section~\ref{sec:v17-optional} proves
the posterior-process estimate and its microscopic sufficient
condition. The remaining sections develop the complete finite
certificate, task, regeneration, graph-domain and physical-presentation
machinery used for broader acquisitions, including the explicitly
solvable collective benchmark. Appendix~\ref{sec:v17-dependencies}
records the theorem dependencies in mathematical terms.

All physical conclusions have their stated finite-time and
preparation hypotheses. A binary scattering chart and a fixed-particle
optional-projection estimate are different from particle-uniform
recollision estimates or a nonlinear kinetic limit. In particular a
large-deviation action, a hierarchy corrector uniform on action
sublevels, and a limiting nonlinear resolvent require their own
analytic inputs. The comparison theorem transports consequences of
such inputs; it does not create them by naming a dependency.
