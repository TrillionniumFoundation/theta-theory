\author{Qian Qi}

\begin{abstract}
We study comparison of marked causal experiments with fixed persistent
state and separately priced hidden or visible randomness. On the
nonconvex observable behavior image we construct an independent
adaptive testing game and polynomial test classes with attained
finite-level duality. In behavior dimension $a$ and diameter $R$, their
error is at most $4Ra/\sqrt n$, using at most $(n+1)^a$ occupied test
coefficients; this does not charge redundant simulator rows. We then
construct a two-sphere scattering experiment with an actual correlated
mark and a gate chosen after the first noisy report. Its private and
visible deficiencies exceed $9/20$, whereas a hidden two-valued
selector gives error below $13/100$, at the same width-one register.
Removing the collision makes every deficiency zero. Exact finite
values, uniform collision bounds and computable rational presentations
prove this separation without convexifying the private image. Finally,
marked variation controls the entire optional-projection process;
Gaussian observation of graph-core approximants yields a quantitative
microscopic changing-filtration limit. The costed comparison,
strict-certificate, task and domain theory is developed with its
separate information and analytic hypotheses.
\end{abstract}
