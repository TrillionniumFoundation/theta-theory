\section{Certified data for active hard spheres}
\label{sec:v15-physical-data}
The preceding theorems require analytic data, not only matrix
regressions. We give two explicit families. The first has an exactly
computable continuous-report intrinsic deficiency. The second has a
nonzero graph residual obtained from a collision-compatible position
observable. The hard-sphere flow and its almost-everywhere realization
are the same as in the inherited active microscopic construction; no
new assumption on a kicked vector's generator domain is introduced.

\subsection{A conserved mode with an exact marked deficiency}
Take $N\ge2$ identical hard spheres on a flat torus with a nonempty
configuration region. Normalize the equilibrium law to have independent
standard Gaussian velocity coordinates. Its configuration factor is
uniform on the allowed configurations. For a fixed coordinate put
\[
 X=N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^Nv_{i,1},\qquad W=\operatorname{sgn}X.
\]
Then $X$ is standard Gaussian and $W$ is a fair actual initial mark.
Elastic collisions preserve total momentum, hence $U(t)X=X$ and
$X\in\bigcap_{j\ge1}D(L^j)$ with $LX=0$. The actions $a\in\{-1,1\}$
reverse all velocities or leave them fixed, so $C_aX=aX$. They are
physical interventions, even though the selected observable lies in a
conserved sector. In the normalized space $V=\operatorname{span}\{X\}$,
the exact Gram data are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-momentum-gram}
 G=[1],\quad J=[0],\quad B_a=[a],\quad R=[0],\quad S_a=[0].
\end{equation}
All entries are rational. These identities hold for the full colliding
system, not a collision-free restriction of its equilibrium law.

Consider one acquisition after the chosen action and any fixed flight
time. Source $E$ reports a fresh standard Gaussian independent of the
state, while target $F$ reports
\[
 Y=aX+\xi,\qquad \xi\sim N(0,1).
\]
There is no informative earlier report. Source and target carry the
same actual mark $W$. The simulator knows the current action, not $X$
or $W$; every persistent selector is independent of the physical state.

\begin{theorem}[An exact continuous physical certificate]
\label{thm:v15-quarter}
For every nonempty finite private width profile, and every finite hidden
or visible independent-selector budget,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-quarter}
 \delta_b(E,F)=\frac14.
\end{equation}
The upper witness is the stateless simulator that emits an independent
$N(0,2)$ report. A lower witness is the marked event
$\{W=\operatorname{sgn}(aY)\}$. The report-sign coarsening of $F$ has
the exact rational joint table
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-sign-table}
 \Pp_F(W=w,Z=z\mid a)=
 \begin{cases}3/8,&z=aw,\\1/8,&z=-aw,\end{cases}
 \qquad Z=\operatorname{sgn}Y.
\end{equation}
It also has deficiency $1/4$ from a source independent of $W$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any source simulator its emitted report is independent of $W$,
conditional on an exposed selector and the externally fixed action.
The indicated event therefore has probability $1/2$. At the target,
$aY=X+a\xi$. The plane vector $(X,a\xi)$ is rotationally invariant;
the two sign-disagreement wedges between $X$ and $X+a\xi$ have total
angle $\pi/2$. Consequently agreement has probability $3/4$ and the
variation discrepancy is at least $1/4$. The action may be fixed in
this test, so taking the feedback supremum cannot weaken the bound.
This argument holds in each exposed-selector fibre and also after
hiding a selector.

For the upper witness write $\varphi_{\sqrt2}$ for the $N(0,2)$ density.
The conditional Gaussian law $X\mid Y=y$ has mean $ay/2$ and variance
$1/2$. Thus the target joint densities with respect to counting measure
on $w$ and Lebesgue measure on $y$ are
\[
 p(w,y)=\varphi_{\sqrt2}(y)\Phi(way/\sqrt2).
\]
The source witness has joint density
$q(w,y)=\frac12\varphi_{\sqrt2}(y)$. The sign of $p-q$ is positive
exactly on $w=\operatorname{sgn}(ay)$ and negative on its complement.
Hence that event attains the total variation, which is $3/4-1/2=1/4$.
There are only the two possible actions; the same calculation holds
for both, and for mixtures determined by any independent selector.
The witness uses no persistent state. Integrating over the two report
signs and using symmetry proves \eqref{eq:v15-sign-table}. Its variation
discrepancy from the independent fair-sign witness is again $1/4$,
and the same event proves the lower bound.
\end{proof}

The sign table is an exact finite lower-witness presentation, not a
two-sided zero-error reconstruction of the continuous Gaussian report.
The continuous upper bound above is proved directly. For an arbitrary
small two-sided bridge one must instead use the partitions and error
budgets in Section~\ref{sec:v15-finite}. Confusing these two uses of a
coarsening would give an incorrect certificate.

\begin{proposition}[Effective cell data in the conserved sector]
\label{prop:v15-cell-data}
For finitely many repeated acquisitions of $X$, with the two actions
above, rational noise variances bounded away from zero, and the mark
$\operatorname{sgn}X$, every marked Gaussian prefix mass in
\eqref{eq:v15-mass-integral} admits rational enclosures of arbitrarily
small prescribed width. The exact trial space has zero graph and
intervention errors for every horizon. Consequently
Theorem~\ref{thm:v15-main} gives a convergent same-budget finite rational
certificate procedure for this active physical family.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every controlled mean is $X$ or $-X$. The mass is a one-dimensional
integral on one of the half-lines of the standard Gaussian density
times finitely many Gaussian interval probabilities. Its tail outside
$[-M,M]$ is at most $M^{-2}$, by the second moment. On the remaining
compact interval, each interval probability and its derivative has a
computable uniform bound: its derivative is a difference of two
Gaussian densities divided by its positive noise scale. Products
therefore have a computable Lipschitz bound. Uniform Riemann sums have
an explicit mesh error given by this bound times the interval length
and mesh size. Gaussian density and distribution values at algebraic
arguments have rational enclosures: integrate the power series of
$\exp(-u^2/2)$ on a bounded interval with a Taylor remainder bound,
using symmetry and a Gaussian tail bound for unbounded endpoints.
Square roots and the normalizing constant have elementary rational
interval algorithms. Combining these enclosures with the mesh and
tail errors gives the requested rational mass interval. All quantities
are scalar; the configuration-volume normalizer cancels. Equation
\eqref{eq:v15-momentum-gram} removes the analytic trial error for every
controlled word. Apply the finite-report and rational-tree constructions
and then the finite local-certificate theorem. This proves an actual
procedure for this family, not computability of arbitrary preparations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A nonzero collision-compatible graph residual}
Consider two spheres of diameter one on the two-dimensional square
torus of side ten. The allowed ordered configuration volume is
$100(100-\pi)$; velocities remain independent standard Gaussians.
Let $b(u)=(1-u^2)^2$ on $|u|\le1$ and zero elsewhere, and let $h=1/4$.
Place squares of half-side $h$ at $c_1=(2,5)$ and $c_2=(8,5)$.
Let $\chi_1(q_1,q_2)$ be the product of the four rescaled functions $b$
when particle one is near $c_1$ and particle two near $c_2$, and put
$f=\chi_1(q_1,q_2)+\chi_1(q_2,q_1)$. The two supports are disjoint,
are separated from the collision boundary, and define a symmetric
$C^1$ position observable on the torus.

\begin{proposition}[Exact nonzero physical Gram data]
\label{prop:v15-bump}
The vector $f$ belongs to $D(L)$ and
$Lf=v_1\cdot\nabla_{q_1}f+v_2\cdot\nabla_{q_2}f$. With
$V=\operatorname{span}\{\phi\}$, $\phi=f/\|f\|_2$, its exact data are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-bump-gram}
 J=0,\qquad R=[192],\qquad B_{\pm}=1,\qquad S_{\pm}=0,
 \qquad
 \|f\|_2^2=\frac{2h^4(256/315)^4}{100(100-\pi)}.
\end{equation}
Here the actions still reverse all velocities or leave them fixed.
For one acquisition of $f$ with unit Gaussian noise after
$\Delta=1/1000$, under equilibrium preparation and with any initial
finite mark channel, the static trial mean $f$ and the true active
physical acquisition have two-sided marked feedback error less than
$1/100000$. All constants in this last assertion follow from rational
Gram enclosures and $3<\pi<4$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The position support is at positive distance from contact, including
periodic images; its minimal separation is greater than one. On a
regular orbit $f$ is continuously differentiable between collisions,
and its transport derivative vanishes near each collision configuration.
Thus the integral transport identity holds across collisions without a
jump. Its difference quotients are bounded in absolute value by a
constant times the conserved total speed. This is square integrable
under the Gaussian velocity law. Dominated convergence gives the
strong $L^2$ generator derivative claimed. This also proves the required
collision-domain membership rather than presuming a smooth-boundary
extension of a kicked function.

Direct polynomial integration gives
\[
 I_0=\int_{-1}^1b(u)^2\,du=256/315,\qquad
 I_1=\int_{-1}^1b'(u)^2\,du=256/105.
\]
The two disjoint product supports give $\|f\|^2=2h^4I_0^4/Z$,
$Z=100(100-\pi)$. Independence, centering and unit variance of velocity
coordinates give
\[
 \langle f,Lf\rangle=0,\qquad
 \|Lf\|^2=\frac{4I_1}{h^2I_0}\|f\|^2=192\|f\|^2.
\]
Velocity reversal fixes the position vector $f$, so $B_{\pm}=1$ and
$S_{\pm}=0$. The displayed Gram residual follows. Since $f\in V$,
there is no projection error and the one-step residual is
$\Delta\sqrt{192}\|f\|$. The two-action bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-residual} is therefore
\[
 \alpha\le \frac{\Delta\sqrt{384}\|f\|}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]
Using $I_0<1$, $h=1/4$ and $Z>9600$ gives
$\|f\|^2<1/1228800$. Together with $2\pi>6$ this yields
$\alpha^2<64/(10^6\cdot1228800)<10^{-10}$.
Every inequality is rational except the explicitly enclosed $\pi$;
no floating-point Gram estimate is used. A mark coupled to the original
state stays coupled in the first-mismatch argument.
\end{proof}

These two examples answer different questions. A conserved sector can
carry nontrivial hidden information and an exact positive deficiency;
a localized transport sector supplies genuinely nonzero graph residuals
and certified error bounds. The velocity interventions change the
physical dynamics in both cases. Neither example is a proof of the
particle-number-uniform nonlinear kinetic corrector in the historical
B4 program. Their downstream edge is the explicit marked
physical-to-finite certification theorem, with its own stated analytic
inputs and error topology.
