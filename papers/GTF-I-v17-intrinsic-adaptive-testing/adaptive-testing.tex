\section{Adaptive testing on the observable behavior image}
\label{sec:v17-adaptive}

The obstruction to linear randomization criteria at a fixed private
register is the nonconvexity of the behavior image, not a failure of
compactness. We give a variational formulation in which a test is an
independent function of an observable candidate law. The construction
does not give the simulator an extra random variable.

\subsection{The testing game}
Let $B$ be a nonempty compact subset of a finite-dimensional affine
space, and let $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ be affine real functions, including the
zero function. Put
\[
 F(b)=\max_{i\le m}f_i(b),\qquad \delta=\min_{b\in B}F(b).
\]
For the finite comparison problem of Section~\ref{sec:v13-deficiency},
$b$ is the array of discrepancies between the simulated and target
marked laws, indexed by parameters, feedback trees and transcripts;
$f_i$ ranges over their signed event evaluations. In the visible case
the array includes the selector and its target weights. Thus the target
coordinate is part of the array when those weights vary. Private,
fixed-hidden and fixed-visible signatures each give their own compact
set $B$. No convex hull is taken.

An \emph{adaptive test} is a continuous map $\kappa:B\to\Delta_m$,
chosen before a candidate $b$. Its score against that candidate is
\[
 G(b,\kappa)=\sum_i\kappa_i(b)f_i(b).
\]
This is a verification game: the selected candidate law is revealed to
the evaluator of the already chosen function $\kappa$. It is not a
one-observation statistical test, and does not make an unavailable
parameter or mark observable to a physical controller.

\begin{theorem}[Independent adaptive testing duality]
\label{thm:v17-dual}
For every such compact behavior image,
\begin{align}
 \delta
 &=\sup_{\kappa\in C(B,\Delta_m)}\min_{b\in B}G(b,\kappa)
 \label{eq:v17-dual}\\
 &=\min_{\mu\in\cP(B)}\sup_{\kappa\in C(B,\Delta_m)}
                \int_B G(b,\kappa)\,d\mu(b).\nonumber
\end{align}
The minimization over probability measures is attained. If Borel
adaptive tests are allowed, the first supremum is attained as well.
The continuous-test assertion is a supremum; continuous attainment is
not needed or asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\eta>0$. The sets $U_i=\{b:f_i(b)>F(b)-\eta\}$ form an open
cover of the compact metric space $B$. Choose a continuous partition
of unity subordinate to this finite cover. The resulting test has
$F(b)-\eta<G(b,\kappa)\le F(b)$ for all $b$. Consequently the first
supremum equals $\delta$. The same uniform inequality shows, for every
probability $\mu$,
\[
 \sup_\kappa\int G(b,\kappa)\,d\mu(b)=\int F\,d\mu.
\]
Its minimum is $\delta$, attained by a point mass at a minimizer of
$F$. This also proves the displayed minimax exchange without an
assumption that $B$ is convex. Equivalently one can apply the usual
compact-convex minimax theorem to $\cP(B)$ and the convex class of
continuous tests, with the pairing $\int G\,d\mu$.
Finally choose the smallest index attaining $F(b)$. Its level sets are
Borel, and its indicator vector is a Borel test with score exactly
$F$.
\end{proof}

The probability $\mu$ here describes a lottery over \emph{revealed
candidate laws}. The score is $\int G(b,\kappa)\,d\mu(b)$, not
$G(\int b\,d\mu,\kappa)$. Hiding the candidate and replacing it by its
barycenter is a different game; in general it lowers the value. This
distinction is essential for a finite persistent-state budget.

\subsection{A size bound in behavior dimension}
Factor out directions annihilated by every $f_i-f_i(b_0)$, so that on
the resulting difference space
\[
 \|v\|_* =\max_i|f_i(b_0+v)-f_i(b_0)|
\]
is a norm. Denote its dimension by $a$ and let
$R=\diam_*(B)$. All conclusions below are unaffected by this observable
quotient. The case $a=0$ is a single constant comparison and requires
one test. Suppose henceforth that $a\ge1$, so $R>0$.

\begin{lemma}[A bounded behavior chart]
\label{lem:v17-chart}
There exist $b_0\in B$ and vectors $v_j\in B-b_0$, $1\le j\le a$,
forming a basis, such that $\|v_j\|_*\le R$ and every $b\in B$ has
coordinates $b=b_0+\sum_j t_jv_j$ with $|t_j|\le2$.
If $B$ is a rational polynomial image of a product of simplexes, the
points can be chosen rational and can be found effectively.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $b_0\in B$. In any fixed coordinates on the difference space,
let $D_0>0$ be the maximum absolute determinant of $a$ vectors in
$B-b_0$. Compactness and spanning give existence and positivity.
Choose a tuple with determinant greater than $D_0/2$. Replacing its
$j$th vector by $b-b_0$ multiplies the determinant by $t_j$; maximality
gives $|t_j|<2$. The norm bound is the definition of $R$.
For a rational polynomial image, take $b_0$ at rational rows. Rational
row images are dense. An affine basis can be found by enumerating
rational images and testing rational determinants; the true affine
span and the maximum determinant are semialgebraic data. Quantifier
elimination decides whether a proposed nonzero determinant exceeds
half the maximum. Density therefore makes the search terminate. This
is an effectiveness assertion, not a bit-complexity bound for finding
the chart.
\end{proof}

Set $b(x)=b_0+\sum_j(4x_j-2)v_j$ and
$C=\{x\in[0,1]^a:b(x)\in B\}$. The ambient cube is a coordinate
box, not an enlargement of the feasible behavior set. Write
$\ell_i(x)=f_i(b(x))$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-lipschitz}
 |\ell_i(x)-\ell_i(y)|\le4R\sum_j|x_j-y_j|.
\end{equation}
For $n\ge0$ let $\beta_{\alpha,n}$ be the tensor Bernstein basis of
coordinatewise degree $n$. A feasible coefficient array has
$c_{\alpha i}\ge0$ and $\sum_i c_{\alpha i}=1$ for every
$\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,n\}^a$. It specifies the independent test
\[
 \kappa_i^c(x)=\sum_\alpha c_{\alpha i}\beta_{\alpha,n}(x).
\]
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-hierarchy}
 d_n=\max_c\min_{x\in C}
       \sum_{\alpha,i}c_{\alpha i}\beta_{\alpha,n}(x)\ell_i(x).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic adaptive degree and support bounds]
\label{thm:v17-degree}
The maxima in \eqref{eq:v17-hierarchy} are attained and
$d_n\le d_{n+1}\le\delta$. For $n\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-rate}
 0\le\delta-d_n\le \frac{4Ra}{\sqrt n}.
\end{equation}
There is an array with at most $(n+1)^a$ nonzero coefficients, all equal
to one, whose minimum score is at least $\delta-4Ra/\sqrt n$.
Its test has coordinatewise degree $n$, and its score degree at most
$n+1$. Furthermore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-momentdual}
 d_n=\min_{\mu\in\cP(C)}
        \sum_\alpha\max_i\int_C\beta_{\alpha,n}(x)\ell_i(x)\,d\mu(x),
\end{equation}
and a minimizing measure can be supported on at most $(n+2)^a$ points.
In particular a strict level $L<\delta$ admits an adaptive test of
positive uniform margin with
$n>(8Ra/(\delta-L))^2$ and at most $(n+1)^a$ occupied coefficient
entries. These bounds depend on the observable image and its metric,
not on the number of stochastic rows in a realization.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient space is a finite product of compact simplexes, and
the minimum of a jointly continuous function over compact $C$ is
continuous in its coefficients. This proves attainment. Bernstein
degree elevation replaces each coefficient by convex combinations
of adjacent coefficients, preserving every simplex constraint and the
represented function. It proves monotonicity. All scores are convex
combinations of the $\ell_i(x)$, proving the upper bound by $\delta$.

At each grid point $\alpha/n$ choose an index $i_\alpha$ maximizing
$\ell_i$ there. The choice is made even at infeasible grid points; only
the final score is minimized over $C$. Give that index coefficient one
and the others zero. For fixed $x$ let $A_j$ be independent
$\operatorname{Bin}(n,x_j)$ variables. If $i_*$ maximizes at $x$,
\begin{align*}
 \max_i\ell_i(x)-\E\ell_{i_A}(x)
 &\le\E\{\ell_{i_*}(x)-\ell_{i_*}(A/n)
             +\ell_{i_A}(A/n)-\ell_{i_A}(x)\}\\
 &\le8R\sum_j\E|A_j/n-x_j|
 \le \frac{4Ra}{\sqrt n}.
\end{align*}
Here the omitted middle difference is nonpositive, and the last bound
uses $\Var(A_j/n)\le1/(4n)$. This proves the rate and the sparse
coefficient assertion.

For the last formula replace the minimum over $C$ by a minimum over
$\cP(C)$. The pairing is bilinear and continuous, and both feasible
sets are compact convex, so the compact minimax theorem~\cite{Sion1958} exchanges the extrema. Optimizing each coefficient
simplex gives \eqref{eq:v17-momentdual}. The integrands are polynomials
of coordinatewise degree at most $n+1$. Preserve all monomial moments
of that degree by Carath\'eodory's theorem. There are $(n+2)^a$
monomials including the constant; their moment vectors lie in an
affine hyperplane of dimension at most $(n+2)^a-1$. A measure with at
most $(n+2)^a$ atoms therefore has exactly the same objective. The
strict-margin assertion follows from the rate with error less than
$(\delta-L)/2$.
\end{proof}

The score is a nonlinear function of behavior although each event
functional is affine. This is the reason the measure in
\eqref{eq:v17-momentdual} cannot be replaced by its behavior barycenter.
For $n=0$ it can: $d_0$ is precisely the constant-test convex
relaxation. In the two-time unmarked example of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v16-gap}, the target belongs to the convex
hull of private behaviors, so $d_0=0$, whereas
$\lim_n d_n=\sqrt2-1$. Thus the hierarchy recovers a gap which every
constant test misses.

\begin{remark}[What the size bound measures]\label{rem:v17-size}
The bound counts the nonzero entries of the independent adaptive test
and controls its behavior-coordinate degree. It does not bound the
cost of verifying its minimum on the generally nonconvex set $C$,
the bit lengths of a rational chart, or the size of a description of
$C$. Distinct row presentations of the same metric behavior image
satisfy the same existence bound, although their coefficient arrays
need not coincide. For the private fixed-target law array one may use
$R\le1$ with the maximum total-variation metric. For the visible
signed-discrepancy array the uniform bound $R\le2$ is sufficient.
The old row-cube positivity certificate is a separate verification
engine, with its own coordinate-dependent complexity.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A three-weight certificate invisible to constant tests}
\begin{proposition}[A finite intrinsic nonlinear witness]
\label{prop:v17-smallcertificate}
Consider a private width-one simulator producing two independent bits,
with probabilities $p,q$, for the target which assigns probability
$1/2$ to each of $00,11$. Put $u=pq$, $v=(1-p)(1-q)$ and
$J(t)=3t^2-2t^3$. The three behavior-dependent weights
\[
 \kappa_1=J(v),\qquad \kappa_2=J(u),\qquad
 \kappa_3=1-J(u)-J(v)
\]
are nonnegative and sum to one. Against the event discrepancies
$1/2-u,1/2-v,1-u-v$, respectively, their score $G$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-smallcertificate}
 G\ge \frac25+\frac9{8960}.
\end{equation}
The certificate uses cubic weights in observable probabilities,
a nine-term nonnegative derivative identity, and seventeen positive
rational Bernstein coefficients in one variable. Every constant
mixture of event discrepancies has minimum at most zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We have $u+v\le1$. Since $J$ is increasing on $[0,1]$ and
$J(1-t)=1-J(t)$, the weights are feasible. Their score is
\[
 G=1-u-v+J(u)(u-1/2)+J(v)(v-1/2).
\]
Set $a=(p+q-1)^2$ and $b=(p-q)^2$. Then $a,b\ge0$ and
$a+b\le1$. Direct expansion gives
\begin{align*}
64G={}&27-12a+38a^2-20a^3-a^4
 +36b-60ab+36a^2b+4a^3b\\
 &+6b^2-12ab^2-6a^2b^2-4b^3+4ab^3-b^4.
\end{align*}
Let $c=1-a-b$. Another polynomial identity, valid when $a+b+c=1$,
is
\begin{align*}
16\partial_bG={}&4a^3+6a^2c+12ab^2+24abc+12ac^2\\
 &+8b^3+30b^2c+30bc^2+9c^3\ge0.
\end{align*}
Thus $G(a,b)\ge G(a,0)$. The univariate polynomial
\[
 G(a,0)-\frac25=\frac7{320}-\frac3{16}a
                  +\frac{19}{32}a^2-\frac5{16}a^3-\frac1{64}a^4
\]
has degree-$16$ Bernstein coefficients $z_k/349440$, where
\begin{align*}
(z_0,\ldots,z_{16})={}&(7644,3549,1183,351,855,2494,5064,8358,\\
 &12166,16275,20469,24529,28233,31356,33670,34944,34944).
\end{align*}
The Bernstein basis is nonnegative and sums to one. Its smallest
coefficient is $351/349440=9/8960$, proving
\eqref{eq:v17-smallcertificate}. All identities are rational polynomial
identities; no numerical minimization is used.
Finally the target is the barycenter of the two feasible deterministic
laws $00$ and $11$. An affine mixture of signed event discrepancies
has average zero at these two laws and hence minimum at most zero.
The empty event supplies the reverse constant-test bound zero.
\end{proof}

For comparison, the affine weights $v,u,1-u-v$ already give the
smaller bound $3/8$, with the exact identity
\[
 G_{\rm affine}-\frac38
  =\frac12(p-q)^2+2(p-1/2)^2(q-1/2)^2.
\]
Neither certificate changes the private feasible set. The cubic
certificate is an independent test chosen once for the whole behavior
image, not a mixture chosen anew by an optimizer's unknown row values.
Its evaluation does use the revealed behavior, as specified in the
verification game. The 17-coefficient positivity check is classical;
the distinction from constant tests is the behavior-dependent feasible
weight family and its resource-sensitive minimum.

\subsection{Strict certificates and effective physical input}
Given rational finite instruments, a rational chart and the sparse
array constructed above, its strict minimum can be certified by real
quantifier elimination on the original row constraints. Alternatively,
pull the score back through the original polynomial row map and
certify its strict positivity using degree-elevated Bernstein
coefficients. The pullback changes the verification degree and is not
covered by the behavior-coordinate bound. Generic quantifier
elimination also decides the exact finite semialgebraic boundary;
this classical observation does not assert that a finite nonnegative
Bernstein expansion exists at $L=\delta$.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform effective presentation input]
\label{prop:v17-effective}
Suppose a single algorithm, on input $j$, returns rational finite
instruments $E_j,F_j$ and nonnegative rational error enclosures
$a_j,c_j$ for two-sided stateless marked feedback presentations of
$E,F$. Suppose $a_j+c_j\to0$. All alphabets, widths, selector laws and
finite subroutines must be computable uniformly from $j$ and the
requested signature. Then, for every positive rational $\eta$, an
algorithm returns a rational interval of width below $\eta$ containing
$\delta_{\mathfrak b}(E,F)$ at the same signature.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate $j$ until $a_j+c_j<\eta/8$. This terminates without a known
rate because the rational enclosures themselves tend to zero.
For the selected finite problem, enumerate rational same-budget upper
tables and strict rational lower certificates, dovetailing all finite
checks; include the always valid lower bound zero. Compactness,
density and strict completeness provide an interval of width below
$\eta/2$. Enlarge it by $a_j+c_j$ at both ends using stateless
comparison. The enlarged width is below $3\eta/4$. Every enumeration
and comparison is uniform by the hypotheses. A mere sequence of
individually computable presentations without a uniform enumeration
is not an input satisfying this proposition.
\end{proof}
