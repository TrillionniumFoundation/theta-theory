\section{Effective graph data from regular collision charts}
\label{sec:v15-effective-physical}
The effective-data hypothesis can be discharged for a class of genuine
colliding systems; it need not remain an oracle for their exact flow.
Here the particle number is fixed. No bound uniform in particle number,
number of collisions, or kinetic scaling is used or concluded.

Fix $N\ge2$, a flat square or cubic torus of positive rational side
length, and a positive rational sphere diameter smaller than half that
length. Assume the allowed configuration region has nonempty interior.
Let $\mu$ be the normalized equilibrium law with independent standard
Gaussian velocity coordinates, and let the finite intervention family
consist of algebraic orthogonal transformations applied identically
to every particle velocity.
Flight times are rational. Noise scales are computable positive reals
with supplied rational positive lower bounds. We use the classical almost-everywhere
regular hard-sphere flow: on each bounded time interval almost every
phase point has a finite itinerary of simple, nongrazing binary
collisions, and the flow preserves equilibrium measure
\cite{GallagherSaintRaymondTexier}. The following effective statements
are consequences of that flow theorem and the explicit collision
formulas, rather than additional collision-count estimates.

A bounded observable or preparation is called elementary-computable
here if it has a supplied rational bound and admits uniformly computable
rational interval enclosures on compact rational boxes of continuity,
with mesh errors tending to zero. We allow piecewise descriptions whose
exceptional boundaries are a specified finite union of nonzero
polynomial zero sets. Globally computable continuous functions with
known local moduli, and bounded piecewise polynomial functions, are
examples. Preparations are nonnegative normalized densities of this
class. A finite actual-mark channel has elementary-computable
probabilities $\gamma(w\mid x)$ summing to one. These specifications
exclude arbitrary noncomputable measurable inputs.

\begin{lemma}[Effective full-measure chart exhaustion]
\label{lem:v15-charts}
For any fixed finite collection of controlled words and bounded time
windows, regular collision charts can be enumerated with rational box
certificates. Their union has full equilibrium measure. Integrals of
bounded functions which have computable interval enclosures on these
charts have rational enclosures of arbitrarily small width, provided
a uniform bound for the integrand is supplied. The assertion also
holds with finitely many compact time-integration variables.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a velocity-cutoff box and a bounded time window, energy conservation
gives a computable speed bound. Only finitely many periodic images can
participate in a collision; enumerate all images within that displacement
bound. The candidate time for a pair is a root of
\[
 |q_i-q_j+n\ell+t(v_i-v_j)|^2=d_0^2,
\]
where $\ell$ is the torus side and $d_0$ the diameter. On a simple
nongrazing itinerary, the relevant discriminant is positive, the
selected future root is separated from every competing root, and the
normal relative velocity does not vanish. Reflection of the velocities
is an explicit algebraic function of the positions and velocities at
that root. An already realized contact has its zero root removed; the
outgoing sign specifies the next free flight. In unfolded coordinates
there is no coordinate-wrap event to treat as a physical collision.
A pair with zero relative velocity is handled by a strict positive
separation bound on its whole flight interval, not by division by a
vanishing quadratic coefficient. The same no-contact interval test
handles sufficiently small perturbations when no root is present.

Enumerate finite collision lists, rational velocity and position boxes,
and rational strict margins. Interval arithmetic for algebraic
operations and square roots verifies the selected roots, their order,
nongrazing margins, and absence of another collision before the final
time. On sufficiently small boxes about any regular itinerary with a
noncollision final time, all these strict inequalities hold. This is
because there are finitely many collisions and every relevant gap and
nongrazing angle is positive there. Thus enumeration produces a
neighborhood of every such point. Endpoint-collision sets have measure
zero. Reverse time uses the same construction. Algebraic kicks have
computable inverses and preserve $\mu$, so the finite intersections
needed for controlled words and transformed chart inputs again have
full measure. This proves effective enumerability and exhaustion; it
does not assert a computable uniform collision-count bound.

The equilibrium measure itself is computable on boxes. The configuration
normalizer is the volume of a bounded region defined by finitely many
algebraic separation inequalities, with boundary of Lebesgue measure
zero. Inside and outside rational-box approximations converge to its
volume. A rational interior box provides a strictly positive lower
bound for the normalizer; it can be found by enumeration because the
interior is nonempty. Velocity-box masses are products of computable
Gaussian interval probabilities. Gaussian tails provide effective
cutoffs. These facts give rational upper and lower enclosures for
$\mu$ and for the mass of every box used in the construction.

Replace finitely many accepted boxes by a disjoint dyadic refinement.
Continue enumeration and refinement until the certified lower mass of
their union exceeds $1-\varepsilon$. This search terminates since the
countable union has full measure. On the accepted compact boxes use
interval enclosures and subdivision to bound the integrand's integral;
on the complement use its known absolute bound $M$, at cost at most
$M\varepsilon$. Refining the mass and integration enclosures gives any
specified absolute error. Exceptional input-function boundaries are
also null and can be excluded by strict interval tests for their
nonzero defining polynomials. Compact time variables are handled by
product-box integration; their collision-time sets are null by the finite-itinerary property
and Fubini's theorem.
At no point is an uncertified sample estimate for the omitted set used.
\end{proof}

Let $(\psi_j)$ be a countable uniformly described dense family of
bounded, compact-velocity-supported continuous piecewise
polynomial functions on phase space. For instance, use
rational tent functions on successively finer coordinate boxes.
Restrictions of this family are dense in $L^2(\mu)$. For rational
$\delta>0$ set
\[
 \kappa_\delta(s)=\frac{15}{16\delta}
          (1-s^2/\delta^2)^2\1_{[-\delta,\delta]}(s),\qquad
 v_{j,\delta}=\int\kappa_\delta(s)U(s)\psi_j\,ds.
\]
Let $\mathscr D$ be all rational linear combinations of these vectors.

\begin{theorem}[Constructive microscopic graph data]
\label{thm:v15-constructive-core}
For the fixed-particle equilibrium system just specified, $\mathscr D$
is an enumerable graph core for $L$. Each vector has the exact
representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-effective-core}
 Lv_{j,\delta}=-\int\kappa_\delta'(s)U(s)\psi_j\,ds.
\end{equation}
All inner products required in Definition~\ref{def:v15-effective},
for bounded elementary-computable acquisition observables, have
arbitrarily accurate rational enclosures. The marked Gaussian prefix
masses of every finite trial family in $\mathscr D$ also have such
enclosures. Thus the complete effective-data hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v15-main} holds in this class, including its probability
integration clause.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The kernel is continuously differentiable, compactly supported, has
integral one, and vanishes at its support endpoints. The strong group
identity and integration by parts prove domain membership and
\eqref{eq:v15-effective-core}. If $u\in D(L)$, its mollified orbit
converges to $u$ in graph norm as $\delta\downarrow0$. At fixed
$\delta$, approximating $u$ in $H$ by rational combinations of the
$\psi_j$ approximates its mollification in graph norm, since
\[
 \|A_\delta(u-\psi)\|+\|LA_\delta(u-\psi)\|
 \le (\|\kappa_\delta\|_1+\|\kappa_\delta'\|_1)\|u-\psi\|.
\]
A diagonal choice proves graph density. No decision of linear
independence between formal core vectors is needed by the enriched
search.

For inner products insert the time-integral representations on each
side and use Fubini. The resulting integrands are bounded products of
known kernels and of observables evaluated along finitely many exact
collision itineraries, possibly after an algebraic kick. Known sup-norm
bounds for $\psi_j$, $\kappa_\delta$ and $\kappa_\delta'$ bound these
products. Lemma~\ref{lem:v15-charts} therefore computes their integrals,
including cross terms involving $C_av$ and the given bounded $f_k$.
The signs of $\kappa'$ present no difficulty because absolute bounds
are available.

For completeness, the time-integral value of a core function itself
admits uniform enclosures on each compact regular itinerary chart.
Enclose all collision times there. Away from small strips containing
those times, the algebraic flow formulas and input observables have
uniform interval approximations. The strips contribute at most their
total length times the known kernel and observable bounds. Strict
itinerary margins allow these strips to shrink under subdivision.
Thus both $v(x)$ and $Lv(x)$ are computable continuous functions on
that chart, even if a velocity-dependent $\psi_j$ jumps at a collision.
The integral across its moving jump is controlled by the strip bound,
not by pretending the jump is absent.

Every trial mean is a finite rational combination of such bounded
core functions. Gaussian cell probabilities are computable continuous
functions of that mean, including the two tails, and their derivatives
are bounded by the positive noise scales. The integrand in
\eqref{eq:v15-mass-integral}, multiplied by a bounded preparation and
actual-mark probability, consequently satisfies the hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v15-charts}. The lemma computes its mass enclosures.
It also supplies $L^2$ preparation and trial-norm enclosures. This
verifies all finite operations required by Definition~\ref{def:v15-effective}.
The exhaustive searches may have no useful uniform rate, but each
requested enclosure terminates by the full-measure exhaustion proved
above.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Certified approximation for a colliding physical class]
\label{cor:v15-constructive-physical}
For two systems in Theorem~\ref{thm:v15-constructive-core} with a common
finite control interface, finite parameter and mark alphabets, bounded
elementary-computable preparations and observables, rational positive
noise variances and rational observation times, the procedure of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v15-main} produces convergent same-budget intervals
for their physical intrinsic deficiency. It uses certified collision
charts rather than an exact-flow integration oracle. It does not
require the acquisition observables to be conserved quantities.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The theorem supplies the effective graph data and marked prefix
integrals; Theorem~\ref{thm:v15-enriched} supplies convergent analytic
certificates; the finite report and rational-tree bridges preserve the
resource signature. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v15-main}. One may work in the full labeled $L^2$ space. For symmetric input
data the construction also restricts to its symmetric subspace by
symmetrizing the tent family; the flow and identical-particle kicks
commute with that finite symmetrization.
\end{proof}

The use of a null exceptional set is constructive here because its
regular complement has an effective increasing chart enumeration and
because the probability of each accepted finite cover is certified.
A bare assertion of almost-everywhere computability without those two
properties would not justify the conclusion. Conversely, the argument
is an exhaustion algorithm, not a practical complexity bound or a
uniform estimate in the Boltzmann--Grad regime.
