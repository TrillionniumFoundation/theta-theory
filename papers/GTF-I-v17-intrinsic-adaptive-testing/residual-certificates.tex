\subsection{A posteriori bounds from graph and intervention residuals}
\label{sec:v14-residual}
Strong controlled-word convergence supplies an asymptotic comparison.
For a fixed approximation space one can also bound its actual comparison
error using finite Gram data. We retain the physical setup of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-active}: the particle number, interventions, noise,
step length, and actual initial-mark channel are fixed. These statements
do not require the interventions to map $D(L)$ into itself.

Suppress $n$ and let $V\subset D(L)$ have orthonormal basis
$\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_d$. Identify $V$ with $\mathbb R^d$ by an isometry
$\iota$; complexification gives the same formulas with adjoints. Write
\[
 P=\iota\iota^*,\quad J=\iota^*L\iota,\quad
 B_a=\iota^*C_a\iota,\quad M_a=B_a e^{\Delta J}.
\]
Because $L$ is skew-adjoint and $C_a$ is unitary, $J$ is skew-adjoint,
$e^{sJ}$ is unitary and $M_a$ is a contraction. The two residual Gram
matrices are
\begin{align}
 R&=(L\iota)^*L\iota-J^*J
       =((I-P)L\iota)^*((I-P)L\iota),\label{eq:v14-R}\\
 S_a&=I-B_a^*B_a
       =((I-P)C_a\iota)^*((I-P)C_a\iota).\label{eq:v14-S}
\end{align}
In particular both are positive semidefinite. For a coefficient vector
$c$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-defect}
 d_a(c)=\int_0^\Delta
      \sqrt{(e^{sJ}c)^*R(e^{sJ}c)}\,ds
     +\sqrt{(e^{\Delta J}c)^*S_a(e^{\Delta J}c)}.
\end{equation}
All quantities in this formula are finite-dimensional. For a real
observable $f\in H$ put
\[
 c_f=\iota^*f,\qquad
 \tau_f=(\|f\|_H^2-\|c_f\|^2)^{1/2}.
\]
If $w=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$ and
$c_j=M_{a_{j+1}}\cdots M_{a_k}c_f$ (empty product equal to the identity),
define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-word-residual}
 E_w(f)=\tau_f+\sum_{j=1}^k d_{a_j}(c_j).
\end{equation}
The suffix order agrees with the Koopman product for a physically
chronological word; its reversal would approximate a different dynamics.

\begin{theorem}[Residual control of the active marked experiment]
\label{thm:v14-residual}
For every such space $V$ and every controlled word,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-wordbound}
 \|T_{a_1}\cdots T_{a_k}f
      -\iota M_{a_1}\cdots M_{a_k}c_f\|_H\le E_w(f).
\end{equation}
For the preparations $p_\theta$ with $\sup_\theta\|p_\theta\|_2\le D$
and Gaussian acquisitions of Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-active}, the two
stateless marked feedback deficiencies are bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-physical-certificate}
 \widehat\epsilon_{V,T}
 =\min\left\{1,\frac{D}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
       \sum_{k=1}^T\frac{1}{\sigma_k}
          \left(\sum_{w\in A^k}E_w(f_k)^2\right)^{1/2}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The comparison includes every independent exposed seed and is uniform
over all finite consumer widths. Thus it bounds raw paired risks and
lower supports at unchanged widths. When unrestricted benchmarks are
also paired, the centered risk bound is $2\widehat\epsilon_{V,T}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The orthogonal splitting of $L\iota c$ into its $V$ component
$\iota Jc$ and its orthogonal component proves \eqref{eq:v14-R}.
Unitarity of $C_a$ similarly proves \eqref{eq:v14-S}.
For $v=\iota c$, the path $s\mapsto\iota e^{sJ}c$ lies in the finite
space $V\subset D(L)$ and is differentiable in the graph norm. Hence
variation of constants is legitimate and gives
\[
 U(\Delta)v-\iota e^{\Delta J}c
 =\int_0^\Delta U(\Delta-s)(L\iota-\iota J)e^{sJ}c\,ds.
\]
Taking norms and applying \eqref{eq:v14-R} bounds this difference by
the integral in \eqref{eq:v14-defect}. Apply the unitary $C_a$ and then
add the orthogonal projection error
$\|(I-P)C_a\iota e^{\Delta J}c\|$ to obtain
\[
 \|T_a\iota c-\iota M_ac\|\le d_a(c).
\]
No derivative of $C_a\iota c$ has occurred. First remove
$(I-P)f$ at cost $\tau_f$. Telescope the difference of the two word
products with exact prefixes and approximate suffixes. All prefixes
are isometries and all approximate suffixes are contractions. The
$j$th term is bounded by $d_{a_j}(c_j)$, proving
\eqref{eq:v14-wordbound}.

For the probabilistic assertion use the common initial $x_0$, its
actual conditional mark $W$, and the same visible seed, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-active}. Before the first report mismatch the
selected actions agree. Conditional on $x_0$, the weights of the
matched histories leading to the possible words of length $k$ sum to
at most one. If $e_w(x)$ is the corresponding exact-minus-approximate
mean, the first-mismatch contribution at time $k$ is therefore at most
\[
 \frac{1}{\sigma_k\sqrt{2\pi}}
        \int p_\theta(x)\max_{w\in A^k}|e_w(x)|\,d\mu(x).
\]
This retains the history weights until after taking their maximum,
instead of replacing each by one. Cauchy--Schwarz and
$\max_w|e_w|^2\le\sum_w|e_w|^2$ bound the integral by
$D(\sum_w\|e_w\|_2^2)^{1/2}$. Apply
\eqref{eq:v14-wordbound} and sum the first-mismatch probabilities.
The coupling is symmetric and preserves the whole actual marked
transcript on its matching event, so it proves both stateless
inequalities in \eqref{eq:v14-physical-certificate}. It conditions only
on the original preparation, not on a feedback posterior with a
supposed $L^2$ bound. Conditioning on an independent exposed seed gives
the same uniform estimate. Risk and support statements follow by
composition with any consumer and by taking the appropriate infima.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-data average-risk and presentation certificates]
\label{cor:v14-average-certificate}
In the active regenerative model of
Corollary~\ref{cor:v13-active-average}, a finite age cutoff $H$ gives
an error bound for paired average risks and lower supports, uniform
in consumer width,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-average-certificate}
 \widehat e_{V,H}=
 \eta\sum_{k=0}^{H}(1-\eta)^k\widehat\epsilon_{V,k+1}
          +(1-\eta)^{H+1}.
\end{equation}
The same bound applies to identically centered values; separately
paired unrestricted benchmarks give twice the bound. Finite-horizon
bounds \eqref{eq:v14-physical-certificate}, combined with any other
verified stateless presentation bridges, can be inserted into
\eqref{eq:v14-interval} without changing the intrinsic resource budget.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At age $k$ there are at most $k+1$ acquisitions. Use
Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-residual} at those horizons and bound each remaining
age error by one. The remaining geometric mass is exactly
$(1-\eta)^{H+1}$. The cycle formula in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-cycle} proves \eqref{eq:v14-average-certificate}.
The final assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-stability}. A finite-dimensional
vector model is not automatically a finite report experiment; a further
finite-report bridge must actually be proved before invoking the
rational local testing algorithm. No such bridge is implicit in a
matrix representation.
\end{proof}

The data required in \eqref{eq:v14-R}--\eqref{eq:v14-word-residual} are
inner products of $f,\phi_i,L\phi_i,C_a\phi_i$. Once these data have
rigorous enclosures, the formulas give a posteriori bounds without
knowing the exact controlled orbit. For example, certified numbers
$r_0\ge\|R\|$ and $s_{a,0}\ge\|S_a\|$ imply the simpler bound
\[
 d_a(c)\le(\Delta\sqrt{r_0}+\sqrt{s_{a,0}})\|c\|.
\]
With matrix-entry enclosures, interval arithmetic and this inequality
avoid an uncertified numerical integral. Floating-point Gram entries
alone are not an analytic certificate. Nor is it asserted that these
residual majorants converge for every graph-core sequence: cancellation
may make the actual orbit error small while its residual bound is
large. The convergence theorem remains Lemma~\ref{lem:v13-words};
Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-residual} is a complementary finite-space bound.
This distinction separates a proved limiting approximation from a
checkable error estimate for a particular space.
