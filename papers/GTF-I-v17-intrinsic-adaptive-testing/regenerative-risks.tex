\section{Regenerative average risks}\label{sec:v13-regeneration}
\subsection{The reset signature}
Let the physical state space $X$ be standard Borel, with any actual
persistent hidden mark included in $X$. Reports and actions are finite.
A stationary common finite-register design consists of stochastic
encoder rows and task-controller rows, independent of time; their
product of simplexes is a compact set $\Pi_S$. A fixed finite task set
is part of the row index $j\in J$, where $J$ is compact metrizable.
For each design and row, the joint physical--register transition has
form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-reset}
 P_j^\pi=\eta N_j+(1-\eta)Q_j^\pi,\qquad 0<\eta\le1,
 \qquad N_j(x,m;\cdot)=\nu_j\otimes\delta_{m_0}.
\end{equation}
The independent reset flag is physically supplied; it refreshes the
physical preparation and sets the within-policy memory to $m_0$.
Thus $N_j$ is a constant kernel independent of $\pi$. Actions between
resets may change the physical state. No domination of $Q_j^\pi$ by
one reference measure is assumed.

Let $c_j^\pi:X\times[S]\to[0,1]$ be the expected one-stage marked
cost. We assume the following uniform continuity conditions, using the
supremum over starting states for kernel total variation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-continuity}
 (j,\pi)\mapsto Q_j^\pi\text{ is continuous in }\sup_{x,m}\|\cdot\|_{\rm TV},
 \quad j\mapsto\nu_j\text{ in TV},\quad
 (j,\pi)\mapsto c_j^\pi\text{ in }\|\cdot\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
For fixed physical kernels with finite action and report alphabets,
continuity in $\pi$ follows directly by expanding the finite sums of
encoder and controller probabilities; the physical kernels can be
singular. Row dependence must independently satisfy
\eqref{eq:v13-continuity}. In particular changing a deterministic flow
with the row does not automatically satisfy a total-variation
continuity hypothesis.

\begin{theorem}[Nonsummable average-risk duality]\label{thm:v13-average}
Under \eqref{eq:v13-reset}--\eqref{eq:v13-continuity}, every $(j,\pi)$
has a unique invariant probability $\zeta_j^\pi$. Its average risk
\[
 r_\pi(j)=\zeta_j^\pi c_j^\pi
\]
is the limit of expected Ces\`aro average cost from every initial law.
For $N\ge1$ and $0<\beta<1$ the following errors are uniform in
$j,\pi$, and the initial law $\xi$:
\begin{align}
 \left|\frac1N\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\xi(P_j^\pi)^tc_j^\pi-r_\pi(j)\right|
 &\le\frac1{N\eta},\label{eq:v13-cesaro}\\
 \left|(1-\beta)\sum_{t=0}^\infty\beta^t
              \xi(P_j^\pi)^tc_j^\pi-r_\pi(j)\right|
 &\le\frac{1-\beta}{1-\beta(1-\eta)}.\label{eq:v13-discount}
\end{align}
The map $\pi\mapsto r_\pi$ is continuous into $C(J)$ and has compact
image. The attainable average-risk body under independent selection
of a stationary design is compact and convex in $C(J)$. For continuous
$b:J\to\mathbb R$ its common-design minimax value satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-average-dual}
 \min_{\Lambda\in\mathcal P(\Pi_S)}\max_{j\in J}
       \left(\int r_\pi(j)\,d\Lambda(\pi)-b(j)\right)
 =\max_{w\in\mathcal P(J)}\min_{\pi\in\Pi_S}
       \int_J(r_\pi(j)-b(j))\,dw(j),
\end{equation}
and all displayed extrema are attained. For finite $J$ a selector of
at most $|J|+1$ values realizes every required optimum. More generally
Proposition~\ref{prop:v13-dimension} gives an intrinsic finite-dimensional
selector approximation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A probability kernel contracts total variation. Subtracting two
probabilities in \eqref{eq:v13-reset} cancels the common reset part,
giving
\[
 \|\xi P_j^\pi-\xi'P_j^\pi\|_{\rm TV}
            \le(1-\eta)\|\xi-\xi'\|_{\rm TV}.
\]
The explicit probability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-invariant}
 \zeta_j^\pi=\eta\sum_{k=0}^\infty(1-\eta)^k
             (\nu_j\otimes\delta_{m_0})(Q_j^\pi)^k
\end{equation}
is invariant by direct substitution, with convergence in total
variation. Contraction proves uniqueness and
$\|\xi(P_j^\pi)^t-\zeta_j^\pi\|_{\rm TV}\le(1-\eta)^t$.
Integrating the bounded cost and summing the geometric series proves
\eqref{eq:v13-cesaro} and \eqref{eq:v13-discount}, including their
uniformity.

For two parameter--policy pairs let $P,P'$ denote their kernels and
$\zeta,\zeta'$ their invariant laws. Invariance, the preceding
contraction, and a one-step difference give
\[
 \|\zeta-\zeta'\|_{\rm TV}
 \le(1-\eta)\|\zeta-\zeta'\|_{\rm TV}
              +\sup_{x,m}\|P(x,m;\cdot)-P'(x,m;\cdot)\|_{\rm TV}.
\]
Thus the invariant-law difference is at most the last supremum divided
by $\eta$. Together with the cost difference, this proves joint
continuity of $(j,\pi)\mapsto r_\pi(j)$. Compactness of $J\times\Pi_S$
upgrades it to continuity of $\pi\mapsto r_\pi$ in the uniform norm.
Its image is compact.

Probabilities on compact $\Pi_S$ form a weakly compact set. The
barycenter map into $C(J)$ is norm continuous: approximate the compact
risk image uniformly by finitely many continuous coordinates, or use
uniform equicontinuity of $r_\pi(j)$ and a finite net in $J$.
The image is therefore compact and convex. The payoff
$\int(r_\pi-b)\,d\Lambda\,dw$ is continuous and affine in both
probabilities on compact spaces. Convex minimax gives
\eqref{eq:v13-average-dual}. The inner minimum over a probability on
$\Pi_S$ equals the minimum over $\pi$, and compactness attains it.
A finite-dimensional barycenter is reduced by the affine-dependence
argument already proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main}. This proves the
finite selector assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Which policies are optimized]
The theorem optimizes stationary finite-register common policies with
the reset signature \eqref{eq:v13-reset}. It does not assert that a
stationary policy is optimal among arbitrary nonstationary policies
without that signature. A selector choosing a stationary policy is
sampled once; its mode persists through resets while that mode's
within-policy register is reset. If the selector must be private,
store the mode and the register, paying their product. Conditioned on
the mode, \eqref{eq:v13-cesaro}--\eqref{eq:v13-discount} hold with the
same constants, so they also hold after averaging. The joint chain
including a persistent mode need not have a unique invariant law
across modes, and no such claim is needed.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Cycle comparison before optimization}
Consider two reset experiments with the same reset probability and
reset flag. A comparison within a fresh cycle starts with the specified
physical preparation and initial register. Suppose, for every feedback
design and every independent visible side seed, the joint laws of the
marked information needed to evaluate the age-$k$ stage cost differ
by at most $\varepsilon_k\le1$. Here age zero means the first stage
following a reset; its law includes any reports used at that stage.
The same control law and encoder rows are used on both sides. This is
a uniform channel comparison, not a comparison obtained after separately
optimizing policies.

\begin{theorem}[Regenerative resource transport]\label{thm:v13-cycle}
The resulting paired average risks satisfy, for every consumer width,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v13-cycle}
 \|r_E-r_F\|_\infty\le
       \mathfrak e:=\eta\sum_{k=0}^\infty(1-\eta)^k\varepsilon_k.
\end{equation}
The bound holds with a priced simulator by replacing the consumer width
$S$ by its product with the simulator width and by charging the selector
as in Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main}. For a sequence of stateless two-sided
comparisons with $\varepsilon_{n,k}\to0$ for each fixed $k$,
$\mathfrak e_n\to0$, uniformly over all consumer widths. At every width,
the corresponding average-risk bodies and optimized lower support
values have the same error bound. The minimax values in
\eqref{eq:v13-average-dual} with the same centering $b$ differ by at
most $\mathfrak e_n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Formula~\eqref{eq:v13-invariant} expresses a stationary average risk as
the geometric mixture of costs at each cycle age. The expectation of
a $[0,1]$ marked stage cost changes by at most $\varepsilon_k$ in that
age's joint law, yielding \eqref{eq:v13-cycle}. The proof is uniform in
the consumer because its error hypothesis is uniform before integration.
For a priced transducer, include its retained state in the joint
register and reset it to its specified initial state at the cycle
boundary. The same product construction and conditioning on independent
mode choices applies.

The geometric weights sum to one. Given $\delta>0$, first choose $L$
so their tail beyond $L$ is less than $\delta/2$, and then choose $n$
so the finite weighted sum through $L$ is less than $\delta/2$.
This proves $\mathfrak e_n\to0$ without a rate uniform in $k$.
Pairing each design with the same design on the other side gives the
risk-body comparison. Taking weighted infima or the supremum of
centered row risks proves the optimized statements; taking mixtures
preserves the same bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A nondominated physical kernel]
On $X=[0,1)$, let $Q^a(x,\cdot)=\delta_{(x+a)\bmod1}$ for finitely
many rotations $a$, and let $\nu$ be Lebesgue probability. The transition
$\eta\nu+(1-\eta)Q^a(x,\cdot)$, as $x$ ranges over $X$, is not dominated
by a common $\sigma$-finite measure when $\eta<1$: such a measure would
have to give positive mass to uncountably many singleton points.
A $\sigma$-finite measure has at most countably many positive-mass
atoms, by decomposing into finite-measure sets and positive lower
bounds on atomic mass. Finite report partitions and any common finite
encoder fit Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-average}. This is a nonsummable
controlled risk theorem with a genuinely nondominated physical
transition family, not an application of full-path density compactness.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Why the reset is explicit]
A hidden physical process may mix while a retained controller bit never
changes. Physical mixing alone then gives no uniform contraction on
the joint physical--register space. Equation~\eqref{eq:v13-reset}
requires an actual clearing of that register. Conversely, without a
cycle tail or another uniform long-time condition, finite-prefix
comparison errors need not control average cost: a sequence of
processes that agrees with a zero-cost process for its first $n$ stages
and incurs unit cost forever afterward has vanishing error at each
fixed prefix but average cost one. The positive theorem uses the
explicit geometric cycle law to control this precise obstruction.
\end{remark}
