\subsection{Local testing at the prescribed private budget}
\label{sec:v14-local}
The convex converse in Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main} has an explicit selector
price. We now give a different converse that leaves the private register
unchanged. Its witnesses are finite covers of the policy space by local
testing inequalities, rather than one separating functional on a convex
hull. Throughout this subsection the experiments, horizon, and alphabets
are finite; for effective statements their instrument probabilities are
rational. The prescribed widths and, when present, selector cardinality
are fixed.

Let $\mathcal P$ be the product of the row simplexes of the private
transducer. For a hidden or visible selector with at most $r$ values,
include its simplex and $r$ copies of the transducer rows in $\mathcal P$.
Zero selector weights are permitted. The relevant deficiency has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-objective}
 \delta=\min_{q\in\mathcal P}F(q),\qquad
 F(q)=\max_{i\in I}f_i(q),\qquad 0\le F\le1.
\end{equation}
Here $I$ consists of all parameter, feedback-tree, and marked-event tests.
The empty event is included. For a visible selector a test specifies a
feedback tree and event in each selector fiber. Thus
\[
 f_i(\lambda,q)=\sum_u\lambda_u
       \{Q_{\theta,g_u}(B_u)-P^{q^u}_{\theta,g_u}(B_u)\}.
\]
For a hidden selector the target term is independent of $\lambda$.
Every $f_i$ is affine in each row separately, with rational coefficients.
This follows by expanding complete controlled paths: a time-indexed row
is used at most once along each path. It holds simultaneously for all
feedback tests, not just for open-loop words.

For an integer $M\ge1$, intersect a row simplex $\Delta_{d-1}$ with each
closed coordinate box
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^d[b_j/M,(b_j+1)/M],\qquad b_j\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}.
\]
Discard empty intersections. Products of these row polytopes form a
finite closed cover $\mathcal C_M$ of $\mathcal P$. Overlapping faces
cause no ambiguity. Write $V(C)$ for the products of row vertices of a
cell $C$. These are exactly its vertices. They are rational and are
obtained by setting all but at most one coordinate of each row to a box
endpoint and imposing that the coordinates sum to one. This prescription
also includes lower-dimensional cells and one-point rows.
Define
\begin{align}
 \ell(C)&=\max_{i\in I}\min_{v\in V(C)}f_i(v),&
 u(C)&=\min_{v\in V(C)}F(v),\label{eq:v14-cell}\\
 L_M&=\min_{C\in\mathcal C_M}\ell(C),&
 U_M&=\min_{C\in\mathcal C_M}u(C).\label{eq:v14-bounds}
\end{align}

For time $t$, let $d_t$ be the largest row alphabet cardinality,
including both emitted symbol and next retained label. Put
\[
 \rho_t(M)=\begin{cases}0,&d_t=1,\\
       \min\{1,d_t/(2M)\},&d_t>1,
       \end{cases}\qquad
 b_M=1-\prod_{t=0}^{T}(1-\rho_t(M)).
\]
For a selector row set $s_M=0$ if $r=1$, and
$s_M=\min\{1,r/(2M)\}$ otherwise. The three bounds used below are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-modulus}
 \omega_M^{\rm p}=b_M,\qquad
 \omega_M^{\rm h}=s_M+(1-s_M)b_M,\qquad
 \omega_M^{\rm v}=\min\{1,2s_M+(1-s_M)b_M\}.
\end{equation}
They do not count the number of histories or the number of policy rows.
The visible-selector bound includes a second selector term because the
reference joint experiment changes when its selector law changes.

\begin{theorem}[Complete local testing certificates]\label{thm:v14-local}
For any of the three fixed resource signatures in
\eqref{eq:v14-objective}, the rational quantities
\eqref{eq:v14-bounds} satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-mainbound}
 0\le L_M\le\delta\le U_M\le1,\qquad
 U_M-L_M\le\omega_M.
\end{equation}
The upper witness is an executable rational transducer at the prescribed
widths and selector cardinality. A lower witness consists of one test
$i_C$ for each cell and its inequalities
$f_{i_C}(v)\ge L_M$ at all $v\in V(C)$.

For $M=2^n$, the lower bounds increase and the upper bounds decrease to
$\delta$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-complete}
 \delta=\sup_n\ \max_{(i_C)_{C\in\mathcal C_{2^n}}}
           \min_{C\in\mathcal C_{2^n}}\min_{v\in V(C)}f_{i_C}(v)
       =\inf_n U_{2^n}.
\end{equation}
If $\delta>\varepsilon$, a finite local testing certificate eventually
proves this strict inequality. If $\delta<\varepsilon$, a rational
original-budget simulator eventually proves feasibility below
$\varepsilon$. For rational $\varepsilon$, non-strict feasibility,
including equality, is decidable by real quantifier elimination.
The optimum is algebraic, and an optimal row table can be chosen with
real algebraic entries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Express each row of $q\in C$ as a convex combination of its row vertices.
Expand a separately affine $f_i$ in those combinations, one row at a
time. The resulting weights on $V(C)$ are nonnegative and sum to one.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-vertex}
 \min_{q\in C}f_i(q)=\min_{v\in V(C)}f_i(v).
\end{equation}
It follows that $\ell(C)\le\min_C F\le u(C)$ and hence
$L_M\le\delta\le U_M$. No assertion that $F$ itself is separately affine
has been made. This distinction is essential at a randomized optimum.
The empty event gives $\ell(C)\ge0$.

We next prove the oscillation estimate used for the gap. Two probability
rows in the same $M$-cell differ in total variation by at most
$\rho_t(M)$. Run the two transducers against the same tester and source.
As long as all earlier emitted symbols and retained labels coincide,
the controls and source laws agree. Maximally couple the next joint
output--state row. The conditional chance of a mismatch is at most
$\rho_t(M)$. Thus the chance of any mismatch through time $T$ is at most
$b_M$. On the event of no mismatch the source can be coupled identically,
including its actual mark. This bounds the difference of the probability
of every marked feedback event by $b_M$, uniformly in the parameter and
tester. A finite causal instrument provides precisely the conditional
kernels needed for this coupling.

For a hidden selector first maximally couple its two laws. The selector
mismatch has probability at most $s_M$; on matched fibers the preceding
bound is $b_M$. The difference of any fixed test is at most
$s_M+(1-s_M)b_M$. There is only one executed fiber, so this estimate has
no extra factor $r$. For a visible selector write its fixed test as the
expectation of $a_u(q)=Q_{\theta,g_u}(B_u)-P^{q^u}_{\theta,g_u}(B_u)$.
These numbers lie in $[-1,1]$. On mismatched selector fibers their
difference is at most two; on matched fibers it is at most $b_M$.
Thus its oscillation is at most $2s_M+(1-s_M)b_M$. This estimate does not
forget the selector in either compared experiment.

Choose a vertex $v_*$ minimizing $F$ on $V(C)$ and an active test $i_*$
there. For every other vertex,
\[
 f_{i_*}(v)\ge F(v_*)-\omega'_M=u(C)-\omega'_M,
\]
where $\omega'_M$ is the untruncated corresponding oscillation bound.
It follows that $u(C)-\ell(C)\le\omega'_M$. Since both bounds lie in
$[0,1]$, truncation by one is valid. Choose a cell attaining $L_M$ to
obtain $U_M-L_M\le\omega_M$. A vertex attaining $U_M$ is an original
row table; it is not a mixture of different designs.

Dyadic refinement only replaces each cell by subsets. By
\eqref{eq:v14-vertex}, its descendants cannot lower the minimum of a
fixed test and hence cannot lower $L_M$. Every coarse vertex belongs
to a finer cell and remains an extreme point of that subset; therefore
the set of available vertices can only increase, and $U_M$ cannot
increase. The bounds in \eqref{eq:v14-modulus} tend to zero. Independent
choices of the maximizing test in each of the finitely many cells give
\eqref{eq:v14-complete} and the two strict-decision assertions.

Finally $q\in\mathcal P$ and $f_i(q)\le\varepsilon$ are finitely many
polynomial conditions over the rationals. Quantifier elimination over
real closed fields decides their existential conjunction; this is the
classical effective Tarski--Seidenberg theorem, not a new elimination
algorithm \cite[\S2.1, Theorem 2.1]{Basu2014}. The set of feasible
objective values is a semialgebraic ray with included endpoint $\delta$.
Its endpoint is algebraic over $\mathbb Q$. The nonempty optimum fiber
has a point over the real algebraic numbers by the same real-closed-field
transfer, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Private feasibility is locally test complete]
\label{cor:v14-converse}
At fixed private widths, $\dpr_K(E,F)\le\varepsilon$ if and only if no
finite dyadic local testing certificate has value greater than
$\varepsilon$. For any finite prescribed list of resource profiles, all
strictly feasible and strictly infeasible profiles can be separated by
rational original-budget witnesses and finite local certificates.
With rational tolerance, exact feasibility and the minimal elements in
that finite list are decidable. These assertions require neither a free
public selector nor a convex private behavior set.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first claim is \eqref{eq:v14-complete}. Apply the strict parts of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-local} to each profile in the finite list and its
quantifier-elimination clause to profiles on the boundary. The partial
order is coordinatewise state and selector cost. Enumeration of feasible
profiles then determines the minimal elements. It need not produce a
single least profile, and no polynomial running time is asserted.
\end{proof}

For the three-symbol channel $V_*$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:v13-rank},
$\dmix_2=0$ whereas $\dpr_2\ge1/(4\sqrt3)>1/8$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-local} therefore eventually gives a finite local
certificate exceeding $1/8$ at private width two. A single convex
testing separator cannot have that conclusion, since the hidden
convex hull already contains the target. This example identifies the
logical work done by localization; it does not claim a polynomial
bound on the size of the certificate.

The exact use of row vertices and real algebraic decision procedures
is classical. So is the unrestricted filtered randomization criterion:
The precise filtered antecedent is
\cite[Lemma 1 and Theorem 5]{Weisshaupt2002}, discussed in the introduction. The conclusion here is instead a complete system
of local tests with executable witnesses at that fixed register, together
with an explicit feedback modulus. These are different conclusions even
on the finite delayed-channel slice. The original-source audit records
separately the still-unverified comparison with Norberg's full proof and
with the original incomplete-machine minimization papers.

\subsection{Transferring intrinsic certificates across presentations}
\label{sec:v14-stability}
For compatible marked experiments write
$E\simeq_a\widetilde E$ when two stateless, seed-free, marked causal
channels, one in each direction, have feedback error at most $a$.
Their report timing and action interfaces are the same. On a visible
signature they compare the complete joint interface with the independent
selector. These are assumptions about executable channels, not about
unseeded marginal distributions.

\begin{theorem}[Original-budget stability of intrinsic certificates]
\label{thm:v14-stability}
Suppose $E\simeq_a\widetilde E$ and
$F\simeq_b\widetilde F$. For private, fixed hidden-selector, and fixed
visible-selector deficiencies at the same budget,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-stability}
 |\delta_K(E,F)-\delta_K(\widetilde E,\widetilde F)|\le a+b.
\end{equation}
If the approximating experiments are finite and rational, their local
certificates consequently give the rigorous interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v14-interval}
 \max\{0,L_M-a-b\}\ \le\ \delta_K(E,F)\ \le\
       \min\{1,U_M+a+b\}.
\end{equation}
An upper witness consists of the rational transducer and the two
stateless bridge channels and has the original persistent resource
budget. A lower certificate excludes every simulator at that budget
for the original experiments whenever $L_M>a+b+\varepsilon$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compose $E\to\widetilde E$, a near-optimal
$\widetilde E\to\widetilde F$ transducer, and
$\widetilde F\to F$. The optimized composition inequality in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main}, or its identical channel proof on measurable
carriers, adds the three errors and multiplies the widths by $1,K,1$.
The bridges introduce no selector, so a prescribed hidden or visible
cardinality remains unchanged. Reverse the bridges for the opposite
inequality and take the infima. Equation~\eqref{eq:v14-interval} follows
from Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-local}. For the last assertion any original
simulator would, after reverse bridging, contradict the finite lower
certificate. Thus the lower statement does not assume the existence of
an optimal simulator on a nonfinite carrier.
\end{proof}
