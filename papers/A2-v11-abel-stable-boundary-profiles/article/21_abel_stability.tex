\section{The Abel geometry of the boundary invariant}
\label{sec:v11-abel}
The scalar boundary law is obtained by two residual-time integrations
of an autoconvolution. Its natural inverse topology is therefore better
expressed on the integrated flux than on the law divided by $d^2$.
For two profiles define
\[
 H(V,W)(d)=d^2T(V,W)(d),\qquad H_V=H(V,V),
\]
where $T$ is the normalized positive integral
\eqref{eq:v9-energy-integral}. This notation agrees with
\eqref{eq:v10-forward-map} below. For $H\in C^3([0,D])$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v11-abel-transform}
 \begin{split}
 (\mathscr AH)(x)
 &=\frac\pi2\left\{H''(0)+\sqrt{x}
              \int_0^x\frac{H'''(s)}{\sqrt{x-s}}\,\dd s\right\},
                  \qquad x>0,\\
 (\mathscr AH)(0)&=\frac\pi2 H''(0),\qquad
 |H|_{\mathscr A}=\|\mathscr AH\|_{C^0([0,D])}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The operator is linear. Equivalently, with $k_*(x)=x^{-1/2}$ and
$Q=(\pi/2)H''$, it is $\sqrt{x}(k_* *Q)'$. Formula
\eqref{eq:v11-abel-transform}, not a derivative of a singular
expression at zero, defines its endpoint value. The transform is
unchanged by adding an affine function to $H$.

\begin{theorem}[A nonlinear inverse in the Abel flux norm]
\label{thm:v11-abel-inverse}
Let $V,W\in C^3([0,D])$, $V(0)=W(0)=1$, and suppose
$\|V'\|_\infty,\|W'\|_\infty\le B$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v11-two-sided}
 \begin{split}
 |H_V-H_W|_{\mathscr A}
       &\le 2\pi(1+BD)\|V-W\|_\infty,\\
 \|V-W\|_\infty
       &\le \frac{1+BD e^{BD/2}}{2\pi}
                                |H_V-H_W|_{\mathscr A}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
In particular, on every bounded normalized positive profile class of
Section~\ref{sec:v10-acquisition}, the physical same-type integrated
law is a bi-Lipschitz coordinate for the full energy profile in this
specified data norm. No smallness of $V-1$ is assumed.

The nullspace of $|\cdot|_{\mathscr A}$ on $C^3$ consists exactly of
affine functions. Consequently it is a norm on
$\{H\in C^3:H(0)=H'(0)=0\}$, which contains all profile-law
differences. For arbitrary $H\in C^3$ one also has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v11-mixed-bound}
 |H|_{\mathscr A}\le\frac\pi2|H''(0)|
   +2\pi\sqrt D\,
       \sqrt{\|H''-H''(0)\|_\infty\|H'''\|_\infty}.
\end{equation}
The same statements apply to $C^{m-1,1}$ profiles, $m\ge4$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $u=V-W$, $f=x^{-1/2}u$ and $a=x^{-1/2}(V+W)$. The
normalization and the first derivative bounds make $f$ continuous,
with $f(0)=0$. The convolution identities in
Section~\ref{sec:v9-compatibility} give, for
$Q=(\pi/2)(H_V-H_W)''$,
\[
 Q=a*f,\qquad k_* *a=2\pi+R,\qquad R(0)=0.
\]
Indeed,
\[
 R(x)=\int_0^1
       \frac{V(xt)+W(xt)-2}{\sqrt{t(1-t)}}\,\dd t,
 \qquad \|R'\|_\infty\le\pi B.
\]
The constant follows from
$\int_0^1t\{t(1-t)\}^{-1/2}\dd t=\pi/2$.
Differentiating $k_* *Q=(2\pi+R)*f$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v11-weighted-volterra}
 \mathscr A(H_V-H_W)(x)
  =2\pi u(x)+\sqrt{x}\int_0^xR'(x-s)\frac{u(s)}{\sqrt{s}}\,\dd s.
\end{equation}
All kernels are locally integrable; the differentiation follows first
by writing the constant convolution as $2\pi\int_0^x f$ and then
using $R(0)=0$. The displayed identity extends to zero by continuity.
Its integral term has absolute value at most
$2\pi Bx\|u\|_\infty$, proving the first estimate.

Let $E=|H_V-H_W|_{\mathscr A}/(2\pi)$ and
$v(x)=\int_0^x|u(s)|s^{-1/2}\dd s$. The same identity implies
\[
 |u(x)|\le E+\frac B2\sqrt{x}\,v(x),\qquad
 v'(x)\le E x^{-1/2}+\frac B2v(x)
\]
almost everywhere. Multiplication by $e^{-Bx/2}$ and integration,
with $v(0)=0$, yield
\[
 v(x)\le E\int_0^x e^{B(x-s)/2}s^{-1/2}\dd s
                       \le2E\sqrt{x}\,e^{Bx/2}.
\]
Substitution proves the inverse estimate, including its stated
constant. This argument uses the weighted forcing directly; it does
not estimate $f$ by dividing a uniform forcing bound by $\sqrt{x}$.

For the nullspace assertion, $\mathscr AH=0$ first gives $H''(0)=0$.
For $x>0$ it then gives $k_* *H'''=0$. Convolution once more with
$k_*$, whose square is the constant $\pi$, gives
$\pi(H''-H''(0))=0$. Thus $H$ is affine. Conversely every affine
function is annihilated by the definition.

To prove \eqref{eq:v11-mixed-bound}, write
$q=H''-H''(0)$, $M_0=\|q\|_\infty$ and
$M_1=\|q'\|_\infty$. If either is zero the assertion follows
directly. Otherwise $q(0)=0$ implies
$\ell=M_0/M_1\le D$. For $x\le\ell$,
$|(k_* *q')(x)|\le2M_1\sqrt{x}$. For $x>\ell$, split the
integral at $x-\ell$. Integration by parts on $[0,x-\ell]$,
using $q(0)=0$, bounds that portion by $2M_0/\sqrt\ell$;
direct integration on the remaining interval bounds it by
$2M_1\sqrt\ell$. Hence
\[
 \|k_* *q'\|_\infty\le4\sqrt{M_0M_1}.
\]
Insert this into \eqref{eq:v11-abel-transform}. The entire proof uses
only the displayed derivatives and locally integrable convolutions,
so also applies in the stated $C^{m-1,1}$ class.
\end{proof}

The product on the right of \eqref{eq:v11-mixed-bound} is an estimate,
not the definition of a discrepancy norm. In particular, our
regularization will minimize the seminorm of the linear transform
$\mathscr A$, which satisfies the triangle inequality. Treating a
geometric mean of two norms as a norm would not give that argument.

\subsection{An exact half-order benchmark}
The following calculation locates the loss between the two neighboring
integer derivative norms. It does not identify an optimal statistical
experiment or a sharp endpoint H\"older space.

\begin{proposition}[Linearization and fixed-class instability]
\label{prop:v11-half-order}
Let $F(V)=T(V,V)$. Its linearization at the constant profile is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v11-linearization}
 DF_1[w](d)=\frac{16}{3\pi d^2}
              \int_0^d x^{-1/2}(d-x)^{3/2}w(x)\,\dd x.
\end{equation}
For $w(x)=x^n$, $n\ge0$, this equals $\lambda_n d^n$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v11-monomial}
 \lambda_n=\frac{4(1/2)_n}{(n+2)!}
  =\frac{4\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n(n+1)(n+2)}
                   \sim\frac4{\sqrt\pi}n^{-5/2}.
\end{equation}
At every such monomial the integrated transform is exactly inverted:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v11-exact-linear-inverse}
               \mathscr A(\lambda_n d^{n+2})=2\pi d^n.
\end{equation}
Fix $D=1$, $m\ge4$, $B>1$ and $0<\beta<1$. On the single class
$\mathcal K_m(B,\beta;1)$ defined below there is no uniform Lipschitz
inverse from the $C^2$ norm of $F(V)-F(W)$, or of $H_V-H_W$, to
$\|V-W\|_\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Bilinearity gives $DF_1[w]=2T(1,w)$. Integrating out the unperturbed
variable in the simplex, with
$\int_0^{d-x}(d-x-y)y^{-1/2}\dd y=\tfrac43(d-x)^{3/2}$,
gives \eqref{eq:v11-linearization}. The beta integral, or
\eqref{eq:v9-beta-coefficients}, gives the first expression for
$\lambda_n$. The central binomial identity and Stirling's formula
give the other two. For $n\ge1$,
\[
 \sqrt{x}\,(k_* *(x^n)')(x)
       =n\,\mathrm B(n,1/2)x^n
       =\frac{n!\sqrt\pi}{\Gamma(n+1/2)}x^n.
\]
After two derivatives of $\lambda_n x^{n+2}$, all factors in
\eqref{eq:v11-abel-transform} cancel to $2\pi$. For $n=0$, its
endpoint term gives the same identity directly.

Choose $a>0$ with $(m+1)a<B-1$ and $a<1-\beta$ and, for
$n\ge m$, put $V_n^\pm(x)=1\pm a n^{-m}x^n$. Every derivative
of the perturbation through order $m$ is bounded by $a n^{r-m}$.
The last such bound controls the Lipschitz constant of the preceding
derivative. Thus both signs belong to this same sum-norm class.
Bilinearity cancels the quadratic perturbation terms exactly:
\[
 F(V_n^+)-F(V_n^-)=2a n^{-m}\lambda_n d^n.
\]
For the maximum convention on $C^2$, the ratio of profile error to
law error is $\{\lambda_n n(n-1)\}^{-1}$; for integrated-law
error it is $\{\lambda_n(n+1)(n+2)\}^{-1}$. Both tend to
infinity like a positive multiple of $\sqrt n$. A sum convention
changes these ratios by bounded factors. This proves the assertion
on a fixed nontrivial class, rather than on classes with growing
smoothness bounds.
\end{proof}

The monomial and mixed-norm benchmarks were given in the independent
memorandum~\cite{A2ReviewV10}. The two-sided flux norm and its use
in the sampling and calibration theorems below make a further
observation statement. The alternatives in
Proposition~\ref{prop:v11-half-order} are abstract profile alternatives;
no billiard realization or preparation lower bound is deduced from
them. The genuinely physical input remains the relative flux theorem
and the geometric meaning of the profiles in
\eqref{eq:v9-energy-profile}.
