\begin{abstract}
For completely positive instruments from $M_d$ to $M_n$ with $m$
outcomes, we determine the high-resolution description exponent
$s=d^2(mn^2-1)$ in diamond norm and give an exact rational codec using
$s$ independent coordinates. On a fixed strictly positive Choi body,
a reusable description of one memoryless instrument, accurate at fixed
error against arbitrary common
adaptive quantum testers for $N$ uses has optimal length
$(s/2)\log_2N+O(1)$. The proof combines a two-point classical programme
bound with repeated Choi tests; references, feedback and bounded public
stopping are included. Boundary unitary channels exhibit a different
continuity scale. These are description-length laws, not unknown-channel
learning or physical classical simulation theorems. We retain the
reference-stable Choi compiler and variable-description numerical
trajectory upper bound, with polynomial validation charged separately
from streaming storage and expected sampling time. The spectral entropy,
sharp fixed-error widths, exponential-accuracy bounds and fixed-program
streaming-space results remain under their respective full-action and
output-interface hypotheses.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle
\noindent\textit{Operational interfaces.}
Description compilation returns a mathematical quantum instrument;
numerical streaming processes fully specified matrix data. Neither is a
physical classical device acting on an unknown quantum input, and
neither is merely a transcript-only simulation task. The new code lengths
charge the reusable description, with fixed public dimensions and error.
The old label-width invariant leaves the clock, real tables, construction,
arithmetic and exact sampling uncharged; the separate bit-space models
charge these computational resources as specified. All joint-state errors
are unhalved trace norms; transcript total variation is half the classical
block trace norm.
