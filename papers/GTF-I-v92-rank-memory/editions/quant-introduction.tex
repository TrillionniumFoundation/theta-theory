\section{Introduction and realization model}

A finite quantum instrument may be described once and then used many
times. The number of description bits needed to withstand all common
adaptive tests depends on both the number of uses and the required
accuracy. Our main results determine this dependence, first on a
strictly positive Choi body and then throughout the singular boundary
of the preparation family.

For a fixed positive margin and intrinsic affine dimension
$s=d^2(mn^2-1)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:jointcode68} gives
\[
 \log_2\mathcal M_N(\delta;a)
 =\frac s2\log_2N+s\log_2(1/\delta)+O_{d,n,m,a}(1),
 \qquad N\ge1,\quad 0<\delta\le1/32.
\]
The error is the unhalved trace distance of the final joint state,
maximized over one common causal quantum tester. The same memoryless
instrument is reused. Entangled references, quantum memory, feedback
and bounded public stopping are allowed. The remainder is independent
of both $N$ and $\delta$, not merely a fixed-error asymptotic.

\pagebreak
Theorem~\ref{thm:rankentropy68} proves a complementary boundary law.
An outcome-retaining preparation instrument sends
$X$ to $(\tr(X)\sigma_y)_y$, where $\sigma_y\ge0$ and
$\sum_y\tr\sigma_y=1$. For rank bounds $r_y$, the coefficient is
\[
                  v=\sum_y r_y(2n-r_y)-1.
\]
Its optimal description length is
$(v/2)\log_2N+v\log_2(1/\delta)+O(1)$ uniformly on the same
range. No lower eigenvalue bound is imposed, and zero outcomes are
included. The explicit factor code in Theorem~\ref{thm:factorcode68}
has rational decoded matrices, preserves zero outcomes, and never
increases a block rank. Singular Cholesky pivots and exact integer
square roots suffice; irrational factors are not stored by the encoder.

The lower argument is a local binary-testing estimate which remains
useful at arbitrarily small separation. It supplements, rather than
replaces, the earlier exponential testing estimate. The upper argument
on a positive body uses the two-point programme lemma. For preparation
instruments an exact product-state reduction replaces this positive
margin argument; purification controls the rational factor code at
all ranks. The elementary testing, purification and covering methods
are credited separately from the combined coding statements. In
particular, quantum population compression \cite{YBCH68} concerns
unknown physical copies and a different memory interface.

Theorem~\ref{thm:entropy67} and the intrinsic coordinate codec remain
the single-use description results. Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptiveentropy67}
retains its fixed-error statement as a precursor to the joint law.
No action spectral gap occurs in any of these description theorems.
They are not mutable-workspace lower bounds for arbitrary quantum
trajectory simulators. The remaining computational and geometric
results retain their original hypotheses and full proofs.

A finite-precision description of an operation and a finite-memory
simulation of its trajectory are different objects. We first state the
additional distinction needed here. Theorem~\ref{thm:choiround66}
constructs rational Choi blocks with an exact joint partial trace and
an explicit diamond-error bound. Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptivediamond66}
then controls every common adaptive tester, including an entangled
reference and public stopping. The compiler never rounds an unknown
input state. Theorem~\ref{thm:uniformchoi66}, separately, is a numerical
program whose dimension and rational Choi description are input data.
It charges their size rather than hiding it in fixed-input constants.
Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior66} gives posterior control in true-law
expectation and high probability at a chosen stopping time.

The earlier sharp space law remains a lower statement about each fixed
program, with a program-dependent starting horizon and constant,
under a full action gap and cap hypotheses. The new general upper
construction does not remove those hypotheses from the lower theorem.
The following development retains the complete spectral and positive
realization arguments on which this distinction rests.


The problem is to retain a legal numerical state while reading a
physical command stream only once. For a fixed rational unitary
alphabet, Theorem~\ref{thm:matrixspace65} gives
\[
 s(N,L)=\Theta_{\rm input}\bigl(\min\{N,L+\log_2(N+1)\}\bigr)
\]
under a full Koopman action gap and a cap estimate uniform over
all unit directions. All matrices, their dimension and denominators,
the pure seed and the spectral data are fixed before $N,L$ vary.
The lower bound holds for mean correctness of a randomized fixed
program. The upper program instead gives pointwise trace-norm
accuracy and legal outputs at every prefix. The explicit rational
adjacent-coordinate alphabets yield this law in every fixed dimension.

The positive rounding lemma also permits real measurement disturbance.
Theorem~\ref{thm:instrumentstream65} treats fixed Gaussian-rational
Kraus instruments with an external adaptive classical controller.
It bounds the trace norm of the entire classical--quantum history,
hence the transcript total variation, in the same space order from
above. Zero and arbitrarily small outcome probabilities are allowed.
This is a construction, not a general finite-state classification.
Its lower-bound counterpart applies to families containing an
expanding unitary subsystem and retaining the legal numerical-state
interface.

The lower proof passes through a more permissive resource: a finite
stochastic register with uncharged horizon-dependent real tables.
Such a register can encode a continuum of probability rows.
Consequently, orbit-point counting proves a converse only at special
extreme-output endpoints. Away from those endpoints we compare loss
of conditional-centroid mass with spectral dissipation of a smoothed
directional entropy. We first define this auxiliary realization model.

\begin{definition}[Clocked numerical realization]\label{def:machine54}
Let $H$ be a compact group, $\mathcal A$ a finite nonempty command
alphabet, and $F_{s,j}:H\to Y_j$ continuous interfaces for finite seed
and query sets. Each $Y_j$ is a compact convex set in a finite-dimensional
normed space. At horizon $N$ choose finite nonempty registers
$S_0,\ldots,S_N$, initialization rows $E_s$, stochastic matrices
$T_{t,a}:S_{t-1}\to S_t$, and terminal decoder maps $D_j:S_N\to Y_j$.
The realized output on $w=a_1\cdots a_N$ is
$E_sT_{1,a_1}\cdots T_{N,a_N}D_j$, and the physical product is
$u_w=u_{a_N}\cdots u_{a_1}$. Write $W_{N,\varepsilon}$ for the least
$\max_t|S_t|$ with worst-word, worst-seed and worst-query error at most
$\varepsilon$.
\end{definition}
Every advertised persistent label, including unused padding and retained
private information, is counted. The horizon, external clock, arbitrary
real tables, their construction and lookup, exact arithmetic and exact
atomic sampling are uncharged in this primary invariant. A sampled
answer register is a separate resource. A numerical density-matrix
output is not quantum-state preparation. The rational compiler prices
finite descriptions and fair-bit sampling separately.

Let $R$ be a fixed-point-free orthogonal representation of a compact
connected Lie group. Its least unit-orbit dimension $p_*$ is the optimal
exponent of a cap bound uniform in every direction, including normalized
conditional centroids outside the target orbit. Under a full Koopman action $L^2$ spectral
gap, Theorem~\ref{thm:driftoccupation62}
gives
\[
 \#\{1\le t\le N:|S_t|\le k\}\le C_\varepsilon k^{2/p_*},
 \qquad W_{N,\varepsilon}\ge c_\varepsilon N^{p_*/2}.
\]
There is no restriction on intervening widths. The entropy potential
is carried only along the narrow cuts, so there is no logarithmic
mixing cost at each cut. Fixed physical fillers rotate this potential
without changing it; no identity command or return word is needed.
This is a relative entropy against round measure, not the Shannon
entropy of the hidden register. A quadratic inner approximation of the target
orbit hull supplies a positive common-row upper machine of width
$O(N^{q/2})$, where $q$ is that orbit's real dimension. When $p_*=q$,
these are matching power laws. For legal density matrices of size $d$,
they give $\Theta(N^{d-1})$ at every fixed subcritical tolerance, with
strict amplitude or with positive error at unit amplitude.

The exact boundary is very different. One fixed rational unitary pair
and pure seed has profile $2\cdot3^t-1$ at cut $t$, still necessary for
$\varepsilon\le625^{-N}/16$, but width $\Theta(N)$ at every fixed
$0<\varepsilon<1/\sqrt2$. A second experiment, given by three explicit
rational Bloch rotations and their inverses, has exact profile $5^t$,
stable for $\varepsilon\le25^{-N}/16$. The classical
Lubotzky--Phillips--Sarnak theorem supplies the exact spherical spectral
norm $\sqrt5/3$ for this second alphabet. Theorem~\ref{thm:effectivelps61}
therefore gives the fully numerical bounds
\[
 \frac{(1-\sqrt2\varepsilon)N}{6500}
 \le W_{N,\varepsilon}\le52+104N/\varepsilon.
\]
The constants are not optimal. For the first rational unitary pair, the
constant imported from Bourgain--Gamburd remains non-effective. Neither
freeness nor a spectral gap is inferred from finite enumeration.

Retaining the small loss $\tau=1-\rho+\varepsilon$ in the entropy
budget gives a genuinely joint statement. When $p_*=q$ and the residual
amplitude stays bounded away from zero, Theorem~\ref{thm:jointlaw62}
proves
\[
 W_N(\rho_N,\varepsilon_N)=\Theta((N/\tau_N)^{q/2})
 \quad\hbox{if }\log(1/\tau_N)=o(N).
\]
For the six nonidentity Bloch commands alone,
Theorem~\ref{thm:noidle62} gives explicit joint bounds and the exact
profile $5^t$ without adding an idle instruction. It yields
$\Theta(N/\varepsilon_N)$ for positive subexponential-accuracy sequences.
The finer label-width theorem determines the exponential transition rate itself.
Under a full spherical Ramanujan bound for a symmetric $2r$-command
alphabet, Theorem~\ref{thm:crossover63} proves, uniformly in the error,
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\exp[-C\sqrt{N\log(N+2)}]
      \min\{(2r-1)^N,((N+1)/\varepsilon)^{p/2}\}\\
 &\hspace{2em}\le W_{N,\varepsilon}
      \le C'\min\{(2r-1)^N,((N+1)/\varepsilon)^{p/2}\}.
 \end{split}
\]
where $p$ is the sphere dimension. A corresponding inequality counts
arbitrarily placed narrow cuts. For the six rational Bloch rotations,
\[
 \frac{\log(1/\varepsilon_N)}N\longrightarrow a
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \frac{\log W_{N,\varepsilon_N}}N\longrightarrow\min\{a,\log5\}.
\]
The mechanism is different from using a long mixing block at every
cut with a fixed entropy defect. We retain the logarithmic affinity
bound for entropy loss. Applied to a nonbacktracking average of length
$b$, its defect is $b\log(2r-1)-O(\log b)$. The cost of transporting
centroid directions is kept with its small error factor. Choosing
$b$ of order $\sqrt{N\log N}$ then loses only a sublinear amount in
the logarithm of width. Classical radial-polynomial estimates and the
R\'enyi affinity bound are explicitly credited. No claim is made that
the subexponential multiplicative loss or the exact saturation interval
is optimal.

The chain of estimates is
\[
 \text{centroid-mass loss}\ \Longrightarrow\
 \text{transport of directions}\ \Longrightarrow\
 \text{entropy budget at narrow cuts}.
\]
The kernel, transport inequality and entropy dissipation are proved
below. Entropy--transport continuity and arithmetic spectral gaps are
credited to their classical antecedents. The earlier mixing-block proof
is retained as an alternative weaker bound, not used to hide a
logarithm in the sharp result.

The finite-label converse has a further computational consequence.
For the fixed six rational rotations and $0<\varepsilon\le1/4$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:streamspace64}
determines the uniform one-pass space order
\[
 \Theta\bigl(\min\{N,\log(N+1)+\log(1/\varepsilon)\}\bigr).
\]
Every writable register, temporary integer and private address is charged.
The converse permits arbitrary randomization and only asks that the mean
numerical output be accurate. A single deterministic program attains
the upper order by truncating rational rotations toward zero, or by
retaining exact denominator-five numerators when that is cheaper.
The returned matrix is always positive semidefinite. Thus the result
does not rely on real-table advice, exact sampling, or an uncharged
arithmetic instruction. It is a separate space theorem, not an assertion
that the primary width invariant already measures workspace.

A prescribed joint terminal law alone does not generate a causal
observation process. The repeatable classical-probe compiler and its
strict-positivity hypotheses remain separate from the quantum-instrument
construction in Section~\ref{sec:instruments65}, which allows disturbance
and uses no outcome-probability floor. The structural companion's
classification still requires repeatable nondisturbing probes.
Section~\ref{sec:cutpoint65} compares the numerical interface with
strict-cutpoint simulation, including the 2026 results of Chen--Wu.
Neither companion nor any predecessor PDF is a proof dependency here.
