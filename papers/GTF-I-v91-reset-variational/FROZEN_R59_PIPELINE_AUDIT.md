## 3. Device and strategy interface

### 3.1 Unknown device

An ordered measurement is a tuple

```text
E_j >= 0,
sum_j E_j = I.
```

One use is the memoryless, input-consuming, classical-output channel

```text
M_E(rho) = sum_j tr(E_j rho) |j><j|.
```

No residual device quantum system is available.

All operational distances use unhalved trace norm and lie in `[0,2]`.

### 3.2 Complete acquisition group

A group of size `n_r` includes:

- all queried input systems;
- every reference and purification;
- all quantum memory used by adaptive controls inside the group; and
- the complete accessible output delivered to the receiver.

The resource cut is therefore not defined on probe marginals alone.

### 3.3 Independent profile tester

For profile `(n_1,...,n_l)`, the initial state is a tensor product across complete groups. Entanglement inside a group is unrestricted. All final outputs and references may be processed jointly.

Public mixtures are allowed with their common label retained.

### 3.4 Prescribed-profile reset tester

At round `r`, the reserved size is the public integer `n_r`, independent of history. Conditional on the classical history:

- preparation and within-block controls may depend on that history;
- the whole fresh acquisition is tensor-independent of all old receiver memory;
- the rule is common under both hypotheses;
- old conditional receiver states may differ;
- receiver memory dimension is unrestricted;
- the receiver may jointly process old memory and new output after the block; and
- no old quantum subsystem enters a later fresh acquisition.

### 3.5 General reset policy

At history `h`, a policy reserves a positive integer `n(h)`, may stop at block boundaries, and obeys hard pathwise bounds.

Every unused internal slot remains charged.

The same formal decision rule is defined at histories null under one hypothesis.

### 3.6 Resources

For one policy,

```text

```

These are not:

## 5. Audit of the unequal-signal lemma

### 5.1 Statement

Let independent signs have base law `P_0` with zero bias and alternative biases `u_r`, with public envelopes

```text
alpha x_r <= u_r <= x_r,
0 < alpha <= 1,
0 <= x_r <= 1/2.
```

The lemma constructs one binary randomized readout depending only on `x` and obtains

```text
(alpha/64) min{1,sqrt(sum x_r^2)}.
```

### 5.2 Construction

Put

```text
S = sum x_r^2,
A = max{sqrt(S),S},
t_r = x_r/(16A),
T(X)=sin(sum t_r X_r).
```

The `S=0` case is separated before division.

### 5.3 Size estimates

Because `A>=sqrt(S)`,

```text
sum t_r^2 <= 1/256.
```

Also each `t_r<=1/16`.

### 5.4 Complex expectation

Under the alternative,

```text
E exp(i sum t_r X_r)
 = product_r (cos t_r + 2 i u_r sin t_r)
 = R exp(i phi).
```

Each factor has positive real part.

The modulus satisfies

```text
R >= product cos t_r
  >= 1 - (1/2)sum t_r^2
  >= 1/2.
```

The product-to-sum inequality is valid because all cosine factors lie in `[0,1]` and `1-product(1-a_r)<=sum a_r`.

### 5.5 Phase estimates

For `t<=1/16`,

```text
t <= tan t <= 2t.
```

The arguments `2u_r tan t_r` lie in `[0,1]`, and on this interval

```text
v/2 <= arctan v <= v.
```

Therefore

```text
alpha S/(16A) <= phi <= S/(4A) <= 1/4.
```

No phase-wrapping ambiguity remains.

### 5.6 Separation

Since `sin phi>=phi/2` on `[0,1/4]`,

```text
E_u T = R sin phi >= alpha S/(64A).
```

Under `P_0`, symmetry gives `E_0 T=0`.

Because `T` lies in `[-1,1]`, accepting with probability `(1+T)/2` is a legal randomized binary readout. The unhalved binary `l1` separation is exactly the expectation difference.

Finally

```text
S/A = min{1,sqrt(S)}.
```

### 5.7 Audit conclusion

**Pass.** The lemma is finite, uniform in the actual biases, and uses no unknown-remainder advice. Constants are coarse and should not be described as sharp.

## 10. Audit of certified reset deviations

### 10.1 Hypothesis

At every acquisition history, the implemented preparation channel has the same input/output systems as the ideal map

```text
R -> R tensor sigma_h
```

and differs by at most `delta_h` in unhalved diamond norm, including all references.

The largest terminal-path sum is at most `epsilon`.

### 10.2 One-hypothesis hybrid

Fix one device. For arbitrary positive incoming `R`, current replacement costs at most

```text
delta_h tr(R).
```

The ideal block and receiver instrument are trace preserving when all classical branches are retained. Child traces sum to `tr(R)`.

Backward induction therefore bounds all remaining continuation differences by the remaining path allowance times `tr(R)`.

For a normalized initial state, the final ideal and implemented outputs differ by at most `epsilon`.

### 10.3 Two hypotheses

Triangle inequality gives

```text
D_tilde(pi;E,F)<=D(pi;E,F)+2epsilon,
```

capped at two.

### 10.4 Countable branching

The proof uses nonnegative trace sums over a hard finite depth. Finite truncation and monotone convergence justify the countable direct-sum convention.

### 10.5 Scope

The proposition does not derive the channel certificate from:

- probe marginal separability;
- a finite policy graph;
- an average calibration error;
- a dimension declaration; or
- a finite regression.

A fixed positive `epsilon` can dominate a signal tending to zero.

### 10.6 Audit conclusion

**Pass.** This is a standard but correctly stated hybrid stability result.

A symmetric absolute-difference version follows if the same comparison is available in the reverse direction; the current one-sided form suffices for the manuscript.
