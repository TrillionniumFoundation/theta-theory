
\section{Round-fourteen positive closure: two-topology cotangents and a common form bundle}
\label{sec:r14-c2}

The measure-theoretic cotangent topology and the strong spectral/graph
topology are not identified.  The first has invariant signed Radon measures
as its dual; the second has distributional duals and is used only for
response.  Memory is differentiated through a uniformly coercive family of
closed forms on one real common domain.

\subsection{The weighted strict quotient}

Let \(E\) be a Polish path space and \(W\ge1\) a continuous coercive weight.
Put
\[
 C_W(E)=\{F\in C(E):F/W\text{ vanishes at weighted infinity}\},
\]
with the weighted strict topology \(\beta_W\).  Let
\(\mathcal M_W(E)\) be the signed Radon measures with
\(\int W\,d|\mu|<\infty\).  Define
\[
 \mathscr N_{\rm inv}
 =
 \overline{\operatorname{span}}^{\beta_W}
 \{U-U\circ\Theta_t:U\in C_W(E),\ t\in\mathbb Q_+\}.
\]
Constants are not included.

\begin{theorem}[Invariant signed-Radon dual]
\label{thm:r14-c2-radon}
The continuous dual of \(C_W(E)\) is \(\mathcal M_W(E)\).
The continuous dual of
\(C_W(E)/\mathscr N_{\rm inv}\) is the vector space
\[
 \mathcal M_W^{\rm inv}(E)
 =\{\mu\in\mathcal M_W(E):\Theta_t{}_\#\mu=\mu,\ t\ge0\}.
\]
Invariant probabilities form only the normalized positive slice of this
vector space.  Two functions have the same integral under every invariant
probability exactly when their difference lies in
\(\mathscr N_{\rm inv}\); equality up to one common constant corresponds to
\(\mathbb R1+\mathscr N_{\rm inv}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The weighted Riesz representation theorem identifies every
\(\beta_W\)-continuous functional with a signed Radon measure of finite
\(W\)-moment.  The annihilator of the closed coboundary span consists exactly
of measures satisfying
\(\mu(U)=\mu(U\circ\Theta_t)\) for rational \(t\), and continuity in time
extends this to all \(t\).  Hahn--Banach gives the converse annihilator
identity.  Testing the constant function distinguishes equality of all
integrals from equality modulo one common constant.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The strong response space}

For each platform let \(\mathscr A_W^m\subset C_W(E)\) be its weighted
H\"older/anisotropic/graph algebra.  Its norm contains dynamical regularity,
so its continuous dual may contain distributions.  Let
\[
 \iota_m:\mathscr A_W^m\longrightarrow C_W(E)
\]
be the continuous inclusion.

\begin{proposition}[Two-topology cotangent functor]
\label{prop:r14-c2-two-topology}
Pullback by \(\iota_m\) embeds invariant Radon cotangents into
\((\mathscr A_W^m)^*\), but does not exhaust the strong dual.
Variational phases are taken in the strict Radon dual, while spectral
derivatives and seam/contact currents are taken in the strong dual.  Every
crossing between the two is the explicit pullback \(\iota_m^*\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Continuity of \(\iota_m\) gives the dual pullback.  Derivatives of point
evaluation, seam traces, and graph boundary forms are continuous on the
strong space but need not be Radon measures, showing that the inclusion can
be strict.  The stated typing is therefore forced.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Full pressure-functional rigidity}

For a potential \(F\), let
\[
 Q_F(H)=P(F+H)-P(F)
\]
on a strong source neighborhood.

\begin{theorem}[Platform-typed rigidity]
\label{thm:r14-c2-rigidity}
Suppose \(Q_F(H)=Q_G(H)\) for every \(H\) in a neighborhood of zero.
Then the equilibrium linear functionals of \(F\) and \(G\) agree on the
strict Radon quotient.

On the mixing Sinai symbolic platform, if \(F,G\) are dynamically H\"older,
then
\[
 F-G=c+U-U\circ\Theta
\]
by the periodic-orbit Liv\v{s}ic theorem.  On the finite-horizon hard-sphere
platform, the corresponding conclusion is the full balance-coboundary
identity of B3 on the declared short-time source space.  No platform-wide
Liv\v{s}ic theorem is imported from one model to the other.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the identity at every small \(H\).  The exposed equilibrium
functional is the derivative of pressure, so the two families of equilibrium
Radon functionals agree.  At \(H=0\) their invariant integrals agree on all
directions.

For the Sinai coding, equality of the Gibbs functional under all local
perturbations implies equality of conditional Jacobians.  Taking products
around a periodic word shows that the periodic sums of \(F-G\) equal one
constant times the period.  The H\"older Liv\v{s}ic construction on the
mixing Markov extension gives the coboundary.

For hard spheres, B3 proves that a source annihilating every local covariance
and every balanced variation is an endpoint/transport/collision
coboundary.  Applying that theorem to the derivative identity gives the
stated model-specific quotient.
\end{proof}

Equality of one scalar pressure value is never used.

\subsection{A real common closed form domain}

Fix a regular parameter chart \(\eta\in U\).  On an algebraic cylinder core
\(\mathcal C\), let \(a_\eta\) be the sectorial form of the Doob or kinetic
generator.  Assume the estimates proved in A4/B4:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Re a_\eta(F,F)+c\|F\|_{\eta}^2
 &\asymp
 \Re a_{\eta_0}(F,F)+c\|F\|_{\eta_0}^2,\\
 |a_\eta(F,G)-a_{\eta_0}(F,G)|
 &\le\epsilon\|F\|_V\|G\|_V ,
 \end{aligned}
\]
uniformly on the chart.  Define the real norm
\[
 \|F\|_V^2=
 \Re a_{\eta_0}(F,F)+c\|F\|_{\eta_0}^2
\]
and let \(V\) be the completion of \(\mathcal C\).

\begin{theorem}[Common-domain type-(B) form bundle]
\label{thm:r14-c2-form}
The embedding \(V\hookrightarrow H_\eta\) is dense and continuous for every
\(\eta\in U\), and the closures of \(a_\eta\) all have the same form domain
\(V\).  The maps
\[
 \eta\mapsto a_\eta\in\mathcal L(V,V^*)
\]
are \(C^q\), and
\[
 D(z-L_\eta)^{-1}
 =(z-L_\eta)^{-1}(DL_\eta)(z-L_\eta)^{-1}
\]
holds as a bounded composition
\(V^*\to V\to V^*\to V\).  The norm uses the real coercive part, never the
generally complex number \(a_\eta(F,F)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Uniform equivalence of the coercive real form norms makes the completion of
\(\mathcal C\) independent of \(\eta\).  Since \(\mathcal C\) is the graph
core proved in A4 or B4, it is dense in each \(H_\eta\); equivalence preserves
density.  The relative form estimates and their parameter derivatives give
bounded maps \(V\to V^*\).  Kato's type-(B) form theorem constructs the
m-sectorial generators and their resolvents.  Differentiate the weak
resolvent equation
\[
 z(u,v)_{H_\eta}+a_\eta(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle
\]
on the fixed domain \(V\) to obtain the formula.
\end{proof}

This avoids both an impossible regularity-losing Kato ODE and an unsupported
finite-Gram identification of the full generator domain.

\subsection{Resolved observables and memory response}

Let \(R_\eta\subset V\) be a finite-dimensional family of physical resolved
observables after quotienting constants and conserved null modes.  Its
correlation Gram matrix is uniformly positive on a smaller chart, so a
\(C^q\) orthonormal frame exists.  Let \(P_\eta\) be the corresponding
finite-rank projection in \(H_\eta\).

\begin{theorem}[Covariant compressed-memory derivative]
\label{thm:r14-c2-memory}
With the common form-domain resolvent of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r14-c2-form}, define
\[
 C_\eta(z)=P_\eta(z-L_\eta)^{-1}P_\eta,\qquad
 \widehat K_\eta(z)
 =zP_\eta-P_\eta L_\eta P_\eta-C_\eta(z)^{-1}.
\]
On every compact subset avoiding the genuine Schur-complement poles,
\(\widehat K_\eta(z)\) is \(C^{q-1}\) in \(\eta\).  Its derivative contains
the three explicit terms
\[
 DP_\eta,\qquad
 (z-L_\eta)^{-1}(DL_\eta)(z-L_\eta)^{-1},
 \qquad
 -C_\eta^{-1}(DC_\eta)C_\eta^{-1}.
\]
After the A4 principal-part decomposition, differentiation commutes with
inverse Laplace transformation in the exponentially weighted distribution
space.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite Gram matrix gives a smooth projection because its smallest
eigenvalue is uniformly positive after the null quotient.  Apply the form
resolvent identity and the ordinary inverse-matrix rule.  A4 supplies
integrable vertical bounds for the final Schur complement after pole--zero
cancellation; the same bounds hold for one parameter derivative by the form
estimate.  Dominated Bromwich inversion proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Typed contraction derivatives}

Let \(\mathcal C:X\to Y\) be a measurable state contraction and let
\[
 Q_{\mathcal C}(\varphi)=Q(\varphi\circ\mathcal C).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Source and state chain rules]
\label{thm:r14-c2-chain}
For a source perturbation \(h:Y\to\mathbb R\),
\[
 DQ_{\mathcal C}(\varphi)[h]
 =
 DQ(\varphi\circ\mathcal C)[h\circ\mathcal C].
\]
If separately \(\mathcal C_\eta\) and \(\varphi_\eta\) vary with a parameter,
then the state derivative is
\[
 {d\over d\eta}
 (\varphi_\eta\circ\mathcal C_\eta)
 =
 (\partial_\eta\varphi_\eta)\circ\mathcal C_\eta
 +D\varphi_\eta(\mathcal C_\eta)
  \,\partial_\eta\mathcal C_\eta .
\]
The two formulas are not identified.  Their second derivatives are obtained
by differentiating the same typed maps.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first identity is the chain rule for the linear pullback of source
functions.  The second is the ordinary state/parameter chain rule.  Their
domains are different, which proves the typing assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Optional projections and stochastic representations}

Let \(M_t^\varepsilon\) be the exact complete-history likelihood martingale
and let \(\mathcal G_t^\varepsilon\) be the resolved filtration.

\begin{theorem}[Resolved likelihood limit]
\label{thm:r14-c2-likelihood}
Assume the A4 conditional-kernel rough convergence and the B3 uniform
\(L^{1+\delta}\) likelihood bound.  Then
\[
 \mathbb E[M_t^\varepsilon\mid\mathcal G_t^\varepsilon]
\]
converges stably to the optional projection of the limiting
Cameron--Martin exponential.  On the nondegenerate resolved Brownian
filtration this projection is the Girsanov stochastic exponential, and its
logarithmic value solves the quadratic BSDE with the declared terminal
condition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A4 gives joint convergence of conditional kernels and enhanced paths; the
uniform \(L^{1+\delta}\) bound gives uniform integrability.  Approximate the
conditional expectation by bounded finite-history cylinders, pass to the
limit, and remove the approximation using the kernel convergence.  The
Brownian martingale representation theorem identifies the optional
projection as a stochastic exponential.  It\^o's formula for its logarithm
gives the quadratic BSDE, whose bounded solution is unique by exponential
transformation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-fourteen C2 closure]
\label{thm:r14-c2-main}
The series has a correctly typed invariant signed-Radon quotient, a separate
strong response dual, platform-specific full-pressure rigidity, a real common
closed form bundle for memory response, and distinct source/state
contraction derivatives with the resolved likelihood representation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r14-c2-radon},
\ref{thm:r14-c2-rigidity}, \ref{thm:r14-c2-form},
\ref{thm:r14-c2-memory}, \ref{thm:r14-c2-chain}, and
\ref{thm:r14-c2-likelihood}, with
Proposition~\ref{prop:r14-c2-two-topology}.
\end{proof}
