\section{Round-three positive closure: platform-labelled cotangent functors}
\label{sec:r3-c2}

There is no unlabelled parent law spanning physically different systems.  The
universal object is a typed variational functor acting componentwise on a
disjoint union of platforms.  This section constructs the strict dual, proves
the required continuity or exponential approximation of the mechanical
maps, derives pressure derivatives from the proved spectral or cluster
packets, and supplies explicit likelihood, diffusion, BSDE, and memory
commutation statements.

\subsection{The platform coproduct}

Let \(\mathfrak A\) index the platforms actually constructed in the series:
the periodic Sinai path platform, the A1 Hamiltonian impact platform, and the
hard-sphere Boltzmann--Grad platform.  Define
\[
 \mathfrak P
 =\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak A}
 \{\alpha\}\times\mathcal P_w(\Omega_\alpha),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak I(\alpha,\nu)=I_\alpha(\nu).
\]
The label has the discrete topology.  No cost is attached to the label because
no probability law randomly changes the physical platform.

\begin{theorem}[Typed contraction functor]
\label{thm:r3-c2-functor}
Let \(C_\alpha:\mathcal P_w(\Omega_\alpha)\to E_\alpha\) be continuous, or
admit an exponentially good continuous approximation under the microscopic
laws of platform \(\alpha\).  Then
\[
 I_{\alpha,C}(a)
 =\inf\{I_\alpha(\nu):C_\alpha(\nu)=a\}
\]
is the contraction rate on that platform.  A map between two different
labels may be used only when a separate exponentially good scaling theorem
between their microscopic laws has been proved.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On a fixed labelled component this is the ordinary contraction theorem, with
the extended version for exponentially good approximations.  The coproduct
topology makes every component open and closed, so no minimizing sequence can
change labels.  A cross-platform conclusion therefore requires an actual map
between the two families of laws; notation alone cannot create one.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Mechanical maps on the Sinai component}

On graph-completed Sinai paths, define homological displacement, physical
clock, bounded occupation, and a collision statistic by truncating at
collision depth \(L\) and distance \(\delta\) from singularities.

\begin{lemma}[Exponential continuity ledger]
\label{lem:r3-c2-continuity}
Each of the following maps has continuous truncations whose approximation
error is exponentially good on every rate sublevel:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item collision and physical homological currents;
\item roof and occupation integrals with the A3 weight;
\item collision activity and bounded impact marks;
\item bounded local path work.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently every listed Sinai observable satisfies the contraction theorem
of Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-c2-functor}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For bounded depth and away from singular curves, the collision expansion,
roof integration, path evaluation, and every bounded impact mark are uniformly
continuous.  A3 proves that long blocks, singularity neighborhoods, and clock
residuals have arbitrarily large exponential cost in the ordered
\((L,\delta)\) limit.  Homological displacement is constant on regular
branches and is therefore included.  The same shield controls collision
activity because the number of retained contacts is bounded by the truncated
return length.
\end{proof}

The A1 branch current is continuous on its common symbolic path space.  The B2
density and actual-collision observables are continuous in the weighted weak
state used by its joint LDP.  Thus all claimed componentwise contractions now
have a verified map.

\subsection{A strict path-potential topology}

Fix a continuous weight \(W\ge1\) with compact sublevels and put
\[
 C_W(\Omega)
 =\{F\in C(\Omega):F/W\text{ vanishes at weighted infinity}\}.
\]
Give it the weighted strict topology \(\beta_W\), generated by seminorms
\[
 p_g(F)=\|gF/W\|_\infty,
 \qquad g\in C_0(\Omega),
\]
together with the bounded-set control \(\sup|F|/W\).  Let
\(\mathcal M_W(\Omega)\) be countably additive signed Radon measures with
\(\int Wd|\nu|<\infty\).

\begin{theorem}[Strict dual and compact phase sets]
\label{thm:r3-c2-strict}
The continuous dual of \((C_W(\Omega),\beta_W)\) is
\(\mathcal M_W(\Omega)\) under integration.  Let
\[
 Q(F)=\sup_\nu\{\nu(F)-I(\nu)\}
\]
for a good weighted path rate.  On every \(\beta_W\)-bounded source set,
\(Q\) is convex and continuous, and
\[
 \partial_{\beta_W}Q(F)
 =\operatorname*{argmax}_\nu\{\nu(F)-I(\nu)\}.
\]
The subdifferential is a nonempty compact set of countably additive path
probabilities.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(L\) is \(\beta_W\)-continuous, its restrictions to compact sublevels of
\(W\) are sup-norm continuous and hence represented by compatible Radon
measures.  Bounded-set continuity makes their weighted total variations
uniformly summable, so they paste to one member of \(\mathcal M_W\).  The
converse follows directly from weighted tightness and dominated convergence.

Goodness of \(I\) and the weight coercivity make the variational supremum
attained on a compact rate sublevel.  The usual convex subgradient inequality
shows that every maximizer is a subgradient.  Conversely, separation in the
strict dual represents every subgradient by a Radon measure; testing constants
and positive functions makes it a probability, and Fenchel equality makes it
a maximizer.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The closed coboundary quotient}

On the Sinai component use the A3 dynamically H\"older weighted algebra
\(\mathscr A_\beta\) and its closed subspace
\[
 \mathscr N_\beta
 =\overline{\operatorname{span}}
 \left(
 \mathbb R\mathbf1+
 \{U-U\circ\Theta_t:t\in\mathbb Q,
 U\in\mathscr A_\beta\}
 \right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Pressure cotangents]
\label{thm:r3-c2-cotangent}
Long-time excess pressure descends to the Hausdorff quotient
\(\mathscr A_\beta/\mathscr N_\beta\).  Two potentials have identical
integrals under every invariant finite-weight phase exactly when their
difference belongs to \(\mathscr N_\beta\).  At an exposed phase, the pressure
tangent is its countably additive path law; at coexistence the tangent set is
the compact convex set of maximizing phases from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-c2-strict}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Time averages of a rational-time coboundary have a bounded endpoint remainder,
so their pressures differ only by the constant part.  The annihilator theorem
proved in A3 gives the converse.  The tangent statements follow from the
strict-dual subdifferential identity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{First and second derivatives from model packets}

Let \(F,G\) be dynamically H\"older path directions in the common complex
spectral ball of A2.  Let \(Q_R\) be the physical pressure root.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral pressure response]
\label{thm:r3-c2-response}
On every simple Sinai phase branch,
\[
 DQ_R(\Psi)F=\mathbb P_R^\Psi(F)
\]
and
\[
 D^2Q_R(\Psi)(F,G)
 =\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\mathbb P_R^\Psi}
 (F,G\circ\Theta_t)\,dt.
\]
The integral is absolutely convergent and the derivatives are continuous in
\(R\).  On the B2 hard-sphere source ball, the analogous first and second
joint density--collision derivatives exist and equal the B3 mean and
covariance.  No second derivative is asserted solely from uniqueness of a
variational maximizer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For Sinai paths, A2 gives an analytic simple eigenvalue and analytic left and
right eigenvectors for higher-block twists.  Differentiate the eigenvalue
once.  Differentiating the eigenprojector produces the reduced resolvent
series, whose exponential spectral decay sums to the bilateral Green--Kubo
integral.  Uniform spectral constants give absolute convergence and
\(R\)-continuity.  For hard spheres, B2 normal convergence and B3 microscopic
normalization give the stated derivatives.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Entropic rigidity with an operational calibration}

Let \(\mathfrak C_t\) be a monotone, cash-additive, strictly relevant,
strongly time-consistent scalar certainty equivalent generated by one
\(C^2\) utility.

\begin{theorem}[Rigidity and canonical-cocycle calibration]
\label{thm:r3-c2-rigidity}
The family is either conditional expectation or
\[
 \mathfrak C_t(X)
 =\vartheta^{-1}\log
 \mathbb E[e^{\vartheta X}\mid\mathcal F_t]
\]
with one time-independent \(\vartheta\ne0\).  The axioms alone do not choose
\(\vartheta\).  If, in addition, the likelihood-ratio cocycle is required to
be the mechanically prepared canonical cocycle generated by
\(\theta G\), and work is transported in the same cotangent bundle, then
\(\vartheta=\theta\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Cash additivity gives the translation functional equation for the utility.
Twice differentiating shows that absolute risk aversion is constant, yielding
an affine or exponential utility.  The conditional tower makes the exponential
coefficient independent of the time split.  Under the extra canonical-cocycle
condition, applying both likelihood ratios to the generator \(G\) gives
\(e^{\vartheta G}/Ee^{\vartheta G}=e^{\theta G}/Ee^{\theta G}\); nonconstancy
of \(G\) forces \(\vartheta=\theta\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Likelihood ratios through the proved Sinai diffusion tangent}

Let the A4 homological tangent be
\[
 X_t=x+bt+\sigma W_t,
 \qquad A=\sigma\sigma^T>0,
\]
and let \(\Phi\in C_b^3(\mathbb R^d)\).  Put
\[
 u(t,x)=\theta^{-1}\log
 \mathbb E\exp\{\theta\Phi(X_T^{t,x})\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Convergent likelihood, Girsanov, and BSDE diagrams]
\label{thm:r3-c2-girsanov}
The finite-horizon deterministic path likelihood ratios
\[
 M_t^\varepsilon
 =\frac{\mathbb E[e^{\theta\Phi(X_T^\varepsilon)}
 \mid\mathcal F_t]}{
 \mathbb E e^{\theta\Phi(X_T^\varepsilon)}}
\]
converge jointly with the A4 rough diffusion tangent to
\[
 M_t=
 \mathcal E\left(
 \int_0^t\theta\sigma^TDu(s,X_s)dW_s
 \right).
\]
Under the tilted law,
\[
 dX_t=(b+\theta A Du(t,X_t))dt+\sigma dW_t^\theta.
\]
Moreover \((Y_t,Z_t)=(u(t,X_t),\sigma^TDu(t,X_t))\) is the unique bounded
solution of
\[
 Y_t=\Phi(X_T)+\int_t^T\frac\theta2|Z_s|^2ds-
 \int_t^TZ_sdW_s.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A4 proves joint rough-path convergence and a complex exponential-moment bound
on a neighborhood of the origin.  Conditional convergence on cylinder
filtrations and uniform integrability therefore pass the deterministic
likelihood ratios to the diffusion likelihood ratio.  Gaussian convolution
makes \(u\) a bounded classical solution of
\[
 \partial_tu+b\cdot Du+	frac12A:D^2u
 +\tfrac\theta2Du^TADu=0.
\]
It\^o's formula gives the stochastic exponential and the drift shift.  The
same formula gives the BSDE.  Boundedness of \(u\) and \(Du\) gives uniqueness
by the exponential transform.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A commutative memory--pressure identity}

Let \(P\) be a finite resolved projection under one prepared Sinai phase, let
\(L_\Psi\) be the tilted Koopman/Feynman--Kac generator, and let
\(\mathcal E_t^\Psi\) be the A4 nonlinear history semigroup.

\begin{theorem}[Memory is the Laplace tangent of history pressure]
\label{thm:r3-c2-memory}
For resolved linear observables \(A,B\),
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-zt}
 D\mathcal E_t^\Psi(0)[A](B)\,dt
 =\langle B,P(z-L_\Psi)^{-1}PA\rangle,
 \qquad \Re z>0.
\]
Consequently the compressed-resolvent memory transform
\[
 \widehat K_\Psi(z)
 =zP-PL_\Psi P-
 [P(z-L_\Psi)^{-1}P]^{-1}
\]
is obtained by linearizing the nonlinear history pressure and then taking its
Laplace transform.  Linearizing first, projecting, and forming the A4 memory
kernel gives the same operator.  Source differentiation changes both sides by
the same Duhamel insertion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the Feynman--Kac formula at zero source.  The derivative is the
prepared conditional expectation of the resolved observable propagated for
time \(t\).  Pairing with \(B\) gives
\(\langle B,Pe^{tL_\Psi}PA\rangle\).  Laplace transformation gives the
compressed resolvent.  The formula for \(\widehat K_\Psi\) is the algebraic
identity proved in A4.  A further source derivative on either side is the same
Duhamel integral, proving the commutation statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-three C2 closure]
\label{thm:r3-c2-main}
The series has a platform-labelled contraction functor, verified mechanical
maps, a strict path-potential dual with countably additive phase laws, a closed
coboundary cotangent quotient, model-derived first and second pressure
responses, operational entropic calibration, a proved deterministic-to-
diffusion likelihood/BSDE limit, and an exact memory--pressure commutative
identity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r3-c2-functor},
\ref{thm:r3-c2-strict}, \ref{thm:r3-c2-cotangent},
\ref{thm:r3-c2-response}, \ref{thm:r3-c2-rigidity},
\ref{thm:r3-c2-girsanov}, and \ref{thm:r3-c2-memory}.
\end{proof}
