\section{Round-six positive closure: coercive typed contractions and history--Doob representations}
\label{sec:r6-c2}

Every physical platform retains its label.  Weighted pressures are considered
only on source domains controlled by an explicit rate--moment coercivity
inequality.  Finite likelihoods are exact conditional-expectation martingales
on the complete history filtration.  The memory identity is paired in one
declared Doob Hilbert space and is constructed directly from the Doob
generator; no unproved identification with an untitled Koopman compression is
used.

\subsection{The platform coproduct and source direct sum}

Let \(\mathfrak A\) index the constructed platforms: the A1 Hamiltonian port
system, the Sinai collision/physical path system, and the hard-sphere
Boltzmann--Grad density/contact system.  For each \(\alpha\), let
\(\Omega_\alpha\) be its Polish graph-completed path space, let
\(W_\alpha\ge1\) be continuous with compact sublevels, and let
\(I_\alpha\) be its good rate.  Assume the proved platform coercivity
\[
I_\alpha(\nu)\ge a_\alpha\nu(W_\alpha)-b_\alpha,
\qquad a_\alpha>0.
\]
For hard spheres this is read on each bounded Maxwellian source chart and
then by lower-semicontinuous exhaustion; for Sinai paths it follows from the
A3 recurrent--defect rate.

Define
\[
\mathfrak P=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak A}
\{\alpha\}\times\mathcal P_{W_\alpha}(\Omega_\alpha),
\qquad
\mathfrak I(\alpha,\nu)=I_\alpha(\nu).
\]
The source space is the locally convex direct sum
\[
\mathfrak X=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathfrak A}
(C_{W_\alpha,0}(\Omega_\alpha),\beta_{W_\alpha}),
\]
whose continuous dual is the product of the weighted Radon measure spaces.
A physical phase occupies one labelled component; the product dual is a
bookkeeping object and does not randomize the platform label.

\begin{theorem}[Coercive strict pressure and phase compactness]
\label{thm:r6-c2-strict}
For \(F\in C_{W_\alpha,0}\) with
\(\|F/W_\alpha\|_\infty<a_\alpha\),
\[
Q_\alpha(F)=\sup_\nu\{\nu(F)-I_\alpha(\nu)\}
\]
is finite, strictly continuous on bounded subsets of every smaller source
ball, and attains its supremum on a compact set.  Its strict subdifferential is
exactly
\[
\partial Q_\alpha(F)=
\operatorname*{argmax}_\nu
\{\nu(F)-I_\alpha(\nu)\},
\]
a compact convex set of countably additive weighted Radon probabilities.
The componentwise pressures define a continuous convex functional on each
bounded set of the locally convex direct sum \(\mathfrak X\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(c<a_\alpha\) with \(|F|\le cW_\alpha+C\).  Then
\[
\nu(F)-I_\alpha(\nu)
\le C+b_\alpha-(a_\alpha-c)\nu(W_\alpha),
\]
so maximizing sequences have uniformly bounded \(W_\alpha\)-moment and lie in
one compact rate sublevel.  Strict convergence on a bounded source set is
uniform on that compact set, proving continuity and attainment.  The standard
Fenchel equality shows that every maximizer is a subgradient.  Conversely, the
weighted-strict dual is the Radon measure space; testing constants and positive
functions makes a subgradient a probability, and Fenchel equality makes it a
maximizer.  Direct-sum continuity is componentwise because bounded sets have
only finitely many active components.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Typed contraction maps}

A platform map
\(C_\alpha:\mathcal P_{W_\alpha}(\Omega_\alpha)\to E_\alpha\) is admitted if
it is continuous on rate sublevels or has continuous approximants
\(C_{\alpha,n}\) satisfying
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
\frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}\log
\mathbb P_\varepsilon
\{d(C_\alpha,C_{\alpha,n})>\delta\}=-\infty.
\]
For Sinai paths, A3 supplies such shields for collision expansion, physical
clock, occupation, homology, and bounded local work.  For hard spheres,
density/contact linear maps are continuous in the B2 weighted weak topology.
The A1 current is continuous on its common symbolic factor and has the
physical trace realization proved there.

\begin{theorem}[Platform-labelled contraction functor]
\label{thm:r6-c2-contraction}
Every admitted map has rate
\[
I_{\alpha,C}(y)=\inf_{C_\alpha(\nu)=y}I_\alpha(\nu).
\]
For a continuous linear observable map with adjoint \(C_\alpha^*\),
\[
Q_{\alpha,C}(\eta)=Q_\alpha(C_\alpha^*\eta).
\]
First and second pressure derivatives commute with the map on every local
analytic phase chart.  A cross-platform statement is valid only after a
separate scaling theorem supplies an actual map between the two labelled
microscopic law families.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the ordinary or extended contraction theorem on one open-and-closed
labelled component.  The finite-volume pressure identity follows from
\(\langle\eta,CX\rangle=\langle C^*\eta,X\rangle\); local uniform analytic
convergence permits differentiation.  Since the coproduct topology prevents a
sequence from changing labels, no cross-platform contraction exists without a
separate microscopic scaling map.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Closed coboundary cotangents and pressure responses}

On a stationary path component let \(\mathscr A_W\) be the weighted
dynamically Hölder algebra and let
\[
\mathscr N_W=\overline{\operatorname{span}}^{\mathscr A_W}
\left(
\mathbb R\mathbf1+
\{U-U\circ\Theta_t:U\in\mathscr A_W,\ t\in\mathbb Q\}
\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Path cotangent quotient]
\label{thm:r6-c2-cotangent}
The quotient \(\mathscr A_W/\mathscr N_W\) is Hausdorff.  Two potentials have
the same integral under every invariant finite-weight phase if and only if
they have the same quotient class.  On a simple Sinai phase, the first
pressure derivative is the prepared path law and the second is the absolutely
convergent Green--Kubo covariance derived from the A2 spectral projector.  On
a regular hard-sphere phase, the corresponding derivatives are the B3 exact
mean and quotient covariance.  At coexistence, the strict subdifferential is
the compact maximizing phase set of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r6-c2-strict}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Closedness makes the quotient Hausdorff.  Invariant laws annihilate rational-
time coboundaries.  Conversely, if a quotient class is nonzero, Hahn--Banach
supplies a continuous strict-dual separator.  Averaging it over rational time
shifts yields an invariant signed Radon functional; its Jordan components,
after balancing their equal total masses, give invariant weighted
probabilities separating the class.  The response formulas follow by
differentiating the A2 simple eigenvalue or the B2/B3 local analytic pressure.
The strict subgradient statement is
Theorem~\ref{thm:r6-c2-strict}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact history likelihood martingales}

Let \(X^\varepsilon\) be a resolved slow functional of a deterministic
platform and let \(\mathcal F_t^{\rm hist}\) be its complete resolved-history
filtration.  For a bounded Lipschitz functional \(\Phi\) of a finite future
enhanced path window, define
\[
M_t^\varepsilon=
\frac{
\mathbb E[e^{\theta\Phi(X^\varepsilon)}\mid
\mathcal F_t^{\rm hist}]
}{
\mathbb E e^{\theta\Phi(X^\varepsilon)}
}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact finite likelihood]
\label{lem:r6-c2-likelihood}
The process \(M^\varepsilon\) is a positive mean-one uniformly integrable
martingale.  It is represented by the complete-history kernel
\[
M_t^\varepsilon=
\frac{K_{t,T}^\varepsilon
(e^{\theta\Phi})(H_t^\varepsilon)}
{\mathbb E e^{\theta\Phi(X^\varepsilon)}}.
\]
No present-state Markov identity is assumed at finite \(\varepsilon\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The numerator is the optional projection of the bounded positive terminal
variable.  The conditional-expectation tower gives the martingale property,
and boundedness of \(\Phi\) gives uniform integrability.  Regular conditional
probability on the standard Borel history space gives the kernel
representation.
\end{proof}

The A4 conditional-kernel theorem states that on compact history sets these
kernels converge uniformly, stably with initial history variables, to the
kernel of the area-corrected rough diffusion.  B2/B3 exponential containment
supplies compact-history localization on the hard-sphere side whenever a
separate kinetic-to-diffusion scaling is used.

\begin{theorem}[Optional-projection convergence]
\label{thm:r6-c2-optional}
For every bounded Lipschitz finite-window functional \(\Phi\),
\[
(X^\varepsilon,M^\varepsilon)\Longrightarrow(X,M)
\]
stably in path space, where
\[
M_t=
\frac{
\mathbb E[e^{\theta\Phi(X)}\mid\mathcal F_t^X]
}{
\mathbb E e^{\theta\Phi(X)}
}.
\]
If \(\Phi=\phi(X_T)\), the limit depends on the present diffusion state; for a
general future-path functional it is the corresponding path-kernel on the
augmented Markov state and is not replaced by an endpoint semigroup.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Localize to a compact history set whose complement has arbitrarily small
probability uniformly in \(\varepsilon\).  On that set, A4 gives uniform
conditional-kernel convergence for the enhanced slow path.  The denominator
converges by bounded convergence.  Stable convergence follows by multiplying
with any bounded initial-history variable, which is included in the A4
conditional theorem.  Remove the localization.  The final distinction is
simply whether the limiting conditional functional is measurable from the
present Markov state or requires a path-augmented state.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Girsanov and BSDE on a smooth diffusion chart}

Assume now
\[
dX_t=b(X_t)dt+\sigma(X_t)dW_t,
\qquad A=\sigma\sigma^T,
\]
with bounded \(C_b^3\) coefficients, uniformly elliptic \(A\), and
\(\phi\in C_b^3\).  Put
\[
u(t,x)=\theta^{-1}\log
\mathbb E_{t,x}e^{\theta\phi(X_T)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Likelihood--Girsanov--BSDE representation]
\label{thm:r6-c2-girsanov}
The likelihood limit in Theorem~\ref{thm:r6-c2-optional} is
\[
M_t=\mathcal E\left(
\int_0^t\theta\sigma^TDu(s,X_s)dW_s
\right).
\]
Under the tilted law,
\[
dX_t=(b+\theta A Du)(t,X_t)dt+\sigma(X_t)dW_t^\theta.
\]
Moreover
\((Y_t,Z_t)=(u(t,X_t),\sigma^TDu(t,X_t))\) is the unique bounded solution of
\[
Y_t=\phi(X_T)+
\int_t^T\frac\theta2|Z_s|^2ds-
\int_t^TZ_sdW_s.
\]
For a rough diffusion inherited from A4, its area anomaly is included in the
effective drift \(b\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Uniform ellipticity and the smooth bounded coefficients make \(u\) the bounded
classical solution of
\[
\partial_tu+b\cdot Du+\tfrac12A:D^2u+\tfrac\theta2Du^TADu=0.
\]
Itô's formula identifies the normalized terminal exponential with the stated
stochastic exponential.  Girsanov's theorem gives the drift shift.  Applying
Itô's formula to \(u(t,X_t)\) gives the BSDE, and the exponential transform
gives uniqueness in the bounded class.  The deterministic rough-area
correction contributes its standard Lie-bracket drift before this diffusion
calculus is applied.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One Hilbert-space Doob memory identity}

Fix one exposed potential \(\Psi\).  Let
\(\widetilde P_t^\Psi\) be the genuine A4 eigenfunction Doob semigroup and let
\(\nu_\Psi^D\) be its invariant phase.  Work solely in
\[
\mathcal H_D=L^2(\nu_\Psi^D),
\]
with generator \(L_D\).  Let \(V\subset D(L_D^2)\) be finite dimensional and
let \(P_D\) be its \(\mathcal H_D\)-orthogonal projection.  Define
\[
\mathcal E_t^{\Psi,\theta}F
=\theta^{-1}\log
\widetilde P_t^\Psi(e^{\theta F}).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Typed pressure-tangent/memory commutation]
\label{thm:r6-c2-memory}
For \(A,B\in V\),
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-zt}
\left\langle B,
D\mathcal E_t^{\Psi,\theta}(0)A
\right\rangle_{\mathcal H_D}dt
=
\left\langle B,
P_D(z-L_D)^{-1}P_DA
\right\rangle_{\mathcal H_D}.
\]
The Doob compressed-memory transform is therefore
\[
\widehat K_D(z)=
zI-P_DL_DP_D-
[P_D(z-L_D)^{-1}P_D]^{-1}.
\]
It is the same operator obtained by first linearizing the nonlinear Doob
pressure in \(\mathcal H_D\) and then applying the A4 compressed-correlation
Volterra construction.  Source differentiation on either side gives the same
Duhamel insertion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct differentiation at zero gives
\[
D\mathcal E_t^{\Psi,\theta}(0)A
=\widetilde P_t^\Psi A,
\]
not a statewise normalized Feynman--Kac ratio.  Pairing in the declared Hilbert
space and taking the Laplace transform gives the compressed resolvent.  A4's
Volterra theorem applies directly to the strongly continuous Doob semigroup
and the orthogonal projection \(P_D\), yielding the memory formula.  Both
source derivatives are obtained from
\[
\partial_\eta e^{t(L_D+\eta V)}|_{\eta=0}
=\int_0^te^{(t-s)L_D}Ve^{sL_D}ds,
\]
so the diagram commutes.
\end{proof}

A Feynman--Kac generator before the Doob transform may be related by
multiplication with the bounded positive eigenfunction, but no equality with
an untitled Koopman memory is asserted unless a bounded unitary intertwiner and
the transformed orthogonal projection have been explicitly supplied.

\subsection{Conditional entropic calibration}

Cash additivity and strong time consistency classify a smooth
utility-generated certainty equivalent as affine or entropic with one constant
coefficient.  Equality with a mechanical preparation field follows only when
the two normalized likelihood cocycles are required to coincide in common
work units.

\begin{theorem}[Round-six C2 closure]
\label{thm:r6-c2-main}
The theory has coercive platform-labelled pressures, countably additive strict
phase duals, closed path cotangent quotients, and typed contraction maps.
Finite likelihoods are exact complete-history martingales and converge through
the A4 conditional-kernel theorem to the appropriate diffusion optional
projections, yielding the smooth Girsanov and BSDE representations.  The
pressure-tangent/memory identity is constructed in one Doob Hilbert space with
one orthogonal projection.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The strict and contraction assertions are
Theorems~\ref{thm:r6-c2-strict},
\ref{thm:r6-c2-contraction}, and
\ref{thm:r6-c2-cotangent}.  Likelihood convergence and its stochastic
representations are Theorems~\ref{thm:r6-c2-optional} and
\ref{thm:r6-c2-girsanov}.  The memory statement is
Theorem~\ref{thm:r6-c2-memory}.  The calibration qualification was proved in
the final subsection.
\end{proof}
