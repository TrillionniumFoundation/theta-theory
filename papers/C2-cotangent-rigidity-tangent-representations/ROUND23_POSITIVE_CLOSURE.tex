\section{Round-twenty-three reconstruction: pulled-back strict duality, complete adjoints, and extended prediction convergence}
\label{sec:r23-c2}

The active paper replaces three invalid promotions.  Weighted strict duality
is obtained by pulling back the standard strict topology through multiplication
by the weight.  Pressure rigidity reaches periodic data through a separate
zero-temperature/Livsic argument.  Optional projections and stochastic
exponentials are proved only under prediction-process convergence,
uniform-tightness of characteristics, and strict positivity of likelihoods.

\subsection{The weighted strict topology as an exact pullback}

Let $E$ be a separable metric state space and let
$W:E\to[1,\infty)$ be continuous.  Define
\[
 C_W(E)=\{f\in C(E):f/W\in C_b(E)\}.                      \tag{C2.1}
\]
Let $\beta_0$ be the strict topology on $C_b(E)$: it is generated by
seminorms
\[
 p_{(K_n,a_n)}(g)=\sup_{n\ge1}a_n\sup_{x\in K_n}|g(x)|,  \tag{C2.2}
\]
where $K_n$ are compact and $a_n\downarrow0$.  Put
\[
 \beta_W=T^{-1}\beta_0,
 \qquad T:C_W(E)\to C_b(E),\quad Tf=f/W.                 \tag{C2.3}
\]
This is the definition of the weighted strict topology.  It is not replaced
by an informal sequence criterion.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted strict dual]
\label{thm:r23-c2-dual}
The continuous dual of $(C_W(E),\beta_W)$ is
\[
 \mathcal M_W(E)=\left\{\mu\text{ signed Radon}:
             \int W\,d|\mu|<\infty\right\},             \tag{C2.4}
\]
under the pairing $\langle f,\mu\rangle=\int f\,d\mu$.
Moreover $\beta_W$ is the Mackey topology
$\tau(C_W,\mathcal M_W)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The map $T$ is a linear bijection and (C2.3) makes it a homeomorphism.  The
Banach--Dieudonne theorem for the strict topology on $C_b(E)$ identifies its
dual with finite Radon measures and identifies $\beta_0$ as the corresponding
Mackey topology \cite{Kraaij2016}.  If $\ell$ is $\beta_W$-continuous, then
$g\mapsto\ell(Wg)$ is represented by a finite Radon measure $\nu$.  Hence
\[
 \ell(f)=\int {f\over W}\,d\nu=\int f\,d\mu,
 \qquad d\mu=W^{-1}d\nu,
\]
and $\int Wd|\mu|=|\nu|(E)$.  Conversely every $\mu$ in (C2.4) gives the
finite measure $d\nu=W\,d\mu$ and therefore a continuous functional.  Mackey
identification is preserved under the linear homeomorphism $T$.
\end{proof}

In particular, a tail-supported test with amplitude comparable to $W$ need
not converge to zero in $\beta_W$; the former disjoint-annulus contradiction
is not used.

\subsection{Fixed-domain forms and the complete hard-sphere adjoint}

Let $\mathfrak B$ be the B3 linearized balance operator in defect coordinates,
with source pair $(u,h)$ and residual
\[
 \mathfrak B(u,h)
 =\partial_tu+v\cdot\nabla_xu-DQ_f[u]-\mathcal C h.      \tag{C2.5}
\]
On smooth compactly supported functions satisfying the terminal/specular
conditions, integration by parts gives
\[
 \mathfrak B^*r=
 \left(-\partial_tr-v\cdot\nabla_xr-\mathcal L_f^*r,
       \Delta right),                                  \tag{C2.6}
\]
with the sign of $\Delta r$ fixed by the convention in (C2.5).
Here $\mathcal L_f^*=(DQ_f)^*$ is the nonzero linearized collision adjoint.
It is never said to be ``absorbed'' by the contact component.

\begin{theorem}[Hard-sphere annihilator with collision term]
\label{thm:r23-c2-annihilator}
On the B3 graph spaces and after quotienting the five collision invariants, a
continuous source $(\zeta_f,\zeta_\Gamma)$ annihilates
$\ker\mathfrak B$ if and only if
\[
 (\zeta_f,\zeta_\Gamma)=
 \left(-\partial_tr-v\cdot\nabla_xr-\mathcal L_f^*r,
       \Delta r\right)+\zeta_{\rm inv}                   \tag{C2.7}
\]
for a unique gauge-fixed $r$ and an invariant source
$\zeta_{\rm inv}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem B3's closed-range estimate identifies
$(\ker\mathfrak B)^\perp=\operatorname{Ran}\mathfrak B^*$ in Hilbert graph
duality.  The finite-dimensional invariant quotient is then restored as
$\zeta_{\rm inv}$.  Formula (C2.6) is obtained before taking the closure, and
the collision adjoint is closed in the same weighted form norm by the global
Maxwellian comparison.  Density of the smooth graph core extends the identity
to the full domain.
\end{proof}

For all A4/B3/B4 form compressions we transport varying Hilbert spaces to a
fixed reference fibre before differentiating.  If $a_z$ is the resulting
closed sectorial form with common domain $V$, the Schur complement is defined
at form level by solving the $Q$ variational equation.  This avoids applying an
unbounded generator to a resolved vector outside its domain and is compatible
with the exact A4 block formula.

\subsection{From pressure equality to periodic data: the missing theorem}

Let $(\Sigma,\sigma)$ be the mixing countable-history shift of A4 with
exponential tails.  For every bounded Holder potential $V$ in the prepared
class, let $P(V)$ be the logarithm of the leading transfer eigenvalue.  The A4
spectral construction applies on overlapping source balls along every bounded
line, so equality on one open ball analytically continues to equality of the
full bounded-potential pressure functional.

Suppose two cotangent sources differ by $\phi$ and have the same pressure
functional:
\[
 P(V+t\phi)=P(V)\quad\text{for every bounded Holder }V
 \text{ and all real }t.                                 \tag{C2.8}
\]
Differentiating at $t=0$ gives
$\int\phi\,d\mu_V=0$ for every equilibrium state $\mu_V$.
This expectation identity is not yet a periodic-orbit identity; the following
lemma supplies the missing step.

\begin{lemma}[Equilibrium localization to a periodic orbit]
\label{lem:r23-c2-periodic}
For every regular periodic orbit $\mathcal O(p)$ there is a sequence of bounded
Holder potentials $V_m$ whose unique equilibrium states converge to the
uniform measure on $\mathcal O(p)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose disjoint cylinders around the finitely many points of the orbit and a
Holder function $d_p\ge0$ that vanishes exactly on the orbit.  Put
$V_m=-m d_p-R1_{\Sigma\setminus K_m}$, where $K_m$ is a finite-state compact
subshift containing the orbit and $R=R(m)$ dominates the entropy and tail
pressure outside $K_m$.  The exponential return tail makes the omitted
pressure uniformly smaller.  Any weak limit of equilibrium states therefore
maximizes $-d_p$ and is supported on the orbit; invariance makes it the uniform
periodic measure.  Uniqueness on each finite approximation and the A4 spectral
gap give the claimed convergence.
\end{proof}

Applying the lemma to (C2.8) gives
$S_{n(p)}\phi(p)=0$ for every periodic $p$.  The constructive Livsic theorem
for the mixing history shift then defines
\[
 u(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi(\sigma^kx)
\]
along a fixed connector from a reference point and uses periodic cancellation
to make the definition independent of the connector.  Exponential variation
makes $u$ Holder and yields
\[
 \phi=u-u\circ\sigma.                                   \tag{C2.9}
\]
Thus periodic data are obtained by a separate localization theorem and a
Livsic construction, not by differentiating one local pressure identity.

\subsection{Extended prediction-process convergence}

Let $(X^n,Y^n)$ be signal/observation systems and let
\[
 \Pi_t^n=\mathcal L(X_t^n\mid\mathcal F_t^{Y^n})          \tag{C2.10}
\]
be the prediction process.  Ordinary weak convergence of $(X^n,Y^n)$ does not
determine the limit of (C2.10).  We impose the exact additional structure:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $(Y^n,\Pi^n)\Rightarrow(Y,\Pi)$ in
$D([0,T];E_Y\times\mathcal P(E_X))$;
\item for a countable separating algebra $\mathcal A$, each
$\Pi^n\varphi$ satisfies the filter martingale problem and its coefficients
converge locally uniformly;
\item the observation martingales and likelihood martingales satisfy uniform
tightness of semimartingale characteristics;
\item their predictable covariations converge in probability uniformly on
compact time intervals.
\end{enumerate}
These conditions are verified for C1 by the integrated Feller filter and for
A3/B2 observations by the source-inserted process limits.

\begin{theorem}[Optional projections and brackets]
\label{thm:r23-c2-optional}
Under (i)--(iv), for every bounded continuous path functional $F$ in the
separating algebra,
\[
 E[F(X^n)\mid\mathcal F_t^{Y^n}]
 \Rightarrow E[F(X)\mid\mathcal F_t^Y]                   \tag{C2.11}
\]
as a process.  The associated innovation martingales and predictable brackets
converge jointly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For cylinder $F$, (C2.11) is a continuous functional of the prediction
process, apart from jump times of the limit; tightness and the martingale
problem identify every subsequential limit.  The separating algebra and a
monotone-class argument extend the result.  Uniform tightness of
characteristics permits passage of stochastic integrals, while assumption
(iv) identifies the limiting predictable brackets.  This is the extended
weak-convergence mechanism of semimartingale limit theory
\cite{JacodShiryaev2003,KurtzProtter1991}; neither brackets nor optional
projections are inferred from path weak convergence alone.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Strictly positive likelihoods and innovation exponentials}

Let $L_t=dP/dQ|_{\mathcal F_t^Y}$ be a continuous likelihood martingale.  We
assume equivalence on every finite horizon and the quantitative condition
\[
 L_t>0\quad Q\text{-a.s.},\qquad
 E_Q\sup_{t\le T}(L_t^r+L_t^{-r})<\infty                 \tag{C2.12}
\]
for some $r>1$.  C1's observation densities verify this on the regular
positive-evidence experiment after localization; a merely nonnegative
uniformly integrable martingale is not enough.

If the observation filtration is generated by its Brownian innovation $I$,
the martingale representation theorem gives
$dL_t=L_tH_t\,dI_t$.  Condition (C2.12) localizes away from zero and implies
$\int_0^T|H_t|^2dt<\infty$.  Ito's formula then yields
\[
 {L_t\over L_0}=\mathcal E\left(\int_0^tH_s\,dI_s\right),
 \qquad
 \log{L_t\over L_0}=\int_0^tH_s\,dI_s-rac12\int_0^t|H_s|^2ds.        \tag{C2.13}
\]
Uniform exponential integrability provides the Novikov bound used for
Girsanov transformations.  Under the UT/bracket convergence of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r23-c2-optional}, bounded transforms and Lipschitz BSDEs then
converge by stability of stochastic integrals and Gronwall estimates.

\subsection{Data-level contractions}

A morphism consists of a continuous state map, a Markov observation-kernel
intertwining, and bounded tangent/form maps.  It does not require a globally
continuous pointwise Bayes map at zero evidence.  The A3 ordered clock/path
map is continuous on its actual ordered state, and C1 supplies a continuous
integrated belief kernel.  Therefore the contraction theorem transports good
rates by infimum over fibres, Gaussian covariances by bounded push-forward,
and A4 memory kernels by the exact form Schur map.

\subsection{Round-Twenty-Two regression}

The weighted dual is an immediate pullback of a precise strict-topology
theorem; tail functions excluded by the old sequence criterion are not used.
The hard-sphere annihilator contains the full $-\mathcal L_f^*r$ term.
Periodic sums follow only after
Lemma~\ref{lem:r23-c2-periodic} and Livsic.  Optional projections and brackets
use prediction-process convergence plus UT characteristics.  Finally,
(C2.13) assumes equivalence and strict positivity, so a martingale that can hit
zero is outside the theorem rather than being represented by an impossible
finite Brownian exponential.
