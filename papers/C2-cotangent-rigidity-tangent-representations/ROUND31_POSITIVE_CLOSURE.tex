\section{Round-thirty-one reconstruction: parameter-specific likelihood filters and stable path prediction}
\label{sec:r31-c2}

Weighted strict duality is stated on arbitrary Polish spaces without a proper
weight or compact exhaustion.  The kinetic annihilator sees the full B3
contact space.  Pressure localization uses only the certified finite-amplitude
bridges of A4.  Most importantly, every hidden-model likelihood uses the
filter generated under its own parameter.  Optional projections are obtained
from a quantitative filter-stability theorem rather than from weak kernel
convergence alone, and BSDE stability is separated by observation type.

\subsection{Weighted mixed strict duality}

Let $E$ be Polish and $W:E\to[1,\infty)$ positive and continuous; no compact
sublevel assumption is made.  Put
\[
 C_W(E)=\{f\in C(E):f/W\in C_b(E)\}.                      \tag{C2.1}
\]
The mixed strict topology $\beta_0$ on $C_b(E)$ is generated by seminorms
\[
 p_{K,a}(g)=\sup_{n\ge1}a_n\sup_{x\in K_n}|g(x)|,          \tag{C2.2}
\]
for arbitrary sequences of compact sets $K_n$ and $a_n\downarrow0$, together
with compact-open convergence.  Let $\beta_W$ be the pullback under $f\mapsto
f/W$.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted strict dual on a Polish state]
\label{thm:r31-c2-dual}
The continuous dual of $(C_W(E),\beta_W)$ is
\[
 \cM_W(E)=\{\mu\text{ finite signed Radon}:
                    \int W\dd|\mu|<\infty\}.              \tag{C2.3}
\]
Equicontinuous sets have bounded weighted variation and are uniformly
$W$-tight over the directed family of all compact subsets of $E$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The map $f\mapsto f/W$ is a linear homeomorphism onto
$(C_b(E),\beta_0)$.  Restriction of a continuous functional to each compact
set gives compatible Riesz measures; the seminorms (C2.2) yield the tightness
needed to glue them into one finite Radon measure.  Pullback gives (C2.3).
The proof uses all compact subsets and does not imply local compactness or
sigma-compactness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Full contact annihilator}

Use the B3 tangent $(u,h_{\rm n},h_{\rm cyc})$ and balance operator
\[
 \mathfrak B(u,h)=
 (\partial_tu+v\cdot\nabla_xu-DQ_f[u]-\cC h_{\rm n},u(0)).              \tag{C2.4}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Full kinetic annihilator]
\label{thm:r31-c2-annihilator}
A source triple $(\zeta_f,\zeta_c,\zeta_0)$ annihilates the homogeneous joint
tangent kernel if and only if
\[
 \zeta_c\perp\ker\cC,                                    \tag{C2.5}
\]
and, modulo the five invariant gauges, there are $(r,r_0)$ with $r(T)=0$
such that
\[
 \zeta_f=-\partial_tr-v\cdot\nabla_xr-\cL_f^*r,\qquad
 \zeta_c=-\Delta r,\qquad
 \zeta_0=r_0-r(0).                                       \tag{C2.6}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Testing against $(0,0,h_{\rm cyc})$ gives (C2.5).  On the normal graph, B3's
closed range gives
$(\ker\mathfrak B)^\perp=\operatorname{Ran}\mathfrak B^*$.
Integration by parts in time and collision duality give (C2.6), including the
initial output.  Terminal and invariant gauges give uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rigidity along certified pressure bridges}

For a regular periodic orbit $\cO(p)$ let
$d_p=1\wedge d(\cdot,\cO(p))^\alpha$.  Choose bounded tail cutoffs $\chi_m$
and finite potentials
\[
 V_{t,m,R}=-t d_p-R\chi_m.                                \tag{C2.7}
\]
The rigidity hypothesis includes an A4 certified potential bridge from zero
to every finite $V_{t,m,R}$.  Thus existence and simplicity of the equilibrium
state are conclusions of Theorem A4 bridge continuation, not consequences of
boundedness alone.

\begin{theorem}[Certified pressure-to-Livsic rigidity]
\label{thm:r31-c2-rigidity}
Suppose two pressure functionals agree along all the certified bridges to
(C2.7) and in the cocycle direction at their endpoints.  If the bridge family
has a uniform Lyapunov tail bound, then every periodic sum of the cocycle
difference $\phi$ vanishes and
\[
 \phi=u-u\circ\sigma                                     \tag{C2.8}
\]
for a Holder transfer $u$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The certified bridge gives a unique equilibrium state at every finite
potential and differentiability of pressure in the $\phi$ direction.  First
send $R$ along the tail-tight diagonal and then $t\to\infty$; the variational
principle localizes the equilibrium state on $\cO(p)$.  Pressure equality
therefore gives zero average on every periodic orbit.  The connector
construction defines $u$, and exponential variation gives its Holder norm.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Parameter-specific filtering likelihoods}

For a parameter $\vartheta$, let
\[
 \Pi_k^\vartheta=
 \mathcal L_\vartheta(X_k\mid Y_{1:k},A_{1:k})             \tag{C2.9}
\]
be the filter recursively generated under that same parameter, and let
$r_\vartheta(\Pi_{k-1}^\vartheta,A_k,Y_k)$ be its predictive evidence.

\begin{proposition}[Correct discrete hidden-model likelihood]
\label{prop:r31-c2-discrete-likelihood}
For two parameters $\vartheta$ and $\vartheta_0$,
\[
 L_n(\vartheta,\vartheta_0)=
 \prod_{k=1}^n
 {r_\vartheta(\Pi_{k-1}^\vartheta,A_k,Y_k)
  \over
  r_{\vartheta_0}(\Pi_{k-1}^{\vartheta_0},A_k,Y_k)}        \tag{C2.10}
\]
is the Radon--Nikodym likelihood martingale.  Its score contains both the
direct channel derivative and the derivative of the parameter-specific
filter.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under parameter $\vartheta$, disintegrate the observation law successively:
\[
 p_\vartheta(Y_{1:n}\mid A_{1:n})
 =\prod_{k=1}^nr_\vartheta(\Pi_{k-1}^\vartheta,A_k,Y_k).
\]
Divide by the corresponding factorization under $\vartheta_0$.  Already at
$k=2$ the two filters are generally different, which is why a common filter
cannot appear in (C2.10).
\end{proof}

For a marked point observation with parameter-specific predictable
intensities $\lambda_t^\vartheta$ and $\lambda_t^{\vartheta_0}$, the
likelihood is the Dol\'eans exponential of their compensator ratio.  For a
Brownian diagnostic channel, and only there, it is the innovation Girsanov
exponential built from the two parameter-specific conditional drifts.

\subsection{Quantitative filter and continuation stability}

Fix $T$ and the common stopped-path space
\[
 \mathbb D_T=D([0,T];E_X),\qquad
 (S_tx)(s)=x(s\wedge t).                                  \tag{C2.11}
\]
The prediction state is
\[
 \overline\Pi_t^\vartheta
 =\mathcal L_\vartheta(S_tX,X_t\mid\cF_t^Y),              \tag{C2.12}
\]
and carries a continuation kernel
$K_{t,T}^\vartheta((S_tx,x_t),\dd\widetilde x)$.

Consider models indexed by $n$.  Assume:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(P\arabic*)]
\item primitive hidden and observation kernels converge uniformly on the C1
      cylindrical determining class and satisfy one common Lyapunov bound;
\item the C1 finite-window induced-law observability constant is bounded below
      uniformly, and its derivative-filter Riccati estimate has one contraction
      factor $1-c_0$ on the observable quotient;
\item continuation kernels converge uniformly on compact stopped-path sets;
\item primitive martingale characteristics have a common $2+\delta$ moment.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}[Quantitative filter stability]
\label{lem:r31-c2-filter-stability}
For each fixed parameter in a compact chart and finite horizon,
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}E\,d_{\rm BL}(
   \overline\Pi_t^{n,\vartheta},\overline\Pi_t^\vartheta)
 \le C_T(\epsilon_n+\eta_n),                              \tag{C2.13}
\]
where $\epsilon_n$ is the primitive-kernel error and $\eta_n$ is the initial
prediction error.  The same estimate holds for the first parameter derivative
in the C1 projective distribution norm.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write one finite-window filtering step as the exact model step plus a kernel
perturbation.  On the observable quotient, C1 gives the contraction
$1-c_0$; the latent coordinate is transported continuously and is tested only
through the convergent continuation kernel.  Iterating the discrete Gronwall
recursion yields (C2.13).  Between diagnostic windows use the common Lyapunov
and Feller modulus.  Differentiating the same recursion gives the derivative
estimate.  Thus uniqueness and stability of the limiting filter are proved,
not inferred from weak convergence alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stable optional projections of full paths]
\label{thm:r31-c2-optional}
Under (P1)--(P4), for every bounded continuous
$F:\mathbb D_T\to\R$,
\[
 E[F(X^n)\mid\cF_t^{Y^n}]
 \Longrightarrow E[F(X)\mid\cF_t^Y]                     \tag{C2.14}
\]
as a process, jointly with the primitive observation martingales and their
predictable characteristics.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a cylinder $F$, the optional projection equals the integral of $F$ against
$\overline\Pi_t^{n}K_{t,T}^{n}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r31-c2-filter-stability} and
(P3) give uniform convergence on compact stopped-path sets.  A3/B3 path
tightness approximates every bounded continuous $F$ by cylinders on a common
compact set.  Primitive characteristic convergence in (P1), rather than an
assumed bracket conclusion, identifies predictable brackets; (P4) gives UT
and stochastic-integral convergence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Channel-specific stochastic and BSDE stability}

\begin{theorem}[Discrete BSDE stability]
\label{thm:r31-c2-discrete-bsde}
For discrete observations, suppose terminal values converge in $L^2$, drivers
are uniformly Lipschitz and converge locally uniformly, and the product
likelihoods (C2.10) are uniformly integrable.  Then the backward recursions and
their martingale differences converge jointly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use backward induction.  Conditional expectations converge by
Theorem~\ref{thm:r31-c2-optional}; the Lipschitz driver gives a discrete
Gronwall estimate.  Uniform integrability permits the parameter-specific
likelihood change of measure.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Marked-point BSDE stability]
\label{thm:r31-c2-point-bsde}
For marked point observations, assume convergence of compensators, a common
exponential compensator bound, terminal $L^2$ convergence, and Lipschitz
convergence of drivers in the continuous and jump integrands.  Then the
solutions converge in the product of the supremum norm and compensated-jump
$L^2$ norm.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the It\^o formula with jumps to the squared solution difference.
Compensator convergence controls the jump norm and Gronwall controls the value
process.  The parameter-specific Dol\'eans exponentials are uniformly
integrable by the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Brownian BSDE stability]
\label{thm:r31-c2-brownian-bsde}
For a Brownian diagnostic channel, assume Novikov for the difference of the
two parameter-specific conditional drifts, convergence of terminal data in
$L^2$, and locally uniform Lipschitz driver convergence.  Then the Girsanov
likelihoods, innovation integrals, and Brownian BSDE solutions converge
jointly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Brownian martingale representation theorem applies only in this channel.
Novikov gives uniform integrability of the exponential, UT gives stochastic
integral convergence, and the standard It\^o--Gronwall estimate gives the BSDE
limit.
\end{proof}

The likelihood, prediction, and BSDE statements now use the filter and
stochastic calculus belonging to the actual parameter and actual observation
channel, while the pressure-rigidity argument imports only A4 spectral bridges
that have been separately certified.
