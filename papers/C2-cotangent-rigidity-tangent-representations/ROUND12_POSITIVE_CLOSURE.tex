\section{Round-twelve positive closure: signed cotangents, form bundles, and covariant memory}
\label{sec:r12-c2}

Invariant-integral equivalence, equality of one pressure number, and equality
of the full perturbed pressure functional are different notions.  The
continuous dual is a vector space of signed invariant Radon measures;
probabilities are a normalized positive slice.

\subsection{The weighted invariant quotient}

Let $\mathscr A_W$ be the weighted strict potential space and
\[
 \mathscr N_{\rm inv}=\overline{\operatorname{span}}
 \{U-U\circ\Theta_t:t\in\mathbb Q\}.
\]
Constants are kept separate.

\begin{theorem}[Signed invariant duality]
\label{thm:r12-c2-dual}
Assume the platform pointwise Lyapunov estimate
\[
 {1\over T}\int_0^T W(\Theta_tx)dt\le C(1+W(x))
\]
for all $x$ and $T\ge1$.  Then
\[
 (\mathscr A_W/\mathscr N_{\rm inv})'
 =\{\text{finite invariant signed Radon measures with finite }W\text{-moment}
 \}.
\]
Invariant probabilities are exactly the positive normalized slice.  Two
potentials have equal integrals under every invariant probability iff their
difference lies in $\mathbb R1+\mathscr N_{\rm inv}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The weighted strict dual is the signed weighted Radon space.  A quotient
functional is therefore a signed measure annihilating every rational-time
coboundary, hence invariant.  Conversely invariance annihilates the closed
span.  For separation, average a signed separator in time.  The pointwise
Lyapunov estimate gives uniform weighted tightness after integration against
its total variation; every weak limit is invariant and preserves the pairing.
Jordan components of an invariant signed measure are invariant by uniqueness
of the Jordan decomposition.  Normalizing a nonzero positive component gives
the probability separator.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Full-pressure rigidity, platform by platform}

For a base potential $F$ write
$\mathcal P_F(H)=P(F+H)-P(F)$.

\begin{theorem}[Perturbed-pressure rigidity]
\label{thm:r12-c2-rigidity}
On the Sinai H\"older platform,
$\mathcal P_F(H)=\mathcal P_G(H)$ for every small H\"older $H$ iff
$F-G$ is a constant plus a H\"older temporal coboundary.  On the regular
hard-sphere source platform the same identity holds iff the source difference
belongs to the closed B3 balance/endpoint covariance gauge plus a constant.
Equality of one scalar pressure value is not used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the Sinai platform, equality of all perturbations gives equality of every
perturbed equilibrium law.  Periodic approximation then gives equal sums of
$F-G$ on all periodic orbits; the standard stable/unstable telescoping
construction solves the H\"older cohomological equation.  On the hard-sphere
platform, differentiating all finite source perturbations gives equality of
the complete mean and covariance functionals.  B3's exact local-noise
representation identifies the annihilator with the closed balance/endpoint
gauge.  Constants account for the pressure normalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transfer jets and a separate Hilbert form bundle}

The finite Riesz range of the Sinai transfer operator is trivialized by a
fixed family $e_i(\eta)=\Pi_\eta v_i$ on each chart.  Its $q$th derivative is
viewed as a map from strong level $m+q$ to level $m$; no infinite Picard
transport is used.

For memory, let $\mathcal H_\eta=L^2(\widehat\pi_\eta)$ and let
$\mathfrak a_\eta$ be the closed Doob generator form on a common Gelfand domain
$\mathcal V$.  Pull the inner products to one reference Hilbert space by the
positive square root of the correlation Gram operator.

\begin{theorem}[Common-domain form bundle]
\label{thm:r12-c2-form}
On a regular phase chart, $\mathfrak a_\eta$ is a type-(B) differentiable
family of closed sectorial forms on the common domain $\mathcal V$.
Consequently the generators have norm-resolvent derivatives
\[
 D(z-L_\eta)^{-1}
 =(z-L_\eta)^{-1}(DL_\eta)(z-L_\eta)^{-1}
\]
in the graph-form sense.  After quotienting constant/conservation null modes,
the Gram matrix of a fixed finite resolved observable family has a uniform
positive lower eigenvalue and defines a differentiable orthogonal projection
$R_\eta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A2/A4 source derivatives of correlations are bounded on fresh strong levels;
their pullbacks define bounded form derivatives on $\mathcal V$.  Uniform
sector bounds and Kato's form theorem give the common-domain resolvent family.
The finite correlation Gram matrix is continuous; remove its exact nullspace
at the base point and shrink the chart so the remaining eigenvalues stay
positive.  The usual finite-matrix formula then differentiates $R_\eta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Covariant compressed memory]
\label{thm:r12-c2-memory}
Away from the finitely many tracked transmission zeros, the compressed memory
\[
 \widehat K_\eta(z)=zR_\eta-R_\eta L_\eta R_\eta-
 [R_\eta(z-L_\eta)^{-1}R_\eta]^{-1}
\]
is differentiable in source.  Its derivative includes the form derivative of
$L_\eta$, both derivatives of $R_\eta$, the correlation pairing derivative,
and the derivative of every descriptor residue.  The formula is independent
of the finite transfer-eigenline frame.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:r12-c2-form} supplies a common resolvent domain and
$DR_\eta$.  Differentiate the finite compressed matrix and use
$D(C^{-1})=-C^{-1}(DC)C^{-1}$.  A4's normalized meromorphic decomposition
tracks zeros and residues; differentiation of their Riesz contours gives the
descriptor terms.  The transfer frame enters only the proof of correlation
response, not the Hilbert-space identification.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Likelihoods and contractions}

\begin{theorem}[Optional projection and diffusion likelihood]
\label{thm:r12-c2-optional}
Under the A4 uniform quenched kernel theorem and the common exponential moment
bound, finite-history likelihood martingales and their optional projections
converge stably to the Brownian stochastic exponential.  In the limiting
Brownian filtration the martingale representation theorem gives the Girsanov
drift and the unique bounded quadratic BSDE with the declared terminal data.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditional-kernel convergence identifies finite-cylinder conditional
expectations.  Uniform $L^{1+\delta}$ bounds give uniform integrability and
permit passage from cylinders to the complete filtration.  Joint rough
convergence identifies the limiting martingale integrand.  Brownian martingale
representation gives the stochastic exponential; exponential transformation
of the bounded terminal value gives the unique quadratic BSDE.
\end{proof}

Let $C:X\to Y$ be a differentiable state contraction.  Source and state
derivatives are written separately.

\begin{proposition}[Typed contraction derivatives]
\label{prop:r12-c2-chain}
For a source perturbation $h:Y\to\mathbb R$,
\[
 DQ_C(\varphi)[h]=DQ(\varphi\circ C)[h\circ C].
\]
For a state tangent $v\in T_xX$,
$D(\varphi\circ C)(x)v=D\varphi(Cx)DC(x)v$.  The corresponding Hessian
contains the ordinary second state derivative of $C$; it is not a source
Hessian identity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first identity follows by differentiating the exact source composition.
The second and its Hessian are the ordinary Banach-space chain rule.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-twelve C2 closure]
\label{thm:r12-c2-main}
The cotangent dual is the invariant signed-measure space, full-pressure
rigidity is platform-specific, transfer regularity loss is explicitly typed,
the physical memory bundle has a common form domain, and likelihood and
contraction derivatives are correctly separated.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r12-c2-dual},
\ref{thm:r12-c2-rigidity}, \ref{thm:r12-c2-form},
\ref{thm:r12-c2-memory}, and \ref{thm:r12-c2-optional}, with
Proposition~\ref{prop:r12-c2-chain}.
\end{proof}
