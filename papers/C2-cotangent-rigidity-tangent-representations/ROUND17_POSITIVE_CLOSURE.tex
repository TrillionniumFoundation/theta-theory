\section{Round-twenty-one positive closure: weighted strict duality, verified form transport, and innovation-stable contractions}
\label{sec:r21-c2}

This section replaces the former Round-Seventeen source.  The weighted strict
topology is defined by explicit seminorms.  Coboundary separation is stated
only for the actual platforms, where an invariant weighted probability has
already been constructed.  Form transport, rigidity, changing-filtration
limits, and contractions are proved separately rather than inserted into the
definition of a morphism.

\subsection{The weighted strict topology and its dual}

Let $E$ be a Polish space and $W:E\to[1,\infty)$ a continuous proper weight.
Put
\[
 C_W(E)=\{f\in C(E):\|f/W\|_\infty<\infty\}.
\]
For every nonnegative $\psi\in C_0(E)$ define
\[
 p_\psi(f)=\|\psi f\|_\infty,
\]
and for $R>0$ define
\[
 q_R(f)=\sup_{W(x)>R}{|f(x)|\over W(x)}.
\]
The weighted strict topology $\beta_W$ is generated on each norm-bounded set
by all $p_\psi$ together with the requirement $q_R(f)\to0$ uniformly as
$R\to\infty$.  Equivalently, a net $f_i\to0$ when
$\sup_i\|f_i/W\|_\infty<\infty$, $f_i\to0$ uniformly on every compact set, and
its normalized tails vanish uniformly.

Let
\[
 \mathcal M_W(E)=\left\{\mu:\int W\,d|\mu|<\infty\right\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact weighted strict dual]
\label{thm:r21-c2-dual}
Every $\beta_W$-continuous linear functional on $C_W(E)$ is uniquely of the
form
\[
 L(f)=\int f\,d\mu,
 \qquad \mu\in\mathcal M_W(E),
\]
and conversely every such measure is continuous.  On norm-bounded subsets,
$\beta_W$ is the Mackey topology of the dual pair
$(C_W(E),\mathcal M_W(E))$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $K_n=\{W\le n\}$.  Continuity gives a neighborhood
\[
 |L(f)|\le1
 \quad\text{whenever}\quad
 p_{\psi_j}(f)<\varepsilon,\quad q_R(f)<\varepsilon
\]
for finitely many $\psi_j$ and one $R$.  On functions supported in $K_R$, the
supremum norm is controlled by these seminorms after enlarging the compact
support of the $\psi_j$.  Riesz representation therefore gives a finite
measure $\mu_R$ on $K_R$.  Uniqueness makes these measures compatible as $R$
increases, so they define one countably additive Borel measure $\mu$.

To obtain the global weighted bound, choose disjoint compact annuli and
continuous functions $f_n$ supported there with
$|f_n|\le W$ and sign equal to the local polar sign of $\mu$.  If
$\int W\,d|\mu|=\infty$, finite signed sums of the $f_n$ have uniformly bounded
$W$-norm, converge to zero on compacts after shifting to the tail, and have
$q_R$ tending to zero, but $|L|$ is unbounded.  This contradicts continuity.
Hence $\mu\in\mathcal M_W(E)$.

Conversely, split the integral over $K_R$ and its complement.  Uniform
convergence on $K_R$ controls the first part, while
$q_R(f_i)\int_{K_R^c}W\,d|\mu|$ controls the second.  This proves continuity.
Uniform integrability characterizes weakly compact subsets of
$\mathcal M_W(E)$, and the seminorm description is uniform convergence on
those subsets, giving the Mackey statement.
\end{proof}

Suppose now that $\Theta_t$ is either the A4 history suspension or the B4
kinetic semigroup trajectory map.  In both cases there is an invariant
probability $\pi\in\mathcal M_W(E)$ and
$W(\Theta_tx)\le C_te^{ct}W(x)$.

\begin{corollary}[Coboundary annihilator]
\label{cor:r21-c2-coboundary}
The closed weighted-strict span
\[
 \mathcal C=\overline{\operatorname{span}}
 \{g-g\circ\Theta_t:g\in C_W(E),\ t\ge0\}^{\beta_W}
\]
has annihilator equal to the invariant measures in $\mathcal M_W(E)$.  In
particular, a nonzero constant is not in $\mathcal C$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-c2-dual}, annihilation of every coboundary is exactly
invariance of the representing measure.  The actual platform invariant
probability $\pi$ annihilates $\mathcal C$ but integrates a nonzero constant to
a nonzero number.  No existence assertion is made for an arbitrary Polish
flow.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Verified type-(B) form transport}

Let $H_\eta=L^2(E,\pi_\eta)$ be a family of Hilbert realizations and let
$U_\eta:H_0\to H_\eta$ be multiplication by the square root of the positive
Radon--Nikodym density followed, when needed, by the A4/C1 coordinate
transport.  Let $V\subset H_0$ be the transported common form domain.

For the A4 suspension, $V$ is the flight $H^1$ domain with the Doob boundary
trace.  For the B4 tangent platform, $V$ is the graph form domain of B3.  The
transported forms are denoted $a_\eta:V\times V\to\mathbb C$.

\begin{theorem}[Platform type-(B) theorem]
\label{thm:r21-c2-typeb}
On compact regular parameter/source charts:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $V$ is independent of $\eta$ and dense in $H_0$;
\item for some $\omega,c,C$,
\[
 \operatorname{Re}a_\eta(u,u)+\omega\|u\|_{H_0}^2
 \ge c\|u\|_V^2,
 \qquad |a_\eta(u,v)|\le C\|u\|_V\|v\|_V;
\]
\item $\eta\mapsto a_\eta$ is $C^2$ in
$\mathcal L(V,V^*)$, including the derivative of the Hilbert metric and of
$U_\eta$;
\item the associated generators $L_\eta$ are maximal dissipative after the
fixed shift, and
\[
 \nabla_\eta(z-L_\eta)^{-1}
 =(z-L_\eta)^{-1}(\nabla_\eta L_\eta)
  (z-L_\eta)^{-1}
\]
as a map $V^*\to V$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For A4, the fixed collision charts identify the flight derivative and the
Doob boundary trace.  The positive eigenfunction and its inverse are bounded
multipliers on the fixed A4 weighted space; hence multiplication transports
the boundary condition without changing $V$.  Flight integration by parts,
the A4 spectral drift, and the trace estimate give the sectorial coercivity.
The source and geometric derivatives are bounded by the A4 fixed-space
analytic theorem.

For B4, B3's graph observability gives coercivity on the tangent quotient, and
the positive multiplier transfer identifies nearby measures and collision
forms on one graph domain.  The logarithmic density derivatives are bounded on
the regular chart, so the metric connection and form derivatives lie in
$\mathcal L(V,V^*)$.  The representation theorem for closed sectorial forms
gives the associated generators.  Differentiate the weak equation
\[
 a_\eta(R_\eta f,v)+z(R_\eta f,v)=(f,v)
\]
in $V^*$; coercivity justifies inversion and yields the displayed formula with
all terms typed in $V,V^*$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Form compression and memory connection}

Let $\mathcal R\subset V$ be finite dimensional and let $P$ be the
$H_0$-orthogonal projection.  Assume, as verified for the A4 resolved spaces,
that $P:V\to V$ is bounded.  Put $Q=I-P$.  For
$\operatorname{Re}z>0$, solve the complementary form equation
\[
 a_\eta(w,Qv)+z(w,Qv)=-a_\eta(r,Qv),
 \qquad v\in V,\quad w\in QV.
\]
Coercivity defines $w=W_\eta(z)r$.  The Schur form on $\mathcal R$ is
\[
 s_\eta(z)[r,r']=(z r,r')+a_\eta(r+W_\eta(z)r,r').
\]

\begin{theorem}[Form Schur complement and covariant derivative]
\label{thm:r21-c2-schur}
The matrix $S_\eta(z)$ representing $s_\eta(z)$ is invertible for
$\operatorname{Re}z>0$ and equals the inverse of the compressed resolvent:
\[
 S_\eta(z)^{-1}=P(z-L_\eta)^{-1}P|_{\mathcal R}.
\]
Its covariant derivative is the sum of the form derivative, the derivative of
the metric projection, and the derivative of the complementary inverse.  All
three terms are continuous uniformly on compact right-half-plane sets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Boundedness of $P$ on $V$ gives the topological decomposition
$V=\mathcal R\oplus QV$.  Coercivity on $QV$ gives the unique complementary
solution by Lax--Milgram.  Blocking the weak resolvent equation in these two
subspaces yields the Schur formula.  If $S_\eta(z)r=0$, then the corresponding
full weak solution has zero forcing and hence is zero; thus $r=0$.  Finite
dimensionality gives invertibility.  Differentiate the Lax--Milgram equation
for $W_\eta(z)$ and the definition of the metric projection.  The type-(B)
bounds in Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-c2-typeb} control every term.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Model-specific pressure rigidity}

For the Sinai platform, let $\mathcal V_{\rm Sin}$ be the A4 H\"older-weighted
potential space on the exact history shift.  For the hard-sphere platform, let
$\mathcal T_{\rm HS}$ be the B3 tangent graph space.  Pressure functionals are
those already constructed in A4 and B2/B4.

\begin{lemma}[Countable-history Liv\v{s}ic theorem]
\label{lem:r21-c2-livsic}
If $F\in\mathcal V_{\rm Sin}$ has zero sum on every regular periodic history,
then there is $u\in\mathcal V_{\rm Sin}$ with
$F=u-u\circ\sigma$.  The coboundary range is closed in the weighted norm.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a regular reference history $x_*$.  For a history $x$ in its homoclinic
class, close a length-$n$ central word by a connector of uniformly bounded
cost and define $u(x)$ as the limit of the partial sum of $F$ relative to the
closed reference word.  Exponential variation makes successive definitions
differ by $C\theta^n$, so the limit exists and has the same weighted H\"older
norm.  Periodic zero sums make it independent of the connector.  Mixing makes
the homoclinic class dense, and the H\"older bound extends $u$ uniquely.
The construction is a bounded linear right inverse of
$u\mapsto u-u\circ\sigma$ on the zero-periodic-sum subspace, proving closedness.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hard-sphere annihilator]
\label{lem:r21-c2-hsann}
A continuous pair of density/contact sources $(\phi,\psi)$ annihilates every
smooth B3 balanced tangent if and only if
\[
 (\phi,\psi)=
 (-\partial_tr-v\cdot\nabla_xr,\Delta r)+\zeta,
\]
where $r$ lies in the adjoint graph domain and $\zeta$ is a linear combination
of the exact mass, momentum, energy, and endpoint constraint functionals.
The annihilator is closed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The ``if'' direction is the weak balance identity.  Conversely, quotient by
the finite-dimensional constraint functionals and apply B3's closed-range
theorem to the linearized balance operator.  The annihilator of its kernel is
the range of the adjoint because the range is closed.  Thus the source is
$\mathcal B^*r=(-\partial_tr-v\cdot\nabla_xr-\mathcal Lr,\Delta r)$; the
collision part of the adjoint equation absorbs $\mathcal Lr$ into the
linearization of the density source, giving the displayed gauge pair.  The
closed range and finite-dimensional constraint space prove closedness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pressure rigidity]
\label{thm:r21-c2-rigidity}
Suppose two regular potentials have identical perturbed pressure on a
neighborhood of every strong source.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item On the Sinai platform their difference is a constant plus a closed
      dynamical coboundary.
\item On the hard-sphere platform their difference is a balance gauge plus
      collision invariants and endpoint constraints as in
      Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-c2-hsann}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the pressure identity in every source direction.  Analyticity
from A4 and B2 identifies all equilibrium expectations and hence every
periodic-orbit derivative in the Sinai core and every balanced tangent pairing
in the hard-sphere chart.  Subtract the common mean to remove the constant.
Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-c2-livsic} gives the Sinai coboundary.  Lemma
\ref{lem:r21-c2-hsann} gives the hard-sphere gauge.  The closedness statements
prevent passage to a larger informal closure.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Changing filtrations and the innovation representation}

Let $(X^\varepsilon,Y^\varepsilon,L^\varepsilon)$ be exact microscopic
state, observation, and likelihood processes, and let
$\mathcal G_t^\varepsilon=\sigma(Y_s^\varepsilon:s\le t)$.  Put
\[
 M_t^\varepsilon=\mathbb E[L_T^\varepsilon\mid\mathcal G_t^\varepsilon].
\]
Assume the A3/B3 tightness conditions, the C1 regular conditional-kernel
convergence, and
$\sup_\varepsilon\mathbb E|L_T^\varepsilon|^{1+\delta}<\infty$.

\begin{theorem}[Extended weak convergence of optional projections]
\label{thm:r21-c2-optional}
The quadruples
\[
 (X^\varepsilon,Y^\varepsilon,L^\varepsilon,M^\varepsilon)
\]
are tight in the product Skorokhod topology, and every limit satisfies
\[
 M_t=\mathbb E[L_T\mid\mathcal G_t],
 \qquad \mathcal G_t=\sigma(Y_s:s\le t)^{\rm rc}.
\]
Finite-dimensional conditional laws converge jointly at every collection of
continuity times, and $M^\varepsilon\Rightarrow M$ in process law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Approximate $L_T$ in $L^1$ uniformly by bounded continuous functions of a
finite collection of future observation cylinders.  C1's kernel convergence
then gives convergence of their conditional expectations jointly at finitely
many times.  Uniform $L^{1+\delta}$ integrability removes the approximation.

For process tightness, Doob's inequality gives compact containment.  For
stopping times $\tau_\varepsilon$ and $h\le\eta$, write the increment as the
conditional expectation of the part of the cylinder approximation lying in
$(\tau_\varepsilon,\tau_\varepsilon+h]$ plus its approximation error.  The
A3/B3 stopping-time modulus and C1 kernel continuity make the first term tend
to zero uniformly; the second is uniformly small.  This is the Aldous
condition, not merely a maximal bound.  Right-continuous augmentation and a
monotone-class argument identify the limiting conditional expectation.
\end{proof}

In the Gaussian limit supplied by A4 or B3, write the observation as
\[
 dY_t=b_tdt+\sigma_t dB_t,
\]
with uniformly nondegenerate observed covariance.  Define the innovation
\[
 I_t=\int_0^t(\sigma_s\sigma_s^*)^{-1/2}
       (dY_s-\widehat b_sds).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Innovation martingale representation]
\label{lem:r21-c2-innovation}
The process $I$ is a Brownian motion in the observation filtration, and every
square-integrable observation martingale has a unique representation
$N_t=N_0+\int_0^tH_s\,dI_s$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The conditional drift makes $I$ a continuous local martingale with bracket
$tI$.  L\'evy's characterization gives Brownian motion.  Nondegeneracy and the
C1 filter imply that the observation filtration equals the completed
innovation filtration: the observation is reconstructed from the innovation
by the pathwise unique filtering equation, while the innovation is a
functional of the observation.  The Brownian martingale representation
theorem then gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Projected stochastic exponentials, Girsanov, and BSDE limits]
\label{thm:r21-c2-girsanov}
Let $M$ be the optional-projection limit in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-c2-optional}.  Then
\[
 {M_t\over M_0}=\mathcal E\left(\int_0^tH_s\,dI_s\right)
\]
for the unique innovation integrand $H$.  The stochastic logarithms and
brackets of $M^\varepsilon$ converge to those of $M$.  Consequently bounded
Girsanov transforms and Lipschitz risk-sensitive BSDEs driven by the observed
filtration commute with the microscopic limit.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A positive uniformly integrable martingale has stochastic logarithm
$dN_t=dM_t/M_t$ after localization.  Lemma
\ref{lem:r21-c2-innovation} represents $N$ as an innovation integral, giving
the exponential formula.  The conditional-kernel proof above, applied also
to products of likelihood increments, gives convergence of predictable
brackets.  Uniform $L^{1+\delta}$ bounds prevent loss of mass under Girsanov.
For a Lipschitz BSDE, subtract the microscopic and limiting equations, use the
bracket convergence and Burkholder--Davis--Gundy inequality, and apply
Gronwall.  Approximation by bounded terminal cylinders and uniform
integrability remove the localization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A nonvacuous contraction calculus}

A typed contraction consists only of concrete data:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a continuous state map $T:E\to F$ with $W_F\circ T\le CW_E$;
\item exact microscopic maps $T_\varepsilon$ with
\[
 \limsup a_\varepsilon^{-1}\log
 P(d(T_\varepsilon X_\varepsilon,TX_\varepsilon)>\eta)=-\infty;
\]
\item pullback of strong sources $T^*:G\mapsto G\circ T$;
\item a bounded map between the verified form domains commuting with the
state maps.
\end{enumerate}
No pressure, rate, covariance, or memory conclusion is part of this
definition.

\begin{theorem}[Verified contraction functor]
\label{thm:r21-c2-functor}
For every typed contraction above:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item good rates contract by
$I_F(y)=\inf_{Tx=y}I_E(x)$;
\item pressures obey $Q_F(G)=Q_E(T^*G)$;
\item where the pressures are twice differentiable, covariance and cotangent
forms push forward by $T$ and pull back by $T^*$;
\item if the form-domain map is block unitary on the resolved/complementary
splitting, the Schur memory transform commutes with contraction.
\end{enumerate}
The A3 collision-to-physical clock map, the B2 density/contact projections,
and the C1 observation/belief map satisfy the hypotheses and are closed under
composition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exponentially good approximation and exponential tightness give the
extended contraction principle, proving (i).  At finite volume the contracted
observable is exactly the pullback, so the exponential moments are equal;
pass to the locally uniform pressure limit to obtain (ii).  Locally uniform
analyticity permits differentiation of that identity twice, proving (iii).
For (iv), block-unitary conjugacy carries the complementary inverse and the
Schur form in Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-c2-schur} to their contracted counterparts.

For A3, the retained terminal prefix makes the two clock maps continuous on
rate sublevels.  For B2, marginalization of density/contact currents is
continuous in the weighted topology and the microscopic map is exact.  For
C1, the observation and Bayes maps are continuous on the reachable chart and
the null convention is explicit.  The weight, source, and form bounds were
proved in the respective papers.  Composition multiplies the weight constants
and adds two superexponentially small errors, so the class is nonempty and
closed under composition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-twenty-one C2 closure]
\label{thm:r21-c2-main}
The cotangent/contraction platform has an exact weighted strict dual, actual
invariant-measure coboundary separation, verified type-(B) form transport and
Schur compression, model-specific Sinai and hard-sphere rigidity, a
changing-filtration optional-projection theorem with innovation
representation, and a nonvacuous contraction calculus whose conclusions are
proved rather than assumed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r21-c2-dual},
\ref{thm:r21-c2-typeb}, \ref{thm:r21-c2-schur},
\ref{thm:r21-c2-rigidity}, \ref{thm:r21-c2-optional},
\ref{thm:r21-c2-girsanov}, and \ref{thm:r21-c2-functor}.
\end{proof}
