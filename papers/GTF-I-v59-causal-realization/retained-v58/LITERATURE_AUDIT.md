## Deep inputs and what is actually effective

Bourgain–Gamburd, *A spectral gap theorem in SU(d)*, arXiv:1108.6264, Theorem 1, is the spectral-gap input. Its algebraic dense-generation hypothesis is checked for the exact displayed rational pair by an elementary Lie-generation argument; lazification converts the upper spectral gap into an absolute contraction on the mean-zero space. The theorem is not a source of a computed spectral-gap constant here. The exact/robust exponential lower bound and the rational upper compiler do not use it.

The retained higher-dimensional existential example uses Breuillard–Gelander's dense-free-subgroup theorem. The 2026 correction note *On dense free subgroups of Lie groups — revisited*, arXiv:2605.20568v1, specifically repairs Proposition 2.7 and consequences concerning general connected Lie groups. It was checked rather than assuming all old references were unchanged. The new rational example does not require any generic dense-free existence theorem: its freeness and seed stabilizer are proved modulo five and by reduced words.

The inherited matrix-group effectivity discussion separates group-closure algorithms from finite-group existential-real feasibility. The primary de Graaf preprint arXiv:2505.02577v2 (9 January 2026) gives an algebraic closure algorithm avoiding Groebner-basis computations and reports an OSCAR implementation; that fact is not an implementation of this paper's entire closure/real-components/radius/minimization pipeline. The new least-orbit rank decision assumes exact algebraic infinitesimal matrices. The new qubit compiler instead constructs its rows explicitly and uses only rational linear systems; optional floating-point facet enumeration supplies candidates, and every row is independently validated by exact rational arithmetic with exhaustive fallback.

## New quantifiers, stated separately

The structural companion retains same width and same closed error, horizon-specific advice, arbitrary intervening widths, randomized initialization and equality at the finite-quotient boundary. These are not assertions that each ingredient is independently new. The quantitative article adds all-direction cap uniformity from a least-rank certificate, an explicit rational seed and alphabet fixed for all horizons, exact pointwise width profiles at zero and exponentially small noise, and a uniform rational table-construction algorithm. The latter is polynomial in the numerical horizon and inverse error, not their logarithms. Enlarging the legal matrix decoder set changes the problem.

No new claim of a log-free nonabelian law is made. No ordinary finite-memory theorem is obtained by silently ignoring the external clock or description costs. No independent review or journal-level significance verdict is inferred from this comparison.
