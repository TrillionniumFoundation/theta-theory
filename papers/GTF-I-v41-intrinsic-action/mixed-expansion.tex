\section{A faithful action without a full group gap}\label{sec:mixed41}
The action formulation is not merely a way to remove a superfluous
factor from the notation. Here the nonexpanding factor acts nontrivially
and is observed by the experiment.

\begin{proposition}[An expanding dominant summand is enough]\label{prop:mixed41}
Let $R_x,R_z\in\SO(3)$ be rotations about the first and third axes
with cosine $3/5$ and sine $4/5$, and let
\[
 \mu_3=\tfrac15(\delta_I+\delta_{R_x}+\delta_{R_x^{-1}}
                         +\delta_{R_z}+\delta_{R_z^{-1}}).
\]
Let $R\in\SO(2)$ have the same cosine and sine, and let
$\nu=\tfrac12(\delta_I+\delta_R)$. The ten block-diagonal commands
with product law $\mu_3\otimes\nu$ act faithfully on
$V=\R^3\oplus\R^2$. For seeds $\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_5\}$,
$0<\rho\le1/40$ and $0\le\varepsilon<\rho/(2\sqrt3)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed41}
 W_{N,\varepsilon}=\Theta(N).
\end{equation}
The effective group is $\SO(3)\times\SO(2)$ and its mean-zero regular
convolution norm is one. Thus \eqref{eq:mixed41} is outside the
full-effective-group-gap hypothesis as well as outside the full
presenting-group-gap hypothesis.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The complex number $(3+4i)/5$ is not a root of unity: a root of unity
and its inverse are algebraic integers, whereas their sum $6/5$ is
rational and not integral. The closure of its powers is therefore the
circle. Powers of $R_x$ and $R_z$ give the two continuous axis rotation
subgroups. Their infinitesimal generators and bracket span
$\mathfrak{so}(3)$, so they generate a dense subgroup of $\SO(3)$.
The product alphabet includes the identities needed to generate the two
factors separately, proving the assertion about the effective group.

The matrices are rational. Benoist--de Saxc\'e's algebraic-generator
theorem \cite[Theorem 1.2]{BdS} supplies a regular spectral gap on the
compact simple factor $\SO(3)$. Since $\mu_3$ is symmetric, its
operator is self-adjoint and spectral radius equals norm. (Its identity
mass also bounds the negative spectral edge away from $-1$.)
Proposition~\ref{prop:action41} gives $g_{\R^3}>0$.
On the effective circle, put $R=e^{2\pi i\alpha}$ in complex notation.
The functions $e^{2\pi i l\theta}$ have eigenvalues
$(1+e^{2\pi i l\alpha})/2$. Infinitely many $l$ have
$\|l\alpha\|\to0$, by the pigeonhole approximation argument. Their
moduli tend to one, proving the failure of a full product norm gap.

The two real irreducible summands have minimum orbit dimensions two
and one. The first is dominant and has $c=1/\sqrt3$: its standard
basis seeds have projection norm one and its coordinate-query norm
is $\sqrt3$. Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-width41} now applies with
$r=5$. The exact upper compiler uses $O(N)$ sphere labels and
$O(N^{1/2})$ circle labels. No Diophantine assumption on $\alpha$ and
no numerical value of the qualitative $\SO(3)$ gap is used.
\end{proof}

The external input in this example is the algebraic spectral-gap theorem
for the simple factor, not a newly proved expansion result. The conclusion
shows why requiring expansion on every activated type would also be
unnecessarily restrictive for a maximum-exponent law.
