\section{Geometric, probabilistic and computational boundaries}
\label{sec:comparison}
The new theorem concerns a growing execution constrained by a single
retained label. It neither changes the classical dimension of an orbit
nor identifies numerical simulation with a threshold language.

\subsection{What the covering exponent does and does not prove}
For a smooth compact $s$-manifold, covering by $O(h^{-s})$ small balls
is classical. Iacobelli's manifold quantization theorem
\cite[Theorem 1.4]{Iacobelli} concerns the minimum integrated
$r$th-power distance from a \emph{fixed measure} to a codebook of
$k$ points, with its $k^{-r/s}$ asymptotics under the stated hypotheses.
The codebook in that problem sees one sample from that measure;
there are no stochastic command rows to be implemented at all layers.
Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit-upper} uses the classical covering count, a
quadratic support loss, and an explicit stochastic realization. Its
new lower partner is not a static quantization lower bound. The
conditional means of a hidden machine generally leave the seed orbit,
and neither their distribution nor their orbit type is fixed.
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-orbits} controls all those directions at once;
the entropy budget then charges every small-state epoch to one finite
quadratic loss. This is the mechanism that prevents hidden lifts from
outperforming the geometric synthesis order.

Orbit convex hulls and their representation-theoretic geometry are
classical objects \cite{Barvinok,Orbitopes}. The covariance identity
and local covering calculation are reproved here to fix constants
and hypotheses, not claimed as new geometric discoveries. Our
minimum-orbit theorem does not give local volume asymptotics uniformly
for each spectrum; only its uniform exponent is sharp. The elementary
finite-rank chart argument also explains why no separate spectral
projector calculation is needed for a new representation. The earlier
projector argument is retained because it gives an explicit, albeit
large, constant in its matrix family.

\subsection{Positive rank and quantum dimension}
At a fixed cut, a numerical classical realization factors the matrix
of future response probabilities through $K_t$ positive normalized
state continuations. With one output alphabet this is a nonnegative
factorization; with several selectable queries, each decoder block
must normalize. Quantum prepare--measure probabilities instead admit
positive-semidefinite factorizations. These static dimension
relations are classical \cite{FMPST}. They do not by themselves
supply common transition rows connecting independently chosen
factorizations at successive cuts. In the projective experiment a
fixed $q$-dimensional quantum register supplies all layers, while the
compatible classical numerical width grows with $N$. No new quantum
operation, seed reset, or quantum-memory model is introduced by that
comparison.

\begin{proposition}[Probabilistic ordered programs]\label{prop:program}
Definition~\ref{def:machine} is equivalently a nonuniform ordered
read-once layered program with stochastic command-labelled edges and
real terminal columns. A selected column can be replaced by a
Bernoulli acceptance row without changing any command-layer width;
a retained final answer needs at most two labels.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Identify $T_{t,a}(s,s')$ with the outgoing edge weight at layer $t$.
Summing path probabilities gives \eqref{eq:machine}, and conversely
those edge weights define the stochastic rows. At terminal label $s$
the selected query accepts with probability $(1+d_j(s))/2$.
\end{proof}
The Fourier-growth and pseudorandomness results in
\cite{RSV,SVW,LPV} have their stated deterministic, regular or
permutation hypotheses and different objectives. The dominant action used by the converse
has a norm gap; hidden reset rows and nonregular matrices remain
allowed. Width is not total branching-program size, and real edge
tables are not a uniform finite-bit implementation.

\subsection{Expansion and implementation}
Expansion in the present main theorem is a property of the actual
command action on an activated irreducible space. It is unchanged by
adding unused presenting factors. Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-width41}
requires it only on one dominant active type; Proposition~\ref{prop:mixed41}
even allows a faithfully acting circle factor with no regular gap.
Full effective-group expansion remains a sufficient certificate via
Proposition~\ref{prop:action41}. The algebraic alphabets in
Proposition~\ref{prop:algebraic-gates} and in the mixed example use the
deep external theorems \cite{BdS,BG12}; their constants are qualitative.
Density or a finite harmonic calculation is not itself such a certificate.
The optional numerical six-gate result uses exactly the separate
external inequality \eqref{eq:LPS5}. Its original-source limitation
is recorded in Appendix~\ref{sec:normalization}.

Only one query is answered. A request for jointly independent answers
to all queries changes the channel and need not satisfy the
maximum law of Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-width41}. The model charges branch
identifiers, but not the description length or construction of the
row tables. Rational net coordinates do not make all transition
weights rational when commands are merely algebraic. Carath\'eodory
sparsity bounds successors, not table size. The separate fair-bit
compilers in the preserved development incur further states and
possibly error; they do not equate atomic width with bit complexity.

\subsection{Conclusions}
The effective action and the query-calibrated isotypic activation
remove presentation and multiplicity choices from the width theorem.
Arbitrary finite seeds first determine their invariant span. A single
expanding type of maximal minimum-orbit dimension then determines the
matched exponent; other genuinely acting types may fail to expand.
The upper realization charges its branch labels, while the lower bound
continues to use the actual entire hidden register.

The root-deletion and singular-value calculations identify minimum
orbits on the stated complete representation families. They do not
classify all highest weights. Likewise the width law does not specify
finite integer optima, sharp leading constants, arbitrary vanishing
signals, nongapped dominant types, or uniform computational resources.
Numerical probability preservation and strict-cutpoint recognition are
separated by an explicit constant-width attenuating machine. The
version-pinned comparison makes this semantic boundary independent of
which Chen--Wu upper constant is quoted.
