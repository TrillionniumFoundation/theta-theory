\section{The experiment and its positive realization}\label{sec:model}
In the general notation below, $G$ may be taken to be the effective
matrix group $H$ of Section~\ref{sec:action41}; $U_a=A_a$. The main
hypothesis is the constituent action gap, not the full regular gap of
an arbitrary presenting group. Older sufficient full-group hypotheses
are explicitly retained only where stated.
Let $G$ be a compact group, let $U:G\to O(V)$ be a continuous orthogonal
representation on $V=\R^D$, and fix a finite command alphabet
$\mathcal A\subset G$. A finite seed set $\mathcal X\subset\mathbb S(V)$
is given initially. For $w=(a_1,\ldots,a_N)$ write
$U_w=U_{a_N}\cdots U_{a_1}$. Relative to an orthonormal basis
$e_1,\ldots,e_D$, the prescribed numerical experiment is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
 \Pp(B=b\mid x,w,j)=\frac{1+b\rho\langle e_j,U_wx\rangle}{2},
 \quad b\in\{-1,1\},\quad j\in[D],\quad0<\rho\le1/10.
\end{equation}
All seeds, words and queries are required, not only almost-sure words
under one source law. The probabilities are between $(1-\rho)/2$ and
$(1+\rho)/2$.

\begin{definition}[Clocked atomic-row realization]\label{def:machine}
A horizon-$N$ machine consists of finite sets $\mathcal S_t$ with
$|\mathcal S_t|=K_t$, a stochastic initialization $E_x$ on $\mathcal S_0$,
stochastic matrices $T_{t,a}:\mathcal S_t\to\mathcal S_{t+1}$, and
columns $d_j:\mathcal S_N\to[-1,1]$. Its mean response is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:machine}
 E_xT_{0,a_1}\cdots T_{N-1,a_N}d_j.
\end{equation}
It has error at most $\varepsilon$ if this differs from
$\rho\langle e_j,U_wx\rangle$ by at most $2\varepsilon$ for every
$(x,w,j)$. Available labels, including padding, count. All rows, including
unreachable ones, are normalized. Only the updated label persists.
The seed, word prefix, old random choices and any data-dependent protocol
information cannot be consulted again unless represented by that label.
The external time, horizon, fixed program and exact sampling from its
rows are free. The final binary answer may be sampled when $j$ arrives;
if retained, it uses a two-label final cut. Write $W_{N,\varepsilon}$ for
the minimum of $\max(2,K_0,\ldots,K_N)$.
\end{definition}
The state alphabets and matrices may be chosen anew for each $N$, but the
command symbols and their experimental meanings are fixed. Nothing
requires one autonomous device or one decoder to serve all stopping
lengths. No adaptive choice of the command is optimized in this model.
A complex unitary representation can be treated by its realification;
all inner products in the entropy argument are real.

Fix one seed $x$ and an independent command law at each epoch for the
converse. Let $Y_t=U_{a_t}\cdots U_{a_1}x$ and let $S_t$ be the actual
machine state under this test law. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centroids}
 Z_t=\E[Y_t\mid S_t],\qquad q_t=\E\norm{Z_t},\qquad
 v_t=\E\norm{Z_t}^2.
\end{equation}
These random vectors and their laws are analytical devices, not an
additional runtime register or oracle.
\begin{lemma}[Quadratic budget and terminal calibration]\label{lem:terminal}
For the fresh command $a$, put $X_t=U_aZ_t$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional}
 Z_{t+1}=\E[X_t\mid S_{t+1}],\quad
 \Delta_t:=\E\norm{X_t-Z_{t+1}}^2=v_t-v_{t+1},\quad
 \sum_{t<N}\Delta_t\le1.
\end{equation}
The sequences $q_t,v_t$ are nonincreasing and start at one. A machine
with error at most $\varepsilon$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
 q_t\ge q_N\ge\kappa:=\rho/\sqrt D-2\varepsilon
 \qquad(0\le t\le N).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The current command is independent of the past, and the new label is
generated from $(S_t,a)$ by a common row. Conditioning first on
$(S_t,a,S_{t+1})$ and then on $S_{t+1}$ proves the first identity.
The orthogonal projection identity for conditional expectation gives
the second; Jensen and orthogonality give both monotonicities.
The fixed seed has unit norm, even with randomized initialization.
For calibration form $d=(d_1,\ldots,d_D)$ only in the proof, so
$\norm d\le\sqrt D$. Conditioning on the complete word and using
\eqref{eq:law}--\eqref{eq:machine} gives
$\E\langle Y_N,d(S_N)\rangle\ge\rho-2\sqrt D\varepsilon$.
On conditioning instead on $S_N$, this is at most $\sqrt D q_N$.
This proves \eqref{eq:kappa}. It does not require a joint measurement
of the $D$ coordinates.
\end{proof}
For each fixed profile, the optimum actual error is attained: all
stochastic rows and terminal columns form a finite product of compact
sets and the objective is a maximum of finitely many continuous
functions. Thus the subsequent lower bounds concern the actual
optimization, not a sum of local error certificates.
