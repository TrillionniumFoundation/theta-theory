\begin{abstract}
We study the nonuniform clocked atomic-row label width of numerical
experiments generated by a finite orthogonal command alphabet. We
formulate expansion on the effective action rather than on a presenting
group, and remove the dependence of calibration on a choice of
irreducible copies. A commutant covariance formula computes an intrinsic
isotypic activation magnitude; an optimized query norm gives a canonical
error threshold. For arbitrary finite seeds, let $R$ be their invariant
span and let $\sigma$ be the largest minimum unit-orbit dimension among
its nontrivial irreducible types. If one type attaining $\sigma$ has a
positive action gap, then at the stated fixed signal and error the
minimum hidden width is $\Theta(N^{\sigma/2})$. No expansion is required
on the other types. We exhibit a faithful rational alphabet with an
effective circle factor, no full group norm gap, and linear numerical
width. We compute the minimum-orbit invariant for all compact simple
adjoint representations and for classical defining and second exterior
power modules, including their minimizing stabilizers. The lower bound
retains the actual hidden register; the upper construction prices its
branch labels and realizes every numerical response exactly. Epoch,
horizon-specific design, tables, arithmetic and exact atomic sampling
remain uncharged; this is not a uniform-space theorem.
\end{abstract}
