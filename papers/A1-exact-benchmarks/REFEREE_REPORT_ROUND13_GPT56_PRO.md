## Executive assessment

Round 13 correctly separates the physical square from its zero-dimensional symbolic natural extension, uses the coupled map `(x,y) -> (sigma x,x_0 y)`, and no longer presents a symbolic inverse limit as a symplectic lamination. It also makes the exponential-family closure of the work tilt explicit. These are genuine corrections.

The new response space, however, is mathematically degenerate. Because its norm is an infimum over all refinements and every refinement is multiplied by factors satisfying `4 rho_- < 1` and `4 rho_+ < 1`, even the nonzero constant bulk density has norm zero. Thus the asserted Hausdorff Banach complex does not exist, and the all-order response theorem has no functional-analytic domain. The proposed “Hamiltonian impact realization” also remains a Hamiltonian flight with an externally prescribed canonical boundary relation, not an autonomous Hamiltonian flow whose return map is generated without a reset law.

## Decisive objections

### 1. The refinement norm annihilates the constant density

Let `U` be the bulk current whose density is identically one on the physical square and whose flag components vanish. Represent the same current on the partition generated by `m` past and `n` future seam levels. The restrictions still have `C^r` norm one. The number of cells is at most a fixed constant times `4^{m+n}`. Hence the definition gives

\[
 \|[U]\|_{r;\rho_-,\rho_+,\kappa}
 \le C(4\rho_-)^m(4\rho_+)^n.
\]

Because both `4 rho_-` and `4 rho_+` are strictly less than one, the right side tends to zero as the common refinement deepens. The norm is an infimum over precisely these equivalent presentations, so

\[
 \|[U]\|=0
\]

although `U` pairs nontrivially with the constant test form and is not the zero current.

This directly refutes the proof sentence claiming that a zero-norm class has zero pairing with every compatible test. The weights chosen to make refinement “summable” instead collapse the entire bulk space. The same problem affects nonzero face and flag currents.

### 2. The complete graded flag complex is therefore not Hausdorff

Once a nonzero bulk current has zero seminorm, quotient completion cannot repair the defect. One would have to normalize refinements by a mass-preserving projective/direct-limit norm, or take a supremum/compatible variation norm rather than an infimum with exponentially shrinking generation weights.

Consequently the following claims are unsupported:

- bounded physical push-forward on `mathfrak C_a^r`;
- closed flag boundary;
- bounded material derivatives `D_a^q`;
- absolute convergence of the response expansion; and
- the cancellation theorem for transported observables.

The central new theorem of the paper fails before any spectral argument is reached.

### 3. An exact Lagrangian impact graph is still an imposed reset relation

The manuscript constructs Hamiltonian motion inside disjoint flight chambers and, at the outgoing port, imposes the graph of `B_{a,i}` as an exact canonical impact relation. Exactness of that graph proves that the reset is symplectic. It does not prove that the reset is generated by the autonomous Hamiltonian vector field on the manifold with boundary.

A Hamiltonian trajectory reaches the boundary and stops unless the boundary faces are glued by a genuine symplectic identification or an independently specified impact law is applied. The manuscript chooses the latter. Calling the relation deterministic rather than stochastic does not turn it into a smooth autonomous Hamiltonian flow. The theorem should be typed as a hybrid symplectic impact system, not as an autonomous Hamiltonian realization in the ordinary sense.

### 4. The work coordinate is not incorporated into the symplectic impact system

The autonomous impact theorem uses `(q,p,tau,E)`. The later work construction merely appends an affine coordinate satisfying

\[
 u(1)=u(0)+c_i.
\]

No conjugate momentum, symplectic form, Hamiltonian, or canonical impact relation for this coordinate is given. The work variable is therefore an external additive cocycle, not a mechanical degree of freedom of the stated Hamiltonian impact system.

The exponential-family calculation for this cocycle is correct because the family was defined to be closed under the tilt. It does not establish a general mechanical selection principle beyond this engineered Bernoulli benchmark.

### 5. “All-order” response is not obtained from the stated finite-regularity spaces

For each finite `r`, the theorem permits only `q<r` derivatives and loses one regularity level per derivative. No Fréchet/projective limit over all `r`, uniform tame estimate, or analytic radius is constructed. Even after fixing the collapsing norm, the result would be finite-order response on a scale, not a single all-order theorem.

### 6. Corrected scope remains below the top-four novelty threshold

After replacing the degenerate current norm and honestly typing the impact relation, the surviving content is an explicit generalized baker coding, a designed exponential family, a work cocycle, and Hadamard differentiation of moving rectangular partitions. This may be useful as a benchmark note, but it does not approach the depth or novelty required by the four journals named above.

## Dependency and scope assessment

A1 is largely independent of the other ten manuscripts. It must not be cited as a completed Banach response calculus or as a smooth autonomous Hamiltonian derivation of the work tilt. At most it presently supplies the elementary symbolic likelihood identity.

## Required reconstruction

A viable revision must:

1. replace the refinement-infimum seminorm by a nondegenerate compatible current norm;
2. define the full flag direct/projective system and prove boundedness without shrinking every fixed current to zero;
3. distinguish a hybrid canonical impact law from a smooth Hamiltonian flow, or construct an actual symplectic gluing; and
4. integrate the work coordinate into a genuine symplectic extension if it is to be called mechanical.

## Recommendation

**Reject.** Round 13 fixes the symbolic geometry, but its main response space has a one-line zero-norm counterexample, and the claimed mechanical realization still relies on an imposed boundary relation. The corrected remainder is a benchmark rather than a top-four theorem.
