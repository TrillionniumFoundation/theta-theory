\section{Round-twenty-one positive closure: proper suspension, incidence-dual currents, and a proved Hilbert FCLT}
\label{sec:r21-a1}

This section replaces the former Round-Seventeen module.  The parameter family
is now part of the mathematical data rather than an assumption introduced in
a proof: the seams $s_i(a)$ are $C^\infty$ on a compact interval $K$, and
$w_i(a)=s_i(a)-s_{i-1}(a)\ge w_*>0$.  Finite-order statements require only the
corresponding finite number of derivatives.  The construction below never
restricts an arbitrary negative-Sobolev distribution to a face.  All traces
are taken on positive-regularity test jets and are then dualized.

\subsection{The labelled section and its proper mapping torus}

Let $\widehat S_a$ be the disjoint union of the closures of the rectangles
$S_{ijk}(a)$, with every oriented face and quadrant germ retained as part of
the label.  Thus two geometric points on a seam are distinct whenever their
one-sided germs are distinct.  On the component with active branch $j$ put
\[
 F_a(q,p;i,j,k)=\left({q-s_{j-1}\over w_j},
 s_{j-1}+w_jp; j,k,\ell\right),
\]
where the oriented germ of the first coordinate determines $\ell$.  The
horizontal target label records $j$, so the inverse branch is unique even on
a labelled face.

\begin{lemma}[Labelled baker diffeomorphism]
\label{lem:r21-a1-labelled}
The map $F_a:\widehat S_a\to\widehat S_a$ is a $C^\infty$ diffeomorphism of a
finite compact manifold with corners, stratum by stratum.  It preserves
$dq\wedge dp$ and satisfies
\[
 F_a^*(p\,dq)-p\,dq=dG_a
\]
for a smooth function $G_a$ on the labelled section.  The family
$a\mapsto(F_a,G_a)$ is $C^\infty$ in fixed affine cell charts.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On each labelled rectangle the derivative is
$\operatorname{diag}(w_j^{-1},w_j)$ and has determinant one.  The target
horizontal germ is the old branch $j$; together with the target vertical germ
it determines the unique inverse formula
$q=s_{j-1}+w_jQ$ and $p=(P-s_{j-1})/w_j$.  Incidence maps between labelled
faces are affine restrictions of these formulas, so the cellwise maps paste
as a diffeomorphism of the labelled corner complex.  Direct calculation gives
\[
 F_a^*(P\,dQ)-p\,dq={s_{j-1}\over w_j}\,dq
 =d\left[{s_{j-1}\over w_j}(q-s_{j-1})\right]
\]
on a branch.  Add constants, determined successively on the finite incidence
graph, so that the primitives agree on every labelled common face.  There is
no compatibility cycle obstruction because the sum of primitive differences
around a cycle is the primitive of the identity map and is therefore
constant; the constants remove it.  Smooth parameter dependence follows in
the fixed coordinates
$u=(q-s_{j-1})/w_j$, $v=(p-s_{i-1})/w_i$.
\end{proof}

Define the mapping torus
\[
 Q_a=(\widehat S_a\times[0,1])/
       ((x,1)\sim(F_a x,0)).
\]
Choose $\chi\in C^\infty([0,1])$ equal to zero near $0$ and one near $1$.
On the cylinder put
\[
 \Lambda_a=p\,dq+d(\chi(t)G_a),
 \qquad
 \Theta_a=\Lambda_a-E\,dt,
 \qquad
 \Omega_a=d\Theta_a.
\]
The trace at $t=1$ is $F_a^*$ of the trace at $t=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Proper exact Hamiltonian suspension]
\label{thm:r21-a1-suspension}
The space $Q_a$ is a Hausdorff, second-countable smooth manifold with corners.
The form $\Omega_a$ descends to an exact symplectic form on
$\mathcal M_a=Q_a\times\mathbb R_E$.  For the convention
$\iota_{X_H}\Omega_a=dH$, the Hamiltonian $H=E$ has $\dot t=1$, is complete,
and has first return time one and first return map $F_a$ on the section
$t=0$.  Forgetting the labels factors this return to the ordinary baker map
outside the null union of seam iterates.  No additional return occurs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a compact Hausdorff space and a homeomorphism, the equivalence relation
used in the mapping torus is closed: away from the two boundary collars it is
the diagonal, and on the collars it is the graph of $F_a$ or $F_a^{-1}$.
It is also proper because its equivalence classes have at most two cylinder
representatives.  Hence the quotient is Hausdorff and second countable.
Choose corner collars $U\times[0,\varepsilon)$ at the two cylinder ends.  The
map $(x,r)\mapsto(F_a x,r)$ identifies the collars and supplies the quotient
charts; compatibility follows from the ordinary cocycle identity for the
single diffeomorphism $F_a$.  This proves the smooth corner structure rather
than inferring it from separate face graphs.

The boundary equality
$\Lambda_a|_{t=1}=F_a^*(\Lambda_a|_{t=0})$ proves that $\Lambda_a$ descends.
Consequently so does $\Theta_a$, and
\[
 \Omega_a=d(p\,dq)+dt\wedge dE
\]
in every cylinder chart.  It is closed and nondegenerate, including on each
corner stratum.  Since $\iota_{\partial_t}\Omega_a=dE$, the Hamiltonian vector
field of $E$ is $\partial_t$.  Its orbit crosses the section exactly when the
clock passes an integer and the mapping-torus identification then applies
$F_a$.  Completeness follows from the complete translation flow in the clock
and the compactness of the section.
\end{proof}

Adjoin a work pair $(z,\pi_z)$ and replace $\Theta_a$ by
$\Theta_a-\pi_z\,dz$.  In a clock window disjoint from the gluing collars add
$\pi_zc_j\rho(t)$, where $\int_0^1\rho=1$ and $j$ is the retained branch
label.  The resulting globally smooth Hamiltonian translates $z$ by $c_j$ on
that return and leaves $(q,p)$ unchanged.

\subsection{An incidence-completed positive test scale}

Let $\mathscr F_n(a_0)$ be the finite family of all labelled bulk, face, and
corner strata obtained from forward and backward seam preimages of depth at
most $n$ at a fixed reference parameter $a_0$.  A fixed affine transport
$U_{F,a}:F(a_0)\to F(a)$ identifies every stratum throughout $K$; at a birth
the two oriented germs are retained separately, so no dimension-changing
identification is made.  There are constants $C_0,d_0$ with
$\#\mathscr F_n\le C_0d_0^n$.

Fix $0<\beta<d_0^{-2}$, integers $q\ge0$ and
$m>2q+6$, and write $c(F)$ for codimension.  Define the Hilbert test-jet space
\[
 \Phi_q^m=
 \bigoplus_{n\ge0}\ \bigoplus_{F\in\mathscr F_n}
 \ \bigoplus_{0\le r\le q-c(F)}
 \beta^{-n/2}H^{m+q+2-c(F)-r}
     (F;N_F^{\otimes r}).
\]
Repeated occurrences of the same geometric set retain their labels.  A
subdivision is represented by the orthogonal sum of its children, with the
parent component identified with their restriction-compatible diagonal.  The
closed span of these diagonal relations is denoted $\mathcal R_q^m$.  Put
\[
 \widehat\Phi_q^m=\Phi_q^m/\mathcal R_q^m,
 \qquad
 \mathbb J_q^m=(\widehat\Phi_q^m)' .
\]
Thus a current is a continuous functional on positive-regularity labelled
jets.  It is not an arbitrary ambient element of $H^{-m}$.

\begin{theorem}[Typed incidence calculus]
\label{thm:r21-a1-incidence}
For every $q$ the following maps are bounded on the displayed scale.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Restriction and normal differentiation of a test component to a child
      stratum, by the ordinary positive Sobolev trace theorem.
\item The adjoint extension of a child current to its parent and the signed
      cellular boundary map.  These are the only maps called face or corner
      maps on currents.
\item Pullback by $F_a$ on tests and hence push-forward by $F_a$ on currents.
      A depth shift has norm at most $C\sqrt\beta$ and is summable.
\item Refinement and coarsening maps; they are mutually inverse isometries on
      the quotient and preserve every label not removed by an internal-face
      relation.
\end{enumerate}
The spaces are separable Hilbert spaces, and distinct labelled currents cannot
cancel merely because their ambient distributions agree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All test exponents exceed the relevant codimension divided by two plus the
normal order, so the positive trace theorem applies before dualization.  Its
adjoint is an extension map, exactly the direction supplied by duality.  The
finite incidence matrices are triangular and have uniformly bounded entries
in the fixed affine charts.  A branch changes depth by at most one; after
squaring the norm, its combinatorial multiplicity is bounded by $d_0$ and its
weight gain is $\beta$, so $d_0\beta<1$.  The diagonal subdivision relations
are closed because restriction is bounded.  Quotients of Hilbert spaces by
closed subspaces are Hilbert, and the countable direct sum is separable.
Orthogonal labels remain different coordinates until an explicit
internal-face relation identifies them, so ambient cancellation is impossible.
\end{proof}

Let $V_{F,a}=\partial_aU_{F,a}\circ U_{F,a}^{-1}$.  On the smooth test core
define
\[
 \mathcal A_a\phi=\partial_a\phi+
 \mathcal L_{V_{F,a}}\phi
\]
componentwise, including the induced normal-bundle connection.  Its transpose
defines the material derivative
\[
 \langle\mathfrak D_aJ,\phi\rangle
 =-\langle J,\mathcal A_a\phi\rangle .
\]

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-fibre material derivative]
\label{prop:r21-a1-derivative}
For every $q$ the operator $\mathfrak D_a$ is bounded from
$\mathbb J_{q+1}^{m+2}$ to $\mathbb J_q^m$, uniformly for $a\in K$, and is
therefore closed after taking the graph closure of the smooth core.  Moreover
\[
 \|\partial_a^r(F_a)_\#J\|_{\mathbb J_q^m}
 \le C_q^{r+1}r!\,
 \|J\|_{\mathbb J_{q+r}^{m+2r}}
\]
for an analytic seam family, and with $r!$ replaced by the corresponding
$C^r$ seminorm for a merely $C^r$ family.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the fixed charts, $\mathcal A_a$ is a first-order differential operator
with smooth bounded coefficients and one bounded triangular incidence term.
It maps $\widehat\Phi_q^m$ continuously into
$\widehat\Phi_{q+1}^{m+2}$; transposition gives the stated bounded map in the
opposite direction.  Hence if $J_n\to0$ in the domain space and
$\mathfrak D_aJ_n\to K$ in the target, then for every test jet $\phi$,
\[
 \langle K,\phi\rangle
 =\lim_n-\langle J_n,\mathcal A_a\phi\rangle=0,
\]
so $K=0$.  This is the fixed-fibre graph criterion for closability.  Repeated
differentiation gives finite Fa\`a di Bruno sums of coefficient derivatives,
Lie derivatives, and bounded incidence maps.  Cauchy estimates for an
analytic family give the factorial bound; the depth weight controls every
preimage shift.
\end{proof}

\subsection{All-order response on the coupled Bernoulli extension}

Let $\Sigma=\{1,2,3,4\}^{\mathbb Z}$ with product law
$\mu_a=\bigotimes_{k\in\mathbb Z}(w_1(a),\ldots,w_4(a))$ and shift $\sigma$.
The coding of the regular baker section is a measure isomorphism.  On future
and past one-sided H\"older spaces define
\[
 P_a^+f(x)=\sum_iw_i(a)f(ix),
 \qquad
 P_a^-g(y)=\sum_iw_i(a)g(iy).
\]
Their centered powers contract by $\rho^n$ for one common $\rho<1$.  Denote
the reduced resolvents by $R_a^\pm$.  A mixed cylinder is conditioned on its
present symbol; this finite-rank conditioning operator is denoted $C_a$.
Scores are the centered multipliers
$S_a^{(r)}(i)=\partial_a^r\log w_i(a)$ with their lower-order Bell
corrections.

For a rooted ordered tree $T$ with total derivative order $r$, label every
vertex by one of: a material derivative $\mathfrak D_a^{(j)}$, a future score
$S_a^{(j)}R_a^+$, a past score $S_a^{(j)}R_a^-$, or a derivative of the finite
present coupling $C_a$.  Let $\mathcal T_r$ be the finite set of such trees and
$b(T)$ its ordinary Fa\`a di Bruno coefficient.

\begin{theorem}[Convergent response tree formula]
\label{thm:r21-a1-response}
If $J_a\in\mathbb J_{r+1}^{m+2r+2}$ is a $C^{r+1}$ transported current, then
\[
 {d^r\over da^r}\int J_a\,d\mu_a
 =\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_r}b(T)\,\Lambda_a(\mathcal O_TJ_a),
\]
where every composition $\mathcal O_T$ is typed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-a1-incidence} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:r21-a1-derivative}.  The sum is the limit of the exact
finite-cylinder derivative and obeys
\[
 \sum_{T\in\mathcal T_r}|b(T)|
 \|\Lambda_a\mathcal O_T\|
 \le C_r(1-\rho)^{-2r-2}.
\]
It is invariant under dynamic refinement.  For a fixed physical observable,
the material, score, and incidence contributions sum to zero, as required by
Lebesgue invariance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a cylinder depending on coordinates $[-N,N]$, differentiation of the
finite product density and of the transported current is ordinary finite
dimensional calculus.  Center every score.  Successively condition the
future and past tails and solve the two Poisson equations; this inserts
$R_a^+$ and $R_a^-$.  Conditioning at coordinate zero gives $C_a$.  Repeating
Leibniz' rule produces exactly the ordered trees and their Bell coefficients.
The resolvents have norm at most $(1-\rho)^{-1}$, and the material and
incidence operators have the bounds proved above.

If $J_a^{(N)}$ is the conditional cylinder approximation, the Bernoulli tail
and the depth weight give
$\|J_a-J_a^{(N)}\|\le C(\sqrt{d_0\beta})^N$, uniformly with all derivatives
through order $r$.  Hence the finite formulas converge absolutely and may be
differentiated term by term.  Refinement changes a presentation by an element
of $\mathcal R_q^m$, annihilated by every quotient functional.  Finally, for
a fixed physical observable, the exact change-of-variables identity
$\int F\circ F_a\,dqdp=\int F\,dqdp$ holds for every $a$; differentiating the
already justified response formula yields the stated cancellation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Hilbert-valued projective estimate and FCLT}

Let $Y_k^{(q)}$ be the centered order-$q$ current increment, coded on
$\Sigma$ and taking values in $\mathbb J_q^m$.  Let
$\mathcal F_j=\sigma(x_i:i\le j)$.  The depth-$n$ part of $Y_0^{(q)}$ depends
only on symbols in $[-n,n]$ and its squared norm is bounded by
$C(d_0\beta)^n$.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit projective summability]
\label{lem:r21-a1-projective}
There are $C<\infty$ and $0<\vartheta<1$ such that
\[
 \|\mathbb E(Y_0^{(q)}\mid\mathcal F_{-n}^0)
 -\mathbb E(Y_0^{(q)}\mid\mathcal F_{-n+1}^0)\|_{L^2(\mathbb J_q^m)}
 \le C\vartheta^n.
\]
The same estimate holds for the future filtration.  Consequently the two
projective series and the series of trace norms of covariance operators are
summable.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conditional expectations cancel all components whose oldest required symbol
is earlier than $-n$.  What remains is the orthogonal sum of strata of depth
at least $n$.  Its squared $L^2$ norm is at most
$C\sum_{r\ge n}(d_0\beta)^r$.  Take
$\vartheta=\sqrt{d_0\beta}<1$.  For covariance, write for $k>0$
\[
 \|\mathbb E(Y_0\otimes Y_k)\|_1
 \le \|Y_0\|_{L^2}
      \|\mathbb E(Y_k\mid\mathcal F_0)\|_{L^2}
 \le C\vartheta^k,
\]
using the rank-one trace norm $\|u\otimes v\|_1=\|u\|\|v\|$ and the future
projective estimate.  Negative $k$ is identical.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Radon current-valued functional central limit theorem]
\label{thm:r21-a1-fclt}
The series
\[
 \Sigma_q=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
 \mathbb E(Y_0^{(q)}\otimes Y_k^{(q)})
\]
converges absolutely in trace norm and defines a positive trace-class
operator.  Moreover
\[
 n^{-1/2}\sum_{j<nt}Y_j^{(q)}
 \Longrightarrow W_q(t)
\]
in $D([0,1],\mathbb J_q^m)$, where $W_q$ is the Radon Brownian motion with
covariance $\Sigma_q$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-a1-projective} gives the Hilbert Gordin decomposition
$Y_j=M_j+H\circ\sigma^{j+1}-H\circ\sigma^j$, with $M_j$ a stationary
square-integrable martingale difference and $H\in L^2(\mathbb J_q^m)$.  The
conditional bracket averages converge in trace norm by the ergodic theorem
applied first to finite-rank projections and then using the summable trace
bound.  The Hilbert martingale invariance principle follows from the scalar
martingale theorem on every finite-rank projection, the Lindeberg estimate
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl[\|M_0\|^2
  \mathbf1_{\{\|M_0\|>\varepsilon\sqrt n\}}\bigr]\longrightarrow0,
\]
and compact containment obtained from the trace tails of the bracket.

For the coboundary,
\[
 \max_{j\le n}\|H\circ\sigma^j\|/\sqrt n\longrightarrow0
\]
in probability because $H\in L^2$ implies
$n\,\mathbb P(\|H\|>\varepsilon\sqrt n)\to0$.  Hence its two endpoint terms
are uniformly negligible.  The trace-class covariance gives a Radon Gaussian
law on the separable Hilbert space, completing tightness and identification.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-twenty-one A1 closure]
\label{thm:r21-a1-main}
The benchmark admits a proper exact autonomous Hamiltonian suspension, an
incidence-completed labelled current calculus with a fixed-fibre closed
material derivative, a convergent all-order response formula, and a
trace-class current-valued functional CLT.  Each construction is made in the
active manuscript and none uses restriction of an arbitrary negative
Sobolev distribution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r21-a1-suspension},
\ref{thm:r21-a1-incidence}, \ref{thm:r21-a1-response}, and
\ref{thm:r21-a1-fclt} with
Proposition~\ref{prop:r21-a1-derivative}.
\end{proof}
