\section{Referee-round positive closure: common path typing and a genuine collision suspension}
\label{sec:rr2-a1}

This section is controlling for the interpretation of the benchmark.  It
keeps the exact four-branch calculations, but supplies the two structures
that were implicit in the first circulation: a common path space for all
parameter values and an explicit deterministic collision suspension whose
return map is the baker family.

\subsection{The common symbolic factor}

Let
\[
 \Sigma=\{1,2,3,4\}^{\mathbb Z},\qquad
 \sigma(\omega)_k=\omega_{k+1},
\]
and let \(\nu_a\) be the Bernoulli law with weights \(w(a)\).  Away from the
seam orbit, the itinerary map
\[
 \mathfrak c_a:[0,1]^2\longrightarrow\Sigma,\qquad
 (\mathfrak c_a z)_k=i \Longleftrightarrow B_a^kz\in I_i(a)\times[0,1],
\]
is defined for every \(k\in\mathbb Z\).

\begin{theorem}[Common-path coding and typed change of law]
\label{thm:rr2-a1-common-path}
For every \(a\in(-1,1)\), \((\mathfrak c_a)_\#m=\nu_a\).  If
\(F:\Sigma\to\mathbb R\) is bounded and depends on finitely many coordinates,
then for \(a=\Lambda'(\theta)\),
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E_m\!\left[
 e^{\theta A_n(\mathfrak c_{a_0}z)}
 e^{\theta F(\mathfrak c_{a_0}z)}\right]}
 {\mathbb E_m e^{\theta A_n(\mathfrak c_{a_0}z)}}
 =
 \mathbb E_m e^{\theta F(\mathfrak c_a z)} .
\]
More generally, for coordinate observables \(G_a\) on the square, the same
identity holds precisely when their symbolic pullbacks agree:
\(G_a=F\circ\mathfrak c_a\) almost everywhere for one common \(F\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cylinder calculation in Proposition~\ref{prop:natural} gives
\((\mathfrak c_a)_\#m=\nu_a\).  The Radon--Nikodym derivative of
\(\nu_a^{(n)}\) relative to \(\nu_{a_0}^{(n)}\) is
\(\exp\{\theta A_n-n\Lambda(\theta)\}\).  Integrating the common measurable
function \(e^{\theta F}\) proves the identity.  The last assertion follows by
applying the identity to the pullbacks; without a common pullback there is no
single random variable whose law is being changed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A deterministic collision suspension}

For each branch \(i\), let
\[
 C_i(a)=I_i(a)\times[0,1]\times[0,1]
\]
be a flight channel with coordinates \((x,y,s)\).  Motion is uniform in the
flight coordinate, \(\dot s=1\), until \(s=1\).  At the terminal wall apply
the canonical reset
\[
 R_i^a(x,y,1)=
 \left(
  \frac{x-s_{i-1}(a)}{w_i(a)},
  s_{i-1}(a)+w_i(a)y,
 0
 \right).
\]
The Jacobian on the transverse coordinates equals one, so the reset preserves
the collision two-form \(dx\wedge dy\).  Glue the terminal face of \(C_i(a)\)
to the incoming section by \(R_i^a\).

\begin{theorem}[Mechanical collision-network realization]
\label{thm:rr2-a1-suspension}
The resulting piecewise smooth collision flow is deterministic, preserves
\(dx\,dy\,ds\), and has \(B_a\) as its first-return map to the incoming
section \(s=0\).  The signed branch observable is the flux of the port
one-form
\[
 \eta=c_i\,ds\quad\hbox{on }C_i(a),
\]
in the sense that the integral of \(\eta\) over one flight equals \(c_i\).
Thus \(A_n\) is an actual additive collision-port work of this suspension.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Between collisions the volume form is invariant.  The reset has transverse
Jacobian
\(
 w_i(a)^{-1}w_i(a)=1
\)
and leaves the flight coordinate fixed at the glued boundary, hence the
glued volume is invariant.  Reading the transverse reset at \(s=1\) gives
exactly the displayed formula for \(B_a\).  Finally,
\(\int_0^1 c_i\,ds=c_i\), and summing over flights gives \(A_n\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conjugate-field calibration}

The current preparation determines a canonical field; an entropic valuation
uses the same scalar only after a work-unit identification.  The required
identification is now stated as a theorem rather than left in prose.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness of the calibrated work field]
\label{thm:rr2-a1-calibration}
Let \(\mathcal W\) be the linear space generated by bounded cylinder
perturbations of the collision-port action.  Suppose a cash-additive
log-partition functional has the form
\[
 \mathfrak E_\beta(F)=\beta^{-1}
 \log\mathbb E_{\nu_a}e^{\beta F},\qquad F\in\mathcal W,
\]
and is required to be invariant under simultaneous change of the current
unit \(c\mapsto rc\), \(F\mapsto rF\).  If the prepared canonical density is
\(\exp\{\theta A_n-n\Lambda(\theta)\}\), then the dimensionless coefficient
on \(\mathcal W\) is uniquely \(\beta=\theta\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After the unit change, the same canonical density is represented by the
coefficient \(\theta/r\), while the valuation coefficient is \(\beta/r\).
Compatibility of the valuation with the prepared path partition function
requires equality on the generator \(A_n\), hence \(\beta/r=\theta/r\).
Taking \(r=1\) gives \(\beta=\theta\).  The conclusion is deliberately
restricted to the collision-work space \(\mathcal W\); no unrelated
preference parameter is inferred.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Explicit response words}

Let \(\Pi_a h=(\int h\,dm)\mathbf1\), \(Q_a=I-\Pi_a\), and
\[
 S_a=(I-L_a)^{-1}Q_a=\sum_{n\ge0}L_a^nQ_a
\]
on \(X_r\).  A response word is
\[
 W_{\mathbf k,\mathbf n}(a)
 =
 L_a^{n_0}(\partial_a^{k_1}L_a)L_a^{n_1}\cdots
 (\partial_a^{k_j}L_a)L_a^{n_j}Q_a .
\]

\begin{proposition}[Summable reduced-resolvent arrays]
\label{prop:rr2-a1-words}
If \(\sum_{\ell=1}^j k_\ell\le q\), then
\[
 \sum_{\mathbf n\in\mathbb N^{j+1}}
 \|W_{\mathbf k,\mathbf n}(a)\|_
 {W^{r+q,1}\to W^{r,1}}
 \le
 C_{K,r,q,j}(1-\rho_K)^{-(j+1)}
\]
uniformly for \(a\in K\Subset(-1,1)\).  Consequently every finite derivative
of \(S_a\), every bilateral array obtained by inserting a bounded functional
on the left, and every finite parameter difference obtained by Taylor's
formula converges in the stated operator norm.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each parameter derivative loses at most its order in the Sobolev ladder.
Between insertions, Theorem~\ref{thm:response} contributes
\(\rho_K^{n_\ell}\).  Multiplying the insertion bounds and summing the
\(j+1\) independent geometric series proves the estimate.  Differentiating
\((I-L_a)S_a=Q_a\) expands derivatives of \(S_a\) into finite sums of these
words.  Taylor's integral remainder is bounded by the next derivative and
the same uniform sum.
\end{proof}
