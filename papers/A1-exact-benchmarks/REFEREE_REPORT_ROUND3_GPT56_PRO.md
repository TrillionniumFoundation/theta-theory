## Executive assessment

This revision makes several real corrections. It places the Bernoulli laws on one symbolic path space, restricts coordinate observables to common pullbacks, strengthens the fixed-window statement to a uniformly rooted process statement, distinguishes a mechanical conjugate field from an unrelated preference coefficient, and finally defines the response words that were previously only named.

Those improvements do not validate the new headline theorem. The alleged autonomous Hamiltonian impact realization is not a well-defined Hamiltonian system on the announced phase space. The central affine baker branch is written in cut-square coordinates but is declared to be a symplectic map of a torus; the proposed Hamiltonian `qp` is not a globally defined function on that torus. The proof therefore does not turn the engineered Bernoulli baker family into an independently specified mechanical collision model. After removing this invalid mechanical layer, the remaining content is an exact symbolic benchmark built from standard product-measure calculations and elementary affine transfer operators, below the novelty threshold of the four journals named above.

## Fatal mathematical objections

### 1. The branch map is not a well-defined symplectomorphism of the announced torus

The section is declared to be

\[
\mathcal S=\mathbb T^2
\]

with canonical coordinates `(q,p)`, while on the port `I_i(a) × T` the second coordinate is sent to

\[
p\longmapsto s_{i-1}(a)+w_i(a)p.
\]

This is not a well-defined map of the circle. The representatives `p=0` and `p=1` define the same point of `T`, but their images differ by `w_i(a)`, which is not an integer. The formula is meaningful only on a cut interval `[0,1)`, not on the quotient circle. Likewise the full baker map is a piecewise map of a cut square with seams, not a globally defined smooth return diffeomorphism of `T^2`.

A Poincaré map of a smooth Hamiltonian flow on a transverse section is locally a smooth symplectomorphism. A discontinuous impact correspondence can be used instead, but then the manifold with boundary, port gluing, impact relation, and invariant flux measure must be defined explicitly. None of that structure is supplied.

### 2. The proposed Hamiltonian is not globally defined on `T^2`

The middle layer uses

\[
H_i^a(q,p)=-(\log w_i(a))qp.
\]

The functions `q`, `p`, and `qp` are not globally defined smooth functions on a torus. They depend on chosen lifts to the unit square. Consequently the displayed vector field is not an autonomous Hamiltonian vector field on the announced phase space.

Saying that one composes translations, a dilation, and then “autonomizes with a clock pair” constructs at most a local canonical map in Euclidean coordinates. It does not construct a global autonomous Hamiltonian flow whose return section is the stated torus. The port-dependent choice of branch is itself discontinuous at the seams and is not generated by one globally specified Hamiltonian.

### 3. The hard-wall regularization paragraph does not repair the obstruction

Replacing an impulsive layer by a narrow bump in the clock variable does not make `qp` periodic, does not define the branch selector smoothly, and does not prove that a single smooth autonomous Hamiltonian has the exact or limiting scattering map on all ports. The claim of `C^r` convergence on compact port subsets is asserted without a global phase space, energy surface, boundary conditions, or error estimate.

A viable mechanical theorem would require a cut symplectic surface or a disjoint union of ports, an explicit symplectic gluing relation, and an autonomous suspension theorem proved in that category. The current statement is false as written.

### 4. The “canonical-cocycle uniqueness” proof assumes the form it is meant to derive

Assumption (c) says that “exponentiating the value gives a positive linear conditional functional,” but it does not specify which exponential map or why cash additivity forces a representation `T(e^{beta F})` for one scalar `beta`. The proof simply introduces such a scalar and then identifies it with `theta`.

To make this rigorous, the paper must state a functional equation on a specified Banach lattice, prove the exponential representation from the axioms, and then use the likelihood cocycle to identify its coefficient. In its present form the result is close to a restatement of the desired representation.

### 5. The controlling manuscript is not standalone

The round-three `main.tex` contains only the preamble, abstract, and `\input{ROUND3_POSITIVE_CLOSURE.tex}`. The controlling module uses `w_i(a)`, `s_i(a)`, `B_a`, `a_0`, `Lambda`, `A_n`, `L_a`, `Pi_a`, and `rho_K` without defining all of them in the active manuscript. Successful LaTeX compilation does not detect undefined mathematical objects.

A top-journal submission must contain a logically complete definition chain, not depend on superseded text that has been deleted from the controlling source.

### 6. The corrected symbolic core does not meet the novelty threshold

Once the mechanical claim is removed, the substantive results are:

- exponential tilting of a four-symbol product law;
- hypergeometric-to-Bernoulli approximation at a uniformly chosen root;
- a conditional log-Laplace tower; and
- derivative bounds for an affine transfer operator with an explicit contraction.

These are useful validation examples. They are not an *Annals/Acta/Inventiones/JAMS*-level theorem.

## Editorial recommendation

**Reject.** The revision improves the probabilistic typing and the honesty of the calibration statement, but its new mechanical centerpiece is not a well-defined Hamiltonian system. A publishable benchmark note should keep the common symbolic space, random-root conditioning, and explicit response arrays, delete the claim of an autonomous Hamiltonian collision realization unless it is rebuilt on a correct cut symplectic phase space, and present the entropic coefficient as a calibrated modeling identification.
