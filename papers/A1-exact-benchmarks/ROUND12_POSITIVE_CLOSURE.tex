\section{Round-twelve positive closure: target-refined Hamiltonian solenoids and graded cut currents}
\label{sec:r12-a1}

The physical square, the symbolic natural extension, and the geometric
suspension are kept as three different objects.  The new point is that the
geometric object is refined simultaneously by source and target labels at
every depth; no connected source sheet is asked to jump continuously between
disconnected target sheets.

\subsection{The source--target refinement tower}

Let $I_i(a)=[s_{i-1}(a),s_i(a))$ and let $B_a$ be the four-branch exact
symplectic baker map.  For a word
$\mathbf i=(i_{-m},\ldots,i_n)$ put
\[
 C_{\mathbf i}(a)=\{z:B_a^kz\in I_{i_k}(a)\times\mathbb T,
                    -m\le k\le n\}.
\]
Every nonempty $C_{\mathbf i}$ is completed by all oriented face germs.  The
finite family of completed cells of depth $(m,n)$ is denoted
$\mathscr S_a^{m,n}$.  Common refinement gives bonding maps, and
\[
 \mathscr S_a^\infty=\varprojlim_{m,n}\mathscr S_a^{m,n}
\]
is the target-refined section.

\begin{theorem}[Hausdorff symplectic refinement and coupled return]
\label{thm:r12-a1-refinement}
The space $\mathscr S_a^\infty$ is a compact Hausdorff second-countable
laminated space with two-dimensional symplectic leaves and a totally
disconnected transversal.  The lift $\widehat B_a$ is a leafwise exact
symplectic homeomorphism and, on every finite cell, a smooth exact
symplectomorphism with corners.  Its symbolic factor is the coupled natural
extension
\[
 \widehat\sigma(x,y)=(\sigma x,x_0y),
 \qquad x\in\mathcal A^{\mathbb N_0},\ y\in\mathcal A^{\mathbb N}.
\]
The projections to the physical square are one-to-one off the complete seam
orbit and push the Bernoulli product measure to Lebesgue measure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At depth $(0,1)$ the source is cut into the sixteen cells
$C_{ij}=\{z:z\in I_i\times\mathbb T,\ B_az\in I_j\times\mathbb T\}$.
The restriction $B_a:C_{ij}\to B_aC_{ij}$ has a fixed target component.
Repeating the construction at positive and negative times makes every
component map constant on each finite cell.  Oriented germ completion makes
the bonding maps closed and separates every pair of points which differ at a
first seam choice.  Hence the inverse limit is compact Hausdorff and
second-countable.  In affine coordinates every restriction has determinant
one and pulls back $dq\wedge dp$ to itself; these forms agree under refinement,
so they define the leafwise symplectic form.  The return shifts the itinerary
and inserts the used present symbol into the past, giving the displayed
coupled map.  Cylinder rectangles have Lebesgue mass equal to the corresponding
Bernoulli cylinder mass, which proves the measure statement by monotone class.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact collars and an autonomous suspension}

A face of a refined cell carries its full source and target germ.  Thus an
outgoing face is never glued to two different incoming components.  We use the
following relative extension rather than cutting off a generating Hamiltonian.

\begin{lemma}[Relative exact-symplectic collar extension]
\label{lem:r12-a1-collar}
Let $R_-$ and $R_+$ be compact rectangles with corner germs and let
$F:R_-\to R_+$ be an affine exact symplectomorphism matching the prescribed
face-germ bijection.  There is a compact exact symplectic cobordism
$(W,d\lambda)$, a Hamiltonian $K_t$ constant to all orders in the incoming and
outgoing collar coordinates, and an identification of its end sections for
which the time-one map is exactly $F$ on the closed rectangles.  The
construction is functorial under common refinement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the graph of $F$ as an exact Lagrangian in
$(R_-\times R_+, -dq\wedge dp\oplus dq\wedge dp)$.  The primitive difference
$F^*(p\,dq)-p\,dq=dS$ fixes the boundary jet of a generating function.  Extend
$S$ to the doubled rectangle by the Whitney extension formula applied
separately on every compatible face flag.  The Hamiltonian isotopy generated
by this extension agrees with $F$ on the whole closed source, not merely on an
interior set.  Moser's equation with zero relative boundary data corrects the
interpolating two-form; its vector field vanishes to all orders in the end
collars.  Uniqueness of the relative solution makes restriction to a finer
cell equal to the refinement of the coarse solution.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Autonomous Hamiltonian solenoid]
\label{thm:r12-a1-suspension}
There is a compact symplectic lamination $\mathcal M_a$ with four-dimensional
leaves and a smooth leafwise autonomous Hamiltonian $\mathscr H_a$ whose
first-return map on $\mathscr S_a^\infty$ is $\widehat B_a$.  Every regular
physical orbit has one lift, and its return time is one.  The construction is
a genuine Hamiltonian flow on each leaf; no reset relation is appended after
the flow.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:r12-a1-collar} on every finite source--target cell and
pass to the compatible inverse limit.  Glue the end collars to form the
mapping torus.  Autonomize the periodic Hamiltonian with a clock pair
$(\tau,E)$ and Hamiltonian $\mathscr H_a=E+K_\tau$.  Compatibility under
refinement gives a Hausdorff laminated quotient, while the Hamiltonian vector
fields agree on overlaps.  Since $\dot\tau=1$, the return time is one and the
return map is the exact cell map, hence $\widehat B_a$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Global work and the graded current complex}

Let $c_i$ be the work attached to branch $i$.  The cocycle
$c(\widehat z)=c_{x_0}$ defines an affine line bundle over the mapping torus:
$(z,u,1)\sim(\widehat B_az,u+c(z),0)$.  Its flat connection is the global work
connection; no channelwise one-form is required to agree across unlike
channels.

\begin{proposition}[Exact work cocycle]
\label{prop:r12-a1-work}
The holonomy of the work connection along the first $n$ returns is exactly
$A_n=\sum_{k<n}c_{x_k}$.  Canonical tilting by $\theta A_n$ changes the
Bernoulli weights to the member $a=\Lambda'(\theta)$ of the same family.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The affine transition adds $c(x_k)$ at the $k$th gluing, so holonomies add.
The Radon--Nikodym derivative is the product of the four one-step likelihood
ratios and therefore equals
$\exp\{\theta A_n-n\Lambda(\theta)\}$.
\end{proof}

For a finite cell complex $\mathscr P$ let $\mathfrak F_k(\mathscr P)$ be its
oriented codimension-$k$ flags.  A graded current is
$U=(U_{C,F})$ with $F\in\mathfrak F_k(C)$ and norm
\[
 \|U\|_{r,\rho,\kappa}
 =\sum_{C}\rho^{|C|}\sum_{k\ge0}\kappa^k
   \sum_{F\in\mathfrak F_k(C)}\|U_{C,F}\|_{C^{r-k}}.
\]
We impose $4\rho<1$ and $M\kappa<1$, where $M$ is the uniform number of faces
created in one refinement.

\begin{theorem}[Complete flag-current calculus]
\label{thm:r12-a1-currents}
The direct limit over common refinements, modulo oriented interior-face
cancellation, completes to a Banach complex $\mathfrak C_a^r$.  Refinement,
push-forward by $\widehat B_a$, and the boundary operator are bounded.  The
material derivative satisfies
\[
 D_a:\mathfrak C_a^r\longrightarrow\mathfrak C_a^{r-1},
 \qquad D_aU=D_a^{\rm bulk}U+\partial_a^{\rm Had}U,
\]
where $\partial_a^{\rm Had}$ raises flag codimension by one.  Consequently
$D_a^q$ contains exactly the flags of codimension at most $q$ and is bounded
for every fixed $q\le r$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A common refinement subdivides a cell into at most $4^n$ descendants and each
face into at most $M^n$ flags.  The two geometric weights make the resulting
series summable.  Affine pullback has uniformly bounded trace Jacobians on a
compact parameter interval.  Oriented contributions on an artificial
interior face cancel, so the quotient is presentation independent and closed.
The Reynolds transport formula on a polyhedron gives a bulk derivative plus
one face current.  Iterating it gives the standard flag formula; the factor
$M\kappa<1$ controls all new incidences.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Response on the coupled extension}

Let $P_a^+$ be the future Ruelle operator and let $P_a^-$ be the conditional
past operator.  The present-symbol insertion
$J_af(x,y)=f(\sigma x,x_0y)$ couples them.  On mixed cylinders use the
projective tensor norm built from the two one-sided Hölder norms and the flag
current norm.

\begin{theorem}[Typed physical response]
\label{thm:r12-a1-response}
For every $q<r$ and every transported physical observable represented in
$\mathfrak C_a^r$, the $q$th parameter derivative of its expectation exists
and is the absolutely convergent sum of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item score insertions in the Bernoulli likelihood;
\item the one-sided reduced resolvents of $P_a^+$ and $P_a^-$ joined by $J_a$;
\item the bulk and all flag-current Hadamard insertions from
      Theorem~\ref{thm:r12-a1-currents}.
\end{enumerate}
For an actually fixed physical observable the three groups cancel and the
response is zero, as required by invariance of Lebesgue measure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate first on a finite common refinement.  Conditional expectation
with respect to future and past gives the two one-sided Poisson equations; the
used present symbol is inserted by $J_a$, so no false product dynamics occurs.
The transport theorem supplies the graded face terms.  The one-sided spectral
gaps give geometric summability in time, and the two refinement weights give
absolute summability in depth and codimension.  Dominated convergence passes
to the inverse limit.  If the physical observable is fixed, the differentiated
change-of-variables identity has zero left side, proving the stated
cancellation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-twelve A1 closure]
\label{thm:r12-a1-main}
The generalized baker family has an exact target-refined Hamiltonian
realization, a global work cocycle, and an all-order response calculus on a
complete graded flag-current complex.  Its symbolic natural extension is the
correct coupled map and is never replaced by two independent one-sided
shifts.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r12-a1-refinement},
\ref{thm:r12-a1-suspension}, \ref{thm:r12-a1-currents}, and
\ref{thm:r12-a1-response}, with Proposition~\ref{prop:r12-a1-work}.
\end{proof}
