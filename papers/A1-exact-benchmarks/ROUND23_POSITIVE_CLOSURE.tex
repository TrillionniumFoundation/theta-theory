\section{Round-twenty-three reconstruction: measurable current observables, response domains, and a trace-class Hilbert FCLT}
\label{sec:r23-a1}

The Round-Twenty-Two report identified a precise domain error in the previous
response theorem: membership of a vector in a Hilbert completion does not imply
any prescribed cylinder-approximation rate.  We therefore separate three
objects which had previously been conflated: a geometric current fibre, a
measurable current-valued observable on the symbolic probability space, and a
response domain carrying an explicit approximation norm.  No convergence rate
is inferred from bare Hilbert membership.

\subsection{The exact suspension and the fixed current fibre}

Let $\widehat S_a$ be the finite labelled corner complex obtained by retaining
all one-sided germs of the four-branch baker section, and let
$F_a:\widehat S_a\to\widehat S_a$ be the labelled baker diffeomorphism.  On a
branch of width $w_j(a)$ it is
\[
 F_a(q,p)=\left({q-s_{j-1}(a)\over w_j(a)},
 s_{j-1}(a)+w_j(a)p\right).
\]
The labels make the inverse single valued on every face.  With
$\lambda=p\,dq$, direct calculation gives a smooth branch primitive $G_a$ with
$F_a^*\lambda-\lambda=dG_a$.  Additive constants are fixed by one base vertex
on each connected component of the finite labelled incidence graph; the
closed-loop compatibility follows because the composite around a labelled
incidence loop is the identity and hence has zero differential.

Define
\[
 Q_a=(\widehat S_a\times[0,1])/(x,1)\sim(F_ax,0).
\]
If $\chi$ is zero near $0$ and one near $1$, then
$\Lambda_a=\lambda+d(\chi G_a)$ satisfies
$\Lambda_a|_{t=1}=F_a^*(\Lambda_a|_{t=0})$.  Consequently
\[
 \Omega_a=d(\Lambda_a-E\,dt)
\]
descends to $Q_a\times\mathbb R_E$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact labelled suspension]
\label{prop:r23-a1-suspension}
For every $a$ in the compact parameter interval, $Q_a$ is a Hausdorff,
second-countable manifold with corners and $\Omega_a$ is exact and
nondegenerate.  The Hamiltonian $H=E$, with convention
$\iota_{X_H}\Omega_a=dH$, has complete flow, return time one, and return map
$F_a$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equivalence relation is the diagonal in the cylinder interior and the
graphs of $F_a$ and $F_a^{-1}$ in two compact collars.  It is therefore closed
and proper.  Quotient collar charts give the asserted smooth corner
structure.  The boundary identity for $\Lambda_a$ proves descent.  In each
quotient chart, $\Omega_a=dq\wedge dp+dt\wedge dE$, so it is nondegenerate and
$X_E=\partial_t$.  Translation in $t$ is complete and applies $F_a$ exactly at
integer crossings.
\end{proof}

We next define the geometric fibre.  Let $\mathscr F_n$ be the finite family
of labelled bulk, face, corner, and higher flag strata generated by seam
preimages of depth at most $n$.  Choose $m$ above all trace thresholds and
$0<\beta<1$ so that
$\sum_n\beta^n\#\mathscr F_n<\infty$.  On positive test jets put
\[
 \Phi^m=
 \left\{\phi=(\phi_F):
 \sum_{n\ge0}\beta^{-n}
 \sum_{F\in\mathscr F_n}
 \|\phi_F\|_{H^{m+\operatorname{codim}F+2}(F)}^2<\infty\right\}.
\]
Restriction-compatible subdivision diagonals form a closed subspace
$\mathcal R^m$; set
\[
 \widehat\Phi^m=\Phi^m/\mathcal R^m,
 \qquad \mathbb J^m=(\widehat\Phi^m)'.
\]
All traces are taken on the positive test side and only then transposed.  A
fixed affine transport of each labelled stratum identifies the fibres for
nearby $a$.  The resulting test connection $\nabla_a^\Phi$ is a closed
first-order operator from $\widehat\Phi^{m+2}$ to $\widehat\Phi^m$; its
transpose defines the closed material derivative
$\nabla_a^{\mathbb J}:\mathbb J^m\to\mathbb J^{m+2}$ in the graph sense.
This is a fibre operator, not yet a random observable.

\subsection{The measurable response domain}

Let $\Sigma=\{1,2,3,4\}^{\mathbb Z}$, let $\sigma$ be the shift, and let
$\mu_a=\bigotimes_{k\in\mathbb Z}(w_1(a),\ldots,w_4(a))$.  Write
$\mathcal F_{-N}^{N}$ for the coordinates from $-N$ to $N$, and let
$\Pi_N^a$ be Bochner conditional expectation onto this sigma-field.

\begin{definition}[Admissible current observable]
\label{def:r23-a1-domain}
Fix $r\in\mathbb N$ and $\eta>0$.  The response domain
$\mathfrak C_{\eta}^{r}(\mathbb J^m)$ consists of strongly measurable maps
$\mathbf J_a:\Sigma\to\mathbb J^m$ for which the material derivatives
$D_a^j\mathbf J_a$, $0\le j\le r$, exist in
$L^2(\mu_a;\mathbb J^m)$ and
\[
 \|\mathbf J\|_{\mathfrak C_{\eta}^{r}}
 :=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\left(
 \|D_a^j\mathbf J_a\|_{L^2(\mu_a;\mathbb J^m)}
 +\sup_{N\ge0}e^{\eta N}
 \|D_a^j\mathbf J_a-\Pi_N^aD_a^j\mathbf J_a\|_{L^2(\mu_a;\mathbb J^m)}
 \right)<\infty,
\]
uniformly in $a$.  Here $D_a$ is the fixed-fibre material derivative plus the
ordinary derivative of the measurable coefficient map.
\end{definition}

For such an observable its mean is the Bochner integral
\[
 \mathcal M_a(\mathbf J)=\int_\Sigma\mathbf J_a(\omega)\,d\mu_a(\omega)
 \in\mathbb J^m.
\]
Thus the previously ambiguous notation $\int J_a\,d\mu_a$ is replaced by a
map whose source, target, measurability, and integrability are explicit.  A
fixed deterministic current is included as a constant observable and has zero
cylinder tail; an arbitrary nonconstant Hilbert-valued observable is included
only when its displayed norm is finite.

Let
$S_a(i)=\partial_a\log w_i(a)$ and, for a cylinder, let
$\mathscr S_{a,N}=\sum_{|k|\le N}S_a(\omega_k)$.  Higher derivatives are
written with the usual complete Bell polynomials in the derivatives of
$\log w_i$.

\begin{theorem}[Closed response formula on the explicit domain]
\label{thm:r23-a1-response}
If $\mathbf J\in\mathfrak C_{\eta}^{r}(\mathbb J^m)$ and the weights are
$C^r$ and bounded away from zero, then
$a\mapsto\mathcal M_a(\mathbf J)$ is $C^r$ in $\mathbb J^m$.  For $r=1$,
\[
 {d\over da}\mathcal M_a(\mathbf J)
 =\mathcal M_a(D_a\mathbf J)
 +\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
   \mathcal M_a\bigl((\mathbf J-\mathcal M_a\mathbf J)
                    S_a\circ\sigma^k\bigr),
\]
and the series converges absolutely in $\mathbb J^m$.  For general $r$ the
finite-cylinder Bell expansion converges term by term, and every order-$r$
remainder is bounded by
\[
 C_r\|\mathbf J\|_{\mathfrak C_{\eta}^{r}}
 \sum_{n\ge0}(1+n)^r e^{-\eta n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $\Pi_N^a\mathbf J_a$ the assertion is finite-dimensional
differentiation of a finite product measure.  Centering the score permits the
$k$th term to be replaced by
$(\mathbf J-\Pi_{|k|-1}^a\mathbf J)S_a\circ\sigma^k$ when $|k|>0$.
Cauchy--Schwarz and Definition~\ref{def:r23-a1-domain} give the summable bound
$Ce^{-\eta|k|}$.  The same argument after each differentiation bounds a Bell
word by a polynomial in its outermost coordinate times the same exponential
tail.  Dominated convergence in the Bochner space passes from the exact
finite-cylinder identity to the displayed formula.  The material derivative
is closed, so the limit remains in its graph.  No projection rate has been
inferred from Hilbert membership; it is precisely the second term in the
response norm.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A current-valued martingale approximation}

Put $Y=\mathbf J-\mathcal M_a\mathbf J$ and
$Y_n=Y\circ\sigma^n$.  Let
$\mathcal F_n=\sigma(\omega_k:k\le n)$ and
$P_nX=\mathbb E(X\mid\mathcal F_n)-\mathbb E(X\mid\mathcal F_{n-1})$.
The two-sided cylinder estimate implies
\[
 \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\|P_0Y_j\|_{L^2(\mu_a;\mathbb J^m)}<\infty.
\]
Indeed, replacing $Y_j$ by its radius-$|j|/2$ cylinder projection makes it
independent of the innovation at time zero on the appropriate side, while the
error is $O(e^{-\eta|j|/2})$.  Therefore
\[
 D_0=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}P_0Y_j
\]
converges in $L^2(\mu_a;\mathbb J^m)$ and
$D_n=D_0\circ\sigma^n$ is a stationary Hilbert-valued martingale difference.
The standard telescoping construction gives
\[
 \left\|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(Y_k-D_k)\right\|_{L^2(\mu_a;\mathbb J^m)}=o(\sqrt n).
\]
This is the Hilbert version of the Gordin decomposition
\cite{Gordin1969,HallHeyde1980}; the preceding projection estimate verifies
its hypotheses for the present current observable rather than merely naming
the theorem.

Define $Q_a:\mathbb J^m\to\mathbb J^m$ by
\[
 Q_ah=\mathbb E_a\bigl[\langle D_0,h\rangle_{\mathbb J^m}D_0\bigr].
\]

\begin{theorem}[Trace-class current FCLT]
\label{thm:r23-a1-fclt}
For every $\mathbf J\in\mathfrak C_\eta^0(\mathbb J^m)$, the polygonal
process
\[
 W_n(t)=n^{-1/2}\left(
 \sum_{k<nt}Y_k+(nt-\lfloor nt\rfloor)Y_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\right)
\]
converges in $C([0,1];\mathbb J^m)$ to the $\mathbb J^m$-valued Brownian
motion with covariance $Q_a$.  The operator $Q_a$ is positive and trace
class, with
\[
 \operatorname{tr}Q_a=\mathbb E_a\|D_0\|_{\mathbb J^m}^2<\infty.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For an orthonormal basis $(e_j)$, Tonelli and Parseval give
\[
 \sum_j\langle Q_ae_j,e_j\rangle
 =\mathbb E_a\sum_j|\langle D_0,e_j\rangle|^2
 =\mathbb E_a\|D_0\|^2.
\]
Hence $Q_a$ is trace class; no coordinatewise variance assertion is used in
place of this trace identity.  Conditional quadratic variations of the
finite-dimensional projections converge by the ergodic theorem, and their
Lindeberg terms vanish by $L^2$ truncation.  The Hilbert martingale FCLT then
applies.  To pass from finite-dimensional projections to the full space, use
Doob's inequality and
$\mathbb E\|(I-P_M)D_0\|^2\to0$; the same estimate controls the polygonal
interpolation.  Finally, the $o(\sqrt n)$ martingale-approximation remainder is
uniform on compact time intervals by the maximal form of the telescoping
bound.  This proves the asserted functional convergence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Regression against the Round-Twenty-Two objections}

\begin{proposition}[Domain and covariance regression]
\label{prop:r23-a1-regression}
The following statements hold simultaneously.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item An arbitrary element of $\mathbb J^m$ is never assigned an exponential
cylinder rate.  Such a rate occurs only in
$\mathfrak C_\eta^r(\mathbb J^m)$ and is part of its norm.
\item Every expectation is a Bochner integral of a strongly measurable
$\mathbb J^m$-valued observable.
\item The FCLT increment, filtration, centering, projective series, martingale
difference, and covariance operator are the objects defined above.
\item Trace class follows from the exact identity
$\operatorname{tr}Q_a=\mathbb E\|D_0\|^2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Items (i)--(iii) are definitions followed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:r23-a1-response} and the displayed projective estimate.
Item (iv) is Parseval's identity in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r23-a1-fclt}.
\end{proof}

The suspension benchmark is standard; the contribution of this paper is the
typed incidence fibre together with the explicit measurable response domain.
The use of closed graph arguments follows the fixed-domain discipline of
operator perturbation theory \cite{Kato1995}, while the probabilistic step is
reduced to a verified Hilbert martingale approximation rather than an
untyped appeal to symbolic mixing.
