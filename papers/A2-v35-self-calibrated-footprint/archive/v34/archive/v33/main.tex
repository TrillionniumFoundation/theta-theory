\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct periodic dispersing tables from localized reciprocal
collision commands, each returning one bit, with solid starts and free
misses both charged as zero outcomes. On bounded $C^{6,\beta}$ classes
with known geometric and nonperiod-patch margins, a fixed-depth local
inverse and adaptive boundary searches attain $C^2$ accuracy $\nu$ with
$C\nu^{-1/(4+\beta)}\log(C/\nu)\log(C/(\nu\delta))$ attempted bits.
The nominal controls have finite dyadic descriptions. A centered physical
packing gives the matching power for both deterministic caps and
worst-case expected stopping times, with logarithmic factors unresolved.
We also prove a converse for calibration: under arbitrary bounded start
errors, uniform accuracy requires position tolerance of order
$\nu^{(6+\beta)/(4+\beta)}$ or finer, since coarser tolerances admit
distinct smooth periodic tables with identical complete adaptive
collision experiments. This matches the sufficient
calibration power of the reciprocal construction; additional bits
cannot compensate for coarser worst-case calibration. Primitive orbit
counts and rational period relations are recovered under the stated
known patch margin, while real generators remain estimates. Digital
controls, numerical work and physical preparation costs are distinguished.
The conclusions concern active calibrated collision laws, not passive
counts or spectral invariants; all preceding results are retained.
\end{abstract}
