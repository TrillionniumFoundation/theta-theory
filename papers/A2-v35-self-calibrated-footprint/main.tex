\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct periodic dispersing tables from active reciprocal collision
commands returning one bit per attempted preparation. On bounded
$C^{6,\beta}$ classes, write $s=6+\beta$. With shrinking localization,
adaptive boundary search has attempted-bit power $\nu^{-1/(s-2)}$
for $C^2$ accuracy $\nu$, with matching deterministic and expected-length
lower powers up to logarithms. Finite dyadic descriptions do not include
physical calibration production. For a fixed convex launch footprint
and an unknown stationary density bounded below by a calibrated power
of boundary distance, we give a different reconstruction: one-sided
pooled collision tests on short lines improve the sufficient attempted-bit
power to $\nu^{-((\gamma+2)s+1)/(s-2)}$. Random directions remain
unobserved. A separate experiment on one calibrated reference obstacle
also recovers the footprint, including its laboratory placement, without
learning either nuisance density and with the same total sample power.
Uniform geometric and footprint bounds, precise nominal commands and
fixed protective apertures remain assumptions. Uniform finite primitive
period decisions use a known positive nonperiod-patch margin. All attempts
in both scenes are charged. The stationary upper bound is not asserted
minimax; the retained position-resolution converse concerns two different
adversarial implementations, not one common noise law. The conclusions
are active inverse results, not rigidity from passive counts or spectra.
\end{abstract}
