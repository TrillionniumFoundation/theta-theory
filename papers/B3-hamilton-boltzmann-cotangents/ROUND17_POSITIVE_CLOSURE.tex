\section{Round-twenty-one positive closure: cutoff graph coercivity, nuclear process limits, and nonconvex second epi-derivatives}
\label{sec:r21-b3}

This section replaces the former Round-Seventeen source.  The collision kernel
is the angular-cutoff hard-sphere kernel of B2.  Its Dirichlet form controls a
weighted microscopic $L^2$ norm, not an $H^1_v$ norm.  Hypocoercivity is
formulated in the correct transport--collision graph space.  The second-order
variational theorem is proved on the smooth manifold of positive balanced
pairs and does not assume that the nonlinear action is globally convex in
$f$.

\subsection{The linearized balance operator}

Fix a smooth positive finite-action path $(f,qA_f)$ supplied by the B2
recovery theorem.  On every compact velocity set,
\[
 cM(v)\le f(t,x,v)\le CM(v),
 \qquad c\le q\le C,
\]
for a Maxwellian $M$, and the coefficients have the finite derivatives used
below.  Let
\[
 \mathcal N=\operatorname{span}\{1,v_1,v_2,v_3,|v|^2\}
\]
and let $\Pi$ be the $L^2(M^{-1})$ projection onto $\mathcal N$.  For a
potential $p$ define the collision difference
\[
 \Delta p=p(v')+p(v_*')-p(v)-p(v_*).
\]
The cutoff Dirichlet seminorm is
\[
 \|p\|_{\mathcal D_{f,q}}^2=
 {1\over4}\int|\Delta p|^2q\,dA_f.
\]

Let $\mathcal B$ be the derivative of the weak balance map at $(f,qA_f)$.
For a density tangent $h$ and relative collision tangent $k$,
\[
 \mathcal B(h,k)=
 \partial_th+v\cdot\nabla_xh-\mathcal Q'_{f,q}h-\mathcal Q_{f,q}(k),
\]
with the exact initial, terminal, mass, momentum, and energy gauge conditions.
Its formal adjoint is
\[
 \mathcal B^*p=
 \left(-\partial_tp-v\cdot\nabla_xp-\mathcal L_{f,q}p,
       \Delta p\right).
\]

Define the adjoint graph norm
\[
 \|p\|_{\mathcal X}^2=
 \|(I-\Pi)p\|_{L^2_{t,x,v}(\nu M^{-1})}^2
 +\|\nabla_x\Pi p\|_{L^2_tH^{-1}_x}^2
 +\|\partial_t\Pi p\|_{L^2_tH^{-1}_x}^2
 +\|p(0)\|_{H^{-1}_x}^2+\|p(T)\|_{H^{-1}_x}^2,
\]
where $\nu(v)\asymp1+|v|$ is the cutoff collision frequency.

\begin{lemma}[Cutoff microscopic spectral gap]
\label{lem:r21-b3-gap}
There is $c_0>0$ such that
\[
 \|(I-\Pi)p\|_{L^2(\nu M^{-1})}^2
 \le c_0^{-1}\|p\|_{\mathcal D_{f,q}}^2
\]
uniformly on the regular source chart.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the Maxwellian hard-sphere operator, compactness of the gain part relative
to multiplication by $\nu$ and the collision-invariant characterization give
a positive spectral gap on $\mathcal N^\perp$.  The ratios $f/M$ and $q$ are
bounded above and below, so the biased Dirichlet form is uniformly equivalent
to the Maxwellian form.  No velocity derivative is inferred from this
estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nonautonomous graph observability]
\label{thm:r21-b3-observability}
For every $p$ in the transported adjoint graph domain and orthogonal to the
exact gauge kernel,
\[
 \|p\|_{\mathcal X}^2
 \le C\left(
 \|\mathcal B^*p\|_{\mathcal Y^*}^2
 +\int|\Delta p|^2q\,dA_f\right),
\]
where $\mathcal Y^*$ is the dual transport graph space.  Consequently
$\mathcal B$ has closed range, its kernel and cokernel are finite dimensional,
and it has a bounded right inverse on the orthogonal complement of its
compatibility conditions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $p=p_0+p_1$ with $p_0=\Pi p$ and $p_1=(I-\Pi)p$.  Lemma
\ref{lem:r21-b3-gap} controls $p_1$ by the collision component of
$\mathcal B^*p$.  Take the five velocity moments of the transport equation.
They form a first-order symmetric hyperbolic system for the coefficients of
$p_0$, forced by $p_1$ and by $\mathcal B^*p$.  On each nonzero spatial Fourier
mode, the standard Kawashima compensator
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle i\widehat v\cdot k\,p_0,
                         K(k)p_1\rangle
\]
controls $|k|^2(1+|k|^2)^{-1}|p_0|^2$.  On the zero mode, the endpoint and
exact gauge conditions control the collision invariants.  Integrating in time
gives the displayed graph estimate for frozen coefficients.

The coefficients $f,q$ vary in time and space but stay in a compact regular
chart.  Partition time into intervals on which their graph-norm variation is
smaller than the frozen coercivity constant.  Treat the variation as a bounded
perturbation, absorb it on each interval, and concatenate the estimates by
Gronwall.  The graph domains are transported by the same positive multiplier
and remain fixed.  The Hilbert closed-range theorem applied to this adjoint
estimate gives closed range and the bounded right inverse.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A concrete nuclear test triple}

For $m\ge1$, let $H_m$ be the completion of smooth density/contact tests in
\[
 \|\phi\|_{H_m}^2=
 \sum_{|\alpha|+j\le m}
 \int e^{m|v|^2}(1+|v|)^{2m}
 |\partial_{t,x}^\alpha\partial_v^j\phi|^2
 +\sum_{|\alpha|\le m}\|\partial^\alpha\phi_c\|_{L^2(A_f)}^2,
\]
with collision symmetry and endpoint compatibility imposed as closed linear
conditions.  Put
\[
 \mathscr S=\bigcap_{m\ge1}H_m,
 \qquad \mathscr S'=\bigcup_{m\ge1}H_{-m}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Nuclearity]
\label{lem:r21-b3-nuclear}
For a sufficiently fast increasing subsequence $m_j$, the embedding
$H_{m_{j+1}}\hookrightarrow H_{m_j}$ is Hilbert--Schmidt.  Hence
$\mathscr S\subset H_0\subset\mathscr S'$ is a nuclear Gelfand triple, and the
same is true for the direct sum of density and contact tests.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand in Fourier modes in $(t,x)$, Hermite functions in $v$, and a fixed
orthonormal angular basis for contacts.  Raising $m$ increases polynomial
mode weights and the Gaussian velocity weight.  Choose
$m_{j+1}-m_j$ larger than the total compact base dimension plus four.  The
squares of the singular values are then summable over every mode index.
Closed symmetry and endpoint subspaces inherit Hilbert--Schmidt embeddings.
A countably Hilbert space with such a cofinal sequence is nuclear.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Localized cumulants and process tightness}

Let $Z_\varepsilon$ be the centered, $\mu_\varepsilon^{-1/2}$-scaled pair of
empirical density and actual-contact current.  For a test $\Theta$ supported
in a time interval $I$, write $Z_\varepsilon(\Theta)$ for the pairing.

\begin{theorem}[Localized connected cumulants]
\label{thm:r21-b3-cumulants}
For every $r\ge2$ and tests $\Theta_j$ supported in one interval $I$,
\[
 |\kappa_r(Z_\varepsilon(\Theta_1),\ldots,
           Z_\varepsilon(\Theta_r))|
 \le C_r\mu_\varepsilon^{1-r/2}
 (|I|+\mu_\varepsilon^{-1})
 \prod_j\|\Theta_j\|_{H_m}.
\]
The estimate holds under the B1 exact-number and microcanonical conditioning,
with the second cumulant replaced by its finite-rank Schur complement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the correctly normalized B2 connected logarithmic series $r$
times.  A connected graph carrying all $r$ marks must meet the earliest
marked time interval; choosing its first marked vertex gives the factor
$|I|$.  All graphs lying inside one microscopic collision cell contribute the
additional $\mu_\varepsilon^{-1}$.  The B2 Catalan majorant includes the
$1/k!$ symmetry factor, so the remaining genealogy sum is convergent.  Test
norms control the marked kernels by duality.

B1 conditioning is coefficient extraction at a source-dependent saddle.
Differentiating the saddle equation adds only a fixed-dimensional matrix of
particle-number and conserved-constraint derivatives.  Hence locality is
unchanged and the second cumulant is the Schur complement; higher cumulants
retain the displayed scaling.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Aldous and compact containment]
\label{lem:r21-b3-tight}
For $m$ sufficiently large, the laws of the density coordinate are tight in
$C([0,T],H_{-m})$ and the contact coordinate is tight in
$D([0,T],H_{-m})$.  For every stopping time $\tau_\varepsilon$ and
$0\le h\le\delta$,
\[
 \mathbb E\|Z_\varepsilon(\tau_\varepsilon+h)-
 Z_\varepsilon(\tau_\varepsilon)\|_{H_{-m}}^2
 \le C(\delta+\mu_\varepsilon^{-1}).
\]
The maximum jump tends to zero in probability.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose an orthonormal basis of $H_m$ diagonalizing the Hilbert--Schmidt
embedding into $H_{m-2}$.  Sum the second- and fourth-cumulant estimates of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-b3-cumulants} over the basis; the Hilbert--Schmidt tail
gives compact containment in $H_{-m}$.  The same connected-interval estimate
holds conditionally after a stopping time because genealogies are ordered by
their first vertex after the stopping sigma-field; taking conditional
expectations removes all earlier unmarked components.  This yields the
Aldous estimate.

A single empirical density or contact atom has $H_{-m}$ norm
$O(\mu_\varepsilon^{-1/2})$.  Exponential particle/contact moment bounds make
the maximum number of simultaneous regular binary contacts logarithmic with
high probability, while multiple contacts are negligible by B2.  Hence the
maximum jump vanishes.  The density coordinate satisfies the weak balance
equation, so its jump part cancels with the integrated contact flux in the
weaker $H_{-m}$ topology, giving continuity of its limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nuclear-dual process CLT]
\label{thm:r21-b3-clt}
The processes $Z_\varepsilon$ converge in
$C([0,T],\mathscr S')\times D([0,T],\mathscr S_c')$ to a centered Gaussian
process $Z$.  Its covariance is the second derivative of the B2 pressure,
with the B1 Schur correction in the microcanonical ensemble.  It is the unique
Gaussian solution of the linearized weak balance equation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every finite family of tests, Theorem
\ref{thm:r21-b3-cumulants} makes all cumulants of order at least three vanish,
while the second cumulants converge by differentiability of the B2 pressure.
Thus finite-dimensional distributions are Gaussian.  Lemma
\ref{lem:r21-b3-tight} and Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-b3-nuclear}, through Mitoma's
criterion, give process tightness.  Every limit satisfies the linearized
balance identity because the microscopic Green identity is exact and the
singular-boundary mass vanishes.  If two Gaussian solutions have the same
noise covariance, their difference solves the homogeneous adjoint problem;
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-b3-observability} forces it to vanish.  This proves
uniqueness and identifies the full limit.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Second epi-derivative on the positive balance manifold}

Use exponential local coordinates around the positive pair:
\[
 f_\eta=f\exp\{\eta h+\eta^2r_\eta-c_\eta\},
 \qquad
 q_\eta=q\exp\{\eta k+\eta^2s_\eta\},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_\eta=q_\eta A_{f_\eta}.
\]
The scalar $c_\eta$ and the collision-invariant part of $r_\eta$ impose the
exact conserved moments.  Positivity and absolute continuity are automatic.

\begin{lemma}[Balanced implicit chart]
\label{lem:r21-b3-chart}
If $(h,k)$ satisfies the linearized balance and gauge conditions, there are
bounded $r_\eta,s_\eta$ converging in the graph space such that
$(f_\eta,\Gamma_\eta)$ satisfies the full nonlinear balance equation and all
endpoint and conservation constraints exactly.  Conversely every
finite-action sequence converging at order $\eta$ has a tangent satisfying the
linearized conditions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In exponential coordinates, the balance map is $C^2$ from the positive graph
chart to $\mathcal Y^*$.  Its derivative is $\mathcal B$.  Theorem
\ref{thm:r21-b3-observability} supplies a bounded right inverse on the
compatibility subspace.  Apply the implicit-function theorem after splitting
kernel and range.  The finite-dimensional collision-invariant coordinates
solve the moment constraints.  The converse follows by dividing the exact
balance identity by $\eta$ and using graph compactness.
\end{proof}

Let $I$ be the B2 action and let $z=(h,k)$ be a tangent class modulo the gauge.
Its quadratic form is
\[
 \mathcal Q(z)=I_0''(h_0,h_0)+
 {1\over2}\int q\,|k+\mathcal A_fh|^2\,dA_f,
\]
where $\mathcal A_fh$ is the explicit logarithmic derivative of
$A_f=ff_*B/2$.

\begin{theorem}[Nonconvex second epi-derivative and Mosco limit]
\label{thm:r21-b3-mosco}
On the Hilbert tangent quotient,
\[
 {I(f_\eta,\Gamma_\eta)-I(f,\Gamma)
  -\eta\,DI(h,k)\over\eta^2}
 \xrightarrow[M]{ }\mathcal Q(h,k).
\]
The form is closed and positive, and its inverse on the quotient is the
covariance operator of Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-b3-clt}.  No global convexity of
$f\mapsto I(f,\Gamma)$ is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Taylor-expand the smooth perspective integrand in the positive exponential
chart.  Since
$A_{f_\eta}/A_f=\exp\{\eta\mathcal A_fh+O(\eta^2)\}$, the second variation is
the displayed square plus the initial contribution.  For the liminf, bounded
rescaled action gives weak compactness of the relative logarithmic tangents;
Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-b3-chart} identifies their linearized balance constraint,
and weak lower semicontinuity of the square gives $\mathcal Q$.

For the recovery, take a smooth tangent, use Lemma
\ref{lem:r21-b3-chart} to choose the exact second-order correction, and apply
the Taylor expansion with uniform integrability.  Density of smooth graph
vectors and diagonal approximation give every tangent in the closed form
domain.  The form is coercive modulo the gauge by Theorem
\ref{thm:r21-b3-observability}, hence closed.  Differentiating the B2 pressure
and using Legendre duality on this local chart shows that its Hessian is the
inverse form; the Gaussian covariance constructed independently in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-b3-clt} has the same bilinear pairings on a dense test
class, proving equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-twenty-one B3 closure]
\label{thm:r21-b3-main}
The cutoff hard-sphere theory has a correctly typed graph observability and
closed-range theorem, an explicit nuclear test triple, localized cumulant and
stopping-time tightness estimates, a nuclear-dual process CLT, and a positive
exactly balanced nonconvex second epi-derivative/Mosco theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r21-b3-observability},
\ref{thm:r21-b3-cumulants}, \ref{thm:r21-b3-clt}, and
\ref{thm:r21-b3-mosco}.
\end{proof}
