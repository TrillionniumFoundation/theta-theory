\subsection{Localization and increasing experiments}
The distinction between the preceding fixed-experiment results and a
size-uniform theorem is substantive. A positive first-block event on
all edges can have exponentially small mass, and a tensor recovery map
can have a graph-dependent norm. We address these two issues separately.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v28-localized} uses a finite family of local acquired
witnesses at one compulsory boundary. Its event requires enough regular
edges on every candidate cut, not simultaneous regularity of all edges.
Its proof retains the selected-edge subprobability densities and counts
only the prediction centres the decoder can actually distinguish.
It applies in particular to the original tensor experiment, with the
recovery norms displayed rather than suppressed.

For a theorem uniform over growing graphs we specify an enlarged query
menu in Section~\ref{sec:v28-readout}. Half its queries are the original
tensor probes, with their original target. The other half read a
uniformly selected edge, while still executing and counting all remaining
raw trials. Both types belong to the same exact future test space.
This supplies local recovery at a controlled norm without discarding
the original prediction task. For linear-size graphs whose balanced
cuts retain a positive density of independently acquired regular edges,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v28-size-uniform} proves
\[
                  B_G^\diamond(\varepsilon,a)
                            \asymp |V|\,b(\varepsilon,a)
\]
at sufficiently small resolutions, with constants independent of graph
size and calibration, including exact additive collisions. Its additive
form records the finite-size losses explicitly. The lower bound allows
arbitrary history-dependent acquisition; the upper bound uses a
fixed order and one joint label. An active edge is quantized only when
first acquired, so the construction accumulates no error with the
number of intervening vertices. Proposition~\ref{prop:v28-graph-existence}
constructs nonempty families satisfying the hypotheses, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:v28-joint-limit} allows graph growth and analytic
calibration collision to occur simultaneously.

For clarity, the decoder conventions used in the graph results are
summarized below. In every row the predictor receives a finite label;
no decoder receives discarded continuous observations. Statements in
the first four rows retain the original tensor menu.

Only the acquisition law or order is reused in the whole-curve and menu
results. Predictive encoders may be redesigned at every resolution.
The size-uniform theorem, by contrast, allows the acquisition rule to
depend on the requested resolution; it does not rely on a fixed-rule
quantifier. These are separate assertions, with separate proofs.
