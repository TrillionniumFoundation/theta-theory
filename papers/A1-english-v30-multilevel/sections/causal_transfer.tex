\section{From attainable geometry to causal memory}
\label{sec:causal-transfer}
The argument separates a geometric question at one time from the
compatibility of the answers at successive times. The following theorem
makes that separation precise. Its hypotheses concern a set, a measure,
and a family of maps; they do not presuppose a quantization law. In
particular, a small singular value or a differential of maximal rank is
not a substitute for the measure hypothesis.

Fix a finite horizon $N$ and a parameter set $\mathcal K$. For each
known $\lambda\in\mathcal K$, let $S_{n,\lambda}\subset\R^{q_n}$ be
the nonempty compact set of states reachable after $n$ reports, and let
$v_n(h)$ denote the exact state. The initial set is a singleton. At
checkpoint $n$, the vector of conditional query probabilities, weighted
by the square roots of their fixed selection probabilities, is
\[
 p_{n,\lambda}(v)=b_{n,\lambda}+G_{n,\lambda}v.
\]
The query is drawn after the label is formed. Thus squared prediction
regret is the squared Euclidean error in this vector. Assume that $G$
and a left inverse of $G$ have norms at most a common constant. The
hypothesis concerns physical query coordinates, not a whitening by
vanishing singular values.

For $1\le n<N$, fix $1\le p_n\le q_n$ and an ordered scale list
$s_{n,1}(\lambda)\ge\cdots\ge s_{n,q_n}(\lambda)\ge0$. The
following requirements have constants independent of $n$ and $\lambda$.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(G) Global geometry.}
There are invertible linear maps $T_{n,\lambda}$, with both $T$ and
$T^{-1}$ uniformly bounded, and translations $c_{n,\lambda}$ such that
\[
 \widetilde S_{n,\lambda}=T_{n,\lambda}S_{n,\lambda}-c_{n,\lambda}
 \subset\prod_{i=1}^{q_n}[-B s_{n,i},B s_{n,i}].
\]
These sets have uniformly bounded semialgebraic format and dimension
at most $p_n$. Only the format of each set is bounded; its coefficients
need not depend semialgebraically or continuously on $\lambda$.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(A) Acquired measure.}
There is one law of command exploration, independent of all coding
randomness. At every checkpoint and for each $\ell\le p_n$ with
$s_{n,\ell}>0$, the first $\ell$ coordinates of
$T_{n,\lambda}v_n-c_{n,\lambda}$ have a law dominating $\beta$
times uniform probability on a translated rectangle with half-widths
$\rho s_{n,1},\ldots,\rho s_{n,\ell}$. Here $\beta,\rho>0$ are
uniform. A subprobability carried by one report word suffices, provided
its probability is included. All these laws are marginals of the same
exploration experiment.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(C) Causal compatibility.}
For $0\le n<N-1$ and every admitted current command $u$ and report $y$, the exact update
is a map, jointly Borel in its state and input arguments,
\[
 F_{n,u,y}^{\lambda}:S_{n,\lambda}\longrightarrow S_{n+1,\lambda}
\]
with Lipschitz constant at most $L_n$, uniformly in $u,y,\lambda$.
It reads the current state, command and report only. The transformed
coordinates in (G) are not required to have a well-conditioned update.

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer-profile}
 Q_n(M,\lambda)=\max_{1\le\ell\le p_n}
       \left(\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{n,i}(\lambda)}{M}
       \right)^{2/\ell}.
\end{equation}
Zero products contribute zero. The clock and the known parameter are
read-only; the persistent resource is the cardinality of the retained
label, as in Definition~\ref{def:finite-state}.

\begin{theorem}[Attainable-geometry transfer]
\label{thm:causal-transfer}
Under \textup{(G)}, \textup{(A)} and \textup{(C)}, the optimal
checkpoint regret with $M$ labels is comparable to $Q_n(M,\lambda)$,
both under the exploration law and in the worst-history criterion.
The constants are uniform in $\lambda$ and every integer $M\ge1$.

More generally, prescribe integers $M_1,\ldots,M_{N-1}\ge1$.
There is one deterministic causal filter retaining at most $M_n$ labels
at time $n$, for which the exact-state error satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer-recurrence}
 e_n\le C\sum_{j=1}^n
    \left(\prod_{i=j}^{n-1}L_i\right)
                     Q_j(M_j,\lambda)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The empty product is one. Consequently, if $\mathfrak R_{\boldsymbol M}$
is the optimal maximum-checkpoint regret with these stage budgets, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer-causal-law}
 c\max_{1\le n<N}Q_n(M_n,\lambda)
 \le\mathfrak R_{\boldsymbol M}^{\rm av}
 \le\mathfrak R_{\boldsymbol M}^{\rm max}
 \le C_N\max_{1\le n<N}Q_n(M_n,\lambda).
\end{equation}
Here the average criterion is the maximum of the expected checkpoint
losses, and the worst-history criterion is the maximum of their suprema;
one selected checkpoint query is executed in a run.
The lower bound permits private or public coding randomness independent
of the exploration law. The constants in this assertion belong to the
fixed horizon and the stated uniform hypotheses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:tame-rectangle} to $\widetilde S_{n,\lambda}$.
It supplies at most $M$ centers in that set with radius at most
$C Q_n(M,\lambda)^{1/2}$, for the actual integer $M$, including
small budgets and zero-width axes. Pulling the centers back by $T^{-1}$
gives reachable states with the same bound up to a uniform factor.
The bounded norm of $G$ gives the checkpoint upper bound. If all scales
vanish, (G) makes the reachable set a singleton and one center is exact.

For the converse, average a randomized decoder conditional on its label
and any public seed. Squared loss cannot increase. There remain at most
$M$ prediction centers. Apply a bounded left inverse of $G$, then $T$,
to these centers, subtracting the fixed translations. Prediction error
bounds below, up to a uniform factor, the distance in any first
$\ell$ transformed coordinates to the corresponding $M$ centers.
For a rectangle in (A), the fraction covered by $M$ balls of radius $r$
is at most
\[
 C_\ell M r^\ell\left(\rho^\ell
                              \prod_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{n,i}\right)^{-1}.
\]
Take $r=a_\ell(\prod_{i\le\ell}s_{n,i}/M)^{1/\ell}$ with a
sufficiently small positive $a_\ell$. At least half of the dominated
mass lies outside these balls. Its squared error is at least
$\beta r^2/2$, up to the fixed metric factor. Maximizing over the
positive products gives the lower bound. Products that vanish require
no lower estimate. Randomized assignment to centers cannot beat the
nearest-center distance. Conditioning on independent public coding
randomness and integrating proves the same assertion for public seeds.
The history law has not been conditioned on an event of probability one
in place of the original report word.

Choose the preceding reachable codebooks with budgets $M_n$.
Starting from the exact initial state, update the retained representative
by $F_{n,u,y}^{\lambda}$ and quantize in the next codebook, using a
fixed nearest-center tie rule. The candidate state is reachable by (C);
hence it belongs to the quantizer's domain. The resulting maps are
Borel. Only the representative's label is retained. With $e_0=0$,
\[
 e_{n+1}\le L_n e_n+C Q_{n+1}(M_{n+1},\lambda)^{1/2}.
\]
Induction gives \eqref{eq:transfer-recurrence}. The bounded physical
query map and the finite sum give the last inequality in
\eqref{eq:transfer-causal-law}. At a fixed checkpoint, every causal
label is also an encoder of the full prefix with at most $M_n$ labels.
The checkpoint lower bounds therefore hold for every causal filter.
They refer to marginals of one exploration law, so their maximum is a
valid lower bound before taking the infimum over filters. This proves
the first inequality. The average criterion is bounded by the
worst-history criterion, giving the middle inequality.
\end{proof}

The set condition (G) controls every history, whereas (A) only needs
one sufficiently rich positive-probability family of histories. These
are different obligations. Condition (C) prevents a small codebook from
being counted as an algorithm that recovers discarded inputs. The
conclusion is a bounded-factor equivalence of checkpoint and causal
resolution, not an equality of optimal constants or a bound uniform in
an unbounded horizon.

\subsection{Positive updates in remaining-test coordinates}
The update hypothesis can be checked before any interpolation or
quantization is undertaken. It is independent of an ordering of scales.

\begin{lemma}[Remaining-test update]
\label{lem:positive-remaining-update}
Let $X$ be compact, and let
$\psi_n=(1,\psi_{n,1},\ldots,\psi_{n,q_n})$ be bounded continuous
tests, not necessarily linearly independent. Suppose that multiplication
by each next report likelihood $L_{n,u,y}\ge\kappa>0$ maps every
component of $\psi_{n+1}$ into $\operatorname{span}\psi_n$, with
uniformly bounded coefficient representations, Borel in the current
input. For posterior moment
states $v=(\nu\psi_{n,i})_{i\ge1}$, the next moment state is a
rational function of $(1,v)$ alone. It is uniformly Lipschitz on
segments joining reachable states, and a reachable representative is
mapped to a reachable next state. The constant depends on $\kappa$,
the coefficient and test bounds, and the dimensions, but not on linear
independence or a nonzero separation between tests.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $L_{n,u,y}=b\psi_n$ and
$L_{n,u,y}\psi_{n+1,i}=a_i\psi_n$ in the given representations.
Bayes' formula gives
\[
 v'_i=\frac{a_i(1,v)^t}{b(1,v)^t}.
\]
A segment joining the moment states of $\nu_0$ and $\nu_1$ is the
moment state of their convex mixture. Its denominator is the integral
of $L_{n,u,y}$ under that mixture and is at least $\kappa$.
Numerators, coefficient vectors and moment coordinates are uniformly
bounded. The quotient rule therefore bounds the derivative on the
segment by a constant of the stated kind. Integration along the
segment gives the Lipschitz estimate. Updating the posterior that
represents a reachable state by the admitted input and report is an
actual extended history, proving reachability. Distinct coefficient
representations give the same integrals on moment states and their
mixture segments; independence of the tests was never used.
\end{proof}

This lemma covers remaining products of arbitrary continuous detector
cells on an arbitrary compact latent space whenever the stated
coefficient bounds hold. The monomial and circular experiments require
additional, and different, proofs of (G) and (A). Neither the lemma nor
Theorem~\ref{thm:causal-transfer} asserts that every positive detector
possesses the same collision scales.
