\begin{abstract}
We study the information acquired by finite positive experiments and the persistent memory needed to predict their future reports. For scalar generalized-polynomial experiments we identify the attainable posterior flags, including confluent limits at exponent collisions. Collision-uniform covering and small-ball estimates give all-budget prediction laws in terms of attained determinant profiles. The upper bounds are realized by causal filters with one retained finite label; they do not require division by an exponent gap.

For independent experiments attached to the edges of a fixed graph, shared memory is governed by a minimax cut profile with separate attained-dimension caps. The converse permits randomized history-dependent acquisition and retains the evidence of the actual reports. A first-block selection bound leads to a visited-set separator certificate, and analytic collision paths yield finite piecewise affine order and memory laws.

We then consider an acquisition rule whose law is fixed across predictive resolutions. We prove that one deterministic order, selected independently of the predictive budget and codebook, dominates its entire prediction curve up to a uniform constant. This remains true when the competing rule receives its complete revealed history and the competing decoder receives the finite order prefix. A finite menu of such adaptive rules is likewise matched, to within bounded additive bits at every resolution, by an equally large deterministic menu. This gives an exact leading characterization of the optimal uniform-resolution excess and a two-configuration realization in a positive three-edge experiment.

Finally, in an actual two-trial experiment we derive the full mixed law of the acquired posterior mean, determine its sharp high-resolution average-distortion coefficient, and evaluate every factor in the separator certificate. The calculation gives a same-model comparison of first-block and complete-word events while distinguishing a quantitative certificate from the sharp optimum.
\end{abstract}
