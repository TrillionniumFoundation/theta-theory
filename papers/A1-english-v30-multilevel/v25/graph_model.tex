\section{Experiments indexed by a graph}\label{sec:v25-graph}
The collision profile also determines the order in which several
experiments should be acquired. We study a batch protocol constrained
by a finite graph. The deterministic product calculation was developed
in the earlier shared-memory manuscript \cite{QiSharedMemory2026}; we
reproduce the version needed here. The comparison with adaptive orders
will require a further measure argument.

\subsection{The observation protocol}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a fixed finite loopless undirected multigraph, with
$v=|V|\ge2$ and labelled edges. For each edge $e$ fix an independent
scalar experiment of Section~\ref{sec:collision-geometry}, a full-support
prior $\mu_e$, and integers $n_e,m_e\ge1$. Write $N_e=n_e+m_e$.
The joint prior is $\bigotimes_e\mu_e$; raw trials are conditionally
independent given the latent vector. The graph, detectors, priors and
horizons are fixed. The known calibration is
$a\in\prod_eK_e$, where each $K_e$ is an admitted compact chamber.

Visit each vertex once. At the first endpoint visited of edge $e$,
execute its first $n_e$ trials; at its second endpoint, execute its
last $m_e$ trials. Incident blocks at a vertex are processed in a fixed
order. A vertex is one atomic observation batch. Its fresh commands
are independent uniform commands in the admitted edge cubes and are
revealed only after that vertex is selected. The scheduler may select
a vertex, but cannot choose command values or inspect them beforehand.
All reports, including failures, are observed, and all raw trials count.
The total raw trial count is $T=\sum_eN_e$, independently of the order.

Memory is charged at vertex boundaries. The current vertex batch is
one input; temporary work while processing it is not retained. This is
a specified batch experiment, not a statement about memory charged
after each constituent raw report. The single-trial classification
proved above remains unchanged. An admissible controller retains at
most $M$ joint labels at every boundary. All history-dependent scheduler
information belongs to this label. The graph, calibration, budget and
clock are read-only. The next-vertex rule uses the previous label and
independent coding randomness, visits an unvisited vertex, and finishes
the graph. A fixed order stored in the program is admissible. The
predictor cannot recover discarded commands or reports.

After a boundary with visited set $S$, independently select one member
of the full tensor product of the scalar future failure-product menus.
An unopened edge has $N_e$ remaining trials, an edge in
\[
 \delta(S)=\{e\in E: \text{exactly one endpoint of }e\text{ belongs to }S\}
\]
has $m_e$, and a completed edge contributes the constant factor $1$.
Execute only this selected query in a fixed canonical order of the
remaining vertices. Its binary target is that every remaining report
is a failure. Execute and count all remaining trials even after a
nonfailure. Acquisition and query together thus use exactly $T$ trials.
There is one queried checkpoint per run, not simultaneous execution of
all counterfactual queries. The finite query descriptor identifies the
visited set and menu, but contains no continuous history information.

Here is a common sample space for the risk criteria. Sample a full
command and report tape $\tau$ for each edge, with $N_e$ uniform
commands and reports conditionally independent given its latent
parameter. The first visit reveals its first block and the second
reveals its last block. This construction gives the actual exploration
law for every nonanticipatory controller; it does not give that
controller access to unrevealed inputs. Let $\xi$ collect independent
coding seeds. Write $h_j(\tau,\xi)$ for its revealed history at boundary
$j$, and $\mathcal L_j(\tau,\xi)$ for squared-prediction excess,
averaged over the independently selected query, with the coding seeds
fixed. Equivalently, $\mathcal L_j$ is the weighted squared error of
the query-probability vector relative to the full-history conditional
predictor. Private decoder seeds may also be included in $\xi$.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-risk-definitions}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal R^{\rm av}_{G,M}(a)
   &=\inf_{\mathcal F}\max_{1\le j<v}
                    \E_{\tau,\xi}\mathcal L_j(\tau,\xi),\\
 \mathcal R^{\rm max}_{G,M}(a)
   &=\inf_{\mathcal F}\max_{1\le j<v}
                    \sup_{\tau}\E_\xi\mathcal L_j(\tau,\xi).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The supremum ranges over admitted tapes. Every finite report word has
positive likelihood. Coding randomness is averaged before the
supremum; the infimum over one controller is outside the boundary
maximum. The tape law is independent of $\xi$, so the average criterion
is at most the maximum criterion. The tapes formalize this convention
without providing a history-dependent random seed as free storage.

\subsection{Cut profiles}
At the active checkpoint of edge $e$ put
\[
 p_e=\min\{n_e(r_e-1),q_{e,m_e}\},\qquad V_{e,0}=1,\qquad
 V_{e,\ell}=\mathcal V_{e,m_e,\ell}(a^{(e)})\quad(1\le\ell\le p_e).
\]
The notation for formal labels and determinant volumes is the scalar
notation with an edge subscript. Coincident labels are retained.
For an edge set $F$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-cut-profile}
 \begin{split}
 A_{F,k}&=\max_{\substack{0\le\ell_e\le p_e\ (e\in F)\\
                              \sum_e\ell_e=k}}
                         \prod_{e\in F}V_{e,\ell_e},\qquad A_{F,0}=1,\\
 \Theta_F(M,a)&=\max_{1\le k\le\sum_{e\in F}p_e}
                                    (A_{F,k}/M)^{2/k}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Set $\Theta_\varnothing=0$; zero products contribute zero. If $\pi$ is
a vertex permutation and $S_j^\pi$ consists of its first $j$ vertices,
put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-width-profile}
 Q_G(M,a)=\min_\pi\max_{1\le j<v}
                            \Theta_{\delta(S_j^\pi)}(M,a).
\end{equation}
Each edge has its own cap $p_e$. A truncation only by the total dimension
of an ambient tensor rectangle would generally give a different answer.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive order and shared memory]\label{thm:v25-adaptive}
For the fixed graph experiment there are $0<c\le C<\infty$ such that,
for every known calibration and every integer $M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-adaptive-law}
 cQ_G(M,a)\le\mathcal R^{\rm av}_{G,M}(a)
 \le\mathcal R^{\rm max}_{G,M}(a)\le CQ_G(M,a).
\end{equation}
The upper bound is attained by a deterministic vertex order and one
causal joint-label filter. The lower bound remains valid when the
scheduler reads its full past and the predictor additionally receives
the finite vertex-order prefix, but retains only $M$ labels for its
command and report information. No regularity of the scheduler's
decision regions is required. The constants may depend on the fixed
graph, priors, detectors and horizons, but not on calibration, budget
or scheduler. Exact exponent collisions are included. If $E$ is empty,
all these risks are zero.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The deterministic product calculation}
For a fixed order, grouping likelihood factors by edge gives a product
posterior and the entire product of active scalar reachable sets. Every
tuple of admitted prefixes can be interleaved. For an adaptive realized
history the posterior still factors: nonanticipatory choices have
probabilities depending on the observed past, not on the latent
parameter once that past is fixed. This posterior identity does not
assert independence of histories after conditioning on a chosen order.

Let $v_e$ be the formal raw remaining-moment vector, including its
constant entry $1$. Unopened vectors are known from the prior and
completed vectors are $(1)$. The full query vector is
\[
 q(v)=\left(\bigotimes_eG_e\right)\bigotimes_ev_e,
\]
where $G_e$ is the fixed full-column-rank coefficient matrix of its
product probes. A left inverse of $\bigotimes_eG_e$, followed by
selection of tensor entries with all but one index constant, recovers
every $v_e$. This bounded linear map is defined on arbitrary prediction
centres. Conversely, telescoping differences of tensor products with
bounded factors proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-tensor-metric}
 c\sum_e\|v_e-w_e\|^2\le\|q(v)-q(w)\|^2
                         \le C\sum_e\|v_e-w_e\|^2.
\end{equation}
Query-selection weights affect only fixed constants. No bound contains
a reciprocal exponent gap.

\begin{proposition}[A prescribed order]\label{prop:v25-fixed-order}
For any prescribed deterministic order $\pi$, both optimal
maximum-boundary risks are comparable to
$\max_j\Theta_{\delta(S_j^\pi)}(M,a)$, uniformly in $M$, $a$ and
$\pi$. The upper bound uses one causal joint $M$-label filter.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $d_{e,1}\ge\cdots\ge d_{e,q}\ge0$ for the Newton scales and
$D_{e,\ell}=\prod_{i\le\ell}d_{e,i}$, with $D_{e,0}=1$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:leja-scales} gives
$D_{e,\ell}\le V_{e,\ell}\le\ell!D_{e,\ell}$, including zeros.
The whole-image argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-checkpoint}
covers each active raw image at radius $r$ by at most
$C_e\sum_{\ell=0}^{p_e}D_{e,\ell}r^{-\ell}$ balls, after a fixed
change of radius. Taking product covers at radius $r/\sqrt{|F|}$
and expanding their counts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-product-cover}
 \mathcal N\left(\prod_{e\in F}\mathcal S_e,r\right)
      \le C_0\sum_{k=0}^{\sum_ep_e} A_{F,k}r^{-k}.
\end{equation}
The finitely many allocations preserve every edge cap. Put
$r_*^2=\Theta_F(M,a)$. For $M\ge2C_0$, choose fixed $L$ with
$C_0\sum_{k\ge1}L^{-k}\le1/2$ and substitute $Lr_*$ to obtain at
most $M$ balls. Discard empty balls and replace their centres by
reachable tuples, at most doubling radii. For $M<2C_0$, one tuple
suffices after increasing the constant: the image is bounded and
$r_*\ge1/M$, since $A_{F,1}=1$ for nonempty $F$. Empty cuts are
singletons. Equation~\eqref{eq:v25-tensor-metric} gives the checkpoint
upper bound.

For the checkpoint converse choose any positive allocation of total
$k>0$. Under this deterministic exploration order the active edge
histories are independent. The actual all-failure subprobability cubes
of Lemma~\ref{lem:newton-attainment}, projected and scaled, give a
product rectangle of side scales $d_{e,i}$, $i\le\ell_e$, carrying a
fixed positive amount of history mass. Evidence has not been removed.
The tensor left inverse and the leading nonzero Newton inverses recover
these scaled coordinates from arbitrary query centres with uniformly
bounded norms. The fraction of the uniform rectangle covered by $M$
radius-$r$ balls is at most $CMr^k/\prod_eD_{e,\ell_e}$. Choose $r$
to be a small multiple of
$(\prod_eD_{e,\ell_e}/M)^{1/k}$; a fixed positive mass remains
uncovered. Maximize over allocations and $k$. Randomized assignments
cannot beat nearest-centre distance; averaging private decoder
randomness and conditioning on independent public seeds gives the same
bound.

Choose reachable joint codebooks at successive boundaries. Within a
vertex batch update the affected components in raw remaining moments,
starting unopened components at their exact priors and discarding
completed components. The positive-denominator update estimate in
Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-streaming}, composed finitely many times,
is uniformly Lipschitz. Updated representatives remain reachable.
Quantize only at the next vertex boundary. The concatenated errors obey
\[
 e_0=0,\qquad e_{j+1}\le L_j e_j+
                 C_j\Theta_{\delta(S_{j+1}^\pi)}(M,a)^{1/2}.
\]
Finite iteration retains all preceding errors and proves the common
filter bound. Only its representative index persists. Every fixed
common filter satisfies the checkpoint lower bounds before its
infimum is taken. There are finitely many boundaries and orders, so
all constants are uniform in $\pi$.
\end{proof}

The proposition does not yet compare with adaptive orders. Conditioning
on the order selected from a history can destroy the product
minorization. The following proof instead uses a common positive-mass
event and upper small-ball bounds before any trace is selected.
