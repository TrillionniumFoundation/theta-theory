\begin{abstract}
We study the finite-horizon positive realization of numerical word
experiments with prescribed seeds and one terminal binary query.
A sum-of-squares Gram recursion gives a polynomial-size exact
feasibility formula and checks all words of a supplied stochastic
machine without enumerating them. Even residual moments converge,
with an explicit finite bound, to the actual globally optimal
worst-word error at any prescribed state profile. Rational row grids
give a second two-sided certificate, preserving the boundary label
counts and distinguishing strict-error approximation from exact
rational realization. A finite planar example has a complete error
frontier. Classical weighted-automata equivalence, nonnegative
factorization complexity and real-algebraic elimination are explicitly
identified as ingredients. The prior universal distortion--dilation
comparison, arithmetic width laws, Liouville fluctuations and uniform
finite-bit compiler are retained with their proofs. The basic resource
is nonuniform clocked atomic-row label width; finite workspace and
random-bit resources are stated separately. No polynomial-time
unrestricted minimization or new arithmetic growth exponent is claimed.
\end{abstract}
