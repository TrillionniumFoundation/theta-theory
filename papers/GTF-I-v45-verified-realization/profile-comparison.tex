\section{A comparison of packet loss and enclosure dilation}\label{sec:profile-comparison45}
Recall the distinct optimized quantities in Section~\ref{sec:profiles}. Their definitions do not imply equality with each other or with the hidden-state optimum. There is nevertheless a universal one-sided comparison, proved in revision 44 \cite{GTF44} and reproduced here. Define the packet loss rate
\[
 \mathfrak r_{\calA}(k)=\sup_{B\ge1}\frac{\chi_{\calA}(k,B)}{B}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Packet--enclosure comparison]\label{thm:comparison45}
For every finite orthogonal alphabet, positive inradius $a$, and $k$ for which the enclosure family is nonempty,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile-comparison45}
 \mathfrak r_{\calA}(k)\le\log\lambda_{\calA}(k,a).
\end{equation}
In particular, an invariant enclosure with at most $k$ vertices and positive inradius implies $\Gamma_{\calA}(k,B)=0$ at every length $B$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First take any feasible enclosure $P=\conv(v_1,\ldots,v_m)$, $m\le k$, contained in the unit ball and containing $a\Ball$, with $U_aP\subset\lambda P$ for every command. Choose common stochastic rows representing $\lambda^{-1}U_av_i$ by the vertices of $P$. For any fixed unit initial vector $u$, choose an initial distribution with mean $au$. For every word of length $n$ the vertex mean is
\[
 \E[v_{S_n}\mid w]=a\lambda^{-n}U_wu.
\]
Under any independent length-$B$ packet law repeated $j$ times, let $Y_{jB}=U_wu$. Then
\[
 a\lambda^{-jB}
   =\E\ip{Y_{jB}}{v_{S_{jB}}}
   \le\E\norm{\E[Y_{jB}\mid S_{jB}]}=q_{jB}.
\]
The equality is obtained by conditioning on the entire word; the inequality uses $\norm{v_s}\le1$. Repeated application of Lemma~\ref{lem:packet} gives
$q_{jB}\le(1-D_k(p))^j$. Taking $j$th roots and then $j\to\infty$ yields $\lambda^{-B}\le1-D_k(p)$. Optimize over $p$, take logarithms and divide by $B$. Then optimize over $B$ and take the infimum over feasible enclosures. No bounded final decoder at all these lengths was assumed; only the bounded auxiliary vertex column was used in the pairing. This avoids imposing an anytime response task.
\end{proof}

The proof yields a lower bound for every common positive enclosure from a converse applicable to all hidden machines. It does not reconstruct such an enclosure from an arbitrary compatible flag in Theorem~\ref{thm:flag45}. The high-dimensional continuation cube in that theorem is exactly where additional hidden degrees of freedom may occur.

\begin{proposition}[A geometric meaning of zero packet distortion]\label{prop:zero-packet45}
For any $k,B$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Gamma_{\calA}(k,B)=0$;
\item for one, hence every, full-support word law $p$ on $\calA^B$, $D_k(p)=0$;
\item there is a unit $u$ with $|\{U_wu:|w|=B\}|\le k$.
\end{enumerate}
If $\Gamma_{\calA}(k,B)=0$ for every $B$, then a unit vector has a synchronization orbit of cardinality at most $k$. Conversely such a vector makes all those packet values zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each scalar product in the full-support average is at most one. Equality of the maximum with one requires every orbit point to equal one of the centers, and attainment makes this an equivalence. The same set of centers then witnesses zero distortion for every word law. For the all-length assertion, use the nested compact sets $C_B$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:stabilize45}. Conversely normalized word orbits lie in the finite synchronization orbit, and multiplication by the reference command preserves their cardinality.
\end{proof}

An invariant direction may therefore make a whole-space packet profile zero even when active directions elsewhere require growing memory. The projection to $R\ominus R^{\sync^\circ}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main45} is not cosmetic: it removes precisely this obstruction to using the packet profile for the boundedness problem.
