\section{The numerical experiment and the register}\label{sec:model}
Let $V=\R^D$, let $\calA$ be a nonempty finite alphabet, and fix orthogonal matrices $U_a$, $a\in\calA$. Write $U_w=U_{a_N}\cdots U_{a_1}$ in the order of execution. Let $X\subset\Sphere^{D-1}$ be a nonempty finite seed set and $0<\rho\le1$. The required law is \eqref{eq:target}. Commands are externally supplied, not chosen by a policy inside the simulator. No distribution on command words is part of the specification.

\begin{definition}[Clocked atomic-row realization]\label{def:machine}
At horizon $N$, a machine consists of finite sets $S_t$ with $|S_t|=K_t$ for $0\le t\le N$, an initialization probability row $E_x$ on $S_0$, stochastic matrices $T_{t,a}:S_{t-1}\to S_t$, and terminal columns $d_j:S_N\to[-1,1]$. Its numerical mean is
\[
 E_x T_{1,a_1}\cdots T_{N,a_N}d_j.
\]
After the atomic query $j$, it draws and holds the binary answer with that mean. The final answer cut has two labels. Its peak is $\max(2,K_0,\ldots,K_N)$. Available labels count even when unreachable; adding unused normalized rows is permitted.
\end{definition}

All persistent randomness, input history and protocol distinctions must be in the current label. Only the external epoch, the prescribed horizon and the read-only row tables are free. Table construction, lookup, arithmetic and exact sampling of known real stochastic rows are uncharged. Matrices, rows and state sets may depend on $N$; this is nonuniform positive-realization width, not uniform computational space. A common-row construction uses the same command matrices at all command epochs but may still have an $N$-dependent decoder. It need not answer at earlier stopping lengths.

All logarithms in finite budgets are natural. In particular, every width is at least two because the held answer is charged.

Let $W_{N,\varepsilon}$ be the least peak among machines whose every binary row differs from \eqref{eq:target} by at most $\varepsilon$ in total variation. For fixed finite $K_\bullet$ the optimal actual error $\mathcal E_N(K_\bullet)$ is attained: the row parameters lie in a finite product of compact sets and the error is a finite maximum of continuous functions. For planar results we use $X=\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2\}$ and include the identity letter. We keep $\rho\le1/10$ and $\varepsilon<\rho/(2\sqrt2)$.

\begin{lemma}[Calibration under any external test law]\label{lem:calibration}
Fix a seed $x\in X$ and any law on the complete command word, independent of private initialization. Let $Y_t=U_{a_t}\cdots U_{a_1}x$, $Z_t=\E[Y_t\mid S_t]$ and $q_t=\E\norm{Z_t}$. Every machine with row error at most $\varepsilon$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:calibration}
 q_N\ge\kappa:=\rho/\sqrt D-2\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
At a boundary preceding an independent complete packet, the next boundary admits the conditional-mean identity used in Lemma~\ref{lem:packet}. No single-step independence inside a packet is asserted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed word, every coordinate of $\E[d(S_N)\mid w]-\rho Y_N$ has modulus at most $2\varepsilon$. Since $\norm{Y_N}=1$, averaging gives
\[
 \E\ip{Y_N}{d(S_N)}\ge\rho-2\varepsilon\sqrt D.
\]
On the other hand this pairing equals $\E\ip{Z_N}{d(S_N)}$ and is at most $\sqrt D q_N$. This proves \eqref{eq:calibration}. Combining terminal columns is a proof device, not a requirement to provide their joint output distribution.
\end{proof}
