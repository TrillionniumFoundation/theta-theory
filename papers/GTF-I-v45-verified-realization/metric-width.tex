\section{Minimal ordinary type and almost-everywhere widths}\label{sec:metric}
Uniform bad approximation is substantially stronger than the condition needed to determine the logarithmic width exponent. We first deduce the minimal-type assertion without any measure-theoretic hypothesis.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \eqref{eq:log-law} in Theorem~\ref{thm:metric-main}]
If $\omega^*(\alpha)=r$, then for every $0<h<1/(2r-1)$ there is $b_h>0$ such that
\[
 \norm{m\cdot\alpha}_{\R/\Z}\ge b_h\norm{m}_1^{-r-h}
                          \quad(m\ne0).
\]
Indeed the definition of the limsup gives this inequality for all sufficiently large $\norm{m}_1$, and rational independence makes the finite remaining minimum positive. Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:power-type} with $\tau=r+h$. It gives
\[
 \frac{r}{2r+2h+1}\le
 \liminf_N\frac{\log W_{N,\varepsilon}}{\log N}
 \le\limsup_N\frac{\log W_{N,\varepsilon}}{\log N}
 \le\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{r(1+h)}{2r+1+h}\right\}.
\]
The second term is the minimum because $h(2r-1)<1$. Let $h\downarrow0$. This proves \eqref{eq:log-law}. No positive uniform constant at exponent $r$ was used.
\end{proof}

For completeness we prove a more quantitative metric statement, using only the summable part of metric Diophantine approximation. It does not claim a new metric theorem.

\begin{lemma}[A summable logarithmic separation]\label{lem:metric-separation}
For Lebesgue-almost every $\alpha\in[0,1]^r$, and every $s>1$, there is $b_s>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-separation}
 \norm{m\cdot\alpha}_{\R/\Z}\ge
       \frac{b_s}{\norm{m}_1^r(\log(2+\norm{m}_1))^s}
                     \quad(m\ne0).
\end{equation}
Such a vector has $\omega^*(\alpha)=r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a nonzero integer vector $m$, the map $\alpha\mapsto m\cdot\alpha\pmod1$ sends Haar measure on the torus to uniform circle measure. There are at most $C_rH^{r-1}$ integer vectors with $\norm{m}_1=H$. Hence the measures of the events
\[
 \norm{m\cdot\alpha}_{\R/\Z}<[\norm{m}_1^r(\log(2+\norm{m}_1))^s]^{-1}
\]
have finite sum, bounded by a constant times
$\sum_{H\ge1}[H(\log(2+H))^s]^{-1}$.
The measure of their tail union tends to zero. Almost every vector violates the unscaled inequality only finitely often. Remove the measure-zero rational hyperplanes and reduce the constant to cover the finite exceptions. Intersecting the full-measure sets for rational $s>1$ gives the assertion for every real $s>1$: choose rational $1<s_0<s$ and use $(\log(2+H))^{-s}\le(\log(2+H))^{-s_0}$.

This bound implies $\omega^*(\alpha)\le r$. For the reverse inequality, place the $(M+1)^r$ values $m\cdot\alpha\pmod1$, $m\in\{0,\ldots,M\}^r$, in fewer than $(M+1)^r$ intervals of equal length. A collision gives a nonzero difference of norm at most $rM$ and distance $O(M^{-r})$. As $M\to\infty$ the norms of some such differences must tend to infinity, since rational independence excludes an exact relation. Thus $\omega^*(\alpha)\ge r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative bounds under logarithmic separation]\label{prop:metric-rates}
Suppose \eqref{eq:log-separation} holds for some $s>0$ and $b_s>0$. Then there are $c,C>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-rate}
 cN^{r/(2r+1)}(\log(N+2))^{-2rs/(2r+1)}
 \le W_{N,\varepsilon}(\alpha)
 \le CN^{r/(2r+1)}(\log(N+2))^{2r^2s/(2r+1)}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The packet bound \eqref{eq:psi-lower} gives
\[
 N<B+b_s^{-2}B^{2r+1}(\log(2+B))^{2s}\log(1/\kappa).
\]
With $B\le r(2K)^{1/r}$ this implies
$N\le C_0K^{(2r+1)/r}(\log(2+K))^{2s}$.
If $K>N$, the proposed lower bound is already true after reducing its constant. Otherwise $\log(2+K)\le\log(2+N)$, and rearrangement proves the first inequality of \eqref{eq:log-rate}.

For the upper inequality repeat the residue argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:weak-denominator}. It produces $q\le Q^r$, $\eta_\alpha(q)\le Q^{-1}$ and $H\le rq^{1/r}$ with
\[
 \frac{b_s}{H^r(\log(2+H))^s}\le\frac{H}{qQ}.
\]
Since $q\le Q^r$, it follows for $Q\ge2$ that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-denominator}
                   q\ge c_0\frac{Q^r}{(\log(Q+2))^{rs}}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $b_sqQ\le H^{r+1}(\log(2+H))^s
\le C_rq^{1+1/r}(\log(Q+2))^s$, and raising the rearranged inequality to the $r$th power gives \eqref{eq:log-denominator}.

Choose an integer
\[
 Q=\left\lceil C_2N^{1/(2r+1)}
                   (\log(N+2))^{2rs/(2r+1)}\right\rceil.
\]
For sufficiently large fixed $C_2$, \eqref{eq:log-denominator} implies
$N\eta_\alpha(q)/q^2\le1/100$ and $q\ge4$. To see the constant choice explicitly, for large $N$ one has $\log(Q+2)\le C_3\log(N+2)$ with $C_3$ fixed; thus the ratio is bounded by a constant times $C_2^{-(2r+1)}$. Enlarge the final constant for the finitely many initial horizons. Theorem~\ref{thm:arithmetic} gives an exact machine with $q\le Q^r$ labels, proving the upper bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Completion of Theorem~\ref{thm:metric-main}]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:metric-separation} with $s=1+\delta$ and then Proposition~\ref{prop:metric-rates}. This yields \eqref{eq:ae-log-bounds} on one full-measure set for all fixed $\delta>0$. These statements concern a fixed alphabet before the horizon grows. None of their constants is asserted uniform in $r$ or near a rational relation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rational dependencies without redundant exponents}
The number of listed rotations need not be the relevant arithmetic rank. The following result covers finite rational reparametrizations rather than assigning one exponent to every redundant letter.

\begin{corollary}[Finite rational extensions]\label{cor:rational-extension}
Suppose an alphabet contains $I$ and rotations through angles $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_d$, where $1,\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_d$ are rationally independent and $\omega^*(\beta)=d$. Suppose every additional angle belongs to $\Q+\sum_{j=1}^d\Q\beta_j$. Then
\[
       \lim_N\frac{\log W_{N,\varepsilon}}{\log N}=\frac{d}{2d+1}.
\]
The same assertion holds on a full-measure set of $\beta$ with the logarithmic upper and lower powers of \eqref{eq:ae-log-bounds}, replacing $r$ by $d$. Reflection may be added. If all angles are rational, exact width is bounded independently of $N$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Restriction to the displayed $d$-angle subalphabet gives the lower bound. Choose one common denominator $h$ and integers $t_i,n_{ij}$ so that every angle is
$t_i/h+\sum_jn_{ij}\gamma_j$ with $\gamma_j=\beta_j/h$. Multiplication by $h$ shows
$\norm{m\cdot\gamma}_{\R/\Z}\ge h^{-1}\norm{m\cdot\beta}_{\R/\Z}$.
Thus every separating power or logarithmic inequality for $\beta$ holds for $\gamma$ with a changed constant.

Take a simultaneous approximant $p_j/q$ to $\gamma$ from the proof above. A command's irrational part has residual angle at most
$2\pi(\sum_j|n_{ij}|)/(qQ)$. The same polygon construction therefore works, after changing only fixed constants. Store in addition the rational phase $c\in\Z/h\Z$, updated by $c\mapsto c+t_i$. The decoder rotates the stored polygon vector by $2\pi c/h$. This uses a peak of $\max(2,hq)$ labels, not an uncharged phase. Reflection sends $c$ to $-c$ and conjugates the polygon phase; the regular polygon and the construction are reflection-invariant. The exact expectation identity is unchanged. The finite multiplier $h$ does not change a logarithmic exponent or the displayed logarithmic powers.

For a purely rational alphabet, choose a common period $h$ and retain the seed identifier and the accumulated phase. This uses at most $4h$ command labels for our four planar seeds, plus the held binary answer convention. No infinite-gap hypothesis is involved.
\end{proof}
