## 12.1–12.4: a structural boundedness theorem, including nongapped actions

`thm:main45` restricts commands to the actual invariant seed span R. It forms the closed normal subgroup L generated by `U_a^(-1)U_b`. Equivalently, L is generated topologically by differences of equal-length word products. Exact horizon-specific numerical widths are uniformly bounded **if and only if L is finite**. In the infinite case width tends to infinity, not just along a subsequence, at every fixed error below the explicit activated threshold `rho*eta_X/(2*sqrt(D))`.

The active generated group H by itself gives the wrong answer. A single irrational rotation generates an infinite group but its motion is completely determined by the length and can be put into the allowed horizon-dependent decoder. The relative subgroup removes precisely this common motion. Thus the result is sensitive to the clocked resource actually defined, rather than borrowing an autonomous or anytime impossibility theorem.

The main new steps are `lem:tail45`, `lem:stabilize45` and `lem:positive-packet45`. Relative word orbits are nested after removal of a reference command. A uniformly bounded orbit cardinality would stabilize to a finite set invariant under every sufficiently late conjugate of the command differences. Compact recurrence of the reference matrix makes every tail generate L. On the complement of the fixed space of its identity component, no such finite orbit exists. Nested compactness then supplies one finite word length at which every starting direction has more than k distinct endpoints, and the uniform complete-word law has strictly positive k-center distortion.

Only after that geometric step is the inherited endpoint conditional-centroid lemma used. Independent complete packets repeatedly lose a positive fraction of amplitude. All internal computation in a packet remains unrestricted; the endpoint kernel is common only because the machine has no extra persistent transcript. Terminal coordinate calibration supplies the positive amplitude that makes bounded width impossible. The proof allows all hidden states, cut-dependent matrices, and an entirely new machine at every horizon.

`cor:planar-dichotomy45` consequently classifies **all** finite planar rotation alphabets for boundedness, including rational dependencies, Liouville parameters and the absence of an identity letter: boundedness holds exactly when all angle differences from one reference angle are rational. This is not an all-angle rate formula. Noncommuting infinite-group examples with finite relative subgroup, noncommuting divergent SO(3) examples, and unused-coordinate examples distinguish the quantifiers explicitly.

## 12.1 and 11.2: exact positive continuation and Hankel comparison

`thm:flag45` states the exact minimum terminal-error formula for a prescribed state profile, in the full suffix-response cubes. The minimum is half the distance of the target initial means to a compatible root polytope, optimized over flags whose command restrictions lie in the next polytope. Both directions and attainment are proved. Hidden state continuations may lie outside the small signed target span, which is why the full continuation coordinates are retained.

This is a normalized finite controlled version of classical shift-cone realization, not a priority claim for invariant cones or Hankel rank. Revision 44 already supplied the corresponding normal form, row-defect dual, and three-rank example. The current text preserves their substance, makes the actual-error formula explicit, and extends the separate rank-three statement to planar alphabets without an identity letter and signals up to one half. The displayed rank chain now distinguishes ordinary, nonnegative, normalized stochastic factor rank, and actual state count. The dual row certificate is stated in mean coordinates, so its final TV factor is one half.

`thm:comparison45` reproduces revision 44's one-sided inequality between optimized packet rate and common enclosure dilation. It is credited and is not claimed as a new result of v45. `prop:zero-packet45` adds the finite-orbit characterization of vanishing packet distortion, thereby connecting the packet invariant to the new boundedness theorem. Projection to the moving active space is essential: a fixed direction could otherwise make a whole-space distortion zero despite growing memory elsewhere.

The dedicated literature section compares Benvenuti–Farina Theorems 2–3 and Section VII, signed Hankel rank and Balle–Panangaden–Precup Theorems 2 and 11, Ohta's reachable/null-space and tensor discussion, and Finesso–Grassi–Spreij's distinct factorization and HMM-parametrization steps. It separates stationary all-length realization, finite-horizon controlled rows, signed linear minimization, and the actual probability-error objective. Originality is not asserted merely because one uses different terminology.

## A finite algebraic consequence

`thm:decision45` decides the boundedness question for matrices and seeds in a specified real number field. It computes the invariant seed span by exact rank tests, generates command differences and their conjugates under a reference command, and closes under multiplication and inversion. A finite subgroup of GL_d(F), with field degree e, has order below `3^((de)^2)`, by restriction of scalars, an invariant integral lattice and the torsion-free level-three congruence kernel. Therefore closure stabilizes in the finite case, while exceeding the bound certifies the infinite case. The proof supplies a finite stopping criterion, not a truncation guess.

Finite linear-group recognition is classical, and Detinko–Flannery–O'Brien provide substantially better algorithms. The new consequence is that this algebraic decision answers the width boundedness question. The manuscript does not claim practical complexity or a new general group-recognition algorithm. The accompanying code executes finite rational examples and exact certificates, not the entire worst-case enumeration for arbitrary algebraic data. For a rational axis rotation of trace 11/5, the infinite-order certificate avoids even the enormous enumeration bound. No external group-expansion theorem is used in that conclusion.

## 11.1: the minimum in the minimal-type proof

The sharper expression `min{1/2,r(1+h)/(2r+1+h)}` is retained, and the proof chooses `0<h<1/(2r-1)` before sending h to zero. The active branch is justified explicitly. For clarity, dropping a minimum from an **upper** bound yields a weaker bound which is still logically implied; it is not by itself a false inference. At r=1,h=2, the weaker bound 3/5 follows from the sharper 1/2 bound, although the two expressions cannot be equated. The revision therefore improves the display and removes any ambiguity without claiming a counterexample to a logically valid weaker inequality. The same point was recognized in the intervening v44 response.

## 11.3–11.6 and minor comments: scope and precise conventions

The abstract and the formal definition name nonuniform clocked atomic-row positive-realization label width. Horizon, epoch, tables, real arithmetic and atomic sampling remain free in that model. The separate finite-bit compiler and its full proof/code from v44 are retained unchanged; work bits, random words and counters are charged there. No result about clean labels is substituted for a total-work bound.

The almost-everywhere theorem now states inside the theorem that constants depend on the fixed angle vector, logarithmic parameter, alphabet size, signal and error; they are not uniform near rational relations or as r grows. German's definitions fix ordinary/individual versus uniform exponents, and the direct DGLPS v2 comparison remains. The monotonic passage from rational logarithmic powers to all real powers is explicit. Natural logarithms, the two-label held answer, the rational-phase peak `max(2,hq)`, and the independent complete-packet convention are stated. Circle centers have unit norm; exponential cone-program size remains explicit; the case of infinite packet cost is given its direct exclusion interpretation.

The packing–dilation balance is a conditional corollary. The new boundedness criterion is an unconditional qualitative classification within the specified orthogonal-response model. It does not identify optimal finite widths, sharp constants, every divergent growth rate, the exact Liouville upper limit, or a critical error formula. No arbitrary nonorthogonal or nonlinear process is included by implication.
