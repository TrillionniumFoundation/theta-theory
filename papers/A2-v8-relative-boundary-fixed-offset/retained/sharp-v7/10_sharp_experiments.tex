\section{Moving supports and sharp experiment sizes}
\label{sec:v7-sharp}
Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-transfer} gives a uniform sufficient condition for replacing the physical experiment by its boundary law. We now determine an exact tangent benchmark and transfer its transition to positive offsets. Throughout this section only, the two facing contact graphs are identical and even in their tangent coordinates. The selected channel and the remaining geometric parameters range over a compact family satisfying the general hypotheses. No extra symmetry is added to Theorems~\ref{thm:g-stability} and~\ref{thm:v6-transfer}.

Write $a=\sinh\gamma/g$, $u_j=e^{-j\gamma}$, and use the common scaled endpoint and residual-time observation space from Section~\ref{sec:v6-transfer}. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-quadratic-hessians}
 H_j=a\begin{pmatrix}\coth(j\gamma)&-\csch(j\gamma)\\-\csch(j\gamma)&\coth(j\gamma)\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad H_\infty=aI.
\end{equation}
Both matrices have determinant $a^2$. Let $\Pi_j^0$ and $\Pi_\infty^0$ have constant density $a/\pi$ on, respectively,
\[
 D_j=\{(z,s):s\ge0,\ s+\tfrac12z^{\mathsf T}H_jz<1\},
 \qquad D_\infty=\{(z,s):s\ge0,\ s+\tfrac a2|z|^2<1\}.
\]
These are probability laws: a section at $s$ has area $2\pi(1-s)/a$, and integration over $0\le s\le1$ gives volume $\pi/a$. For $d>0$ small enough set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-p0}
 p_j^0(d)=\frac{d^2}{2A\sinh(j\gamma)},\qquad
 P_j^0(d)=(1-p_j^0(d))\delta_\partial+p_j^0(d)\Pi_j^0,
\end{equation}
and define $P_\partial^0(d)$ by replacing $\Pi_j^0$ with $\Pi_\infty^0$, keeping the same mass. Superscript zero denotes a quadratic tangent experiment, not an exact finite-offset billiard probability.

\begin{proposition}[Exact tangent overlaps]\label{prop:v7-overlap}
With total variation defined as $\sup_B|P(B)-Q(B)|$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-delta}
 \delta_j=\frac2\pi\arcsin u_j
 =1-\frac4\pi\arctan\sqrt{\tanh(j\gamma/2)}.
\end{equation}
For positive integers $k,n$,
\begin{align}
 \|\Pi_j^0-\Pi_\infty^0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}&=\delta_j,\label{eq:v7-single-tv}\\
 \|(\Pi_j^0)^{\otimes k}-(\Pi_\infty^0)^{\otimes k}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       &=1-(1-\delta_j)^k,\label{eq:v7-selected-exact}\\
 \|(P_j^0(d))^{\otimes n}-(P_\partial^0(d))^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       &=1-(1-p_j^0(d)\delta_j)^n.\label{eq:v7-raw-exact}
\end{align}
The conclusions are exact for the defined tangent experiments.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the orthogonal coordinates proportional to $z_0+z_1$ and $z_0-z_1$, the two eigenvalues of $H_j/a$ are $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}$, where
\[
 \lambda=\tanh(j\gamma/2)=\frac{1-u_j}{1+u_j}.
\]
A common rescaling of a section at $s<1$ therefore compares the unit disk with $\lambda x^2+\lambda^{-1}y^2<1$. The crossing angle in the first quadrant is $\theta_0=\arctan\sqrt\lambda$. The intersection area is
\[
 2\left[\theta_0+\int_{\theta_0}^{\pi/2}
 \frac{\dd\theta}{\lambda\cos^2\theta+\lambda^{-1}\sin^2\theta}\right]
 =4\theta_0.
\]
For the equality, substitute $t=\tan\theta$ in the integral to get
$\int_{\sqrt\lambda}^{\infty}\lambda\,\dd t/(\lambda^2+t^2)=\theta_0$.
Every section has the same overlap fraction $4\theta_0/\pi$. The two three-dimensional densities have the same constant value wherever both are nonzero. Their overlap mass is consequently $4\theta_0/\pi$. Since $\cos(2\theta_0)=u_j$, subtraction from one gives \eqref{eq:v7-delta}.

For equal-density uniform laws, the minimum of the two product densities equals their common product density on the intersection of the product supports and vanishes elsewhere. Its integral is the $k$th power of the one-record overlap mass. This proves \eqref{eq:v7-selected-exact}. In the raw experiment the failure atom has identical mass under both laws. On every stratum specifying which coordinates are failures, the two remaining densities are again equal on their common support. Summing the overlap masses over those strata gives $(1-p_j^0\delta_j)^n$, proving \eqref{eq:v7-raw-exact}. This calculation does not invoke a Gaussian approximation or independence of consecutive collisions.
\end{proof}

The moving support accounts for the first-order discrepancy: $\delta_j=(2/\pi)u_j+O(u_j^3)$. The proposition and its interpretation as a benchmark are due to the source-pinned calculation in \citet{RefereeV6}. To use it for physical positive-offset records, a separate uniform nonlinear estimate is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform approximation by the tangent experiments]
\label{lem:v7-tangent}
On the compact identical-even family there are $d_*>0$ and $C<\infty$, independent of $j\ge1$, such that for $0<d\le d_*$,
\begin{align}
 \|\Pi_{j,d}-\Pi_j^0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 +\|\Pi^\partial_{j,d}-\Pi_\infty^0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}&\le Cd,
                                                    \label{eq:v7-tangent-selected}\\
 \|P_{j,d}-P_j^0(d)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 +\|P^\partial_{j,d}-P_\partial^0(d)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       &\le Cp_j^0(d)d.\label{eq:v7-tangent-raw}
\end{align}
All laws are compared in the same observation coordinates. The superscript $\partial$ laws here are the nonlinear boundary laws of Section~\ref{sec:v6-transfer}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Evenness of the local flight action under simultaneous sign reversal and uniqueness of the small stationary segment imply
\[
 E_j(-z_0,-z_1)=E_j(z_0,z_1),\qquad
 b_j(-z_0,-z_1)=b_j(z_0,z_1).
\]
The half-line construction has the same symmetry. The uniform $C^4$ action and $C^2$ normalized-twist bounds follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:g-relative} and Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-factorization}; these are bounds on a common endpoint box, not separate Taylor constants for each $j$. At zero the normalized twists equal one. Taylor's formula therefore gives, uniformly on that box,
\begin{align*}
 E_j(Y)&=\tfrac12Y^{\mathsf T}H_jY+O(|Y|^4),&
 b_j(Y)&=1+O(|Y|^2),\\
 E_\infty(Y)&=\tfrac a2|Y|^2+O(|Y|^4),&
 b_\infty(Y)&=1+O(|Y|^2).
\end{align*}
The Hessians in \eqref{eq:v7-quadratic-hessians} follow from the finite Jacobi formula and the half-line Hessian formula with equal facing curvatures.

Uniformly positive quadratic lower bounds put all scaled supports inside one fixed $z$-ball. On this ball the action errors after substitution $Y=\sqrt d\,z$ and division by $d$ are at most $Cd|z|^4$; the amplitude errors are at most $Cd|z|^2$. At fixed $z$ the allowed residual interval starts at zero. The difference of its two lengths is bounded by the action error, because $x\mapsto(1-x)_+$ is 1-Lipschitz. Integrating this symmetric difference and the amplitude error gives a signed-mass error at most
\[
 C\frac{d^2t_j}{2\pi A}\,d,
 \qquad t_j=a\csch(j\gamma),
\]
between each nonlinear successful submeasure and its tangent version. The tangent success mass equals $(d^2t_j/(2\pi A))(\pi/a)=p_j^0(d)$ exactly. Since $a$ stays in a compact positive interval, this is $Cp_j^0(d)d$. The nonlinear success masses divided by $p_j^0(d)$ lie between fixed positive constants and differ from one by $O(d)$. Normalizing gives \eqref{eq:v7-tangent-selected}; retaining the failure atom instead gives \eqref{eq:v7-tangent-raw}. In particular, an error in success probability is included, rather than cancelled by an artificial equal-mass convention for the nonlinear laws.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp positive-offset experiment transition]
\label{thm:v7-phase}
Under the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:v7-tangent}, for every $j,k,n$ and $0<d\le d_*$,
\begin{align}
 \left|\|\Pi_{j,d}^{\otimes k}-(\Pi^\partial_{j,d})^{\otimes k}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       -\{1-(1-\delta_j)^k\}\right|&\le Ckd,
                                                   \label{eq:v7-selected-error}\\
 \left|\|P_{j,d}^{\otimes n}-(P^\partial_{j,d})^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       -\{1-(1-p_j^0(d)\delta_j)^n\}\right|&\le Cnp_j^0(d)d.
                                                   \label{eq:v7-raw-error}
\end{align}
Now let $j\to\infty$, allow the geometry to vary within the specified compact family, and write $u_j=e^{-j\gamma}$ with its corresponding parameter value. Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-joint-regime}
                         d_j>0,\qquad d_j/u_j\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
If $k_ju_j\to b\in[0,\infty]$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-selected-phase}
 \|\Pi_{j,d_j}^{\otimes k_j}-(\Pi^\partial_{j,d_j})^{\otimes k_j}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
                    \longrightarrow 1-e^{-2b/\pi}.
\end{equation}
If $n_jp_j^0(d_j)u_j\to b\in[0,\infty]$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-raw-phase}
 \|P_{j,d_j}^{\otimes n_j}-(P^\partial_{j,d_j})^{\otimes n_j}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
                    \longrightarrow 1-e^{-2b/\pi}.
\end{equation}
We interpret $e^{-\infty}=0$. In this regime, vanishing distance is equivalent to $k_ju_j\to0$ or $n_jp_j^0(d_j)u_j\to0$, respectively.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For probability measures, the total-variation distance between product laws is at most the sum of the one-factor distances. Apply this fact to Lemma~\ref{lem:v7-tangent}, and use the triangle and reverse triangle inequalities for the four finite, boundary, and tangent products. Proposition~\ref{prop:v7-overlap} then proves \eqref{eq:v7-selected-error} and \eqref{eq:v7-raw-error}.

Uniform positivity of $\gamma$ on the compact family gives $u_j\to0$. When $k_ju_j\to b<\infty$, we have $k_j\delta_j\to2b/\pi$ and $k_j\delta_j^2\to0$. Thus $(1-\delta_j)^{k_j}\to e^{-2b/\pi}$. Moreover $k_jd_j=(k_ju_j)(d_j/u_j)\to0$, so \eqref{eq:v7-selected-error} proves \eqref{eq:v7-selected-phase}. For raw preparations the same argument applies to $p_j^0(d_j)\delta_j$: it tends to zero, its product with $n_j$ tends to $2b/\pi$, and the error $n_jp_j^0(d_j)d_j$ tends to zero. This proves \eqref{eq:v7-raw-phase} at finite $b$.

It would be incorrect to use the error bounds with the original sample sizes without further argument when $b=\infty$. Instead fix $B>0$. In the selected case project onto $\lfloor B/u_j\rfloor$ observations; in the raw case project onto $\lfloor B/(p_j^0(d_j)u_j)\rfloor$ observations. Each is eventually a subexperiment of the given experiment. The finite-$b$ result gives limiting distance $1-e^{-2B/\pi}$ for that projection. Total variation cannot increase under projection. Letting $B\to\infty$ proves the two infinite-$b$ statements. The same projection argument along any subsequence on which the relevant scaled sample size is bounded away from zero proves necessity in the final assertion. Sufficiency is the already proved $b=0$ case.
\end{proof}

The theorem concerns actual positive offsets and allows growing sample sizes; it does not interchange those limits without \eqref{eq:v7-joint-regime}. The critical number of successful records is of order $e^{j\gamma}$, whereas the critical raw-preparation order is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-critical-cost}
                        d_j^{-2}e^{2j\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $p_j^0(d_j)\asymp d_j^2e^{-j\gamma}$. The extra factor records how difficult it is to acquire a success. Conditional experiments in the theorem comprise a prescribed number of independently prepared successes; they do not additionally retain their waiting times. Raw experiments retain failures and use a prescribed number of preparations.

There is also an exact decision interpretation. For testing two specified finite and boundary laws with equal prior probabilities, the smallest error probability is $(1-\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}})/2$: the sum of the two testing errors is the overlap mass $\int\min\{\dd P,\dd Q\}$. The limit in \eqref{eq:v7-selected-phase} or \eqref{eq:v7-raw-phase} therefore gives limiting optimal error $e^{-2b/\pi}/2$. At $b=0$, all bounded-loss risks are interchangeable under the identity observation map. At a positive critical value there are observable tests with a nonvanishing distinction. These are assertions about replacing two probability families on a common observation space, not a parameter-free simulator for an unknown geometry, a global inverse theorem, or a total-variation claim for the full embedded collision array.
