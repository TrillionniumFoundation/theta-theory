\section{A collision bit at a common finite-memory budget}\label{sec:common-risk}
This section compares total risks for one fixed target, not regrets against
two different Bayes forecasters. It concerns an uncensored raw detector,
so that erasure is literal postprocessing. It does not identify this operation
with erasure \emph{before} a commanded comparator.

\subsection{The counted mechanical realization}
Let the triangular lattice have fundamental area
$\mathfrak a=\sqrt3/2$, let $I_R=[9/20,47/100]$, and put a reflecting
disk of radius $R$ at each lattice point. In each cartridge prepare a
uniform position in the ambient torus, a uniform velocity direction,
and independent uniform apparatus coordinates. A guarded insertion fails
when the position is inside a disk. Otherwise a unit-speed particle is
released. Every attempted insertion consumes one cartridge.

Fix $0<T<3/50$ and $\epsilon>0$ with
$\epsilon(47/100)^2<1$. Distinct lifted disks have separation at least
$3/50$; an outgoing reflected ray cannot reenter the same convex disk
before another collision. Thus each released particle has at most one
collision by time $T$. At a collision let $z$ be its impact coordinate
relative to the disk center. The fixed detector reports $+$ when its
prepared uniform coordinate is at most
$1/2+\epsilon|z|^2/\pi$, and otherwise reports $-$. The controller
never receives $z$ or the uniform seed. This is an internally exact-coordinate
apparatus, not a lower bound on the complexity of physical measurement.

For completeness, incoming one-collision states are parameterized by
\[
 q=Rn_\alpha-sv^-,\quad
 v^-=-\cos\phi\,n_\alpha+\sin\phi\,t_\alpha,
 \quad 0<s<T,\quad |\phi|<\pi/2.
\]
The backward segment remains outside its own disk by convexity and cannot
reach a distinct disk within $T$ by separation. The forward reflected
segment has the same exclusion. Every regular colliding initial state
has its unique impact triple. At fixed incoming laboratory angle the
position Jacobian is $R\cos\phi$, and the angular coordinate change
has determinant of absolute value one. Integration against ambient
preparation $\dd q\,\dd\vartheta/(2\pi\mathfrak a)$ gives collision
mass
\[
 \frac{R}{2\pi\mathfrak a}
      \int_0^T\int_0^{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}
                       \cos\phi\,\dd\phi\,\dd\alpha\,\dd s
 =\frac{2TR}{\mathfrak a}.
\]
Tangencies are null, not an excluded positive-mass neighborhood.
Since $|z|=R$ at impact, the four uncensored raw probabilities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mechanical-cells}
 \begin{split}
 k_\partial&=\pi R^2/\mathfrak a,\qquad
 k_0=1-\pi R^2/\mathfrak a-2TR/\mathfrak a,\\
 k_\pm&=TR/\mathfrak a\ \pm 2T\epsilon R^3/(\pi\mathfrak a).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
They are positive on $I_R$ and sum to one. For $k_0$, positivity follows
already at $R=47/100$, $T=3/50$, using $\mathfrak a>0.86$ and
$\pi<22/7$; the other inequalities follow from the stated bounds.
Their coefficient matrix in degrees zero through three is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&1&0&0\\
 0&-2T/\mathfrak a&T/\mathfrak a&T/\mathfrak a\\
 \pi/\mathfrak a&-\pi/\mathfrak a&0&0\\
 0&0&2T\epsilon/(\pi\mathfrak a)&-2T\epsilon/(\pi\mathfrak a)
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
with determinant of absolute value $4T^2\epsilon/\mathfrak a^3$.
A bounded lookup comparator therefore realizes $A=\{0,1,2,3\}$.
Merging the signs gives the three probabilities
$k_\partial,k_0,k_++k_-$, which span $A=\{0,1,2\}$.
This supplies the dense special cases of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from
one counted apparatus. The stronger complete-record response, positive
filter, boundary-control and surrogate derivations are preserved in the
source companion \cite{A1v5}; the collision calculation above uses their
whole-preparation normalization rather than conditioning away insertion
failures.

\subsection{One target and all state budgets}
Consume two cartridges without censoring. Let $X\in\{\partial,0,+,-\}$
be the first raw report, and let $Y$ be the placement-failure indicator
on the second cartridge. After the first report the controller stores
one of $M$ states, then announces its forecast $a\in[0,1]$ and incurs
$(a-Y)^2$. The erased experiment observes
$e(X)\in\{\partial,0,H\}$ with $e(+)=e(-)=H$.
Both experiments use the same prior, both ambient attempts, and the same
second-cartridge variable $Y$. There is no acceptance charge or saved
cartridge. This is a separate uncensored experiment; it is not subject
to the lower acceptance margin of the streaming theorem.

Put $h(R)=\pi R^2/\mathfrak a$ and define
\[
 p_x=\mu k_x>0,\quad z_x=\mu(k_xh),\quad b_x=z_x/p_x,
 \qquad p_B=\sum_{x\in B}p_x,\quad z_B=\sum_{x\in B}z_x.
\]
For a nonempty subset $B$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:merge-cost}
 \delta(B)=\sum_{x\in B}\frac{z_x^2}{p_x}-\frac{z_B^2}{p_B}
 =\frac1{p_B}\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset B}p_xp_y(b_x-b_y)^2.
\end{equation}
All these quantities are finite combinations of prior moments through
order five and the exact physical constants.

\begin{theorem}[Complete common-risk finite-state comparison]\label{thm:finite-bit-value}
For either alphabet $\mathcal X$ (four raw symbols or three erased tags),
its optimal $M$-state risk, allowing independent randomization, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition-risk}
 R_M(\mathcal X)=\mu h-
  \max_{\substack{\mathcal Q\text{ partition of }\mathcal X\\|\mathcal Q|\le M}}
             \sum_{B\in\mathcal Q}\frac{z_B^2}{p_B}.
\end{equation}
A maximizing partition, its block index and block forecasts $z_B/p_B$
give an attained finite-state implementation.

Let $R_M^+$ and $R_M^0$ denote the raw and erased risks and
$\Gamma_M=R_M^0-R_M^+$. Then $\Gamma_M\ge0$ for every $M$,
$\Gamma_1=0$, and, with $\Delta=\delta(\{+,-\})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-budgets}
 \Gamma_3=\Delta-\min_{x\ne y}\delta(\{x,y\}),\qquad
 \Gamma_M=\Delta\quad(M\ge4).
\end{equation}
The case $M=2$ is exactly \eqref{eq:partition-risk}: it compares the seven
two-block raw partitions and the three two-block erased partitions
(one-block partitions are dominated). Equivalently, if
$D_M^+$ and $D_M^0$ are the minimum sums of merge costs in the two
alphabets, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-decomposition}
 \Gamma_M=\Delta+D_M^0-D_M^+.
\end{equation}
These are common \emph{total} Bayes risks, not two separately normalized
memory regrets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on a block message, the optimal forecast is $z_B/p_B$.
Expanding squared loss gives block contribution
$z_B-z_B^2/p_B$; summing proves the risk of every deterministic partition.
For arbitrary random encoders and decoders, first replace a randomized
forecast at each message by its conditional mean. Holding these at most
$M$ forecasts fixed, each raw symbol can be assigned deterministically to
a forecast minimizing its conditional squared loss. This cannot increase
risk. Reoptimizing each resulting block forecast also cannot increase risk.
Thus a deterministic partition is optimal, and the finite maximum is
attained. A fresh public seed independent of $X,Y$ merely averages such
risks and does not improve the optimum.

The identity \eqref{eq:merge-cost} is obtained by expanding the weighted
variance. The lossless raw risk is $\mu h-\sum_x z_x^2/p_x$; the
lossless erased risk exceeds it by $\Delta$. Adding the optimal merging
distortion in each alphabet gives \eqref{eq:common-decomposition}.
Any erased code is a raw code after the deterministic map $e$, proving
$\Gamma_M\ge0$ directly. With one state both forecasts equal $\mu h$.
With at least four states both alphabets are losslessly encoded. With
three states the erased alphabet is lossless, and an optimal raw partition
can be refined to exactly three blocks without increasing risk. Such a
partition merges exactly one pair. This proves \eqref{eq:all-budgets}.
The partition counts for $M=2$ are the numbers of nontrivial subsets
modulo complementation, namely $(2^4-2)/2=7$ and $(2^3-2)/2=3$.
\end{proof}

Write $\mu_j=\mu(R^j)$, let
$\mu_H(\dd R)=R\mu(\dd R)/\mu_1$, and put
\[
 v=\Var_{\mu_H}(R^2),\qquad
 \bar c=1/2+(\epsilon/\pi)\mu_H(R^2).
\]
The exact lossless gain retained from \cite{A1v5} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical-gain}
 \Delta=\frac{2T\mu_1}{\mathfrak a}
    \frac{(\epsilon v/\mathfrak a)^2}{\bar c(1-\bar c)}>0.
\end{equation}
Indeed conditional on a first hit the sign probability is
$c(R)=1/2+\epsilon R^2/\pi$, and
$\Cov_{\mu_H}(h,c)=\epsilon v/\mathfrak a$.
The two conditional-mean shifts are respectively this covariance divided
by $\bar c$ and its negative divided by $1-\bar c$.
Their weighted squared sum is the covariance squared over
$\bar c(1-\bar c)$. Multiplication by the hit probability proves
\eqref{eq:physical-gain}, which is also precisely the pair cost
$\delta(\{+,-\})$. Full support makes $v>0$. This is an explicit
conditional-variance calculation, not a new general comparison-of-experiments
principle \cite{Blackwell}.

\begin{corollary}[A genuine finite-resolution threshold]\label{cor:threshold}
Fix $T>0$ and a full-support prior on $I_R$. There exists
$\epsilon_*>0$ such that, for $0<\epsilon<\epsilon_*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold}
 \Gamma_M=0\quad(M=1,2,3),\qquad
 \Gamma_M=\Delta>0\quad(M\ge4).
\end{equation}
Thus a four-state, two-bit controller realizes the strict gain, whereas
all smaller state budgets have exactly zero gain in this amplitude range.
This does not identify exact algebraic dimension with useful accuracy.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At $\epsilon=0$, $b_+=b_-=b_H$. The three distinct tag means satisfy
\[
 b_0<\mu h<b_H<b_\partial.
\]
The first strict inequality follows because $k_0$ is strictly decreasing
and $h$ strictly increasing, so $\Cov_\mu(k_0,h)<0$.
For example this sign follows by integrating
$(k_0(R)-k_0(R'))(h(R)-h(R'))/2$ against two independent prior draws.
The second inequality uses the positive covariance of $R$ and $h$.
The last follows in the hit-tilted prior from the same positive covariance
of $R$ and $h$, since the placement posterior is its additional $R$-tilt.
Full support makes all these covariances strict.

For three states the unique zero-distortion merging pattern at amplitude
zero is to keep the three distinct tag means separate and merge the two
signs. Every other pair has positive merge cost. For two states, no optimal
partition at amplitude zero splits the equal-mean signs between its two
blocks. To prove this, use the optimal block means as centers. Equal-mean
points assigned to different centers must be equidistant from them;
otherwise reassignment to the closer center strictly improves risk.
If the centers are distinct, $b_H$ is their midpoint. Each block must
then contain a different extreme tag to place its mean strictly on the
corresponding side of $b_H$. Move one sign into the other block, holding
centers fixed; the cost is unchanged. The vacated-sign block still contains
its extreme tag, so reoptimizing its center strictly reduces the cost,
a contradiction. If the two centers agree, the achieved risk is the
one-state risk, strictly worse than separating one extreme tag. Thus an
optimal two-state partition never splits the signs.

There are finitely many partitions. At amplitude zero every partition
that splits the signs and uses at most two states has a strictly positive
risk gap to the optimum, as does every non-sign-merging three-state
partition. All their risks depend continuously on $\epsilon$ because
the cell masses stay positive. Therefore, for a sufficiently small common
positive $\epsilon_*$, none of these partitions is optimal for $M=2,3$.
Every remaining partition keeps the signs together and has exactly the
risk of the corresponding erased partition, because both $p_++p_-$
and $z_++z_-$ are independent of $\epsilon$. This proves equality of
risks for $M=2,3$; $M=1$ and $M\ge4$ follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-bit-value} and \eqref{eq:physical-gain}.
\end{proof}

The threshold is checkable from finitely many moment expressions: enumerate
the partitions in \eqref{eq:partition-risk}, find their positive gaps at
zero, and bound their continuous rational derivatives while cell masses
stay bounded away from zero. No prior-uniform or horizon-uniform threshold
is claimed. In particular \eqref{eq:physical-gain} can be extremely small
for a concentrated prior. The threshold theorem gives an exact resource
comparison, not a promise of a practically large gain.
