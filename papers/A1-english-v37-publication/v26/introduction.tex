\subsection{Selection geometry and the resolution of an order}
There are two different finite structures in the graph experiment.
An observation order is a path through the lattice of visited sets,
whereas an admissible decoder has only $M$ prediction centres at a
fixed visited set. Counting complete paths discards this distinction.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-separator} bounds adaptive prediction by a minimum
separator whose capacities are the acquired small-ball probabilities.
The bound retains the local recovery constants and the actual
first-block evidences. Completion reports are integrated rather than
forced to follow a prescribed word. The graph optimization is a
vertex-capacitated flow problem on the subset lattice.

For deterministic orders the same lattice gives a minimax recursion.
Along any analytic collision path, determinant contact orders yield a
finite piecewise affine law for the leading memory requirement
(Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-phases}). Minimizing over orders need not preserve
convexity, even though every scalar leading bit profile is convex.
The three-edge experiment has four phases and exhibits this failure.
Its optimal fixed-order penalty over a full interval of resolutions
is twice the optimal penalty at the two endpoints
(Corollary~\ref{cor:v26-starphase}). These statements distinguish a
resolution-dependent order from a single order required to serve an
entire range of resolutions.
