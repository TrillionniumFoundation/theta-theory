\section{Collision thresholds and statistical geometry}
\label{sec:geometric-results}
A collision count near its earliest possible time probes an unusually
rigid part of a dispersing billiard: almost normal repeated flights
between two facing contacts. The probability of such a record decreases
exponentially with its length. Its nonlinear transverse action, however,
has only two free endpoints. Relating these facts requires control of the
physical flux relative to its exponentially small leading value, on a
neighborhood independent of the number of flights.

We construct that relative control and identify its limit as a pair of
semi-infinite boundary actions with normalized determinant amplitudes.
The limiting probability is a nonlinear statistical datum of the contact,
not merely its quadratic Hessian. In the identical-even contact class,
each successive offset coefficient determines one further even graph
jet by a triangular formula with a strictly nonzero diagonal. This gives
analytic contact determination, and finite-dimensional families of actual
obstacles with independent nonlinear statistical coordinates while every
leading count and endpoint metric stays fixed.

Two further inverse questions can be treated quantitatively. For three
unlabelled equal-gap channels near coalescence, the first four count
amplitudes recover the area and the unordered curvature triple with a
sharp pairwise one-third-H\"older estimate. For conditional endpoint
measurements, count probabilities themselves provide the fine timing
calibration needed by high-order extrapolation. The preparation cost of
that calibration has the same order as the conditional acquisition cost,
under an explicitly stated local bracket on the onset.

\subsection{Periodic configurations and the observation}
Fix a full-rank lattice $\Lambda\subset\R^2$ and a positive finite number
of bounded $C^\infty$ strictly convex obstacles $D_1,\ldots,D_s$ with
everywhere positive curvature. All distinct translates $D_a+\lambda$,
$\lambda\in\Lambda$, have disjoint closures. Let $A>0$ be the free area
per lattice cell. The unit-speed billiard is prepared with normalized
phase volume $\dd q\dd\vartheta/(2\pi A)$ on the torus complement.
We observe the collision count $N_t$ and, when specified, the first and
last collision positions. Finite horizon, congruence of the obstacles,
and symmetry are not assumed for the general threshold and boundary-law
results.

Consider a fixed such configuration and a smooth finite-dimensional
family $D_{a,\xi}$ through it. The parameter $\xi$ ranges over a compact
set $K_0$ in a sufficiently small neighborhood of the reference parameter.
These are local statements about arbitrary reference configurations,
not only about circles. A compact family is covered by finitely many
such neighborhoods. Constants at each fixed derivative order may depend
on the corresponding higher boundary norms, the positive separation and
curvature bounds, and the lattice. They do not depend on the collision
order. Statements involving analytic germs additionally assume
real-analytic boundaries; smooth Taylor jets alone do not determine a
smooth graph.

Let $\mathcal E$ be the finite set of oriented closest pairs attaining
the reference minimum gap, modulo common lattice translation. An index
$e=(a,b,\lambda)$ represents a segment from $D_{a,\xi}$ to
$D_{b,\xi}+\lambda$, with reverse $\bar e=(b,a,-\lambda)$.
Section~\ref{sec:g-localization} proves their smooth continuation,
clearance, and completeness in a common ground-onset collar. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-general-data}
 \begin{split}
 g_*&=\min_{e\in\mathcal E}g_e,\qquad
 c_{e,b}=1+g_e\kappa_{e,b}\quad(b=0,1),\\
 c_e&=\sqrt{c_{e,0}c_{e,1}},\qquad
 \gamma_e=\arcosh c_e,\qquad d_{j,e}=t-jg_e.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The two contact curvatures $\kappa_{e,b}>0$ need not agree. The channel
event $E_{j,e}(d)$ consists of $j+1$ impacts alternating between specified
facing patches in the window $jg_e+d$. There are $j$ complete flights.
The patches are fixed around the reference contacts before observation;
they specify a measurable part of a physical record. At a nonminimal
channel's own onset, such selection is required. Its unselected count
law is a different datum.

Parameter derivatives are taken in fixed Euclidean charts; offset
derivatives hold $d$ fixed as an independent coordinate. Matrix norms
are Euclidean operator norms unless a trace norm is specified. Growing
record arrays use the maximum norm. Physical-time derivatives can carry
powers of $j$ and are not identified with normalized offset derivatives.

\subsection{The uniform threshold and conditional metric}
\begin{theorem}[Uniform thresholds for periodic dispersing configurations]
\label{thm:g-stability}
There is $\varepsilon_0>0$, independent of every integer $j\ge1$, with
$\varepsilon_0<\min_{K_0}g_*/4$, such that for
$0\le t-jg_*<\varepsilon_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-competition}
 \Pr_\xi(N_t\ge j+1)=\Pr_\xi(N_t=j+1)
 =\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \frac{d_{j,e,+}^{\,2}}{2A(\xi)\sinh(j\gamma_e(\xi))}
       [1+d_{j,e,+}\mathcal R_{j,e}(\xi,d_{j,e,+})].
\end{equation}
Every fixed mixed derivative of $\mathcal R_{j,e}$ is bounded uniformly
in $j$, with a smooth right extension at zero. For $t\le jg_*$ the
probability is zero. At a selected channel's own window $t=jg_e+d$,
$0<d<\varepsilon_0$, its individual summand is exactly
$\Pr_\xi(E_{j,e}(d))$, whether or not $e$ minimizes the gap.
\end{theorem}

The exponent two is independent of $j$. The essential estimate is
relative to its coefficient, which is exponentially small. The proof
uses a weighted Dirichlet construction and a trace-norm estimate for a
relative determinant. The needed local hypotheses are verified directly
from the configuration. In the triangular one-obstacle near-circle
class, $\mathcal E$ consists of the six unit lattice directions, and
\eqref{eq:g-competition} includes all their competing onsets.

Lift the first and last collision positions to the fixed patches in
$\R^2$, and call them $Q_0,Q_j$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-observed-variance}
 V_{j,b}(d)=\frac1d\left\{
 \E(|Q_b|^2\mid E_{j,e}(d))-
       |\E(Q_b\mid E_{j,e}(d))|^2\right\},\qquad b=0,j.
\end{equation}
These physical variances are invariant under translations and rotations
of the chosen lifts. Their definition supplies neither the unknown
contact point nor its tangent or curvature. The channel gap can be read
from its onset; quantitative calibration is treated separately below.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform two-variance curvature recovery]
\label{thm:v3-tomography}
For every odd $j\ge1$, the right limits $v_{j,0},v_{j,1}$ of
$V_{j,0}(d),V_{j,j}(d)$ exist, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-variance-limits}
 (v_{j,0},v_{j,1})=
 \frac{g_e\coth(j\gamma_e)}{3\sinh\gamma_e}
 \left(\sqrt{\frac{c_{e,1}}{c_{e,0}}},
       \sqrt{\frac{c_{e,0}}{c_{e,1}}}\right).
\end{equation}
Each difference from its right limit is $d$ times a smooth function with
uniform fixed-order derivative bounds. At known $g_e$, the two limits
determine the ordered pair $(\kappa_{e,0},\kappa_{e,1})$, without a
supplied $A$, by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-inverse-map}
 \begin{gathered}
 \frac{\coth(j\gamma)}{\sinh\gamma}
     =\frac3g\sqrt{v_0v_1},\qquad r=\sqrt{v_1/v_0},\\
 \kappa_0=\frac{r\cosh\gamma-1}{g},\qquad
 \kappa_1=\frac{r^{-1}\cosh\gamma-1}{g}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
On every compact positive gap--curvature box this observation map and
its inverse are Lipschitz, with constants independent of odd $j$,
including at $\kappa_{e,0}=\kappa_{e,1}$. If the measured variances at
offset $d$ have error at most $\delta$, a reconstruction constrained to
such a box has curvature error at most $C(d+\delta)$.
\end{theorem}

The conditional metric keeps the two contact curvatures separately,
whereas a scalar leading flux need not do so. Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-fibers}
realizes this distinction by smooth periodic obstacles with the same
gap, area, and every leading count amplitude. Proposition~\ref{prop:v4-saturation}
shows that the entire leading hierarchy is determined by one-flight
leading measurements. Acquiring the conditional positions costs
independent physical preparations; it is not justified by treating
successive impacts as independent samples.

\subsection{Nonlinear boundary laws and their inverses}
For one channel abbreviate
\[
 E_j(u,v)=W_{j,e}(u,v)-jg_e,\qquad
 b_j(u,v)=\frac{-W_{j,e,uv}(u,v)}{-W_{j,e,uv}(0,0)},\qquad
 F_j(d)=\frac{2A\sinh(j\gamma_e)}{d^2}\Pr(E_{j,e}(d)).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-factorization} constructs boundary actions $S_b$
and positive amplitudes $B_b$ from half-line reflected segments, and
proves, for $p=j\bmod2$,
\[
 E_j=S_0(u)+S_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),\qquad
 b_j=B_0(u)B_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),\quad 0<\tau<1.
\]
The endpoint box and rate can be fixed independently of $j$; each
fixed differentiability order has its own constant. The second estimate
is for the normalized twist, not an absolute approximation to a
vanishing quantity. Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-law} passes this factorization
to the actual full-phase probabilities on one closed offset interval:
\[
 F_j(d)\longrightarrow\mathcal F_p(d)
 =\frac{\sqrt{a_0a_p}}{\pi d^2}
   \int(d-S_0(u)-S_p(v))_+B_0(u)B_p(v)\dd u\dd v,
 \qquad a_b=S_b''(0).
\]
It also gives the conditional endpoint and residual-time law, with
bounded-Lipschitz convergence in scaled coordinates. The common energy
constraint couples the endpoints; the limiting probability is not a
product measure.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-jets} identifies a triangular inverse from the
coefficients of this law to all even contact jets, when the facing
graphs are identical and even. Its nonzero diagonal is explicit at every
order. For real-analytic boundaries, Corollary~\ref{cor:v5-analytic-rigidity}
gives contact determination and analytic continuation of each
participating obstacle in its contact frame. Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-realization}
realizes arbitrary finite collections of those statistical coordinates
by fixed-area, fixed-gap, fixed-curvature analytic obstacles.
Proposition~\ref{prop:v5-one-flight} supplies the corresponding one-flight
inverse. Thus the higher information is nonlinear, not an exclusive
advantage of long records; the long-bridge contribution is its uniform
relative factorization and limiting physical law.

For unlabelled equal-gap counts, the exact infinite-sequence inverse in
Theorem~\ref{thm:g-inverse} is complemented by a finite quantitative one.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-pairwise} recovers the area and unordered curvature
triple near coalescence from $C_1,C_2,C_3,C_4$. Symmetric coefficient
coordinates have a nonsingular analytic observation map there. Recovering
its three roots gives the sharp pairwise exponent $1/3$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-sharpness}. The distinct circular-reference exponent
$1/2$ remains in Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-sequence-stability}.

Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration} includes the fine timing
measurement in the preparation budget. It starts from the coarse local
bracket $|j(g-g_0)|\le h/4$ and estimates the zero of a polynomial
interpolating square roots of count probabilities. It reaches gap error
$O_m(h^{m+1}/j)$ and curvature error $O_m(h^m)$ with sufficient
preparation order $e^{j\gamma}h^{-(2m+2)}$ times a confidence logarithm.
The initial coarse bracket has to be supplied; the theorem is not a
global onset-search or minimax result.

\subsection{Marked thresholds and record response}
Let $x_{e,0},x_{e,1}$ be the normal outgoing states and $f_\xi$ a smooth
collision mark near them. Put $a_{e,b}=f_\xi(x_{e,b})$,
$\bar f_e=(a_{e,0}+a_{e,1})/2$, and
$w_{j,e}=\sum_{i=0}^j a_{e,i\bmod2}$.
\begin{theorem}[Marked response and the exact axial source domain]
\label{thm:g-marked}
At the selected channel's own threshold,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-marked}
 Z_{j,e}(\xi,q,d)=
 \frac{d^2e^{qw_{j,e}(\xi)}}{2A(\xi)\sinh(j\gamma_e(\xi))}
       [1+d\mathcal R^f_{j,e}(\xi,q,d)].
\end{equation}
Every fixed mixed derivative of the remainder is uniformly bounded in
$j$ on compact parameter and source sets. The complete marked event in
the ground collar is the sum at the positive channel offsets. For fixed
$\xi$, real $q$, and sufficiently small fixed $d\ge0$, the series
$\sum_{j,e}Z_{j,e}(\xi,q,d)/d^2$ converges exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-source-domain}
              \max_{e\in\mathcal E}(q\bar f_e-\gamma_e)<0.
\end{equation}
Right limits are used at $d=0$. Every fixed mixed derivative is summable
on compact subsets of this domain. Finitely many real sources are
allowed, with a scalar product in place of $q\bar f_e$.
\end{theorem}

This series ranges over different onset windows. It is not a generating
function for unrestricted itineraries in a single long observation
window. Its first poles and the interface jumps follow from the same
uniform construction. The subsequent sections retain the full smooth
and moving-cut record responses. The circular appendices supply all
explicit specializations, and the source-pinned two-collision companion
addresses its separate regular-window problem. The comparison with
marked-length rigidity, Prony inversion, and other statistical limits
is given after the main proofs.
