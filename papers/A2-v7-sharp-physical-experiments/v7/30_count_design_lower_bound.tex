\section{The acquisition cost of coalescing unlabelled curvatures}
\label{sec:v7-count-lower}
The count-only upper bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-count-only} has both a rare-event cost and a singular-inverse cost. We show that these powers, including the confidence logarithm, are necessary within its Bernoulli query design. The experiment restriction is part of the theorem, not an implicit claim about all available billiard data.

\subsection{The design and two physical alternatives}
Fix a sufficiently small compact neighborhood of the circular member $R=1/4$, $\alpha=\beta=\zeta=0$ in \eqref{eq:g-isogap-family}, containing a disk of $(\beta,\zeta)$ perturbations at $\alpha=0$. Fix $J<\infty$ and $H<\infty$. A query chooses a flight number $1\le j\le J$ and a physical time $t$, then obtains only
\[
                         X=\one_{\{N_t\ge j+1\}}
\]
from a fresh independent preparation. A strategy may randomize independently of the parameter, choose its queries from the past returned bits, and stop at a stopping time $T$. For every realized history its actual excess must satisfy $0<t-jg\le Hh$. This is a Bernoulli observation, even though a physical apparatus could retain additional values of $N_t$. Such additional values are not included in the lower-bound experiment.

The alternatives below have the same gap $g=1/2$. We may give this exact gap to the strategy; the lower bound will then also apply with only the supplied coarse bracket of the upper-bound procedure. Write $\mathsf P_s$ for the law of its entire stopped transcript when $(\beta,\zeta)=(s,0)$ and $\alpha=0$, including its independent randomization and final estimate if these are retained.

\begin{lemma}[Cubic indistinguishability in finite-offset counts]
\label{lem:v7-cubic-count}
Uniformly for $j\le J$, $0<d\le d_*$, and sufficiently small $|s|$, the success probabilities $p_{j,d}(s)=\Pr_s(N_{jg+d}\ge j+1)$ satisfy
\begin{align}
 |p_{j,d}(s)-p_{j,d}(-s)|&\le C_Jd^2|s|^3,\label{eq:v7-cubic-p}\\
 c_Jd^2\le p_{j,d}(\pm s)&\le C_Jd^2<\tfrac12.\label{eq:v7-p-bounds}
\end{align}
The two curvature multisets satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-curvature-separation}
 c|s|\le\operatorname{dist}_{\rm match}(\kappa(s),\kappa(-s))\le C|s|.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A spatial rotation through $\pi/3$ preserves the triangular lattice. Applied to the support function \eqref{eq:g-isogap-family} with $\alpha=0$, it acts on $(\beta,\zeta)$ by
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\beta\\\zeta\end{pmatrix}\longmapsto
 \begin{pmatrix}-1/2&-\sqrt3/2\\ \sqrt3/2&-1/2\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}\beta\\\zeta\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This follows by replacing $\theta$ with $\theta-\pi/3$; the factor $1-\cos6\theta$ is unchanged. Rotation preserves phase volume and the unlabelled count event. Thus the normalized function
\[
                  f_{j,d}(\beta,\zeta)=p_{j,d}(\beta,\zeta)/d^2
\]
is invariant under this order-three rotation. Its differential at the origin is zero, since the rotation has no nonzero invariant real linear functional. Theorem~\ref{thm:g-stability} gives bounded third parameter derivatives of $f_{j,d}$, uniformly at these fixed flight numbers and throughout the closed offset collar. Taylor expansion at zero makes the constant and quadratic terms cancel between $(s,0)$ and $(-s,0)$; the linear term vanishes by the symmetry. This proves \eqref{eq:v7-cubic-p} for actual finite-offset probabilities. Positivity and boundedness of the normalized onset laws prove \eqref{eq:v7-p-bounds} after reducing $d_*$. No replacement by leading amplitudes is made in this argument.

The radii are $(R+36s,R-18s,R-18s)$ and $(R-36s,R+18s,R+18s)$. At $s>0$, two of the first triple's curvatures equal $1/(R-18s)$, whereas two of the second triple's curvatures equal $1/(R+18s)$. Any matching pairs at least one of those two large first-triple curvatures with a small second-triple curvature. Their difference is
\[
                     \frac{36s}{R^2-324s^2}.
\]
This proves the lower bound. A direct coordinate matching and the bounded derivative of $r\mapsto1/r$ on a compact positive interval prove the upper bound. Interchanging the alternatives treats negative $s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Entropy of a stopped Bernoulli design]
\label{lem:v7-stopped-kl}
If the design remains in the preceding all-history collar and $\E_sT<\infty$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-stopped-kl}
 D_{\rm KL}(\mathsf P_s\Vert\mathsf P_{-s})
                         \le C_JH^2h^2s^6\E_sT.
\end{equation}
For predetermined queries with offsets $d_i$, the sharper bound is $C_Js^6\sum_i d_i^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For Bernoulli probabilities $p,q\in(0,1)$, $\log x\le x-1$ gives
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(\operatorname{Ber}(p)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(q))
 \le\frac{(p-q)^2}{q(1-q)}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:v7-cubic-count} therefore bounds the one-query divergence by $C_Jd^2s^6$. Conditional on a common past history and the same randomization, the strategy chooses the same query in the two alternatives. Their true gap is the same, so its actual offset is the same as well. The chain rule for relative entropy bounds the divergence of the first $M$ query positions, padded with a stop symbol after termination, by
\[
            C_JH^2h^2s^6\E_s(T\wedge M).
\]
For a randomized strategy, condition first on its parameter-independent random seed and integrate; its own law contributes zero divergence. The increasing finite transcript sigma-fields generate the complete stopped transcript. Relative entropy on that transcript is the supremum of its finite-coordinate relative entropies. One may verify this identity from the variational formula for relative entropy, first for bounded cylinder functions and then by bounded conditional approximation. Sending $M\to\infty$ and using monotone convergence proves \eqref{eq:v7-stopped-kl}. Appending any estimator that is a measurable function of the transcript adds no divergence. Additivity proves the predetermined-query statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[High-confidence lower bound for the specified design]
\label{thm:v7-count-lower}
There are $\varepsilon_0,h_0,c>0$, depending only on the fixed neighborhood, $J$, and $H$, with the following property. Let $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$, $0<h\le h_0$, and $0<\eta<1/4$. Suppose a stopped Bernoulli design as above produces a measurable curvature estimate satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-uniform-confidence}
 \inf_{\theta\in K}
 \Pr_\theta\{\operatorname{dist}_{\rm match}(\widehat\kappa,\kappa(\theta))
                                      \le\varepsilon\}\ge1-\eta.
\end{equation}
Then its worst-case expected number of preparations obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-high-confidence-lower}
                  \sup_{\theta\in K}\E_\theta T
                  \ge c h^{-2}\varepsilon^{-6}\log(1/\eta).
\end{equation}
The conclusion remains true if the common exact gap is supplied to the strategy. For nonadaptive queries the analogous necessary information budget is $\sum_i d_i^2\ge c\varepsilon^{-6}\log(1/\eta)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $s=L\varepsilon$ with a sufficiently large fixed $L$ that the separation in \eqref{eq:v7-curvature-separation} exceeds $2\varepsilon$, and then decrease $\varepsilon_0$ so both alternatives remain in $K$. Decide between them by choosing the multiset closer to $\widehat\kappa$, breaking ties deterministically. The matching distance is a metric on unordered triples; thus \eqref{eq:v7-uniform-confidence} makes both testing errors at most $\eta$. If $B$ denotes the decision in favor of $s$, then $\mathsf P_s(B)\ge1-\eta$ and $\mathsf P_{-s}(B)\le\eta$.

Data processing to $\one_B$, followed by monotonicity of binary relative entropy on this rectangle, gives
\begin{align*}
 D_{\rm KL}(\mathsf P_s\Vert\mathsf P_{-s})
 &\ge (1-2\eta)\log\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}\\
 &\ge \tfrac14\log(1/\eta),\qquad 0<\eta<1/4.
\end{align*}
For the last bound, use $1-2\eta\ge1/2$ and $1-\eta\ge\sqrt\eta$ on this interval. Combine with Lemma~\ref{lem:v7-stopped-kl} and $s^6=L^6\varepsilon^6$. If the worst-case expected stopping time is infinite there is nothing to prove; otherwise the entropy bound applies. This proves \eqref{eq:v7-high-confidence-lower} and, with the corresponding entropy budget, its nonadaptive form. Both alternatives have $g=1/2$, so revealing the exact gap does not change the argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Matching order at each fixed extrapolation order]
\label{cor:v7-design-optimal}
Fix $m\ge1$. In the local physical three-amplitude family, take the supplied bracket $|g-g_0|\le h/4$ and $h=c_m\varepsilon^{3/m}$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-count-only}. There is an $H_m<\infty$ for which that theorem's pilot and second stage belong, on every history, to the bounded-flight Bernoulli design with offsets at most $H_mh$. Within this design the optimal worst-case expected preparation order for confidence $1-\eta$, $0<\eta<1/4$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v7-matching-order}
            \varepsilon^{-(6+6/m)}\log(1/\eta),
\end{equation}
up to constants depending on fixed $m$ and the compact family. The upper bound also supplies its previously stated high-probability preparation bound.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The pilot offsets are in $[3h/4,(m+5/4)h]$. With $L=J+1$, every second-stage offset lies in $[(L-3J/4)h,(Lm+3J/4)h]$, even after a failed pilot. Choose $H_m$ at least the two upper constants; for small $h$ the common collar is respected. The procedure keeps only Bernoulli success indicators and uses fresh independent preparations, so it belongs to the stated class. Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-count-only} gives the upper order, and Theorem~\ref{thm:v7-count-lower} on the $R=1/4$ subfamily gives the lower order. For fixed $m$ and $\eta<1/4$, $\log(C_m/\eta)$ and $\log(1/\eta)$ are comparable. Substitution of the prescribed $h$ proves \eqref{eq:v7-matching-order}.
\end{proof}

The same physical subfamily is an obstruction to any larger admissible Bernoulli model that contains it, including an ambient four-amplitude model. The theorem does not assert a lower bound for procedures that retain the complete lower-count values, positions, nonshrinking offsets, or unbounded flight numbers. It also does not allow $m$ to increase with accuracy while ignoring its constants. The exact deterministic one-third exponent and the statistical cost therefore remain distinct statements, now connected by a finite-offset experiment and a confidence-dependent testing argument. The constant-confidence precursor of the lower bound is attributed to \citet{RefereeV6}; the entropy argument here records the additional confidence and stopping quantifiers explicitly.
