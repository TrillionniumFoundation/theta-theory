\begin{abstract}
We study identification and statistical approximation from near-threshold collision records in periodic dispersing billiards. A uniform relative two-boundary law controls the nonlinear stationary action and the exponentially small physical flux without symmetry or finite-horizon assumptions. It yields total-variation comparisons for growing experiments, with unsuccessful preparations included. For identical even contacts, we establish a sharp positive-offset transition governed by the number of successful records times the exponentially small endpoint coupling. We also prove a confidence-dependent acquisition lower bound that matches a calibrated count-only estimator within a specified shrinking-offset Bernoulli design. Finite-jet inverses remain uniform in flight number at each fixed order; unlabelled curvature reconstruction includes coalescence and a physical three-amplitude inverse. The analysis distinguishes information in counts from information in selected positions and accounts explicitly for timing calibration and rare-event acquisition.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Statistical experiments; rare events; identification; dispersing billiards; relative determinants; singular inverse problems.\par
