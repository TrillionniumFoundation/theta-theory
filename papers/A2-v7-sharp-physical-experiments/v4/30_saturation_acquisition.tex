\section{Leading-data factorization and quantitative acquisition}
\label{sec:v4-acquisition}
\subsection{What increasing collision order does not add at leading order}
For one selected channel write
$A_j=\lim_{d\downarrow0}d^{-2}\Pr(E_{j,e}(d))$; this uppercase
subscripted symbol is distinct from the free area $A$.

\begin{proposition}[One-flight factorization of the leading data]
\label{prop:v4-saturation}
At known gap $g$, the entire leading selected-channel probability and
endpoint-metric hierarchy factors through
$(A_1,v_{1,0},v_{1,1})$. More explicitly, the two one-flight variances
determine $\gamma,c_0,c_1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-saturation}
 A_j=A_1\frac{\sinh\gamma}{\sinh(j\gamma)},\qquad
 v_{j,b}=v_{1,b}\frac{\coth(j\gamma)}{\coth\gamma}
       \quad(j\text{ odd},\ b=0,1).
\end{equation}
The full endpoint matrix at either parity is then given by
\eqref{eq:v3-inverse-hessian}. Also
$A=[2A_1\sinh\gamma]^{-1}$, when the normalizer is unknown.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The one-flight case of Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-tomography} recovers both
curvatures. Divide \eqref{eq:g-competition} at zero offset by its
one-flight version and do the same in
\eqref{eq:v3-variance-limits}. These are precisely
\eqref{eq:v4-saturation}. The displayed Hessian formula covers the
even orders as well, including their off-diagonal entries. Solving
the first amplitude for $A$ proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

Thus access to long odd records preserves access to the same leading
contact parameters; it does not produce new leading inverse coordinates.
The collision-order-uniform nonlinear calculus supplies something
else: the relative boundary factorization, fixed-offset limiting law
and higher-jet separation of Sections~\ref{sec:v4-boundary} and
\ref{sec:v4-nonlinear}. Its all-order smooth remainder also permits
the following controlled bias cancellation. The latter uses classical
polynomial extrapolation, rather than a new interpolation algorithm.

\subsection{Finite-order bias cancellation}
Fix an integer $m\ge1$. All constants with subscript $m$ below may
depend on this fixed order; no uniform assertion as $m\to\infty$ is
made. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-richardson}
 \omega_l=(-1)^{l-1}\binom ml,\qquad d_l=lh,
                     \quad 1\le l\le m,
\end{equation}
where $mh<\varepsilon_0$. Each observation uses the same selected
channel at the different physical window $jg+d_l$, with $j$ odd.
The true gap and the indicated offsets are assumed known unless
otherwise stated. At each window use $2K$ successful records from
independent full-phase preparations, independently across the windows,
and form the paired-position estimators
$\widehat V_{l,b}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:v3-sampling}.

\begin{theorem}[Extrapolated recovery and preparation cost]
\label{thm:v4-acquisition}
Set $\widehat v_b^{[m]}=\sum_{l=1}^m\omega_l\widehat V_{l,b}$.
There is a reconstruction on the compact gap--curvature boxes of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-tomography} such that, with probability at least
$1-\eta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-extrap-error}
 \max_b|\widehat\kappa_b-\kappa_b|
 \le C_m\left(h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log(8m/\eta)}K}\right).
\end{equation}
The expected total number $T$ of independent unconditioned
preparations is comparable, with constants depending on $m$, to
$Kh^{-2}e^{j\gamma}$. In addition, the reconstruction and the bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-highprob-cost}
 T\le C_m e^{j\gamma}h^{-2}[K+\log(2m/\eta)]
\end{equation}
hold together with probability at least $1-\eta$, after adjusting
constants in \eqref{eq:v4-extrap-error}. For each sufficiently small
accuracy $\epsilon>0$, this yields the sufficient expected and
high-probability acquisition order
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-accuracy-cost}
 C_m e^{j\gamma}\epsilon^{-(2+2/m)}\log(C_m/\eta)
\end{equation}
for curvature error at most $\epsilon$. The experiment counts
independent preparations, not independent successive impacts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The $m$th forward difference annihilates every polynomial of degree
less than $m$. Apply it at the integers $0,1,\ldots,m$ to obtain
\[
 \sum_{l=1}^m\omega_l=1,\qquad
 \sum_{l=1}^m\omega_l l^r=0\quad(1\le r<m).
\]
The smooth right extension of $V_{j,b}(d)$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:v3-metric} has uniformly bounded derivatives
through order $m$. Taylor's integral remainder at zero consequently
proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-extrap-bias}
 \left|\sum_{l=1}^m\omega_l V_{j,b}(lh)-v_{j,b}\right|
                                        \le C_mh^m.
\end{equation}
This step uses the uniform differentiated nonlinear theorem. A
pointwise $O(d)$ statement with a length-dependent constant would
not suffice for the stated uniform assertion.

Each paired summand at offset $lh$ lies in a common bounded interval,
by endpoint coercivity and its normalization by $2lh$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:v3-sampling} and a union bound over the $m$
windows give simultaneous errors
$C\sqrt{\log(8m/\eta)/K}$ at both endpoints with failure probability
at most $\eta/2$. Multiplication by
$\sum_l|\omega_l|=2^m-1$, together with
\eqref{eq:v4-extrap-bias}, gives the same form for the leading
variance estimate. Projection onto the compact image of the leading
observation map, followed by its uniform Lipschitz inverse, proves
\eqref{eq:v4-extrap-error}. It also defines the estimator when the
extrapolated values do not belong to the exact image.

For the cost, put $p_l=\Pr(E_{j,e}(lh))$. The uniform threshold law
and a smaller collar give
\[
 c(lh)^2e^{-j\gamma}\le p_l\le C(lh)^2e^{-j\gamma}.
\]
Waiting for $2K$ independent successes has mean $2K/p_l$. Summing
over the fixed number of windows proves the expected-cost assertion.
For a tail estimate let $T_l$ be that waiting time. Then
$\Pr(T_l>N)=\Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(N,p_l)<2K)$.
If $Np_l\ge4K$, the exponential Markov inequality for the binomial
law gives
\[
 \Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(N,p_l)<Np_l/2)\le e^{-Np_l/8}.
\]
For example this follows by multiplying the Bernoulli moment
generating functions and optimizing a negative parameter; the bound
$\Pr(X\le(1-t)\E X)\le\exp(-t^2\E X/2)$ for $0<t<1$
follows from $1+x\le e^x$ and
$t+(1-t)\log(1-t)\ge t^2/2$.
Choose $N$ with
$Np_l\ge\max(4K,8\log(2m/\eta))$.
The preceding lower bound on $p_l$ and a union bound show that
\eqref{eq:v4-highprob-cost} fails with probability at most $\eta/2$.
Combining with the estimation event proves the simultaneous assertion;
no independence between those two events is needed.

Finally take $h=c_m\epsilon^{1/m}$, with a sufficiently small fixed
$c_m$, and $K$ at least $C_m\epsilon^{-2}\log(C_m/\eta)$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:v4-extrap-error} is then at most $\epsilon$, and
both cost bounds have order \eqref{eq:v4-accuracy-cost}.
\end{proof}

At $m=1$ the sufficient cost is
$e^{j\gamma}\epsilon^{-4}\log(C/\eta)$, the explicit
accuracy--cost interpretation of the original paired estimator.
At $m=2$ it is $e^{j\gamma}\epsilon^{-3}\log(C_2/\eta)$.
These are guarantees for the specified procedures, not minimax lower
bounds or a statistical advantage of high collision order. The
exponential preparation factor persists, and the extrapolation
constants and required boundary differentiability grow with $m$.
There is no independent sensor-noise model hidden in the estimates.

\subsection{Gap calibration and the physical window}
\begin{proposition}[Propagation of a timing error]
\label{prop:v4-timing}
Suppose the programmed windows are $j\widehat g+lh$, whereas the
true gap is $g=\widehat g+\Delta g$. Assume
$|j\Delta g|\le h/2$ and $mh+|j\Delta g|<\varepsilon_0$.
Use the programmed offset $lh$ in each paired-position denominator.
The reconstruction based on the supplied gap $\widehat g$ then has
error, with the same independent-preparation confidence convention,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v4-timing-error}
 C_m\left[h^m+\sqrt{\frac{\log(8m/\eta)}K}
              +\frac{j|\Delta g|}h+|\Delta g|\right].
\end{equation}
A sufficient calibration for preserving an $O(h^m)$ deterministic
bias is $|\Delta g|\le c_mh^{m+1}/j$. In particular the original
$m=1$ procedure requires an $O(h^2/j)$ gap accuracy by this direct
normalization rule.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The actual offset is $d_l=lh-j\Delta g>0$. The expectation of the
paired statistic divided by $2lh$ is
\[
 \frac{d_l}{lh}V_{j,b}(d_l).
\]
Uniform boundedness and a bounded offset derivative imply that its
difference from $V_{j,b}(lh)$ is at most
$C[j|\Delta g|/(lh)+j|\Delta g|]$. Since $h$ is bounded above
on the chosen collar, the first term absorbs the second after changing
$C_m$. Sum with the fixed weights in \eqref{eq:v4-richardson} and
use \eqref{eq:v4-extrap-bias}. The random summands remain uniformly
bounded because $d_l/(lh)$ lies between $1/2$ and $3/2$.
The concentration proof is unchanged. The joint gap--variance inverse
bound \eqref{eq:v3-bilip} adds $C|\Delta g|$ for using the supplied
gap. This proves \eqref{eq:v4-timing-error} and its stated sufficient
calibration. Thus a gap error is not treated as a cost-free shift of
the physical observation window.
\end{proof}
