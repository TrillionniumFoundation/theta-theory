\begin{abstract}
We prove relative two-boundary laws for near-threshold collision records
in smooth periodic dispersing billiards, without symmetry or finite
horizon assumptions. A nonlinear stationary bridge admits a fixed
endpoint neighborhood, uniform in its length, on which the action and
the normalized flux separate exponentially into half-line boundary
contributions. Full-phase integration gives a nonlinear limiting law
with uniform differentiated control down to zero offset. We strengthen
the endpoint and residual-time comparison to total variation and transfer
it to growing independent experiments while retaining their rare-event
preparation cost. For identical even contacts, every finite-flight
nonlinear germ determines the contact, and finite-jet inverses are
uniform in flight number at each fixed jet order. Analytic realizations
preserve the full leading hierarchy of a selected horizontal ground
channel while varying independent higher statistical coordinates. For
three unlabelled equal-gap channels with equal facing curvatures within
each channel, four leading amplitudes recover an independently unknown
area and the curvature triple with sharp pairwise exponent $1/3$.
Three amplitudes suffice locally in a constrained physical family.
Finally, a count-only experiment combines fine onset calibration and
amplitude acquisition to give a sufficient curvature-accuracy cost
$C_m\varepsilon^{-(6+6/m)}\log(C_m/\eta)$, starting from a coarse local
bracket. Conditional-position acquisition remains a separate observation
model.
\end{abstract}
