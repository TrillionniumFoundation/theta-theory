\section{Uniform stability of optional projections}
\label{sec:v17-optional}

Marked comparison controls more than terminal bounded risk when the
compared laws share their latent variable. We prove a whole-process
estimate for the resulting posterior martingales. The distinction
between a probabilistic coupling and an executable causal channel is
important in this section.

Let $Z$ take values in a standard Borel space and let $Y$ take values
in a standard Borel canonical path space on $[0,T]$, with a
right-continuous observation filtration. Consider equivalent joint
probability measures $P,Q$ on $(Z,Y)$, with the same $Z$-marginal
$\nu$. Use the usual completions, which agree for equivalent output
laws. For a bounded measurable latent variable $f(Z)$ write
\[
 M^P_t=\E_P[f(Z)\mid\mathcal F^Y_t],\qquad
 M^Q_t=\E_Q[f(Z)\mid\mathcal F^Y_t].
\]
Take their right-continuous martingale versions. All suprema below
include $T$ and can be evaluated on a countable dense time set.

\begin{theorem}[Marked variation controls the whole posterior process]
\label{thm:v17-optional}
Let $\varepsilon=\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}$ and $|f|\le1$. When both
posterior functions are evaluated on the same canonical output path,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-samepath}
 \E_Q\sup_{t\le T}|M^P_t(Y)-M^Q_t(Y)|^2\le80\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
There is a coupling of $(Z,Y^P)$ and $(Z,Y^Q)$ which retains exactly
the same latent variable and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-optionalcoupling}
 \Pp(Y^P\ne Y^Q)\le\varepsilon,\qquad
 \E\sup_{t\le T}|M^P_t(Y^P)-M^Q_t(Y^Q)|^2
                                      \le84\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
The coupling is not asserted to be causal or to have finite state.
The variation bound itself, not this coupling, is used for resource
transport.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $D=dP/dQ$, $D_t=\E_Q[D\mid\mathcal F^Y_t]$, and set
\[
 A_t=\E_Q[(D-1)f\mid\mathcal F^Y_t],\qquad B_t=D_t-1.
\]
Bayes' formula gives
\[
 M^P_t-M^Q_t=\frac{A_t-B_tM^Q_t}{1+B_t}.
\]
Since $\E_Q|D-1|=2\varepsilon$, both martingales $A,B$ have
terminal absolute expectation at most $2\varepsilon$. Doob's weak
$L^1$ inequality gives, for $0<u\le2$,
\begin{align*}
 Q\{\sup_t|M^P_t-M^Q_t|>u\}
 &\le Q\{\sup_t|B_t|>1/2\}
       +Q\{\sup_t|A_t|>u/4\}+Q\{\sup_t|B_t|>u/4\}\\
 &\le4\varepsilon+16\varepsilon/u.
\end{align*}
Integrating $2u$ times this bound on $(0,2)$ gives
$16\varepsilon+64\varepsilon=80\varepsilon$, proving
\eqref{eq:v17-samepath}. No tightness conclusion is being inferred
from finite-dimensional convergence.

Disintegrate both joint laws over their common latent marginal.
Measurable maximal coupling of the two output kernels, conditional on
$Z$, has disagreement probability
\[
 \int\|P(dy\mid z)-Q(dy\mid z)\|_{\rm TV}\,d\nu(z)
 =\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}.
\]
Such a coupling can be constructed by using the minimum of the two
kernel densities with respect to their sum, then their residual
kernels. It is measurable on standard Borel spaces. On the event of
identical full output paths use \eqref{eq:v17-samepath} with the
$Q$-marginal; on its complement the squared difference is at most
four. This proves \eqref{eq:v17-optionalcoupling}. The maximal coupling
may depend on the full output path; it has no asserted online
implementation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Changing-filtration convergence from marked comparison]
\label{cor:v17-changing}
Let $P_n$ and $P$ be equivalent joint laws with the same latent
marginal $\nu$, and $\|P_n-P\|_{\rm TV}\to0$. They admit a common
probability-space realization $(Z,Y,Y^1,Y^2,\ldots)$ such that, for
every $f\in L^2(\nu)$,
\[
 \E\sup_{t\le T}
 \left|\E[f(Z)\mid\mathcal F^{Y^n}_t]
             -\E[f(Z)\mid\mathcal F^Y_t]\right|^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
The same realization gives convergence uniformly in time in
probability for $f\in L^1(\nu)$. It also permits $f_n\to f$ in
$L^2(\nu)$, respectively in $L^1(\nu)$, in the first conditional
expectation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Glue the conditional maximal couplings of $(Z,Y^n)$ with the fixed
reference $(Z,Y)$ by taking their conditional kernels independently
on a countable product. The coupling does not depend on $f$.
For the truncation $f^{(K)}=(-K)\vee(f\wedge K)$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v17-optional} bounds the middle difference in the
$L^2$ norm of the supremum by $K\sqrt{84\varepsilon_n}$.
Doob's $L^2$ inequality bounds each truncation tail by
$2\|f-f^{(K)}\|_{L^2(\nu)}$. The triangle inequality therefore gives
\[
 \left\|\sup_t|M^{P_n,f}_t-M^{P,f}_t|\right\|_2
 \le4\|f-f^{(K)}\|_2+K\sqrt{84\varepsilon_n}.
\]
First let $n\to\infty$, then $K\to\infty$. For $L^1$ use instead
the weak maximal inequality for each tail and the bounded-case
estimate for the middle term. An additional difference $f_n-f$ is
handled by the same maximal inequalities under $P_n$, whose latent
marginal remains $\nu$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A microscopic sufficient condition}
Let $(\Phi_t)_{t\in\R}$ be an almost-everywhere regular hard-sphere
flow on a labeled phase space, with invariant probability $\mu$.
Fix a prepared law $\nu$ with $d\nu/d\mu\le C$, and bounded real
observables $h,h_n$. These may be collision-sensitive tagged
observables. Put $\eta_n=\|h_n-h\|_{L^2(\mu)}$. With a common latent
initial state $Z\sim\nu$, consider the continuous observation laws
\begin{align*}
 Y_t&=\int_0^t h(\Phi_s Z)\,ds+\sigma B_t,\\
 Y^n_t&=\int_0^t h_n(\Phi_s Z)\,ds+\sigma B^n_t,
 \qquad \sigma>0,
\end{align*}
where the Brownian noise is independent of $Z$ in each model. The
notation does not prescribe a synchronous coupling.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-flow observation-approximation limit]
\label{thm:v17-physicaloptional}
The joint latent-output laws are equivalent and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-observationtv}
 \|P_n-P\|_{\rm TV}\le\frac{\sqrt{CT}}{2\sigma}\eta_n.
\end{equation}
For every $|f(Z)|\le1$ they admit a latent-preserving coupling with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-physicaloptional}
 \E\sup_{t\le T}|M^{P_n,f}_t-M^{P,f}_t|^2
                       \le\frac{42\sqrt{CT}}{\sigma}\eta_n.
\end{equation}
In particular $\eta_n\to0$ supplies whole-process optional-projection
convergence, not merely convergence at finitely many times. This
applies when $h_n$ are orbit-mollified graph-core approximants to any
bounded $h\in L^2(\mu)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on $Z$, the signals are deterministic bounded drifts.
The exponential change-of-measure identity for Brownian motion gives
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(P_n\|P)
 =\frac1{2\sigma^2}\E_\nu\int_0^T
                 |(h_n-h)(\Phi_s Z)|^2\,ds
 \le\frac{CT}{2\sigma^2}\eta_n^2.
\]
The last inequality uses $\nu\le C\mu$ and invariance of $\mu$;
it does not require mixing, differentiability across a collision,
or a claim that a tagged velocity belongs to the generator domain.
Bounded drifts ensure equivalence and justify the exponential
identity. Pinsker's inequality gives
\eqref{eq:v17-observationtv}. Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:v17-optional} proves
\eqref{eq:v17-physicaloptional}.

For the last assertion choose a nonnegative smooth approximate
identity $\rho_n$ on the time line and set
$h_n=\int\rho_n(s)h\circ\Phi_s\,ds$.
Strong continuity of the Koopman group in $L^2(\mu)$ gives
$\eta_n\to0$ and $\|h_n\|_\infty\le\|h\|_\infty$.
Repeated integration by parts puts $h_n$ in every power domain of
the generator, with derivatives obtained from $\rho_n^{(k)}$.
This is the orbit-core construction already used in the microscopic
domain theory; the new conclusion is the uniform posterior estimate.
\end{proof}

Sampling these observation paths at finitely many checkpoints and
applying a common causal output postprocessing are contractions in
marked variation. Thus the identity report comparison transfers every
bounded finite-register risk at its original state and selector
budgets with error bounded by \eqref{eq:v17-observationtv}. That is the
resource-comparison consequence. The latent-preserving coupling in
\eqref{eq:v17-physicaloptional} has a different role: it realizes
convergence of the changing observation filtrations and is not used
as a finite-register simulator.

The theorem discharges a sufficient conditional-law hypothesis for
optional projection in a genuine microscopic observation class.
The constants depend on the fixed preparation, time and noise level.
No particle-uniform kinetic estimate, changing microscopic path
limit, limiting stochastic-exponential identification or nonlinear
BSDE limit is implied without its additional hypotheses. The
whole-process estimate is what replaces an unsupported appeal to
Doob's inequality as a tightness argument.
