\section{Confidence tests with priced finite-state amplification}
\label{sec:v22-confidence}
The signed-sum construction in Section~\ref{sec:v21-confidence}
converts expectation into confidence. There is also a direct
sample--memory choice for the amplifier itself. Its formulation keeps
the binary testing objective distinct from the expected-score task.

\begin{theorem}[Two finite-state confidence amplifiers]
\label{thm:v22-confidence}
Suppose a reset audit uses $b$ training trials, two validations, peak
width $W\ge3$, and reports $D\in\{-1,0,1\}$. Under the equality null
$\E D=0$, and under every alternative in a fixed class $\E D\ge\gamma>0$.
Fix a known dyadic $0<g\le\gamma$, and put $\theta=g/8$.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(i)} For integers $m,n\ge1$, there is a binary test with both
errors at most $e_{m,n}(\theta)$, using $n(b+2)$ preparations and peak
width at most $(2m+1)\max(W,9)$. Thus both errors are at most $\alpha$
with the choices \eqref{eq:v22-polynomial}, with $\delta=\alpha$.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(ii)} With $L,R$ as in \eqref{eq:v21-LR}, using
$\delta=\alpha$ and the same $\theta$, there is another test with both
errors at most $\alpha$, $2LR(b+2)$ preparations, and peak width at
most $4\max(W,9)$. Its very long external schedule is charged
separately. The confidence error, not the input parameter, determines
these read-only horizon constants.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Run independent copies of the base audit with fresh preparations and
fresh private bits. Conditional on each output $D$, generate an
additional coin $Z$ with probability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-confidence-coin}
 \Pp(Z=1\mid D)=\frac12+\frac{D-g/2}{4}.
\end{equation}
This is between zero and one, since $0<g\le\gamma\le1$ and
$-1\le D\le1$. If $g$ has denominator $2^s$, the probability has
denominator dividing $2^{s+3}$ and is sampled with $s+3$ fair bits.
The coins are independent under each fixed parameter. Their mean is
$1/2-g/8$ under the null and at least $1/2+g/8$ under the alternatives.
Apply the walk of Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-walk}, rejecting the null for a
positive result, to prove (i). Apply the block comparison of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-block} to prove (ii).

At every base-audit epoch retain the amplifier state alongside its $W$
labels. After obtaining $D$, retain that value and at most three
Bernoulli-sampler labels while drawing \eqref{eq:v22-confidence-coin}.
This requires at most nine labels per amplifier state. Update the
amplifier and reset the base audit before the next repetition. The
terminal rejection decision is a function of the amplifier state and
requires no additional persistent random seed. This proves the stated
full-cut peak bounds. Even after the amplifier is absorbed, padded
base-audit repetitions remain charged to the preparation count. The
clock supplying the repetition, base-audit epoch and fair-bit depth is
external; storing it multiplies the displayed bound by at most one
plus the total number of such epochs.
\end{proof}

For the physical expected-score theorem one may take $g=3/8$, since
$3/8<2/5$. The additional coin then needs at most six fair bits.
Thus confidence can be purchased either with a growing absorbing
counter and polynomial repetition count, or with a constant register
and a much longer clocked schedule. Neither statement identifies the
minimum state count of the binary testing problem with the three
conditional validation responses needed by the expected-score problem.
The signed-accumulator and train-once confidence theorems remain
available as different resource regimes.

The output-law transfer in Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-active} applies to a
completed binary test. In particular, if perturbations change its
output law by at most $\varepsilon$, its type-I and type-II bounds each
increase by at most $\varepsilon$. Transporting all repeated raw data
instead counts every affected preparation, as in
Section~\ref{sec:v21-confidence}. Independence of the base repetitions
and uniformity over the fixed alternative class are assumptions of the
amplification theorem; adaptation of the alternative between repetitions
is not being asserted.
