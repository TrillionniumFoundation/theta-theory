\section{Confidence-level certification}
\label{sec:v21-confidence}
An expected signed score is an operational separation, not a confidence
statement about one validation pair. We now distinguish these two tasks.
The null hypothesis below is equality of the candidate and target
validation laws; it need not belong to the private alternative family.
All preparations in the theorem are independent resets with one fixed
candidate law. The test cannot be given a new adversarial candidate after
each reset.

\begin{theorem}[Confidence lift with a charged accumulator]
\label{thm:v21-confidence}
Suppose a base reset audit uses $n$ training trials, one candidate and
one target validation, and peak width $W$, with score $D\in\{-1,0,1\}$.
Assume its expected score is at least $\gamma>0$ for every alternative.
Run $J$ independent copies and reject equality when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-test}
 \sum_{j=1}^J D_j\ge J\gamma/2.
\end{equation}
Then both the type-I error under equality and the worst-case type-II
error are at most $e^{-J\gamma^2/8}$. The whole test has a realization
with at most $W(2J+1)$ states and $J(n+2)$ trials. In particular
$J\ge8\gamma^{-2}\log(1/\alpha)$ suffices for both errors to be at
most $\alpha$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Under equality, conditional on the training and selected event, the two
independent validations have equal means; hence $\E D=0$. Under each
fixed alternative its mean is at least $\gamma$. The copies are
independent and $D_j\in[-1,1]$. The exponential-moment estimate for a
variable in an interval of length two is
$\E e^{s(D_j-\E D_j)}\le e^{s^2/2}$, by the same tilted-variance
argument used in Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-spectrum}. Markov's inequality
and minimization in $s$ give
$\Pr(J^{-1}\sum(D_j-\E D_j)\ge a)\le e^{-Ja^2/2}$, and the same
lower-tail bound. Set $a=\gamma/2$.

Keep the running integer sum, with at most $2J+1$ values, beside the
base machine's register. At each completion add its emitted score and
reset only the base register. The sum is not stored in an external
history. The clock supplies the repetition index and the fixed terminal
threshold; all $J(n+2)$ trials are counted.
\end{proof}

The $399$-trial physical audit has mean strictly above $2/5$, so one may
use the rational choice $\gamma=2/5$ in this theorem. This gives the
fully explicit sufficient count $J\ge50\log(1/\alpha)$ and
$401J$ total trials (or $402J$ with the inherited ignored slot). The
confidence guarantee uses repetition and its charged sum, not one pair
of validation observations.

\begin{theorem}[One training phase and repeated validation]
\label{thm:v21-fixedwitness}
For the physical audit of Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-physical}, put
$\gamma_0=\sqrt2-1-1/300-\theta>0$. Retain its chosen event and use
$m$ independent candidate--target validation pairs. Reject equality when
their score sum is at least $m\gamma_0/2$. The type-I error is at most
$e^{-m\gamma_0^2/8}$ and the worst-case type-II error is at most
\[
 2\delta+e^{-m\gamma_0^2/8}.
\]
The audit uses $4LR+2m$ trials and at most $12(2m+1)$ states throughout
training and validation. Thus $\delta\le\alpha/4$ and
$m\ge8\gamma_0^{-2}\log(2/\alpha)$ suffice for error at most $\alpha$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-physical} gives an event of training
probability at least $1-2\delta$ on which the conditional mean score of
the selected event is strictly greater than $\gamma_0$. Conditional on
that event and its selected label, the validation pairs are independent.
Apply the tail bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-confidence} conditionally
and add the training failure probability. Under equality every selected
event has zero mean, regardless of training, so no training failure term
is needed for type-I error. Keep the three-valued event label, the
within-pair indicator and first-bit buffer, and a sum with $2m+1$
values. Training itself needs no more than ten states, so the displayed
bound covers every phase.
\end{proof}

The tests in this section have a different output task from maximizing
an expected witness score. The exact three-state decision-cut theorem is
not asserted to be an optimal memory theorem for these binary tests.
For perturbations of training and validation laws, the coupling argument
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-transfer} bounds changes of each rejection
probability by the sum of the per-call total variation errors. In a
source-preserving physical transport, alternatively lower the conditional
mean margin by the single-row target variation and reapply the conditional
tail estimate. This latter argument transports the confidence guarantee
without pretending that an entire $m$-sample validation law has the
same total variation distance as one validation observation.
