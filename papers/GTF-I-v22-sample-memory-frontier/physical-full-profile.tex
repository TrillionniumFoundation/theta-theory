\section{Full-profile certification of the collision family}
\label{sec:v21-physical}
The short audit of Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-physical} is retained with its
exact continuation profile. We now give a second audit of the same
physical class whose entire acquisition-and-validation register is
bounded independently of its accuracy parameters. Its cost is a much
longer, explicitly counted, preparation schedule.

\subsection{The row-indexed decision invariant}
For the original row--event interface define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-kapparows}
 \kappa_c^{\rm rows}(B,Q)=
 \min\{K:\mathfrak V_K^{\rm rows}(B,Q)>c\}.
\end{equation}
The minimum is $+\infty$ when the set is empty. This is not an implicit
change of the one-row symbol $\kappa_c$. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:v20-rows}, it is the exact minimum decision-cut
alphabet for a strict score, allowing a finite training schedule and a
post-cut distribution on row--event pairs.

Let $W_c^{\rm phys}$ be the minimum peak width among all finite auditors
for the original physical row interface, now charging each report-bit,
mark, decision and validation epoch. Its clock is externally scheduled,
as in the earlier physical implementation. All persistent random
selectors are charged.

\begin{theorem}[A twelve-state physical audit]\label{thm:v21-physical}
In the physical parameter range of Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-physical}, put
$\beta=1/300$. For $0<\theta<1/4$ and $0<\delta<1$, let $L,R$ be at least the respective quantities in
\eqref{eq:v21-LR}. There is a deterministic audit with $N=4LR$ candidate
training trials and two validation trials whose expected score is
strictly greater than
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-smallscore}
 \sqrt2-1-\beta-\theta-2\delta.
\end{equation}
It has at most five states at completed training-trial cuts, ten at
intermediate training report-bit cuts, three at the decision cut, and
twelve throughout validation. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-physicalwidth}
 \kappa_{2/5}^{\rm rows}=3,
 \qquad 3\le W_{2/5}^{\rm phys}\le12.
\end{equation}
One entirely integer schedule takes
$\theta=1/200$, $\delta=1/1000$, $L=400$ and $R=8\cdot2^{400}$,
so $N=12800\cdot2^{400}$. Its lower bound exceeds $2/5$.
The upper bound twelve is not asserted to be the exact minimum integer
peak width.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the report-pair probabilities $a=(1-p)(1-q)$ and $c=pq$ from
Section~\ref{sec:v20-physical}. In the first $2LR$ training trials run
Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-block} on the indicator of the pair $00$. If it
returns positive, choose $A_1$ and ignore the remaining training trials.
Otherwise, in the next $2LR$ trials run the same comparison on the
indicator of $11$, choosing $A_0$ if positive and $A_D$ if negative.
Both phases have fixed lengths, even after an absorbing decision.

A comparison whose parameter is outside its indifference band is
correct with probability at least $1-\delta$. For purposes of analysis,
one may define the second comparison on its independent observations
even when the implementation ignores them. A union bound gives
probability at least $1-2\delta$ that neither comparison errs outside
its band. Since $\min(a,c)\le1/4$ and $\theta<1/4$, at most one of
$a,c$ lies in $[1/2-\theta,1/2+\theta]$.

On this good event the chosen payoff is within $\theta$ of
\[
 E(a,c)=\max\{1-a-c,1/2-a,1/2-c\}.
\]
Indeed, if one coordinate exceeds $1/2+\theta$, its associated event
is selected and is optimal. If both are below $1/2-\theta$, $A_D$ is
selected and is optimal. In the remaining case the low coordinate's
comparison is negative, and choosing either of the two relevant events
costs at most the distance of the near coordinate from $1/2$.
The range of the three payoffs at any fixed $(a,c)$ is at most one.
The expected ideal score is therefore at least
$E(a,c)-\theta-2\delta$. Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-envelope} gives
$E(a,c)\ge\sqrt2-1$. Changing the target to the physical target loses
strictly less than $\beta$, proving \eqref{eq:v21-smallscore}.

The first comparator has four states. The second phase has these four
states and one extra absorbing label for a previously chosen $A_1$,
hence five. Retaining the first report bit doubles that count to ten.
On the second bit, determine the relevant indicator, update the block
state, and discard both bits. The training mark is read and discarded.
At the phase boundary the fixed schedule maps the first decision to
either the $A_1$ label or the initial state of the second comparator.
At the end it maps the remaining decisions to $A_0$ or $A_D$.
During validation keep the chosen event, the candidate-event indicator,
and a first-bit buffer when needed. This requires at most
$3\cdot2\cdot2=12$ states; all events ignore the mark. These are actual
phase-compatible transitions, not a bound only on terminal labels.

For the integer schedule, the error estimate in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-block} is $2e^{-8}<1/1000$.
To check it rationally, $e>8/3$ and $(8/3)^8>2000$. Further
$\sqrt2>707/500$, and hence
\[
 \sqrt2-1-\frac1{300}-\frac1{200}-\frac1{500}-\frac25
 >\frac{11}{3000}>0.
\]
This proves the strict score claim without numerical optimization.
The training count is exactly $4LR$; early absorption does not delete
trials from that count.

For the lower bounds in \eqref{eq:v21-physicalwidth}, every full-profile
auditor has its decision cut among its charged cuts. The feedback
pushforward subfamily of Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-feedback} bounds any
width-two decision continuation by $3/8+\beta<2/5$, for every finite
adaptive training schedule. The all-on construction just given is a
member of the original row interface and has three decision labels.
This proves the row-indexed equality and both peak inequalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two physical resource regimes]\label{cor:v21-regimes}
The same physical score threshold $2/5$ admits the two schedules
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{training trials}&\text{decision width}&\text{peak width}\\\hline
 399&3&40602\\
 12800\cdot2^{400}&3&12
 \end{array}
\]
followed by two validations. The first schedule may be padded to $400$
training trials as before. For either fixed machine, changing $d$ to
$d_k$ at fixed positive noise level changes its expected score by at
most $\epsilon_k$ in \eqref{eq:v20-transportscore}. If the candidate
training kernels also change, their additional cost is the training
term in Corollary~\ref{cor:v21-calibrated}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first line is Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-physical}; the second is
Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-physical}. In the moving-collision comparison the
candidate training source is identical. Conditional on its chosen event,
only one target validation mean changes. The common-preparation Gaussian
bound therefore contributes $\epsilon_k$, independently of the length
of the unchanged training. The general perturbation formula applies
when this equality of sources is absent.
\end{proof}

The long schedule is an existence bound on the resource frontier, not a
practical substitute for the $399$-trial rule. In an autonomous model the
clock must also be stored; Proposition~\ref{prop:v21-compose} gives its
explicit additional factor. Both schedules use deterministic transitions,
so no precision-dependent randomizer is hidden in the physical upper
bound. The one-collision construction remains the verified physical
realization. No many-particle or zero-noise theorem is inferred from the
larger class of finite experiments.
