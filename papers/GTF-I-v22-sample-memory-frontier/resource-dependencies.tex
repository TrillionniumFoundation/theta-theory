\section{Dependencies of the statistical resource frontier}
\label{sec:v22-dependencies}
The general preparation bound uses the decision spectrum only to
supply a finite response list and its strict margin. Dyadic event
realization supplies its executable randomizers. The absorbing-walk
lemma then supplies both a finite comparison error and the expected
number of active updates. Thus
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{decision cover and rational event sampler}\\
 \Downarrow\\
 \text{finite-horizon whole-schedule tournament}\\
 \Downarrow\\
 \text{physical resource regimes and finite-state confidence}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The occupation-weighted transfer theorem acts on the induced retained
kernels of each implemented machine. In the independent reset setting
it consumes the walk's active-time estimate. Its proof does not invoke
any claim about convergence of changing filtrations.

The exact revelation theorem is an independent, fully solved finite
statistical model inside the same profile-constrained decision layer.
Its necessary direction applies to every stochastic machine; its
sufficient direction constructs compatible nested labels. It is not
used as a physical collision lower bound. The lower bound three in
that family still comes from the original feedback-row obstruction.
Likewise, the complete finite-word residual profile of the short
physical selector is retained as an exact implementation theorem and
not relabeled as a statistical optimum.

These distinctions give an actual resource--precision--error consumer
for the foundation blueprint: preparation and state budgets come from
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-tournament}, calibration and rationalization from
its explicit coin construction, perturbation from
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-active}, and a confidence-level readout from
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-confidence}. The exact frontier provides a sharp
case of the same optimization with nonconstant alphabet and arbitrary
state profiles. None of these arrows presupposes the historical B4
kinetic range theorem, the broader C2 rigidity aggregate, or an
unproved dependency of the independent A2 chain.
