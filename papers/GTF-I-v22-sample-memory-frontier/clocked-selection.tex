\section{From a decision cover to an end-to-end machine}
\label{sec:v21-clocked}
The unrestricted empirical table in Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-spectrum} can
be replaced by a bounded-state training procedure. This replacement
trades preparation count against memory, rather than assuming that the
training computation has already been performed. The elementary block
mechanism belongs to the classical finite-statistics theory of
Cover~\cite{Cover1969}; the result below tracks a finite horizon, a
uniform indifference band, rational precision, the whole validation
phase, and a compact nonconvex candidate image.

\begin{lemma}[A finite-horizon four-state comparison]\label{lem:v21-block}
Let $Z_i$ be independent Bernoulli observations of unknown mean $u$.
For $0<\theta<1/2$ and $0<\delta<1$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-LR}
 L=\left\lceil\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{4\theta}\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 R=\left\lceil2^L\log(2/\delta)\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
There is a deterministic clocked machine with four states at every
observation cut, using exactly $2LR$ observations, that decides the sign
of $u-1/2$ with error at most $\delta$ whenever $|u-1/2|\ge\theta$.
No assertion is imposed within the indifference band.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Partition the observations into $R$ pairs of blocks, each block of length
$L$. A positive block succeeds when all its observations are one; the
following negative block succeeds when all are zero. Stop updating the
decision at the first successful block and retain its sign. If none
succeeds, decide negative at the end. The prescribed remaining
observations are still consumed, but ignored.

Two states are absorbing positive and negative decisions. The other two
mean undecided with the current block still alive or already failed.
At a block boundary the clock resets the undecided flag to alive. Its
polarity and position are supplied by the clock; they are not inferred
from an unrecorded sample. The last observation of each block either
latches the sign or starts the next block. Thus four states suffice even
at the block boundaries.

Write $x=u^L$, $y=(1-u)^L$ and $s=x+(1-x)y$. The probability of a
positive decision is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-hazard}
 H_{L,R}(u)=\frac{x}{s}\{1-(1-s)^R\}.
\end{equation}
Here $s>0$, including at $u=0,1$. For $u\ge1/2+\theta$, the error is
at most $y/x+(1-s)^R$. For $u\le1/2-\theta$, it is at most $x/y$;
the default negative decision is then correct. Since
\[
 \log\frac{1/2+\theta}{1/2-\theta}\ge4\theta,
 \qquad s\ge\max(x,y)\ge2^{-L},
\]
both errors are bounded by
$e^{-4\theta L}+e^{-R2^{-L}}\le\delta$.
The logarithmic inequality follows by differentiation from its equality
at zero. This also proves uniformity over the two composite hypotheses.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The whole-schedule continuation value}
In the one-row reset interface of Section~\ref{sec:v20-spectrum}, let
$W_c(B,Q)$ be the least peak width over all finite training lengths and
all auditors whose worst-case expected signed score is strictly greater
than $c$. Every training-observation cut, the decision cut, every fresh
random-bit cut, and both validation cuts are charged. Observations in
$\Omega$ are atomic for this definition. The event is selected before
the two validations, exactly as in that interface. The schedule clock
and read-only constants are recorded as separate resources. The class
includes randomized machines; its rational subclass uses only finite
fair-bit randomizers. Write $W_c^{\rm rat}$ for the corresponding
minimum in the latter class. Empty feasible sets have value $+\infty$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform end-to-end realization of a decision cover]
\label{thm:v21-endtoend}
Let $B\subset\Delta(\Omega)$ be nonempty and compact, $Q$ fixed, and
$d=|\Omega|$. For every strict threshold $c$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-equivalence}
 \kappa_c(B,Q)\ \le W_c(B,Q)\ \le W_c^{\rm rat}(B,Q)
              \ \le 12d\,\kappa_c(B,Q).
\end{equation}
The last inequality is interpreted only for finite $\kappa_c$; when
$\kappa_c=+\infty$ all three quantities are infinite.

More explicitly, fix $K\ge2$ and a response list with uniform envelope
at least $v$. Round its coordinates to denominator $2^r$, and choose
$q_i\in[0,1]\cap2^{-r}\mathbb Z$ with
$|q_i-Qh_i^{(r)}|\le\rho$. For $\theta,\delta$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-block}, a rational auditor uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-tournament-N}
 N=2LR(K-1)
\end{equation}
candidate training observations and two validation observations. Its
expected score is at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-tournament-score}
 v-2^{1-r}-2\rho-4(K-1)\theta-2(K-1)\delta.
\end{equation}
Its completed-training-observation cuts have width at most $4K$, its
decision cut has width $K$, and its entire random-bit-refined schedule
has width at most $12Kd$. No empirical histogram is constructed.
The response and comparison thresholds have a direct read-only
representation of size
$O(K^2d(r+3)+\log L+\log R+\log K)$ bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use a sequential tournament. Initially response $1$ is incumbent.
At stage $j=2,\ldots,K$, compare incumbent $i$ with challenger $j$ using
fresh candidate observations. For one observation $w\sim b$, generate
a Bernoulli variable with conditional success probability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-comparison-coin}
 \frac12+\frac{q_j-q_i-h_j^{(r)}(w)+h_i^{(r)}(w)}4.
\end{equation}
This lies in $[0,1]$ and is dyadic of denominator $2^{r+2}$.
Its unconditional mean is
$1/2+(\widetilde f_j(b)-\widetilde f_i(b))/4$, where
$\widetilde f_i(b)=q_i-bh_i^{(r)}$. Conditional on the incumbent at
the start of this stage, all these variables are independent and have
this mean. The fixed candidate $b$ is not changed by the comparison.
Run Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-block} and replace $i$ by $j$ exactly when its
decision is positive.

At each comparison, conditionally on all earlier stages, the probability
of choosing the inferior response when their $\widetilde f$ values
differ by at least $4\theta$ is at most $\delta$. With probability at
least $1-(K-1)\delta$, no such error occurs. On this event each stage
loses at most $4\theta$ relative to the better of its two responses.
Induction on $j$ gives
\[
 \widetilde f_{\widehat i}(b)\ge
           \max_i\widetilde f_i(b)-4(K-1)\theta.
\]
Replacing the calibrated means by their true means loses at most
$2\rho$. Rounding the original list loses at most $2^{1-r}$ in its
envelope, by Proposition~\ref{prop:v21-rational}. Any response payoff
belongs to $[-1,1]$, so failure of the good event loses at most
$2(K-1)\delta$ in expectation. This proves
\eqref{eq:v21-tournament-score}, uniformly over $b$.

During a comparison keep the incumbent index and one of the four block
states. The challenger and block positions are fixed by the schedule.
At the end of a comparison the sign updates the incumbent and is
forgotten. This gives the $4K$ bound at completed-observation cuts.
While drawing the coin in \eqref{eq:v21-comparison-coin}, keep this
register and the current observation, and use the three-label binary
sampler of Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-dyadic}. Thus at most $12Kd$ labels occur
at any intervening random-bit cut. At the final decision cut only the
incumbent remains. Proposition~\ref{prop:v21-rational} gives at most
$K(2d+1)$ labels during event selection and $2K(d+1)$ during validation;
both are bounded by $12Kd$. The three-valued terminal output fits this
bound as well. These are compatible transitions over the entire schedule.

The coin thresholds can be precomputed for all triples $(i,j,w)$ and
stored as $r+3$-bit integers. The event thresholds and $L,R,K$ give the
stated description bound; no arbitrary real coefficient is used by the
executed machine. Formula~\eqref{eq:v21-tournament-N} counts every
training observation, including ignored observations after a block
decision has been latched.

For \eqref{eq:v21-equivalence}, a width-$W$ auditor has at most $W$
labels at its decision cut. The operational upper bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-spectrum} implies $\kappa_c\le W$. If
$K=\kappa_c<\infty$, its optimal list has a positive margin over $c$.
Take $r$ large, $\rho\le2^{-r}$, and then $\theta,\delta$ small enough
that the loss in \eqref{eq:v21-tournament-score} is smaller than that
margin. If $K=1$, no training is needed: round the single response and
use Proposition~\ref{prop:v21-rational}. Rational auditors are a
subclass of all auditors, proving the remaining inequality. Finally a
finite-width successful machine would give finite $\kappa_c$, proving
the assertion at infinity.
\end{proof}

The dependence on the schedule is essential. The theorem is a
constant-factor classification for fixed observation alphabet, not an
optimal sample-complexity theorem. In particular $R$ is exponential in
$L$. For an autonomous implementation, a coarse bound on the number
of epochs is $T\le N(r+3)+r+4$; Proposition~\ref{prop:v21-compose}
then gives at most $12Kd(T+1)$ states. The free-clock and autonomous
statements are different resource bounds. Cover's theorem already
exhibits the importance of this distinction~\cite[Theorem 2 and
Section 4]{Cover1969}; bounded-state learning itself is not a novelty
claim of the present theorem.

\begin{corollary}[Finite-precision, calibrated certificates]
\label{cor:v21-calibrated}
Suppose the true training kernels differ from those used in an ideal
auditor by at most $\alpha$ per training call, and the candidate and
target validation laws differ by at most $\beta_{\rm c}$ and
$\beta_{\rm t}$. The right side of
\eqref{eq:v21-tournament-score} is decreased by at most
\[
 2\min\{1,N\alpha\}+\beta_{\rm c}+\beta_{\rm t}
\]
for the same implemented machine. A calibration error $\rho$ in the
known response means is already accounted for separately in that
formula. When the training source is unchanged, $\alpha=0$, regardless
of how large $N$ is.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Couple the training executions until their first mismatch, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-transfer}. The selected-event payoff has range
$[-1,1]$, giving the factor two. Conditional on a common selected event,
its two validation means change by at most the two total variation
errors. This uses no independence between a failed coupling and its
terminal event.
\end{proof}
