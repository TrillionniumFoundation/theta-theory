\section{Approximate continuation profiles and resource transfer}
\label{sec:v21-resources}
A cut profile records the whole causal schedule, rather than its final
compression. We give both a realization certificate and an operational
value. These notions keep the compatibility condition of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-continuation}; separate nonnegative factorizations
at individual cuts are not a substitute for that condition.

\subsection{A causal comparison norm}
For two normalized finite causal suffix kernels $p,q$ at cut $t$, put
\[
 d_t^{\rm ca}(p,q)=\sup_\pi\|P_p^\pi-P_q^\pi\|_{\rm TV},
\]
where $\pi$ ranges over deterministic causal input policies on the
remaining schedule and $P_p^\pi$ is the law of its complete output
transcript. Randomized policies give the same supremum, by conditioning
on their independent random tables. In particular this distance includes
feedback; the supremum over open-loop input words alone is not used.

For stochastic coefficients $q_t(z,j\mid m,x)$ and suffix kernels
$v_{t+1,j}$, denote by $\mathcal Q_t v_{t+1,m}$ the suffix kernel which,
upon input $x$, emits $z$ and chooses $j$ with these coefficients, then
uses $v_{t+1,j}$. The notation denotes one first-step operation, not a
conditioning on an event of zero probability.

\begin{theorem}[Approximate compatible realization]\label{thm:v21-compatible}
Let $K_0,\ldots,K_L$ be positive integers. Suppose normalized causal
kernels $v_{t,m}$, $m\le K_t$, and stochastic rows $q_t$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-local}
 d_t^{\rm ca}(v_{t,m},\mathcal Q_t v_{t+1,m})\le\eta_t
 \quad(t<L,m\le K_t).
\end{equation}
At the terminal cut the suffix kernels are the unit empty kernel. If
$\rho\in\Delta_{K_0}$ and
$d_0^{\rm ca}(p,\sum_m\rho_m v_{0,m})\le\eta_{-1}$, then the
machine with initial law $\rho$ and transition rows $q_t$ has the whole
profile $(K_t)$ and realizes a kernel $\widehat p$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-global}
 d_0^{\rm ca}(p,\widehat p)\le
 \min\{1,\eta_{-1}+\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}\eta_t\}.
\end{equation}
Conversely, a machine with this profile within distance $\varepsilon$
of $p$ supplies such a certificate with $\eta_{-1}=\varepsilon$ and
all local errors zero. If a deterministic initial state is required,
use $K_0=1$ throughout both statements.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\widehat v_{t,m}$ be the actual continuation of the rows $q$ from
state $m$. Fix a future policy. Conditional on the first output and next
state, its remaining actions form an admissible suffix policy. Replacing
$v_{t+1,j}$ by $\widehat v_{t+1,j}$ therefore changes its transcript law
by at most $\max_j d_{t+1}^{\rm ca}(v_{t+1,j},\widehat v_{t+1,j})$.
Mixtures and pushforwards contract total variation. The triangle
inequality and \eqref{eq:v21-local} imply, backwards from $L$,
\[
 \max_m d_t^{\rm ca}(v_{t,m},\widehat v_{t,m})
 \le\sum_{s=t}^{L-1}\eta_s.
\]
Mixing with $\rho$ proves \eqref{eq:v21-global}. The converse takes the
actual normalized continuations of the given machine, including arbitrary
valid continuations for unreachable states. Their one-step equations are
identities. There is no assertion that independently optimal approximate
lists at successive cuts have compatible updates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The full value and its effective approximation}
Fix a finite schedule with finitely many observation and action symbols,
$N$ calls to an experiment, and a bounded terminal payoff $G\in[-1,1]$.
An environment $b\in B$ specifies the conditional observation kernels
at all possible visible histories and actions. Assume $B$ is a nonempty
compact subset of the resulting finite product of probability simplexes.
A controller $A$ has a specified persistent profile $\mathbf K$; the
clock has no access to sample history. All action choices and all retained
randomness factor through the current register. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-value}
 V_{N,\mathbf K}(B,G)=\max_{A\in\mathcal M(\mathbf K)}
                    \min_{b\in B}\E_b^A G.
\end{equation}
Unused states pad smaller machines, so $\mathcal M(\mathbf K)$ is a
finite product of stochastic-row simplexes, with any prescribed causal
zeros imposed. The terminal readout is included among its rows.

\begin{theorem}[Full-profile resource transfer]\label{thm:v21-transfer}
The maximum in \eqref{eq:v21-value} is attained. Its value is
nondecreasing in each $K_t$. Removing a charged intermediate cut gives
a relaxation, whereas inserting identity-holding cuts preserves every
implementation with the same held register.

Suppose two environments at each of their $N$ calls differ in conditional
total variation by at most $\delta_i$, uniformly over corresponding
histories, actions and parameters. Replace the controller rows at its
$L$ transitions by dyadic rows of denominator $2^r$, with at most $s_t$
possible next-state/output pairs at transition $t$. Any fixed controller
loses at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-budget}
 2\min\left\{1,\sum_{i=1}^N\delta_i+
                         \sum_{t=1}^L(s_t-1)2^{-r}\right\}
\end{equation}
in expected payoff. The same bound compares optimal values on the same
profile, with the appropriate dyadic approximation on one side.
More generally an approximate continuation certificate contributes its
error in \eqref{eq:v21-global} inside the braces.

If $B_0\subset B$ is a finite $\omega$-net in the maximum conditional-TV
metric and $v_0$ is the maximum over the dyadic controller grid of the
minimum over $B_0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-sandwich}
 v_0-2N\omega\ \le V_{N,\mathbf K}(B,G)
 \le v_0+2\sum_{t=1}^L(s_t-1)2^{-r}.
\end{equation}
The bounds are certificates whenever the net and the finite evaluations
are certified. Compactness alone supplies existence of nets, not an
algorithm for an arbitrary presentation of $B$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $b$, each path probability is a finite product of controller
and environment coordinates. Summing the finitely many paths gives a
jointly continuous payoff. Its minimum over compact $B$ is continuous
in the controller rows; the controller space is compact. This proves
attainment. Padding proves monotonicity. Removing a constraint enlarges
the feasible set, and identity transitions prove the insertion assertion.

For the perturbation bound, couple two executions until their first
mismatch. On equal histories the conditional observation laws can be
maximally coupled, with mismatch probability at most $\delta_i$.
Couple the controller transitions similarly, using
Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-dyadic}. The probability of any mismatch is at most
the sum in \eqref{eq:v21-budget}, capped at one. The payoff changes by
at most two on that event. This coupling is recursive on finite histories
and remains valid for feedback controls. Alternatively, approximate
continuation kernels are compared in the causal distance and then
composed, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-compatible}.

For a fixed grid controller, a minimizing point of $B$ has a point of
$B_0$ within $\omega$; the corresponding payoffs differ by at most
$2N\omega$. Thus the grid optimum on all of $B$ is at least
$v_0-2N\omega$. Conversely approximate a maximizing unrestricted
controller row by row. Its minimum over $B$ decreases by at most the
row-rounding bound, and its minimum over $B_0$ is no smaller. Taking the
grid maximum proves the upper bound. No minimax interchange or
convexification of the fixed-width behavior image occurs.
\end{proof}

The formula records preparation count, persistent labels, calibration
error, causal simulation error and transition precision in one execution.
The training-count factor is present when training kernels change. It
is absent only when those kernels are identical. Failure probabilities
are also transported, with factor one rather than two, since their
indicators have range $[0,1]$.

\begin{proposition}[Composition and schedule refinement]\label{prop:v21-compose}
A causal simulator of profile $\mathbf R$ followed on the same synchronized
schedule by a controller of profile $\mathbf K$ has a realization with
profile $(R_tK_t)$. Causal simulation errors add under successive
composition. Two disjoint phases with a genuine reset and no retained
bridge use the maximum of their peak widths. If a bridge with $H$ labels
must survive the second phase, its direct width bound is $H$ times the
second-phase width.

If one atomic input $x$ is encoded by $\ell$ symbols, let $D_j$ be the
number of possible length-$j$ prefixes. Holding the pre-input state and
the prefix realizes the refined acquisition cuts with widths
$K_{\rm before}D_j$, followed by the original update. A time-dependent
machine running for $T$ scheduled epochs becomes autonomous by retaining
its clock, with at most $(T+1)\max_t K_t$ states. These are implementation
bounds, not claims of optimality for every refinement.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Keep the ordered pair of simulator and controller registers and compose
their transition kernels. The simulator's emitted observation is the
controller's current input. Conditional coupling until the first
simulation discrepancy gives the error statement. A genuine reset
permits reusing the alphabet, whereas a surviving bridge is another
register. A partial encoded input is determined by its prefix, giving
the stated refinement construction. Finally the augmented state
$(t,m)$ implements the time-indexed transition without an external
clock. These constructions also show why neither hidden selectors nor
an external transcript may be omitted from the register being counted.
\end{proof}
