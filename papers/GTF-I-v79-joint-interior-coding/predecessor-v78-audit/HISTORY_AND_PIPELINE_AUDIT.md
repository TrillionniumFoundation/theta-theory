### 11.2 The training-block resource law

Definition `def:freshblocks78` fixes the training resource precisely. At a block boundary, an arbitrary common instrument may process old retained outputs. The resulting full classical history chooses stopping or the next length `1<=t<=b` and a fresh tester. Conditional on that history, the tester and its fresh reference are parameter-independent and tensor-separated from all old retained systems. The whole tester remains isolated from those systems until its last query. Thus old outputs may be stored and collectively processed, and classical feedback may select later probes, while coherent old-memory-to-future-probe coupling is outside this model.

Lengths are declared before the block, charged in full, and sum to at most `M` on every record. Internal early exits are padded inside their declared block. Stopping between blocks may depend on the complete classical history. This distinction is necessary to prove the adaptive lower bound and is not a synonym for an entanglement-depth restriction on arbitrary quantum networks.

The upper proof is a direct use of the retained common matrix learner. Section 58 proves
\[
 d_N(E,F)\le\lceil N/b\rceil d_b(E,F)
\]
by replacing consecutive groups in an arbitrary reference-assisted future tester. Applying `thm:matrixlearning77` at horizon `b` and accuracy `delta/ceil(N/b)` gives
\[
 M\le C d^4\frac{N^2}{b\delta^2}\log(d/\eta)
\]
with fresh blocks no larger than `b` and the same every-record budget convention.

The lower proof uses a projective two-dimensional orbit embedded in every `d>=2`. A single channel change moves the Bures angle by at most `|theta|/2`, so an arbitrary fresh `t`-call tester has root fidelity at least `cos(t|theta|/2)`. For `b|theta|<=1`, this is at least `exp(-b t theta^2/4)`. The proof does not multiply unconditional history probabilities. It keeps subnormalized classical–quantum retained states `A_h,B_h` and the weighted potential
\[
 \sum_h e^{-a(M-m(h))}\,\mathfrak f(A_h,B_h),
 \qquad a=b\theta^2/4.
\]
Common boundary instruments preserve or increase the summed fidelity; conditional tensor factorization supplies the fresh-block factor; full histories preserve the charged-count weight. General measurable classical outcomes are treated by the corresponding integral identities. At termination the final root fidelity is at least `exp(-Mb theta^2/4)`.

A parallel GHZ parity experiment attains the exact future distance
\[
 d_N^{\rm na}(E_0,E_\theta)=d_N(E_0,E_\theta)
 =2\sin(N|\theta|/2),\qquad N|\theta|\le\pi.
\]
The exact formula belongs to this hard pair family. With `theta=4delta/N`, a two-point reduction gives the lower bound
\[
 M\ge\frac{N^2}{24b\delta^2}\log(1/\eta).
\]
Consequently `thm:blocklearning78` is sharp in `N,b,delta,eta` for every fixed `d>=2`. Dimension one uses the independent Bernoulli law `Theta(N delta^-2 log(1/eta))`; increasing the block cap does not change it.

The new lower bound answers a full-body future-loss question about that class itself. It is stronger than a statement obtained only by inserting a sufficient one-use tolerance into an upper bound, and it does not include unrestricted coherent feedback between blocks.
