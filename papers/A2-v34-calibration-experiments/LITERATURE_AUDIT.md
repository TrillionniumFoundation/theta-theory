# Primary-source literature comparison — A2 v34

Checked 4 October 2026. This is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive priority search.

Brunel–Klusowski–Yang, *Estimation of convex supports from noisy measurements*, arXiv:1804.09879v1 (26 April 2018): https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09879 and the linked HTML text. Their data are noisy continuous vectors from a convex support with additive Gaussian errors; the geometric estimator avoids Fourier inversion and is studied in Hausdorff loss. Our returned data are pooled collision bits at controlled centers. The fixed compact calibrated footprint and boundary-mass condition are different assumptions. No rate ordering between these different experiments is asserted.

Schneider, *Convex Bodies: The Brunn–Minkowski Theory*, second expanded edition, Encyclopedia of Mathematics and its Applications 151, Cambridge University Press (2014). Publisher entry: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/convex-bodies-the-brunnminkowski-theory/400F6173EE613859F144E9598DDD8BDF . Support addition, rolling geometry and Brunn–Minkowski concavity are credited as classical. The new text gives the actual overlap/depth derivation and does not invent a numbered theorem attribution or claim this standard convex geometry as new.

Locatelli–Carpentier–Kpotufe, *An Adaptive Strategy for Active Learning with Smooth Decision Boundary*, PMLR 83 (2018), 547–571: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v83/locatelli18a.html . The official entry confirms authors, bibliographic data and adaptation to unknown distributional parameters. Our known-smoothness controlled collision experiment first computes an indeterminate boundary label; direct membership labels and unknown-smoothness adaptation are not supplied as new results.

The retained Castro–Nowak, Lawler–Limic and Shannon comparisons remain attached to their original mechanisms and information categories. No new full audit of those books/articles is implied by preserving the v33 bibliography. The revised comparison separates localized bounded-error controls from fixed stationary noise and does not infer journal significance from the fixed-spread upper exponent.
