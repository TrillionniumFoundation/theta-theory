\section{Conditional endpoint metrics and curvature observability}
\label{sec:v3-observability}
The scalar coefficient in \eqref{eq:g-competition} contains only the
ratio in \eqref{eq:g-ratio}. We now retain the metric which appears
before that ratio is formed. All expectations in this section are for
the actual full-phase preparation, conditioned on $E_{j,e}(d)$.

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic endpoint statistics]\label{prop:v3-metric}
In the common transverse endpoint coordinates $Y=(u,v)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-metric-law}
 \E Y=d\,m_{j,e}(\xi,d),\qquad
 \frac1d\operatorname{Cov}(Y)
    =\frac13\mathsf H_{j,e}^{-1}+d\,B_{j,e}(\xi,d).
\end{equation}
The vector and matrix remainders are smooth for $d\ge0$ with uniform
fixed-order derivative bounds. For physical positions the scalar
variance in \eqref{eq:v3-observed-variance} has the corresponding
leading diagonal entry of $\mathsf H_{j,e}^{-1}/3$, with the same
$O(d)$ remainder. In particular the leading metric is determined
without a phase-space normalizer or a chosen transverse initial law.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Integrating the first residual time in \eqref{eq:g-morse-density} gives
the conditional endpoint density, in $w=\sqrt{2d}\,z$ coordinates,
\[
 \frac{(1-|z|^2)a_{j,e}(\xi,\sqrt{2d}\,z)}
 {\int_{|z|<1}(1-|z|^2)a_{j,e}(\xi,\sqrt{2d}\,z)\dd z}\,\dd z,
 \qquad |z|<1.
\]
Its denominator is uniformly positive. The area and the exponentially
small flux coefficient cancel in this conditional expression, but
were included in the event probability. At $d=0$ the density is
$2(1-|z|^2)/\pi$. Direct radial integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-disk-moments}
 \E_0 z=0,\qquad \E_0(zz^t)=I_2/6.
\end{equation}
For example $\E_0|z|^2=1/3$ and rotational symmetry divides this
between the two coordinates.

The endpoint map is $Y=\Phi_{j,e}(\xi,w)$, with linear part
$\mathsf H_{j,e}^{-1/2}w$. For any smooth amplitude, integration
on this radial domain annihilates its odd Taylor terms. Applied to
each component of $Y a$ and $YY^t a$, the smooth radial lemma gives
\[
 \E Y=d\,m_{j,e},\qquad
 \E(YY^t)=\frac d3\mathsf H_{j,e}^{-1}+d^2\widetilde B_{j,e}.
\]
All statements include smooth right extensions and uniform derivatives:
the endpoint maps and amplitudes have the bounds in
Lemma~\ref{lem:g-radial}, and each division by $d$ uses the
vanishing constant term of a smooth even integral. Subtracting
$(\E Y)(\E Y)^t$ proves \eqref{eq:v3-metric-law}.

The physical endpoint graph has the form
$Q_b=q_b+\mathbf t y_b\pm\mathbf n\psi_b(y_b)$, with
$|\mathbf t|=|\mathbf n|=1$ and $\psi_b=O(y_b^2)$.
Its mean is $q_b+d\widetilde m_b$. The leading term in
$|Q_b-q_b|^2$ is $y_b^2$, since the two frame vectors are orthogonal.
The remaining squared term starts at degree four. Apply the same
radial argument and subtract the squared mean displacement.
This proves the physical scalar variance formula. In particular no
unknown $q_b$ occurs in the observable itself: it is eliminated by
centering the measured positions.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-tomography}]
The determinant-one middle matrix in \eqref{eq:g-effective} has inverse
obtained by changing the signs of its off-diagonal entries. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-inverse-hessian}
 \mathsf H_{j,e}^{-1}=\frac{g}{c\sinh\gamma}D_j
 \begin{pmatrix}\coth(j\gamma)&\csch(j\gamma)\\
 \csch(j\gamma)&\coth(j\gamma)\end{pmatrix}D_j.
\end{equation}
For odd $j$, $\sigma_0^2=c_1$ and $\sigma_j^2=c_0$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:v3-metric} gives
\eqref{eq:v3-variance-limits}, with its differentiated error estimate.
Taking the geometric mean and ratio gives \eqref{eq:v3-inverse-map}.
The function
\[
 F_j(\gamma)=\frac{\coth(j\gamma)}{\sinh\gamma}
\]
is strictly decreasing from infinity to zero on $(0,\infty)$, since
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-monotone}
 -F_j'(\gamma)=
 \frac{j\csch^2(j\gamma)}{\sinh\gamma}
 +\frac{\coth(j\gamma)\cosh\gamma}{\sinh^2\gamma}>0.
\end{equation}
This proves unique recovery of $\gamma$, then of the two curvatures.
At $j=1$, the inversion is even explicit:
$F_1(\gamma)=c/(c^2-1)$, so if
$P=3\sqrt{v_0v_1}/g$, then
$c=(1+\sqrt{1+4P^2})/(2P)$.

We verify uniform conditioning, not just nonvanishing of a fixed-length
Jacobian. On a compact positive gap--curvature box,
$0<\gamma_-\le\gamma\le\gamma_+<\infty$. The second term of
\eqref{eq:v3-monotone} gives a lower bound for $-F_j'$ independent
of $j$. Its upper bound is also uniform because
$j\csch^2(j\gamma_-)\le C$ and
$\coth(j\gamma_-)\le\coth\gamma_-$. Both variances are bounded
above and bounded away from zero. Taking their ratio and geometric
mean is consequently Lipschitz; monotone inversion of $F_j$, then
the last line of \eqref{eq:v3-inverse-map}, is uniformly Lipschitz.
Forward differentiation of \eqref{eq:v3-variance-limits} gives the
reverse bound with the same type of constants. More explicitly,
for data from two points of this box,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-bilip}
 C^{-1}\|(g,\kappa_0,\kappa_1)-(\widetilde g,
          \widetilde\kappa_0,\widetilde\kappa_1)\|_\infty
 \le \|(g,v_0,v_1)-(\widetilde g,\widetilde v_0,\widetilde v_1)\|_\infty
 \le C\|(g,\kappa_0,\kappa_1)-(\widetilde g,
          \widetilde\kappa_0,\widetilde\kappa_1)\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
No factor $(\kappa_0-\kappa_1)^{-1}$ appears. Nor do we divide by
the exponentially small off-diagonal entry in
\eqref{eq:v3-inverse-hessian}.

For data outside the exact image, minimize its distance to that image
on the compact curvature box at the known $g$; a minimizer exists.
The true pair is an admissible comparison, so the selected image point
is within twice the data error of the true image. The error is bounded
by $\delta+Cd$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:v3-metric}. Apply the inverse
bound in \eqref{eq:v3-bilip}. This proves the last assertion and also
specifies a reconstruction for noisy or finite-offset data. A small
error in the supplied $g$ adds its size to the same bound, provided the
conditional variances are still those of the stated physical window;
we do not replace an onset-location error by a cost-free timing change.
\end{proof}

The distinction between odd and even lengths is informational. At an
even length the first and last impacts are on the same obstacle, and
the two diagonal leading variances coincide. At an odd length they
sample the two endpoints. The theorem therefore uses the two diagonal
entries at an odd length rather than an exponentially small covariance
or an assumed equality of endpoint curvatures.

\begin{proposition}[Independent-preparation estimation]\label{prop:v3-sampling}
Suppose that $2K$ independent full-phase preparations are retained upon
success of $E_{j,e}(d)$, for a fixed odd $j$. The two variances have
estimators $\widehat V_0,\widehat V_1$, using only their endpoint
positions and the known offset $d$, such that
\[
 \Pr\left(\max_b|\widehat V_b-V_{j,b}(d)|>\delta\right)
                \le4\exp(-cK\delta^2).
\]
A reconstruction as in Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-tomography} consequently
has error at most $C[d+\sqrt{\log(4/\eta)/K}]$ with probability
at least $1-\eta$. All constants are independent of odd $j$.
The expected number of independent unconditioned preparations needed
for these $2K$ successes is comparable to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-preparation-cost}
                  Kd^{-2}e^{j\gamma_e}.
\end{equation}
No independence of successive impacts in one trajectory is assumed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pair the independent successful records and use, at each endpoint,
\[
 \widehat V_b=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K
       \frac{|Q_b^{(2k)}-Q_b^{(2k-1)}|^2}{2d}.
\]
Independence gives its expectation as the variance in
\eqref{eq:v3-observed-variance}. Coercivity confines each endpoint
to a set of diameter $C\sqrt d$, independently of $j$. The summands
are therefore in one bounded interval $[0,C_0]$. For completeness,
a random variable in an interval of length $C_0$ satisfies
$\E e^{t(X-\E X)}\le e^{t^2C_0^2/8}$: the second derivative of
the log moment generating function is a variance under a tilted law,
bounded by $C_0^2/4$, and its value and first derivative vanish at zero.
Multiplication over the independent pairs, the exponential Markov
inequality, and optimization over $t$ yield
$2e^{-2K\delta^2/C_0^2}$ at each endpoint. A union bound proves
the displayed estimate. The deterministic inverse estimate gives the
curvature bound. Rejection from independent preparations gives
independent conditional successes, and the expected waiting time is
$2K/\Pr(E_{j,e}(d))$. Theorem~\ref{thm:g-stability} and
$\sinh(j\gamma)\asymp e^{j\gamma}$ prove
\eqref{eq:v3-preparation-cost}. Uniform conditional conditioning is
therefore not a uniform unconditioned sampling cost.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Realized scalar fibers and their separation by records}
At known $g,A$, all leading selected-channel count coefficients depend
on $c_0,c_1$ only through $c_0c_1$. Conversely, their first two values
recover this product, since their ratio is $2\cosh\gamma$. The
following construction shows that this scalar fiber occurs for actual
periodic billiards, not just for algebraic local jets.

\begin{theorem}[A geometric fiber of the full leading count hierarchy]
\label{thm:v3-fibers}
On the rectangular lattice $3\Z\times4\Z$ there is a real-analytic family
of strictly convex scatterers $D_s$, $|s|<s_0$, with $D_0$ the unit
disk, for which the only shortest unoriented channel is horizontal,
\[
 g(s)=1,\qquad A(s)=12-\pi,\qquad
 c_0(s)=2e^s,\quad c_1(s)=2e^{-s}.
\]
All maximal-count threshold locations and every leading coefficient
$\lim_{d\downarrow0}d^{-2}\Pr_s(N_{j+d}=j+1)$ are independent of $s$.
The ordered contact curvatures vary. The two endpoint variances at any
odd order distinguish $s$, with constants uniform in that order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the support function of the disk, $h_0\equiv1$. Put
\[
 \chi_0=\tfrac14(1+\cos\theta)\sin^2\theta,\quad
 \chi_\pi=\tfrac14(1-\cos\theta)\sin^2\theta,\quad
 \chi_*=\sin^4\theta.
\]
At $0,\pi$ all three functions and their first derivatives vanish.
The second derivatives of $\chi_0$ at these two points are $(1,0)$,
those of $\chi_\pi$ are $(0,1)$, and those of $\chi_*$ are $(0,0)$.
These explicit trigonometric polynomials permit independent contact
jets and a compensating change of area. Set
\[
 r_0(s)=(2e^s-1)^{-1},\qquad r_1(s)=(2e^{-s}-1)^{-1},
\]
and
\[
 h_{s,a}=1+(r_0(s)-1)\chi_0+(r_1(s)-1)\chi_\pi+a\chi_*.
\]
For small $s,a$, $h+h''>0$, so this is a smooth strictly convex body.
At normals $0,\pi$, its support values stay one and its first
support derivatives stay zero. Its two curvature radii are exactly
$r_0(s),r_1(s)$, since the compensator has zero second jet at both normals.
The supporting lines between neighboring horizontal translates
therefore stay a distance one apart, and their support points attain
that distance. The closest horizontal segment is unchanged.

The obstacle area is
$\mathcal A(h)=\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}[h^2-(h')^2]\dd\theta$.
Its derivative in $a$ at $(s,a)=(0,0)$ is $\int\chi_*\dd\theta>0$.
Since $\int\chi_*\dd\theta=3\pi/4$, the analytic implicit function
theorem supplies a real-analytic $a=a(s)$ with
$a(0)=0$ and $\mathcal A(h_{s,a(s)})=\pi$ exactly. This variation
has not altered either contact jet. At the disk, the vertical gap is
two and every other nonhorizontal pair has still larger gap. The
positive gap to the horizontal minimum, disjointness of translates,
and clearance persist for small $s$. Hence the two horizontal
orientations are the entire ground-onset event. Their sum in
\eqref{eq:g-competition} has leading coefficient
\[
       \frac1{(12-\pi)\sinh(j\arcosh2)},
\]
independent of $s$. This assertion concerns leading coefficients,
not equality of the finite-offset remainders or all other billiard data.
Finally \eqref{eq:v3-variance-limits} gives
$v_{j,1}/v_{j,0}=e^{2s}$ at every odd $j$. It separates the family;
uniform conditioning follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-tomography}.
\end{proof}

The fixed contact patches can be chosen once for this entire family.
Even when several minimizing pairs compete, a recorded first-impact
patch and its outgoing direction select the oriented channel. On a
nonminimal channel one must also use its own window and the itinerary
selection specified in Section~\ref{sec:geometric-results}. Counts in
the ground collar alone eventually suppress a fixed gap difference
$\Delta>0$, since activation requires $j\Delta<\varepsilon_0$.
The marked theorem neither changes that condition nor implicitly
provides unobserved channel labels.
