\subsection{Differentiated operator bounds and moving poles}
For use in differentiated forms of the relative factorization, the
operator estimates can be recorded in a single common norm convention.
Let $G$ denote either a finite reference Green matrix or a half-line
Green operator, and let $\Delta H$ be the corresponding tridiagonal
Hessian perturbation on its stationary segment. For every fixed $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-operator-bounds}
 \max_{|\alpha|\le k}\|\partial_\xi^\alpha G\|_{\rm op}\le C_k,
 \qquad
 \max_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\le k}
       \|\partial_\xi^\alpha\partial_Y^\beta\Delta H\|_1\le C_k,
 \qquad \|G\Delta H\|_{\rm op}\le q<1.
\end{equation}
Here $Y$ is the finite endpoint vector or the single half-line endpoint.
There is no assertion that derivatives of $G\Delta H$ are themselves
small. They are bounded in trace norm, whereas its zeroth-order operator
norm is small on the fixed contact box.

For completeness, the first bound follows directly from the Green
kernels: their parameter derivatives introduce fixed powers of the
indices into exponentially summable kernels, absorbed by the strict
exponential margin. The Schur row and column estimates give the operator
bound independently of matrix size. For the second, each tridiagonal
entry is a local smooth function of neighboring stationary coordinates.
The endpoint localization and its differentiated bounds make the sum
of its absolute entries bounded. This sum bounds the trace norm.
The last inequality follows by choosing the common box once, using
$\Delta H(0)=0$. The product rule consequently gives trace-norm bounds
for every fixed derivative of $T=G\Delta H$.

These estimates also explain the differentiated determinant comparison.
Differentiating $\operatorname{tr}(T^n)$ a fixed $k$ times produces at
most $C_kn^k$ products with at most $k$ differentiated factors. One
factor is bounded in trace norm and all others in operator norm. The
undifferentiated factors contribute the geometric decay $q^{n-k}$;
the finitely many $n<k$ are harmless. In a difference of two such
expressions, telescope one factor at a time. The differentiated
trace-norm differences from the block comparison in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-factorization} supply $C_k\tau^j$, and summation
in $n$ is uniform. Thus differentiated logarithmic determinants retain
a trace-class factor and never acquire a factor equal to the number
of interior sites.

In Corollary~\ref{cor:v4-poles}, simple poles refer to the undifferentiated
series at a fixed geometry. If a parameter moves $\gamma$, differentiation
of its displayed rational principal part can increase the pole order.
After $k$ parameter derivatives it is at most $k+1$. For example,
$\partial_\xi(1-ze^{-\gamma})^{-1}
=-ze^{-\gamma}(\partial_\xi\gamma)/(1-ze^{-\gamma})^2$.
The differentiated remainder remains holomorphic on the stated strictly
smaller common domains. This separates the regularity of the remainder
from the order of the moving principal poles.
