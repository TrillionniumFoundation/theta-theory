\title[Scalar collision laws]{Scalar collision laws and recognition of periodic dispersing billiards}
\author{Qian Qi}\date{October 3, 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{37D50, 52A27, 52C23, 62G05}
\keywords{Controlled collision laws, stopped inverse, geometric calibration,

\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct separated convex obstacles from controlled collision
probabilities, recording one bit per attempted launch and assigning the
same zero to a solid start and a free miss. Opposed preparations recover
an occupation difference; bounded component size supplies a finite-chain
inverse. We extend this mechanism to prescribed distributions of opposed
directions and durations by a finite stopped averaging recursion. A local
convex-tube estimate controls coupled position, timing and angular errors,
including grazing commands, with bias proportional to their displacement
error divided by the input localization scale. For a bounded periodic
class with a known positive nonperiod patch margin and uniform smoothness,
a threshold grid, component graph, convex hulls and compensated support
smoothing give a constructive whole-table inverse. At localization
$\sigma\asymp\nu^{3/2}$ it uses $O(\nu^{-3})$ spatial commands and
$O(\nu^{-3}\log(C/(\nu\delta)))$ one-bit attempts for conditional
$C^2$ error $O(\nu)$ with confidence $1-\delta$. A nonoptimized finite
arithmetic implementation uses $O(\nu^{-6})$ operations. Exact discrete
period relations are recovered in an approximately known real basis.
Repeated motifs and arbitrary individual symmetries are allowed. The
calibration, probability, arithmetic and periodicity priors remain
explicit; this is an active-input result, not uniform-count or passive
spectral rigidity. All preceding scalar and intrinsic-law results are
retained.
\end{abstract}\maketitle
