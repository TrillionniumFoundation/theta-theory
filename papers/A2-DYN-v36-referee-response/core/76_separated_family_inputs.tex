\section{Separated inputs, finite covers, and source conventions}
\label{sec:v36-family-inputs}
The compact-family theorem uses several logically different inputs.
We separate them so that neither connectedness nor a weak-space
identification conceals an ergodic or functional-analytic hypothesis.
The collision probability, normalized arclength, lattice coordinates,
and stationary suspension are those of
Section~\ref{sec:compact-family-local-principle}.

\subsection{The seven inputs}
\paragraph{(H1) Mechanical geometry.}
On a compact parameter space the scatterers form a continuous $C^3$
family of disjoint strictly convex obstacles on a fixed Euclidean
lattice, with uniform positive curvature, separation and finite-horizon
bounds. There is one obstacle per primitive cell. In particular the
single-flight roof has common bounds $0<\tau_-\le\tau_\lambda\le\tau_+$.
No change of the Euclidean reflection rule is allowed.

\paragraph{(H2) Local common collision spaces.}
A finite cover of parameter space admits local common strong and weak
spaces with the norms of Section~\ref{sec:action-norm-details}, the
unweighted splitting \eqref{eq:collision-split}, a continuous mass
functional, compact strong-to-weak embedding, and the geometric sums
\eqref{eq:v35-geometric-sums-a}--\eqref{eq:v35-geometric-sums-b}.
Smooth densities are dense in the strong space. Its global-test
representation is faithful by Lemma~\ref{lem:v35-faithful-strong},
whose proof now concludes directly in both strong seminorms.
All constants are common on each neighborhood and hence on the finite
cover. A single Banach space over the whole parameter set is not required.

\paragraph{(H3) Finite-cover mixing and product structure.}
For each positive integer $p$, the collision map on the actual
$p\Lambda$ lattice cover is mixing for its normalized collision
probability. The regular local stable and unstable product rectangles
have the absolute-continuity and contraction properties used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v35-rotation-free-rigidity}. These are independent
unweighted dynamical inputs. The mixing assertion is qualitative for
each fixed table and cover; its rate need not be uniform in $p$.
In that theorem mixing excludes a nontrivial unit eigenvalue when
$c_0\ne0$, whereas ergodicity alone excludes the mean-zero invariant
function when $c_0=0$.

\paragraph{(H4) Action and regular matching.}
The roof satisfies $d\tau=T^*\vartheta-\vartheta$ for the bounded
one-form $\vartheta=\sin\varphi\,dr$. The homogeneous inverse pieces
and their matched regular connectors satisfy the contraction,
trimming and Jacobian bounds used in
\eqref{eq:v35-action-telescope}--\eqref{eq:v35-weight-match-detail}.
The lattice label is constant on each regular branch. The unmatched
family includes every intermediate singularity or homogeneity cut.

\paragraph{(H5) Preceding-flight multipliers.}
The preceding record has the finite-complexity partition and common
piecewise $C^{1/2}$ bounds preceding
Lemma~\ref{lem:v35-multiplier-detail}. In particular its exponential
and fixed-order derivatives are bounded strong multipliers on each
compact complex frequency set. This condition is imposed on one
preceding flight, not on an accumulated section-occupation word.

\paragraph{(H6) Parameter comparison.}
Finite collision itineraries converge off the limiting singularities.
The one-step matched/unmatched comparison is continuous from the
strong to the weak space. The preceding root converges on matched
pieces in $C^q$ by interpolation of the common $C^{1/2}$ bound.
The common invariant measure in normalized coordinates permits
dominated convergence of finite-lag covariances.

\paragraph{(H7) Observation and selector geometry.}
There are uniformly finitely many one-flight cell labels. Their age
intervals have endpoints converging uniformly off exceptional open
neighborhoods whose closures have arbitrarily small limiting measure
and null boundary. This is the closed-neighborhood condition in
Corollary~\ref{cor:local-endpoint-measures}. The same requirement is
imposed on varying endpoint selectors. A selected probability uses
nonnegative bounded selectors; a uniform lower denominator requires
a uniform positive lower bound on the product of their stationary
means. Arbitrary bounded path tests enter a posterior comparison,
not an assertion of a Gaussian amplitude for an arbitrary selector.

These inputs imply Theorem~\ref{thm:v35-action-family-LLT} by its
written proof: (H2), (H4)--(H6) give the fixed-band spectrum; (H3)
gives physical peripheral exclusion and nondegeneracy; (H7) gives the
exact stationary age passage. Every spectral constant is chosen after
the fixed band $B$. The resulting local theorem has a qualitative
$o(1)$ error, not a stated polynomial rate.

\subsection{Mixing of the actual finite covers}
\begin{proposition}[The finite-cover input for the concrete tables]
\label{prop:v36-cover-mixing}
Let an unforced periodic planar billiard have finitely many disjoint
$C^3$ strictly convex scatterers on a flat torus, strictly positive
curvatures and finite horizon. Its collision map on each actual finite
lattice cover is mixing. In particular \textup{(H3)} holds for every
oriented ellipse in \eqref{eq:v35-ellipses}, and for the tables of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v36-support-family} below.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a finite cover. It is again a flat torus with finitely many disjoint
smooth strictly convex scatterers. The covering is a local Euclidean
isometry: curvatures, free flights, reflection and the horizon bound
are unchanged. Apply \cite[Section 8.1, Theorem 6(b)]{Young} to this
covered collision map. That theorem gives exponential decay of
correlations for H\"older observables. The sentence immediately
preceding it states total ergodicity of the collision map in this
class. Thus it is a map theorem excluding finite cyclic components,
not a deduction of collision mixing from mixing of a suspension flow.

For arbitrary $f,g\in L^2$ on this fixed cover, approximate them in
$L^2$ by smooth functions. The error in their lag correlation is
uniformly bounded by the approximation errors times their $L^2$
norms, by invariance and Cauchy--Schwarz. Take the lag to infinity
before removing the approximation. The correlation therefore tends
to the product of means. This proves the asserted mixing property
for the bounded physical eigenfunctions used in the finite-character
argument. Neither the approximation nor Young's correlation constant
is claimed uniform in the number of sheets.

For the triangular lattice, a rectangular Euclidean cover with periods
$(p,0)$ and $(0,p\sqrt3)$ is also available. The $p\Lambda$ cover is
its factor; mixing passes to that factor by lifting the two test
functions. This uses no anisotropic linear transformation of the
reflection law. The geometric verification in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v35-ellipse-geometry} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:v36-support-family} places the stated families
in the class just checked. Connectedness verifies the configuration,
but is not used as a substitute for the cited mixing theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Pinned locations and variation convention}
For equation numbering we use these preprint versions:
\cite{DZ}, arXiv:1210.1261v1, and \cite{DPZ},
arXiv:1902.06850v1. In the former,
Lemma 3.2(a), (b), (c), (d), on PDF page 12, are respectively the
never-long, unweighted, length-normalized, and fractional-Jacobian
sums in \eqref{eq:v35-geometric-sums-a}--\eqref{eq:v35-geometric-sums-b}.
The proof of (4.10)--(4.11), on PDF pages 20--21, specifies the
last-long-ancestor payment. Lemma 3.3, on page 13, and the construction
before (4.12), on pages 21--22, specify regular matching; (4.14)--(4.19)
on pages 22--23 give the unmatched, test and Jacobian payments.
These are unweighted geometric inputs. The additional roof-weight
calculation is Proposition~\ref{prop:v35-expanded-LY}.

For the multiplier and completion conventions use \cite[Lemmas 3.2(c),
3.3(b), footnote 7, and Lemma 3.9]{DPZ}. Footnote 7 distinguishes the
injective strong-to-weak inclusion from the potentially noninjective
map of the whole weak space to global tests. Our revised proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v35-faithful-strong} does not invoke either injectivity
as its final step. The cover theorem above is pinned to the author
preprint ESI 445, Section 8.1, Theorem 6(b), PDF page 46 (printed page
45), of \cite{Young}. The exact source locations and the author-side
verification boundary are also recorded in \texttt{SOURCE\_INPUT\_MAP.md}.

Throughout the new results,
\[
 \|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}=|\alpha|(\Omega)
       =\sup_{|f|\le1}\left|\int f\,d\alpha\right|
\]
is the total-variation norm of a signed or complex measure. For two
probabilities this norm is twice their supremum discrepancy on
measurable sets. Thus the factor $2$ in the normalization inequality
in Corollary~\ref{cor:v35-family-posterior} has not been removed by a
change of convention.
