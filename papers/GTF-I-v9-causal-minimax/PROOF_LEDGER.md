## Deliberately distinct assertions

The risk body uses row risks of actual admissible rules; a minimax task's scalar risk is obtained by maximizing its rows. Rows are not separately optimized with knowledge of the true parameter. A common encoder can react to a declared control input, but does not receive an additional unpriced task index.

The saturation theorem is minimal within its stated likelihood/prediction/recursion coordinate class, not among all task-specific lossy machines or all possible exact simulators. Exact rational reconstruction probabilities need not be dyadic; bounded-bit sampling pays approximation error. Shared-seed equivalence at constant multiplicative memory overhead requires a fixed finite task-row family and the declared initialization contract.

The Gaussian theorem controls cardinality and actual sequential acquisition timing. Its unrestricted real posterior evaluation is not represented as a finite-arithmetic implementation. The old all-priors Wasserstein result and old terminal deficiency statement retain their respective marks and computational conventions.
