\begin{abstract}
We prove a controlled common-encoder minimax theorem for finite causal
experiments. One encoder is selected before the decision task, while each
task controller remains independent of the unknown parameter. The
attainable upper risk body is the convex hull of executable deterministic
designs under an independent shared seed; its support is given by an
exact occupancy recursion. Controlled successor-compatible partitions
characterize the feasible encoders. Private randomization has a distinct
nonconvex flow description, and an optimal shared scheme can be realized
privately by charging at most one additional factor equal to the number
of decision rows plus one. The same principle yields a common quadratic
variance formula and a risk-sensitive moment duality. We construct the
least saturated predictive quotient of a finite instrument and compile
rational finite sources with finite description, randomization and
execution bounds. A delayed-query Gaussian experiment has causal excess
of order $S^{-2/d}$ despite a scalar terminal target whose query-aware
checkpoint error is of order $S^{-2}$. Complete predictive-tree,
measure-valued, terminal Gaussian and count-experiment developments are
retained with their precise hypotheses.
\end{abstract}
