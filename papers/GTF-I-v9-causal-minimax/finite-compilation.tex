\section{Finite compilation and several charged resources}
\label{sec:v9-compilation}
The finite variational principle also has an effective implementation.
This is a separate source of multi-resource control from the energy
oracle used in the predictive-tree construction. The price of synthesis
is explicit and can be large; it is not moved into a free online oracle.

Use the finite instrument and finite decision alphabets of
Section~\ref{sec:v9-minimax}, with rational probabilities and losses.
Let $S=\max_tS_t$, $Y=\max_t|Y_t|$, $B=\max_d|B_d|$, and $m=|D|$.
A table implementation of one deterministic design has description
length bounded, up to an absolute coding constant, by
\begin{align}
 P_0={}&\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} S_t|A||Y_{t+1}|
                                      \lceil\log_2(S_{t+1}+1)\rceil
                                                        \nonumber\\
 &+m\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}S_t\lceil\log_2(|A|+1)\rceil
   +mS_T\lceil\log_2(B+1)\rceil
   +O((T+m)\log_2(T+S+|A|+Y+B+2)).
 \label{eq:v9-tablebits}
\end{align}
Unused states and forbidden controller actions can simply be retained
as redundant table entries. The externally specified time index is the
same clock convention as in \eqref{eq:v8-register}. If no external clock
is permitted, its $T+1$ values must also be included in the register.

\begin{theorem}[Effective finite-source realization]
\label{thm:v9-compiler}
An optimal shared-seed common-encoder scheme for the rational finite
instrument can be synthesized by a finite exact calculation. It can use
$k\le M+1$ deterministic tables with rational mixing weights. For every
integer $p\ge1$ there is a dyadic implementation whose excess is at most
\begin{equation}
                  \Theta_{\rm sh}({\bf S};D)+k\,2^{-p}.
 \label{eq:v9-compileerror}
\end{equation}
Its fixed description has $O(kP_0+kp)$ bits. Initialization uses exactly
$p$ independent fair bits; no random bits are required subsequently.
With an explicitly permitted read-only shared seed its persistent
register has width at most $S$. Without that convention the same scheme
has width at most $kS$, including the stored design index.

Each update reads one supplied finite-alphabet report. On a bit-serial
table implementation, transient space can be bounded by
$O(p+\log(P_0k+2)+\log(S+|A|+Y+B+T+m+2))$ bits and the running time of
an update or initialization by $O(kP_0+kp+p+1)$ bit operations, after
increasing the universal coding constant. No unit-cost access to an
arbitrary real constant is required. Reading the physical report itself
remains an input cost, rather than a claim about how an underlying
continuous measurement was acquired.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate the finite deterministic designs. Their risks are rational
numbers obtained from finite products and sums of rational transition
entries and losses. Full-history baselines can likewise be computed by
enumerating full-history policies and solving their finite minimax
linear programmes; for a Bayesian task their minimum is already a
finite linear optimization. Thus the primal programme
\eqref{eq:v9-primal} has rational data and has a rational optimal basic
solution. Rational linear algebra and the usual elimination of affine
dependence among its risk vectors give a rational optimal mixture of at
most $M+1$ designs. This gives a finite synthesis algorithm, not a bound
polynomial in the horizon or the number of reports. Integer determinants
of the finite rational constraint matrix give finite bit bounds for all
weights, so synthesis does not require a probability oracle.

Let its weights be $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k$. For $r<k$ set
$\widetilde\lambda_r=2^{-p}\lfloor2^p\lambda_r\rfloor$ and let the
last weight be the nonnegative remainder. Then
\[
 \|\lambda-\widetilde\lambda\|_{\rm TV}
       =\sum_{r<k}(\lambda_r-\widetilde\lambda_r)
       \le(k-1)2^{-p}.
\]
For every row, its risk lies in $[0,1]$, so the difference of its mixed
risks is at most this total variation. Taking the maximum and subtracting
the unchanged baseline proves \eqref{eq:v9-compileerror}.

A uniform $p$-bit integer, compared to the cumulative integer weights,
selects the dyadic design exactly. Its deterministic tables then
implement the scheme. There are $k$ copies of the tables counted in
\eqref{eq:v9-tablebits} and $k$ integer weights of at most $p+1$ bits,
which proves the description bound. In the private implementation the
index is part of $(r,I_t)$ and hence multiplies the width by $k$. In the
shared implementation the seed is explicitly allowed throughout the run;
its index can instead be reread from that permitted input. A modified or
data-dependent seed would not qualify for this exception.

For a conservative machine model, store the immutable description as a
sequential binary table with self-delimited addresses. Locate an update,
a controller action, or a terminal answer by scanning it. Counters need
only logarithmic space in its length and in the finite alphabet sizes.
Comparing the $p$-bit initialization integer with its cumulative weights
requires $O(p)$ extra space and $O(kp)$ time by a sequential scan.
Copying the selected finite answer and updating the label has the same
bounds. Thus the displayed time and space bounds are valid without a
random-access assumption. They are deliberately not optimized.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Stability of finite synthesis]\label{prop:v9-stability}
Suppose two finite instruments have matched alphabets, losses and timing.
Their initial hidden laws have total variation distance at most $\eta_0$
for every parameter, and the corresponding transition--report rows have
distance at most $\eta_t$ uniformly in hidden state and control. Then,
for every admissible common-encoder/controller design, the difference
of each bounded-loss risk is at most
$\varepsilon=\min\{1,\eta_0+\sum_{t<T}\eta_t\}$.
Their full-history baselines differ by at most $\varepsilon$, and their
shared or private excess spectra at the same signature differ by at most
$2\varepsilon$. An additional uniform error $\zeta$ in the loss adds
$2\zeta$ to this last bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Couple the initial hidden states maximally. Until the two trajectories
first disagree, choose the same controls, report-driven updates and
random bits in both executions. Couple the corresponding hidden/report
transition rows maximally. The probability of a disagreement by the last
time is at most $\eta_0+\sum\eta_t$. A bounded loss changes in expectation
by at most that probability. This argument is uniform over every
parameter and every legal controller, including full-history controllers.
Infima over controllers and common encoders, and maxima over tasks or
parameters, are nonexpansive in uniform error. Apply that observation
once to the constrained risk and once to its baseline. A uniformly
perturbed loss changes each of these by at most $\zeta$.
\end{proof}

This provides a multi-resource finite theorem with computable synthesis,
finite randomization, finite description and finite execution cost.
Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:v9-saturation}, it also makes finite
sufficient reconstruction effective whenever the expanded finite source
is given rationally. It does not compute multiplicative energies for an
arbitrary infinite predictive tree, nor does it turn a merely measurable
sufficient statistic into a finite algorithm. Those are different input
classes with different resource signatures.
