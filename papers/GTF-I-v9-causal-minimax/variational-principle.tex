\section{One variational principle: occupancies, Bayes envelopes and pressure}
\label{sec:v9-variational}
The support function in Theorem~\ref{thm:v9-minimax} can be computed
without first constructing a quotient or listing full decision trees.
The relevant state for this calculation is an \emph{offline} joint
occupancy. It is not an additional observation available during execution.

\subsection{An exact support-function recursion}
For $w\in\Delta_J$ put
\[
 z_0^{d\theta}(z,i)=\1_{\{i=1\}}\nu_\theta(z)
              \sum_{j:d(j)=d}w_j\kappa_j(\theta).
\]
For arbitrary nonnegative arrays $z=(z^{d\theta}(x,i))$, a prescription
consists of actions $a^d(i)$ and a \emph{single} table $f(i,a,y)$.
Define its linear occupancy update by
\begin{equation}
 (\mathcal T_{a,f}z)^{d\theta}(x',i')
  =\sum_{x,i,y}z^{d\theta}(x,i)
       K_{\theta,t}(x',y\mid x,a^d(i))
       \1_{\{f(i,a^d(i),y)=i'\}}.
 \label{eq:v9-occupancy}
\end{equation}
The time $t$ is understood from the two spaces in this formula. Define
\begin{align}
 V_T(z)&=\min_{b^d(i)\in B_d}
                \sum_{d,\theta,x,i}z^{d\theta}(x,i)
                          \ell_{d\theta}(x,b^d(i)),\label{eq:v9-terminalbellman}\\
 V_t(z)&=\min_{a,f}V_{t+1}(\mathcal T_{a,f}z),
                                     \qquad t=T-1,\ldots,0.
 \label{eq:v9-bellman}
\end{align}
\begin{theorem}[Occupancy representation of the spectrum]
\label{thm:v9-occupancy}
For the finite controlled instrument,
\begin{equation}
 h_E({\bf S};w)=V_0(z_0(w)),\qquad
 \Theta_{\rm sh}({\bf S};D)
       =\max_{w\in\Delta_J}\{V_0(z_0(w))-w\cdot b\}.
 \label{eq:v9-occupancyspectrum}
\end{equation}
Every $V_t$ is a positively homogeneous, concave, piecewise-linear
function on its nonnegative occupancy cone. A minimizing sequence of
prescriptions compiles into an actual common-encoder design with the
specified widths. No realized task index or hidden parameter is
supplied to $f$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a deterministic sequence of prescriptions. Induction in
\eqref{eq:v9-occupancy} shows that $z_t^{d\theta}$ is the joint law of
$(Z_t,I_t)$ under that task and parameter, multiplied by its initial
weight. Its terminal linear cost is therefore $w\cdot R(\omega)$.
At the last time the terminal tables in
\eqref{eq:v9-terminalbellman} give precisely all legal terminal rules:
$b^d(i)$ is common to every $\theta$ in task $d$.

At an earlier time, choosing $a^d(i)$ and one common $f$ determines the
entire next occupancy. Conversely every possible first prescription of
a deterministic continuation has this form. Backward induction therefore
proves that \eqref{eq:v9-bellman} minimizes over exactly all legal future
designs. This proves the first equality in
\eqref{eq:v9-occupancyspectrum}; Theorem~\ref{thm:v9-minimax} proves the
second. For any fixed continuation the cost is linear in the initial
occupancy. There are finitely many continuations, so $V_t$ is the minimum
of finitely many linear forms. The stated geometric properties follow.

The occupancy is propagated during design, starting at the known array
$z_0(w)$. Once a sequence of minimizing tables is chosen, its future
occupancies are deterministic functions of that sequence. Only the
finite tables and the actual label are used online. In particular,
one must not store the occupancy array without charging its size, nor
allow a different prescription for the unobserved realized parameter.
\end{proof}

This recursion is a designer's dynamic programme with coupled information
constraints. Common-information prescriptions and belief-state dynamic
programming have substantial antecedents
\cite{NayyarMahajanTeneketzis2013,Witsenhausen1979}.
In the cited common-information preprint, Corollary~5 already treats
absent common memory and finite local memories; its unconditional
information state is an offline occupancy. The additional assertion
here is the exact equality with the
\emph{common-encoder minimax spectrum}, including its dual adversarial
weights, controlled continuation constraint and explicit seed cost.
The recursion does not assert polynomial complexity: the number of
linear pieces and the number of finite designs can be very large.

\subsection{The common quadratic task family}
There is a particularly transparent intrinsic form when reports are
uncontrolled. Let the finite history law $p_t(h)$ be fixed, let
$U_{dt}$ be square-integrable Hilbert-valued targets, and let
$\alpha_{dt}\ge0$. Task $d$ has loss
$\sum_t\alpha_{dt}\|U_{dt}-\widehat U_{dt}\|^2$.
The decoder's answers do not affect later observations or updates.
Set $m_{dt}(h)=\E[U_{dt}\mid H_t=h]$. For a successor-compatible
partition family $\mathcal P$, define
\begin{equation}
 D_d(\mathcal P)=\sum_t\alpha_{dt}\sum_{C\in\mathcal P_t}
       \sum_{h\in C}p_t(h)\|m_{dt}(h)-\overline m_{dt,C}\|^2,
 \qquad
 \overline m_{dt,C}=\frac{\sum_{h\in C}p_t(h)m_{dt}(h)}{p_t(C)}.
 \label{eq:v9-vectorvariance}
\end{equation}
Zero-mass cells are omitted. Define the single weighted geometry
\[
 M_{w,t}(h)=\big(\sqrt{w_d\alpha_{dt}}\,m_{dt}(h)\big )_{d\in D}
\]
to lie in the Hilbert direct sum of the target spaces.
\begin{corollary}[Common-encoder variance duality]\label{cor:v9-quadratic}
For a shared independent seed and the declared widths,
\begin{align}
 \Theta_{\rm sh}({\bf S};D)
 &=\min_{\lambda\in\Delta(\mathcal P)}
                       \max_d\sum_{\mathcal P}\lambda_{\mathcal P}
                                                   D_d(\mathcal P)\nonumber\\
 &=\max_{w\in\Delta_D}\min_{\mathcal P}
        \sum_t\E\left\|M_{w,t}(H_t)
                -\E[M_{w,t}(H_t)\mid\mathcal P_t]\right\|^2.
 \label{eq:v9-quadraticdual}
\end{align}
The minima range over \emph{one} successor-compatible family of width
at most $S_t$ at each time. This is an exact characterization of the
maximum over tasks, not a statement only about one scalarized loss.
Its private version lies between the values given by this formula at
$S$ and by its implementation with $(|D|+1)S$ states.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Conditional expectation gives the orthogonal decomposition of each
squared risk into its full-history Bayes risk and prediction of
$m_{dt}(H_t)$. On a deterministic state cell its unique optimal readout
is the displayed cell mean. All tasks can use their respective means
simultaneously without changing the common encoder. Thus the
coordinatewise best risk vector of a deterministic partition is exactly
$D(\mathcal P)$. There are finitely many history partitions, although
the target Hilbert spaces need not be finite dimensional.

Conditioning on an independent seed gives a distribution of these
partitions; conversely their mixtures are executable under the shared
convention. The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v9-minimax} now applies to this
finite vector set. The identity
$\sum_dw_dD_d(\mathcal P)=\sum_t\E\|M_{w,t}-\E(M_{w,t}\mid\mathcal P_t)\|^2$
is the direct-sum norm formula. Storing the mixture index proves the
last assertion exactly as in \eqref{eq:v9-seedprice}.
\end{proof}

For $|D|=1$, \eqref{eq:v9-quadraticdual} reduces to the earlier
conditional-variance characterization, Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-congruence}.
For several tasks it supplies its missing minimax convexification. The
outer maximizing weight is common to every time; selecting a separate
favourable weight or encoder at each checkpoint is not permitted.
Compatible predictive trees are one class of feasible partitions of this
same functional. Their energy estimates in the supporting theory supply
quantitative bounds for that class; they are not hypotheses of
Theorems~\ref{thm:v9-minimax} or \ref{thm:v9-occupancy}.

\begin{corollary}[A1: maximum over finite query and checkpoint tasks]
\label{cor:v9-a1}
Consider the actual finite-command accepted-cell/failure protocol of
Corollary~\ref{cor:v8-a1}, with its fixed prior, finite horizon and
parameter-independent command laws. Fix any finite family of weighted
quadratic query/checkpoint tasks. Its optimal shared-seed common causal
encoder is given by \eqref{eq:v9-quadraticdual}, using the accepted and
\emph{summed failure} likelihoods of that protocol. At most $|D|+1$
partition designs are needed. Retaining their index gives the same
optimum with at most $(|D|+1)S$ private states.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The observation likelihoods and posterior means are derived in
Corollary~\ref{cor:v8-a1} from $g_jk_j(\theta)$ on acceptance and
$\sum_j(1-g_j)k_j(\theta)$ on the one failure report. These give one
finite history law for all tasks. Insert its posterior query means in
\eqref{eq:v9-vectorvariance} and apply Corollary~\ref{cor:v9-quadratic}.
The unseen failed raw-cell label is never used in a partition update.
Unlike the earlier scalarization, the present formula retains the
maximum over the whole prescribed task family.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A1 with private randomness at unchanged width]
\label{prop:v9-a1-private}
In the same finite-command protocol, let $p_t(h)$ be its actual history
law, and let $m_{dt}(h)$ be the finite vector of posterior query means
for task $d$, with query weights absorbed into that vector. Write
$\eta_t^q(i\mid h)$ for the conditional label law generated recursively
by one common private encoder $q$. The private maximum-over-tasks excess
at unchanged width is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \min_{q,v}\max_{d\in D}
 \sum_{t,h,i}\alpha_{dt}p_t(h)\eta_t^q(i\mid h)
                           \|m_{dt}(h)-v_{dt}(i)\|^2.
 \label{eq:v9-a1-private}
\end{equation}
The readouts can be restricted to the compact boxes containing the
weighted query means. This is a finite polynomial optimization problem
in the stochastic update rows and readout coordinates, with coefficients
given by the actual likelihood integrals. It has a minimum. No finite
arithmetic claim is made when those coefficients are arbitrary reals.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given a supported history, the label law is a finite sum of products of
the encoder's transition probabilities. The same row is used whenever
the preceding label and observed command/report agree, in particular on
the common failure report. Conditional orthogonality subtracts the
full-history Bayes risk, leaving precisely the displayed expression.
Conversely every listed stochastic row and readout is an admissible
private machine, so both infima are equal. Projecting readouts into the
coordinatewise range of the query means cannot increase squared error.
The feasible set is compact, and its objective is the maximum of finitely
many polynomials. Continuity proves attainment.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exponential criteria and hidden-phase control}
The same support calculation has a multiplicative form. Let $\gamma>0$
and let $C_{d\theta}$ be a bounded cumulative cost on the finite
trajectory, including a terminal decision. Put
\[
 M_j(\omega)=\sum_\theta\kappa_j(\theta)
                       \E_\theta^{\omega,d(j)}e^{\gamma C_{d(j)\theta}}.
\]
For task $d$ the risk is $\gamma^{-1}\log\max_{j:d(j)=d}M_j$;
a Bayesian task has its single prior row. Let $\beta_d^{\exp}$ denote
its corresponding full-history baseline.
\begin{corollary}[Risk-sensitive common-encoder duality]
\label{cor:v9-pressure}
The shared-seed excess under these exponential risks equals
\begin{equation}
 \Theta^{\exp}_{\rm sh}({\bf S};D)
 =\frac1\gamma\log\left[
   \max_{w\in\Delta_J}\min_{\omega\in\Omega_{\bf S}}
       \sum_jw_j e^{-\gamma\beta_{d(j)}^{\exp}}M_j(\omega)\right].
 \label{eq:v9-pressure}
\end{equation}
For additive costs, the inner minimum is computed by
\eqref{eq:v9-occupancy}--\eqref{eq:v9-bellman} after replacing the
transition by $K_{\theta,t}e^{\gamma c_{d\theta,t}}$ and the terminal
loss by $e^{\gamma c_{d\theta,T}}$. The initial row weights are multiplied
by $e^{-\gamma\beta_d^{\exp}}$. These are nonnegative occupancies,
not normalized posterior probabilities.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a mixture of designs, the exponential moment is
$\sum_\omega\lambda_\omega M_j(\omega)$. It is \emph{not} the exponential
of an average of logarithmic risks. Since logarithm is increasing,
subtracting the baseline and maximizing rows gives
\[
 \frac1\gamma\log\max_j
       \left[e^{-\gamma\beta_{d(j)}^{\exp}}
                      \sum_\omega\lambda_\omega M_j(\omega)\right].
\]
Minimize over $\lambda$ and apply finite minimax to the bracketed
positive moment table. This proves \eqref{eq:v9-pressure}.
Expanding the product of transition weights proves the Feynman--Kac
occupancy assertion by the same induction as before. Positivity, not
normalization, is what that induction requires.
\end{proof}

For example, let an unobserved phase $\theta\in\{0,1\}$ have probabilities
$1/2,1/2$, provide no report, and charge $C=\1_{\{a\ne\theta\}}$.
Every observation-admissible action, randomized or not, has moment
$(1+e^\gamma)/2$ and risk
$\gamma^{-1}\log((1+e^\gamma)/2)>0$. Optimizing separately within each
phase would instead give zero. The single $a^d(i)$ in the occupancy
recursion forbids precisely that exchange. This supplies a finite-model
resolution of the common-policy issue arising in phase mixtures; it does
not presume that a latent phase decomposition of a microscopic system
has already been constructed.
