\section{Controlled common encoders and the minimax risk body}
\label{sec:v9-minimax}
The causal constraint is a constraint on the encoder, not on each task
separately. We now give a variational characterization with this constraint
inside the feasible set. The distinction between a shared random seed and
private fresh randomization is essential to the statement.

\subsection{Finite instruments and the information pattern}
Fix a horizon $T$, finite parameter set $K$, finite hidden spaces $Z_t$,
report alphabets $Y_t$, and a finite control alphabet $A$. The instrument
is specified by probability vectors $\nu_\theta$ and stochastic matrices
\[
 K_{\theta,t}(z',y\mid z,a),\qquad 0\le t<T.
\]
The hidden variable is not available to either the encoder or the
controller. A finite collection $D$ of tasks has finite terminal action
sets $B_d$ and losses $\ell_{d\theta}(z,b)\in[0,1]$. Any bounded loss on a
finite trajectory, including losses at several decision times, is of this
form after adjoining that trajectory to the hidden state. This enlargement
is a description of the experiment, not memory supplied to its controller.

The encoder has widths ${\bf S}=(S_0,\ldots,S_T)$, with $S_0\ge1$. A parameter-independent initialization law chooses
the initial label before the first control; this label is visible to
the controller and is charged in $S_0$. Deterministic initialization can
be labelled $1$ by relabelling states. Before transition $t$, the task-$d$ controller chooses
$a$ from its current label $i$. The encoder then observes $(a,y)$ and
updates the label. At the last time, a task-$d$ terminal rule chooses $b$.
The encoder's update table is the \emph{same} for all tasks and parameters;
the controller's tables may depend on $d$ but not on $\theta$. The current
control is an input to the ensuing update, not a retained action history.
Task-dependent restrictions on legal controls may be imposed on the
controller tables. The controller has no additional persistent register.

For a minimax task retain one row $j=(d,\theta)$ per parameter. For a
Bayesian task retain one row $j$ with its specified prior $\kappa_j$ on
$K$. More generally a row has a task $d(j)$ and a probability vector
$\kappa_j$; point masses give the minimax rows. Let $J$ be this finite row
set, $M=|J|$, and let $\beta_d$ be the full-history optimal risk for the
\emph{whole} task $d$. In particular, the baseline of a minimax task is
not a separate parameterwise optimum. Write $b_j=\beta_{d(j)}$.

A \emph{deterministic design} $\omega$ consists of one family of updates
$f_t(i,a,y)\in[S_{t+1}]$, together with all controller tables
$a_t^d(i)\in A$ and terminal tables $b^d(i)\in B_d$. Let
$\Omega_{\bf S}$ be the finite set of these designs. Its raw risk vector
is denoted $R(\omega)=(R_j(\omega))_{j\in J}$, where
\begin{equation}
 R_j(\omega)=\sum_{\theta}\kappa_j(\theta)
       \E_\theta^{\omega,d(j)}\ell_{d(j)\theta}(Z_T,b^{d(j)}(I_T)).
 \label{eq:v9-riskrows}
\end{equation}
The expectation uses the actual closed-loop law. In particular, controls
are not optimized separately inside hidden-parameter components.

There are two randomization conventions. In the \emph{shared-seed}
convention an independent variable is available to the encoder and the
task controller throughout the run. It is chosen independently of the
task and parameter; the risk averages its realization before taking the
supremum over tasks or parameters. The adversary does not select a new
parameter after seeing that realization. In the \emph{private}
convention there is no such retained shared variable. Each declared
kernel uses fresh randomness, with conditional independence given its
allowed inputs. Any seed remembered across updates is charged in the
label. We write $\Theta_{\rm sh}$ and $\Theta_{\rm pr}$ for these two
specified slices of \eqref{eq:v8-spectrum}. The term shared seed here
has the precise meaning of the public independent seed permitted in
Section~\ref{sec:v8-spectrum}; it never contains data from the experiment.

\subsection{The variational theorem}
Define the upper risk body and its lower support function by
\begin{align}
 \mathcal U_E({\bf S};J)
   &=\operatorname{co}\{R(\omega):\omega\in\Omega_{\bf S}\}
                                      +\R_+^J,\label{eq:v9-body}\\
 h_E({\bf S};w)&=\min_{\omega\in\Omega_{\bf S}}w\cdot R(\omega),
                 \qquad w\in\Delta_J.\label{eq:v9-support}
\end{align}
Here $\Delta_J$ is the probability simplex. The body is defined from
controlled execution risks, not from a presumed predictive quotient.

\begin{theorem}[Controlled common-encoder minimax theorem]
\label{thm:v9-minimax}
For the finite instrument and information pattern just specified:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The shared-seed attainable upper risk set is exactly
$\mathcal U_E({\bf S};J)$. The common-encoder excess satisfies
\begin{align}
 \Theta_{\rm sh}({\bf S};D)
 &=\min_{\lambda\in\Delta(\Omega_{\bf S})}
       \max_{j\in J}\left\{\sum_\omega\lambda_\omega
                                      R_j(\omega)-b_j\right\}
                                                        \label{eq:v9-primal}\\
 &=\max_{w\in\Delta_J}\{h_E({\bf S};w)-w\cdot b\}.
                                                        \label{eq:v9-dual}
\end{align}
An optimum uses at most $M+1$ deterministic designs. Thus the encoder is
chosen once, before the task, in both formulae.
\item The functions $h_E({\bf S};\cdot)$ determine the whole upper risk
body. For two matched task/row systems,
\[
 \mathcal U_F({\bf S}';J)\subseteq\mathcal U_E({\bf S};J)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 h_E({\bf S};w)\le h_F({\bf S}';w)\quad(w\in\Delta_J).
\]
In particular equality of all these support functions is equivalent to
equality of the attainable upper bodies. Relabelling histories, hidden
states or machine labels changes neither assertion.
\item For constant width $S$, cardinality alone being charged,
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_{\rm pr}((M+1)S;D)
       \le\Theta_{\rm sh}(S;D)\le\Theta_{\rm pr}(S;D).
 \label{eq:v9-seedprice}
\end{equation}
The first inequality is implemented by retaining the design index in
the actual register, including the permitted randomized initialization.
If a different signature forbids randomized initialization, this
inequality refers to its explicitly initialization-enlarged signature. For a fixed finite row
family, any existing positive cardinality exponent is consequently the
same under these two randomization conventions.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First include the decoder and controller tables in the design. For one
fixed common encoder their choices for distinct tasks are independent
optimization variables, whereas the same choice for one task must work
for all its parameter rows. Compactness of the finite products of
probability simplexes implies attainment. Hence minimizing jointly over
the common encoder and all its task rules gives exactly the original
order $\inf_Q\max_d(r_E(Q,d)-\beta_d)$; it does not give a decoder
indexed by the unknown parameter.

Realize every update and controller kernel by a uniform variable at its
specified time, independent of the instrument. For a shared-seed scheme,
condition on the entire shared seed and these variables. One obtains a
deterministic common update family and one deterministic controller for
each task. Although different tasks then generate different observation
laws, the frozen maps are defined on \emph{all} their possible inputs.
They therefore form one element of $\Omega_{\bf S}$. Its conditional
risk is computed under the corresponding controlled instrument, not
under an incorrectly frozen uncontrolled history law. Averaging gives a
convex combination of the finite vectors $R(\omega)$.

Conversely sample a design index with probabilities $\lambda$, expose
that independent index to encoder and controller, and execute its tables.
The encoder law is independent of which task will be requested. Its risk
vector is exactly $\sum\lambda_\omega R(\omega)$. Taking coordinatewise
upper bounds proves \eqref{eq:v9-body} and \eqref{eq:v9-primal}.
Finite-dimensional convex minimax gives \eqref{eq:v9-dual}. Equivalently,
the primal is the linear programme minimizing $v$ subject to
$\lambda\ge0$, $\sum\lambda=1$, and
$\sum\lambda_\omega R_j(\omega)\le v+b_j$ for every row.
Carath\'eodory's theorem in $\R^M$ gives support at most $M+1$ without
changing the optimum risk vector. This argument freezes a tape to form
its \emph{distribution of designs}; it never moves a worst-task
supremum through that distribution.

The body in \eqref{eq:v9-body} is closed and convex. If a point lies
outside it, finite-dimensional separation supplies a linear functional
whose infimum on the body exceeds its value at the point. The functional
must be nonnegative on every coordinate direction, because the body
contains every positive ray starting at one of its points. Normalizing
its coefficients places it in $\Delta_J$. Its infimum is
\eqref{eq:v9-support}. This proves the comparison assertion, including
both directions of equality. A relabelling gives a bijection of the
same execution laws and of their deterministic designs.

Every private scheme is also a shared-seed scheme that ignores its
extra input. Conversely an optimal shared scheme can use $k\le M+1$
designs. Sample its index privately before the first observation and
retain $(r,I_t)$, which has at most $kS$ values. Each subsequent update,
control and readout uses this actual pair. This has the same law as the
shared scheme; no external transcript or hidden seed is used. This
proves \eqref{eq:v9-seedprice}. Monotonicity, together with
$\log((M+1)S)/\log S\to1$, proves the exponent assertion. The constant
$M+1$ is not uniform over growing or infinite task families.
\end{proof}

The theorem identifies a finite controlled spectrum by one variational
functional. It is more than the transport of a supplied simulation:
its feasible set is constructed from the instrument and the prescribed
memory, and the converse recovers every admissible execution. Convex
minimax, separation and finite mixed strategies are classical tools.
Their use here does not reassign their priority. The substantive
information restriction is that all tasks share $f$, while all parameters
within a task share its controller, and every retained design index is
accounted for when the shared-seed convention is removed.

\subsection{Controlled continuation partitions}
Let $\mathcal H_t$ be the full finite tree of action--report histories,
including histories null under a particular parameter or task. Histories
null under every possible execution can be assigned arbitrary updates.
A family of partitions $\mathcal P_t$ is controlled successor compatible
when
\begin{equation}
 h\sim_t h'\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
             hay\sim_{t+1}h'ay\qquad(a\in A,\ y\in Y_{t+1}).
 \label{eq:v9-controlledcongruence}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Intrinsic feasible partitions]\label{prop:v9-partitions}
Deterministic common encoders with widths ${\bf S}$ are, up to label
names and unused states, exactly the partition families satisfying
\eqref{eq:v9-controlledcongruence} and $|\mathcal P_t|\le S_t$.
A deterministic design adds, for each task, a control constant on every
current cell and a terminal decision constant on every terminal cell.
Thus the risk body and both sides of \eqref{eq:v9-dual} can equivalently
be defined on controlled continuation partitions with these cellwise
rules.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equal machine states have equal successors on the same action and
report, proving necessity. Conversely label the cells and send $(C,a,y)$
to the cell containing $hay$ for any $h\in C$. Condition
\eqref{eq:v9-controlledcongruence} makes this independent of $h$.
The cellwise controller rule supplies the legal control, and induction
identifies the actual label with the actual history's cell for every
parameter and task. Defining the maps on the whole tree ensures that an
action absent under one task has not made its update task dependent.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Private randomization is a different finite problem}
For completeness there is also an exact, nonconvex description without
a shared seed. Write $g_t^d(a\mid i)$ for the private controller,
$q_t(i'\mid i,a,y)$ for the common private update, and
$v^d(b\mid i)$ for its terminal rule. Let $q_0$ be a parameter-independent
initialization law when randomized initialization is permitted. Then
\begin{align}
 p_0^{d\theta}(z,i)&=\nu_\theta(z)q_0(i),\nonumber\\
 p_{t+1}^{d\theta}(z',i')
 &=\sum_{z,i,a,y}p_t^{d\theta}(z,i)g_t^d(a\mid i)
       K_{\theta,t}(z',y\mid z,a)q_t(i'\mid i,a,y).
 \label{eq:v9-privateflow}
\end{align}
The risk of row $j$ is
\[
 \sum_{\theta,z,i,b}\kappa_j(\theta)p_T^{d(j)\theta}(z,i)
                    v^{d(j)}(b\mid i)\ell_{d(j)\theta}(z,b).
\]
\begin{proposition}[Private flow characterization]\label{prop:v9-private}
Minimizing the largest baseline-subtracted row risk over the probability
simplexes in \eqref{eq:v9-privateflow} gives exactly
$\Theta_{\rm pr}({\bf S};D)$. The minimum is attained. With rational input
this is a finite polynomial optimization problem, not in general the
linear programme \eqref{eq:v9-primal}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Induction on the actual execution gives the displayed occupancies and
risk. Conversely their kernels implement precisely that law with no
extra retained variable. The variables form a compact product of finite
simplexes, and every row risk is a polynomial in them. Continuity gives
attainment. The shared occurrences of $q_t$ cannot be split by task or
parameter; doing so would change the information pattern.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A strict cost for hiding the seed]\label{ex:v9-privategap}
Observe $X=\theta\in\{1,2,3\}$ without error, retain two labels, and
finally guess $\theta$ with zero--one loss under worst-parameter risk.
The full-information baseline is zero. With a shared seed, choose
uniformly one of the three two-element subsets and encode the two
selected symbols without error. The omitted symbol is necessarily
wrong. Each parameter is omitted with probability $1/3$, so the risk is
$1/3$. For any fixed seed, the sum of the three correct-reconstruction
probabilities of a two-label experiment is at most two; averaging proves
the matching lower bound.

Without a shared seed let $r_1,r_2$ be the two decoder distributions on
three symbols. Correctness for symbol $x$ is at most
$\max\{r_1(x),r_2(x)\}$. If all three correctness probabilities exceeded
$1/2$, one of two probability vectors would exceed $1/2$ at two
coordinates, an impossibility. Thus the private risk is at least $1/2$.
It is attained by assigning symbol $1$ to the first label and symbols
$2,3$ to the second, whose decoder tosses a fair coin. Therefore
\[
                  \Theta_{\rm sh}(2)=\tfrac13,
             \qquad\Theta_{\rm pr}(2)=\tfrac12.
\]
The gap is not removed by allowing randomized private encoding. A
single unknown parameter is selected before the seed, as required above.
\end{example}
