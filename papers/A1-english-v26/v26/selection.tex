\section{Selection and separating sets}\label{sec:v26-selection}
The loss incurred by a selected experiment cannot in general be bounded
by conditioning on that selection. We instead keep a common
subprobability measure and bound its mass on small-loss events. The
following elementary form records the dependence on dimension and on
the number of choices.

\begin{lemma}[Finite selection]\label{lem:v26-finite-selection}
Let $L_1,\ldots,L_q$ be nonnegative random variables, $q\ge1$, and
let $\mathcal A$ have probability $\beta>0$. Suppose the measurable
sets $E_i$, $1\le i\le K$, cover $\mathcal A$, and that, for $t\ge0$,
\[
 \Prob\{\mathcal A\cap E_i,\ \max_jL_j\le t\}
       \le a_i t^{k_i/2},\qquad a_i>0,\quad k_i\ge1.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-finite-tail}
 \max_j\E L_j\ \ge\ \frac1q\int_0^\infty
              \left[\beta-\sum_{i=1}^K a_i t^{k_i/2}\right]_+\,dt.
\end{equation}
In particular, if all $k_i=k$ and $A=\sum_i a_i$, the right side is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-homogeneous-selection}
 \frac{k}{q(k+2)}\,\beta^{1+2/k}A^{-2/k}.
\end{equation}
No regularity or independence of the sets $E_i$ is assumed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $Z=\max_jL_j$. The union bound gives
$\Prob(\mathcal A,Z\le t)\le\sum_i a_i t^{k_i/2}$, hence
$\Prob(Z>t)\ge[\beta-\sum_i a_i t^{k_i/2}]_+$.
Integrate this inequality using Tonelli's theorem and use
$q\max_j\E L_j\ge\E\sum_jL_j\ge\E Z$.
For the last assertion the positive integrand is supported on
$0\le t\le(\beta/A)^{2/k}$; its integral is
$\beta(\beta/A)^{2/k}-A(\beta/A)^{1+2/k}/(1+k/2)$.
This equals the expression in \eqref{eq:v26-homogeneous-selection}
after division by $q$.
\end{proof}

A path need not be distinguished by its complete trace when its
prediction centres are already determined by a smaller state of the
observation protocol. Separating sets express this distinction.

Let $\mathscr D$ be a finite directed graph with levels
$0,1,\ldots,q+1$, where $q\ge1$, a unique source $o$ at level zero and a unique sink
$z$ at level $q+1$. Every arc joins consecutive levels. Assume that
there is an $o$--$z$ path and that every vertex belongs to such a path.
An \emph{internal separator} is a set of internal vertices meeting
every $o$--$z$ path. Denote the family of these sets by
$\operatorname{Sep}(\mathscr D)$.

\begin{theorem}[A separator bound for adaptive selection]
\label{thm:v26-network-selection}
On one probability space let a measurable rule select an $o$--$z$
path and nonnegative losses $L_1,\ldots,L_q$. Let
$\Prob(\mathcal A)=\beta>0$. For each internal vertex $x$ at level
$j(x)$ suppose a nondecreasing function $c_x:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-node-smallball}
 \Prob\{\mathcal A,\ x\text{ is visited},\ L_{j(x)}\le t\}
                  \le\beta c_x(t).
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-separator-function}
 H_{\mathscr D}(t)=\min_{C\in\operatorname{Sep}(\mathscr D)}
                                  \sum_{x\in C}c_x(t).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-separator-tail}
 \max_{1\le j\le q}\E L_j\ \ge\
       \frac{\beta}{q}\int_0^\infty[1-H_{\mathscr D}(t)]_+\,dt.
\end{equation}
The conclusion holds with the same right side after averaging
independent coding seeds whenever \eqref{eq:v26-node-smallball}
holds with these functions for every fixed seed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $C\in\operatorname{Sep}(\mathscr D)$. Every selected path visits
some $x\in C$. On the event $\mathcal A\cap\{\max_jL_j\le t\}$
that vertex also satisfies $L_{j(x)}\le t$. Thus the probability of
this event is at most $\beta\sum_{x\in C}c_x(t)$ by
\eqref{eq:v26-node-smallball}. Minimize over the finite family of
separators. The resulting tail bound for $\max_jL_j$, followed by the
same integration and averaging as in Lemma~\ref{lem:v26-finite-selection},
proves \eqref{eq:v26-separator-tail}. For independent seeds, apply the
bound to the sum of the losses with each seed fixed, and then integrate
the seeds before dividing by $q$. Independence leaves the probability
of $\mathcal A$ unchanged. Neither step conditions the acquisition law
on the selected path.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A flow certificate]\label{prop:v26-flow}
For fixed $t$, $H_{\mathscr D}(t)$ is the value of a finite
maximum-flow problem with internal vertex capacities $c_x(t)$.
In particular it can be certified without listing all paths.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Split each internal vertex into an entrance and an exit, joined by an
arc of capacity $c_x(t)$. Replace every original arc by an arc between
the appropriate exits and entrances, of capacity
$\Lambda=1+\sum_xc_x(t)$. Source and sink are not split.
A separator gives a cut of capacity at most the sum of its vertex
capacities. Conversely, a minimum cut cannot use a $\Lambda$-arc:
cutting all vertices at any one internal level costs less than
$\Lambda$. Its split arcs therefore specify an internal separator.
The minimum cut value is consequently \eqref{eq:v26-separator-function}.

For completeness, the flow--cut equality here holds also for real
capacities. The feasible flow set is a nonempty compact polytope, so a
maximum exists. Its residual network has no source--sink path, since
augmenting by the least positive residual capacity along such a path
would increase the flow. The vertices reachable from the source in the
residual network define a cut. Every forward cut arc is saturated and
every reverse cut arc carries zero flow. Conservation therefore equates
the cut capacity to the flow value. The opposite inequality follows by
summing conservation on the source side of any cut. This is the
classical flow--cut argument \cite{FordFulkerson1956}; the statistical
input is \eqref{eq:v26-node-smallball}, not a new flow theorem.
\end{proof}
