We thank the referee for distinguishing the validity of the audited formulas from the information-category and global-scope objections. We keep the intrinsic endpoint-law problem and its full local inverse. Our principal response is a new geometric completeness theorem: all clear shortest normal channels in a sufficiently large finite range generate a connected lifted graph, hence the entire period group. The inverse therefore no longer needs a designed covering primitive network. A second theorem replaces a primitive observed pair by the integer group generated by all cycles. The finite-index ambiguity for partial data remains valid and prominent. We do not treat the referee's editorial judgment as a false theorem or claim that finite checks decide journal significance.

## 1. Section 5.1: the exact meaning of unlabelled

Section 1.1 specifies the local origin, isolated three-impact return branch, source orientation, absolute-time pairing and single coherent reversal. The abstract and Theorems 1.4 and 5.6 say that the catalogue is unlabelled **between records**. Definition 5.3 makes completeness a requirement on acquisition and permits both return orientations for every geometric edge. No claim of an unmarked collision-count or marked-length invariant is made.

## 2. Section 5.2: local and global hypotheses

The smooth local inverse remains Theorem 1.1, with its physical stability hypotheses in Proposition 2.4. Analyticity, asymmetry and distinct shapes are explicitly confined to whole-image recovery and inter-record identification. The geometric completeness theorem, Theorem 5.2, is stronger in a different direction: it holds for every periodic family of disjoint strictly convex bodies, including disks and congruent shapes. It proves connectivity and period generation, not shape identifiability in the symmetric or repeated-shape cases. The global inverse retains its stated shape assumptions rather than hiding them in generic terminology.

## 3. Section 5.3: coverage and unvisited obstacles

For arbitrary selected collections, Section 1 now explicitly assumes that the number of unvisited obstacle orbits is zero. For a complete range-R catalogue, Theorem 5.2 proves coverage when R is greater than twice the covering radius of the obstacle union. Its proof first uses connectedness of a cover by obstacle neighborhoods, then replaces every obstructed shortest bridge by a chain of shorter clear bridges. Periodicity supplies finitely many relevant gap types, so the descent terminates, including at third-body tangencies.

Completeness of the supplied catalogue is not statistically tested from a list with unknown omissions. A known covering-radius upper bound gives a sufficient finite acquisition range, but no channel-discovery or completeness-certification algorithm is claimed. The theorem replaces a designed connected-coverage hypothesis by a uniform geometric acquisition condition; it does not reconstruct unobserved obstacles from area alone.

## 4. Section 5.4: a primitive pair is no longer required

Theorem 5.4 computes the index q of the group generated by **all** recovered cycles. It is the gcd of their pair determinants divided by the recovered covolume. Its proof gives a finite integer-basis construction and reduces the candidate bound from sigma_1(n) for one arbitrary pair to sigma_1(q) for the entire collection. The case q=1 yields uniqueness even when no pair is a basis.

Proposition 5.5 realizes this distinction physically with cycle vectors (1,0), (1,2), (-1,3) on a small asymmetric analytic obstacle table. The three pair indices are 2, 3, 5; their gcd is one. Thus the new hypothesis strictly extends the earlier primitive-pair class. For a complete catalogue, Theorem 5.2 proves q=1 by lifting paths from a root copy to each of its translates. Theorem 5.6 then gives whole-table rigidity with no primitive-pair assumption at all.

The earlier prime-index ambiguity is unchanged as Proposition 3.5. Those selected two-record collections cannot be complete at a range above the covering threshold: further short clear channels must exist. This is a consequence of the new theorem, not a claim that the old physical ambiguity was spurious.

## 6. Section 5.6: mathematical recovery versus efficient algorithms

No noisy real vector is declared an exact integer generator.

## 8. Section 6: conceptual objection and statistical scope

The new global result is a geometric principle rather than only a larger list of shape matches. A uniform finite-range condition forces the lifted normal-channel graph to be connected; path lifting gives every period, and all-cycle saturation removes the unnecessary two-generator selection. This directly addresses the designed primitive-network limitation while remaining within the original endpoint-law topic. Local lens equivalence remains explicit before the first rigidity theorem, and the existing theorem-specific metric/scattering comparisons remain unchanged.

Theorem 5.8 extends the finite-histogram result to saturated cycle groups. Bounded physical candidates have uniformly bounded integer coordinate arrays; the existing integer-locking lemma makes those arrays identical at sufficiently small error, so a common integer basis produces nearby period bases. The prior presentation bound is explicitly enlarged to contain these bases. Complete-catalogue classes retain analytic, physical, shape/symmetry, onset and cutoff/clearance margins. The rate is conditional on that class, not minimax or universal. The existence of the estimator is not a complexity claim. Neither a count-only analytic theorem nor a new priority conclusion is inferred.
