\section{Observation and the completion problem}\label{sec:setting}
A collection of local inverse data can reconstruct every object it sees
and still fail to determine the ambient periodic table. It may omit an
obstacle orbit, or its observed cycles may generate a proper subgroup
of the translation lattice. The issue is not merely how to assemble
known pieces, but how to know from the data that no piece or period is
missing. Absolute probability normalization provides a way to answer
both questions together.

\subsection{Physical records}
Let
\[
 \mathcal O=\bigcup_{i=1}^r\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(C_i+\lambda)
\]
be a periodic family of disjoint compact strictly convex planar bodies
with positively curved smooth boundaries and a full-rank lattice
$\Lambda$. The free cell area is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:area-budget}
 A=\covol\Lambda-\sum_{i=1}^r\area(C_i)>0.
\end{equation}
A selected normal channel is an isolated clear shortest segment between
two physical copies. In its return branch the first, intermediate and
last impacts have types $b,o,b$. Both first and last positions are
measured by intrinsic arclength $s,t$ from the source normal contact.
One coherent reversal $(s,t)\mapsto(-s,-t)$ is allowed for the entire
record. No comparison of origins or signs across records is supplied.

Initial phase has speed one and normalized cell volume
$\dd q\dd\vartheta/(2\pi A)$. Let $\mu_T$ be the subprobability law
of $(s,t)$ for exactly the selected three impacts before absolute time
$T$. Failure is a separate outcome. An exact record contains two
complete density functions $f_1,f_2$ on a fixed rectangle strictly
inside the active support at $T_2>T_1$. It is not two real numbers.
Finite acquisition records two increasingly fine histograms, including
failures and endpoints outside the rectangle. The times, return branch,
local origin and paired-record grouping remain marks.

All records use the same absolute cell normalization. They are not
conditioned on successful returns, nor renormalized to an acquisition
aperture. This convention is essential: the area $A$ below includes
unobserved bodies. Section~\ref{sec:acquisition} separates production
of genuine local records from certification of their collective sufficiency.

\begin{theorem}[The retained intrinsic local inverse]\label{thm:local-main}
For a smooth isolated normal channel, two strictly active endpoint
density functions determine the two reflecting boundary germs in their
relative placement, the gap and the free area, up to a common Euclidean
isometry. The stationary action and area are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-main}
 W=\frac{T_1f_2-T_2f_1}{f_2-f_1},\qquad
 A=\frac{(T_2-T_1)(-W_{st})}{2\pi(f_2-f_1)}.
\end{equation}
On the physical $C^3$ classes with all active, twist, time and geometric
margins specified in Proposition~\ref{prop:local-stable}, the inverse
is Lipschitz from the two $C^2$ densities to the two intrinsic $C^2$
arcs. No increasing contact-jet order or analyticity assumption is
needed for this local assertion.
\end{theorem}

Section~\ref{sec:local} retains the complete local proof. Whole-image
recovery will use analyticity. We call the global table \emph{shape
separated} if its representatives $C_i$ are pairwise noncongruent,
and \emph{asymmetric} if each has trivial Euclidean isometry group.
These are assumptions on the unknown table, not a supplied shape catalogue.
On this class $\Lambda$ is the full translation group of $\mathcal O$:
a translation preserving the union preserves a body's shape type, and
a compact body admits no nonzero translational symmetry.

\subsection{Three principal conclusions}
Recover the complete pair image of each analytic record, then identify
vertices by congruence of individual bodies. Keep every record as an
edge, including loops, repetitions and reverse returns. No edge-key
classification or deduplication is required. A connected component of
this observable multigraph gives placed body representatives $C_i$ for
its visible types $i\in I$, and cycle displacements $d_e$. Write
\[
 \Gamma=\sum_e\Z d_e,\qquad S_I=\sum_{i\in I}\area(C_i).
\]
The constructions and their invariance are proved in
Section~\ref{sec:completion}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact certificate of whole-table completeness]
\label{thm:completion-main}
Let a finite collection of genuine two-density records come from an
analytic asymmetric shape-separated periodic table. No obstacle count,
coverage, connected witness, copy labels or period basis is supplied.
For any connected observed component with $\rank\Gamma=2$, the
quantity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-main}
 \mathcal D=\covol\Gamma-A-S_I
       =([\Lambda:\Gamma]-1)\covol\Lambda
                         +\sum_{i\notin I}\area(C_i)
\end{equation}
is nonnegative. It vanishes if and only if that component visits every
obstacle orbit and generates the full period group. If it vanishes,
the records determine the entire periodic table and its full unmarked
translation lattice, up to one Euclidean isometry and permutation.
A positive defect or a rank-deficient component is not certified as
complete. Arbitrary omissions and repetitions do not create false
positive exact certificates within the stated physical class.
\end{theorem}

The identity does not require the unknown right side to be evaluated.
The left side uses only recovered data. It is precisely the unknown
area of unseen bodies that prevents the earlier calibration
$A+S_I=\covol\Lambda$ from being used before coverage is established.

\begin{theorem}[Reference-free noisy certification]\label{thm:uniform-main}
On the uniform analytic physical classes of Section~\ref{sec:uniform},
there are constants $C,e_0>0$ and $0<\theta<1$ such that the following
holds. For any finite list of genuine records, if its fitted endpoint
densities have simultaneous $C^2$ error at most $e<e_0$, one can recover
its visible incidence and cycle arithmetic and compute a defect estimate
with error at most $Ce^\theta$. A fixed gap test certifies exactly those
connected components that cover the whole table and generate its full
period group. On acceptance the reconstructed table has error at most
$Ce^\theta$ in simultaneous $C^2$ support-function and period-basis errors, modulo
common Euclidean motions and representative choices.
No reference table, witness-key neighborhoods, retained witness, or
integer edge labels are inputs. The class has explicit positive body-area
and covolume bounds, as well as analytic, shape, symmetry and local
physical margins. It is not the unrestricted class of analytic tables.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Finite histograms and an area-testing scale]
\label{thm:stat-main}
Under the same priors, two endpoint histograms and a fixed pilot per
record, with $N$ fresh phase preparations per queried time, give the
certificate and conditional whole-table error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-main}
 C\left\{\frac{\log(CJN/\delta)}{N}\right\}^{\beta\theta/(2\beta+6)},
 \qquad 0<\beta\le1,
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-\delta$, once the deterministic small-error
threshold holds. Here $J$ is the number of records and all preparations,
including failures, are counted. The density regularity is
$C^{2,\beta}$, with a uniform bound. This is a conditional upper bound,
not a whole-table minimax exponent.

In a separate physical subexperiment with fixed observed arcs and finitely
many fixed active windows, detecting an unobserved obstacle of area $a$
has sharp scale $a\asymp N^{-1/2}$ at fixed testing error. This statement
is about the missing-area decision, not reconstruction of every boundary
or the exact analytic count-only inverse.
\end{theorem}

The present emphasis is certification from the observed normalization,
rather than another assumption of exhaustive acquisition. The canonical
scanner and persistence theorems remain valid and are retained in full.
They supply sufficient data when their own geometric access and margins
are available; they are not prerequisites for the positive certificate.
