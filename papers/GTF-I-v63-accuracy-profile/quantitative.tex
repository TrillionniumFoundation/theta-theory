\begin{abstract}
We study finite-label stochastic realizations of compact group experiments,
allowing a new clock-dependent realization at every horizon. For a
symmetric $2r$-command action on $S^p$ whose full Koopman average has
Ramanujan norm at most $2\sqrt{2r-1}/(2r)$, we determine the exponential
accuracy--width rate. Uniformly below any fixed subcritical error,
the width lies between $\min\{(2r-1)^N,((N+1)/\varepsilon)^{p/2}\}$
and that quantity times $\exp[-O(\sqrt{N\log(N+2)})]$, up to a constant
in the upper bound. The converse also bounds the number of narrow cuts
with arbitrary intervening registers. Its proof combines classical
nonbacktracking averages with a logarithmic entropy defect and transport
paid by loss of conditional-centroid mass; no idle command or return
condition is used. For a fixed rational six-rotation Bloch experiment
with legal density-matrix outputs, error $\varepsilon_N=\exp(-aN+o(N))$
has width $\exp((\min\{a,\log5\}+o(1))N)$. Its exact cut profile remains
$5^t$. The previously established matching subexponential-accuracy laws,
compact-orbit bounds, and sparse rational and dyadic constructions are
proved in the same article. The LPS spectral input is imported; the
primary label-width invariant does not charge real tables, the clock,
exact arithmetic, or exact sampling.
\end{abstract}
