The least-orbit proof replaces a separately assumed all-centroid small-ball estimate by the minimum infinitesimal orbit rank, for every fixed-point-free finite-dimensional representation of a compact connected Lie group. It uses local submersions and compactness, not a new spectral-gap theorem. The upper orbit-hull geometry and the logarithmic lower smoothing step are inherited and remain credited as such. The logarithmic gap is not declared closed.

The rational experiment specifies two 2-by-2 unitary matrices and one pure density matrix with rational entries. A six-matrix computation modulo five underlies a written proof for all reduced words, rather than numerical tests being extrapolated to infinite freeness. The auxiliary third quaternion proves that the fixed rational seed has trivial stabilizer. Extreme-output rigidity then gives the exact profile, while rational separation of Bloch vectors gives the robust positive-error interval. The imported Bourgain–Gamburd theorem is used only for the fixed-positive-error converse, on the explicitly displayed dense algebraic alphabet.

The rational compiler is a separate constructive advance over rounding a supplied realization. It constructs a rational spherical net and its sparse common rows, proves legal positive decoders, and gives a full polynomial table-construction bound in the numerical horizon and inverse error. Its finite regression executes the actual compiler. The theorem is not a polynomial bound in the binary horizon length, not a minimum-width solver, and not a spectral-gap algorithm.
