## 2. Joint accuracy

The parameter is `tau=1-rho+epsilon`, not epsilon alone at arbitrary amplitude. With `zeta>=zeta0>0`, the lower inequality is `N<=C0+C1 log k+C2 tau k^(2/p)`. The upper machine is built at amplitude `beta=rho-epsilon/2`, for which `1-beta>=tau/2`; its common-row hull proof never needs an identity command. At epsilon zero this requires rho strictly below one.

For subexponential inverse tau, the upper bound forces `log k=o(N)`, allowing the initial logarithm to be absorbed before inverting the lower inequality. Constants are uniform in the sequence but the eventual starting horizon need not be. Pure amplitude and zero error have tau zero and are not covered by the polynomial upper argument. The full exponential crossover and optimal leading constants are not claimed.

## 4. Classical tail capacity

For an observation-tail event B, the conditional probability given the complete hidden/emission past is a function of the current label under common time-dependent kernels. Upward martingale convergence makes it converge to the indicator of B. For finitely many disjoint positive-probability events, at every sufficiently late time each has a label with conditional probability greater than one half. The labels must differ. Infinitely many deterministic k-width cuts therefore bound the number of such events by k.

This is a classical finite-state tail principle. The proof includes the emitted-tail convention explicitly; it allows irreversible kernels and nonhomogeneous tables. It makes no mixing-rate or positive-lower-mass assertion.

## 5. Uniform product-law converse

The finite-horizon instrument parameter space is a product of simplexes. Truncation proves monotonicity of the optimal error. If its limit were below one, nested compactness would give one infinite instrument satisfying all finite-word TV bounds at that same closed error. Cylinder approximation under the sum of the two measures extends the TV bound to infinite outputs.

Distinct iid laws have disjoint empirical-frequency tail events of full respective measure. Every approximating initial row therefore assigns positive probability to its corresponding event. Mixing initializations uniformly leaves common transition kernels and produces too many positive tail events for k labels. The initialization index is not an uncharged persistent selector: future kernels depend only on the current hidden label.

The resulting finite witness may be found by real quantifier elimination for rational data, but the search is not implemented here and no running-time bound is extracted. The exact binary example is checked separately and does not numerically certify the general compactness theorem.

## 6. Diagnostic cycle and arbitrary-width cuts

The existing common itinerary distinguishes all finite response types. Its command action is a permutation. Repeating it by the order of that permutation returns the response type, not necessarily the physical group element. Its first-itinerary marginal retains pairwise distinction. Fresh nondisturbing probes then make successive block laws iid; discarded probe fillers do not alter the type.

All representative prefixes are padded to a common length before the selected narrow cuts. The same deterministic diagnostic schedule is used for all representatives. After marginalizing prefix, filler, and suffix outputs, each actual composite block is a legal common nonnegative instrument between k-label cuts. No correctness conditional on a rare observed prefix, nor a per-step output approximation, is used. The finite-product witness excludes R+1 sufficiently separated narrow cuts; greedy counting yields `b+ell R`, excluding cut zero.

Below any fixed error strictly less than one, the exact n-type stationary machine is therefore eventually minimal. This does not impose n labels at every isolated interior cut at high error: the binary bottleneck example explicitly disproves that stronger statement. At error one the strong converse does not apply.
