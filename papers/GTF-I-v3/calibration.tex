\section{An exact calibration quotient and singular resolution}
\label{sec:v3-calibration}
The regenerative experiment concerns excess prediction loss relative to a
full-history observer. We now study squared error relative to an unknown
parameter. The distinction is essential: calibration uncertainty persists
even before any label constraint is imposed.

\subsection{Correlated paired measurements}
Let $\Theta$ be a compact metric space, let $f:\Theta\to\R^m$ be
continuous, and observe
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-pair}
 Y=f(\theta)+\eta+\varepsilon_Y,\qquad
 W=\eta+\varepsilon_W,
 \qquad \theta\in\Theta,\quad\eta\in\R^m.
\end{equation}
The joint centred Gaussian noise has a known positive definite covariance
\[
 \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{YY}&\Sigma_{YW}\\
                         \Sigma_{WY}&\Sigma_{WW}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
No independence between the two channels or their coordinates is imposed.
The nuisance $\eta$ is unrestricted and is not available to the observer.
Both measurements are part of the acquisition. The procedure retains one
of $M$ labels and decodes an action in a standard Borel action space;
independent public and private randomization is allowed.

Put
\begin{align}
 D&=Y-W,&
 V&=\Sigma_{YY}+\Sigma_{WW}-\Sigma_{YW}-\Sigma_{WY},\label{eq:v3-difference}\\
 C&=\Sigma_{WY}-\Sigma_{WW},& A&=CV^{-1},&
 R&=\Sigma_{WW}-CV^{-1}C^{\mathsf T}.\label{eq:v3-schur}
\end{align}
The matrices $V$ and $R$ are positive definite. A direct Gaussian
regression calculation gives the invertible transformation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-orthogonal-pair}
 (Y,W)\longmapsto (D,Z),\qquad Z=W-AD,
 \qquad
 D\sim N(f(\theta),V),\quad
 Z\sim N(\eta-Af(\theta),R),
\end{equation}
where $D$ and $Z$ are independent for fixed $(\theta,\eta)$.

For a bounded nonnegative Borel loss $\ell(\theta,a)\le L$, write
$\mathcal V_M^{\rm pair}$ for the minimax risk in
\eqref{eq:v3-pair}, with supremum over $(\theta,\eta)$, and
$\mathcal V_M^{D}$ for the minimax risk from the sole observation
$D\sim N(f(\theta),V)$, with the same action space, loss, label budget,
and randomization convention.

\begin{theorem}[Exact minimax elimination of additive calibration]
\label{thm:v3-quotient}
For every integer $M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-exact-quotient}
 \mathcal V_M^{\rm pair}=\mathcal V_M^D.
\end{equation}
Thus correlated measurement noise enters the decision problem through the
covariance of the observed difference, not through the two marginal
variances considered separately.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Taking differences implements every $D$-procedure on the paired experiment
without changing its label count, so
$\mathcal V_M^{\rm pair}\le\mathcal V_M^D$.
For the reverse inequality fix any paired procedure and any $T>0$.
Average its risk over an independent nuisance
$\eta\sim N(0,T^2I_m)$. For each $\theta$, this average is bounded
above by its worst-nuisance risk. Under this averaging,
\eqref{eq:v3-orthogonal-pair} has product law
\[
 N(f(\theta),V)\otimes N(-Af(\theta),T^2I_m+R).
\]
Replace the second factor by the parameter-independent law
$N(0,T^2I_m+R)$. The relative entropy between the two second factors
is at most $\norm{Af(\theta)}_2^2/(2T^2)$, so their total variation
distance is at most $\norm{Af(\theta)}_2/(2T)$. This follows also
when $A$ is singular. Since $f(\Theta)$ is compact, the bound is
uniform in $\theta$.

A $D$-encoder can sample this parameter-independent replacement $Z$
using private randomness, reconstruct $W=Z+AD$ and $Y=D+W$, and apply
the original paired encoder. Its decoder is unchanged and still sees
at most $M$ labels. Data processing and boundedness of the loss imply
that its maximum risk is at most the maximum paired risk plus
\[
 \frac L{2T}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\norm{Af(\theta)}_2.
\]
Let $T\to\infty$ and then take the infimum over paired procedures.
This proves the opposite inequality in \eqref{eq:v3-exact-quotient}.
\end{proof}

This is an equality of minimax decision problems for $\theta$ under an
unrestricted additive nuisance. It is not a sufficiency assertion for
$(\theta,\eta)$, nor an equivalence of the complete experiments for
estimating $\eta$. The distinction avoids identifying a nuisance-dependent
conditional law with a parameter-independent postprocessing kernel.

\subsection{Contact geometry in the noise metric}
Let now
\[
 \Theta=\prod_{j=1}^d[0,a_j],\qquad a_j>0,
 \qquad k_j\in\N,\ k_j\ge1,\qquad v_j>0.
\]
Assume that, for fixed constants $0<\lambda\le\Lambda<\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-contact-metric}
 \lambda\norm{b(\theta)-b(\theta')}_2
 \le\norm{V^{-1/2}(f(\theta)-f(\theta'))}_2
 \le\Lambda\norm{b(\theta)-b(\theta')}_2,
 \qquad b_j(\theta)=\theta_j^{k_j}/v_j.
\end{equation}
This is a two-sided condition on the actual observation map in the noise
metric. It is stronger than a rank statement at one point, but it allows
nonlinear, nonseparable embeddings of the contact coordinates. No
coordinate independence of the statistical experiment is required.

With $a_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge a_{(d)}$ the ordered widths, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-contact-profile}
 S=\sum_{j=1}^d\min\{a_j^2,v_j^{2/k_j}\},\qquad
 Q_M=\max_{1\le l\le d}
       \left(\frac{\prod_{i=1}^l a_{(i)}}M\right)^{2/l}.
\end{equation}
Write $\mathcal R_M$ for the minimax squared Euclidean parameter risk
in \eqref{eq:v3-pair}, with estimates projected onto $\Theta$.
Let $\omega_l$ denote the volume of the Euclidean unit ball in $\R^l$.

\begin{theorem}[Correlated, nonseparable contact and label law]
\label{thm:v3-contact}
Under \eqref{eq:v3-contact-metric}, for every integer $M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-contact-law}
 c_{d,\Lambda}(S+Q_M)\le\mathcal R_M
       \le 2K_{m,\lambda}S+8dQ_M,
 \qquad K_{m,\lambda}=\max\{1,4m/\lambda^2\}.
\end{equation}
One may take
\[
 c_{d,\Lambda}=\frac12\min\left\{
 \frac{3c^2}{32},\
 \min_{1\le l\le d}\frac12(2\omega_l)^{-2/l}\right\},
 \qquad c=\min\{1/2,1/(2\Lambda)\}.
\]
The comparison constants are independent of the contact orders, widths,
noise scales, label budget, and unknown calibration. A deterministic
minimum-distance estimate followed by one fixed product codebook attains
the upper bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The squared loss is bounded on $\Theta$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-quotient} reduces the minimax problem exactly to
$D\sim N(f(\theta),V)$.

\emph{Estimation.} Choose a measurable minimizer
\[
 \widetilde\theta\in\argmin_{u\in\Theta}
          \norm{V^{-1/2}(D-f(u))}_2.
\]
The minimum exists by compactness. For an explicit Borel selection,
subdivide the parameter box dyadically. At each level choose the first
closed child of the previously chosen box on which the minimum equals
the global minimum. Such a child exists. The minimum over any fixed
compact box is continuous in $D$, so all these finitely many equality
tests are Borel. The selected nested boxes have diameters tending to zero;
their unique limiting point is a Borel minimizer. For $G=V^{-1/2}(D-f(\theta))\sim N(0,I_m)$,
minimality and the triangle inequality imply
\[
 \norm{V^{-1/2}(f(\widetilde\theta)-f(\theta))}_2\le2\norm G_2.
\]
It follows from \eqref{eq:v3-contact-metric} and
$|x-y|^{k_j}\le|x^{k_j}-y^{k_j}|$ for nonnegative $x,y$ that
\[
 |\widetilde\theta_j-\theta_j|^2
 \le\min\{a_j^2,(2v_j\norm G_2/\lambda)^{2/k_j}\}.
\]
Since $\E\norm G_2^{2/k_j}\le m^{1/k_j}$, summing gives
$\sup_\theta\E\norm{\widetilde\theta-\theta}_2^2
\le K_{m,\lambda}S$.

\emph{Compression.} Set $\delta=2\sqrt{Q_M}$ and divide coordinate
$j$ into $n_j=\max\{1,\lfloor a_j/\delta\rfloor\}$ equal intervals.
Their product has at most $M$ cells: if $l$ widths exceed $\delta$,
then $\prod_j n_j\le\prod_{i\le l}a_{(i)}/\delta^l\le M/2^l$,
and the case $l=0$ uses one cell. Each side length is at most $2\delta$.
The midpoint codebook therefore has squared radius at most $4dQ_M$.
Quantize $\widetilde\theta$ to it. The squared triangle inequality
proves the upper bound in \eqref{eq:v3-contact-law}.

\emph{Statistical lower bound.} Set
$r_j=\min\{a_j,v_j^{1/k_j}\}$ and use the $2^d$ parameter vertices
$\theta_j(\xi)=cr_j\xi_j$, $\xi\in\{0,1\}^d$.
For adjacent vertices differing only in coordinate $j$,
\eqref{eq:v3-contact-metric} bounds their Gaussian relative entropy by
\[
 \tfrac12\Lambda^2(cr_j)^{2k_j}/v_j^2
       \le\tfrac12\Lambda^2c^{2k_j}\le\tfrac18.
\]
Their total variation distance is at most $1/4$. Thresholding an estimate
at $cr_j/2$ gives a test of bit $j$; an incorrect bit entails squared
error at least $c^2r_j^2/4$. Pairing adjacent vertices gives average
error probability at least $3/8$, even after compression. Summing over
$j$ yields a minimax lower bound $3c^2S/32$.

\emph{Representation lower bound.} Put uniform probability on $\Theta$.
For each independent public seed an averaged decoder has at most $M$
centres, whatever noisy observation produced its label. Projection onto
the $l$ widest coordinates shows that $M$ balls of radius $r$ cover at
most $M\omega_l r^l/\prod_{i\le l}a_{(i)}$ of the parameter mass.
With $r=(\prod_{i\le l}a_{(i)}/(2M\omega_l))^{1/l}$, at least half
the mass has squared error at least $r^2$. Maximizing over $l$ and
integrating over the seed gives
$\mathcal R_M\ge[\min_l\tfrac12(2\omega_l)^{-2/l}]Q_M$.
Combining this bound with the statistical bound proves the stated lower
constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Nonseparable contact observations]\label{ex:v3-coupled}
In two dimensions let
\[
 b(\theta)=(\theta_1^2/v_1,\theta_2^3/v_2),\qquad
 H(x_1,x_2)=(x_1,x_2+\alpha\sin x_1),\qquad
 f(\theta)=V^{1/2}H(b(\theta)).
\]
Both $H$ and its inverse have Lipschitz constant at most $1+|\alpha|$.
Thus \eqref{eq:v3-contact-metric} holds with
$\lambda=(1+|\alpha|)^{-1}$ and $\Lambda=1+|\alpha|$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-contact} gives a matched law with statistical term
$\min(a_1^2,v_1)+\min(a_2^2,v_2^{2/3})$ and the two-dimensional
anisotropic label floor, despite the coupling of the two mean coordinates.
For an actual correlated pair with any chosen positive definite $V$, take
independent Gaussian variables $\xi_D\sim N(0,V)$ and
$\xi_R\sim N(0,R)$, with $R>0$, and any matrix $A$. Set
$\varepsilon_W=A\xi_D+\xi_R$ and
$\varepsilon_Y=(I+A)\xi_D+\xi_R$. The joint noise covariance is
positive definite, and its observed difference has exactly covariance $V$.
\end{example}

For a scalar channel, correlation coefficient $\rho$ gives
$V=\sigma^2+\tau^2-2\rho\sigma\tau$. Ignoring the cross covariance
can therefore change the resolution scale. The independent-coordinate
experiment, including its zero-noise endpoints, remains
Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-singular}. The positive definite hypothesis here is
used for the exact Gaussian regression and does not replace those
previously proved endpoints.

The observation map and covariance in this section are specified parts
of the experiment. The unknown quantity is the additive calibration,
not an arbitrary unknown map $f$ or an unknown covariance. The theorem
derives the statistical and label rates from the two-sided contact
geometry; it does not infer that geometry from a finite derivative rank.
