\section{Regeneration and the geometry of future predictions}
\label{sec:v3-regeneration}
The obstruction in this section concerns information already acquired and
subsequently discarded. It does not arise from estimating an unknown
transition probability. We first give a converse for a general regenerative
experiment and then compute its consequence for an expanding map.

\subsection{A retained-label experiment}
Let $E$ be a standard Borel space, $T:E\to E$ a Borel map, $\mu$ a
probability measure, and $f:E\to[0,1]$ a Borel readout. Fix $0<p<1$ and
write $q=1-p$. At time zero an observer acquires $U_0\sim\mu$ and
$X_0=U_0$. At each integer time $t\ge1$, an independent mode $R_t$ is
observed, with $\Pr(R_t=1)=p$. If $R_t=1$, a fresh independent
$U_t\sim\mu$ is acquired and $X_t=U_t$. Otherwise $X_t=T(X_{t-1})$;
there is no further observation of $X_t$. All acquisitions and modes are
independent of the coding randomness. The full-history prediction is
$f(X_t)$.

A causal $M$-label machine has a state $L_t\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$.
At time zero it encodes $U_0$. Subsequently its update can use the previous
label, the clock, the current mode, and, at a reset, the current $U_t$.
After the update only its label, the clock, and independent public coding
randomness are available for decoding. In particular the current
acquisition is no longer available at the checkpoint. Private coding
randomness is allowed. Past modes, reset times, previous acquisitions, and
previous decoder outputs are not additional persistent registers.
Time-dependent programs are allowed in the lower bound.
Any random-generator state or data-dependent tape position that persists
also counts as part of the label. Private coins may equivalently be
supplied as independent, time-indexed uniforms.

One may realize the loss physically by selecting, after this compression,
a Bernoulli terminal probe of mean $f(X_t)$. The probe is not an additional
observation in the continuing experiment. Conditional expectation then
identifies squared prediction excess with
\[
 L_t^{\rm pred}=|f(X_t)-\widehat f_t|^2.
\]
Projection of the decoder onto $[0,1]$ cannot increase this loss. Define
\[
 \mathcal R_M^{\rm ca}(p;T,\mu,f)
   =\inf_{\text{causal $M$-label machines}}
      \sup_{t\ge0}\E L_t^{\rm pred}.
\]
The clock is free; the elapsed time since the last reset is not free.

\subsection{The future-orbit converse}
For $u\in E$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-orbit}
 Z_q(u)=\bigl(\sqrt p\,q^{k/2}f(T^ku)\bigr)_{k\ge0}\in\ell^2,
 \qquad
 e_M^2(Z_q)=\inf_{c_1,\ldots,c_M\in\ell^2}
   \int\min_i\norm{Z_q(u)-c_i}_{\ell^2}^2\,\mu(du).
\end{equation}
The map is Borel and has norm at most one. Its geometry records every
future readout that must be recovered from one label if no new acquisition
occurs. In contrast, the checkpoint geometry concerns only $f(X_t)$.

\begin{theorem}[Regenerative future-orbit lower bound]
\label{thm:v3-orbit}
Every causal $M$-label machine in the preceding experiment satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-orbit-lower}
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^N\E L_t^{\rm pred}
 \ge e_M^2(Z_q).
\end{equation}
Consequently $\mathcal R_M^{\rm ca}(p;T,\mu,f)\ge e_M^2(Z_q)$.
The assertion holds for arbitrary independent public and private coding
randomization and does not require a stationary machine or an invariant
law for $T$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $e_{M,K}^2$ be the quantization error of the first $K+1$ coordinates
of $Z_q$. Appending the coordinates $\sqrt p\,q^{k/2}/2$, $k>K$,
to any truncated codebook shows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-truncation}
 e_M^2(Z_q)-\tfrac14q^{K+1}\le e_{M,K}^2\le e_M^2(Z_q).
\end{equation}
Approximate minimizers suffice for this argument; existence is not needed.

Fix a possible reset time $j\ge1$. Condition on its entire past, on the
occurrence of that reset, and on the independent coding random tapes.
The new $U_j$ still has law $\mu$. The update assigns it one of at most
$M$ labels. Along the subsequent word of $K$ nonreset modes, every such
label determines a sequence of $K+1$ decoder values. Future random choices
are part of the fixed coding tapes; the nonreset word does not contain
new data. The weighted squared error of these values, integrated over
$U_j$, is therefore at least $e_{M,K}^2$. This remains true when the
assignment itself is randomized, since fixing its independent random
tape produces an ordinary assignment to the same number of centres.
Integrating back over the past and the tapes gives
\[
 p\sum_{k=0}^Kq^k\,
 \E\bigl[L_{j+k}^{\rm pred}\mid R_j=1,
                 R_{j+1}=\cdots=R_{j+k}=0\bigr]
 \ge e_{M,K}^2.
\]
The displayed conditional expectations also integrate over the
unconditioned past at $j$; future modes beyond $j+k$ play no role.

For each checkpoint $t$, the events that its last reset occurred at
$j=t-k$, $0\le k\le K$, are disjoint. Sum their nonnegative
contributions over $1\le t\le N$ and retain only $1\le j\le N-K$.
Independence of the modes gives
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^N\E L_t^{\rm pred}\ge (N-K)e_{M,K}^2.
\]
First let $N\to\infty$ at fixed $K$, and then let $K\to\infty$ in
\eqref{eq:v3-truncation}. The supremum of expected checkpoint losses
dominates their lower limiting average, proving the consequence.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a converse for arbitrary causal machines, not only for
machines obtained by quantizing $E$. The centres in \eqref{eq:v3-orbit}
may lie off the orbit image. In particular, replacing their quantization
problem by a covering problem for the current state would lose information
needed for this lower bound.

\section{A sharp causal-memory transition for an expanding experiment}
\label{sec:v3-doubling}
Take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-doubling}
 E=[0,1),\qquad \mu=\operatorname{Leb}|_{[0,1)},\qquad
 T(x)=2x-\lfloor2x\rfloor,\qquad f(x)=x.
\end{equation}
The fresh acquisition is an analog uniform variable. A machine in the
upper bound below reads only a finite binary prefix of it. The map is
piecewise expanding; it is not asserted to be globally Lipschitz for the
Euclidean metric on $[0,1)$. The proof will account explicitly for the
jumps at dyadic boundaries.

Let $\mathcal R_M^{\rm cp}(t)$ be the optimal expected squared excess
when an encoder can use the entire acquisition history at checkpoint $t$
and then send one of $M$ labels. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-phase-profile}
 \Phi_q(M)=
 \begin{cases}
 M^{-2},&0<q<1/4,\\
 (1+\log M)M^{-2},&q=1/4,\\
 M^{-\kappa(q)},&1/4<q<1,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \kappa(q)=\dfrac{\log(1/q)}{\log2}.
\end{equation}
All logarithms except those explicitly marked otherwise are natural.

\begin{theorem}[Identical checkpoint geometry, three causal rates]
\label{thm:v3-phase}
For the regenerative experiment \eqref{eq:v3-doubling}, every integer
$M\ge1$, and every $t\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-checkpoint-exact}
 \mathcal R_M^{\rm cp}(t)=\frac1{12M^2}.
\end{equation}
For each $0<q<1$, constants $0<c_q\le C_q<\infty$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-phase-law}
 c_q\Phi_q(M)\le
 \mathcal R_M^{\rm ca}(1-q;T,\mu,f)
 \le C_q\Phi_q(M)\qquad(M\ge1).
\end{equation}
The lower bound already holds for the lower limiting temporal mean in
\eqref{eq:v3-orbit-lower}. The upper bound is attained in order by a
single deterministic finite program which does not use $p$ or $q$.
The program has at most $M$ persistent labels, including every state used
to record the number of retained binary digits.
\end{theorem}

The threshold is $p=3/4$. Below that reset frequency the causal label
exponent is smaller than the checkpoint exponent; at the threshold a
logarithmic factor is necessary. The current acquired prediction is
uniform in all these regimes. Thus its dimension and even its entire
probability law do not determine the optimal causal memory law.

\subsection{An explicit finite machine}
For an integer $B\ge0$ use the label set
\[
 \mathcal W_B=\bigcup_{b=0}^B\{0,1\}^b,
 \qquad |\mathcal W_B|=2^{B+1}-1,
\]
including the empty word. At each reset retain the first $B$ binary digits
of $U_t$, using the terminating expansion at a dyadic rational. At a
nonreset delete the leading digit, or leave the empty word unchanged.
Decode a word of length $b$ as the midpoint of its binary cylinder; the
empty word decodes as $1/2$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact risk of the suffix machine]\label{lem:v3-tree}
For this machine, $\sup_{t\ge0}\E L_t^{\rm pred}$ equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-tree-risk}
 U_B(q)=\frac1{12}\left[
 p4^{-B}\sum_{k=0}^{B-1}(4q)^k+q^B\right],
\end{equation}
with the sum empty when $B=0$. Equality holds at every $t\ge B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A uniform variable has independent fair binary digits outside the null
set of dyadic rationals. If the most recent reset was $k$ steps ago,
then the label retains its next $(B-k)_+$ digits. Conditional on this
label and the age, the current state is uniform on the corresponding
cylinder, so its midpoint has squared error
$4^{-(B-k)_+}/12$. At time $t$ the age probabilities are $pq^k$ for
$0\le k<t$, and $q^t$ for $k=t$, because time zero was an acquisition.
Equivalently this age has the law of $\min(G,t)$, where
$\Pr(G=k)=pq^k$. The function $k\mapsto4^{-(B-k)_+}/12$ is
nondecreasing and constant for $k\ge B$. Its expectation increases to
\eqref{eq:v3-tree-risk} and is already equal to it for $t\ge B$.
\end{proof}

Choose the largest $B$ such that $2^{B+1}-1\le M$. Then
$(M+1)/4<2^B\le(M+1)/2$. If $4q<1$, the sum in
\eqref{eq:v3-tree-risk} is bounded, and $q^B\le4^{-B}$.
If $4q=1$, the expression is $4^{-B}(pB+1)/12$.
If $4q>1$, it is at most
$[1+p/(4q-1)]q^B/12$.
These estimates prove the upper bound in \eqref{eq:v3-phase-law}
for every integer budget, not just powers of two.

The label can be stored as an integer whose leading sentinel records its
word length. Only $\lceil\log_2(2^{B+1}-1)\rceil$ persistent bits are
needed. Binary prefix acquisition and output formatting use $O(B+1)$
transient bits; deletion can be performed in $O(B+1)$ bit operations.
The only read-only integer parameter is $B$. Neither a real-valued
codebook nor the last reset time is hidden in the program. The cost of
observing the reset mode and reading the new prefix is an acquisition
cost, distinct from these memory bounds.

\subsection{The lower bound without a restriction on encoders}
Define, for $0<\delta\le1$,
\[
 F_q(\delta)=
 \begin{cases}
 \delta^2,&q<1/4,\\
 \delta^2(1+\log(1/\delta)),&q=1/4,\\
 \delta^{\kappa(q)},&q>1/4.
 \end{cases}
\]
Each function is increasing on $(0,1]$.

\begin{lemma}[Separation of future orbits]\label{lem:v3-orbit-separation}
For $u,v\in[0,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-orbit-separation}
 \norm{Z_q(u)-Z_q(v)}_{\ell^2}^2
 \ge \frac p{32}F_q(|u-v|).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The case $u=v$ is immediate. Write $\delta=|u-v|$.
If $\delta\le1/2$, let
$n=\lfloor\log_2(1/(2\delta))\rfloor$. For $0\le k\le n$, the
difference of the fractional parts of $2^ku$ and $2^kv$ has absolute
value either $2^k\delta$ or $1-2^k\delta$. Both are at least
$2^k\delta$, since $2^k\delta\le1/2$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v3-orbit-sum}
 \norm{Z_q(u)-Z_q(v)}^2
 \ge p\delta^2\sum_{k=0}^n(4q)^k.
\end{equation}
For $q<1/4$ the $k=0$ term alone suffices, for every $\delta$.
For $q=1/4$ and $\delta\le1/4$, the integer $n+1$ is at least
$\log(1/\delta)/(2\log2)$ and hence at least
$(1+\log(1/\delta))/4$. For $\delta\ge1/4$, the initial term
$p\delta^2$ is at least
$p\delta^2(1+\log(1/\delta))/(1+\log4)$.
This proves a stronger constant than the stated one in the critical case.

For $q>1/4$ and $\delta\le1/2$, one has
$2^n\delta>1/4$ and $q^n\ge(2\delta)^{\kappa(q)}$.
The last term in \eqref{eq:v3-orbit-sum} is therefore at least
$p\delta^{\kappa(q)}/16$. For $\delta>1/2$, the initial term gives
$p\delta^2\ge p\delta^{\kappa(q)}/4$, since $0<\kappa(q)<2$.
All cases imply \eqref{eq:v3-orbit-separation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-phase}]
Lebesgue measure is preserved by the doubling map, and a reset also has
Lebesgue law. Thus $X_t$ is uniform for every $t$. Given the complete
history it is known exactly. Scalar quantization of the uniform law by
$M$ midpoints has error $1/(12M^2)$. Conversely, after ordering arbitrary
centres their Voronoi cells are intervals of lengths $a_i$ with
$\sum_i a_i=1$; the integral error on an interval is at least $a_i^3/12$.
Convexity gives $\sum_i a_i^3\ge M^{-2}$. Random assignment cannot
improve on the nearest centre, and conditioning on an independent public
seed proves the same bound for randomized encoders and decoders.
This establishes \eqref{eq:v3-checkpoint-exact}.

For the causal lower bound put $h=1/(2M)$ and
$r=\tfrac14\sqrt{pF_q(h)/32}$. Consider any radius-$r$ ball in
$\ell^2$, centred anywhere. Two points of the orbit image lying in this
ball have distance at most $2r$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:v3-orbit-separation}, their preimages cannot be separated
by $h$ or more. The preimage of the ball therefore has Lebesgue measure
at most $h$. A union of $M$ such balls covers at most half the acquired
mass, and hence
\[
 e_M^2(Z_q)\ge r^2/2=\frac p{1024}F_q(1/(2M)).
\]
For $q\le1/4$ this last profile is at least $\Phi_q(M)/4$;
for $q>1/4$ it is $2^{-\kappa(q)}\Phi_q(M)\ge\Phi_q(M)/4$.
Thus $c_q=p/4096$ is one possible lower constant.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-orbit} applies to every causal machine and proves
the lower assertion including the temporal-mean version. The suffix
machine and Lemma~\ref{lem:v3-tree} give the upper bound as already
computed.
\end{proof}

The converse uses Euclidean differences only up to the first relevant
dyadic separation; it does not replace the doubling map by an invalid
global Lipschitz estimate. Nor does it assume that a general machine
stores an interval or a binary word. These restrictions are used only
for the explicit upper construction. The same algorithm works for all
$p\in(0,1)$, so an unknown reset frequency does not change its order of
performance at any fixed true $p$.

At $p=1$ every step is a fresh checkpoint and the exact risk is
$1/(12M^2)$. The assertion above concerns $p>0$; its constants need not
remain uniform as $p\downarrow0$. This dependence is displayed in the
finite geometric sum \eqref{eq:v3-tree-risk}, rather than suppressed in
an unspecified stability constant.
