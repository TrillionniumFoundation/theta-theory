\begin{abstract}
We study prediction and statistical resolution when an observer can retain
only one of $M$ labels after each acquisition. A regenerative converse
bounds every causal machine by the quantization error of an entire weighted
future orbit, rather than by the geometry of a single checkpoint. For an
expanding binary experiment this gives a sharp transition: the checkpoint
risk is always $1/(12M^2)$, whereas the optimal causal risk has order
$M^{-2}$, $(1+\log M)M^{-2}$, or $M^{-\log(1/q)/\log2}$ according as
the nonacquisition probability $q$ is smaller than, equal to, or larger
than $1/4$. One finite binary-suffix machine attains these rates without
knowing $q$, with all its persistent states charged. We also prove an
exact minimax elimination of unrestricted additive calibration in a
correlated paired Gaussian experiment. A two-sided contact geometry then
yields matched finite-sample statistical and label bounds for nonseparable
singular observations. These results are complemented by nonuniform
causal-resolution bounds, every-positive-refresh hidden Markov laws,
singular acquisition examples, and a finite-reference policy comparison
with unrestricted optimal control. The measurable and information-theoretic
foundations, with their proofs and boundary examples, are developed in
the appendices.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Foundational development and boundary examples}
The following appendices give the measurable, statistical, and asymptotic
foundations used above. Their statements and proofs are retained from the
first edition. The strong-refresh result in Appendix~\ref{sec:refreshing}
is extended by Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-refresh}; the additional statistical
results are Theorems~\ref{thm:v3-quotient} and~\ref{thm:v3-contact}.

model-specific spectral, large-deviation, and operator problems.
