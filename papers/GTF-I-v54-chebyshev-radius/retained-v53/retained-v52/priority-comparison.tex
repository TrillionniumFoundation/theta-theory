\section{Comparison with related realization theorems}\label{sec:priority52}
We distinguish the mathematical contribution from the classical tools
used to prove it. A numerical experiment with common stochastic rows
is closer to positive realization than to a separate nonnegative
factorization at each cut. The cone and intertwiner criteria surveyed
by Benvenuti and Farina \cite{BF04,BF03} are direct antecedents.
Weighted-automaton canonical forms and approximate minimization
\cite{BPP15} concern linear representations; stochastic positivity and
all-horizon numerical calibration are additional restrictions here.
Threshold acceptance in probabilistic automata \cite{Rabin63} is also
a different correctness criterion from uniformly approximating every
specified terminal probability.

D\'orea and Hennet characterize polyhedral invariance by linear matrix
relations \cite{DoreaHennet99}; invariant-polytope algorithms also
address joint spectral radii of matrix families \cite{Mejstrik20}.
Those objectives are not identical to the clocked numerical interface
here. In particular, the forward construction from an invariant
polytope is not presented as novel; the all-width converse and its
positive-error finite-orbit reduction are the assertions requiring
separate evaluation. Likewise the arithmetic height estimate is a
particular finite cap certificate, while stronger Diophantine rates
in the appendix retain their own hypotheses.

This is a theorem-level comparison with the sources examined, not an
exhaustive clearance of priority in all finite semigroup or nonuniform
transducer literature. An independent priority assessment can therefore
challenge originality without being conflated with a gap in the stated
proofs. The source and response records identify the exact predecessor
of each retained mechanism.
