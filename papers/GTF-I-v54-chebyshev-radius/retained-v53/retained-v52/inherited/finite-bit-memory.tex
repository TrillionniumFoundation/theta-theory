\section{Finite random bits and uniform space}\label{sec:bits44}
Exact real rows and finite-bit algorithms are different resources. We
first give a finite perturbation statement and then remove the row table
from a concrete uniform construction. Only this section uses a
computational model. It does not change the exact atomic-row conclusions
proved elsewhere.

\begin{lemma}[Dyadic perturbation of a stochastic transducer]\label{lem:round44}
Suppose initialization and every command row have at most $s$ nonzero
entries. They can be replaced by denominator-$2^b$ rows, on the same
label sets, with row-TV error at most $(s-1)2^{-b}$. Rounding the terminal
binary probabilities adds at most $2^{-b}$. Consequently every numerical
word changes by at most $((N+1)(s-1)+1)2^{-b}$ in binary total variation.
Each rounded row can be sampled with exactly $b$ independent fair bits.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order the $s$ successors, round the first $s-1$ probabilities down to
multiples of $2^{-b}$, and give the remaining mass to the last successor.
The total removed mass is at most $(s-1)2^{-b}$, which is exactly the
row total variation. Couple the initializations and then the command
updates, or telescope products using TV contraction, to sum their
worst-row errors. A terminal Bernoulli parameter can be rounded down
with error at most $2^{-b}$. A uniform integer in $\{0,\ldots,2^b-1\}$
selects one of the cumulative dyadic intervals. This uses a fixed number
of bits, with no retained random tape or rejection-time side channel.
\end{proof}

For a table implementation the storage is
$O(|\calA|Ks(\log K+b))$ bits for common rows of width $K$, in addition
to initialization and decoder data. Cut-dependent rows can require a
factor $N$ more storage. Lemma~\ref{lem:round44} is the elementary
stochastic-rounding principle; it is not claimed as a new finite-bit
simulation method. The next construction uses no such table.

\begin{definition}[Uniform command-stream model]\label{def:uniform44}
A fixed finite program receives $N$ in binary, one of the four planar
seeds, exactly $N$ one-way command symbols, and one final coordinate
query. It has a work tape and fresh independent fair bits. The command
input cannot be reread. All counters, arithmetic registers, stored
parameters, random words and output storage count toward work space.
An external source waits while a command is processed; waiting time is
not an input or a persistent storage channel. The finite control is
fixed independently of $N$. The program has no horizon-dependent advice
or transition table.
\end{definition}

An angle has an admissible digit procedure if a fixed program computes
a dyadic approximation within $2^{-p}$ in polynomial time in $p$ and
$O(p)$ work bits. The same fixed $r$ is used throughout. This explicit
hypothesis excludes a free oracle for an arbitrary noncomputable real.
Algebraic angles, in particular the examples below, meet it by rational
bisection with fixed-degree integer arithmetic.

\begin{theorem}[A table-free finite-bit compiler]\label{thm:uniform44}
Fix $r$ angles with admissible digit procedures, rational
$0<\rho\le1/10$, and rational $0<\varepsilon<1$. There is a uniform
program in Definition~\ref{def:uniform44} whose every binary output row
has TV error at most $\varepsilon$ from the rotation experiment. It uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-resources44}
 O_{r,\alpha,\rho}(\log(N+2)+\log(1/\varepsilon))\text{ work bits},
 \qquad (N+2)b\text{ fair bits},
\end{equation}
where $b=\lceil\log_2(16(N+2)/\varepsilon)\rceil$.
Its total running time is polynomial in $N+\log(1/\varepsilon)$ for
fixed digit procedures. Its clean stochastic register has $q+1$ labels,
including one central label, with $q=O(\sqrt N)$.

If a denominator $q_0\ge4$ satisfies
$N\eta_\alpha(q_0)\le q_0^2/2000$, the search may choose $q\le q_0$.
Consequently the clean-register upper exponents and logarithmic bounds
of Theorem~\ref{thm:metric-main} hold for this compiler as well, with
changed constants. These clean labels do not include its scratch space
or deterministic parameter registers; the full work bound is
\eqref{eq:uniform-resources44}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We give the parameter search, real reference rows, dyadic rounding,
and finite-space evaluation separately.

\emph{Parameter search.}
Put $p=\lceil\log_2(16000(N+2)/\varepsilon)\rceil$ and obtain dyadic
$\widetilde\alpha_i$ with error at most $2^{-p}$. For $q=4,5,\ldots$
compute, by integer arithmetic,
\[
 e(q)=\max_i\|q\widetilde\alpha_i\|_{\R/\Z}+q2^{-p}.
\]
Stop at the first $q$ for which $Ne(q)\le q^2/1000$. The test is
rational and certifies both $\eta_\alpha(q)$ and
$\eta_{\widetilde\alpha}(q)$ by the Lipschitz property of distance to
$\Z$. It succeeds by $q=\max(4,\lceil\sqrt{2000(N+2)}\rceil)$:
$\|q\widetilde\alpha_i\|\le1/2$ and the added term is smaller than
one at this bound. If $q_0$ satisfies the stronger displayed condition,
then $e(q_0)\le\eta_\alpha(q_0)+2q_0 2^{-p}$ and
$2Nq_02^{-p}\le q_0^2/2000$. Hence that candidate succeeds.
No exact comparison of an irrational angle with a rational sector
boundary is performed.

\emph{Reference rows with slack.}
Use the regular $q$-gon $v_j=(\cos(2\pi j/q),\sin(2\pi j/q))$ and
one additional label $*$ representing zero. Set
$\Lambda=1+1/(4N)$. For $N\ge1$ the dilation estimate
\eqref{eq:dilation} and the search condition give
\[
 \log\Lambda\ge1/(5N)>4\pi^2\eta_{\widetilde\alpha}(q)/q^2.
\]
Thus $\Lambda^{-1}R_{2\pi\widetilde\alpha_i}v_j$ lies strictly inside
the polygon. The case $N=0$ is handled by direct rounding of the seed
response and a fixed amount of space.
Write $q\widetilde\alpha_i=n_i+t_i$, with integer $n_i$ and
$0\le t_i<1$, and put $h=2\pi/q$. The command row has successors
$j+n_i$, $j+n_i+1$ modulo $q$, and $*$, with respective weights
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-row44}
 A_i=\frac{\sin((1-t_i)h)}{\Lambda\sin h},\qquad
 B_i=\frac{\sin(t_i h)}{\Lambda\sin h},\qquad 1-A_i-B_i.
\end{equation}
They are nonnegative by the dilation bound, and their vector mean is
the required contracted rotation. The row at $*$ stays there. Initialize
with vector mean $x/2$ using the same chord formula with radial factor
$1/2$ and the exact seed angle $0,1/4,1/2,3/4$. Decode with mean
$2\rho\Lambda^N\ip{e_j}{v_s}$, or zero at $*$. Since
$\Lambda^N\le e^{1/4}$, all decoder means are strictly inside $[-1,1]$.
The usual mean induction realizes every word for
$\widetilde\alpha$ exactly. These are reference real rows only.

\emph{Certified finite rows.}
Compute outward intervals for the first two weights in each row, each
of width at most $2^{-b}$. Clip their lower endpoints at zero and round
down to denominator $2^b$; assign the residue to $*$. Relative to the
reference row, at most $4\cdot2^{-b}$ mass is moved. Evaluate and round
each terminal Bernoulli parameter within $2\cdot2^{-b}$. TV contraction
therefore bounds initialization, $N$ updates and final decoding by
$(4N+6)2^{-b}$. Replacing the true angles by their dyadic approximants
changes a product rotation by at most $2\pi N2^{-p}$ in operator norm,
and changes a target binary row by at most $\pi\rho N2^{-p}$.
The sum is less than $\varepsilon$ with the chosen $p,b$.
Use exactly $b$ fair bits at each draw, including dummy bits when the
central row is deterministic. The random-bit count is $(N+2)b$.

\emph{Work and uniform evaluation.}
Only $q$, the current vertex or central flag, the horizon and command
counter, the $r$ dyadic angles, and $O(r)$ offsets and dyadic weights
are retained. The latter specify circulant rows, not a $q$-row table.
The denominator search uses integers of $O(p+\log q)$ bits.
Elementary sine and cosine on bounded intervals can be evaluated by
fixed-point Taylor recurrences with outward rounding; $\pi$ can be
evaluated by Machin's arctangent identity. There are $O(b+\log q)$
terms and only a fixed number of live accumulators. Since
$\sin(2\pi/q)\ge4/q$ for $q\ge4$, division loses only $O(\log q)$
bits. Repeated squaring evaluates $\Lambda^N$ with $O(\log N)$ stages,
all intermediate true values between $1$ and $e^{1/4}$.
An additional $O(\log(N+2)+\log(b+2))$ guard bits bounds accumulated
rounding errors. Interval precision can instead be doubled until the
stated widths are certified; these error estimates bound the final
precision by the same order. No integer of unbounded exact denominator
is stored. Every live register has
$O(b+p+\log N)$ bits, and the number of registers is fixed for fixed
$r$. This proves the work bound and a polynomial time bound, including
parameter search. Finally the finite arithmetic upper proofs remain
valid with $1/100$ replaced by $1/2000$, since only fixed constants
change. Their candidate denominators therefore bound the search.
\end{proof}

The proof describes a fixed-point implementation, not the numerical
behavior of a floating-point library. The accompanying integer-interval
reference code certifies each rounded row and uses fresh bounded-length
random integers; its Python object overhead is not identified with the
Turing work-space constant.

\begin{corollary}[Sharp work-space order for explicit alphabets]\label{cor:space44}
Let $\alpha_i=2^{i/(r+1)}$, $1\le i\le r$, and fix rational
$0<\varepsilon<\rho/(2\sqrt2)$ and $0<\rho\le1/10$.
In Definition~\ref{def:uniform44}, numerical simulation requires and
suffices with $\Theta(\log N)$ work bits. More precisely every fixed
program using $s(N)$ work bits obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:space-lower44}
 s(N)\ge\frac r{2r+1}\log_2N-O(\log\log(N+2)).
\end{equation}
The compiler's clean register has
$O(N^{r/(2r+1)})$ labels. This is not a claim that all its internal
fair-bit states retain that label exponent or that the leading
coefficient of work space is optimal.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $\theta=2^{1/(r+1)}$. For a nonzero integer vector $m$ with
$H=\|m\|_1\ge1$, choose the integer $p$ nearest to
$-\sum_i m_i\theta^i$. The algebraic integer
$P(\theta)=p+\sum_i m_i\theta^i$ is nonzero: the minimal polynomial
$t^{r+1}-2$ is Eisenstein and has degree $r+1$. Each of its other
$r$ conjugates has modulus at most $(9/2)H$, since
$|p|\le2H+1/2$ and $|\theta^i|<2$.
Its nonzero integer norm gives
\[
 \|m\cdot\alpha\|_{\R/\Z}\ge(2/9)^rH^{-r}.
\]
This classical algebraic example is credited in \cite{DGLPS}; it is
not new arithmetic. The inherited badly approximable theorem,
Corollary~\ref{cor:ba}, now supplies the all-hidden lower bound and
the matching candidate denominators for the compiler.
Bisection for $\theta$ and fixed-degree powers meet the digit hypothesis.
A fixed program with $s(N)$ work bits has at most $C(s(N)+1)^C2^{s(N)}$
command-boundary configurations. Its private randomness since the last
boundary is integrated into the next stochastic row, so it induces a
legal atomic realization of at most that width. Apply the lower bound
$W_{N,\varepsilon}\ge cN^{r/(2r+1)}$ and take logarithms. The polynomial factor accounts for the fixed number of work-tape head positions; it contributes $O(\log\log N)$ unless the displayed lower bound is already automatic.
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform44} supplies the upper work bound.
\end{proof}
