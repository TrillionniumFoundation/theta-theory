\begin{abstract}
We determine the one-pass space needed for legal numerical simulation
of fixed rational quantum systems. In every fixed matrix dimension,
unitary alphabets with a full action spectral gap and an all-direction
cap bound have optimal writable space
$\Theta(\min\{N,L+\log(N+1)\})$ at Frobenius mean error $2^{-L}$.
The lower bound permits randomized fixed programs and unlimited
internal running time. A deterministic integer construction attains
the upper order with pointwise trace-norm error and positive outputs.
More generally, fixed Gaussian-rational quantum instruments admit
an adaptive one-pass simulation with the same space upper bound
and error $2^{-L}$ in the classical--quantum trace norm, without
a lower bound on outcome probabilities. A fixed-denominator positive
rounding map controls the entire path despite measurement disturbance.
The converse comes from spectral dissipation of smoothed conditional
centroids and counts narrow cuts with unrestricted intervening widths.
For spherical Ramanujan alphabets we also determine the exponential
accuracy--width rate, retaining an
$\exp[O(\sqrt{N\log(N+2)})]$ multiplicative uncertainty.
The arithmetic spectral inputs are imported.
Nonuniform label width and charged fixed-program space are separate
resources throughout.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle
\noindent The resource counts persistent hidden labels, including unused
padding. The horizon, clock, real table descriptions, table construction,
exact arithmetic and exact sampling are uncharged. A numerical terminal
decoder is not a sampled answer register; a process error is one budget
for the whole control--output transcript. Sections~\ref{sec:streaming64} and~\ref{sec:instruments65}
introduce separately charged, fixed-program bit-space models.
All dimension and physical-input data are fixed independently of the
horizon and requested precision.
