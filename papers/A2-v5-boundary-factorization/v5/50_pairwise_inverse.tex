\section{A sharp pairwise inverse from four count amplitudes}
\label{sec:v5-pairwise}
At a repeated curvature, stability relative to the circular table and
stability between two nearby tables have different exponents. We now
solve the local pairwise problem for the actual equal-gap family
\eqref{eq:g-isogap-family}. Four direct amplitudes suffice; no
infinite-sequence transform or separation of the curvatures is needed.

Fix $R\in(0,1/2)$ and $g=1-2R$. Write
$\kappa_* =1/R$ and $c_*=1+g\kappa_*>1$. The area of the circular
member is $A_* =\sqrt3/2-\pi R^2>0$. Let $\mathcal K$ be a
sufficiently small compact neighborhood of its unordered curvature
triple in the family \eqref{eq:g-isogap-family}. This means the image
of a small closed parameter neighborhood, and includes all sufficiently
small curvature perturbations. The area remains the symmetric function
in Proposition~\ref{prop:v3-area-coalescence}.

\begin{theorem}[Four-amplitude pairwise recovery]
\label{thm:v5-pairwise}
There are such a neighborhood $\mathcal K$ and a constant $C$ such
that, for any two of its tables, with
$\delta=\max_{1\le j\le4}|C_j-\widetilde C_j|$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-pairwise-bound}
 \operatorname{dist}_{\rm match}(\kappa,\widetilde\kappa)
       \le C\delta^{1/3},\qquad
 |A-\widetilde A|\le C\delta.
\end{equation}
In particular the first four unlabelled amplitudes determine the
curvature multiset, including multiplicities, without a supplied area.
No minimum separation between distinct curvatures is assumed. Data
with absolute error at most $\epsilon$ admit a reconstruction in
$\mathcal K$ with curvature error at most $C\epsilon^{1/3}$ and area
error at most $C\epsilon$.

For every fixed $0\le a<\gamma_-$ on a sufficiently small such
neighborhood, the pairwise exponent $1/3$ is sharp both for these four
amplitudes and for the full-sequence norm
$\|D\|_a=\sup_{j\ge1}e^{aj}|D_j|$. The circular-reference exponent
$1/2$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-sequence-stability} remains valid and
sharp for its different, reference-point question.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Regular coordinates through a triple collision}
Set
\[
 f_j(c)=\csch(j\arcosh c),\qquad c_r=1+g\kappa_r=c_*+y_r.
\]
Let $e_1,e_2,e_3$ be the elementary symmetric functions of
$y_0,y_1,y_2$, so that
$P_e(z)=z^3-e_1z^2+e_2z-e_3$. Temporarily allow $q=A^{-1}$ to vary
independently and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-amplitude-map}
 \Psi_j(q,e)=q\sum_{r=0}^2 f_j(c_*+y_r),\qquad 1\le j\le4.
\end{equation}
This analytic enlargement is a device for inverting the observation;
it does not add an independent area parameter to the physical family.
For its tables, $\Psi_j=C_j$ exactly.

Choose a small circle $|z|=r$ such that every $f_j(c_*+z)$ is
holomorphic on and inside it. For $e$ close to zero all roots of $P_e$
are strictly inside this circle, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-contour-symmetry}
 \sum_r f_j(c_*+y_r)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm i}
       \int_{|z|=r} f_j(c_*+z)\frac{P_e'(z)}{P_e(z)}\,\dd z.
\end{equation}
The logarithmic derivative counts roots with their multiplicities.
The denominator stays uniformly nonzero on the contour, proving real
analyticity in $e$ even at multiple roots; nearby complex roots present
no difficulty for this analytic extension.
The first three Newton sums are
$p_1=e_1$, $p_2=e_1^2-2e_2$,
$p_3=e_1^3-3e_1e_2+3e_3$.
For $k\ge4$, $p_k$ has no term linear in $e$ at zero.
Consequently the four columns of $D\Psi(q,0)$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-jacobian-columns}
 \left(3f_j(c_*),\ q f_j'(c_*),\ -q f_j''(c_*),\
                     \frac q2 f_j'''(c_*)\right)_{j=1}^4.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[The collision Jacobian]\label{lem:v5-jacobian}
For $c>1,q>0$, the determinant of the matrix in
\eqref{eq:v5-jacobian-columns}, with $c_*$ replaced by $c$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-jacobian}
 -\frac{18q^3(64c^8+32c^6+116c^4+4c^2+1)}
 {(c^2-1)^2c^4(4c^2-1)^4(2c^2-1)^4}.
\end{equation}
In particular it never vanishes on the indicated domain.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $h(c)=(c^2-1)^{-1/2}$ and write $f_j=hg_j$. The elementary
multiple-angle identities give
\[
 g_1=1,\quad g_2=\frac1{2c},\quad
 g_3=\frac1{4c^2-1},\quad g_4=\frac1{4c(2c^2-1)}.
\]
Successive column operations in the derivative matrix show that its
Wronskian is $h^4$ times that of $g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4$: the product-rule
change of columns is triangular with diagonal $h$.
Expanding along the constant first row reduces the latter to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-rational-wronskian}
 \det(g_j^{(k)}(c))_{j=2,3,4;\ k=1,2,3}
 =\frac{12(64c^8+32c^6+116c^4+4c^2+1)}
 {c^4(4c^2-1)^4(2c^2-1)^4}.
\end{equation}
This identity follows by differentiating the three displayed rational
functions and putting the determinant over a common denominator. For
example $g_2'=-1/(2c^2)$, $g_2''=1/c^3$, $g_2'''=-3/c^4$;
$g_3'=-8c/(4c^2-1)^2$, $g_3''=8(12c^2+1)/(4c^2-1)^3$,
and $g_3'''=-384c(4c^2+1)/(4c^2-1)^4$. Substitution of these and
the derivatives of $g_4$ gives the numerator in
\eqref{eq:v5-rational-wronskian}. The column factors in
\eqref{eq:v5-jacobian-columns} multiply to $-3q^3/2$.
Multiplication by $h^4$ proves \eqref{eq:v5-jacobian}. Every factor
in its denominator is positive and its numerator polynomial has
positive coefficients.
\end{proof}

Let $J_*=D\Psi(A_*^{-1},0)$. On a sufficiently small convex ball
$U$ about $(A_*^{-1},0)$, continuity gives
\[
 \sup_U\|D\Psi-J_*\|<\frac{1}{2\|J_*^{-1}\|}.
\]
Integrating the differential along the segment between any two points
in $U$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-coefficient-lipschitz}
 |q-\widetilde q|+\|e-\widetilde e\|_\infty
     \le C\|\Psi(q,e)-\Psi(\widetilde q,\widetilde e)\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
Indeed the integral differs from $J_*$ applied to that segment vector
by at most half its lower singular-value bound. Thus these analytic
coordinates have a Lipschitz inverse across the collision, even
though individually labelled roots do not.

\subsection{Root matching and sharpness}
\begin{lemma}[Matching three roots]\label{lem:v5-roots}
For monic cubics whose roots stay in a fixed bounded set, there is $C$
such that the minimum over permutations of the maximum root distance is
at most $C$ times the one-third power of the maximum coefficient error.
Roots are counted with multiplicity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $P,Q$ be the cubics and let $\epsilon$ be their maximum coefficient
difference. For small $\epsilon>0$ choose a radius
$r$ comparable to $\epsilon^{1/3}$ with a sufficiently large fixed
constant, and take the union of the three disks of radius $r$ about
the roots of $P$. On its boundary the distance to each root is at least
$r$, whence $|P|\ge r^3$. All these disks lie in a fixed bounded set,
so $|P-Q|\le C_0\epsilon<r^3$. Choose the radius arbitrarily slightly
larger if necessary to avoid tangencies. Rouch\'e's theorem on each
boundary component shows that each connected component contains the
same number of roots of $P$ and $Q$, with multiplicity. Its diameter
is at most $6r$, because at most three disks are joined. Matching the
roots within each component proves the assertion. For $\epsilon=0$
it is immediate; for errors bounded away from zero increase $C$ using
the assumed bound on the roots.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-pairwise}]
Choose $\mathcal K$ so its $(q,e)$ image lies in $U$. Combine
\eqref{eq:v5-coefficient-lipschitz} with Lemma~\ref{lem:v5-roots}
and $\kappa_r=(c_r-1)/g$ to obtain the first bound in
\eqref{eq:v5-pairwise-bound}. Since $q$ is bounded away from zero,
$A=1/q$ and \eqref{eq:v5-coefficient-lipschitz} give its second bound.
For noisy data minimize the maximum four-amplitude discrepancy over
the compact physical set $\mathcal K$. A minimizer exists, and its
data differ from the true data by at most $2\epsilon$ because the true
table is an admissible competitor. The same pairwise bounds apply.
This specifies a reconstruction without selecting unstable individual
root labels or treating nonphysical coefficient vectors as tables.

It remains to prove the stated sharpness in the physical geometry.
In \eqref{eq:g-isogap-family} take $\alpha=\zeta=0$, $\beta=s$.
Both signs of small $s$ are admissible, with radii and area
\[
 r(s)=(R+36s,R-18s,R-18s),\qquad
 A(s)=A_*+\frac{45\pi}{4}s^2.
\]
Set $\Phi_j(r)=\csch(j\arcosh(1+g/r))$. On a small compact parameter
interval the radius-to-exponent map and $A^{-1}$ have bounded
third derivatives. Differentiating
$2e^{-j\gamma}/(1-e^{-2j\gamma})$ three times gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-third-sequence}
 \sup_{|s|\le s_*}|C_j'''(s)|\le B(1+j)^3e^{-j\gamma_-}.
\end{equation}
Its denominator is bounded below by $1-e^{-2\gamma_-}$ and each
derivative costs at most one further polynomial factor in $j$.
The constant and quadratic Taylor terms cancel between $s$ and $-s$;
the linear term vanishes because $36-18-18=0$ and $A'(0)=0$.
Taylor's integral remainder and $a<\gamma_-$ therefore imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-cubic-sequence}
 \|C(s)-C(-s)\|_a\le B_a|s|^3.
\end{equation}
The cubic coefficient is nonzero already at $j=1$. Direct
 differentiation gives
\[
 \Phi_1(r)=\frac{r}{\sqrt{g(g+2r)}},\qquad
 \Phi_1'''(r)=\frac{3(3g+r)}{\sqrt g\,(g+2r)^{7/2}}>0,
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-cubic-coefficient}
 C_1(s)-C_1(-s)=\frac{11664\Phi_1'''(R)}{A_*}s^3+O(s^5).
\end{equation}
Here $36^3+2(-18)^3=34992$ and subtraction of the two Taylor
expansions supplies its factor $1/3$. The even area contributes no
cubic correction from a linear numerator term. Thus both the
four-amplitude discrepancy and the full-sequence discrepancy are
comparable to $|s|^3$.

Sorted matching minimizes maximum matching distance for real numbers:
uncrossing two crossed pairs cannot increase the larger distance.
For small positive $s$ the middle sorted pair in the two curvature
triples has the largest difference, and gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-curvature-matching}
 \operatorname{dist}_{\rm match}(\kappa(s),\kappa(-s))
       =\frac{36s}{R^2-324s^2}=\frac{36}{R^2}s+O(s^3).
\end{equation}
The other two matched differences have leading coefficient $18/R^2$,
so the strict inequality persists for small $s$. Any uniform pairwise
exponent $\theta>1/3$ would imply $b|s|\le C|s|^{3\theta}$ along
these two tables, which is impossible. Conversely the full-sequence
upper bound follows from the proved four-amplitude estimate, since
its maximum discrepancy is no larger than $\|C-\widetilde C\|_a$.
This proves both attainability and sharpness, and distinguishes it
from the preserved circular-reference theorem.
\end{proof}

The exact infinite-sequence inverse has a larger domain of application
within the equal-gap family. The theorem here is a finite-observation
and quantitative statement on an explicitly local neighborhood of the
triple collision. Its regular coordinates are polynomial coefficients,
not separated Prony nodes. The sharp exponent belongs to the physical
unlabelled observation; the channel-resolved endpoint observation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-tomography} remains Lipschitz because it contains
different information.
