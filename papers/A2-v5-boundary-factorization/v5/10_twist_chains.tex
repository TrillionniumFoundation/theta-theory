\section{Relative boundary factorization for variable twist chains}
\label{sec:v5-chains}
The determinant argument has a formulation which does not require a
periodic orbit, identical flight actions, or an explicit Green kernel.
We give that formulation first. Its hypotheses are local convexity and
a uniform nearest-neighbor twist. They are verified by the billiard
length actions in Section~\ref{sec:g-localization}.

\subsection{The class of chains}
Let $(L_i)_{i\in\Z}$ be real $C^\infty$ functions on a fixed square
$(-r_0,r_0)^2$, with uniformly bounded derivatives of each fixed order.
A smooth parameter $\xi$ in a compact finite-dimensional set is allowed;
the derivative bounds below then include every fixed mixed parameter
order. Suppose that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-chain-hypotheses}
 \begin{gathered}
 L_i(0,0)=0,\qquad \nabla L_i(0,0)=0,\qquad
 0<b_-\le -L_{i,uv}\le b_+,\\
 L_{i,uu}\ge |L_{i,uv}|+\eta,\qquad
 L_{i,vv}\ge |L_{i,uv}|+\eta
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
throughout the square, where $\eta>0$ and the constants are independent
of $i$. Write $b_i^0=-L_{i,uv}(0,0)$. For integers $m<n$, let
$\mathcal W_{m,n}(u,v)$ be the stationary value of
$\sum_{i=m}^{n-1}L_i(x_i,x_{i+1})$ with $x_m=u,x_n=v$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-chain-twist}
 d^0_{m,n}=-\mathcal W_{m,n,uv}(0,0),\qquad
 b_{m,n}(u,v)=\frac{-\mathcal W_{m,n,uv}(u,v)}{d^0_{m,n}}.
\end{equation}
The construction below establishes existence on a common small endpoint
square and positivity of $d^0_{m,n}$. For $n=m+1$ the empty interior
determinant is, by convention, one.

For the right half-line with initial site $m$, fix $x_m=u$ and require
$x_i\to0$ as $i\to+\infty$. For the left half-line ending at $n$, fix
$x_n=v$ and require $x_i\to0$ as $i\to-\infty$. Superscripts $+$ and
$-$ distinguish these directions. Denote their stationary segments by
$x^{m,+}(u),x^{n,-}(v)$ and their action sums by $S_m^+(u),S_n^-(v)$.
Let $H_m^+(u)$ be the right half-line interior Hessian, $G_m^+$ the
inverse of $H_m^+(0)$, and $\Delta H_m^+=H_m^+(u)-H_m^+(0)$.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-chain-boundary-amplitude}
 B_m^+(u)=\exp\left\{
 \sum_{i=m}^\infty\log\frac{-L_{i,uv}(x_i,x_{i+1})}{b_i^0}
 -\log\det(I+G_m^+\Delta H_m^+)\right\}.
\end{equation}
The left amplitude $B_n^-$ is defined by the sum over $i<n$ and its
left half-line Hessian. All determinants here are relative Fredholm
determinants, defined by the convergent trace-log series below.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform relative boundary factorization]
\label{thm:v5-chain-factorization}
Under \eqref{eq:v5-chain-hypotheses}, the finite and half-line segments
exist uniquely in a common sufficiently small coordinate box. The
half-line actions and amplitudes are smooth, with
\[
 S_m^\pm(0)=(S_m^\pm)'(0)=0,\qquad
 (S_m^\pm)''\ge c_*>0,\qquad B_m^\pm>0,\quad B_m^\pm(0)=1.
\]
There are $r_*>0$ and $\tau\in(0,1)$, independent of $m,n$, such that
on $|u|,|v|<r_*$, for every fixed $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-chain-factorization}
 \begin{split}
 &\|\mathcal W_{m,n}-S_m^+\oplus S_n^-\|_{C^k}
   +\|b_{m,n}-B_m^+\otimes B_n^-\|_{C^k}\\
 &\hspace{38mm}\le C_k\tau^{n-m}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The norms include mixed parameter derivatives. The action difference is
$O(\tau^{n-m}(|u|+|v|)^2)$, and the amplitude difference vanishes at
$(0,0)$. Constants may depend on fixed-order derivative bounds, but
neither the endpoint box nor the rate is chosen anew for each length.
For uniformly holomorphic extensions with common bounds the
constructions are analytic on a common smaller complex neighborhood.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Green kernels and the stationary segments}
\begin{proof}
We separate the inverse estimates, the nonlinear gluing, and the
relative determinant. Every interior Hessian, at zero or at a segment
in the coordinate box, is a symmetric tridiagonal matrix with negative
adjacent entries. Its diagonal exceeds the sum of their absolute
values by at least $2\eta$. The same statements hold on half-lines.
Write $H=D-B$, where $D$ is its diagonal and $B$ is nonnegative with
zero diagonal. The uniform upper bound on $D$ gives
\[
 \|D^{-1}B\|_{\ell^\infty\to\ell^\infty}\le q<1.
\]
Thus $G=(I-D^{-1}B)^{-1}D^{-1}$ is well defined, uniformly bounded on
$\ell^\infty$, and, by symmetry of its kernel, on $\ell^1$. It is the
positive $\ell^2$ inverse as well. A product of fewer than $|i-k|$
nearest-neighbor steps cannot connect $i$ and $k$. The Neumann series
therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-chain-green}
 |G_{ik}|\le Cq^{|i-k|}.
\end{equation}
For a finite interval $I=(m,n)$, its zero Hessian is the compression of
the right half-line zero Hessian. In the Neumann expansion, a path
contributing to the difference of the two inverse kernels must leave
$I$ through $n$ and return. Hence, for $m<i,k<n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-green-comparison}
 |(G_I-G_m^+)_{ik}|\le Cq^{(n-i)+(n-k)}.
\end{equation}
Only paths with at least this number of steps are summed; their total
weight at each length is bounded by the same row-sum geometric series.
The reflected estimate holds at the other boundary. The cross-block
bound is already contained in \eqref{eq:v5-chain-green}.

Every fixed parameter derivative of these estimates holds with a
slower exponential. Indeed, differentiating a Neumann product of
length $s$ distributes a fixed number of derivatives among its factors.
The number of terms is polynomial in $s$; each differentiated factor is
uniformly bounded, and the remaining factors retain the geometric row
bound. Summation absorbs this polynomial after choosing a strict
exponential margin. Equivalently, differentiating $HG=I$ gives finite
sums of products $G(\partial^{\alpha_1}H)G\cdots
(\partial^{\alpha_t}H)G$; convolution of the kernels has the same
property. Derivatives of $H^0$ have uniformly bounded operator norm;
they need not be trace class.

Fix $q<\rho_0<\rho_1<1$. The zero quadratic Dirichlet solution on
$I$, and those on either half-line, satisfy respectively
\[
 |x_i^{\mathrm{lin}}|\le C(|u|\rho_0^{i-m}+|v|\rho_0^{n-i}),
 \qquad |x_i^{m,+,\mathrm{lin}}|\le C|u|\rho_0^{i-m}.
\]
For example the finite solution is $G_I$ applied to the two boundary
forcing entries, which are bounded multiples of $u,v$.
In the weighted norm with weight
$w_i=\rho_1^{i-m}+\rho_1^{n-i}$, convolution with $G_I$ is uniformly
bounded. The analogous half-line weight has just one term. Subtracting
the zero quadratic gradient, the nonlinear gradient $N$ obeys
$|N_i(x)|\le C\max_{|k-i|\le1}|x_k|^2$, and its derivative on a
weighted ball of radius $C(|u|+|v|)$ has weighted operator norm
$O(|u|+|v|)$. The equation
$x=x^{\mathrm{lin}}-G_I N(x)$ is consequently a contraction on such
a ball when the endpoints are small, uniformly in $I$. The half-line
argument is identical. Differentiating the equation leaves the same
invertible contraction differential on the highest derivative.
Local products and the convolution estimate give, at every fixed order,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-layer-decay}
 |\partial_\xi^\alpha\partial_u^r x_i^{m,+}(u)|
 \le C_{\alpha,r}\rho_1^{i-m}|u|^{\max(1-r,0)}.
\end{equation}
The rate can be fixed for all finite orders by leaving a margin from
the start. Finite segments have the corresponding sum of two weights.
Uniqueness among all small stationary bounded segments follows by
subtracting two equations and inverting their averaged Hessian by
its diagonal-dominance Neumann series. This also proves uniqueness for
small decaying half-line segments, not only in the contraction ball.

Action sums and all their differentiated sums converge by
\eqref{eq:v5-layer-decay}. First variation leaves only the boundary
term. Strict convexity of each edge gives, for a finitely supported
variation $z$, the lower bound
$\sum_i D^2L_i[z_i,z_{i+1}]\ge
\eta\sum_i(z_i^2+z_{i+1}^2)$.
Applying this to the derivative of a stationary half-line segment and
passing to the limit gives $(S_m^\pm)''\ge\eta$. The zero value and
first differential are immediate.

\subsection*{Gluing the layers}
Write $N=n-m$. At the interior sites put
$z_i=x_i^{m,+}(u)+x_i^{n,-}(v)$ and overwrite the endpoints with
$z_m=u,z_n=v$. The overwrites and missing tails are exponentially
small. Linearity cancels in the stationary residual. Each remaining
interaction contains a factor from each end, and hence is bounded by
$C\rho_1^{i-m}\rho_1^{n-i}$. Summing over the sites and differentiating
gives, with a fixed slower rate $\rho_2<1$,
\[
 \|\partial^\alpha \nabla_I\mathcal L(z)\|_{\ell^1}
 \le C_\alpha(1+N)^{M_\alpha}\rho_2^N,
 \qquad \mathcal L(x)=\sum_{i=m}^{n-1}L_i(x_i,x_{i+1}).
\]
Subtract the finite stationary equation. The averaged Hessian has
uniformly bounded $\ell^1$ inverse. Therefore the difference between
$z$ and the actual finite segment has this same $\ell^1$ bound.
For differentiated differences, move the highest difference derivative
to the left; every term on the right contains a lower difference
or the differentiated residual. Induction gives the same form of
estimate. Locality then proves the action comparison in
\eqref{eq:v5-chain-factorization}: subtract the two half-line sums,
control their tails and their overlapping products, and replace $z$
by the finite segment using its $\ell^1$ error. The difference has zero
value and gradient at zero. Taylor's integral remainder yields the
additional quadratic vanishing.

\subsection*{The normalized determinant}
Put $H_I=H_I^0+\Delta H_I$ on the finite stationary segment.
Tridiagonality, locality and the layer bounds imply, uniformly in $I$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-operator-trace-bounds}
 \begin{gathered}
 \|\partial_\xi^\alpha G_I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C_\alpha,
 \qquad \|\Delta H_I\|_1\le C(|u|+|v|),\\
 \|\partial_\xi^\alpha\partial_{u,v}^\beta\Delta H_I\|_1
       \le C_{\alpha,\beta},\qquad
 \|G_I\Delta H_I\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\tfrac12.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The half-line analogues have $C|u|$ or $C|v|$ in the middle bound.
The sum of the absolute matrix entries bounds the trace norm; it is
summable because each nonzero entry is controlled by adjacent layer
weights. The operator bound in the last inequality follows by one
common decrease of the endpoint box. Product differentiation therefore
bounds every derivative of $G\Delta H$ in trace norm: one factor is
trace class, while all derivatives of $G$ are bounded operators.
This is the precise distinction needed for dimension-independent
bounds.

The tridiagonal cofactor identity gives
\[
 -\mathcal W_{m,n,uv}
 =\frac{\prod_{i=m}^{n-1}[-L_{i,uv}(x_i,x_{i+1})]}{\det H_I}.
\]
It follows either by solving the differentiated interior equations
successively, or by expanding the corner cofactor along its forced
adjacent entries. At zero the same formula gives $d^0_{m,n}>0$.
Dividing these two identities, rather than an action-error estimate,
yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-relative-cofactor}
 \log b_{m,n}=
 \sum_{i=m}^{n-1}\log\frac{-L_{i,uv}(x_i,x_{i+1})}{b_i^0}
 -\log\det(I+G_I\Delta H_I).
\end{equation}
Define the logarithm by
$\sum_{s\ge1}(-1)^{s+1}\tr[(G\Delta H)^s]/s$.
It converges in view of \eqref{eq:v5-operator-trace-bounds}; a trace
norm on one factor and operator norms on the others also justify all
fixed differentiated series. This defines the half-line determinants
in \eqref{eq:v5-chain-boundary-amplitude}.

For $N\ge6$ keep the first and last $r=\lfloor N/3\rfloor$ interior
sites. Deleting all other entries of $\Delta H_I$, including entries
crossing the two retained blocks, costs at most
$C_k(1+N)^{M_k}\rho_2^r$ in differentiated trace norm. Within a
retained block the finite segment can be replaced by its own half-line
segment with an exponentially small entrywise sum, by the gluing
estimate and the opposite tail. For a block-supported perturbation,
$\det(I+AB)=\det(I+BA)$ reduces the determinant to the union of the
blocks. Equations~\eqref{eq:v5-chain-green} and
\eqref{eq:v5-green-comparison} show that the compressed finite Green
matrix differs from the direct sum of the two half-line compressions
by an exponentially small operator norm. There are at most $4r^2$
entries, and their polynomial factor is absorbed by a strict margin.
Multiplication by the uniformly bounded trace-norm perturbation
preserves an exponentially small trace-norm error.

For trace-class $T,T'$ with operator norms at most $q_1<1$,
\[
 |\tr(T^s-(T')^s)|\le s q_1^{s-1}\|T-T'\|_1.
\]
Telescoping powers proves this inequality. Its sum with coefficient
$1/s$ is the trace-norm Lipschitz estimate for the logarithmic
determinant. Differentiating introduces only fixed polynomial factors
in $s$, still summable. The two infinite half-line tails have trace
norm $O(\rho_2^r)$ as well. Thus the determinant in
\eqref{eq:v5-relative-cofactor} converges to the sum of its half-line
logarithms, with the asserted differentiated exponential error.
The edge logarithms converge by the same local product and tail
estimates as the actions. Exponentiation on a uniformly bounded
interval proves the amplitude assertion. Choose $\tau<1$ slower than
the rates just used, and absorb the finitely many short intervals by
enlarging $C_k$. At zero both normalized amplitudes are one. Uniform
complex contractions and the same absolutely convergent determinant
series prove the analytic statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Alternating billiard actions]
\label{cor:v5-billiard-chain}
For the channel actions of Section~\ref{sec:g-localization}, take
$L_i=\ell_{i\bmod2}-g$. They satisfy
\eqref{eq:v5-chain-hypotheses} on a common square. The half-line
objects depend only on the endpoint type, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-chain-factorization} gives their relative
factorization uniformly in collision order.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At zero, the two diagonal Hessian entries are
$(1+g\kappa_b)/g$, and the magnitude of the mixed entry is $1/g$.
The row margins are the positive contact curvatures. Compactness and
continuity preserve a fixed positive margin and negative twist on a
smaller square. Reversal interchanges endpoint types. Uniqueness of
the half-line construction identifies the boundary objects with those
computed explicitly in the next section.
\end{proof}

For example, the chain theorem also applies to
$L_i(u,v)=b_i(v-u)^2/2+a_i u^2/2+\widetilde a_i v^2/2+R_i(u,v)$,
where $b_i,a_i,\widetilde a_i$ have positive uniform bounds and the
uniformly smooth $R_i$ vanish to third order, after shrinking the
square. These sequences need not be periodic or converge at infinity.
There need not then be one stationary limiting law independent of the
endpoint sites: the factorization compares each interval with its own
two half-lines. In billiards, geometric localization and full-phase
preparation are additional ingredients, supplied in the preceding
sections; the abstract chain theorem does not replace them.
