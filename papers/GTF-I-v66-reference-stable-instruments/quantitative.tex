\begin{abstract}
We construct finite rational descriptions of quantum instruments that
are exactly completely positive and jointly trace preserving.
For input dimension $d$, output dimension $n$, and $m$ outcomes, a
coordinate grid of scale $B^{-1}$ gives diamond error at most
$6mn^2d^2(1+mn)/B$, without spectral or Kraus factorization.
The description error accumulates additively under arbitrary common
adaptive testers, entangled reference systems, and bounded public
stopping rules. A separate numerical trajectory algorithm accepts
variable rational Choi data, with explicit dimension and denominator
costs, and controls positive subnormalized history blocks without an
outcome-probability floor. We also prove the conditional sharp fixed-program
space law $\Theta(\min\{N,L+\log(N+1)\})$ for legal numerical matrix
outputs under a full action spectral gap and all-direction cap bounds.
Its converse is supplied by spectral entropy of conditional centroids;
the general instrument construction is an upper theorem, not a lower
classification of noisy processes. For spherical Ramanujan alphabets, the exponential
accuracy--width law has a quantified subexponential multiplicative gap.
\end{abstract}

exact arithmetic and exact sampling are uncharged. A numerical terminal
decoder is not a sampled answer register; a process error is one budget
for the whole control--output transcript. Sections~\ref{sec:streaming64} and~\ref{sec:instruments65}

Their physical data are fixed before $N,L$ vary. In contrast,
Section~\ref{sec:uniformchoi66} charges variable input data and matrix
dimension explicitly. Section~\ref{sec:reference66} approximates
instrument descriptions in diamond norm; it does not implement an
unknown entangled quantum input on a classical numerical machine.
All trace-norm history errors are unhalved; transcript total variation
is bounded by one half of those errors.
