\begin{abstract}
For positive sparse monomial experiments, we determine finite-memory
prediction uniformly through additive collisions of the exponents.
Maximal future Vandermonde products, truncated by the dimension actually
acquired, govern every integer label budget. Attainable confluent flags,
whole-image covering, unconditional acquired mass and one causal filter
are established together; the updates contain no reciprocal collision gap.
Intersecting collision loci yield distinct dimensional regimes and
separated memory crossovers.

For independent edge experiments on a fixed graph, a resolution-dependent
cut profile governs shared memory under history-dependent acquisition.
We retain quantitative separator bounds, analytic order phases, uniform
prediction-curve comparisons for resolution-blind acquisition laws, and
finite policy-menu representations. Localizing acquired coordinates to
active cuts gives graph-size-uniform bit bounds for linear-size graph
families with sufficiently strong balanced cuts, under an explicitly
augmented query menu that retains the tensor task and adds local tests.

We identify the sharp consequence of local random-path small-ball data
as an occupation-flow variational problem. The maximum of expected
checkpoint losses is retained throughout the integration. Conditioning
on independent seeds before the first acquisition gives a stronger
perspective-flow bound, with an exact dual furnishing lower certificates.
These bounds dominate the separator integral, strictly in explicit
families. For the original query task in a positive two-trial experiment,
the resulting certificate is $M^{-2}/8306688$ for every integer label
budget. Its ratio to the previously evaluated restricted-event
separator bound exceeds $1398$, with every density and recovery constant
included. The sharp average-distortion coefficient, determined from the
full mixed acquired law, is less than $4.06$ times the new certificate's
coefficient. This last comparison concerns the high-resolution limit;
the flow exactness concerns local path data, not arbitrary statistical
minimax constants.
\end{abstract}
