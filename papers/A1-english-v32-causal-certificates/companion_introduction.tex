\section*{Introduction and conventions}
This volume contains the complete companion developments to
\emph{Attainable information at exponent collisions}. The results are
grouped by their mathematical hypotheses and dependence, rather than
as additional premises of the monomial classification. The main article
contains that classification and its complete direct proof. Here we
begin with interpolation and an abstract geometry-to-causality
principle, give a second proof of the monomial theorem, and then prove
the prior-uniform law for bounded observation algebras. The remaining
sections treat structural ranks, full-support and exact-kernel
pairings, further attainable geometries, decision consequences, and
effective construction. No proof is replaced by a summary.

Sections, statements and equations local to this volume have prefix C.
References bearing prefix M refer to the main article and are linked
to its PDF. Unprefixed classical citation numbers refer to the
bibliography of the present volume. Each theorem retains the
hypotheses stated in its section; conclusions from different classes
are not combined into a theorem with stronger simultaneous uniformity.

When a section uses the monomial notation, it adopts the experiment
of the main article: $I=[l,u]=[0,1]$,
$A=\{0=a_0<\cdots<a_{r-1}\}$, $D=a_{r-1}$,
$W_A=\operatorname{span}\{t^{a_i}\}$, $h_A(m)=|mA|$,
$0A=\{0\}$, and a fixed full-support prior $\mu$.
The detector and command cube are positive with a fixed report bound
$\kappa$. The horizon is $N$, and $n+m=N$.
The formal labels $\Lambda_m$, determinant volumes
$\mathcal V_{m,\ell}$, acquired cap $p_{n,m}$, and profiles
$\Psi_{n,m}$ and $\Xi_N$ are those of
Section~\ref{sec:collision-geometry}. Risk notation refers to
Definition~\ref{def:monomial-risks-v24}; the algebra class additionally
has its own local definitions. The read-only clock and fixed program
are distinguished from the persistent label as in
Definition~\ref{def:finite-state}.

\paragraph{The two multistep geometries.}
In the monomial problem, future test spaces grow, and the acquisition
argument concerns every complete Newton--Hermite prefix up to the
acquired cap. In a bounded finite observation algebra, multiplication
closure instead gives the identity
\[
 v_n=\mu f+\Sigma_\mu\theta_n,
 \qquad \Sigma_\mu=\operatorname{Cov}_\mu(f,f),
\]
with a uniformly bounded coefficient range over all histories and an
actual acquired coefficient ball. The reachable set contains and is
contained in fixed multiples of the covariance ellipsoid. Its dimension
is $\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_\mu$ at every positive checkpoint.
The eigenvalues of $\Sigma_\mu$, not their square roots, are the
semiaxes governing squared prediction risk. The resulting constants
are uniform over priors and support losses under the stated bounded
multiplication assumptions. The finite observable quotient and
immediate saturation are proved explicitly in
Section~\ref{sec:algebra-multistep}.

\paragraph{Dependence of the arguments.}
The interpolation identities do not imply acquired mass. The abstract
transfer theorem takes global covering, actual acquired mass and
reachable-state update bounds as separate hypotheses; its monomial
application verifies all of them. The direct saturated algebra proof
uses elementary rectangular covers and its acquisition chart without
requiring semialgebraic entropy. Its alternative transfer proof is
retained in Section~\ref{sec:algebra-transfer-alternative}.
Exact-kernel feasibility, operational reconstruction and uncertainty
have further hypotheses of their own. Table~\ref{tab:scope} records
these distinctions.
