\section{A marked resource gap generated by a binary collision}
\label{sec:v17-collision}

We now realize a private--hidden--visible separation in a physical
experiment whose information vanishes when the collision is removed.
The controller chooses a later sensor gate after its first report.
The gate controls acquisition, not the mechanical collision law.
Both the source and the target carry the same actual, unobserved mark.

\subsection{The exact marked two-report experiment}
Let $B,W\in\{0,1\}$ have the joint distribution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-marktable}
 \Pp(B=b,W=w)=
 \begin{cases}5/16,&b=w,\\3/16,&b\ne w.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The blind source $E_*$ has two constant reports and actual mark $W$.
The target $F_*$ reports $Y_1=B$, receives $A\in\{0,1\}$ after that
report, and reports $Y_2=AB$. The feedback tester may choose $A$ as
any function of its earlier report. A width-one private simulator
cannot reread its own first emission, except through the current gate
$A$. Hidden selectors persist but are not revealed; visible selectors
are included in the joint target law independently of $(B,W)$.
Put
\[
 d_* =\sqrt{5/2}-9/8.
\]

\begin{theorem}[An exact marked causal separation]
\label{thm:v17-markedgap}
At the width profile $(1,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-markedgap}
 \dpr(E_*,F_*)=d_*,\qquad
 \dhi_{2}(E_*,F_*)=1/8,\qquad
 \dvi_{r}(E_*,F_*)=d_*\quad(r\ge1).
\end{equation}
The hidden upper bound is realized by two deterministic tables.
The private upper bound is realized by two independent Bernoulli
variables with parameter $\sqrt{5/8}$, followed by the gate. These
bounds hold against every feedback rule, not just open-loop gates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First choose the constant gate $A=1$. The two private emissions are
independent Bernoulli variables with parameters $p,q$ and are
independent of $W$. Let
\[
 u=pq,\qquad v=(1-p)(1-q),\qquad a=3/8,\quad b=5/8.
\]
The overlap of the target and simulated marked laws is
$H(u)+H(v)$, where
\[
 H(t)=\tfrac12\{\min(t,a)+\min(t,b)\}.
\]
Indeed the target is supported on equal report pairs, and its two
$W$-masses at each equal pair are $3/16$ and $5/16$. Total variation
is one minus this overlap.

Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\sqrt u+\sqrt v\le1$. By symmetry suppose
$u\ge v$. If $u\le1/4$ the overlap is at most $1/2$. If $u\ge1/4$,
then $v\le(1-\sqrt u)^2\le1/4<a$, so the overlap is at most
\[
 H(u)+(1-\sqrt u)^2.
\]
This function is convex on each of $[1/4,a]$ and $[a,b]$ and
nonincreasing on $[b,1]$. Its maximum is therefore attained at
$1/4$, $a$ or $b$. The values at $a$ and $b$ are
$1+2a-2\sqrt a$ and $3/2+b-2\sqrt b$ respectively. The second is
larger, since
\[
 (3/2+b-2\sqrt b)-(1+2a-2\sqrt a)
 =3/8-\frac{1/2}{\sqrt a+\sqrt b}>0,
\]
using $\sqrt a>3/5$, $\sqrt b>3/4$. It also exceeds $1/2$.
Taking $p=q=\sqrt b$ attains this maximum, and hence the minimum
error is $1-(3/2+b-2\sqrt b)=d_*$.

For any hidden selector, the all-on report pair is still independent
of $W$. If its probabilities of $11$ and $00$ are $u,v$, then
$u+v\le1$. Concavity and monotonicity of $H$ give
\[
 H(u)+H(v)\le2H((u+v)/2)\le2H(1/2)=7/8.
\]
A fair hidden bit $J$, emitting $J$ twice, attains this overlap.
This requires precisely two deterministic selector tables and gives
error $1/8$. In particular hiding a seed does not erase the actual-mark
obstruction.

These all-on bounds are lower bounds for the feedback problem. For
the upper bounds first generate the corresponding all-on pair
$(X_1,X_2)$, emit $X_1$, and after receiving $A$ emit $AX_2$.
For a deterministic feedback $A=g(X_1)$ the full gated marked
transcript is the pushforward of the all-on marked pair. Applying the
same map to the target pair cannot increase total variation. The
construction is executable at width one: the second fresh Bernoulli
variable is independent in the private case, and the bit $J$ is the
priced persistent hidden selector in the hidden case. Randomized
feedback follows by conditioning on its independent random table.

Finally, in the visible case the two compared laws are
\[
 \sum_j\lambda_j\delta_j\otimes P^{q^j}_{g_j}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \sum_j\lambda_j\delta_j\otimes Q_{g_j}.
\]
The target selector is independent of $(B,W)$. The all-on gate in
every fiber has error at least $d_*$ in every private fiber. Joint
variation is the weighted sum of these fiber errors. Ignoring the
visible selector and using the private optimizer attains $d_*$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A full-dimensional collision preparation}
Consider two labeled equal-mass hard spheres in $\R^3$ with contact
distance $d$. The center of mass $Q=(q_1+q_2)/2$ ranges over any fixed
rational position box. Write $r=q_1-q_2$, $g=v_1-v_2$, and
$V=(v_1+v_2)/2$. Conditional on $(B,W)$ in
\eqref{eq:v17-marktable}, take the uniform product preparation on
\begin{align}
 r&=(-a_0,(2B-1)b_0,c_0),
 &a_0&\in[29/10,31/10],& b_0&\in[9/20,11/20],
 \label{eq:v17-packets}\\
 |c_0|&\le1/100,
 &g_x&\in[19/10,21/10],& |g_y|,|g_z|&\le1/100,\nonumber\\
 |V_x|,|V_y|&\le1/100,
 &&&|V_z-(2W-1)|&\le1/100.\nonumber
\end{align}
The boxes have positive volume in the full phase space. The actual
mark is $W=\1_{\{V_z>0\}}$; total momentum conservation retains it.
The informative quantity will instead be the transverse velocity
created at a collision. Put
\[
 h(z)=\operatorname{sgn}(z)\min\{1,(|z|-1/4)_+\}.
\]
This is a bounded $1$-Lipschitz detector which is identically zero
on the incoming transverse-velocity range.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform transverse collision signal]
\label{lem:v17-scattering}
For every preparation in \eqref{eq:v17-packets} and every
$d\in[9/10,11/10]$, exactly one collision occurs before time $3$.
Its time $t_c$ lies in $(4/5,3/2)$, and afterwards
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v17-signal}
 (2B-1)v_{1,y}>1/2,
 \qquad (2B-1)h(v_{1,y})>1/4.
\end{equation}
For $0<d\le2/5$ no collision occurs before time $3$, and the detector
is zero throughout that interval.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Up to a collision $r(t)=r+tg$. For $t\le4/5$, its $x$ coordinate
is at most $-29/10+(4/5)(21/10)=-61/50$, so its norm exceeds
$11/10$. At time $3/2$ the absolute coordinate bounds are
$1/4$, $113/200$ and $1/40$, whose squared sum is less than
$(9/10)^2$. Thus a first contact occurs in the stated interval.
At the free zero of the $x$ coordinate, whose time is at most $31/19$,
the transverse norm is less than $9/10$ as well. The first contact
therefore has $n_x<0$, where $n=r(t_c)/d$.
The entry contact is strictly incoming. The bounds above give
\[
 |n_x|^2\ge1-
 \frac{(113/200)^2+(1/40)^2}{(9/10)^2}>(77/100)^2,
 \qquad (2B-1)n_y\ge\frac{87}{220}.
\]
It follows that $-g\cdot n> (19/10)(77/100)-2/100=1443/1000$.
The equal-mass elastic collision rule is
\[
 v_1'=V+g/2-(g\cdot n)n,
 \qquad g'=g-2(g\cdot n)n.
\]
Consequently
\[
 (2B-1)v'_{1,y}>
 -3/200+\frac{1443}{1000}\frac{87}{220}>1/2.
\]
The relative velocity is outgoing, $r(t_c)\cdot g'>0$, so the squared
separation increases strictly for all later times. There are only two
spheres and no walls; no second collision is possible. This proves
\eqref{eq:v17-signal}. For $d\le2/5$ and $0\le t\le3$,
$|(r+tg)_y|\ge9/20-3/100=21/50>d$. Thus there is no collision.
The incoming $|v_{1,y}|\le3/200$ then makes $h$ zero.
\end{proof}

At time $2$ the physical target $F_{d,\sigma}$ reports
\[
 Y_1=\1_{\{h(v_{1,y}(2))+\sigma\xi_1>0\}}.
\]
It then receives a gate $A\in\{0,1\}$, and at time $3$ reports
\[
 Y_2=A\1_{\{h(v_{1,y}(3))+\sigma\xi_2>0\}},
\]
where the $\xi_i$ are independent standard normal variables,
independent of the preparation. The source $E_d$ has the same
preparation, evolution, control interface and actual mark, but emits
two constant reports. The gates do not change the particle motion.
This is a feedback acquisition experiment: taking $A=0$ suppresses
later information and taking $A=1$ exposes another noisy observation
of a collision-generated quantity.

\begin{theorem}[Collision-sensitive physical resource separation]
\label{thm:v17-physicalgap}
Let $d\in[9/10,11/10]$ and $0<\sigma\le1/12$. At retained width
$(1,1)$ the physical deficiencies obey, uniformly in $d$,
\begin{align}
 |\dpr(E_d,F_{d,\sigma})-d_*|&<1/300,
 &|\dvi_r(E_d,F_{d,\sigma})-d_*|&<1/300\quad(r\ge1),
 \label{eq:v17-physicalgap}\\
 |\dhi_2(E_d,F_{d,\sigma})-1/8|&<1/300.&&\nonumber
\end{align}
In particular private and visible error exceed $9/20$, while hidden
two-selector error is less than $13/100$. More generally the errors
in these three comparisons are at most
$2\Phi(-1/(4\sigma))$, which tends to zero as $\sigma\downarrow0$.
For $0<d\le2/5$ all three deficiencies are zero. The interacting and
noninteracting regimes use the same preparation and detector.
The conclusions are unchanged by simultaneously scaling positions,
diameter and observation times by any positive factor, with velocities
and detector noise unchanged.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Couple the ideal and physical target by the same $(B,W)$ and the same
feedback random table. Lemma~\ref{lem:v17-scattering} bounds each
potential bit error by $\Phi(-1/(4\sigma))$. With probability at least
$1-2\Phi(-1/(4\sigma))$ both potential reports agree with their ideal
bits; the gates and all emitted transcripts then agree as well.
This gives a marked feedback variation bound uniform over all gates.
The channels in both directions are the identity on the binary report
alphabet and have one retained state. The blind source is already
exactly $E_*$. Stateless comparison therefore changes each fixed-budget
deficiency by at most the stated error, without adding memory or
hiding an exposed selector.

For completeness, the elementary Gaussian tail bound
$\Phi(-x)\le e^{-x^2/2}/(x\sqrt{2\pi})$ at $x=3$ gives
$\Phi(-3)<1/600$: use $e^{9/2}>80$ and
$\sqrt{2\pi}>5/2$. Hence the total error is below $1/300$.
The inequalities $d_*-1/300>9/20$ and
$1/8+1/300<13/100$ follow by squaring positive rational bounds.

When $d\le2/5$, both potential reports are independent fair noise
bits and are independent of $W$. A private width-one simulator draws
those two bits and applies the current gate, exactly reproducing every
feedback transcript. Hidden and visible versions can ignore their
selectors. Finally elastic scattering is invariant under
$(q,d,t)\mapsto(sq,sd,st)$ with $v$ unchanged. This proves the scaling
statement; it is geometric similarity, not a particle-number or
Boltzmann--Grad limit.
\end{proof}

The theorem combines three effects. Collisions create the sign of the
informative transverse velocity. A gate chosen after the first report
changes future information. A retained hidden bit, but not a visible
one, permits temporal correlation without charging the width-one
register. The actual mark remains partially inaccessible in every
signature. The conserved longitudinal momentum labels that mark; it
does not generate the observed transverse signal.

\subsection{Uniformly effective finite presentations}
The binary physical interface is already finite, but its probabilities
are not generally rational. We make their effective approximation
explicit rather than treating individual computability as a uniform
algorithm.

\begin{proposition}[Collision-chart rational presentations]
\label{prop:v17-collisioncompute}
For computable $d\in[9/10,11/10]$ and $\sigma>0$, one algorithm
produces rational marked causal instruments and certified two-sided
stateless errors tending to zero for $(E_d,F_{d,\sigma})$. On a compact
computable parameter interval with $\sigma\ge\sigma_0>0$, the errors
and the construction are uniform. Thus
Proposition~\ref{prop:v17-effective} applies at every fixed finite
state and selector signature, not only at $(1,1)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On every preparation box the entry time and outgoing velocity are
\[
 t_c=\frac{-r\cdot g-\sqrt{(r\cdot g)^2-|g|^2(|r|^2-d^2)}}{|g|^2},
 \quad n=(r+t_cg)/d,
 \quad v_1'=V+g/2-(g\cdot n)n.
\]
The positive square root is separated from zero: it equals
$-d(g\cdot n)> (9/10)(1443/1000)$. All denominators are likewise
bounded away from zero. These explicit bounds and the compact boxes
allow interval differentiation of the displayed formulas, producing
a rational common Lipschitz upper bound $C_{\rm geo}$ for $v'_{1,y}$
in the packet variables and $d$. This computation terminates because
only addition, multiplication, division by separated positive
quantities and a separated square root occur.

Conditional on the microscopic preparation the two potential all-on
bits are independent Bernoulli with probability
$p=\Phi(h(v'_{1,y})/\sigma)$. Their four probabilities are
$p^2$, $p(1-p)$, $p(1-p)$ and $(1-p)^2$.
The normal distribution function is uniformly computable on bounded
intervals, for example by integrating its convergent exponential
series with a remainder bound. Its Lipschitz constant is less than
one. Together with the $1$-Lipschitz detector and the geometric bound,
this yields a computable Lipschitz constant at most
$2C_{\rm geo}/\sigma_0$ for these four integrands on fixed-noise
compact parameter sets. Equal-box Riemann sums of mesh $h$ therefore
have explicit error at most a computable constant times $h$, plus the
chosen rational evaluation error. Parameter uncertainty is included
by its interval enclosure.

Integrate the four all-on probabilities separately on each packet,
with the exact rational weights \eqref{eq:v17-marktable}. For each
$W$, round its conditional probability vector to a rational simplex
point on a sufficiently fine grid; the rounding error is bounded by
the sum of the coordinate errors and the grid mesh. Keep
$\Pp(W=0)=\Pp(W=1)=1/2$ exact. Sample this finite joint law once at
initialization, emit its first bit, and gate its second bit. This is
a finite latent realization of the target instrument, not a simulator
with extra free state. It makes every causal prefix consistent and
never divides by a possibly small prefix probability. Every gated
law is a pushforward of the all-on law, so the same marked variation
bound holds uniformly under feedback. Identity report channels give
both stateless directions. Choosing summable evaluation and mesh
errors produces the asserted uniform sequence.
\end{proof}
