# Literature crosswalk and novelty boundary

## Directly inspected primary texts

**T. M. Cover, “Hypothesis Testing with Finite Statistics,” Annals of Mathematical Statistics 40 (1969), 828–835; DOI 10.1214/aoms/1177697590.** The original eight-page scan was inspected, including Theorem 2 (pp. 833–834) and the explicit clock/memory discussion in Section 4 (p. 835). Bounded-state learning with time-dependent transitions is classical. The new finite block lemma and tournament do not claim that mechanism as novel. V21 connects a compact nonconvex response cover to a finite-horizon, rational, all-cut realization, gives its error and resource ledger, and uses it for the physical witness. These additional conclusions are proved in the article rather than inferred from bibliographic priority.

**H. Weisshaupt, “A General Randomization Theorem for Functionals of Statistical Experiments,” EURANDOM report 2002-017.** The filtered-operator definitions and Lemma 1 on p. 14, and Theorem 5 on pp. 15–16, were inspected. The relevant comparison uses filtered stochastic operators and compact convex operator classes, with a risk/randomization equivalence. V21's controllers are a specified product of finite stochastic row simplexes with state labels charged at every cut. Their induced behavior image need not be convex. No minimax interchange over that image is asserted. The full-profile realization supplies an explicit memory and simulation budget; it does not contradict or replace the classical filtered comparison theorem.

**M. E. Hellman and T. M. Cover, “Learning with Finite Memory,” Annals of Mathematical Statistics 41 (1970), 765–782**, and the finite-time finite-memory literature cited in the retained bibliography distinguish autonomous transition restrictions from a supplied clock. The v21 comparison theorem is in the clocked model. Its autonomous conversion is separately charged; the manuscript does not import a fixed-state, time-invariant lower bound into a time-varying model.

The inherited references and theorem/page crosswalk for causal realization, Nerode residual quotients, finite-state testing, Blackwell comparison and randomization remain in the article and archived v20 audit. Previously reported inspections are not relabeled as newly completed ones.
