\section{Finite precision and charged randomization}
\label{sec:v21-precision}
We distinguish the number of persistent labels from the description of a
transition and from the clock that selects it. An observation in a finite
alphabet is initially an atomic input. Every random bit used after that
input is a separate, charged epoch. Section~\ref{sec:v21-resources}
gives the additional cost of splitting the observation itself.

\begin{lemma}[Dyadic rows and finite randomizers]\label{lem:v21-dyadic}
A probability vector on $s$ points has a dyadic approximation with
denominator $2^r$ at total variation distance at most $(s-1)2^{-r}$.
It can be sampled using exactly $r$ independent fair bits and at most
$2s-1$ persistent labels at each intermediate bit epoch. The label count
uses the bit position supplied by the schedule. No partial random integer
is retained outside these labels.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Round down the first $s-1$ coordinates and assign the remaining mass to
the last coordinate. The positive and negative parts of the change have
equal mass, at most $(s-1)2^{-r}$. For sampling, partition
$\{0,\ldots,2^r-1\}$ into consecutive intervals of the prescribed integer
lengths. A binary prefix describes a dyadic interval. If that interval is
contained in one output interval, keep the output label and ignore the
remaining bits. Otherwise keep the prefix interval. At a fixed depth,
every unresolved interval contains a partition boundary in its interior;
there are at most $s-1$ such intervals. Together with at most $s$ resolved
outputs this gives $2s-1$ labels. The children and their resolved labels
are fixed by the read-only partition and the depth. Thus the construction
is an actual finite transition table, not a real-valued random seed.
Zero-length intervals cause no difficulty.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rational decision continuations]\label{prop:v21-rational}
Let $d=|\Omega|$. A list of $K$ responses in $[0,1]^\Omega$ can be
replaced by dyadic responses of denominator $2^r$, losing at most
$2^{1-r}$ in its uniform score envelope. After a decision cut retaining
$K$ labels, the randomized event for the chosen dyadic response can be
drawn using $r$ fair bits, at most $K(2d+1)$ labels during this draw, and
at most $K(d+1)$ labels afterwards. Keeping the event through one
candidate and one target validation requires at most $2K(d+1)$ labels
at the intervening atomic-observation cuts. Its final signed score has
three values.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Round every coordinate down. The sup-norm error is at most $2^{-r}$,
and $\sum_w|Q(w)-b(w)|\le2$. This proves the envelope estimate.
For a fixed rounded response $h$, draw a uniform dyadic integer $U$ and
choose $A_U=\{w:U<2^rh(w)\}$. These events are nested and have at most
$d+1$ distinct values. Their mean response is exactly $h$. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-dyadic} to their interval lengths, retaining the
chosen response index during sampling. Afterwards the event label and
the candidate-event indicator suffice; the target indicator emits their
difference. All event masks are read-only data fixed before observing
the candidate.
\end{proof}

For $K$ responses, their rounded coordinates require $Kd(r+1)$ bits in
a direct read-only representation. These bits are not charged as
persistent sample-dependent labels, but are not declared costless
precision. A construction using them records both quantities. In
particular, the proposition turns every strict response-cover margin
into a finite-randomness implementation; it says nothing about exact
attainment of an irrational boundary value.
