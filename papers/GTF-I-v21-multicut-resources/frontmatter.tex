\begin{abstract}
We study causal certification when the auditor's register is charged at
every acquisition, randomization and validation cut. For a compact,
possibly nonconvex, behavior image on a finite alphabet of size $d$, the
strict decision-continuation complexity $\kappa_c$ and the minimum
whole-schedule peak width $W_c$ satisfy $\kappa_c\le W_c\le12d\kappa_c$
in the externally clocked model. A finite-horizon tournament gives a
rational realization with explicit preparation, calibration and
precision costs, without storing an empirical histogram. Compatible
approximate continuations give full-profile error transfer, and a shared
word-capacity obstruction converts multiple cut constraints into
statistical regret. Growing point-source and overlapping-retention
families provide quantitative lower bounds. For a marked two-sphere
collision experiment we retain the exact three-state decision optimum
and the short $399$-trial construction, and give a second audit with at
most twelve states throughout its bit-level schedule. Independent
repetition yields confidence guarantees with a charged accumulator.
The physical certificate and a bounded entropic conditional-law consumer
remain stable under common-preparation Gaussian transport.
\end{abstract}
