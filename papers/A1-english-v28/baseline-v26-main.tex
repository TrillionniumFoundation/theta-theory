\begin{abstract}
For positive sparse monomial experiments at a fixed horizon, we determine
optimal finite-memory prediction uniformly through all additive
collisions of the exponents. The error is given by maximal Vandermonde
products of the future exponents, truncated by the dimension acquired
by the past. The same profile governs every integer memory budget,
both under one unconditional acquisition law and over all admitted
histories, and is attained by one causal finite-state filter.
The proof establishes simultaneous acquisition of complete confluent
flags, a dimension-truncated cover of the whole posterior image, and
updates in raw moments with no reciprocal collision gap. A
four-cell experiment exhibits six-, eight- and nine-dimensional
regimes at intersecting collision loci, with two separated memory
crossovers along paths of arbitrary finite contact order.
For independent experiments indexed by a fixed graph, we further identify
the optimal shared memory under history-dependent vertex-batch scheduling.
It is governed by a resolution-dependent weighted cutwidth. A common
positive-mass event and a finite-trace small-ball argument give the
adaptive converse. A three-edge experiment exhibits a change of the
optimal order with accuracy and an unbounded bit penalty for keeping
one order at two separated resolutions.
A quantitative refinement replaces complete traces by separating sets
of the visited-set lattice and retains only first-block evidences in
the common event. Along analytic collision paths we obtain a finite
piecewise affine law for optimal memory and uniform-resolution order
regret. The same three-edge experiment has a nonconvex four-phase law;
its best fixed-order penalty on a resolution interval is twice the
corresponding penalty at the two endpoints.
\end{abstract}
