\begin{abstract}
We classify finite-memory prediction in positive sparse monomial
experiments at a fixed horizon, uniformly through additive collisions of
exponents. The optimal error is determined by maximal Vandermonde
products of future exponents, truncated by the dimension acquired by the
past. The classification holds for every integer memory budget, under
one unconditional acquisition law and in the worst-history criterion,
and is attained by a single causal finite-state filter. Its geometric
content is the simultaneous attainment of complete confluent flags and
a dimension-truncated cover of the whole posterior image; raw remaining
moments give updates whose bounds do not involve reciprocal collision
gaps. We also prove a prior-uniform covariance law for bounded finite
observation algebras. In that class acquisition saturates after one
observation, and a direct ellipsoid argument gives the complete
multistep law through support and rank loss. The full proofs of the
associated rank, moment-pairing, operational reconstruction, uncertainty,
and effective-realization results are included in the appendices.
\end{abstract}
