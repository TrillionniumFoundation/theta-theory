\section{Posterior geometry and information loss}\label{sec:information}
\subsection{Posterior barycentres}
The following statements concern a common preparation. Let $Z$ be a hidden
target, $Y$ an original observation, and $W$ a parameter-independent
postprocessing of $Y$. Conditional independence is denoted by the Markov
chain $Z\to Y\to W$.

\begin{theorem}[Posterior barycentre and convex order]\label{thm:barycentre}
For bounded measurable $f_1,\ldots,f_q$ of $Z$, put
\[
 p_Y=(\E[f_i(Z)\mid Y])_i,
 \qquad p_W=(\E[f_i(Z)\mid W])_i.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barycentre}
 p_W=\E[p_Y\mid W].
\end{equation}
Consequently $\E\varphi(p_W)\le\E\varphi(p_Y)$ for every convex function
for which the expectations are defined. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barycentre-variance}
 \Cov(p_Y)-\Cov(p_W)=\E\Cov(p_Y\mid W)\succeq0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Markov property gives
$\E[f_i(Z)\mid Y,W]=\E[f_i(Z)\mid Y]$. Taking conditional expectation
with respect to $W$ proves \eqref{eq:barycentre}. Conditional Jensen yields
the convex-order inequality. Subtract the common mean and expand the
orthogonal decomposition
$p_Y-\E p_Y=(p_Y-p_W)+(p_W-\E p_W)$; its cross covariance is zero by
conditioning on $W$. This gives \eqref{eq:barycentre-variance}.
\end{proof}

For a measure-valued posterior, the same identity holds against every
bounded test. It is a conditional barycentre, not a function sending each
original posterior to a single coarser posterior. In particular the image
of a finite set of original posteriors may generate a continuous family of
conditional barycentres. This is why convex-order monotonicity is not a
statement about the cardinality of a predictive representation.

\subsection{Relative entropy and recovery}
For $P\ll Q$, set
$D(P\|Q)=\int\log(dP/dQ)\,dP$, allowing $+\infty$; set it to infinity
otherwise. Let $G:X\rightsquigarrow W$ be a Markov kernel and form the
joint laws $\bar P(dx,dw)=P(dx)G(dw\mid x)$ and
$\bar Q(dx,dw)=Q(dx)G(dw\mid x)$.

\begin{theorem}[Entropy loss and a common recovery kernel]\label{thm:entropy-gap}
If $D(P\|Q)<\infty$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-gap}
 D(P\|Q)=D(PG\|QG)+
 \int D(\bar P(dx\mid w)\|\bar Q(dx\mid w))\,PG(dw).
\end{equation}
In particular, entropy decreases under $G$. Suppose a family $P_\lambda$
is dominated by one $P_0$, every $D(P_\lambda\|P_0)$ is finite, and
\[
 D(P_\lambda G\|P_0G)=D(P_\lambda\|P_0)
 \quad\text{for every }\lambda.
\]
Then $R(w,dx)=\bar P_0(dx\mid w)$ is a parameter-independent recovery
kernel satisfying $P_\lambda GR=P_\lambda$ for every $\lambda$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The joint likelihood ratio is $d\bar P/d\bar Q(x,w)=dP/dQ(x)$.
Disintegrating both joint laws gives the factorization into the marginal
likelihood ratio on $W$ and the conditional likelihood ratio on $X$.
Take logarithms and integrate. Standard truncation of the densities
justifies the formula when logarithms are not bounded; finite total
entropy makes the nonnegative conditional entropy integrable. This proves
\eqref{eq:entropy-gap}. Alternatively the marginal ratio is the conditional
expectation under $\bar Q$ of $dP/dQ$, and conditional Jensen proves the
inequality directly, also for infinite entropy.

For the family, equality makes the conditional entropy vanish
$P_\lambda G$-almost everywhere. Hence the two conditional distributions
coincide there. The conditional law under the one reference $P_0$ is the
same measurable kernel for all parameters; $P_\lambda G\ll P_0G$ handles
its null-set convention. Integrating that conditional law against
$P_\lambda G$ recovers $P_\lambda$.
\end{proof}

This is a sufficient recovery criterion under a common reference. An
isolated equality for one pair does not certify an unrelated parameter
family. Nor does it make the recovery kernel causal or memory free.

\subsection{Scores and Fisher information}
To avoid hiding regularity in notation, first take a finite observation
space with $p_\lambda(y)>0$ and continuously differentiable probabilities
on an open parameter domain in $\R^k$. Let
\[
 s_\lambda(y)=\nabla_\lambda\log p_\lambda(y),
 \qquad\mathcal I_\lambda=\E_\lambda[s_\lambda s_\lambda^{\mathsf T}].
\]
If $G(w\mid y)$ is a parameter-independent finite channel, restrict to
output symbols having positive probability; identically impossible symbols
can be removed.

\begin{theorem}[Score projection]\label{thm:score}
The processed score and information satisfy
\begin{align}
 s_\lambda^G(W)&=\E_\lambda[s_\lambda(Y)\mid W],\label{eq:score-proj}\\
 \mathcal I_\lambda-\mathcal I_\lambda^G
 &=\E_\lambda\Cov_\lambda(s_\lambda(Y)\mid W)\succeq0.
 \label{eq:fisher-gap}
\end{align}
For a direction $v$, equality of information in that direction is equivalent
to $v^{\mathsf T}s_\lambda(Y)$ being determined by $W$ almost surely.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate $q_\lambda(w)=\sum_y p_\lambda(y)G(w\mid y)$ and divide by
$q_\lambda(w)$. The coefficients are precisely the conditional
probabilities of $Y$ given $W$, proving \eqref{eq:score-proj}.
Differentiating $\sum_y p_\lambda(y)=1$ gives $\E_\lambda s_\lambda=0$.
The conditional variance identity now gives \eqref{eq:fisher-gap}.
Its quadratic form in $v$ is the mean conditional variance of
$v^{\mathsf T}s_\lambda$, which vanishes exactly under the stated
measurability condition.
\end{proof}

The same proof works in a dominated differentiable model if differentiation
under the integral is justified and the score lies in $L^2$. More generally,
the QMD version is the classical score-projection theorem described by
Pollard~\cite{Pollard2013}; when it is invoked, quadratic-mean
differentiability is an additional statistical hypothesis. The raw
experiment axioms by themselves impose no differentiability.

Information equality in \eqref{eq:fisher-gap} is a statement at a parameter
point. It is not the recovery conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:entropy-gap}.
Even information preservation throughout a model need not make a statistic
sufficient without stronger assumptions; see \cite{Pollard2013}.

Under a smooth parameter map $\lambda=r(\zeta)$, the information pulls
back as $Dr^{\mathsf T}\mathcal I\,Dr$. Under a deterministic linear
map of a random vector, its covariance pushes forward as $A\Sigma A^{\mathsf T}$.
These are different operations on different objects. Neither formula says
that an arbitrary covariance equals inverse Fisher information.

\subsection{Sequential information in finite positive models}
A useful controlled specialization follows without differentiating the
controller. Suppose the conditional report probabilities are finite,
positive, and differentiable, and the feedback policy has no explicit
unknown-parameter dependence. Along a history define the local score
\[
 \xi_{t+1}=\nabla_\lambda\log
 p_\lambda(Y_{t+1}\mid H_t,A_t).
\]
The policy factors contribute no score. Conditional normalization gives
$\E_\lambda[\xi_{t+1}\mid H_t,A_t]=0$.

\begin{proposition}[Sequential score decomposition]\label{prop:sequential-score}
If the local scores are square integrable, the path score is
$S_N=\sum_{t<N}\xi_{t+1}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sequential-info}
 \E_\lambda[S_NS_N^{\mathsf T}]
 =\sum_{t<N}\E_\lambda[
 \xi_{t+1}\xi_{t+1}^{\mathsf T}].
\end{equation}
For bounded stopping, each term is restricted to active steps. No
independence of successive reports is required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The likelihood factors into conditional report probabilities and
parameter-independent policy factors. Its logarithmic derivative is the
displayed sum. For $s<t$, $\xi_{s+1}$ is measurable before $\xi_{t+1}$,
so their cross moment is zero by the conditional mean-zero property.
Expanding the square proves \eqref{eq:sequential-info}. A bounded stopping
indicator is predictable for the next increment; the same argument applies.
\end{proof}

This is an information identity for the actual controlled law. It does not
make an information-optimal strategy automatically prediction-memory
optimal, and it does not supply an asymptotic normal approximation.
