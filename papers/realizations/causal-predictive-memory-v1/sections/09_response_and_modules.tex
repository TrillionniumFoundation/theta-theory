\section{Normalized response and observation modules}\label{sec:response}
\subsection{The covariance pairing}
Let $\pi$ be a probability measure and $f,g$ bounded measurable functions.
For $u$ near zero define
\[
 \pi_u(dx)=\frac{e^{ug(x)}\pi(dx)}{\pi(e^{ug})}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Normalized response]\label{prop:normalized-response}
The derivative of a normalized prediction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-response}
 \left.\frac{d}{du}\pi_u(f)\right|_{u=0}
 =\pi(fg)-\pi(f)\pi(g)=\Cov_\pi(f,g).
\end{equation}
More generally, if $L_u>0$ is differentiable with the dominated derivatives
needed for the quotient rule, then the derivative at $u_0$ of
$\pi(fL_u)/\pi(L_u)$ is the covariance of $f$ with
$\partial_u\log L_u|_{u_0}$ under the normalized law at $u_0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate numerator and denominator. At zero their values are $\pi(f)$
and one, and their derivatives are $\pi(fg)$ and $\pi(g)$. This proves
\eqref{eq:normalized-response}. Apply the same quotient rule at $u_0$
and divide the derivative of $L_u$ by $L_{u_0}$ to obtain the general
statement. Boundedness, or the stated domination in the general case,
justifies integration of the derivatives.
\end{proof}

For vector tests and several commands this gives a cross-covariance matrix.
One index may represent an acquisition direction and the other a future
test. That matrix is not automatically the Fisher information for an
unknown physical parameter. In \eqref{eq:refresh-update}, command changes
are selected by the observer; in Theorem~\ref{thm:score}, parameter changes
vary the family of physical laws. The similar algebra does not equate the
two statistical problems.

\subsection{Finite positive functionals and exact product fibres}
Suppose a real algebra of functions contains finite-dimensional spaces
$U_1,\ldots,U_L$, and let $V$ contain all their products. A parameter is a
linear functional $\ell\in\Omega\subset V^*$, where $\Omega$ is open in
$V^*$. Positivity of measured blocks may define this open set. The observed
bilinear forms are
\[
 B_\nu(f,g)=\ell(fg),\qquad f,g\in U_\nu.
\]
There are no cross-contact products unless the experiment actually measures
them. Set
\[
 W=\sum_{\nu=1}^L\Span\{fg:f,g\in U_\nu\}\subset V.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Product-observation fibre]\label{thm:product-fibre}
The observation fibre through $\ell$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-fibre}
 (\ell+W^\perp)\cap\Omega.
\end{equation}
Its local dimension is $\dim V-\dim W$. Recovery of every functional in
$\Omega$ is equivalent to $W=V$. If the parameter domain is relatively
open in an affine subspace with direction $L\subset V^*$, the local
invisible space is instead $L\cap W^\perp$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Two observations agree precisely when the difference functional vanishes
on every generating product, equivalently on $W$. This gives
\eqref{eq:product-fibre}. Openness allows all sufficiently small
perturbations in the annihilator, so the local dimension is its dimension.
The affine-domain version restricts those perturbations to $L$.
\end{proof}

This is linear duality. The difficult model-specific work can lie in
proving that these forms are actually observed, determining $W$, describing
the locus where its dimension drops, and controlling real positive
realizability. For the calibrated Hankel family of \cite{QiA2},
$V$ is a polynomial space and the observation is a normal compression of an
inverse information matrix. The theorem supplies its abstract fibre
mechanism, not the paper's complete degeneracy classification.

\subsection{The contact calculation}
For clarity, the local geometric interface can also be derived directly.
Let $M\subset\R^k$ have an embedded smooth germ at $x_0$, and let $R>0$.
Assume the local minimizing projection onto that germ computes the global
distance in a neighborhood of $x_0$. Define
\[
 C_R(b)=\inf_{x\in M}(x-b)^{\mathsf T}R^{-1}(x-b).
\]
Let $Z$ have orthonormal columns spanning the Euclidean normal space at
$x_0$, and put $B=Z^{\mathsf T}RZ$.

\begin{proposition}[Normal second jet]\label{prop:normal-jet}
Under these local projection assumptions,
\[
 C_R(x_0+h)=h^{\mathsf T}ZB^{-1}Z^{\mathsf T}h+o(\norm h^2),
 \qquad D^2C_R(x_0)=2ZB^{-1}Z^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Consequently the full second jet determines exactly the positive normal
block $B$ and has the tangent space as its kernel.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Replace the germ by its tangent space $T$ to compute the quadratic term.
The first-order minimizing condition for $\min_{v\in T}(h-v)^{\mathsf T}
R^{-1}(h-v)$ says $R^{-1}(h-v)=Zn$ for some $n$. Hence
$h-v=RZn$, and normal projection gives $Z^{\mathsf T}h=Bn$.
The minimum is $(Z^{\mathsf T}h)^{\mathsf T}B^{-1}(Z^{\mathsf T}h)$.
A smooth local parametrization of the germ has derivative spanning $T$;
its tangent Gram matrix in the positive $R^{-1}$ metric is invertible.
The implicit function theorem gives a differentiable minimizing parameter
with the stated linear term. The quadratic expansion and the projection
assumption identify it with $C_R$ near $x_0$. Positive block inversion is
one-to-one, proving the observation assertion.
\end{proof}

If $R=JH(\theta)J^{\mathsf T}$ with Hankel entries
$H(\theta)_{ab}=\theta_{a+b}$, then $v^{\mathsf T}Rw$ evaluates the
moment functional on the product of the two corresponding coefficient
polynomials. Theorem~\ref{thm:product-fibre} applies after this explicit
identification. A higher-order distance jet has additional curvature and
projection terms; replacing $\operatorname{Sym}^2$ by
$\operatorname{Sym}^m$ is not a derivation of a higher-order experiment.

\subsection{Why general dynamic tests need not form a finite algebra}
\begin{proposition}[Finite continuous algebras have finite quotients]
\label{prop:finite-algebra}
Let $X$ be compact Hausdorff and let $W\subset C(X,\R)$ be a finite-dimensional
unital subalgebra. Then $W$ is the algebra of all functions constant on a
finite clopen partition of $X$. The number of nonempty cells is $\dim W$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $f\in W$, sufficiently many powers of $f$ are linearly dependent.
A nonzero polynomial therefore vanishes on $f(X)$, so that image is finite.
A finite basis of $W$ has only finitely many joint values; its fibres form a
finite clopen partition. Every member of $W$ is constant on these fibres.
Conversely, for a joint value $a$, choose for every distinct joint value $b$
a basis function $f_{i(a,b)}$ separating them. The product
\[
 \prod_{b\ne a}
 \frac{f_{i(a,b)}-f_{i(a,b)}(b)}
      {f_{i(a,b)}(a)-f_{i(a,b)}(b)}
\]
is in $W$ and is the indicator of the fibre at $a$. These indicators are
linearly independent and span all functions on the partition, which proves
the result.
\end{proof}

Thus a finite multiplication-closed continuous algebra is a useful model,
but it cannot by itself represent arbitrary continuous predictive
information. Growing test spaces and positive operator words are the
more general primitive. Neither the algebra dimension nor one tangent rank
replaces the actual predictive law or its quantization geometry.
