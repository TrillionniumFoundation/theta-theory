\section{Two-ended certificates and the remaining scalar source}
\label{sec:v102-certificate}
The clock has an explicit geometric sufficient test. In particular,
short original residence is not by itself a reason for transverse
failure. The test also states which additional control is still
required on the full paired family.

\begin{theorem}[A relative two-ended transverse certificate]
\label{thm:v102-certificate}
Fix a starting pair $(\theta_0,s)$ with $0<\ell(\theta_0)\le\delta$
and $c^\varepsilon(\theta_0)>0$. Suppose that in at least one angular
direction there is an interval of length $L_\theta>0$ in the same
regular pairing chamber on which, at this fixed relative coordinate,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v102-local-certificate}
 |v|\ge\nu>0,\qquad
 |\partial_\theta\log\ell|\le A,\qquad
 |\partial_\theta\log c^\varepsilon|\le A,\qquad
 |\partial_\theta\log|v||\le A
\end{equation}
almost everywhere. Positive $\ell,c^\varepsilon$ and the sign of
$v$ persist on this interval. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v102-certificate-radius}
 0<r\le\nu\min\{L_\theta,\log 2/(3A)\},
\end{equation}
with the second term interpreted as infinity when $A=0$, then
$k_{r,2\delta}^\perp(\theta_0,s)\ge1/2$.
The conclusion has no lower bound on $\ell(\theta_0)$.

A sufficient condition for the bounds on $v$, simultaneously for
all $s\in(0,1)$, is that $a',b'$ have the same sign,
$|a'|,|b'|\ge\nu$, and
$|a''|\le A|a'|$, $|b''|\le A|b'|$ on that interval.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose the roof direction corresponding to the supplied angular
side and the sign of $v$. Since $d\mathscr F/d\theta=v$, every
roof displacement of size at most $r$ is reached before an angular
displacement of $r/\nu$. Positivity and the logarithmic bounds give
\[
 \ell(\theta)\le e^{Ar/\nu}\ell(\theta_0)
                      \le2^{1/3}\delta<2\delta,
 \qquad
 \frac{\varrho^\varepsilon(\theta,s)}
      {\varrho^\varepsilon(\theta_0,s)}
                      \ge e^{-3Ar/\nu}\ge\frac12.
\]
Thus the entire one-sided comparison interval exists in
$\mathcal D_{2\delta}^\varepsilon$, and its contribution to
\eqref{eq:v102-clock} is at least $1/2$. A statement at an interval
endpoint is obtained from smaller times and monotone convergence.
The last assertion follows because $v=(1-s)a'+sb'$ is a convex
combination of two derivatives of the same sign and
$|\partial_\theta v|\le A((1-s)|a'|+s|b'|)=A|v|$.
\end{proof}

For example, on an actual chamber satisfying
$a=a_0+v_0\theta$, $b=a+\ell_0$ and
$c^\varepsilon=c_0e^{\beta\theta}$, the Jacobian is exactly
$\exp(\beta h/v_0)$ and the admissible radius is independent of
$\ell_0>0$. This calculation is a test of the physical-chamber
hypotheses, not an alternative realization of the Lorentz problem.
A lower residence cutoff would miss precisely this uniformity.
Conversely, at a backward critical entrance $a'=0$ one has
$v=s b'$; the theorem can apply for interior $s$, but gives no
uniform conclusion as $s$ tends to zero. That possible positive
critical trace remains in the failure source.

Keep the original schedule from the preceding scalar theorem:
\[
 \varepsilon=\varepsilon(B)=A_0B^{-1/12},\quad
 q_0^*=1/8,\quad r_0=c\varepsilon^4,\quad
 a_0=\sqrt{q_0^*r_0},\quad\delta=6q_0^*r_0.
\]
The starred symbol denotes only the fixed clock threshold, not the
optical contraction constant $q_0$. Let $\mathsf S$ be the original
low-clock short residual for this schedule. Independently choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v102-new-schedule}
 \Delta=2\delta,\qquad r_\perp=\delta,\qquad
                         q_\perp=\varepsilon^{1/32}.
\end{equation}
Define the actual nonnegative remaining paired source
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v102-transverse-residual}
 \mathsf U_B(t)=T_{\mathsf S,\mathrm{fail}}^{\varepsilon(B)}(t)
       \quad\text{for }(r_\perp,\Delta,q_\perp)
                  \text{ in }\eqref{eq:v102-new-schedule}.
\end{equation}
It includes every positive paired trace not admitted by the
transverse comparison, including nonregular pairing strata of
positive paired mass and zero transverse speed. The target selector
is still the original $\mathsf S$.

\begin{theorem}[Schedule-compatible positive-trace reduction]
\label{thm:v102-scalar}
Under the retained physical and four-record analytic inputs and the
proved uniform-capacity theorem, for all sufficiently large $B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v102-scalar}
               \mathcal E_M\le\mathcal H_M(\mathsf U_B)
                                      +C_MB^{-1/384}.
\end{equation}
The admitted part of the positive paired trace satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v102-paid-part}
 \mathcal H_M(T_{\mathsf S,\mathrm{adm}}^{\varepsilon(B)})
                    \le C(\varepsilon^{1/32}
                                      +\varepsilon^{95/32}).
\end{equation}
Along this schedule the original pointwise theorem is equivalent
to $\mathcal H_M(\mathsf U_B)\to0$ for every fixed $M$.
No vanishing assertion about $\mathsf U_B$ is made here.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The paired trace splits positively and exactly into the admitted
and failure sources at finite collision count. Insert
\eqref{eq:v102-new-schedule} in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v102-admitted-height}. Since $\Delta=O(\varepsilon^4)$,
its two costs are $\varepsilon^{1/16-1/32}$ and
$O(\varepsilon^{4-1-1/32})$, proving
\eqref{eq:v102-paid-part}. The previously established scalar bound
has the additional terms
$C_MB^{-1/192}$, $O(\varepsilon^{257/64})$ and
$O(\varepsilon^3)$. Each is dominated by a constant times
$B^{-1/384}$, proving \eqref{eq:v102-scalar}. Every new comparison
width and threshold is fixed as a function of $B$ before the
collision limit; no band or cutoff depends on that count.

Sufficiency follows from \eqref{eq:v102-scalar}. For necessity,
scalar closure and the positive scalar remainder make the original
bad source small in the prescribed ordered height. The selected
short volume source is below it. The established uniform short
trace-comparison error is $O((1+\varepsilon^{-1})\delta)$, so the
whole positive $T_{\mathsf S}^\varepsilon$ tends to zero.
Since $0\le\mathsf U_B\le T_{\mathsf S}^\varepsilon$, its height
also tends to zero. This argument does not require transversality
of a critical entrance or a bound on the number of physical sheets.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:v102-certificate} discharges transverse admission
on every physical chamber family satisfying its numerical tests.
It does not establish uniform tests for all actual pairs. Controlling
the remaining mass at short angular reach, nearly stationary boundary
roofs or rapid relative trace variation is the outstanding geometric
step. This is a positive boundary source, not a signed cancellation,
an averaged target, or a replacement exact event. The uniform
pressure theorem alone does not provide these transverse tests.
The original upper pointwise half and unrestricted point-conditioned
consequences are therefore not promoted. Their target and arithmetic
restrictions are unchanged.
