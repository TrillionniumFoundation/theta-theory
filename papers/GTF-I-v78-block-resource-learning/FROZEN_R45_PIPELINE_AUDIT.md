## 4. Detailed audit: local binary lemma

### 4.1 Statistic and derivative

For `p<q` with `q-p <= N^(-1/2)`, the proof fixes

```text
b = N(p+q)/2
```

and takes a clipped ramp with affine interval `[b-4sqrt(N),b+4sqrt(N)]` and slope `1/(8sqrt(N))`.

If `S ~ Bin(N,t)` and `T ~ Bin(N-1,t)`, direct differentiation of the binomial polynomial gives

```text
(d/dt) E_t f(S)
  = N E_t[f(T+1)-f(T)].
```

For `t in [p,q]`,

```text
|E_t T-b| <= sqrt(N)/2+1.
```

On `|T-E_tT| <= sqrt(N)`, both `T` and `T+1` remain inside the affine interval. Since `Var(T)<=N/4`, Chebyshev assigns this event probability at least `3/4`. The derivative is therefore at least `3sqrt(N)/32`. Integration over `[p,q]` proves the local branch.

For `q-p>N^(-1/2)`, the intermediate parameter `q_0=p+N^(-1/2)` lies below `q`. The ramp is nondecreasing, so stochastic monotonicity of binomials gives at least the local constant at `q_0`. The proof remains valid at `p=0` and `q=1`.

### 4.2 Metric convention

Total variation is the half-`l1` convention in the binary lemma. The adaptive instrument metric is an unhalved final-state trace norm. Hence the factor of two in the Choi corollary is required and present.

### 4.3 Pair-specificity

The ramp depends on `p,q`, and the Choi spectral projection depends on `J,L`. This is acceptable in a pairwise packing lower bound. The theorem does not provide a common decoder or simultaneous identification measurement.

## 5. Detailed audit: joint interior description law

### 5.1 Affine dimension

There are `m` Hermitian Choi blocks of size `dn`, giving real ambient dimension `m d^2 n^2`. The joint trace-preservation constraint is one Hermitian `d x d` equality, imposing `d^2` real conditions. Hence

```text
s = d^2(mn^2-1).
```

### 5.2 Interior ball

The central outcome blocks are scalar identities with eigenvalue `1/(mn)`. In the affine translation space, a tuple Frobenius perturbation of norm at most

```text
R = 1/(mn)-a
```

has every block operator norm at most `R`, so every block remains at least `aI`.

### 5.3 Packing constant

Take a maximal set separated by

```text
h = 32 d delta / sqrt(N).
```

Maximality makes `h`-balls cover the affine `R`-ball, so volume comparison gives at least `(R/h)^s` points when this exceeds one.

For any two packing points, the local Choi bound gives

```text
(3/16) min{sqrt(N)h/(2d),1}
 = (3/16) min{16delta,1}.
```

If the first branch is active, the separation is `3delta`. If saturated, it is `3/16`, which is greater than `2delta` for `delta<=1/32`. One legal decoded center cannot cover two packing points.

### 5.4 Upper bound and uniform remainder

The inherited rational intrinsic code uses `s` affine coordinates and grid scale proportional to `sqrt(N)/delta`, with dimension- and margin-dependent constants. Its decoder image is legal. Combining the finite upper and lower inequalities gives a remainder independent of both `N` and `delta` over the entire declared range.

This step does not convert the theorem into a variable-dimensional statement and does not make the constants uniform as `a` tends to zero.

## 6. Detailed audit: preparation metric identity

### 6.1 One-use object

The preparation instrument outputs the cq block state

```text
sigma_block = direct_sum_y sigma_y
```

with total trace one. The input system is discarded.

### 6.2 Adaptive processor representation

At each call, regardless of the tester's input/reference state, the instrument supplies a fresh copy of the same cq state. All tester operations between calls, including coherent quantum memory, feedback based on the classical outcome, and bounded public stopping, can be absorbed into one common CPTP map taking `N` copies of the cq state to the final tester state.

The same map is used under both hypotheses. Trace-norm contraction gives


### 6.3 Equality

## 7. Detailed audit: singular Cholesky factors

### 7.1 Zero-pivot rule

For a positive semidefinite residual matrix `A`, if `A_jj=0`, the `2 x 2` principal-minor inequality gives

```text
|A_ij|^2 <= A_ii A_jj = 0.
```

Thus the entire residual row and column vanish. Skipping that column is exact.

### 7.2 Rank and coordinate count

Every positive pivot creates one linearly independent factor column. The number of positive pivots equals the rank.

For a pivot at position `j`, the factor column contributes one real diagonal coordinate and `2(n-j-1)` real coordinates below it. For at most `r` pivots, the maximum occurs at the earliest positions and equals

```text
r + 2[(n-1)+...+(n-r)]
 = r(2n-r).
```

Across blocks, the total number of available coordinates is at most `D`; trace normalization restricts them to a unit sphere of dimension at most `v=D-1`.

### 7.3 Rational squared coordinates

The residual matrices remain Gaussian-rational. A diagonal factor square is a rational pivot. An off-diagonal factor coordinate has the form

```text
residual component / sqrt(pivot),
```

so its square is rational, and its sign is the sign of the rational residual component. The encoder can therefore compute every directed ratio digit using integer arithmetic and rational comparison.

### 7.4 Integer square roots

For a nonnegative rational `q`,

```text
floor(sqrt(q)) = isqrt(floor(q)).
```

Consequently

```text
floor(B sqrt(q))
 = isqrt(floor(B^2 q)).
```

The implementation uses this identity.

### 7.5 Anchor and normalization

The factor coordinate vector has Euclidean norm one. Its largest coordinate has magnitude at least `1/sqrt(h)`. Dividing by that anchor makes the anchor exactly `+1` or `-1`. Rounding the other `h-1` coordinates at denominator `B` incurs Euclidean error below `sqrt(h-1)/B`. Renormalization increases the comparison by at most a factor two.

### 7.6 Decoded legality

For integer factor matrices `Z_y`, set

```text
tilde_sigma_y = Z_y Z_y^* / T,
T = sum_y tr(Z_y Z_y^*).
```

The anchor makes `T>0`. Every block is PSD and total trace is one. The number of nonzero factor columns cannot exceed the target's number of pivots, so block ranks do not increase. A target zero block has no pivots, no coordinates, and decodes exactly to zero.

## 8. Detailed audit: product error and rank converse

### 8.1 Factor purification

Vectorizing all factor blocks, including the classical outcome label, gives a purification of the cq preparation state. Euclidean distance of normalized factor coordinate vectors is the Hilbert norm of the two purification vectors.

For unit vectors `u,v`,

```text
|| |u><u|^⊗N - |v><v|^⊗N ||_1
 <= 2 sqrt(N) ||u-v||_2.
```

Partial trace cannot increase trace norm. Combining this with the factor rounding bound proves the adaptive estimate.

### 8.2 Maximal stratum dimension

For one rank-`r` block, use an `n x r` lower-trapezoidal factor with positive leading diagonal. The real parameter count is

```text
r + 2 sum_{j=1}^r (n-j)
 = r(2n-r).
```

For all nonzero-rank outcomes the total is `D`. Unit total Frobenius norm removes one coordinate, giving `v=D-1`.

### 8.3 Gauge fixing and inverse

Positive leading diagonal fixes the right-unitary factor gauge. The leading square block has the standard Cholesky inverse. Remaining lower rows are recovered from the bottom-left part by multiplication with the inverse adjoint of the leading factor. Hence the factor-to-state map is a smooth local diffeomorphism on the patch.

Shrinking to a compact coordinate ball gives quantitative bi-Lipschitz constants. This is local; no global chart over all support patterns is asserted.

### 8.4 Packing

A `v`-dimensional coordinate ball yields a packing of cardinality proportional to `(sqrt(N)/delta)^v`. The state images have tuple Frobenius separation sufficient for the local binary test. Triangle inequality excludes one decoded center from covering two packing points.

### 8.5 Smaller ranks

The upper code includes every smaller-rank stratum because actual pivot count `h` may be below `D`. These strata have no larger dimension. The maximal rank patch already supplies the lower exponent.

## 9. Implementation audit

### 9.1 `preparation_blocks`

The implementation checks the exact input-erasing Choi form: every input-diagonal block equals the same output block, and every off-diagonal input block is zero. It rejects an identity channel even though that channel is CP/TP.

### 9.2 `triangular_data`

The routine performs the singular no-pivot Schur recursion over exact `Fraction` data. It rejects negative residual pivots and zero pivots with nonzero residual rows. It records pivot positions and signed squared real coordinates. It verifies that all squared coordinates sum exactly to one.

### 9.3 Body encoding

The body stores:

- anchor index;
- anchor sign;
- all other signed grid digits.

The pivot sets are separate charged metadata. The decoder checks sorting, uniqueness, bounds, rank promises, coordinate count, family dimension, fixed-length capacity, canonical hexadecimal representation, payload range, and error-certificate identity.

### 9.4 Decoding

The decoder reconstructs integer lower factors, forms exact Gram matrices, checks a deterministic denominator interval, and lifts them to input-erasing Choi blocks. The shared `Instrument.validate()` then checks instrument legality.

### 9.5 Adaptive wrapper

For dimension `v`, horizon `N`, and rational requested error `epsilon`, the wrapper chooses

```text
B >= 4 sqrt(Nv)/epsilon.
```

Since `h-1<=v`, the certified squared error is at most `epsilon^2`. The wrapper validates the exact rational string and rejects altered certificates.

### 9.6 Target-bound verification

`verify()` re-runs the deterministic encoder on the supplied target and compares the complete canonical JSON object. A syntactically valid code therefore does not certify another target.

### 9.7 Finite regression
