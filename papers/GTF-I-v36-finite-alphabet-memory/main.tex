\begin{abstract}
We study the width of finite stochastic transducers that reproduce every
conditional probability of a prescribed orthogonal command experiment.
For a fixed finite alphabet whose action on the sphere has an $L^2$
spectral gap, we prove lower and upper widths of orders
$(N/\log(N+1))^{(d-1)/2}$ and $N^{(d-1)/2}$, respectively.
The upper realization is exact; the lower bound permits arbitrary hidden
states, unrelated transition rows at different epochs, and fixed small
output error. Five explicit rational three-dimensional commands therefore
have classical width between $cN/\log(N+1)$ and $CN$, although a single
qubit realizes the same probabilities. This probability task is distinct
from strict-cutpoint language equivalence. A second finite, noncommuting
class, consisting of permutations and conjugations of resonant planar
modes, has the matched order $N^{1/3}$. Its full group need not possess an
irrational one-dimensional character. The common argument is a packet
compression inequality formulated for the entire fresh command word,
without assuming that its product is sufficient for the hidden machine.
We also give a finite fair-bit compiler for sparse rows, with logarithmic
rather than denominator-sized control overhead. The principal width is
nonuniform clocked atomic-row width; the fair-bit interface is separately
self-paced. The spectral-gap bounds determine a polynomial exponent,
not a matched order up to constants.
\end{abstract}
