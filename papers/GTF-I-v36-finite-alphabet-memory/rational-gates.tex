\section{Five rational commands and a qubit}\label{sec:rational}
Consider the fixed matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gates}
 R_x=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&-7/25&-24/25\\0&24/25&-7/25\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 R_z=\begin{pmatrix}-7/25&-24/25&0\\24/25&-7/25&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
and the five-letter alphabet
$\mathcal A_* =\{\Id,R_x,R_x^{-1},R_z,R_z^{-1}\}$.
Use the six seeds $\{\pm e_j:1\le j\le3\}$, signal $\rho=1/10$,
and three terminal coordinate queries. Every specified probability lies
in $[9/20,11/20]$ at every horizon. Both the alphabet and the matrices
are independent of $N$.

\begin{theorem}[A finite noncommuting rational family]\label{thm:rational}
For the experiment \eqref{eq:gates}, every fixed
$0\le\varepsilon<1/(20\sqrt3)$ has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-rate}
 c_\varepsilon\frac N{\log(N+1)}
 \le W_{N,\varepsilon}(\mathcal A_*)
 \le W_{N,0}(\mathcal A_*)\le CN.
\end{equation}
The exact upper machine can have rational entries and common command
rows. Every separate positive minimum is four. If $\lambda<1$ is the
sphere gap constant for the lazy walk with weights $1/2,1/8,1/8,1/8,1/8$,
then a peak-$K$ machine necessarily obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-finite}
 N<B_K\{1+4K\log(1/\kappa_3)\},
 \quad B_K=\left\lceil\frac{2\log(12K)}{-\log\lambda}\right\rceil,
 \quad \kappa_3=\frac1{10\sqrt3}-2\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
No numerical value of the spectral gap is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z$ denote the Pauli matrices and put
$A_x=(3I-4i\sigma_x)/5$, $A_z=(3I-4i\sigma_z)/5$ in $\SU(2)$.
Their adjoint actions on the traceless Hermitian matrices are precisely
$R_x,R_z$. Their eigenvalues include $(3+4i)/5$. This number is not a
root of unity: otherwise its sum with its inverse, $6/5$, would be a
rational algebraic integer, hence an integer. Their powers are therefore
dense in the respective one-parameter axial subgroups. The Lie algebra
generated by $i\sigma_x,i\sigma_z$ contains their bracket, a nonzero
multiple of $i\sigma_y$, so the closed subgroup they generate is all
of $\SU(2)$. In particular the generators do not commute.

The matrices have algebraic entries. Bourgain--Gamburd's spectral-gap
theorem \cite[Theorem~1]{BG12} applies to their symmetric averaging
operator on $L^2(\SU(2))$. Adding probability $1/2$ at the identity
makes its spectrum nonnegative and its mean-zero norm strictly below
one. The action on the sphere is a homogeneous quotient: lifting
$f(v)$ to $f(gv_0)$ embeds $L^2(\mathbb S^2)$ isometrically into
$L^2(\SU(2))$ and intertwines the averaging operators. Hence the same
gap holds on the sphere.

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}, with the explicit constants in
\eqref{eq:B3}. Rational synthesis follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:net-upper}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:separate} gives four at each separate cut, since
$3\rho<1$. The spectral-gap theorem is the substantive external input;
no finite collection of eigenvalue computations is used as its proof.
\end{proof}

This theorem has a different arithmetic content from the binary example
with rational rotation entries. One rotation may have exceptionally slow
circle recurrences. Two noncommuting algebraic axes instead invoke a
spectral gap on the entire sphere. We do not infer that either individual
rotation angle is badly approximable.

\begin{proposition}[Quantum probabilities versus threshold languages]
\label{prop:qubit}
The experiment in Theorem~\ref{thm:rational} has an exact single-qubit
realization with a finite, horizon-independent collection of operations.
A general one-way quantum automaton of dimension two realizes its
probabilities on valid prepare--command--query words. For any fixed
strict cutpoint, its threshold language has a PFA realization with at
most five states by \cite[Theorem~3.2]{CW26a}. Nevertheless any classical
clocked transducer approximating these numerical probabilities at the
stated tolerance has width bounded below by \eqref{eq:rational-rate}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For seed $x$ prepare
\[
 \varrho_x=\tfrac12(I+\rho(x_1\sigma_x+x_2\sigma_y+x_3\sigma_z)).
\]
It is a density matrix because $\norm x=1$ and $\rho<1$.
A command uses the corresponding unitary $I,A_x^{\pm1},A_z^{\pm1}$.
Their conjugations rotate the Bloch vector by \eqref{eq:gates}.
Measuring $\sigma_j$ yields
$\tr(\varrho_w(I+b\sigma_j)/2)
 =(1+b\rho e_j^TU_wx)/2$, exactly the required law.
A query-dependent unitary before a fixed $\sigma_z$ measurement implements
all three queries; a seed symbol is a completely positive reset channel.
Thus the operations define a dimension-two general quantum automaton on
a finite alphabet, including all words outside the promised protocol
with its usual automaton semantics.

The cited strict-cutpoint conversion concerns that entire language and
therefore also its valid words. It preserves threshold signs, not
numerical probabilities. In the conversion, the deviation from the
classical cutpoint is multiplied by a positive length-dependent shrinking
factor \cite[Proposition~3.1]{CW26a}. Such attenuation is harmless for
language equivalence and cannot supply the fixed-correlation requirement
of Lemma~\ref{lem:terminal}. The last assertion is exactly
Theorem~\ref{thm:rational}; it even permits horizon-dependent and
nonstationary classical simulators.
\end{proof}

The variable in \eqref{eq:rational-rate} is input length, with quantum
dimension fixed. The quadratic state-cost results of Chen--Wu
\cite{CW26a,CW26b} instead vary quantum dimension under strict-cutpoint
equivalence. Neither result is contradicted or strengthened in its own
metric by changing to the other objective.
