\section{A matched noncommuting finite class}\label{sec:monomial}
The preceding finite spectral-gap class determines a growth exponent,
with a logarithmic order gap. There is also a finite noncommuting class
with a matched order. Its orbit structure is different from a sphere.

Identify $\R^{2r}$ with $\C^r$, fix an irrational $\alpha$, and put
$\zeta=e^{2\pi i\alpha}$. A \emph{resonant monomial command} has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monomial}
 (U_a z)_j=\zeta^{n_{a,j}}
 \begin{cases}
 z_{\pi_a^{-1}(j)},&\epsilon_{a,j}=1,\\
 \overline{z_{\pi_a^{-1}(j)}},&\epsilon_{a,j}=-1,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_a$ is a permutation, $n_{a,j}$ is an integer, and
$\epsilon_{a,j}\in\{-1,1\}$. Fix finitely many such commands, and let
$M=\max(1,\max_{a,j}|n_{a,j}|)$. Include the identity and the command
$A$ that multiplies the first coordinate by $\zeta$ and fixes the rest.
Take the $4r$ seeds $(\pm1\pm i)e_j$, signal
$0<\rho\le1/(10r)$, and one of the $2r$ real coordinate queries.
The specified mean is the corresponding coordinate of $\rho U_wx$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp monomial width]\label{thm:monomial}
If $b_\alpha:=\inf_{l\ge1}l\|l\alpha\|_{\R/\mathbb Z}>0$, then
for each fixed $0\le\varepsilon<\rho/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monomial-width}
 W^{\rm mon}_{N,\varepsilon}
 =\Theta_{r,\mathcal A,\alpha,\rho,\varepsilon}(N^{1/3}).
\end{equation}
The exact upper machine has common command rows and at most four
successors in any such row. Its final decoder may depend on $N$.
Every separate exact positive minimum is $2r+1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lower bound]
Restrict to seed $(1+i)e_1$, commands $\Id,A$, and the first two real
queries. The conditional target is the planar phase
$\rho(1+i)\zeta^{L_N}$. Combining its two mean columns shows that
$q_N\ge\kappa=\rho-2\varepsilon$, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:terminal} with
the sharper planar normalization.

For a proposed peak $k$, put $B=2k-1$ and choose the length-$B$ word
$A^J\Id^{B-J}$ with $J$ uniform on $\{0,\ldots,B\}$. Its $2k$ circle
points are separated by
$s_B=\min_{1\le l\le B}\|l\alpha\|$. Each open arc of radius $s_B/2$
contains at most one of these points. Any $k$ centers therefore leave
at least half of them at distance at least $s_B/2$. The average loss is
at least
$\tfrac12(1-\cos(\pi s_B))=\sin^2(\pi s_B/2)\ge s_B^2$.
The full-word lemma applies although the packet's letters are correlated.
Consecutive independent packets imply
\[
 \lfloor N/B\rfloor\frac{b_\alpha^2}{B^2}
 \le\log(1/\kappa),\qquad
 N<B+b_\alpha^{-2}B^3\log(1/\kappa).
\]
This proves the lower order against every hidden realization. No
character of the full command group was used, only a sublanguage.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Sparse exact upper construction]
Take a convergent $p_0/q_0$ of $\alpha$, put $q=2q_0$ and
$\delta=2\pi(\alpha-p_0/q_0)$. Choose the first convergent for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qchoice}
 q\ge\max\{8M,8,(160MN)^{1/3}\}.
\end{equation}
Then $|\delta|<8\pi/q^2$ and $|n\delta|<\pi/q$ for $|n|\le M$.
Set $a=\pi/q$, and for each needed integer $n$ put
\[
 \lambda_n=\frac{\cos(a-|n\delta|)}{\cos a},\qquad
 \omega_n=\frac{\sin|n\delta|}{\lambda_n\sin(2a)},\qquad
 \Lambda=\max_{|n|\le M}\lambda_n.
\]
These numbers obey $1\le\lambda_n\le\Lambda$ and
$0\le\omega_n\le1$. Let $u_l=e^{2\pi il/q}$, indices modulo $q$.
The two-point law on labels
$l+2np_0$ and $l+2np_0+\sgn(n\delta)$, of weights
$1-\omega_n,\omega_n$, has mean $\zeta^n u_l/\lambda_n$.
For $n=0$ interpret it as the single label $l$.
This is the intersection of a polygon edge with the rotated ray.

The register is a pair $(j,l)$, comprising one mode and one polygon
label. Command $a$ sends the mode to $j'=\pi_a(j)$ and, if needed,
replaces $l$ by $-l$ to conjugate. Draw the just described edge label
for $n=n_{a,j'}$. With probability
$(1+\lambda_n/\Lambda)/2$ retain it; otherwise replace it by its
antipode, label $l+q/2$. The expected complex label is exactly the
commanded one divided by $\Lambda$. At most four labels occur. This
antipodal step prices a common radial factor without a free zero state
or a dense uniform redraw.

Associate $a_tu_l e_j$ with label $(j,l)$, where $a_t=\Lambda^t/4$.
The conditional mean update is exactly $U_a$. Moreover
\[
 \log\Lambda\le M|\delta|\tan a
 \le16\pi^2M/q^3<160M/q^3,
 \qquad a_N\le e/4<1.
\]
The seed occupies one mode. Its planar vector has radius
$\rho\sqrt2\le\sqrt2/10<\cos(\pi/8)/4$, hence lies in the initial
polygon and can be initialized using at most three vertices. Decoder
means are the real coordinates of $a_Nu_le_j$, with zeros on other modes.
Induction gives the exact specified law on all words, not just the
restricted lower-bound words. Only $(j,l)$ persists.

Bad approximability is equivalent to bounded partial quotients, which
bound the ratio of consecutive convergent denominators. Thus the first
choice in \eqref{eq:qchoice} has $q=O_{\alpha,M}(N^{1/3})$, proving
the upper order with $rq$ labels.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Separate minima]
The seed vectors span $\R^{2r}$; identity suffixes separate their
coordinates, so normalized residual rank is $2r+1$.
All predictive vectors have norm $\rho\sqrt2$. A regular simplex with
inradius $\rho\sqrt2$ has circumradius $2r\rho\sqrt2\le\sqrt2/5<1$.
Its vertices define legal continuations under every orthogonal command.
The selected-cut construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:separate} gives
the matching positive minimum, with finite raw-word storage elsewhere.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A full group not seen by a character]
\label{prop:no-character}
For every $r\ge2$, at most five commands suffice to generate an instance
of Theorem~\ref{thm:monomial} whose compact closure is
$O(2)^r\rtimes S_r$. Its real representation on $\R^{2r}$ is irreducible.
Every continuous one-dimensional unitary character is trivial on the
connected torus $SO(2)^r$, hence cannot see $A$'s irrational phase.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use $\Id$, $A$, conjugation of the first mode, the cyclic permutation
of modes, and the transposition of the first two modes. The last two
permutations generate $S_r$; for $r=2$ duplicate commands are removed.
Irrational powers of $A$ fill one circle, and permutation conjugates
fill all coordinate circles. Conjugations supply their reflections,
proving the asserted closure.

An invariant real subspace under these circle actions decomposes into
coordinate planes: averaging over all other circles projects onto a
chosen plane. A nonzero projection contains that whole plane by rotation,
and permutation invariance then supplies all planes. This proves
irreducibility. Finally, conjugation of a coordinate circle element
sends it to its inverse. In a one-dimensional character its value must
therefore equal its inverse. Continuity on the connected circle forces
that value to be one. Apply this in each coordinate.
\end{proof}

This example is genuinely noncommuting and is not an application of a
character of its entire compact group. Its lower bound still uses a
cyclic sublanguage, and its upper bound uses the monomial structure.
It does not classify arbitrary finite dense subsets of $\SO(d)$.
The case $r=1$, $\rho=1/10$ and commands $\Id,A$ recovers the original
sharp rotation experiment of \cite{GTF35} without changing its signal,
seed set, error tolerance or hidden-state quantifier.
