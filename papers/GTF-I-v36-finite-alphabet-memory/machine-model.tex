\section{Finite experiments and persistent registers}\label{sec:model}
Fix a finite alphabet $\mathcal A$, matrices $U_a\in O(d)$, a finite seed
set $X\subset\mathbb S^{d-1}$ and $0<\rho\le1/10$. Unless otherwise
stated, $X=\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_d\}$ and $\Id\in\mathcal A$.
We use the full specification \eqref{eq:law}; no distribution or fairness
assumption is imposed on the external word.

\begin{definition}[Nonuniform clocked stochastic transducer]
\label{def:machine}
For horizon $N$, a transducer consists of finite state sets $[K_t]$,
initial distributions $E_x$, stochastic matrices
$T_{t,a}\in\R_+^{K_t\times K_{t+1}}$, and terminal columns
$d_j\in[-1,1]^{K_N}$. Its mean on a word is
$E_xT_{0,a_1}\cdots T_{N-1,a_N}d_j$.
Its error is half the maximum absolute discrepancy from the mean in
\eqref{eq:law}. The peak counts the available labels $\max_tK_t$,
and also two terminal labels when the emitted answer is retained at a
charged cut. The least peak with error at most $\varepsilon$ is
$W_{N,\varepsilon}$.
\end{definition}

Only the updated register persists. The seed and the query appear once;
the current command is consumed in its update. Private randomness does
not persist outside the register. An input-dependent clock, a previous
command, or an installation tag would be charged if retained.
The epoch, $N$, complete row tables, their construction and arithmetic,
and exact atomic sampling are free. Rows may vary with $t$ and $N$.
Unused rows are normalized, and padding counts as available labels.
This is maximum layer width of a nonuniform probabilistic ordered
read-once program, not uniform program space or total branching-program
size. Section~\ref{sec:coins} specifies a different, finite-coin interface.

For comparison with the earlier compact-input theorem, a compact command
space requires each matrix entry $a\mapsto T_{t,a}(s,s')$ to be Borel.
Such a row function is nonuniform program data, not a finitely represented
real-input algorithm. None of the finite-alphabet theorems below requires
that access. Every input to their machines is a symbol from a fixed
finite set.

\begin{proposition}[Separate positive minimum]\label{prop:separate}
If $\rho d<1$ and the identity is available, the normalized residual rank
and the separate positive minimum at every cut are $d+1$.
A separate minimum places no cap on the other registers.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Residual rows are affine in $v=\rho U_wx$. Idle prefixes from $\pm e_j$
span $\R^d$, and an idle suffix followed by the coordinate queries
separates the coordinates. Normalization adds the constant row, giving
rank $d+1$. A regular simplex centered at zero with circumradius $d\rho$
has inradius $\rho$. It contains every reachable vector and all its
vertices have norm less than one. Each vertex consequently defines a
valid future binary channel after every orthogonal command word.
At the selected cut decompose $v$ into these vertices. Before this cut
one may store the raw prefix; afterwards one may store a simplex label
and the raw suffix. At $l$ later inputs this costs at most
$(d+1)|\mathcal A|^l$ labels, and remains finite. It embeds the factorization
in a complete machine rather than an amputated static matrix. Rank gives
the converse.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Terminal correlation]\label{lem:terminal}
For a fixed unit seed $x$, put $Y_t=U_{a_t}\cdots U_{a_1}x$ and
$q_t=\E\norm{\E[Y_t\mid S_t]}$ under any external word law independent
of the machine's private randomness. If the wordwise error is at most
$\varepsilon$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:calibration}
 q_N\ge\kappa_d:=\rho/\sqrt d-2\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Combine the $d$ decoder columns into $D(S_N)\in[-1,1]^d$.
Conditionally on a word, its mean differs from $\rho Y_N$ by a vector
of norm at most $2\sqrt d\varepsilon$. Thus
$\E\langle Y_N,D(S_N)\rangle\ge\rho-2\sqrt d\varepsilon$.
Conditional expectation bounds the absolute value of this quantity by
$\sqrt d q_N$. Two or more queries are not jointly sampled in this
argument; their decoder columns are only combined in the proof.
\end{proof}
