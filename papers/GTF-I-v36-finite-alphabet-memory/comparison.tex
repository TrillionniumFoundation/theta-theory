\section{Objectives, antecedents, and remaining distinctions}
\label{sec:comparison}
\subsection{Quantum and probabilistic automata}
The prepare--rotate--query law is a numerical behavior, not just a
language. Proposition~\ref{prop:qubit} gives its exact quantum model and
an explicit classical length-dependent obstruction. Chen--Wu's revised
paper \cite{CW26a} has a different title and sharper theorem than its
April version: its stated cost is $n^2+1$ under strict cutpoints.
Their companion work \cite{CW26b} treats the simulation cost of reversible
quantum models through prepare--test and sign geometry. These compare
threshold languages as quantum dimension varies. A shrinking positive
acceptance margin can preserve such a language at all lengths and fail
every fixed numerical tolerance in the present task.

The September symmetry study \cite{ChenSym} likewise distinguishes
reachable--observable rank from operational threshold memory. Its
strict-cutpoint objective is not wordwise preservation of numerical
probabilities. Our sphere example already has fixed real predictive
rank four, so a rank theorem alone cannot yield its diverging compatible
width. The present comparison does not claim that quantum/classical
state separation itself is new.

Lumbreras--Ma--Thompson--Gu \cite{LMTG} analyze a stationary world model
with an irrational phase, repeated histories, value error and decision
loss, and supply an exact qutrit realization. Their Theorems 28--30
concern that stationary decision task. Here the classical machine may be
redesigned for every finite horizon and may change its rows at every cut;
one externally prescribed terminal probability is tested. Neither a
stationary peripheral-spectrum argument nor a strict-cutpoint conversion
supplies our finite nonuniform width bounds.

\subsection{Spectral and geometric inputs}
Bourgain--Gamburd \cite{BG12} supplies the full spectral-gap input for
Theorem~\ref{thm:rational}. We do not prove a new expansion theorem or a
new sphere-covering law. The gap converts finite command length into a
uniform Lipschitz mixing scale; the full-word contraction then converts
that scale into a hidden-register cost. This distinguishes the finite
alphabet from the all-rotation-input theorem retained in \cite{GTF35}.
The gap constant is fixed but not evaluated numerically. Constants may
deteriorate with dimension, the generating set and the error margin.

The lower bound uses a probability law on external commands, not an
adaptive policy implemented by the simulator. In the spectral-gap class
the test letters are independent. In the monomial class they are
correlated within width-dependent packets. Berkes--Borda \cite{BB} study
the harmonic and Diophantine behavior of the uncompressed circle walk;
compression into an endpoint register is an additional operation.

The transducer is a probabilistic ordered read-once branching program
with real readout. For a binary alphabet its usual Boolean-input matrix
Fourier coefficient is the ordered product of the matrices
$(T_{t,0}+(-1)^{s_t}T_{t,1})/2$. This algebraic identity is not the packet
converse. The Fourier-growth results for regular and width-three programs
\cite{RSV,SVW,LPV} have their stated hypotheses and objectives. An arbitrary
stochastic row need not preserve the uniform state law. Conversely,
bounds valid for all deterministic tables can sometimes pass by convexity
to randomized rows. Private row choices should not be mistaken for a
persistent shared seed available to the decoder.

\subsection{Inherited statements and preservation}
The sharp bounded-type binary theorem, nonuniform occupation, actual
terminal-error bounds, the angular coefficient $l^2-1$, and the separate
compact-input result of \cite{GTF35} remain in the unaltered supporting
article. The corrected full-word lemma supplies the advertised general
packet implication without a product-sufficiency restriction. For a
compact command table, a Borel convex choice can be made by triangulating
a finite polytope and using the first containing simplex in a fixed order;
affine barycentric coordinates on finitely many closed simplices are
Borel. No such infinite table is used by the finite-alphabet results.

The accumulated local deficiency certificate from the preceding work
is not the minimum actual terminal error. Given a fixed profile in the
sphere case, the present proof directly yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-error}
 \mathcal E_N(K)\ge\frac12\left[
 \frac\rho{\sqrt d}-
 \exp\{-c_dK^{-2/(d-1)}\lfloor N/B_K\rfloor\}\right]_+.
\end{equation}
Indeed Lemma~\ref{lem:terminal} holds for every candidate's actual error,
and the packet product bounds $q_N$ above. This remains valid when
intermediate approximation errors cancel. A sum of executable local
garbling defects is only an upper certificate for a particular terminal
error, by TV contraction; applying \eqref{eq:actual-error} to it does not
turn that certificate into an intrinsic equality.

\subsection{Conclusion}
There are now two finite noncommuting classes with distinct quantitative
laws. Spectral-gap actions on spheres have polynomial width exponent
$(d-1)/2$, with the displayed logarithmic order gap. Resonant monomial
actions have matched cube-root order and need not be detected by a
character of the full group. Both conclusions allow arbitrary hidden
states and layer-dependent stochastic rows. The explicit rational gates
give a numerical-probability comparison with a single qubit, not a new
strict-cutpoint state-cost theorem.

The logarithmic gap for spectral-gap alphabets, optimal finite constants,
and classification of all irrational cyclic angles remain quantitative
questions. General adaptive experimental design is a different task.
The finite-coin corollary concerns a self-paced nonuniform sampler and
does not identify program-table space with label space. These distinctions
leave the proved finite-alphabet results intact; they are not hypotheses
silently replaced during implementation.
