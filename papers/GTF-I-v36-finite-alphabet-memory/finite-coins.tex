\section{Finite coins with charged control}\label{sec:coins}
The atomic model permits arbitrary exact row probabilities. We now
separate a finite random-bit implementation. The environment supplies
an input only at a ready boundary and waits until the update has
finished. Fair bits are private. The row table may contain precomputed
finite binary thresholds, but the bit position, current input, old label
and control state are all charged. There is no free microstep clock.

\begin{theorem}[Sparse-row dyadic compiler]\label{thm:compiler}
Suppose a finite command transducer has peak $K$, at most $L$ successors
per row, fixed finite seed and query alphabets, and common command rows.
For any $\delta>0$ it has a self-paced fair-bit implementation whose
uniform terminal row-TV error increases by at most $\delta$, using
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coin-cost}
 C_{\mathcal A,X,J,L}K(b+1)\quad\hbox{persistent labels},\qquad
 b=\max\left\{1,\left\lceil\log_2\frac{C_L(N+2)}{\delta}\right\rceil\right\},
\end{equation}
and at most $(L-1)b$ fair bits for a command update. The same bound
holds for sparse initialization and the binary terminal decoder.
Tables and the prescribed macroscopic final boundary remain nonuniform.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A categorical row with at most $L$ outcomes is a binary decision tree
with at most $L-1$ internal nodes. At each node express the probability
of choosing one subtree conditionally on reaching that node. A zero-mass
node is unreachable and may use an arbitrary conditional row. Round each
conditional probability down to a multiple of $2^{-b}$. Coupling the
binary decisions shows that the row-TV discrepancy is at most
$(L-1)2^{-b}$, without needing the original probabilities to be rational.
Initialization and binary decoding are treated in the same way.

Thresholds zero and one are deterministic. For $0<m<2^b$,
to sample the dyadic Bernoulli threshold $m/2^b$, compare independent fair
bits with the $b$ binary digits of $m$, from most to least significant.
At the first mismatch the comparison is decided; if all digits agree,
return the prescribed strict-comparison outcome. No prefix integer is
stored. The comparison retains only its position $i\in\{1,\ldots,b\}$;
while all bits agree, their values are already the fixed threshold prefix.
A categorical tree additionally retains one of at most $L-1$ nodes.
It must retain the old label and the current input until a leaf gives
the new label. These are all included in \eqref{eq:coin-cost}, along with
ready, seed and query phases. There is no second uncharged output index.
The common-row assumption eliminates a retained epoch counter inside
the sampler. An external end query selects the final decoder after the
prescribed number of completed inputs.

TV contraction under stochastic kernels and induction on the actual
word give at most $C_L(N+2)2^{-b}$ terminal discrepancy. This is at most
$\delta$ by the chosen $b$. The argument couples legal completed updates
and therefore prices every command even when the bit comparison stops
early. Its duration is at most $(L-1)b$ bits per update.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Leading label bits with finite coins]\label{cor:coin-rate}
For every fixed positive error below the thresholds of the preceding
theorems, their explicit machines admit the following finite-coin peaks:
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 O(N^{1/3}\log(N+1)) &\text{for the resonant monomial class},\\
 O(N^{(d-1)/2}\log(N+1)) &\text{for the spectral-gap class}.
 \end{array}
\]
Consequently the least persistent bits in this self-paced, nonuniform
fair-bit model have leading coefficients $1/3$ and $(d-1)/2$ in
$\log_2 N$, respectively, with additive $O(\log\log(N+1))$ uncertainty.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The monomial upper has at most four successors; the spherical upper has
at most $d+1$. Their initialization is sparse and their decoder binary.
Compile the exact machine with tolerance equal to the fixed desired
error. Formula~\eqref{eq:coin-cost} gives the upper peaks.
Every such sampler, when observed at ready boundaries, is itself an
atomic transducer with at most its total number of labels. Hence the
atomic lower bounds apply, irrespective of its internal timing. Taking
binary logarithms and using \eqref{eq:spectral-width} and
\eqref{eq:monomial-width} proves the stated leading coefficients.
\end{proof}

This is not an exact finite-bit implementation of an irrational row at
zero error. It is a fixed-error compiler with an explicitly charged input
latch and bit counter. It does not make the row description or its
construction free in an ordinary uniform-space model; those costs are
outside both nonuniform measures. For the rational gates, exact rational
atomic synthesis is additionally available, but exact rational sampling
with unbounded rejection duration is a separate implementation choice.
