\section{Introduction}
A small positive factorization at every observation cut does not, by
itself, produce a small machine that realizes all cuts simultaneously.
The additional requirement is causal compatibility: the same retained
state must support each possible next input. We investigate this
requirement for command experiments
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
 \Pp(B=b\mid x,a_1\cdots a_N,j)
 =\frac{1+b\rho e_j^TU_{a_N}\cdots U_{a_1}x}{2},\qquad b\in\{-1,1\}.
\end{equation}
The command alphabet is fixed and finite. The seed is supplied once,
and one coordinate query is supplied after the prescribed horizon.
The command matrices act orthogonally; the realizing matrices act
stochastically and may have arbitrary hidden directions.

The finite-alphabet restriction is substantive. An independent Haar
rotation cannot be treated as one input when only a finite gate set is
available. Nor can a whole word be replaced by its group product in the
hidden transition law: equal products can have different intermediate
computations. The present results retain each input slot and each full
word throughout the converse.

Let $m=d-1$. Suppose a probability distribution on the fixed alphabet
induces an averaging operator $P$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-gap}
 \norm{Pf}_{L^2(\sigma)}\le\lambda\norm f_{L^2(\sigma)}
 \quad\text{when }\int f\,d\sigma=0,\qquad \lambda<1,
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is normalized spherical measure. For fixed positive
signal and sufficiently small uniform error, Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}
proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-rate}
 c\left(\frac{N}{\log(N+1)}\right)^{m/2}
 \le W_{N,\varepsilon}\le W_{N,0}\le C N^{m/2}.
\end{equation}
In particular $\log W_{N,\varepsilon}/\log N\to m/2$.
The logarithmic gap in \eqref{eq:intro-rate} is part of the statement,
not absorbed into a $\Theta$ notation. The proof uses only a finite
command distribution. A logarithmic number of actual commands makes
its orbit distribution sufficiently close to spherical measure at the
resolution imposed by the endpoint width.

Theorem~\ref{thm:rational} instantiates this criterion with five rational
matrices in $\SO(3)$. The spectral gap is an external theorem of
Bourgain--Gamburd \cite{BG12}, not an assumption checked by numerics.
Density and the algebraic hypotheses are verified for the stated gates.
The experiment has separate positive minimum four at every internal cut,
but its compatible hidden width grows almost linearly. A two-dimensional
quantum memory realizes it exactly. Proposition~\ref{prop:qubit} makes
this comparison operational rather than terminological.

A different geometry gives a different order. In
Theorem~\ref{thm:monomial}, finitely many orthogonal monomial commands
permute planar modes, conjugate them, and rotate them by integer multiples
of one badly approximable angle. Their exact and fixed-error widths are
$\Theta(N^{1/3})$. The group generated by the displayed five-letter
example is noncommuting, its real representation is irreducible, and
no continuous one-dimensional character sees its irrational rotations.
This is not an assertion that every finite noncommuting alphabet has the
same exponent.

The methodological distinction is between mixing of the uncompressed
orbit and irreversible loss at a finite register. The former supplies
an orbit quantization scale; the latter converts that scale into a
cumulative width constraint. All lower bounds allow new machines at
each horizon and arbitrary changes of stochastic rows between epochs.
The exact upper machines have common command rows for a fixed horizon,
with a horizon-dependent decoder.

The use of $W$ for a word is local to the packet lemma; width elsewhere
is scalar. Classical positive realization, spherical covering, spectral
gap, and strict-cutpoint simulation are identified at their points of
use. The new finite-alphabet consequences depend on these stated inputs.
The sharp binary rotation result of \cite{GTF35} is retained and its
packet and resonant arguments are reproduced within the larger monomial
construction. Historical results not needed for the present proofs remain
in the separately preserved supporting article.
