\section{Compression of full command words}\label{sec:packets}
Let a compact group act orthogonally on a finite-dimensional real Hilbert
space $H$. Complex unitary representations are covered by the real inner
product. For a group probability measure $\mu$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distortion}
 \Gamma_k(\mu)=\inf_{v,u_1,\ldots,u_k\in\mathbb S(H)}
 \int\min_{i\le k}\{1-\langle U_gv,u_i\rangle\}\,d\mu(g).
\end{equation}
This continuous minimization has a minimum and lies in $[0,1]$.
The upper bound follows by including the direction of
$\int U_gv\,d\mu(g)$ as a center. An invariant direction may make the
coefficient zero. The conclusions below remain valid but then give no
positive loss from that packet.

\begin{lemma}[Full-word packet contraction]\label{lem:packet}
Let $Y\in H$ be integrable and $S$ finite. Let a random packet word $W$
be independent of $\sigma(Y,S)$, and let $g(W)$ be its measurable ordered
product. Suppose $S'$ has at most $k$ positive-mass values and, for every
label $r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-kernel}
 \Pp(S'=r\mid\sigma(Y,S,W))=T_W(S,r)\quad\hbox{a.s.},
\end{equation}
where each $T_w$ is a stochastic kernel. If $\mu=g_*\operatorname{Law}(W)$,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packet}
 \E\norm{\E[U_{g(W)}Y\mid S']}
 \le (1-\Gamma_k(\mu))\E\norm{\E[Y\mid S]}.
\end{equation}
In particular $T_w$ need not be constant on fibers of $g$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $p_s=\Pp(S=s)$, $z_s=\E[Y\mid S=s]$, and let $\nu$ be the law
of $W$. Independence and \eqref{eq:conditional-kernel} imply
\[
 p'_rz'_r=\sum_s p_s\int T_w(s,r)U_{g(w)}z_s\,d\nu(w),
 \qquad z'_r=\E[U_{g(W)}Y\mid S'=r].
\]
For nonzero $z'_r$ take $u_r=z'_r/\norm{z'_r}$, with arbitrary unit
choices for zero centroids. Summing inner products gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_rp'_r\norm{z'_r}
 &=\sum_sp_s\int\sum_rT_w(s,r)\langle U_{g(w)}z_s,u_r\rangle\,d\nu(w)\\
 &\le\sum_{s:z_s\ne0}p_s\norm{z_s}
       \int\max_r\langle U_{g(w)}z_s/\norm{z_s},u_r\rangle\,d\nu(w)\\
 &\le (1-\Gamma_k(\mu))\sum_sp_s\norm{z_s}.
\end{align*}
Only after removing the word-dependent assignment by a pointwise maximum
do we push $\nu$ forward to $\mu$. No product-sufficiency assertion is
used. The old register and all internal packet cuts are unrestricted.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the word matters]\label{rem:collision}
In a group with an involution $r$, the words $ee$ and $rr$ have the same
product. A transducer can nevertheless record which first symbol occurred.
Thus integration of the internal computation gives a kernel $T_w$, not
necessarily a kernel $T_{g(w)}$. Also, independence of the product alone
would be insufficient: an identity-product word correlated with $Y$
could reveal $Y$ through its spelling. The hypothesis concerns the entire
fresh word. This repairs the word/product mismatch in the preceding
formulation without restricting the class of machines or packet supports.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Packet budget]\label{thm:packet-budget}
Start with a fixed unit vector. On disjoint intervals use packet words
whose full laws are fixed in advance, each independent of the earlier
process and of all private random choices. Fixed commands may fill the
gaps. If $k_i$ bounds the register at packet endpoint $i$, and a terminal
correlation forces $q_N\ge\kappa>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
 \kappa\le\prod_i(1-\Gamma_{k_i}(\mu_i)),\qquad
 \sum_i\Gamma_{k_i}(\mu_i)\le\log(1/\kappa).
\end{equation}
The full words and their products may be noncommuting, and the product
map may have collisions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The ordered internal stochastic matrices of a whole packet multiply to
$T_w$. Their Markov property gives \eqref{eq:conditional-kernel}; word
freshness gives the required independence. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:packet}
at successive boundaries. A fixed command followed by stochastic
compression cannot increase $q$, by conditional Jensen. Initially $q=1$.
Multiplication proves the product, and $1-u\le e^{-u}$ proves the sum.
A factor zero precludes positive terminal correlation, as asserted.
\end{proof}

For a wordwise specification the test law may be chosen after the
available-label profile is known. It does not become information given
to the machine. In the next section packets are made of independent
letters. In Section~\ref{sec:monomial} they have correlated letters;
both satisfy the same full-word hypothesis.
