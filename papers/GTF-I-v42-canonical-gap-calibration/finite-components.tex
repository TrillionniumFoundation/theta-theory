\section{Finite components and observable quotients}\label{sec:components}
Connectedness is not harmless bookkeeping. If $H$ is a compact matrix group, its identity component $H^0$ has finite index, and other components can permute its isotypic subspaces. An uncharged component label would therefore change the realization problem.

\begin{proposition}[A paid finite-component compiler]\label{prop:components}
Choose representatives $r_c$ of the finite quotient $H/H^0$, with $r_e=I$, and write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coset}
 A_ar_c=r_{c'}h_{a,c},\qquad h_{a,c}\in H^0.
\end{equation}
Suppose the finite connected-component alphabet $\{h_{a,c}\}$ has an exact common-row vector realization of width $K_N$ for the required seeds and signal, whose terminal represented vectors have norm at most one. Then the original alphabet has an exact realization of width at most $\max(2,[H:H^0]K_N)$. The terminal decoder may depend on $N$.
If $H$ itself is finite, the exact width is at most $\max(2,|HX|)$ at every horizon.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use labels $(c,s)$. Given letter $a$ and label $(c,s)$, update $c$ to the uniquely determined $c'$ in \eqref{eq:coset}, and use the connected compiler's row for $h_{a,c}$ on $s$. The row depends only on the actual stored pair and the input. Inductively, if $A_w=r_c h_w$, the represented internal vector has mean $\rho h_wx$. At the final query use $\langle e_j,r_cv_s\rangle$. Its modulus is at most one and its overall mean is $\rho\langle e_j,A_wx\rangle$. The component identity is part of every label. No fixed choice of $H^0$ isotypic coordinates is assumed preserved by all components.

For finite $H$, retain the actual point in the finite orbit union $HX$, update it by the command permutation, and sample the prescribed bounded terminal row. This gives the stated bound. It is an upper bound, not a claim that every orbit point is a necessary label.
\end{proof}

The minimum-orbit construction in Appendix~\ref{sec:synthesis}, followed by the paid branch construction in Theorem~\ref{thm:dominant-block}, supplies such vector compilers at the stated small signal even without expansion. A matching lower bound for an arbitrary finite extension requires information about actual return words and the action on the activated subspaces; it does not follow solely from the index. The reflection extension in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a matched nongapped example: its upper polygon is reflection-invariant, while restriction to rotation words gives the full lower order.

\begin{proposition}[Metric orbit quotients do not specify numerical memory]\label{prop:quotients}
The metric quotients of the standard actions $\SO(d)\curvearrowright\R^d$, $d\ge2$, are all the half-line. Their minimum nonzero orbit dimensions are $d-1$. The same half-line is the metric quotient of the finite reflection action $\{\pm1\}\curvearrowright\R$.
Moreover there are numerical alphabets with the identical effective standard $\SO(2)$ action, identical seeds and queries, and identical orbit geometry, but infinitely many distinct sharp width exponents.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Two standard orthogonal orbits are spheres of radii $r,s$; their distance is $|r-s|$. The reflection quotient has the same distance between the absolute values. Orbit dimensions in the standard connected actions are those of the spheres. Thus metric quotient-equivalence alone cannot preserve the minimum orbit dimension. The last assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with the explicit angle families of Proposition~\ref{prop:algebraic-angles}: all act on the same plane and their exponents $r/(2r+1)$ are distinct.
\end{proof}

An ambient linear isometry taking every orbit onto an orbit does preserve the dimensions of those orbit submanifolds. To preserve the numerical experiment it must additionally intertwine the actual command letters and transform the seeds and queries. An isometry of the quotient, or a polar section with its finite Weyl group, need not do this. These are distinct equivalence relations, not competing proofs of the same invariant.
