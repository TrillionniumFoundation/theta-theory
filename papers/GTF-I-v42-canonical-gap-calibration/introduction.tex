\section{Introduction}
A finite numerical experiment prescribes probabilities, rather than only signs of probabilities relative to a threshold. Even when its linear response space has fixed dimension, a positive stochastic realization may need a growing number of persistent labels. Two features of the specification must be kept separate: the compact action generated by its commands, and the finite words that implement that action. The second feature is not determined by the first.

The experiment studied here has response
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
 \Pp(B=b\mid x,w,j)=\frac{1+b\rho\langle e_j,A_wx\rangle}{2},
 \qquad A_w=A_{a_N}\cdots A_{a_1}.
\end{equation}
The seed set is finite, the command alphabet is fixed, and exactly one coordinate query arrives after $N$ commands. A simulator must satisfy the specification on every word. Its rows may vary with the epoch, and the simulator may be redesigned for each $N$. The minimum peak label count at binary total-variation error at most $\varepsilon$ is denoted by $W_{N,\varepsilon}$.

For an expanding irreducible action, the earlier occupation and orbit-synthesis theorems give a width exponent equal to half the smallest nonzero orbit dimension. Their proofs are reproduced in the appendices. Expansion is sufficient, but neither a description of all alphabets nor a necessary condition for a growing width. Our first result addresses a class in which every finite-block norm-gap certificate vanishes.

\begin{theorem}[A nongapped alphabet hierarchy]\label{thm:main}
Let $r\ge1$ be fixed. Suppose $\alpha\in\R^r$ and $b_*>0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-ba}
 \norm{m\cdot\alpha}_{\R/\Z}\ge b_*\norm m_1^{-r}
 \quad\text{for every }m\in\Z^r\setminus\{0\}.
\end{equation}
Take the planar commands $I,R_{2\pi\alpha_1},\ldots,R_{2\pi\alpha_r}$, seeds $\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2\}$, and the two coordinate queries. For fixed $0<\rho\le1/10$ and $0\le\varepsilon<\rho/(2\sqrt2)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:toral-order}
 W_{N,\varepsilon}=\Theta_{\alpha,r,\rho,\varepsilon}\bigl(N^{r/(2r+1)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The lower bound allows arbitrary hidden stochastic states and time-dependent rows. An exact upper realization has common command rows and an $N$-dependent decoder. The same order holds after adjoining a reflection. Every finite-block optimized action gap of either alphabet is zero.
\end{theorem}

The hypotheses have explicit examples in every $r$: put $\xi=2^{1/(r+1)}$ and $\alpha_i=\xi^i$. An algebraic-norm calculation proves \eqref{eq:dual-ba} with $b_*=(2/9)^r$. The rotation matrices in this example have computable-real entries; algebraicity of the angles is not asserted to make those entries algebraic. The cases $r=1,2,3$ give exponents $1/3,2/5,3/7$. The connected effective action is the standard $\SO(2)$ action in all of them. Its minimum orbit dimension is always one. Section~\ref{sec:toral} proves the theorem, including every command in a packet and every persistent upper-state label.

A second distinction already occurs before the genuinely nongapped regime. Let $V_a$ be the Koopman command operators on the complement of invariant functions. For a probability law $p$, write
\[
 g(p)=1-\norm{\textstyle\sum_a p(a)V_a}^{2},\qquad
 g_1^*(\mathcal A)=\max_{p\in\Delta(\mathcal A)}g(p).
\]
The optimized quantity depends only on the alphabet and action. Repeating this optimization over laws on actual length-$L$ words gives $g_L^*$. We prove that some $g_L^*>0$ if and only if the uniform one-step average has spectral radius less than one. This need not mean its norm is less than one. A two-letter rational $\SO(3)$ alphabet has $g_1^*=0$ but $g_2^*>0$, and consequently linear numerical width. The proof of its positive block gap uses an explicitly identified qualitative algebraic spectral-gap theorem, not a numerical harmonic truncation.

The error tolerance is also part of the actual experiment. The canonical activation constant involves both an irreducible copy and the physical query basis. Section~\ref{sec:calibration} gives an exact finite projection formulation, a semidefinite relaxation with a dual certificate, and a frame reconstruction problem with quantitative stability. It distinguishes these finite problems from infinite-dimensional gap positivity.

The new assertions are the multi-angle nongapped hierarchy, the alphabet/block formulation and its worked separation, and the finite calibration and stability results. The single-angle packet idea, entropy occupation estimate, uniform orbit bound and minimum-orbit compiler are inherited ingredients, identified as such. Convex spectral optimization, commutant structure, Diophantine approximation and transformation-group geometry are classical tools. No priority claim for those tools is made. The statement is about label width, not uniform computational space, total branching-program size or an exact finite integer optimum.
