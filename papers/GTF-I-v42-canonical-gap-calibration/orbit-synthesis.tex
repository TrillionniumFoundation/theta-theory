\section{Exact synthesis from a minimal orbit}\label{sec:synthesis}
Assume in this section that $V$ is a nontrivial real irreducible
orthogonal representation, of dimension $d$. Choose a unit vector
$v_0$ with orbit dimension $s=s(U)$, and set
\[
 \mathcal O=Gv_0,\qquad C=\conv\mathcal O.
\]
The orbit may be a singular orbit in the unit sphere. As a homogeneous
space in its own right it is nevertheless a smooth compact manifold.

\begin{lemma}[Inradius and quadratic support approximation]
\label{lem:orbit-enclosure}
One has $d^{-1}\mathbb B(V)\subseteq C\subseteq\mathbb B(V)$.
There are $c,C_0,h_0>0$, depending on $U$ and $v_0$, such that for
$0<h\le h_0$ a set $\mathcal V_h\subset\mathcal O$ with
$|\mathcal V_h|\le C_0h^{-s}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit-enclosure}
 (1-ch^2)C\subseteq\conv\mathcal V_h\subseteq C
 \quad\hbox{whenever }ch^2<1.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The mean of $U_gv_0$ is an invariant vector and is zero. Its covariance
is $I/d$: the covariance is self-adjoint and commutes with the
representation, so its eigenspaces are invariant; irreducibility and
trace one give the claim. This is the real Schur orthogonality
calculation, for example \cite[Lemma 5]{Barvinok}.
For unit $H$, let $f(g)=\langle H,U_gv_0\rangle$ and
$M=\max_g f(g)$. Since $-1\le f\le M$,
\[
 0\le\E[(M-f)(f+1)]=M-\E f^2=M-1/d.
\]
Every support function of $C$ is thus at least $1/d$, which proves
the inradius statement.

Equip $\mathcal O$ with its induced Riemannian metric. Finitely many
bounded smooth coordinate patches, with their inverses uniformly
Lipschitz on smaller patches, give an intrinsic $h$-net with at most
$C_0h^{-s}$ points. At a maximizer $y$ of
$z\mapsto\langle H,z\rangle$ on $\mathcal O$, its tangential
first derivative is zero. The second fundamental form of the compact
embedded orbit is bounded. Along a unit-speed minimizing geodesic
of length at most $h$, Taylor's formula therefore gives
\[
 \langle H,y\rangle-\langle H,v\rangle
                    \le L\norm H h^2
\]
for a nearby net point $v$, with $L$ independent of $H,y$.
The inradius bound gives
$\norm H\le d h_C(H)$. Hence
$h_{\conv\mathcal V_h}(H)\ge(1-Ldh^2)h_C(H)$ for every $H$.
Taking $c\ge Ld$, and using the support-function criterion, proves
\eqref{eq:orbit-enclosure}.
\end{proof}

The covering exponent $s$ and quadratic loss at a support maximum
are geometric facts, not new quantization exponents. What is needed
for realization is an enclosure compatible with \emph{every command}
and \emph{every layer}; the following construction supplies it.

\begin{theorem}[A minimal-orbit positive realization]
\label{thm:orbit-upper}
For any finite command alphabet, any finite unit seed set, and
$0<\rho\le1/(8d)$, the experiment \eqref{eq:law} has an exact
realization with at most $C_U N^{s(U)/2}$ available labels, for
$N\ge1$. All command matrices can be chosen independent of the epoch;
the final decoder may depend on $N$. Each row has at most $d+1$
successors. If the seeds belong to $C\cap\mathbb S(V)$, the amplitude
range improves to $0<\rho\le1/10$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose
\[
 h_N=\min\{h_0,(8cN)^{-1/2}\},\qquad
 \eta=1-ch_N^2\ge1-1/(8N),\qquad a_t=\eta^{-t}/4.
\]
Let $v_1,\ldots,v_M$ be the net points. The represented vector of a
label $i$ at layer $t$ is $a_tv_i$. These vectors describe the fixed
rows; they are not stored as real-valued runtime data.

The polytope $\conv\{v_i\}$ contains $\eta C$ and hence a ball
of radius $\eta/d$. For a unit seed $x$,
$4\rho x\in\eta C$ if $\rho\le1/(8d)$, because
$4\rho\le1/(2d)<\eta/d$. Choose its convex coordinates as the
initial distribution. More generally, if $x\in C$ and
$4\rho\le2/5<\eta$, the same initialization is valid.
For each command $a$ and label $i$, invariance of $C$ gives
\[
       \eta U_av_i\in\eta C\subseteq\conv\{v_j\}.
\]
Choose any convex coordinates of this point for the row $T_a(i,\cdot)$.
They do not depend on $t$. Since $a_{t+1}\eta=a_t$, conditional
expectation updates the represented vector by exactly $U_a$.
Induction gives mean $\rho U_wx$ after each complete prefix.

Use the terminal column $d_j(i)=a_N\langle e_j,v_i\rangle$.
Bernoulli's inequality gives $\eta^N\ge7/8$, so
$|d_j(i)|\le2/7<1$. This proves every numerical conditional law
exactly. Carath\'eodory's theorem gives $d+1$ successors. Finally
$M\le C_0h_N^{-s}\le C_UN^{s/2}$, with the fixed two-state output
cut absorbed into the constant. Neither convex feasibility nor row
lookup is priced as computation in this model.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The irreducible width law]\label{cor:irreducible-width}
Suppose a probability law on the finite command alphabet has a full
mean-zero group norm gap. For a nontrivial real irreducible $V$,
any nonempty finite unit seed set, fixed $0<\rho\le1/(8d)$, and
$0\le\varepsilon<\rho/(2\sqrt d)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:irrep-law}
 W_{N,\varepsilon}=\Theta_{U,p,\rho,\varepsilon}(N^{s(U)/2}).
\end{equation}
The same order holds for exact vector-state realizations and for
fixed-length realizations with common command rows. For seeds on a
minimal orbit the amplitude range is $0<\rho\le1/10$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A full group norm gap transfers to \eqref{eq:gap} with a fixed $g>0$.
Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:intrinsic-budget} at the peak $k$, using
$\kappa=\rho/\sqrt d-2\varepsilon>0$. It gives
$gN\le B\kappa^{-3-2/s} k^{2/s}$ and therefore the lower order.
The exact machine in Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit-upper} is an upper bound
for every allowed error, is vector-valued in its representation,
and has common command rows. These subclasses lie inside the
unrestricted clocked class, so the lower bound applies to them too.
The last assertion uses the stronger initialization in that theorem.
\end{proof}
