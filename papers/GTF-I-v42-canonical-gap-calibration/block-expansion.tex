\section{A block-expanded width theorem and a rational example}\label{sec:block-application}
For connected $H$, let $s_\lambda=\min_{\norm u=1}\dim(Hu)$ in each active nontrivial type, and put $\sigma_X=\max_\lambda s_\lambda$. Here activation and reachability use the entire invariant seed span, not an arbitrary displayed decomposition into copies.

\begin{theorem}[Dominant finite-block expansion]\label{thm:dominant-block}
Suppose a type $\lambda$ with $s_\lambda=\sigma_X$ has $g_L^*>0$ for a fixed finite $L$. If $R$ has dimension $D_R$, $0<\rho\le\min(1/10,1/(8D_R))$, and $0\le\varepsilon<\rho c_\lambda/2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-width}
 W_{N,\varepsilon}=\Theta(N^{\sigma_X/2}).
\end{equation}
The constants may depend on the fixed action, alphabet, block length, seeds, signal and error. Other active types need not have any block gap. The exact upper construction has common command rows and a horizon-dependent decoder.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a maximizing seed and copy in \eqref{eq:activation}, and choose a positive-gap length-$L$ word law. Feed independent complete words with this law, followed by any fixed remainder of length less than $L$. The uncompressed constituent orbit stays of norm one. Compressing all intermediate computation gives a common stochastic endpoint row for each starting state and word. Conditional centroids at block boundaries satisfy \eqref{eq:centroids}--\eqref{eq:conditional}; deterministic remaining commands cannot increase their mean norm. Terminal calibration therefore gives $q_t\ge\kappa=\rho c_\lambda-2\varepsilon>0$ at every block boundary.

The uniform orbit estimate of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-orbits} and the occupation theorem in Appendix~\ref{sec:orbit}, now in dimension $d_\lambda$, imply, at peak width $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blocked-budget}
 \lfloor N/L\rfloor g_L^*
 \le B_{d_\lambda,A_\lambda,s_\lambda}
       \kappa^{-3-2/s_\lambda}K^{2/s_\lambda}.
\end{equation}
Sphere and Lebesgue action norms agree by polar coordinates: both averages act trivially on the radial factor and identically on $L^2$ of the sphere. Thus no full presenting-group gap is substituted for the defined action gap. This proves the lower order.

For completeness the exact upper bound prices all branches. Split $R$ orthogonally into irreducible copies of dimensions $d_i$, including trivial copies. Choose branch $i$ initially with probability $w_i=d_i/D_R$ and store its identifier in a disjoint label set. In that branch synthesize the mean $(\rho/w_i)A_w\pi_ix$. The component seed has norm at most one, and $\rho/w_i\le1/(8d_i)$. The construction in Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit-upper}, whose initialization also accepts vectors in the unit ball, gives $O(N^{s_i/2})$ labels. A trivial copy needs a fixed two-label mixture to retain its initial scalar; its commands can be identities. The weighted sum of all branch outputs is exactly $\rho A_wx$. Total width is the sum of the branch widths, hence $O(N^{\sigma_X/2})$. Only one final query is answered, and its row is selected from the branch carried by the label. This proves the matching upper bound and prices the selector.
\end{proof}

The full group norm-gap sufficient theorem and the earlier one-step dominant-action theorem are contained in this statement, not withdrawn. Its hypothesis is still sufficient rather than necessary. Section~\ref{sec:toral} treats a family outside every finite-block expansion certificate.

\begin{theorem}[A rational alphabet whose first useful block has length two]
\label{thm:binary-example}
Let
\[
 a=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&3/5&-4/5\\0&4/5&3/5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 b=\begin{pmatrix}3/5&-4/5&0\\4/5&3/5&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathcal A=\{a,ab\}.
\]
For the standard $\SO(3)$ action on $\R^3$,
\[
 g_1^*(\mathcal A)=0,\qquad g_2^*(\mathcal A)>0.
\]
The second inequality uses the algebraic spectral-gap theorem \cite[Theorem 1.2]{BdS}; it is a qualitative external certificate. With unit coordinate seeds, $0<\rho\le1/24$ and $\varepsilon<\rho/(2\sqrt3)$, the numerical width is $\Theta(N)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The common angle is irrational relative to $2\pi$. Otherwise $2\cos\theta=6/5$ would be a rational algebraic integer, hence an integer. Powers of $a$ and $b$ therefore contain the full rotations about their respective axes in their closures. Their infinitesimal rotations generate $\mathfrak{so}(3)$, so the closure is $\SO(3)$. The given two-letter alphabet generates the same closed group since $a^{-1}(ab)=b$.

For any probability $p$, its average is
$V_a((1-p)I+pV_b)$. There is a nonconstant mean-zero function fixed by $V_b$, for example the third coordinate on the sphere. This average preserves its norm. Every average is a contraction, so every one-step norm is exactly one.

Put $c=a^{-1}ba$. It rotates about an axis different from that of $b$, and $b,c$ again generate a dense subgroup of $\SO(3)$. They have rational matrices. For the symmetric average
\[
 Q=(V_b+V_{b^{-1}}+V_c+V_{c^{-1}})/4
\]
the cited theorem, applied to the compact connected simple group $\SO(3)$, gives a spectral gap. In particular there is $\delta>0$ with
$\langle(I-Q)f,f\rangle\ge\delta\norm f^2$ on the mean-zero spherical subspace. The spherical action is a quotient of the regular group action; all entries of the adjoint matrices are algebraic. This is the sole deep input.

The uniform two-step average equals
\[
 V_{a^2}\,B,\qquad B=(I+V_c)(I+V_b)/4.
\]
For its four unitary summands $U_1=I,U_2=V_b,U_3=V_c,U_4=V_{cb}$, the variance identity gives
\begin{align*}
 \norm f^2-\norm{Bf}^2
 &=\frac1{16}\sum_{i<j}\norm{U_if-U_jf}^2\\
 &\ge\frac{\norm{f-V_bf}^2+\norm{f-V_cf}^2}{16}
 =\frac14\langle(I-Q)f,f\rangle.
\end{align*}
Thus $g_2^*\ge\delta/4>0$. The minimum orbit dimension is two and Proposition~\ref{prop:calibration-examples} gives $c=1/\sqrt3$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:dominant-block}. Finite matrix checks establish the displayed identities, not the external infinite-dimensional value of $\delta$.
\end{proof}
