\begin{abstract}
We distinguish the geometry of a numerical experiment from the dynamics of its command alphabet.
For a planar alphabet consisting of the identity and $r$ rotations whose angles satisfy a dual badly approximable inequality, we prove that the minimum hidden stochastic width is $\Theta(N^{r/(2r+1)})$, including every fixed sufficiently small uniform error. Every finite-block optimized action gap is zero. Thus one circle action, one orbit space and one minimum orbit dimension support infinitely many distinct width exponents. The proof pairs a multivariate command-packet converse with an exact common-row polygon realization.
For general orthogonal actions we optimize the command law, characterize finite-block expansion by the spectral radius of the uniform action average, and give a rational two-letter example with zero optimized one-step gap but positive two-step gap.
We also turn the query-calibrated activation into a finite algebraic optimization, with a semidefinite upper certificate, explicit multiplicity examples and query-frame perturbation bounds.
The resource throughout is nonuniform clocked atomic-row label width: tables and arithmetic are free, the horizon is fixed, and one terminal numerical query is answered.
\end{abstract}
