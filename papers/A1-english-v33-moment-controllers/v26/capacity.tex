\section{A quantitative converse on the subset lattice}
\label{sec:v26-capacity}
We now apply the separating-set bound to the actual memory convention
of Section~\ref{sec:v25-graph}. The scheduler may even read its whole
revealed past. The decoder receives the visited set, the query and its
one $M$-valued label, but not an additional order prefix. This is the
original decoder model, not the stronger oracle decoder allowed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-adaptive}. That stronger qualitative theorem
remains in force.

\subsection{An event using only acquired blocks}
Write $\lambda_e$ for the uniform law of edge $e$'s first command
block, and choose $\Omega_e$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:v25-regular-box}.
Let $\mathcal A_e$ be the event that this block belongs to $\Omega_e$
and its first $n_e$ reports are failures. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-first-block-mass}
 \beta_e(a)=\int_{\Omega_e}\mu_e\!\left(
       \prod_{i=1}^{n_e}L_{e,i,\mathrm{fail}}\right)d\lambda_e,
 \qquad \mathcal A=\bigcap_{e\in E}\mathcal A_e,
 \qquad \beta(a)=\prod_{e\in E}\beta_e(a).
\end{equation}
All completion commands and completion reports are unrestricted.
If $\kappa_e$ is a positive report margin, then
$\beta_e(a)\ge\kappa_e^{n_e}\lambda_e(\Omega_e)>0$, uniformly in
calibration. Empty products are one.

Choose constants $h_{e,\ell}$ so that the restricted command density
in Lemma~\ref{lem:v25-regular-box}, pushed to the positive scaled
prefix of length $\ell$, is at most $h_{e,\ell}/D_{e,\ell}$.
These constants can be chosen uniformly in calibration.
An allocation $\ell=(\ell_e)_{e\in F}$ is \emph{positive} if
$0\le\ell_e\le p_e$, $k=\sum_e\ell_e>0$, and
$D_{F,\ell}=\prod_{e\in F}D_{e,\ell_e}>0$.

\begin{lemma}[Acquired-block subprobability]\label{lem:v26-first-block}
The event $\mathcal A$ is independent of every coding seed and has
probability $\beta(a)$. For a positive allocation on $F$ and every
Borel set $U\subset\R^k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-first-block-density}
 \Prob\{\mathcal A, X_{F,\ell}\in U\}
 \le \frac{\beta(a)}{D_{F,\ell}}
       \prod_{\substack{e\in F\\\ell_e>0}}
              \frac{h_{e,\ell_e}}{\beta_e(a)}\,
              \operatorname{vol}_k(U).
\end{equation}
When $F$ is active, $X_{F,\ell}$ equals its actual acquired
posterior coordinate on $\mathcal A$, regardless of the observed
completion reports on other edges.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The complete edge tapes are independent under the product prior.
Marginalizing each completion block sums its report probabilities and
integrates its command law to one. Hence the first-block events have
probabilities \eqref{eq:v26-first-block-mass} and are independent
across edges. Their intersection does not involve a coding seed.
For a selected edge coordinate, multiply the restricted command law
by its actual first-word evidence. That evidence is at most one, so
the upper density is still $h_{e,\ell_e}/D_{e,\ell_e}$. An edge
without a selected coordinate contributes its mass $\beta_e(a)$.
Taking products and integrating complementary coordinates proves
\eqref{eq:v26-first-block-density}, first for rectangles and then for
all Borel sets by domination of the product density.
An active edge has revealed exactly its first block. Its posterior
is unaffected by the reports on distinct edges. Nonanticipatory vertex
choices introduce no further latent likelihood once the revealed
history is fixed. Thus the displayed coordinates are the actual ones.
No assertion about independence conditional on a visited set is used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Capacities and the risk bound}
The subset lattice $\mathscr B_G$ has vertices $S\subseteq V$ and
arcs $S\to S\cup\{x\}$ for $x\notin S$. Its source is
$\varnothing$, its sink is $V$, and its internal levels are
$1,\ldots,v-1$. For $S$ internal and a positive allocation on
$F=\delta(S)$, let $T_{S,\ell}$ be the affine recovery map from a
query-probability vector to $X_{F,\ell}$. The tensor left inverse and
the leading nonzero Newton inverse used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-adaptive} provide such a map. Choose $L_{S,\ell}$
so that its linear part has norm at most $L_{S,\ell}$ from the
query-weighted Euclidean norm to $\R^k$. The choice is uniform in
calibration; a zero scaled coordinate is never inverted.
Let $\omega_k$ be the volume of the Euclidean unit ball in $\R^k$ and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-capacity-constant}
 C_{S,\ell}(a)=\omega_kL_{S,\ell}^{\,k}
       \prod_{\substack{e\in\delta(S)\\\ell_e>0}}
                           \frac{h_{e,\ell_e}}{\beta_e(a)}.
\end{equation}
For $t\ge0$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-node-capacity}
 c_S(t;M,a)=\min\left\{1,
       M\min_{\ell\text{ positive on }\delta(S)}
                   \frac{C_{S,\ell}(a)t^{k/2}}{D_{\delta(S),\ell}}
                         \right\}.
\end{equation}
When $\delta(S)=\varnothing$, set $c_S(t;M,a)=1$.
Every nonempty cut admits a positive allocation of total one.
Finally set
\[
 H_G(t;M,a)=\min_{C\in\operatorname{Sep}(\mathscr B_G)}
                                      \sum_{S\in C}c_S(t;M,a).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Separator-capacity converse]\label{thm:v26-separator}
For every graph experiment of Section~\ref{sec:v25-graph}, every
calibration and integer $M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-capacity-risk}
 \mathcal R^{\rm max}_{G,M}(a)\ge
 \mathcal R^{\rm av}_{G,M}(a)\ge
 \frac{\beta(a)}{v-1}\int_0^\infty
                             [1-H_G(t;M,a)]_+\,dt.
\end{equation}
The lower bound allows the scheduler its full revealed past, provided
the decoder has only the label and the visited-set query descriptor.
Its separator optimization has $2^v-2$ internal vertices rather than
one vertex for each complete order. The common-event factor involves
only first-block evidences, not completion-word evidences.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the independent coding seeds. For a fixed visited set $S$, the
clock is $|S|$ and the decoder has at most $M$ query-prediction vectors.
It cannot use an unrecorded order prefix. If the loss at this boundary
is at most $t$, the true query vector is within weighted distance
$\sqrt t$ of one of these centres. Applying $T_{S,\ell}$ places
$X_{\delta(S),\ell}$ in a union of at most $M$ balls of radius
$L_{S,\ell}\sqrt t$. The recovery map is defined on arbitrary
centres, not merely on attainable predictions. Lemma~\ref{lem:v26-first-block}
gives
\[
 \Prob\{\mathcal A,\ S\text{ is visited},\ \mathcal L_{|S|}\le t\}
 \le\beta(a)M C_{S,\ell}(a)t^{k/2}/D_{\delta(S),\ell}.
\]
Indeed, intersecting with the visited-set event can only decrease the
unconditioned restricted mass. Minimize over positive allocations and
use the additional bound $\Prob(\mathcal A)=\beta(a)$.
This proves \eqref{eq:v26-node-smallball} with
\eqref{eq:v26-node-capacity}; the empty-cut bound is automatic.

Each realized order traces a source--sink path in $\mathscr B_G$.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-network-selection}, integrate the seeds,
and then take the infimum over a single common controller. The
right side is independent of that controller. Pre-sampling the finite
collection of decoder seeds for all labels, visited sets and queries
is legitimate; no history-correlated randomness is supplied as memory.
The comparison with the maximum-tape criterion is the one in
\eqref{eq:v25-risk-definitions}. Proposition~\ref{prop:v26-flow}
proves the assertion about the separator optimization. Formula
\eqref{eq:v26-first-block-mass} proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

The terms in \eqref{eq:v26-capacity-constant} display the local density
and query-recovery costs. They can deteriorate with the graph and its
menus; \eqref{eq:v26-capacity-risk} does not conceal this dependence
in a graph-independent constant. In particular, reducing the separator
index set is not an assertion of a size-uniform adaptivity gap.

\begin{corollary}[Recovery of the collision profile]
\label{cor:v26-profile-recovery}
Suppose $E\ne\varnothing$, and put $Q=Q_G(M,a)>0$.
Let $\mathcal C_Q$ be all internal subsets for which
$\Theta_{\delta(S)}(M,a)\ge Q$. For each $S\in\mathcal C_Q$ choose
an allocation attaining its cut profile and let $k_S$ be its total.
Put $a_S=C_{S,\ell}(a)\prod_{e\in\delta(S)}\ell_e!$.
Every $\sigma>0$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-sigma}
             \sum_{S\in\mathcal C_Q}a_S\sigma^{k_S/2}\le\tfrac12
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-profile-recovery}
       \mathcal R^{\rm av}_{G,M}(a)
                     \ge\frac{\beta(a)\sigma}{2(v-1)}Q.
\end{equation}
A positive $\sigma$ can be chosen uniformly in calibration and budget
for each fixed graph experiment.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By the minimax definition of $Q$, every complete order encounters a cut
whose profile is at least $Q$. Thus $\mathcal C_Q$ is a separator.
For the selected allocation, writing $V_{F,\ell}=\prod_eV_{e,\ell_e}$,
we have $MQ^{k_S/2}\le V_{F,\ell}$ and
$V_{F,\ell}/D_{F,\ell}\le\prod_e\ell_e!$.
Consequently $c_S(\sigma Q;M,a)\le a_S\sigma^{k_S/2}$.
Equations \eqref{eq:v26-sigma} and \eqref{eq:v26-capacity-risk},
and monotonicity of $H_G$, give \eqref{eq:v26-profile-recovery}
by integrating over $[0,\sigma Q]$. There are finitely many subsets
and allocations; all $a_S$ have a uniform finite upper bound and
$k_S\ge1$. Taking $\sigma\le1$ sufficiently small proves the
last assertion. Uniform positivity of $\beta(a)$ then recovers the
qualitative lower profile without enumerating complete traces.
\end{proof}
