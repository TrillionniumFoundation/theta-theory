\section{Causally compatible finite-state experiments}
\label{sec:v32-compatibility}

The collision classification determines the order of the optimal risk.
We now characterize the optimum itself without replacing a common
transducer by independently chosen checkpoint encoders. The characterization
is finite for a finite command alphabet. Its approximation of the original
command cube is uniform in the number of retained labels and across
additive exponent collisions. The source of this uniformity is positivity
of the report likelihoods, not regularity of an optimal encoding rule.

\subsection{Information and the retained resource}
Fix integers $T\ge1$ and $M\ge1$. At acquisition stage $t$, a command $g_t$ is
chosen before a report $x_t$ is observed. Conditional on a latent variable
$\vartheta$ with law $\mu$, the report has likelihood
$f^a_{g_t,x_t}(\vartheta)$. The same latent variable is used at every stage.
The calibration $a$ is fixed and known. The report alphabet $\mathcal X$ is
finite, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-positive}
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X} f^a_{g,x}(\vartheta)=1,
 \qquad 1\ge f^a_{g,x}(\vartheta)\ge\kappa>0.
\end{equation}
At stage $t$, query $q$ has full-history conditional mean
$p^a_{tq}(h)\in[0,1]$, and fixed weights
$\omega_{tq}\ge0$ sum to one. For the monomial experiment these are the
actual future failure-product queries; a query is revealed after
compression and only the selected checkpoint and its selected continuation
are executed. In particular, query outcomes are not an additional input
stream for the transducer.

The initial retained state is $S_0=1$, and $S_t\in[M]$ after every
acquisition stage. A transition reads only $(S_{t-1},g_t,x_t)$ and the
clock. It may use a fresh private random variable, which is then discarded.
The decoder $d_{tq}(S_t)\in[0,1]$ reads the retained state and the newly
announced query. There is no persistent public seed, no accessible record
of earlier outputs, and no retained command tape outside $S_t$.
Randomization of the decoder alone cannot improve squared loss, so it is
not included in the optimization variables.

There are two acquisition conventions. In the \emph{prescribed-command}
problem, commands are independent with a prescribed law, independent of
$\vartheta$ and the transducer. This is the convention of the scalar
collision theorem. In the \emph{state-controlled} problem, the next
command is selected using only $S_{t-1}$, the clock and a fresh private
coin. This second convention is an additional decision problem. It does
not supply a full-history scheduler for free. Neither convention assumes
regeneration of the latent variable or sufficiency of the current report
for later targets.

For a common transducer $F$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-risk}
 R_t(F)=\mathbb E_F\sum_q\omega_{tq}
       \bigl(d_{tq}(S_t)-p^a_{tq}(H_t)\bigr)^2,
 \qquad V_M=\inf_F\max_{1\le t\le T}R_t(F).
\end{equation}
The maximum is taken after expectation, and the infimum is outside the
maximum. The law in the expectation is prescribed in the first convention
and induced by the one common acquisition controller in the second.

\subsection{The finite compatibility equations}
Let the command alphabet $\mathcal C$ be finite. Write
$h=(c_1,x_1,\ldots,c_t,x_t)$, and put
\[
 L_h(\vartheta)=\prod_{s=1}^t f^a_{c_s,x_s}(\vartheta),
 \qquad Z_h=\mu(L_h),\qquad
 r_x(h,c)=\frac{\mu(L_h f^a_{c,x})}{Z_h}.
\]
The empty product is one. Every denominator is positive by
\eqref{eq:v32-positive}. If the query is a bounded future likelihood
$H^a_{tq}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-target}
 p^a_{tq}(h)=\frac{\mu(L_h H^a_{tq})}{Z_h}.
\end{equation}
The quantities $r_x(h,c)$ and $p^a_{tq}(h)$ are data of the optimization,
not fresh posterior states supplied to a memory-limited decoder.

Use nonnegative variables $a_t(c\mid i)$ and $b_t(c,j\mid i,x)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-action-rows}
 \sum_c a_t(c\mid i)=1,
 \qquad \sum_j b_t(c,j\mid i,x)=a_t(c\mid i)
       \quad\hbox{for every }x.
\end{equation}
The second equation enforces selection of the command before the report.
A controller first draws $c$ with probabilities $a_t(c\mid i)$ and,
after observing $x$, draws $j$ with probabilities
$b_t(c,j\mid i,x)/a_t(c\mid i)$ when the denominator is nonzero.
A zero command probability forces all its $b$ entries to be zero.
In the prescribed-command case impose $a_t(c\mid i)=\nu(c)$ for every
$i,t$, where $\nu$ is the prescribed command law.

Let $\alpha_h(i)$ denote the joint probability of the history $h$ and
retained state $i$. Its compatibility constraints are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-compatible}
 \begin{split}
 \alpha_{\varnothing}(i)&=\mathbf1_{\{i=1\}},\\
 \alpha_{hcx}(j)&=r_x(h,c)
              \sum_i\alpha_h(i)b_{t+1}(c,j\mid i,x),\qquad |h|=t.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
In particular, the same row $b_{t+1}(\cdot\mid i,x)$ must work for all
histories reaching state $i$. Replacing that row by a history-dependent
row gives a different memory resource.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite compatibility]\label{thm:v32-finite}
For either acquisition convention, $V_M$ on a finite command alphabet is
the minimum of $v$ over \eqref{eq:v32-action-rows},
\eqref{eq:v32-compatible}, $0\le d_{tq}(i),v\le1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-finite-program}
 \sum_{h:\,|h|=t}\sum_{i,q}\alpha_h(i)\omega_{tq}
       \bigl(d_{tq}(i)-p^a_{tq}(h)\bigr)^2\le v
                   \quad(1\le t\le T).
\end{equation}
Every minimizer is realized by one $M$-state transducer with fresh private
coins and no persistent seed. Conversely, every such transducer gives a
feasible point with its actual checkpoint risks. All minima in this
statement are attained. The optimization is a finite polynomial
optimization with real coefficients, and is not asserted to be convex.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first verify the posterior used in the coefficients, since commands
may be selected by the retained state. Conditional on an observed history
$h$ with positive probability, the joint law of $\vartheta$ and $S_t$
factors as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-posterior-factorization}
 \frac{L_h(\vartheta)\mu(\mathrm d\vartheta)}{Z_h}\,K_h(i)
\end{equation}
for a probability vector $K_h$. At the empty history this is immediate.
Selection of $c$ reweights $K_h(i)$ by $a_{t+1}(c\mid i)$ and does not
change the first factor. The next report multiplies the first factor by
$f^a_{c,x}$ and has normalizing probability $r_x(h,c)$. The private state
update changes only the second factor. This proves the factorization by
induction; it also proves that the report probability conditional on $h,c$
and on any preceding state of positive conditional mass is $r_x(h,c)$.
Thus multiplication by the command and update probabilities gives exactly
\eqref{eq:v32-compatible}. Histories of zero probability have zero joint
occupancies; their displayed coefficients remain well defined because
$Z_h>0$.

Any permitted controller consequently satisfies the displayed equations,
and its actual risk is the left side of
\eqref{eq:v32-finite-program}. Conversely, realize a feasible set of rows
by the command selection and private update described after
\eqref{eq:v32-action-rows}. Induction on history length proves that the
joint occupancies of this controller are the specified $\alpha_h(i)$.
The fixed decoder entries then realize the stated risks. No decoder reads
$h$, $K_h$ or $\alpha_h$; these are variables of an offline construction.

Every row lies in a compact simplex, and the decoder cube is compact.
The compatibility equations recursively determine the occupancies and
imply that the sum of all occupancies at each stage is one. Thus the
feasible set, with $0\le\alpha_h(i)\le1$, is closed and bounded.
It is nonempty, for example by a fixed command and state and a constant
decoder (or by the prescribed command law). A continuous objective attains
its minimum. The equations and inequalities are polynomial in the listed
variables; their coefficients include the fixed ratios in
\eqref{eq:v32-target}. The bilinear compatibility equations cannot be
removed merely by invoking a flow decomposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional centroids]\label{prop:v32-centroids}
For fixed feasible acquisition and update rows, the mean-risk decoder can
be chosen as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-centroid}
 d_{tq}(i)=
 \frac{\sum_{|h|=t}\alpha_h(i)p^a_{tq}(h)}
      {\sum_{|h|=t}\alpha_h(i)}
\end{equation}
when the denominator is positive, with an arbitrary value otherwise.
This choice minimizes every checkpoint risk simultaneously. Its risk is
\[
 \sum_{h,i,q}\alpha_h(i)\omega_{tq}p^a_{tq}(h)^2
 -\sum_{i,q}\omega_{tq}
  \frac{\bigl(\sum_h\alpha_h(i)p^a_{tq}(h)\bigr)^2}
       {\sum_h\alpha_h(i)},
\]
where a zero-denominator summand is zero. This expression is continuous
at zero occupancy. The proposition concerns mean risk, not a
worst-history centroid rule.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Complete the square in each decoder coordinate. The numerator in
\eqref{eq:v32-centroid} is between zero and its denominator, so the
centroid is an allowed prediction. If that denominator tends to zero,
the corresponding quotient in the risk lies between zero and the
denominator. This proves its continuous extension. Decoder coordinates
belong to individual stages, so these minimizations are simultaneous.
\end{proof}

In the prescribed-command model there is an equivalent conditional
formulation. Assume $\nu(c)>0$ on its finite alphabet. Its history probabilities $\pi(h)$ are then known and positive.
Write $z_h(i)=\alpha_h(i)/\pi(h)$ and
$b_t(c,j\mid i,x)=\nu(c)U_t(j\mid i,c,x)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-conditional}
 z_{hcx}(j)=\sum_i z_h(i)U_{t+1}(j\mid i,c,x),\qquad
 z_{\varnothing}(i)=\mathbf1_{\{i=1\}}.
\end{equation}
Replacing \eqref{eq:v32-finite-program} by the inequalities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-worst-program}
 \sum_{i,q}z_h(i)\omega_{tq}
    \bigl(d_{tq}(i)-p^a_{tq}(h)\bigr)^2\le v
       \quad\hbox{for every }t,h
\end{equation}
gives exactly the common-filter worst-history optimum. This follows by
the same realization argument. It does not average separately optimized
checkpoint encoders.

\begin{remark}[Why seed convexification is a different problem]
An independently selected controller whose identity is retained by the
encoder and decoder for the whole experiment uses a persistent public
seed. Averaging its risk vectors gives a convexified experiment, but need
not produce rows and decoders feasible in the unseeded problem above.
The regenerative flow results retain their explicitly stated seed and
public-descriptor conventions. They are not used to replace
\eqref{eq:v32-compatible} in the nonregenerative problem.
\end{remark}

\section{Uniform finite approximation without collision denominators}
\label{sec:v32-approximation}

We return to the calibrated positive monomial experiment. Put $T=N-1$,
$\mathcal G=[\eta,1-\eta]^{J_0}$, where $J_0$ is the number of detector cells, and use the fixed finite future probe menus.
Let $\mathcal P_\delta$ be a finite Borel partition of the command cube
with representatives $c(B)\in B$ and
\[
 \|g-c(B)\|_\infty\le\delta\quad(g\in B).
\]
Cells have positive volume and their boundaries are assigned by a fixed
rule. Write $Q_\delta(g)=c(B)$ and let $\nu_\delta(c(B))$ be the
normalized volume of $B$. The finite experiment uses these cell
probabilities and the \emph{actual} report likelihoods at $c(B)$, with
the original common latent parameter. Let
$V^{\mathrm{av}}_{\delta,M}(a)$ and
$V^{\max}_{\delta,M}(a)$ be the exact finite compatibility values from
the preceding section.

\begin{lemma}[Positive-likelihood perturbation]\label{lem:v32-perturbation}
If two histories have the same report word and corresponding commands
differ by at most $\delta$ in sup norm, then, at stage $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-query-lipschitz}
 \max_q|p^a_{tq}(h)-p^a_{tq}(\widetilde h)|
                       \le t\delta/\kappa.
\end{equation}
The same bound holds for any one bounded future likelihood in $[0,1]$
that does not read the discarded prefix. The total variation distance
between the report laws at commands $g,\widetilde g$, conditional on
any $\vartheta$, is at most $\delta$. All these bounds are uniform in
$a$, including additive exponent coincidences.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For an accepted detector cell,
$f_{g,j}=g_jk_j^a$; for failure,
$f_{g,\dagger}=\sum_j(1-g_j)k_j^a$. Since the detector probabilities
are nonnegative and sum to one,
\[
 \frac12\sum_x|f_{g,x}-f_{\widetilde g,x}|
 \le \sum_j|g_j-\widetilde g_j|k_j^a\le\delta.
\]
Along the line segment $g_s=\widetilde g+s(g-\widetilde g)$, each
likelihood derivative has absolute value at most $\delta$. For the
product of $t$ likelihoods let
$A_s=\partial_s\log L_{h_s}$. Positivity gives
$|A_s|\le t\delta/\kappa$. With $H\in[0,1]$ the chosen fixed future
likelihood and $\mu_s=L_{h_s}\mu/\mu(L_{h_s})$,
\[
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm ds}\mu_s(H)
       =\mu_s\bigl((H-\mu_s(H))A_s\bigr).
\]
The absolute value is at most $t\delta/\kappa$. Differentiation under
the integral is justified by the finite product, the positive uniform
lower bound and the displayed bounded score. Integration over $s\in[0,1]$
proves \eqref{eq:v32-query-lipschitz}. No exponent difference was inverted
and no derivative with respect to a colliding calibration was taken.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform compatibility approximation]\label{thm:v32-grid}
Under the hypotheses and the original retained-resource convention of
Theorem~\ref{thm:resolution-main}, for every $a\in K$, $M\ge1$ and
partition as above,
\begin{align}
 \left|V^{\mathrm{av}}_{\delta,M}(a)
               -\mathfrak R_{M,N}^{a,\mathrm{av}}\right|
       &\le T(1+2/\kappa)\delta,
                  \label{eq:v32-grid-average}\\
 0\le\mathfrak R_{M,N}^{a,\max}
               -V^{\max}_{\delta,M}(a)
       &\le 2T\delta/\kappa.
                  \label{eq:v32-grid-worst}
\end{align}
An optimizer of the finite program, implemented by rounding the current
command and applying its one common transition table, attains the
corresponding upper bound in the continuous experiment. The mean-risk
optimizer is within $2T(1+2/\kappa)\delta$ of the continuous optimum.
No continuity of the original transitions is assumed. In particular,
the finite values converge uniformly over all calibrations and all
integer budgets in the absolute-error sense of these inequalities.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Introduce a lifted finite experiment. Draw each actual command $g$
uniformly on the original cube, retain its representative $c=Q_\delta(g)$,
and generate its report with likelihood $f^a_{c,x}$ instead of
$f^a_{g,x}$. Keep the same latent parameter throughout. Conditional on
that parameter and the commands, couple the two report sequences.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v32-perturbation} and a union bound give probability at
most $t\delta$ of a discrepancy up to stage $t$. Consequently expectations
of any $[0,1]$-valued loss, including a loss using arbitrary measurable
state kernels and their private coins, differ by at most $t\delta$
when the target function is held fixed. Changing the target from
$p^a_{tq}(h)$ to $p^a_{tq}(Q_\delta h)$ changes squared loss by at most
$2t\delta/\kappa$, since
\[
 |(d-p)^2-(d-p')^2|\le2|p-p'|\quad(d,p,p'\in[0,1]).
\]
Thus the complete risk comparison at stage $t$ has error at most
$t(1+2/\kappa)\delta$.

A finite controller lifted by $g\mapsto Q_\delta(g)$ has exactly its
finite risk in the lifted experiment. The preceding comparison gives
the continuous upper bound. For the reverse comparison take any
continuous controller $U_t(j\mid i,g,x)$, with its fixed decoder.
In the lifted experiment the position of $g$ within its cell, conditional
on that cell, is independent of the latent variable, the reports and the
preceding retained state. Therefore its finite state law is realized by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-cell-average}
 \overline U_t(j\mid i,c,x)=
   \frac{1}{\nu(B)}\int_B U_t(j\mid i,g,x)\,\nu(\mathrm dg),
                    \qquad c=c(B).
\end{equation}
These are stochastic rows. They are implemented with fresh private coins;
the location within a cell is neither retained nor supplied to the decoder.
The averaged controller has precisely the lifted risk with the finite
query targets. Comparing with the original continuous controller and
taking infima proves \eqref{eq:v32-grid-average}. The two applications
of that inequality give the asserted $2T(1+2/\kappa)\delta$ optimality
gap for a lifted finite mean-risk minimizer.

For worst-history risk, restriction of a continuous controller to the
finite set of representatives cannot increase its supremum, and hence
$V^{\max}_{\delta,M}(a)\le\mathfrak R_{M,N}^{a,\max}$. Conversely, at
a fixed actual history, the state distribution of a rounded finite
controller equals its distribution at the representative history with
the same report word. Only the target changes. Applying
\eqref{eq:v32-query-lipschitz} gives the opposite bound in
\eqref{eq:v32-grid-worst}. This argument neither averages histories nor
moves a coding expectation outside the supremum.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Nonregenerative state-controlled acquisition]
\label{cor:v32-controlled}
Let $V^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_M(a)$ be the mean-risk value when each command is
selected from the retained state before the report, with the same positive
monomial likelihoods and the same future targets. Allow arbitrary Borel
command kernels on the cube. Its finite-alphabet compatibility value
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-controlled-grid}
 0\le V^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_{\delta,M}(a)
                  -V^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_M(a)
       \le T(1+2/\kappa)\delta.
\end{equation}
No regeneration is required. This corollary does not assert the collision
profile lower bound for an acquisition law optimized jointly with the
filter; that lower bound uses the prescribed common exploration law.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A finite command is an allowed continuous command, proving the first
inequality. For a continuous controller with command kernel
$A_t(\mathrm dg\mid i)$ and update kernel $U_t(j\mid i,g,x)$, replace
only the report likelihood at $g$ by its value at $Q_\delta(g)$. Define
\[
 a_t(c\mid i)=A_t(B\mid i),\qquad
 b_t(c,j\mid i,x)=\int_B U_t(j\mid i,g,x)A_t(\mathrm dg\mid i).
\]
These satisfy \eqref{eq:v32-action-rows}. In the perturbed experiment,
the within-cell command, conditional on the previous state and the cell,
is independent of the latent parameter and of the new report. Integrating
it therefore yields exactly these finite rows. This assertion can also
be obtained by the induction in \eqref{eq:v32-posterior-factorization},
now retaining only representative commands in the recorded history.

Couple the original and perturbed experiments until their first report
discrepancy. As long as their states agree, draw the same continuous
command and use the same private update randomization. The probability
of discrepancy through stage $t$ is at most $t\delta$. On coincident
histories, the posterior targets at the actual and representative commands
differ by at most $t\delta/\kappa$. The loss comparison used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v32-grid} applies and gives the upper bound after taking
infima. Commands in the full-history benchmark disclose no hidden
parameter beyond their report history: the action probabilities cancel
from its posterior by the factorization proved above.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite global certificates and the collision law}
\label{sec:v32-certificates}

The finite compatibility program has a fixed compact parameter domain
after its recursive variables are eliminated. This permits a global
certificate that does not rely on a local numerical optimum. We state
an elementary exhaustive construction to make its scope explicit.
Its size is not asserted to be practical or polynomial.

\begin{lemma}[Rational-controller nets]\label{lem:v32-controller-net}
In a finite prescribed-command experiment, let $W_{\delta,M,b}$ be the
minimum risk over controllers whose transition probabilities and decoder
entries are integer multiples of $1/b$, where $b\ge1$ is an integer. For either the
mean or worst-history criterion,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-controller-net}
 0\le W_{\delta,M,b}-V_{\delta,M}
       \le E_{M,b}:=\frac{T(M-1)+2}{b}.
\end{equation}
The minimum defining $W_{\delta,M,b}$ is over a finite explicitly
specified set of feasible common controllers.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each stochastic row, round its first $M-1$ entries down to multiples
of $1/b$ and put the remaining mass in its last entry. The resulting row
has total variation distance at most $(M-1)/b$ from the original row.
Round each decoder entry to a multiple of $1/b$ within $1/b$.
At a fixed history, couple original and rounded transitions while their
states agree. Their final states disagree with probability at most
$t(M-1)/b$. A squared loss is in $[0,1]$, and changing a decoder entry
by at most $1/b$ changes that loss by at most $2/b$. Thus each conditional
history risk changes by at most $E_{M,b}$. The same bound holds after
expectation and after either prescribed maximum. Apply it to an exact
finite optimizer. The opposite inequality follows because the net is a
subset of the admissible controllers.
\end{proof}

Let $[\ell_F,u_F]$ be certified enclosures for the objective of every
controller $F$ in this finite net, and put
\[
 \ell=\min_F\ell_F,\qquad u=\min_Fu_F.
\]
If each enclosure has width at most $\tau$, then $u-\ell\le\tau$:
use in this inequality a controller attaining the smallest lower endpoint.
Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:v32-grid}, this gives the following result.

\begin{theorem}[Global risk certificates]\label{thm:v32-certificates}
Write $E_\delta=T(1+2/\kappa)\delta$ and
$E_\delta^{\max}=2T\delta/\kappa$. The original continuous risks satisfy
\begin{align}
 \max\{0,\ell-E_{M,b}-E_\delta\}
 &\le\mathfrak R_{M,N}^{a,\mathrm{av}}
       \le\min\{1,u+E_\delta\},\label{eq:v32-interval-av}\\
 \max\{0,\ell-E_{M,b}\}
 &\le\mathfrak R_{M,N}^{a,\max}
       \le\min\{1,u+E_\delta^{\max}\},
                         \label{eq:v32-interval-max}
\end{align}
where the net and its objective use the indicated criterion. A controller
attaining $u$ gives the corresponding feasible upper certificate by
rounding incoming commands. The widths before clipping are at most
$\tau+E_{M,b}+2E_\delta$ and
$\tau+E_{M,b}+E_\delta^{\max}$, respectively.

For monomial experiments all finite coefficients are determined by the
finite read-only moment list
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-moment-list}
 \left\{\mu(t^{\alpha\cdot a}):
                 \alpha\in\mathbb N_0^r,\ |\alpha|\le N\right\},
\end{equation}
the detector matrix and the prescribed commands. Certified evaluation
of that finite list and the fixed detector and command coefficients
suffices for the enclosures. A mere full-support
assumption is not an effective-access assumption on the prior.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exact finite value is between $\ell-E_{M,b}$ and $u$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:v32-controller-net}. Apply the two inequalities of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v32-grid}. The risk of a particular feasible controller
is bounded above by its own interval upper endpoint; this is the reason
an upper certificate has an operational realization. Taking the minimum
of lower endpoints over the \emph{complete} finite net and subtracting
the net error is the lower certificate. Sampling a few controllers does
not provide that lower bound.

A product of $t$ report factors and one length-$(N-t)$ future probe is
a linear combination of monomials indexed by $NA$. Report evidence uses
only smaller sums. Division occurs only by positive evidences, not by
exponent differences. The original lower bound $Z_h\ge\kappa^t$ makes
interval evaluation of these ratios convergent once moments and the
fixed coefficients are supplied with certified precision. The net is
finite, so its finitely many objective enclosures can all be refined to
the specified width. This is a finite-oracle statement. It makes no
claim that an arbitrary abstract prior possesses a computable moment
oracle. The exact variational and approximation theorems do not need
such an oracle.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Collision-uniform relative certification]
\label{cor:v32-relative}
In the prescribed-command model of Theorem~\ref{thm:resolution-main},
choose command partitions with radius at most $M^{-3}$, take
$b=M^4$, and evaluate every controller in the corresponding finite net
to objective-enclosure width at most $M^{-3}$. There is a constant $C_0$
depending only on $T$ and $\kappa$ such that the enclosing intervals
in Theorem~\ref{thm:v32-certificates} have width at most $C_0M^{-3}$.
For either risk criterion, their width divided by the true risk is at
most $C_0/(cM)$, uniformly in $a\in K$. Here $c$ is the positive lower
constant in the inherited collision classification. The attaining
controllers have exactly the original persistent-label resource.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The displayed choices give $E_{M,b}=O(M^{-3})$ and the same bound for
both command errors and the evaluation width. For every nontrivial
checkpoint, $p_{n,N-n}\ge1$ and $\mathcal V_{N-n,1}=1$. Consequently
\[
 \Xi_N(M,a)\ge M^{-2},\qquad
 \mathfrak R_{M,N}^{a,\mathrm{av}}
       \ge c\Xi_N(M,a)\ge cM^{-2}.
\]
The worst-history risk is no smaller. Divide the certified absolute
width by this uniform lower bound. No lower bound on a nonzero collision
gap, and no constancy of the attained dimension, is required. The
finite-state construction and approximation were proved without using
this geometric lower bound; the final division is therefore not circular.
\end{proof}

The corollary converts the attained collision theorem into a relative
certificate for the original nonregenerative common-filter decision
problem, rather than only for separately optimized local blocks. The
certificate is exhaustive, and its computational cost can be enormous.
The fixed experiment, prior, horizon and read-only program convention
are unchanged. In particular, this is not a graph-size-uniform claim,
not a finite-bit arithmetic bound and not a calibration-blind code.

\section{A positive delayed-use experiment with exact causal risk}
\label{sec:v32-delayed}

The compatibility equations have an elementary test in which two schedules
are both admissible and local prediction costs are strictly positive.
The calculation below also records the effect of \emph{not} supplying a
persistent public seed. It is a separate finite positive experiment,
not a substitute for the general collision theorem.

Fix $0<\gamma\le1/8$ and $0<e\le \gamma/2$. Let
$Z_1,Z_2,V_1,V_2$ be independent symmetric signs. The four acquisition
reports $X_1,X_2,U_1,U_2$ are conditionally independent, with
\[
 \Pr(X_j=x\mid Z_j=z)=\tfrac12(1+xz/2),\qquad
 \Pr(U_j=u\mid V_j=v)=\tfrac12(1+uv/2).
\]
All acquisition probabilities are at least $1/4$, and the reports are
independent symmetric signs unconditionally. Query $j$ is a Bernoulli
trial of latent success probability
\[
             \tfrac12+2\gamma Z_j+2eV_j.
\]
Its success and failure probabilities are at least $1/8$. Once its two
reports have been acquired, the full-history conditional mean is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-delayed-target}
              \tfrac12+\gamma X_j+eU_j.
\end{equation}

There are two public, deterministic admissible schedules. The
\emph{early} schedule reads all four acquisition reports as one block
before checkpoint 1; no informative acquisition occurs between the
checkpoints. The \emph{just-in-time} schedule reads $(X_1,U_1)$ before
checkpoint 1 and $(X_2,U_2)$ before checkpoint 2. In both schedules
memory is charged after each acquisition block and at each checkpoint;
there are two retained labels, the clock is free, no public seed is
retained, and no score or past prediction is available for later updates.
Each report is acquired exactly once. Only one independently selected
checkpoint query is executed. All four scheduled acquisition trials are
completed even when checkpoint 1 is scored, and the score is not fed back.
Thus both schedules use four acquisition reports and one scored query.
Queries at unselected checkpoints are not additional observations. The block-boundary charging
rule is part of this example; it is not the per-report rule of the scalar
monomial theorem.

Let $V_{\rm early}(e)$ and $V_{\rm jit}(e)$ denote the infimum of the
maximum of the two expected squared excesses over the full-history
predictors. Private update randomization is allowed in both infima.

\begin{theorem}[Exact unseeded delayed-use law]\label{thm:v32-delayed}
For the experiment and resource convention just specified,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-delayed-exact}
 \begin{split}
 V_{\rm jit}(e)&=e^2,\\
 V_{\rm early}(e)&=\gamma^2+e^2-
       \max\left\{\frac{\gamma^2}{3},
                       \frac{(2\gamma+e)^2}{15}\right\}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Both values are attained by deterministic two-label encoders and fixed
stage-dependent decoders. In particular,
\[
 \frac{V_{\rm early}(e)}{V_{\rm jit}(e)}\longrightarrow\infty
                         \quad\hbox{as }e\downarrow0.
\]
This comparison is between two admissible common-controller designs with
the same total acquisition count, the same query protocol and the same
retained-label and seed convention. It is not a comparison with
incompatible checkpoint-specific initial designs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We work with the centered targets
$Y_j=\gamma X_j+eU_j$, whose variances are $\gamma^2+e^2$.
For a single current target the four values are
$-\gamma-e,-\gamma+e,\gamma-e,\gamma+e$, all with probability $1/4$.
For squared loss an optimum two-center scalar quantizer partitions an
ordered set into contiguous cells: assigning each point to a nearest
center cannot increase loss. The middle split has distortion $e^2$.
The two extreme splits have explained variance $(\gamma+e)^2/3$, whereas
the middle split has explained variance $\gamma^2$. Since
$e\le \gamma/2$ implies $(\gamma+e)^2/3\le3\gamma^2/4$, the middle split is
optimal. A fresh current pair is independent of all earlier retained
information, which cannot enlarge the two available reconstruction
values at that checkpoint. Resetting to the sign $X_j$ therefore attains
$e^2$ at both checkpoints, and the one-block lower bound proves the
just-in-time formula.

For the early schedule, later private transitions without new information
cannot improve on decoding directly from the first retained label.
Indeed, conditional on that label, averaging any later random prediction
cannot increase squared loss, and its conditional expectation is a
function of that label alone. Thus every early controller is dominated
by a stochastic binary encoder of the four-report word followed by two
fixed decoders.

Write $Q\in[0,1]$ for the conditional probability of label 1 given that
word, let $p=\mathbb E Q$, and put $v_j=\mathbb E(Y_jQ)$. If
$0<p<1$, centroid decoding makes checkpoint $j$'s risk
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-binary-centroid}
 \gamma^2+e^2-\frac{v_j^2}{p(1-p)}.
\end{equation}
A constant encoder explains no variance. Independent reflection of either
coordinate pair $(X_j,U_j)$ changes the sign of $v_j$ without changing
$p$ or the objective. We may therefore take $v_1,v_2\ge0$.
Replacing $Q$ by the average of itself and its coordinate-swapped version
preserves $p$ and changes both $v_j$ to $(v_1+v_2)/2$. This cannot
decrease their smaller square. It is an allowed private randomized
encoder, not a public mixture of decoders. We may consequently optimize
over encoders with $v_1=v_2$.

Put $S=Y_1+Y_2$. At a fixed $p$, the largest possible value of
$\mathbb E(SQ)$ is obtained by taking an upper tail of $S$, randomizing
only at a tied threshold. This follows by transferring any positive
assignment mass from a smaller value of $S$ to a larger one. Symmetric
assignment within ties gives equal coordinate correlations. Relabeling
and joint reflection reduce the range to $0<p\le1/2$.
On an interval between successive tail masses, write
$\mathbb E(SQ)=A+sp$, where $s\ge0$ and $A\ge0$. Apart from a
positive constant, the explained variance is
\[
                       \frac{(A+sp)^2}{p(1-p)}.
\]
Its derivative has the sign of $(s+2A)p-A$ whenever its numerator is
nonzero. It therefore has no interior strict maximum. It is enough to
consider the finite tail endpoints, including a half-mass endpoint.

The sixteen values of $S$ are sums of
$\gamma(X_1+X_2)+e(U_1+U_2)$. For $e\le \gamma/2$, the sums of the
largest $n$ values, $1\le n\le8$, are the following $A_n$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c c c}
\toprule
$n$ & $A_n$ & explained variance $A_n^2/[4n(16-n)]$\\
\midrule
1 & $2\gamma+2e$ & $(\gamma+e)^2/15$\\
2 & $4\gamma+2e$ & $(2\gamma+e)^2/28$\\
3 & $6\gamma+2e$ & $(3\gamma+e)^2/39$\\
4 & $8\gamma$ & $\gamma^2/3$\\
5 & $8\gamma+2e$ & $(4\gamma+e)^2/55$\\
6 & $8\gamma+4e$ & $(2\gamma+e)^2/15$\\
7 & $8\gamma+4e$ & $4(2\gamma+e)^2/63$\\
8 & $8\gamma+4e$ & $(2\gamma+e)^2/16$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Ties at $e=\gamma/2$ do not change these sums. Rows 1 and 2 are bounded
by row 6, and rows 7 and 8 are also bounded by row 6. Row 3 is bounded
by row 4, since
$4\gamma^2-6\gamma e-e^2\ge3\gamma^2/4$.
For row 5, if $e\le \gamma/4$ then
$7\gamma^2-24\gamma e-3e^2\ge13\gamma^2/16>0$, so row 4 dominates.
If $e\ge \gamma/4$, then
$-\gamma^2+5\gamma e+2e^2\ge3\gamma^2/8>0$, so row 6 dominates.
Thus the largest explained variance is the maximum in
\eqref{eq:v32-delayed-exact}.

Both remaining candidates are attained without encoder randomization.
The four-word tail is $\{X_1=X_2=1\}$. Its two conditional centered
means are $(\gamma,\gamma)$ and $(-\gamma/3,-\gamma/3)$.
The six-word tail adds
$\{X_1=-X_2,\ U_1=U_2=1\}$. Its centered means in either coordinate
are $(2\gamma+e)/3$ and $-(2\gamma+e)/5$. Add $1/2$ to obtain the allowed
prediction probabilities. These two encoders attain the computed maximum
of the explained variances, proving the exact formula. Finally,
$V_{\rm early}(e)\to2\gamma^2/3>0$ while $V_{\rm jit}(e)=e^2$.
\end{proof}

The active branch changes at $e=(\sqrt5-2)\gamma$. The formula is an
analytic solution of the private-randomization minimax problem, not merely
an enumeration of deterministic partitions. A retained public coin gives
a different decision problem and need not have this value. For example,
a fair public coin that selects which large sign $X_j$ to retain gives
risk $\gamma^2/2+e^2$ at each checkpoint. This observation is an admissible
upper bound only in that enlarged resource convention. It is not used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v32-delayed}.

At each checkpoint the currently scored marginal law is the same in the
two schedules, and its two-label distortion is $e^2$. Nevertheless, the
common early controller must preserve competing directions for later
use. Equations~\eqref{eq:v32-compatible} retain that constraint. The
example exhibits its consequence in a model with positive likelihoods and
positive local risks; the uniform approximation and relative certificate
results address the whole prescribed monomial family, rather than this
one finite test alone.
