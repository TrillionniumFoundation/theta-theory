\section{Introduction}
A finite future experiment tests only a finite-dimensional part of a
posterior distribution. Its resolution nevertheless need not be the
resolution of that entire test space. The past may acquire fewer
directions, and the directions it acquires may become indistinguishable
as the experiment varies. There is a further constraint in a sequential
experiment: a compressed state must be updated without recovering the
discarded history.

We determine these effects jointly for positive sparse monomial
experiments. The answer is a finite profile of maximal Vandermonde
products of future exponents, truncated by the dimension acquired by
the past. It gives matching upper and lower bounds for every integer
memory budget, uniformly across additive collisions. The upper bound
holds on the whole attainable posterior image and is realized by one
causal finite-state filter. The lower bound holds under one actual,
unconditional acquisition law. In particular, the profile describes
transitions between memory regimes, rather than only the eventual
error exponent at each separate calibration.

\subsection{Experiment and resolution profile}
Let
\[
 A=\{0=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_{r-1}\},\qquad
 W_A=\operatorname{span}\{t^{a_i}:0\le i<r\},\qquad r\ge2.
\]
We write $I=[l,u]=[0,1]$, $D=a_{r-1}$, and
\[
 mA=\{a_{i_1}+\cdots+a_{i_m}:0\le i_j<r\},\qquad
 h_A(m)=|mA|,
\]
with $0A=\{0\}$ and $h_A(0)=1$. The set $mA$ contains distinct
values; formal labels below retain multiplicities. The latent
parameter has a fixed full-support probability $\mu$ on $I$.
No density for $\mu$ is assumed.

The known exponent vector ranges over a compact subset $K$ of the
strictly ordered one-step chamber. One fixed matrix $C=(c_{ji})$ of
column rank $r$ gives detector cells
\[
 k_j^a(t)=\sum_i c_{ji}t^{a_i},\qquad
 \sum_j k_j^a(t)=1,\qquad k_j^a(t)\ge k_*>0
 \quad(a\in K,\ t\in I).
\]
A command $g\in[\eta,1-\eta]^J$, $0<\eta<1/2$, retains cell $j$
with probability $g_j$. A trial reports either that cell or failure.
Every trial, including failure, is counted. All report likelihoods are
bounded below by a fixed $\kappa>0$. The precise experiment and its
future-only continuations are defined in Section~\ref{sec:experiment}.

Fix a total horizon $N\ge2$. At checkpoint $n$ there are $m=N-n$
trials left. Choose
$H\ge\max\{1,N\max_{a\in K}a_{r-1}\}$ and put
\[
 \Lambda_m^+=\{\alpha\in\N_0^r:|\alpha|=m\}\setminus\{me_0\},
 \qquad q_m=\binom{m+r-1}{r-1}-1,
 \qquad x_\alpha(a)=H^{-1}\alpha\cdot a.
\]
Here $\N_0$ denotes the nonnegative integers. Even at a collision,
all the formal labels in $\Lambda_m^+$ are retained. Define
\[
 \mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a)=
 \max_{\substack{F\subset\Lambda_m^+\\|F|=\ell}}
       \prod_{\{\alpha,\beta\}\subset F}
           |x_\alpha(a)-x_\beta(a)|,
 \qquad \mathcal V_{m,1}=1.
\]
Zero products are allowed. The acquired truncation and the checkpoint
and causal profiles are
\[
 \begin{split}
 p_{n,m}&=\min\{n(r-1),q_m\},\\
 \Psi_{n,m}(M,a)&=
   \max_{1\le\ell\le p_{n,m}}
       \left(\frac{\mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a)}{M}\right)^{2/\ell},\\
 \Xi_N(M,a)&=\max_{1\le n<N}\Psi_{n,N-n}(M,a).
 \end{split}
\]
Endpoint profiles are zero. Changing the fixed normalization $H$
changes only comparison constants.

A checkpoint encoder reads the complete acquired prefix and retains
one of $M$ labels. A causal filter instead updates only its previous
label from the current command and report. After the label is formed,
a query is sampled independently from a fixed finite spanning menu of
actual $m$-trial failure products. Only that query is executed. Regret
is excess squared loss relative to the conditional full-history
predictor. Definition~\ref{def:finite-state} specifies the retained-state
resource; Definition~\ref{def:monomial-risks-v24} specifies the risk
criteria and the order of their quantifiers.

\subsection{Intersecting collision loci}
\label{sec:early-collision-example}
The following instance exhibits both the rank changes and the
intermediate memory regimes. Let
\[
 A_{u,v}=\{0,1,2+u,3+v\},\qquad |u|,|v|\le1/16,
\]
and use the four positive detector cells
\[
 k_0(t)=\frac14+\frac{t+t^{2+u}+t^{3+v}}{16},\qquad
 k_i(t)=\frac14-\frac{t^{a_i}}{16}\quad(1\le i\le3).
\]
They sum to one, have a fixed coefficient matrix of rank four, and
are bounded between $3/16$ and $7/16$. Take $N=5$ and write
\[
 \rho=\max\{|u|,|v|\},\qquad
 \tau=\min\{|u|,|v-u|,|v-2u|\}.
\]
Corollary~\ref{cor:two-parameter}, proved in
Section~\ref{sec:collision-phases}, gives both causal risk criteria the
uniform order
\begin{equation}\label{eq:early-two-parameter-v24}
 \max\left\{M^{-1/3},\ \rho^{1/2}M^{-1/4},\
            (\rho^2\tau)^{2/9}M^{-2/9}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The peak acquired dimension is nine off the three collision lines,
eight on any of these lines away from the origin, and six at their
intersection. At the decisive checkpoint $(n,m)=(3,2)$, the nine
positive formal sums form six separated groups. The three internal
gaps are $|u|$, $|v-u|$ and $|v-2u|$. The largest two are comparable,
which makes the putative seven-dimensional branch redundant. Thus the
absence of that branch is a feature of the collision geometry, not
an omission from the maximum.

For $u=\theta$ and $v=\theta+\theta^k$, with any integer $k\ge2$
and sufficiently small $\theta>0$, one has $\rho\asymp\theta$
and $\tau\asymp\theta^k$. The two crossover budget orders are
\[
 M\asymp\theta^{-6},\qquad
 M\asymp\theta^{-(8k-2)}.
\]
The corresponding regret orders are $\theta^2$ and $\theta^{2k}$.
These are comparison orders; their multiplicative constants are not
asserted to be exact transition thresholds. Fixed-calibration rank
alone does not determine either crossover.

\subsection{Acquisition, global geometry, and causality}
\label{sec:contribution-v23}\label{sec:proof-route-v24}
The profile is truncated by what the past acquires, not by an
observation-side affine hull. For example, $A=\{0,1,3\}$ and
$(n,m)=(1,2)$ give five nonconstant future exponents but only two
acquired dimensions. The eventual squared-error order is $M^{-1}$,
not $M^{-2/5}$. The acquired dimension is calculated from commands:
at a binomial product, the product tangent has dimension $n(r-1)+1$;
strict mixed-moment positivity gives the required pairing rank; and
evidence normalization removes exactly one direction.

The collision-uniform assertion needs more than this rank calculation.
A finite Leja ordering supplies decreasing Newton scales whose initial
products are comparable to the maximal determinant volumes. Every
initial node multiset gives a complete Hermite family. Pairing these
families with the same binomial product tangent shows that every
relevant prefix is acquired. The inverse chart includes complementary
command coordinates. Integrating them, with the probability of the
all-failure word retained, supplies uniform positive mass under the
actual acquisition law. This is Lemma~\ref{lem:newton-attainment}.

For the upper bound, each fixed-report-word posterior image is rational
in its command variables, with positive evidence denominator.
Normalizing the individual factors bounds its dimension by $n(r-1)$.
A bounded-format covering estimate then controls the entire image in
the Newton-scaled rectangle. It does not replace the image by a selected
local patch. The lower bound uses the acquired mass of each initial
flag and applies to arbitrary decoder centers. These two arguments
prove Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-checkpoint}.

The recursion uses raw remaining moments, rather than divided
differences. Between two reachable states the Bayes denominator stays
positive on their posterior-mixture segment. The resulting update bound
contains no inverse collision gap. Updating a reachable representative
and re-quantizing at the next stage produces one filter; its error
recurrence retains every preceding quantization error. The converse is
applied to each fixed filter at all checkpoints under marginals of one
exploration law before taking the infimum. This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-streaming} and completes the main theorem.

\subsection{Classical inputs and related approximation problems}
Newton interpolation and complete Hermite data are classical
\cite{deBoor}; finite Leja orderings give a convenient uniform
triangular comparison \cite{Leja,BosEtAl}. We give the required
zero-pivot and acquisition arguments in
Section~\ref{sec:collision-geometry}. The global covering step uses
classical real semialgebraic component and variation estimates
\cite{YomdinComte,ZhangKileel,ComteHalupczok}. These inputs concern
interpolation or the geometry of sets; they do not by themselves
supply the acquired measure in an experiment.

Quantization methods for nonlinear filtering, including error
propagation, are developed by Pag\`es--Pham \cite{PagesPham2005} and
Pag\`es--Sagna \cite{PagesSagna2018}. The resource in the present
problem is the number of complete retained prediction labels. A
finite grid for a signal with an unrestricted vector of filtering
weights is a different resource. Our conclusion includes a converse
over all filters with the stated label budget as well as a
construction. The companion discussion in
Section~\ref{sec:filter-quantization-comparison} gives the
object-by-object comparison; it does not assert that results for
these different models subsume one another.

\subsection{Organization and companion developments}
Sections~\ref{sec:experiment} and \ref{sec:rank} identify the exact
information and prove its acquisition rank. Section~\ref{sec:analytic-inputs}
gives the analytic estimates. Sections~\ref{sec:collision-geometry}
and \ref{sec:direct-classification} prove the collision-uniform
classification, and Section~\ref{sec:collision-phases} gives its
bit and collision-path consequences. This proof does not use the
abstract transfer theorem or the algebra, exact-kernel, uncertainty,
mechanical, or effective-construction developments.

Those developments, with their complete proofs, are organized in the
accompanying volume \emph{Attainable information in positive experiments:
complete companion developments}. Its sections have prefix C;
references from that volume to this article have prefix M. In
particular, its bounded-observation-algebra theorem is uniform over
all priors and support losses, but has immediate acquisition saturation.
It is a separate classification, not an additional hypothesis or a
claim of all-prior uniformity for the monomial theorem. The present
constants are uniform in calibration and memory at the stated fixed
prior and horizon; neither an unrestricted-detector nor an
unbounded-horizon theorem is being inferred from them.
