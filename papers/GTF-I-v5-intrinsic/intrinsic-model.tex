\section{An intrinsic law on Gibbs repellers}\label{sec:v5-model}

Let $b\ge2$, let $0<\gamma\le1$, and let $h_1,\ldots,h_b$ be
increasing $C^{1+\gamma}$ maps of $[0,1]$ into itself. We assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-branches}
 0<r_-\le h_i'(x)\le r_+<1,
 \qquad \dist(h_i([0,1]),h_j([0,1]))\ge g>0\quad(i\ne j).
\end{equation}
Write $\Sigma=\{1,\ldots,b\}^{\N_0}$, let $S$ denote the left shift,
and put
\[
 \pi(\omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{\omega_0}\cdots h_{\omega_{n-1}}(0),
 \qquad K=\pi(\Sigma),\qquad T=\pi S\pi^{-1}.
\]
Strong separation makes $\pi$ a homeomorphism onto $K$. In particular,
$T$ is a well-defined continuous expanding map on the repeller. Our
readout is the ambient coordinate $f(x)=x$.

For a word $w=w_0\cdots w_{n-1}$, set
\[
 h_w=h_{w_0}\circ\cdots\circ h_{w_{n-1}},\quad
 r_w=\sup_{[0,1]}h_w',\quad K_w=h_w(K),\quad r_\varnothing=1.
\]
The notation $w_{k:}$ means the suffix $w_k\cdots w_{n-1}$.
Choose a H\"older potential $\varphi$ on $\Sigma$, normalized by
$P(\varphi)=0$, and let $\mu$ be its shift-invariant Gibbs probability.
We use $\mu$ also for its image on $K$, and write $\mu[w]=\mu(K_w)$.
Here $P$ is topological pressure on the full shift. Thus, for a constant
$C_G\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-gibbs}
 C_G^{-1}e^{S_n\varphi(\omega)}\le\mu[w]
 \le C_Ge^{S_n\varphi(\omega)}\quad(\omega\in[w]),
 \qquad S_nu=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u\circ S^j.
\end{equation}
The derivative potential is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-psi}
 \psi(\omega)=\log h_{\omega_0}'(\pi(S\omega)).
\end{equation}
It is H\"older and is bounded between $\log r_-$ and $\log r_+$.

Fix $0<q<1$. At time zero and at each subsequent acquisition a fresh
$\mu$-distributed state is observed exactly. At every other time the
hidden state is advanced by $T$. The acquisition indicators after time
zero are independent Bernoulli variables with probability $1-q$ and
are independent of all fresh states. After the update, the decoder
receives only the persistent state, its absolute time, and independent
coding randomness. In particular, it does not receive the current
acquisition indicator as a separate uncharged input. An $M$-state
observer has one persistent register with at most $M$ values. Its
Borel update and decoding kernels may be randomized and time dependent;
no other data-dependent quantity survives a checkpoint. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-risk}
 R_M=\inf_{\mathcal A}\limsup_{H\to\infty}\frac1H
       \sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\E|X_t-\widehat X_t^{\mathcal A}|^2.
\end{equation}
The infimum is over this entire observer class. Its deterministic
stationary subclass will suffice for the upper bounds. Program
constants and transient computation are not charged to $M$.

The weighted orbit and its squared quantization error are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-orbit}
 Z_q(x)=(\sqrt{1-q}\,q^{k/2}T^kx)_{k\ge0},\qquad
 e_M^2(Z_q)=\inf_{c_1,\ldots,c_M\in\ell^2}
      \int\min_j\|Z_q(x)-c_j\|^2\,d\mu(x).
\end{equation}
The centres in this definition need not lie on the orbit image.

\subsection{A finite cylinder algorithm}
For every finite word define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-cost}
 V_q(w)=(1-q)\sum_{k=0}^{|w|-1}q^k r_{w_{k:}}^2+q^{|w|},
 \qquad a_q(w)=\mu[w]V_q(w).
\end{equation}
Both quantities at the empty word are one. They require only finite
cylinder masses and derivatives of finite compositions.
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-blockchoice}
 \rho_q=q+(1-q)r_+^2<1.
\end{equation}
View all finite words as the vertices of a full $b$-ary tree.
Starting from the empty word, repeatedly replace a leaf
of maximal $a_q$ by its $b$ children. Resolve ties in lexicographic
order. For $N\ge1$, let $\mathcal P_N$ be the last leaf partition in
this sequence having at most $N$ leaves, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-greedy}
 G_N(q)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal P_N}a_q(w).
\end{equation}
This convention also defines $G_N=1$ for $N<b$. No optimization over
Hilbert-space centres or over observer policies enters the algorithm.

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic profile and finite-state realization]
\label{thm:v5-intrinsic}
Under \eqref{eq:v5-branches}--\eqref{eq:v5-gibbs}, for every $0<q<1$
there are constants $0<c\le C<\infty$ such that, for every integer
$M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-equivalence}
 cG_M(q)\le e_M^2(Z_q)\le R_M\le CG_M(q).
\end{equation}
The lower bound holds also with the liminf time-average loss of any
individual randomized, time-dependent observer. The upper bound is
attained to the displayed factor by a deterministic stationary
observer which, at each acquisition, selects a leaf of a greedy
partition and thereafter deletes the first symbol of its stored word.
For every $N$, the full greedy tree is suffix closed and the
constructed observer has exactly $(b|\mathcal P_N|-1)/(b-1)$
designated states and average loss at most $G_N(q)$. In particular,
it uses at most $(bN-1)/(b-1)\le2N-1$ states.

The same order $G_M(q)$ holds when the objective in \eqref{eq:v5-risk}
is replaced by $\inf_{\mathcal A}\sup_{t\ge0}
\E|X_t-\widehat X_t^{\mathcal A}|^2$.
Constants may depend on the branch, distortion, Gibbs and separation
data and on $q$. They can be chosen uniformly on compact
$q$-intervals in $(0,1)$ when those data are uniformly controlled.
\end{theorem}

There are two separate assertions here. The cylinder algorithm
computes the relevant distortion profile; its suffix closure computes
an implementable observer. The number of suffix states is proportional
to the number of leaves, not to the number of leaves times their
maximum depth. This is the point at which a geometric code becomes a
causal code without a growing multiplicative overhead.

\subsection{The pressure law}
Let $s_g$ and $s_t(q)$ be the unique numbers in $(0,1)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-roots}
 P\bigl(s_g\varphi+2s_g\psi\bigr)=0,
 \qquad P\bigl(s_t(q)\varphi\bigr)+s_t(q)\log q=0,
 \qquad s_*(q)=\max\{s_g,s_t(q)\}.
\end{equation}
Existence and uniqueness are part of the next theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Pressure determines the causal exponent]
\label{thm:v5-pressure}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-intrinsic},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-exponent}
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{-\log R_M}{\log M}
 =\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{-\log e_M^2(Z_q)}{\log M}
 =\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{-\log G_M(q)}{\log M}
 =\frac{1-s_*(q)}{s_*(q)}.
\end{equation}
More precisely, for $0<s<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-mixedpressure}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\sum_{|w|=n}a_q(w)^s
 =\max\{P(s\varphi+2s\psi),\ P(s\varphi)+s\log q\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the transition occurs at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-qcritical}
 q_c=\exp\{-P(s_g\varphi)/s_g\}\in(0,1).
\end{equation}
For $q\le q_c$ the exponent is $(1-s_g)/s_g$; for $q>q_c$ it is
$(1-s_t(q))/s_t(q)$. At the transition, the finite-budget profile is
still given, up to constants, by \eqref{eq:v5-greedy} and
\eqref{eq:v5-equivalence}.
\end{theorem}

Formula~\eqref{eq:v5-mixedpressure} is a maximum of two pressures,
not pressure evaluated at the pointwise maximum of two potentials.
The derivative cocycle enters the first term; survival across
unobserved iterates enters the second. The theorem computes the
quantization exponent from these data rather than postulating its
existence. It does not identify a universal logarithmic correction or
an optimal leading constant at $q_c$ for all Gibbs repellers. The
finite cylinder profile retains such corrections without discarding
them in the exponent limit.
