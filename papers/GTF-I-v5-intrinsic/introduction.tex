\section{Introduction}\label{sec:v5-intro}
A finite representation of a present observation need not be a finite
representation of the predictions that observation will support.
Expansion separates initially close states, and an interval without
new data forces an observer to retain information about several
future readouts. There are consequently two questions. One asks for
the geometry of the weighted future orbit. The other asks whether a
code for that geometry admits a closed update on a finite persistent
state space. This paper gives matching answers on separated Gibbs
repellers and extends the resulting prediction laws to acquisition
kernels that retain hidden memory.

\subsection{The principal results}
Let $T$ be the expanding map defined by finitely many separated
$C^{1+\gamma}$ inverse branches, let $\mu$ be a Gibbs probability,
and suppose an acquisition is followed by $k$ unobserved iterates
with probability proportional to $q^k$. Write $\varphi$ for the
normalized Gibbs potential and $\psi$ for the logarithmic derivative
of an inverse branch. The principal results identify the optimal
$M$-state squared prediction risk $R_M$ with a finite cylinder
profile $G_M(q)$:
\[
 R_M\asymp e_M^2(Z_q)\asymp G_M(q),\qquad
 Z_q(x)=(\sqrt{1-q}\,q^{k/2}T^kx)_{k\ge0}.
\]
Here $G_M$ is obtained by successively splitting a cylinder of
largest mass-weighted orbit diameter. It is a finite construction
from cylinder probabilities and branch derivatives, not a restatement
of an optimization over orbit codebooks.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-intrinsic} supplies this equivalence with the
same state budget, up to constants. Its essential realization lemma
is that the entire greedy tree is suffix closed: a full $b$-ary
tree with $N$ leaves needs exactly $(bN-1)/(b-1)$ designated
states. The proof uses stationarity of the Gibbs law to compare the
energy of a word with the energy of any suffix. This is different
from simply attaching an age register to each codeword, which in
general costs the number of codewords times the truncation depth.
A disjoint-tube estimate on the orbit image supplies the matching
lower bound for arbitrary Hilbert-space centres and hence for all
randomized, time-dependent causal observers.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-pressure} computes the exponent of this profile.
If $P$ denotes full-shift pressure, let
\[
 P(s_g\varphi+2s_g\psi)=0,
 \qquad P(s_t\varphi)+s_t\log q=0.
\]
Then
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{-\log R_M}{\log M}
 =\frac{1-\max\{s_g,s_t\}}{\max\{s_g,s_t\}}.
\]
The two roots distinguish spatial resolution from survival through
unobserved expansion. Their pressure balance determines the
transition, including nonconstant derivative cocycles and nonuniform
Markov branch laws. A variational formula expresses them through
entropy, information and expansion rates. Positive-matrix formulas
and finite pressure enclosures make the invariant explicit in
specialized and general H\"older data, respectively.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-dependent} addresses dependence across acquisitions.
The state transition at an acquisition may retain an arbitrary
component of the preceding hidden state, subject to a common
invariant law and a uniform minorization. A lower bound is obtained
by conditional domination, not by declaring the acquisition to be a
regeneration. A resetting observer has exactly the same expected
loss as in the independent reference experiment. These two facts
transfer the intrinsic pressure law and the noisy expanding-torus
law to genuinely dependent experiments. In the latter case old
observations can affect the current posterior; no identification of
that posterior with a latest-observation posterior is needed.

\subsection{Resource convention}
The constrained resource is the cardinality of one persistent
register. The program, fixed real constants, absolute clock and
transient workspace are separate resources. Every data-dependent
quantity used after a checkpoint must be encoded in the register.
In particular, an acquisition indicator is available to the update
kernel but not separately to the post-update decoder. Changing this
interface can change exact zero-error cardinalities, as
Example~\ref{ex:v4-gap} demonstrates. All principal lower bounds
allow stochastic kernels and arbitrary time dependence. Our upper
constructions are stationary finite-state machines. We prove their
order optimality, not the exact optimality of stationary policies
for every member of the unrestricted class.

\subsection{Relation to the literature}
Classical quantization, causal coding and dynamical information
theory provide different pieces of the problem. They should not be
identified by a shared use of the word ``memory.''

Linder and Y\"uksel \cite{LinderYuksel2014} formulate zero-delay
coding of vector Markov sources by controlled quantizers; their
encoder and decoder can use transmitted histories or posterior
states. Wood, Linder and Y\"uksel \cite[Theorem~3]{WoodLinderYuksel2017}
prove deterministic stationary policy optimality for finite
irreducible aperiodic Markov sources. A stationary policy on a
belief space is not, by itself, a machine with a fixed finite number
of persistent values. Here acquisitions are intermittent and all
retained observation-dependent information is charged to that finite
number. This explains why a separate suffix-cardinality argument is
necessary.

Greedy prefix-tree construction has classical antecedents in
Tunstall's variable-to-fixed coding \cite{Tunstall1967}. The present
priority is the mass-weighted \emph{orbit distortion}, not merely
word probability. Neither greedy tree construction nor the elementary
vertex count is claimed as a new coding principle; the issue is to
prove that this particular distortion tree is shift closed and
matches the unrestricted causal risk.

Hilbert-space functional quantization is also classical. For example,
Luschgy and Pag\`es \cite{LuschgyPages2004} derive sharp Gaussian
functional-quantization asymptotics from covariance eigenvalue
decay. We use finite-centre Hilbert quantization as a converse, but
must additionally compute a non-Gaussian dynamical orbit geometry
and realize its codes causally. Pressure formulas for spatial
quantization on cookie-cutter sets already occur in
Roychowdhury \cite{Roychowdhury2014}. The new calculation concerns
the \emph{weighted future orbit}: its partition exponent is the
maximum of a spatial pressure and a survival pressure, and its
adaptive codes have linear-sized suffix closure. The spatial
pressure alone is not presented as a new principle.

Finite-rate control \cite{NairEvans2000,TatikondaMitter2004,MatveevSavkin2009}
constrains communication over time, whereas dynamical rate distortion
\cite{LindenstraussTsukamoto2018} constrains information in orbit
representations. Those resource models are different from the
persistent-cardinality model here. The following comparison records
the particular distinctions used in the proofs.

\subsection{Organization and dependencies}
Sections~\ref{sec:v5-model}--\ref{sec:v5-pressure} state and prove
the intrinsic profile and pressure theorem. Section~\ref{sec:v5-dependent}
proves the dependent-acquisition comparison. The posterior-orbit
and noisy expanding results needed in these arguments are proved
in full in Sections~\ref{sec:v4-orbits}--\ref{sec:v4-expanding}.
Those sections also retain the exact uniform critical crossover for
the integer expanding experiment.
