\begin{abstract}
We determine the finite-state prediction complexity of separated
$C^{1+\gamma}$ expanding repellers carrying H\"older Gibbs measures.
The optimal causal risk, allowing all randomized time-dependent
observers, is comparable to a finite cylinder profile and to the
quantization error of the weighted future orbit. Adaptive cylinder
codes have suffix-closed full trees, with exactly $(bN-1)/(b-1)$
states for $N$ leaves, yielding a stationary realization without a
growing age factor. The squared-error exponent is computed by the larger root
of two pressure equations, separating spatial distortion from
survival through unobserved expansion. The result applies to
nonconstant derivative cocycles and nonuniform Markov branch laws.
A conditional minorization argument extends the risk laws to
acquisition kernels that retain hidden state across observations;
in particular it combines noisy acquisition with dependent
expanding dynamics. Complete proofs of the general posterior-orbit
and noisy expanding comparison theorems are included.
\end{abstract}
