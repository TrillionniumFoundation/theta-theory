\subsection{Dimension-free full-support feasibility}
\label{sec:kernel-feasibility}
The existence of a full-support annihilating measure is a statement
about a finite-dimensional space of functions. It has no hypothesis
comparing the dimensions of two pairing spaces.

\begin{lemma}[Dimension-free feasibility]
\label{lem:dimension-free-feasibility}
Let $X$ be a nonempty compact metric space and let $W\subset C(X;\R)$
be finite dimensional. The following are equivalent:
\[
 W\cap C(X;\R)_+=\{0\};\qquad
 \exists\mu,\quad\operatorname{supp}\mu=X,\quad\mu W=0.
\]
If $G$ is the evaluation vector of a basis of $W$, these conditions
are also equivalent to $0\in\operatorname{ri}\operatorname{conv}G(X)$.
For every prescribed full-support probability $\mu_0$, a witness can
be chosen as $\delta\mu_0+(1-\delta)\nu$, with $0<\delta<1$ and
$\nu$ supported on at most $1+\dim\operatorname{aff}\operatorname{conv}G(X)$
points. When $W=0$, $G$ maps into $\R^0$, its moment body is $\{0\}$,
and the assertion uses these conventions without an exception.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A nonzero nonnegative continuous function has strictly positive integral
under every full-support probability. This proves necessity. Such
probabilities exist: put positive summable weights on a countable dense
subset of $X$.

Set $K=\operatorname{conv}G(X)$; it is compact in finite dimension.
If $0\notin K$, strict separation from the compact convex set gives a
linear functional positive on $K$. If $0\in K\setminus\operatorname{ri}K$,
then $\operatorname{aff}K$ is a linear space and a supporting linear
functional in that space is nonnegative on $K$ and positive somewhere.
Extend it to the ambient space. In either case its composition with $G$
is a nonzero nonnegative member of $W$, a contradiction. Hence
$0\in\operatorname{ri}K$.

Fix $\mu_0$ of full support and put $b_0=\mu_0G$. For sufficiently
small $\delta>0$, the point $-\delta b_0/(1-\delta)$ lies in $K$:
it is in $\operatorname{aff}K$, and $0$ has a neighborhood there
contained in $K$. The finite-dimensional convex-hull theorem represents
this point by the mean of an atomic probability $\nu$ with at most
$1+\dim\operatorname{aff}K$ atoms. The displayed mixture has full
support and mean zero, proving sufficiency and the witness assertion.
For $W=0$ every probability annihilates $W$; any point mass for $\nu$
and any $0<\delta<1$ give the claimed mixture. This also proves the
zero-dimensional convention directly.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Attainment under every full-support prior}
\label{sec:v18-universal}
The rank formula uses the specified prior. We now characterize when a
square product pairing has full normalized rank for \emph{every}
full-support prior. This replaces separate Chebyshev assumptions by an
exact sign condition on the paired evaluation determinants. The condition
is intrinsic to the two function spaces and does not require an ordering
of $X$.

\subsubsection{Positive histories and their augmented tangents}
Fix an interior command tuple $u_*$ for a positive history product
$P(u,\cdot)\in C(X)$, twice continuously differentiable in $u$. Write
$P=P(u_*,\cdot)>0$, and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v18-augmented-tangent}
 \widetilde T=\operatorname{span}\{P\}+\operatorname{im}DP(u_*).
\end{equation}
Let $p\ge1$ and $\Phi=\operatorname{span}\{1,\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_p\}$, where the
functions are real and continuous, and suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v18-square-pair}
 \dim\widetilde T=\dim\Phi=p+1=:d.
\end{equation}
This is the square pairing regime; no claim that every rectangular
pairing reduces to it is made. For a basis $v_0,\ldots,v_p$ of
$\widetilde T$, with $\phi_0=1$, define the continuous symmetric
function on $X^d$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v18-sign-kernel}
 \Delta_{v,\phi}(x_0,\ldots,x_p)
 =\det[v_i(x_k)]_{i,k=0}^p\,
  \det[\phi_j(x_k)]_{j,k=0}^p.
\end{equation}
It is symmetric because a permutation changes both determinants by the
same sign. Changing either basis multiplies it by a nonzero constant;
being of one weak sign and nonzero somewhere is therefore a property of
the pair $(\widetilde T,\Phi)$, not of its oriented bases.

\begin{theorem}[Universal square-pairing criterion]
\label{thm:v18-universal-pairing}
Under \eqref{eq:v18-square-pair}, the following assertions are equivalent.

\smallskip
\noindent\textup{(i)} For every full-support probability $\mu$ on $X$,
the actual command map
\[
 u\longmapsto\bigl(\mu(P(u)\phi_j)/\mu P(u)\bigr)_{j=1}^p
\]
has rank $p$ at $u_*$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textup{(ii)} The function $\Delta_{v,\phi}$ has one weak
sign on $X^d$ and is nonzero at at least one tuple.

\smallskip
Under these equivalent conditions, independent commands with a density
bounded below near $u_*$ give a positive subprobability minorization of
a $p$-dimensional prediction cube for every such prior. If the positive
history products, tests, interior tuples, and command derivatives vary
continuously in a compact family, the dimensions in
\eqref{eq:v18-square-pair} remain fixed, and \textup{(ii)} holds at
every parameter, the minorization is uniform over that family and all
probabilities $\mu\ge c\mu_0$, where $c>0$ and $\mu_0$ has full support.
A common positive lower bound for the likelihoods, a common command
neighborhood, and a common lower command-density bound are understood
in this uniform assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $\mu$ define $L_\mu Q=(\mu Q,\mu(Q\phi_1),\ldots,
\mu(Q\phi_p))^t$ and $Z=\mu P$. The normalized differential is the
map
\[
 Q\longmapsto Z^{-1}\left(L_\mu Q-
              \frac{L_\mu P}{Z}\,\mu Q\right)
\]
on actual product derivatives, with its constant coordinate omitted.
Adding the direction $P$ does not change its image, because that
direction maps to zero. On $L_\mu(\widetilde T)$ the kernel of the
projection in parentheses is exactly the span of $L_\mu P$, whose
constant coordinate is $Z>0$. Therefore the normalized command rank
is $\rank(L_\mu|\widetilde T)-1$. In the square case it equals $p$
if and only if
\[
 D(\mu):=\det[\mu(v_i\phi_j)]_{i,j=0}^p\ne0.
\]
This argument explains why adjoining the evidence direction in
\eqref{eq:v18-augmented-tangent} is legitimate even when $P$ is not an
actual command derivative.

Determinant expansion, Fubini, and symmetrization give the classical
integration identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v18-andreief}
 D(\mu)=\frac1{d!}\int_{X^d}\Delta_{v,\phi}(x_0,\ldots,x_p)
                     \prod_{k=0}^p\mu(dx_k).
\end{equation}
For completeness, expand both determinants on the right into their
permutation sums. Integrating a term gives a product of $d$ scalar
integrals. Reindexing one permutation relative to the other produces
the determinant on the left exactly $d!$ times. All functions are
bounded, so the expansions and integrations are legitimate. This is
Andr\'eief's identity, not a new integration formula; see
\cite{ForresterAndreief} for its source and attribution.

Suppose \textup{(ii)} holds and orient a basis so that $\Delta\ge0$.
Continuity and a strictly positive value give a product of nonempty
open sets on which $\Delta>0$. Every full-support probability gives
this product positive mass. Equation~\eqref{eq:v18-andreief} therefore
gives $D(\mu)>0$, proving \textup{(i)}.

Conversely, the full-support probabilities form a convex set. If
\textup{(i)} holds, continuity along every line segment of this set
shows that $D(\mu)$ has the same nonzero sign throughout it. Orient a
basis so that the sign is positive and fix one full-support $\mu_0$.
For an arbitrary tuple $(x_0,\ldots,x_p)$ put
$\nu=d^{-1}\sum_k\delta_{x_k}$. The probabilities
$(1-\epsilon)\nu+\epsilon\mu_0$ have full support for every
$0<\epsilon<1$. Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives $D(\nu)\ge0$.
Matrix multiplication at this atomic probability gives, also for
repeated points,
\[
 D(\nu)=d^{-d}\det[v_i(x_k)]\det[\phi_j(x_k)]
       =d^{-d}\Delta_{v,\phi}(x_0,\ldots,x_p).
\]
Hence $\Delta\ge0$ everywhere. Since $D(\mu_0)>0$ in
\eqref{eq:v18-andreief}, $\Delta$ is not identically zero. This proves
\textup{(ii)} and its necessity without assuming either evaluation
determinant separately has a fixed sign.

The minorization follows by augmenting the onto normalized derivative
with orthonormal kernel coordinates, applying the inverse-function
theorem, and integrating the entire kernel-coordinate cube, exactly as
in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:v18-image-mass}. The density of the
specified report word contains its evidence, bounded below by the
product of the likelihood lower bounds.

For uniformity, the set of probabilities dominating $c\mu_0$ is weakly
compact: it is a closed subset of the probabilities on compact $X$,
as is seen by testing the domination inequality against continuous
nonnegative functions. Every limiting probability still has full
support. The normalized command Jacobian is jointly continuous in the
parameter and this weakly varying probability; this follows from the
uniform-norm continuity of the finitely many integrands and the positive
evidence bound. It is onto everywhere by the equivalence just proved.
Compactness gives a positive lower bound for its least row singular
value. Uniform second derivative bounds and uniformly interior tuples
then give uniform inverse-chart radii, while the specified command and
evidence lower bounds give uniform minorization mass. No conclusion
about a later observation operator's small singular values is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Matched score and query spaces on arbitrary latent spaces]
\label{cor:v18-matched-spaces}
If $\widetilde T=P\Phi$ and $\dim\Phi=p+1$, then the normalized
history map has rank $p$ for every full-support prior. More generally,
the same conclusion holds whenever the paired evaluation determinants
have one weak sign and are nonzero somewhere, even if both individual
determinants change sign.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $v_i=P\phi_i$. Then
\[
 \Delta_{v,\phi}(x_0,\ldots,x_p)
   =\left(\prod_{k=0}^pP(x_k)\right)
     \det[\phi_i(x_k)]_{i,k=0}^p{}^2\ge0.
\]
The evaluation determinant is nonzero somewhere. Indeed, if the
vectors $(\phi_i(x))_{i=0}^p$ did not span $\R^{p+1}$ as $x$ varies,
a nonzero linear functional annihilating all of them would contradict
the independence of the functions. Selecting a spanning set of $p+1$
evaluation vectors gives the desired tuple. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-universal-pairing}. Its same equivalence proves
the more general assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Relation to positive products and to rank strata}
For the positive products of Section~\ref{sec:positive-history}, the
original normalized-product criterion remains valid. The present
criterion makes two further distinctions. It uses the actual augmented
tangent, so it does not require a physically available radial command.
It also characterizes, in the square regime, the full-support-prior
quantifier itself. Two separately strict Chebyshev systems with compatible
orientations make \eqref{eq:v18-sign-kernel} positive on distinct ordered
tuples and are a sufficient instance. They are not necessary: the
matched-space corollary works on compact spaces of any dimension.

For example, at $u=0$ in \eqref{eq:v18-affine-likelihood}, the augmented
tangent is $\operatorname{span}\{1,f_1,\ldots,f_b\}$. When the future
tests span that same space and these functions are independent, the
corollary proves maximal acquisition for every full-support prior.
In contrast, for $v=(1,x)$ and $\phi=(1,x^2)$ on $[-1,1]$, the paired
evaluation determinant is
\[
 (x_1-x_0)^2(x_0+x_1),
\]
which takes both signs. The normalized rank can consequently depend on
the full-support prior. Proposition~\ref{prop:v18-interior-example}
computes that dependence and its entire finite-label profile exactly.
These observations link the universal criterion, the algebraic moment
strata, and the covariance classification without substituting a local
pairing argument for either a global cover or a causal construction.


\subsection{Rectangular pairings and attainable rank}
\label{sec:v19-rectangular}
The square criterion has a rectangular counterpart, but the counterpart
is not a choice of one square subpairing. The appropriate object is
the cone of products of functions in the two spaces. We give both a
full-rank criterion and a formula for the smallest rank over all
full-support priors. The latter produces priors whose kernels contain the permitted
subspaces and realizes the minimum rank. Exact realization of a prescribed
subspace is treated in Section~\ref{sec:exact-kernels}.

Throughout this section, $X$ is a nonempty compact metric space and
$E,F\subset C(X;\R)$ have dimensions $d,k$, with $1\le k\le d$.
Choose bases $e_1,\ldots,e_d$ and $f_1,\ldots,f_k$, and set
\[
 e(x)=(e_1(x),\ldots,e_d(x))^t,\qquad
 H_\mu=(\mu(e_a f_i))_{a,i}\in\R^{d\times k}.
\]
The kernel is regarded as a subspace of $F$, independently of the chosen
bases. Relative interiors below are taken in the affine hull of the
indicated convex body.

\subsubsection{Full-support moments}
\begin{lemma}[The full-support moment image]
\label{lem:v19-moment-image}
For a continuous map $G:X\to\R^s$, put $K_G=\operatorname{conv}G(X)$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-ri-moments}
 \{\mu G:\mu\in\cP(X),\ \operatorname{supp}\mu=X\}
       =\operatorname{ri}K_G.
\end{equation}
More precisely, fix any full-support probability $\mu_0$. Each
$b\in\operatorname{ri}K_G$ is realized by
$\mu=\varepsilon\mu_0+(1-\varepsilon)\nu$, where $0<\varepsilon<1$
and $\nu$ has at most $1+\dim\operatorname{aff}K_G$ atoms.
The number $\varepsilon$ may depend on $b$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A compact metric space is separable. Assigning positive summable masses
to a countable dense set supplies a full-support probability, so the
class in \eqref{eq:v19-ri-moments} is nonempty. The convex hull $K_G$
is compact: by the finite-dimensional convex-hull theorem, its points
are convex combinations of at most $s+1$ points of $G(X)$.
If a full-support mean $b$ lay in the relative boundary of $K_G$, a
supporting affine functional on $\operatorname{aff}K_G$ would be
nonnegative on $K_G$, zero at $b$, and positive somewhere on $K_G$.
Its composition with $G$ is nonnegative continuous and positive at
some point of $X$. It has positive integral under every full-support
probability, contradicting its value zero at the mean. A zero-dimensional
$K_G$ has empty relative boundary and causes no exception.

Conversely, write $b_0=\mu_0G$. Since $b$ is a relative interior point,
for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ the point
\[
 c_\varepsilon=\frac{b-\varepsilon b_0}{1-\varepsilon}
             =b+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}(b-b_0)
\]
belongs to $K_G$. The convex-hull theorem in its affine hull represents
$c_\varepsilon$ by a probability $\nu$ with the stated number of atoms.
The displayed mixture has full support and mean $b$.
This proves equality of attainable moment sets, not uniqueness or
full support of every representation of an interior moment.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{A product-cone criterion and all rank defects}
\begin{theorem}[Universal rectangular pairing]
\label{thm:v19-rectangular}
The following three conditions are equivalent:

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(i)}
$\rank H_\mu=k$ for every full-support probability $\mu$ on $X$.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(ii)}
For each $0\ne f\in F$ there is $g\in E$ such that $fg\ge0$ on
$X$ and $fg$ is not identically zero.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(iii)}
For each $0\ne f\in F$,
$0\notin\operatorname{ri}\operatorname{conv}\{f(x)e(x):x\in X\}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A function $f\in F$ is in the kernel precisely when
$\mu(f e)=0$. Lemma~\ref{lem:v19-moment-image}, applied to $G=f e$,
therefore identifies the failure of (i) with the failure of (iii).
If (ii) holds and a full-support $\mu$ satisfies $H_\mu f=0$, then
$\mu(fg)=0$ for every $g\in E$. The particular product in (ii) has
strictly positive integral, a contradiction. Thus (ii) implies (i).

For completeness, let $K$ be a nonempty compact convex subset of a
Euclidean space. If $0\notin\operatorname{ri}K$, there is a linear
functional $\lambda$ nonnegative on $K$ and positive somewhere on
$K$. When $0\notin K$, separate the compact set $K$ strictly from
zero, for example by the nearest point of $K$ to zero. When
$0\in K\setminus\operatorname{ri}K$, its affine hull is a linear
space; a supporting hyperplane in that space has the required property,
and its functional extends to the ambient space. Apply this observation
to $K=\operatorname{conv}\{f(x)e(x)\}$. Write
$\lambda(z)=\sum_a c_a z_a$ and set $g=\sum_a c_a e_a$. Then
$fg\ge0$ everywhere and is positive somewhere. This proves (iii)
implies (ii).
\end{proof}

For finite $X$, the kernel formulation is a positive-diagonal
sign-vector alternative. This algebraic mechanism is already present
in M\"uller--Feliu--Regensburger--Conradi--Shiu--Dickenstein
\cite[Lemma 2.1]{MullerEtAl2016}. The compact-space formulation retains
continuity, actual full-support measures, and the representation of
those measures needed below. The finite square specialization is the
strong-compatibility criterion of Banaji--Pantea
\cite[Definition 2.24 and Lemma 2.36]{BanajiPantea2016}; see also
Section~\ref{sec:v19-comparison}. Neither finite criterion is claimed
as a new algebraic input.

For a subspace $U\subset F$ of dimension $j$, choose a basis
$u_1,\ldots,u_j$ and define
\[
 G_U(x)=(u_i(x)e_a(x))_{i,a}\in\R^{jd},\qquad
 K_U=\operatorname{conv}G_U(X).
\]
The condition $0\in\operatorname{ri}K_U$ is unchanged by any basis
change. For $U=\{0\}$, use the one-point body in $\R^0$.

\begin{theorem}[Rank alternatives with full-support witnesses]
\label{thm:v19-rank-alternative}
There is a full-support probability for which $U\subset\ker H_\mu$
if and only if $0\in\operatorname{ri}K_U$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-minimum-rank}
 \min_{\operatorname{supp}\mu=X}\rank H_\mu
 = k-\max\{\dim U:U\subset F,\ 0\in\operatorname{ri}K_U\}.
\end{equation}
Whenever the condition holds, a witnessing prior can be chosen in the
form $\varepsilon\mu_0+(1-\varepsilon)\nu$ for any prescribed
full-support $\mu_0$, with $\nu$ supported on at most $jd+1$ points.
A subspace of maximal permitted dimension gives a prior attaining the
minimum rank in \eqref{eq:v19-minimum-rank}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The inclusion $U\subset\ker H_\mu$ consists exactly of the $jd$
equations $\mu G_U=0$. The equivalence and the finite-atom mixture
follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:v19-moment-image}. Every actual kernel is
one of the permitted subspaces. Conversely, a prior realizing a
permitted subspace has nullity at least its dimension. The possible
dimensions form a nonempty subset of the finite set $\{0,\ldots,k\}$,
so their maximum is attained by a subspace. A witnessing prior for
such a subspace cannot have larger nullity, since its whole kernel
would then be a permitted subspace of larger dimension. Rank--nullity
gives \eqref{eq:v19-minimum-rank} and actual attainment of its left
side. There is no assertion of a common positive mixture weight for
all the witnesses.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{A quantitative margin and posterior normalization}
Fix the Euclidean coefficient norm on $F$. For $f\in F$ write
$a_f=\mu_0(f e)$ and $K_f=\operatorname{conv}\{f(x)e(x):x\in X\}$.

\begin{proposition}[The dominated-prior margin]
\label{prop:v19-dominated-margin}
For $0<c\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-margin}
 \inf_{\mu\ge c\mu_0}s_{\min}(H_\mu)
 =\min_{\|f\|=1}\operatorname{dist}
                 \bigl(0,c a_f+(1-c)K_f\bigr).
\end{equation}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-rectangular}, this number is positive.
Here $s_{\min}$ is the least of the $k$ singular values. The margin
also remains positive over a compact continuous family of pairs of
bases satisfying that theorem, with their dimensions fixed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $c<1$, every admitted measure has the unique form
$\mu=c\mu_0+(1-c)\nu$ with $\nu\in\cP(X)$. For a fixed unit $f$,
the vectors $H_\mu f$ therefore fill $c a_f+(1-c)K_f$.
Taking the infimum over $f$ and $\nu$ in either order gives
\eqref{eq:v19-margin}. For $c=1$ only $\mu_0$ occurs and the same
formula is immediate. The unit sphere and $\cP(X)$ are compact,
the latter in the weak topology, and the integrands are continuous.
Thus the joint minimum is attained. Every admitted measure has full
support, so a zero minimum would contradict full column rank.
The same compactness argument applies with an additional compact
parameter and bases continuous in the uniform norm. It requires the
criterion at every parameter. It makes no assertion of uniformity over
all full-support priors without domination.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Rectangular positive-history attainment]
\label{cor:v19-rectangular-history}
Let $P(u,x)$ be a positive history product, twice continuously
differentiable in an interior command neighborhood of $u_*$, and let
$\Phi=\operatorname{span}\{1,\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_{d_\Phi-1}\}$
be its finite future test space. Put
\[
 T=\operatorname{span}\{P(u_*,\cdot)\}+\operatorname{im}DP(u_*),
 \qquad k=\min(\dim T,\dim\Phi).
\]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-rectangular} with $F$ the smaller of
$T,\Phi$ and $E$ the other. Its equivalent conditions hold if and only
if, for every full-support prior, the normalized prediction map has
rank $k-1$ at $u_*$. For $k>1$, independent commands having a density
bounded below near $u_*$ then give an unconditional subprobability
minorization on a $(k-1)$-dimensional projection of predictions.

The minorization constants are uniform on compact continuous families
satisfying these conditions, for priors $\mu\ge c\mu_0$, provided the
spaces have fixed dimensions and continuous bases, the products and
tests have uniform bounds through two command derivatives, and there
are common positive likelihood, interior-neighborhood, and command-density
bounds. The output projection may be selected locally in the family.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $L:T\to\Phi^*$ be $Lv=(\mu(v\phi_j))_{j=0}^{d_\Phi-1}$,
where $\phi_0=1$. Write $Y=LP=(Z,Zp)^t$ with $Z=\mu P>0$.
The derivative of normalization at $Y$ sends
$(z,w)$ to $(w-pz)/Z$. Its kernel is precisely $\R Y$.
The image of $L$ is the sum of $\R Y$ and $L(\operatorname{im}DP)$.
Since $Y\ne0$ belongs to that image, normalization of the actual
command derivative has rank $\rank L-1$. Transposing the pairing
when necessary does not change its rank. Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-rectangular}
now proves the equivalence, without assuming that the radial direction
$P$ is a physical command derivative.

Write $r=k-1>0$, choose an $r$-coordinate output projection $z$ with
onto differential at $u_*$, and let $K$ be an orthonormal coordinate
map on its differential kernel. The augmented map
$u\mapsto(z(u),K(u-u_*))$ has invertible derivative. The inverse
function theorem gives a product box in these coordinates. Integrating
the command density over the entire kernel-coordinate box, and
multiplying by the probability of the specified report word, gives a
positive density on the output box. That word probability is
$\mu P(u)$, bounded below by the assumed likelihood bound. Thus the
minorization is a subprobability of the original experiment, not a
conditional-on-word law and not mass placed on a section of zero
ambient measure. This is the construction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:product-criterion}, applied after augmentation.

In a compact dominated family, the least nonzero singular value of
the normalized command derivative is positive uniformly: its rank is
always $r$, its entries are continuous, and the parameter--measure
space is compact. Equivalently one can first use
Proposition~\ref{prop:v19-dominated-margin} on the pairing and then
normalization and the actual tangent map. A maximal output minor and
an interior command box may be selected near each parameter--measure
pair. A finite subcover, the uniform second-derivative bounds, and the
common lower density and likelihood bounds give uniform inverse
radii and density constants. The case $k=1$ asserts rank zero only;
it asserts no positive-dimensional mass.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{A genuinely rectangular physical acquisition}
The next construction explains why a fixed evidence-preserving square
reduction would discard admissible experiments.

\begin{proposition}[A five-point rectangular experiment]
\label{prop:v19-pentagon}
Let $X=\{z_0,\ldots,z_4\}\subset\R^2$ with
\[
 z_0=(0,0),\quad z_1=(2,0),\quad z_2=(3,1),\quad
 z_3=(1,3),\quad z_4=(-1,1),\qquad h(z_i)=i.
\]
The spaces $E=\operatorname{span}\{1,x,y\}$ and
$F=\operatorname{span}\{1,h\}$ satisfy the universal rectangular
criterion. No two-dimensional subspace of $E$ containing $1$ has a
universal nonsingular square pairing with $F$.

These spaces arise from the positive acquisition
\[
 L_\pm(u,z)=\tfrac12(1\pm u^t f(z)),\qquad
 f(z)=(x/8,y/8)^t,\qquad u\in[-1,1]^2,
\]
followed by one physical binary query of success probability
$g(z)=1/4+h(z)/8$. At the interior command $u=0$, every full-support
prior gives normalized prediction rank one. The full one-step memory
profile is the affine profile with the nonzero row covariance
$\Cov_\mu(g,f)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The listed evaluations show that $E,F$ have dimensions three and two.
For each $j=0,1,2,3$, the following affine functions are negative on
$z_0,\ldots,z_j$ and positive on $z_{j+1},\ldots,z_4$:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|rrrrr}
 j&\text{function}&z_0&z_1&z_2&z_3&z_4\\\hline
 0&x+3y-1&-1&1&5&9&1\\
 1&2y-1&-1&-1&1&5&1\\
 2&-x+y-1&-1&-3&-3&1&1\\
 3&-2x-1&-1&-5&-7&-3&1
 \end{array}
\]
Every nonzero $a+bh$ either has one weak sign, in which case the
constant $1$ or $-1$ is an admissible multiplier, or changes sign once
along this list. In the latter case choose the indicated separator,
with its sign reversed when $b<0$. If the zero is at a listed vertex,
either adjacent separator gives a nonnegative product, positive at a
nonzero value of $a+bh$. This proves condition (ii) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-rectangular}.

Every two-dimensional subspace of $E$ containing $1$ is
$\operatorname{span}\{1,ax+by\}$ for $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$.
By the square criterion, its evaluations must be weakly monotone or
weakly antitone along the strictly increasing values of $h$.
For weak monotonicity the consecutive differences require
\[
 2a\ge0,\qquad a+b\ge0,\qquad -2a+2b\ge0,
 \qquad -2a-2b\ge0.
\]
They imply $a\ge0$, $b\ge a$, $b\le-a$, hence $a=b=0$.
Reversing all inequalities gives the same conclusion. The finite
square criterion used here is precisely the corresponding-minor
criterion attributed above.

Finally $|x|+|y|\le4$ on $X$, so $\sum_i|f_i|\le1/2$ and
$L_\pm\ge1/4$. The query values lie in $[1/4,3/4]$.
At $u=0$, the positive history product and its derivatives span
$\{1,x,y\}$, while the future tests span $\{1,h\}$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:v19-rectangular-history} proves the normalized
rank assertion. Equivalently the row covariance cannot vanish for
any positive probability vector. Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-affine-classification}
gives its full profile, and a fixed dominated family has uniform
positive mass constants. No uniform nonzero covariance lower bound
is asserted as arbitrary positive probabilities approach the boundary
of the probability simplex.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Exact kernels of positive moment pairings}
\label{sec:exact-kernels}
The annihilation of a subspace and the realization of that subspace as
an entire kernel are different questions. The distinction is governed
by multiplication of functions, before a prior is chosen. We describe
it by a linear quotient and a determinantal test, and then construct
full-support witnesses arbitrarily close to any admissible prior.

Let $X$ be a nonempty compact metric space, and let $E,F\subset C(X;\R)$
have dimensions $d,k$. No ordering of these dimensions is required in
this section. Measures $\mu$ below are Borel probabilities. For $U\subset F$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-product-spaces}
 \begin{gathered}
 W_U=\spanop\{eu:e\in E,\ u\in U\},\qquad
 V=\spanop(\{1\}\cup EF),\\
 \mathcal P_U=\{\mu:\operatorname{supp}\mu=X,\ \mu W_U=0\}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here $EF$ denotes the set of pointwise products, not a tensor product.
The condition $\mu W_U=0$ means that every member of $W_U$ has integral
zero. It is equivalent to $U\subset\ker H_\mu$, where
$H_\mu f=(\mu(e_a f))_{a=1}^d$ in a fixed basis of $E$.

Suppose for the moment that $\mathcal P_U$ is nonempty. Then
$1\notin W_U$. Choose $v_1,\ldots,v_s\in V$ so that
\[
 [1],[v_1],\ldots,[v_s]\quad\hbox{is a basis of }V/W_U,
 \qquad s=\dim V-\dim W_U-1.
\]
Choose representatives $\bar f_1,\ldots,\bar f_q$ of a basis of $F/U$,
where $q=k-\dim U$. There are unique coefficients with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-pencil}
 e_a\bar f_i\equiv c_{ai0}+\sum_{\nu=1}^s c_{ai\nu}v_\nu
       \pmod {W_U},\qquad
 \mathscr H_U(z)=C_0+\sum_{\nu=1}^s z_\nu C_\nu.
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathscr H_U$ is a $d$ by $q$ affine matrix pencil determined by
the multiplication table modulo $W_U$. Define its algebraic rank by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-generic-rank}
 r_U=\rank_{\R(z_1,\ldots,z_s)}\mathscr H_U.
\end{equation}
For $s=0$ this is the rank of the constant matrix. For $q=0$ it is zero.
A change of bases makes an invertible affine change of moment coordinates
and invertible row or column operations, so $r_U$ is intrinsic.

\begin{lemma}[Local coordinates on a constrained prior slice]
\label{lem:kernel-moment-chart}
If $\mu_*\in\mathcal P_U$, the set
\[
 \mathcal O_U=\{(\mu v_1,\ldots,\mu v_s):\mu\in\mathcal P_U\}
\]
is a nonempty open convex subset of $\R^s$. At every $\mu_*$ it admits
a local parametrization by bounded continuous density perturbations
that preserve all the equations $\mu W_U=0$. More precisely, there are
$h_1,\ldots,h_s\in C(X)$ and a positive definite matrix $B$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-tilt-chart}
 d\mu_t=(1+\textstyle\sum_\nu t_\nu h_\nu)d\mu_*,\qquad
 \mu_t v=\mu_*v+Bt,\qquad \mu_t W_U=0,
\end{equation}
and these are full-support probabilities whenever
$\|\sum_\nu t_\nu h_\nu\|_\infty<1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Full support makes the $L^2(\mu_*)$ inner product positive definite on
any finite-dimensional space of continuous functions: a nonzero
continuous function has nonzero magnitude on a nonempty open set.
Since $\mu_*W_U=0$, that space is orthogonal to the constants. Let
$\Pi_U$ be its $L^2(\mu_*)$ orthogonal projection and set
\[
 h_\nu=v_\nu-\mu_*v_\nu-\Pi_U v_\nu.
\]
These are continuous, have mean zero, and are orthogonal to $W_U$.
They are linearly independent, since a relation among them would put
the same linear combination of the $v_\nu$ in $\R1+W_U$.
Consequently $B_{\alpha\nu}=\mu_*(h_\alpha h_\nu)$ is positive
definite. Orthogonality gives
$\mu_*(v_\alpha h_\nu)=B_{\alpha\nu}$, proving all identities in
\eqref{eq:kernel-tilt-chart}. The strict uniform bound on the tilt
gives a strictly positive density, normalization follows from the
zero means, and full support is retained. Since $B$ is invertible,
the image contains a neighborhood of $\mu_*v$. Repeating this argument
at every point proves openness. Convex mixtures of full-support
probabilities prove convexity. When $s=0$, the moment space is the
one-point space $\R^0$ and the assertion uses the empty family of tilts.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact-kernel criterion and full-support realization]
\label{thm:exact-kernel}
A subspace $U\subset F$ is the kernel of $H_\mu$ for some full-support
probability if and only if the following two conditions hold:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-kernel-criterion}
 W_U\cap C(X;\R)_+=\{0\},\qquad r_U=k-\dim U.
\end{equation}
The first condition is equivalent to $\mathcal P_U\ne\varnothing$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:dimension-free-feasibility}, including $W_U=0$.
It permits the definition of the pencil in \eqref{eq:kernel-pencil}. The second condition says that at
least one maximal column minor of that pencil is a nonzero polynomial.
When $q=0$, the empty minor is one.

More generally, subject to the first condition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-slice-max-rank}
 \max_{\mu\in\mathcal P_U}\rank H_\mu=r_U.
\end{equation}
The priors attaining this maximum form a relatively weakly open dense
subset of $\mathcal P_U$. For every $\mu_*\in\mathcal P_U$ and
$0<\varepsilon<1$, such a prior can be chosen with continuous density
$1+h$ relative to $\mu_*$ and $\|h\|_\infty<\varepsilon$.
In the chart of Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel-moment-chart}, a tensor grid with
$r_U+1$ points per coordinate inside any sufficiently small box contains
a witness. This is a finite algebraic test of at most $(r_U+1)^s$
points, not an assertion that unspecified real moments are computable.

When \eqref{eq:exact-kernel-criterion} holds, for any prescribed
full-support probability $\mu_0$ an exact witness can also be chosen as
$\delta\mu_0+(1-\delta)\nu$, where $0<\delta<1$ and $\nu$ has at
most $\dim V$ atoms. No common positive value of $\delta$ is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:dimension-free-feasibility}, applied to $W=W_U$,
proves the equivalence of the first condition and
$\mathcal P_U\ne\varnothing$ without comparing $d$ and $k$.
This includes $W_U=0$. It also gives $1\notin W_U$:
a probability annihilating $W_U$ cannot annihilate $1$.

For $\mu\in\mathcal P_U$, the matrix induced by $H_\mu$ on $F/U$
is exactly $\mathscr H_U(\mu v)$. All minors of order $r_U+1$
vanish identically, and some minor of order $r_U$ is a nonzero
polynomial, unless $r_U=0$, when every induced matrix is zero.
A nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on a nonempty open set. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel-moment-chart} the nonzero minor therefore has a
nonzero value at an admissible full-support moment. This proves
\eqref{eq:kernel-slice-max-rank}. Rank--nullity now proves
\eqref{eq:exact-kernel-criterion}. Necessity follows from the same
matrix identity and the fact that there are only $q$ columns.

For the stronger local assertion fix $\mu_*$. In its chart, the chosen
minor becomes a nonzero polynomial in $t$, since $B$ is invertible.
Its degree is at most $r_U$. Choose a box so small that every density
perturbation in it has uniform norm less than $\varepsilon$. A
polynomial of degree at most $r_U$ in each variable that vanishes on a
product of $r_U+1$ distinct values per variable is identically zero:
apply the univariate root bound to the last variable and induct on the
number of variables. Hence the grid contains a nonzero value of the
minor. The perturbation retains full support and every annihilation
equation. Arbitrarily small boxes prove density, even by uniform density
perturbations. Maximal rank is relatively weakly open because the
entries are integrals of continuous functions and a nonzero minor stays
nonzero nearby. If $r_U=0$, all priors are witnesses and the grid may
consist of its center. The same convention handles $s=0$.

Finally, take an exact full-support witness already constructed and
record all its moments in a basis of $V$ beginning with $1$. The
nonconstant moment body has affine dimension $\dim V-1$: an affine
relation would be a linear dependence in that basis. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:v19-moment-image} to this vector of moments and the
prescribed $\mu_0$. Its finite-atom representation has at most $\dim V$
atoms and preserves every product in $EF$. The whole pairing matrix,
and therefore its exact kernel, is unchanged.
\end{proof}

The polynomial in this criterion is tested as an identity on the full
affine quotient, not by an unverified search on a constrained set of
priors. Positivity and the annihilation equations have already been
resolved by the first condition and the local chart. The argument uses
finite-dimensional separation, orthogonal projection, and elementary
determinantal geometry; it is not a new generic-matrix rank theorem.
Its content here is the exact compatibility with full support, prescribed
annihilation, and arbitrarily small positive prior perturbations.

\subsubsection{Forced directions and a positive block classification}
A useful preliminary test is the multiplication closure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-closure}
 \operatorname{cl}_E(U)=\{f\in F: ef\in W_U\text{ for every }e\in E\}.
\end{equation}
It records directions forced to vanish by the annihilation equations
alone, whereas the pencil in \eqref{eq:kernel-pencil} retains all
remaining determinantal restrictions.

\begin{proposition}[The forced kernel]
\label{prop:forced-kernel}
If $\mathcal P_U\ne\varnothing$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forced-kernel-intersection}
 \bigcap_{\mu\in\mathcal P_U}\ker H_\mu=\operatorname{cl}_E(U).
\end{equation}
Moreover $W_{\operatorname{cl}_E(U)}=W_U$, and this closure is
idempotent. In particular, exact realization of $U$ requires
$\operatorname{cl}_E(U)=U$. The exact sufficient condition is the
maximal-minor test in Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-kernel}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every member of the right side has all its products in $W_U$, so is
annihilated by every admissible prior. Conversely, let $f$ belong to
every kernel and fix $e\in E$. Put $g=ef$ and choose
$\mu_*\in\mathcal P_U$. Then $\mu_*g=0$. Orthogonally project $g$
onto $W_U$ in $L^2(\mu_*)$ and put $h=g-\Pi_U g$. It has mean
zero, is orthogonal to $W_U$, and is bounded and continuous. For all
sufficiently small real $t$, the measure $(1+th)\mu_*$ belongs to
$\mathcal P_U$. Its integral of $g$ is $t\mu_*(h^2)$, which must
vanish. Thus $h=0$ in $L^2(\mu_*)$, hence identically by continuity
and full support. Therefore $g\in W_U$. Since this holds for every
$e$, the claimed equality follows. The inclusions
$U\subset\operatorname{cl}_E(U)$ and
$E\operatorname{cl}_E(U)\subset W_U$ give equality of the product
spaces. Applying the definition again proves idempotence.
\end{proof}

The following family makes the closure and the exact criterion explicit.
It includes the failure of an unqualified kernel-realization claim, but
also classifies the kernels that are realized in the same positive
experiment.

\begin{proposition}[Exact balanced-block kernels]
\label{prop:block-kernels}
Let $n\ge m+1$, let $J=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and put
\[
 X=J\times\{-1,1\},\quad E=\spanop\{e_j:j\in J\},\quad
 F=\R1\oplus\sigma\mathbb R_{<m}[z].
\]
Here $e_j$ is the indicator of the $j$th two-point block and a polynomial
$p$ represents the function $(j,\sigma)\mapsto p(j)$. Let
$0\ne S\subset\mathbb R_{<m}[z]$, $U=\sigma S$, and
\[
 Z_S=\{j\in J:p(j)=0\text{ for every }p\in S\}.
\]
Then $\mathcal P_U\ne\varnothing$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-kernel-classification}
 \operatorname{cl}_E(U)
   =\sigma\{p\in\mathbb R_{<m}[z]:p|_{Z_S}=0\},\qquad
 r_U=1+|Z_S|.
\end{equation}
Thus $U$ is exactly realizable if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-root-condition}
 S=\left(\prod_{j\in Z_S}(z-j)\right)
                  \mathbb R_{<m-|Z_S|}[z].
\end{equation}
All these assertions have a positive affine acquisition and a fixed
finite physical query realization.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $s_j=\mu(j,+)+\mu(j,-)>0$ and
$d_j=\mu(j,+)-\mu(j,-)$, so $|d_j|<s_j$ and $\sum_j s_j=1$.
The equations $\mu E U=0$ are $d_jp(j)=0$ for every $p\in S$.
They force $d_j=0$ on $J\setminus Z_S$ and impose no condition on
$d_j$ on $Z_S$. The uniform probability is an admissible witness.
Since a nonzero polynomial of degree less than $m$ has at most $m-1$
roots, $|Z_S|\le m-1$ and $J\setminus Z_S$ is nonempty.

In the basis $1,\sigma,\sigma z,\ldots,\sigma z^{m-1}$ the rows of
the pairing are
\[
 (s_j,d_j,d_jj,\ldots,d_jj^{m-1}).
\]
Any kernel element $c+\sigma p$ has $c=0$, by a row outside $Z_S$.
For fixed $d$, its remaining constraint is $p(j)=0$ at those $j\in Z_S$
where $d_j\ne0$. Distinct-point polynomial evaluation has rank equal
to the number of such points, because that number is less than $m$.
It follows that the maximal pairing rank is $1+|Z_S|$. It is attained
explicitly by $s_j=1/n$, $d_j=1/(2n)$ on $Z_S$, and $d_j=0$
elsewhere. All atom masses are positive. The kernel of this witness is
exactly the first space in \eqref{eq:block-kernel-classification}; every
admissible kernel contains that space. Proposition~\ref{prop:forced-kernel}
identifies it with the closure. Rank--nullity proves the exact-realization
criterion, and the factor theorem for polynomials gives
\eqref{eq:block-root-condition}.

For the physical realization use $n-1$ acquisition features
\[
 f_i(j,\sigma)=\frac{e_i(j,\sigma)}{2(n-1)},\qquad
 L_y(u,j,\sigma)=\tfrac12(1+y u^tf(j,\sigma)),
 \quad u\in[-1,1]^{n-1}.
\]
They satisfy $L_y\ge1/4$. At $u=0$, the history product and its command
derivatives span $E$, since $e_n=1-\sum_{i<n}e_i$. The query
probabilities
\[
 g_\ell(j,\sigma)=\tfrac12+\tfrac14\sigma(j/n)^\ell,
 \qquad 0\le\ell<m,
\]
belong to $[1/4,3/4]$, and their span with $1$ is $F$. They can each
be executed as a binary query with the stated success probability.
The detector, acquisition cube, and query menu are fixed while the
prior varies. This proves the physical assertion.
\end{proof}

For $n=4$, $m=2$, and $S=\spanop\{1\}$, the set $Z_S$ is empty.
Thus $U=\spanop\{\sigma\}$ satisfies the annihilation criterion but
its forced closure is $\spanop\{\sigma,j\sigma\}$; the pairing rank
on the entire constrained slice is one. The product-evaluation vectors
are $\pm\mathbf e_j$, so their convex hull contains zero in its
interior. There is nevertheless no exact kernel $U$. In contrast, for
each $j_0\in J$, the line $\spanop\{(j-j_0)\sigma\}$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:block-root-condition} and is an exact kernel of the explicit
full-support witness above. The relative-interior condition and the
exact-kernel condition therefore make different, verifiable predictions
inside the same experiment.

\subsubsection{Return to the acquired prediction state}
\begin{corollary}[Exact constrained prediction ranks]
\label{cor:kernel-history-rank}
Let $P:O\to C(X;\R)$ be a positive history-product map of class $C^2$,
where $u_*$ is an interior point of the command neighborhood $O$, and let
$\Phi=\spanop\{1,\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_b\}$ be a finite continuous
future test space. Apply the preceding construction with
\[
 F=\spanop\{P(u_*,\cdot)\}+\operatorname{im}DP(u_*),\qquad E=\Phi.
\]
For every $U\subset F$ with $\mathcal P_U\ne\varnothing$, the maximal
normalized prediction rank at $u_*$ among these priors is $r_U-1$.
It is attained by the arbitrarily small bounded-density perturbations
in Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-kernel}. If the exact-kernel condition holds,
the attained rank is $\dim F-\dim U-1$.

For each maximizing prior with $r_U>1$, commands with a density
bounded below near $u_*$
and the specified report word give an unconditional subprobability
minorization on an $(r_U-1)$-dimensional projection of predictions.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $L:F\to E^*$ be the moment pairing and let
$Y=LP=(Z,Zp)^t$, where $Z=\mu P>0$. Normalization has kernel
$\R Y$ on the image of $L$. Moreover
\[
 L(F)=\R Y+L(\operatorname{im}DP(u_*)).
\]
Hence the actual command derivative of the normalized map has rank
$\rank L-1$; the evidence column need not itself be a command
derivative. The positive evidence also ensures $\rank L\ge1$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-kernel} gives the maximal rank and its local
attainment, and rank--nullity gives the exact-kernel specialization.

For a maximizing prior choose $r_U-1$ independent output rows of the
command derivative and complete them by orthonormal kernel coordinates.
The resulting square map has an invertible derivative. A sufficiently
small product box in its image has inverse contained in the command
neighborhood, and its inverse Jacobian determinant is bounded below.
The joint density of the commands and the specified report word is the
command density times $\mu P(u)$, which has a positive lower bound on
a smaller neighborhood. Change variables and integrate over the entire
kernel-coordinate box. The resulting positive lower density on the
output box proves the asserted subprobability minorization. It is a
statement about actual histories, not mass assigned to a section.
\end{proof}

For the balanced-block witnesses, normalization yields the prediction
rank $|Z_S|$. Their full one-step resolution law is then supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-affine-classification}, using the covariance of the
fixed acquisition and query functions. In the general multi-step problem,
the present kernel criterion supplies local attainment information; the
whole-image cover and stable causal update remain separate requirements.
In the monomial experiment these are precisely the other two parts of
Theorems~\ref{thm:intrinsic-checkpoint} and
\ref{thm:intrinsic-streaming}.


\subsection{Finite maximum rank and a moving kernel}
\label{sec:finite-pairing-comparison}
There are two distinct quantifiers in positive pairing problems: full
rank under every positive weighting and maximal rank under some positive
weighting. The former was compared with the positive-diagonal criteria
in Section~\ref{sec:v19-comparison}. The latter has the following
classical finite specialization.

\begin{proposition}[Finite common-independence specialization]
\label{prop:finite-matroid-rank}
Let $X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_s\}$, and let the columns of
$A\in\R^{d\times s}$ and $B\in\R^{k\times s}$ be evaluations of
bases of $E$ and $F$. With no annihilation constraint ($U=0$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-matroid-rank}
 \max_{w_i>0,\ \sum_iw_i=1}\rank(A\operatorname{diag}(w)B^t)
 =\max\{|S|: A_{:,S}\text{ and }B_{:,S}\text{ have full column rank}\}.
\end{equation}
The same number is the rank over $\R(w_1,\ldots,w_s)$ of the symbolic
matrix. Thus it is the maximum common independent-set cardinality of
the two represented linear matroids.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For row sets $I,J$ of cardinality $\ell$, Cauchy--Binet gives
\[
 \det(A\operatorname{diag}(w)B^t)_{I,J}
 =\sum_{|S|=\ell}\det A_{I,S}\det B_{J,S}\prod_{i\in S}w_i.
\]
Distinct sets $S$ give distinct monomials. Some $\ell$-minor is a
nonzero polynomial if and only if an $\ell$-element column set is
independent in both matrices. Taking the largest such $\ell$ gives
the symbolic rank. A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on the open
positive orthant. Its minor is homogeneous, so dividing positive
weights by their sum preserves its nonvanishing. This attains the
symbolic rank on the positive probability simplex. No specialization
can exceed symbolic rank because all larger minors vanish identically.
The empty independent set and rank zero are included.
\end{proof}

This symbolic formulation is classical linear matroid intersection; see
Harvey \cite{Harvey2009} and the explicit common-base determinant
criterion recalled by Terao \cite[p.~39:2]{Terao2026}. The proposition
is given to specify the specialization, not to claim a new matroid
intersection result. Once $H_\mu U=0$ is imposed, the weights obey
additional linear equations. Distinct monomials can then restrict to
dependent polynomials, so the unconstrained argument cannot be applied
unchanged to that affine slice. The quotient multiplication pencil and
Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel-moment-chart} identify the correct coordinates
there and realize their open moment image by small positive continuous
density tilts on a general compact space.

\subsubsection{A square obstruction not explained by dimension}
Feasibility and the absence of a common forced direction do not ensure
an injective pairing, even when both spaces contain evidence and have
the same dimension. The following example makes the extra determinantal
condition visible inside a positive experiment.

\begin{proposition}[A square positive pairing with a moving kernel]
\label{prop:square-moving-kernel}
On $X=\{1,2,3,4\}$, let $e_i$ denote the singleton indicators and set
\[
 E=\operatorname{span}\{1,e_1,e_2\},\qquad
 F=\operatorname{span}\{1,e_3,e_4\},\qquad U=0.
\]
For every full-support probability $p$, the pairing has rank two and
\[
 \ker H_p=\operatorname{span}\{p_4e_3-p_3e_4\}.
\]
The intersection of these kernels is zero and $\operatorname{cl}_E(0)=0$,
but zero is not an exactly realizable kernel. The example has a positive
affine acquisition with two future binary queries, whose one-step
regret satisfies, uniformly in the full-support prior and integer $M$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moving-kernel-risk}
 R_M\asymp
 \frac{(p_1^2+p_2^2)(p_3^2+p_4^2)}{512}\,M^{-2}.
\end{equation}
Both unconditional and worst-history criteria have this law.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the indicated bases,
\[
 H_p=\begin{pmatrix}1&p_3&p_4\\p_1&0&0\\p_2&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant vanishes identically and its first two rows and columns
have determinant $-p_1p_3\ne0$. Its kernel is the displayed line.
The ratio $p_3/p_4$ varies among positive probabilities, so no nonzero
vector lies in all these lines. Since $1\in E$, a function satisfying
$Ef=0$ is itself zero. Thus the closure is zero, feasibility is automatic,
and the quotient has three columns, whereas its pencil has rank two.
This is precisely the distinction in Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-kernel}.

For the realization choose $f=(e_1,e_2)^t/4$, uniform
$u\in[-1,1]^2$, and likelihoods
\[
 L_y(u,x)=\tfrac12(1+y u^tf(x)),\qquad y\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
They lie in $[3/8,5/8]$ and sum to one. Choose either of the queries
$g_1=1/2+e_3/4$ and $g_2=1/2+e_4/4$ with probability $1/2$
after encoding. They lie in $[1/2,3/4]$. The augmented acquisition
and query spaces at $u=0$ are $E$ and $F$. In physical query units,
\[
 C_p=\operatorname{Cov}_p\bigl((g_1,g_2)^t/\sqrt2,f\bigr)
 =-\frac1{16\sqrt2}
       \begin{pmatrix}p_3\\p_4\end{pmatrix}
       \begin{pmatrix}p_1&p_2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This follows from $e_i e_j=0$ for the two disjoint indicator groups.
Its sole nonzero singular value is
$\sqrt{p_1^2+p_2^2}\sqrt{p_3^2+p_4^2}/(16\sqrt2)$.
The affine profile of Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-affine-classification} gives
\eqref{eq:moving-kernel-risk}. The normalized rank is $2-1=1$,
in agreement with the physical covariance calculation.
\end{proof}

The example supports, rather than contradicts, the exact-kernel
criterion. Its role is to separate three necessary tests---positive
feasibility, multiplication closure and dimension---from the additional
maximal-minor test. It does not extend a local kernel theorem to a
multistep uniform resolution theorem.


\subsection{Positive products and attainable information}
\label{sec:positive-history}
The lower bound in an information problem requires a measure on histories,
not merely a rank calculation in an ambient test space. We isolate the
passage from a product tangent to such a measure. This formulation also
separates the part of the argument that uses an ordered parameter space
from the part that does not.

Let $X$ be a compact space with probability $\mu$. A command ranges over
a cube $U\subset\R^b$. Fix one report with likelihood $0<f(u,x)\le1$, twice
continuously differentiable in $u$ as a map into $C(X)$. For a tuple
$\boldsymbol u=(u_1,\ldots,u_n)$ write
\[
 P_{\boldsymbol u}=\prod_{i=1}^nf(u_i,\cdot),\qquad
 Z_{\boldsymbol u}=\mu P_{\boldsymbol u},\qquad
 T_{\boldsymbol u}=\operatorname{im}D_{\boldsymbol u}P.
\]
For bounded tests $\phi=(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_p)$, define
\[
 z(\boldsymbol u)=\frac{\mu(P_{\boldsymbol u}\phi)}{Z_{\boldsymbol u}},
 \qquad LQ=(\mu Q,\mu(Q\phi))\in\R^{p+1}.
\]
The command law below has a density bounded below on a neighborhood of
the displayed tuple. The report word is not conditioned away.

\begin{proposition}[Normalized product criterion]\label{prop:product-criterion}
Suppose $\boldsymbol u_*$ is interior, $P_{\boldsymbol u_*}\in
T_{\boldsymbol u_*}$, and $L:T_{\boldsymbol u_*}\to\R^{p+1}$ is
onto. Then $Dz(\boldsymbol u_*)$ is onto $\R^p$. More generally, on
any product tangent containing $P$, normalization decreases the rank of
$L$ by exactly one.

For a family of such maps, suppose the tuples stay a fixed distance from
the command boundary, the least row singular value of $Dz$ is at least
$\sigma>0$, and the first two command derivatives are uniformly bounded.
Suppose also $f\ge\kappa>0$ and the joint command density is at least
$a>0$ on a fixed-radius neighborhood of those tuples. There are uniform $\rho,\beta>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstract-minorization}
 z_*\bigl(Z_{\boldsymbol u}\,d\mathsf Q(\boldsymbol u)\bigr)
 \ \ge\ \beta\,\operatorname{Unif}
       \bigl(z(\boldsymbol u_*)+[-\rho,\rho]^p\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here the measure on the left is the subprobability law of the commands
and the specified report word. The constants do not depend on any
singular values of a subsequent observation map.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $v=LP/Z$ and let $e_0$ select its constant coordinate. Direct
differentiation gives the normalized derivative on $T$ as
\[
 Z^{-1}\bigl(LQ-v\,e_0LQ\bigr).
\]
Since $e_0v=1$ and $v\in L(T)$, the kernel of $w\mapsto w-v e_0w$
on $L(T)$ is exactly $\operatorname{span}\{v\}$. Its range has
constant coordinate zero. Thus precisely one rank is lost; in particular
surjectivity of $L$ gives surjectivity onto the $p$ nonconstant tests.

For the quantitative assertion let $d=nb$, let $J=Dz(\boldsymbol u_*)$,
and choose a matrix $K$ whose rows form an orthonormal basis of $\ker J$.
Use the square map
\[
 F(\boldsymbol u)=\bigl(z(\boldsymbol u),
               K(\boldsymbol u-\boldsymbol u_*)\bigr).
\]
Its central derivative has least singular value at least
$\min(\sigma,1)$: its two row spaces are orthogonal. The second
 derivative bound gives a ball of uniform radius $r$, inside the command
cube, on which
$\|DF-DF(\boldsymbol u_*)\|\le\min(\sigma,1)/2$.
Writing the inverse equation as
\[
 u=u_*+DF(u_*)^{-1}\{y-F(u_*)-[F(u)-F(u_*)-DF(u_*)(u-u_*)]\}
\]
shows, by contraction on that ball, that a uniform ball about $F(u_*)$
is in its image. Choose a product cube in this output ball. If the forward
 derivative norm is at most $B$, then $|\det DF|\le B^d$; hence the
inverse Jacobian on the cube is at least $B^{-d}$. The subprobability
density in command coordinates is at least $a\kappa^n$. Change variables
and integrate over the entire kernel-coordinate cube. This gives a
positive lower density on the $p$-cube, and therefore
\eqref{eq:abstract-minorization}. The integration over kernel coordinates
is essential; a section by itself has zero ambient command measure.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{A criterion valid for every full-support prior}
For $v_0,\ldots,v_p\in C(I)$ on a compact interval $I$, call this a strict Chebyshev
system when its evaluation determinant is nonzero at every ordered
$(p+1)$-tuple of distinct interior points. Reversing one basis vector
fixes its sign. Endpoint values are understood by continuity. We use
only this finite determinant property, not an unproved assertion about
individual derivatives selected from an incomplete Hermite block.

\begin{theorem}[Positive-history attainment from two test systems]
\label{thm:positive-attainment}
Let $W\subset C(I)$ be finite dimensional and contain $1$. Suppose a
positive finite detector spans $W$, with command cube as in
Section~\ref{sec:experiment}. At an interior tuple of its failure factors
$F_1,\ldots,F_n$, suppose the product tangent
\[
 T=\sum_{i=1}^n W\prod_{j\ne i}F_j
\]
contains a $(p+1)$-dimensional strict Chebyshev system. Suppose
$(1,\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_p)$ is another strict Chebyshev system. Then,
for every full-support probability $\mu$ on $I$, the posterior map to
these $p$ tests is a submersion at that tuple. Independent uniform
commands give a positive subprobability minorization of a $p$-cube.

These assertions are uniform in a compact parameter family provided the
chosen command tuples vary continuously and remain uniformly interior,
the factors and tests are continuous in
uniform norm, the command derivatives through order two are continuous
and bounded, and both determinant properties hold at every parameter.
One may take any finite union of such continuous families. The assertion
also holds uniformly over $c_-\mu_0\le\mu\le c_+\mu_0$, where $c_->0$
and $\mu_0$ has full support.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a basis $v_0,\ldots,v_p$ of the stated subsystem of $T$, and put
$\phi_0=1$. Both evaluation determinants have constant nonzero sign on
the connected set of ordered interior points. Orient their bases so the
signs agree. Determinant expansion and Fubini give
\[
 \det[\mu(v_i\phi_j)]_{i,j=0}^p
 =\frac1{(p+1)!}\int_{I^{p+1}}
       \det[v_i(t_k)]_{i,k=0}^p
       \det[\phi_j(t_k)]_{j,k=0}^p\prod_{k=0}^p\mu(dt_k).
\]
The integrand is nonnegative everywhere and strictly positive when the
points are distinct and interior. Full support assigns positive mass to
a product of disjoint ordered interior intervals. The determinant is
therefore nonzero and $L:T\to\R^{p+1}$ is onto. Also $P\in T$, by
varying any one factor along itself. The command-to-factor differential
is onto $W$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:product-criterion} applies to
actual commands, not to fictitious factor variations.

For a fixed prior, continuity and compactness give a positive lower
bound on the least row singular value. For the dominated family, argue
by compactness of the measures as well. Every sequence of probabilities
on the compact interval has a weakly convergent subsequence; the two
measure inequalities pass to its limit by testing against nonnegative
continuous functions. The limiting measure still dominates $c_-\mu_0$
and therefore has full support. Uniform-norm continuity of the factors,
tests and their command derivatives makes the normalized Jacobian jointly
continuous in the parameter and the weakly varying measure. A sequence
of least row singular values tending to zero would thus give a limiting
Jacobian of deficient rank, contradicting the determinant argument just
proved for every full-support prior. This establishes the uniform bound.
The quantitative part of
Proposition~\ref{prop:product-criterion} proves the minorization. Finitely
many parameter charts or test orders require only finitely many minima.
\end{proof}

In the monomial experiment, Lemma~\ref{lem:binomial-tangent} supplies the
first system and a complete confluent future flag supplies the second.
Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-attainment} identifies precisely the
positive-history statement beyond a spectral comparison. Its hypotheses
are verified in Lemma~\ref{lem:newton-attainment}; neither generic rank
nor full support alone is substituted for them. A local minorization
still does not prove a global cover or a causal realization. Those are
separate assertions in Theorems~\ref{thm:intrinsic-checkpoint} and
\ref{thm:intrinsic-streaming}.

The circle experiment of Section~\ref{sec:circular} uses the first
proposition but not the ordered-space hypothesis of the theorem. There
the required submersion comes from elementary symmetric polynomials,
and both acquisition and observation contribute to the disappearing
scales.


\subsection{Realization and degeneration of acquisition geometry}
\label{sec:v19-degeneration}
The affine classification has two consequences that are not supplied
by a rank count. First, every small rectangular covariance matrix can
be realized by varying the prior inside one fixed positive experiment.
Second, along an analytic arc, the determinantal orders give the entire
joint memory--degeneration law. The realization uses uniformly
full-support priors; thus these phenomena need not be caused by loss
of latent support.

The product characters used in the construction are the usual
orthonormal Fourier basis on a finite sign cube
\cite[Section 2.2]{deWolf2008}. Their use in a positive density is an
elementary specialization of Fourier inversion. The conclusions proved
here concern fixed physical acquisition and query functions, and the
uniform memory law supplied by Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-affine-classification}.

\subsubsection{A local realization theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Covariance realization in a fixed experiment]
\label{thm:v19-covariance-realization}
For every $b,q\ge1$ there is one fixed positive binary affine
acquisition, with $b$ command coordinates and a fixed menu of $q$
physical binary queries, having the following property. Every
$A\in\R^{q\times b}$ with $\sum_{j,i}|A_{ji}|\le1/2$ is realized
by a prior $\mu_A$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-density-envelope}
 \tfrac12\mu_0\le\mu_A\le\tfrac32\mu_0,
 \qquad C_{\mu_A}=\frac{1}{8b\sqrt q}\,A,
\end{equation}
where $\mu_0$ is one fixed full-support probability and $C$ is the
weighted acquisition--query covariance of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-affine-classification}.
The map $A\mapsto\mu_A$ is affine and injective.

Consequently every finite-dimensional real analytic matrix germ, after
multiplication by a fixed sufficiently small positive scalar, is the
covariance germ of priors in this same dominated family. All its
matrix-rank strata are realized exactly, not just the maximal rank.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $X=\{-1,1\}^{b+q}$ with uniform probability $\mu_0$. Denote
its coordinate functions by $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_b,\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_q$.
Set
\[
 f_i=\frac{\xi_i}{2b},\qquad
 L_y(u,x)=\frac{1+y\sum_i u_i f_i(x)}2,
 \quad y\in\{-1,1\},\quad u\in[-1,1]^b.
\]
The sum $\sum_i|f_i|=1/2$, so both report likelihoods are at least
$1/4$ on the entire command cube. Query $j$ has success probability
$g_j=1/2+\zeta_j/4$, and is selected with probability $1/q$
independently after the retained label is formed. These are fixed
physical queries with values in $[1/4,3/4]$. Their weighted functions
are $h_j=g_j/\sqrt q$.

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-walsh-prior}
 \frac{\dd\mu_A}{\dd\mu_0}
       =1+\sum_{j=1}^q\sum_{i=1}^b A_{ji}\xi_i\zeta_j.
\end{equation}
Each nonconstant coordinate product has mean zero under $\mu_0$.
The density integrates to one, and the entrywise bound gives the two
inequalities in \eqref{eq:v19-density-envelope}. In particular every
$\mu_A$ has full support on the same finite space. Independence and
symmetry of the coordinate signs give, exactly,
\[
 \mu_A\xi_i=\mu_A\zeta_j=0,\qquad
 \mu_A(\xi_i\zeta_j)=A_{ji}.
\]
For the last identity, the average of
$\xi_i\zeta_j\xi_{i'}\zeta_{j'}$ is one when both indices coincide
and zero otherwise. Thus
\[
 \Cov_{\mu_A}(h_j,f_i)
   =\mu_A\left(\frac{\zeta_j}{4\sqrt q}
                       \frac{\xi_i}{2b}\right)
   =\frac{A_{ji}}{8b\sqrt q}.
\]
This also proves injectivity. An analytic matrix germ has bounded
entries on some sufficiently small closed parameter neighborhood.
One scalar multiplication places it in the entrywise ball used above.
Formula \eqref{eq:v19-walsh-prior} then produces analytic prior
weights, and the nonzero scalar in \eqref{eq:v19-density-envelope}
preserves every determinantal rank locus. The assertion concerns
matrix germs and their rank loci; it does not posit simultaneous
Smith diagonalization in several parameters.
\end{proof}

In this construction the acquisition means are zero for every prior.
Thus the projective coordinate of Lemma~\ref{lem:v18-projective-law}
is simply $\theta=yu$. The same command distribution and physical
query menu serve all the matrices. The changing geometry in
\eqref{eq:v19-density-envelope} is entirely prior geometry.

\subsubsection{Determinantal orders on an analytic arc}
We use the local Smith form over the ring of convergent power series.
The connection between its invariant orders and singular values is
classical; in particular Kaveh--Makhnatch
\cite[Theorem 1.1 and Section 6]{KavehMakhnatch} establish the
singular-value logarithmic limit. The elementary version below includes
rectangular matrices and identically zero singular directions, and
records the two-sided estimates needed for memory budgets varying
with the parameter.

\begin{lemma}[Analytic determinantal orders]
\label{lem:v19-analytic-orders}
Let $C(t)$ be a real analytic $q\times b$ matrix near $t=0$, with
rank $r>0$ over the field of convergent Laurent series. There are
unique integers $0\le\alpha_1\le\cdots\le\alpha_r$ and analytic
invertible matrices $U(t),V(t)$ near zero such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-smith}
 U(t)C(t)V(t)=\operatorname{diag}
           (t^{\alpha_1},\ldots,t^{\alpha_r},0),
\end{equation}
with rectangular zero padding. Both matrices and their inverses are
bounded on a sufficiently small closed interval. Put $A_0=0$ and
$A_\ell=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\alpha_j$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-minor-orders}
 A_\ell=\min_{|I|=|J|=\ell}
             \operatorname{ord}_0\det C_{I,J}(t),\qquad 1\le\ell\le r,
\end{equation}
where the order of an identically zero minor is $+\infty$.
The decreasing singular values satisfy, uniformly for $0<t\le t_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-singular-orders}
 s_j(C(t))\asymp t^{\alpha_j}\ (j\le r),\qquad
 s_j(C(t))=0\ (j>r).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A nonzero convergent power series is $t^a$ times an analytic unit.
Choose a nonzero entry of smallest order, move it to the upper left
corner, and multiply its row by a unit so that this entry becomes
$t^a$. Every other entry is divisible by $t^a$ in the convergent
power-series ring. Analytic row operations kill the rest of its
column, and analytic column operations then kill the rest of its row.
The remaining block still has every entry divisible by $t^a$:
its entries have the form $c_{ij}-c_{i1}c_{1j}/t^a$ after the unit
normalization. Repeat on that block. Each operation is invertible
analytically at zero. The process stops after $r$ pivots, with a zero
remaining block, and the pivot orders are nondecreasing. This proves
\eqref{eq:v19-smith}. Nonvanishing determinants of the multipliers
at zero give the asserted bounds for them and their inverses.

By Cauchy--Binet, analytic invertible row and column operations
preserve the ideal generated by all $\ell$-minors; apply the same
argument to the inverse operations to get equality of ideals.
In a one-variable power-series ring the least order of a finite
set of generators is the order of its ideal. For the diagonal form
this order is $A_\ell$. This proves \eqref{eq:v19-minor-orders},
and its successive differences prove uniqueness of the $\alpha_j$.

For any matrices $B,Q$ with compatible sizes and $Q$ invertible,
the variational characterization of singular values gives
$s_j(BQ)\le\|Q\|s_j(B)$ and
$s_j(BQ)\ge s_j(B)/\|Q^{-1}\|$; the analogous inequalities hold
for left multiplication. Apply these four inequalities to
\eqref{eq:v19-smith} and its inverse. For $0<t\le\min(t_0,1)$
the diagonal entries are already in decreasing order. The multiplier
bounds give \eqref{eq:v19-singular-orders}, including exact zeros.
No inverse power of a vanishing diagonal entry occurs in the
comparison constants.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The joint memory--degeneration law]
\label{thm:v19-analytic-memory}
Fix a positive affine acquisition and a physical query menu as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-affine-classification}. Let $\mu_t$ be priors
whose weighted covariance matrix $C(t)$ is real analytic near zero.
If its generic rank is $r>0$, use the integers $A_\ell$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:v19-analytic-orders}. For every integer $M\ge1$ and
$0<t\le t_0$, both checkpoint criteria satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-joint-law}
 R^{\rm av}_{M,1}(t)\asymp R^{\rm max}_{M,1}(t)
 \asymp\max_{1\le\ell\le r}
                 t^{2A_\ell/\ell}M^{-2/\ell}.
\end{equation}
The constants are independent of $M,t$; they may depend on the fixed
analytic germ and the physical bounds. At $t=0$, the same expression
holds by continuity with $0^0=1$: only the indices for which
$A_\ell=0$ remain. If all $\alpha_j>0$, the endpoint risk is zero.
If $C$ vanishes identically near zero, all these risks are zero there.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the stated physical experiment, the affine theorem gives uniform
two-sided comparisons with
\[
 \max_{1\le\ell\le\min(b,q)}
 \left(\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}s_j(C(t))}{M}\right)^{2/\ell}.
\]
Its constants do not depend on the prior or on a nonzero singular
value. Products with $\ell>r$ vanish; the other products are comparable
to $t^{A_\ell}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:v19-analytic-orders}. There are
only finitely many indices, so the constants can be chosen jointly
for all of them and all integer budgets. This proves
\eqref{eq:v19-joint-law}. At zero, the analytic invertible
multipliers in \eqref{eq:v19-smith} show that the rank is exactly
the number of zero orders. Apply the affine theorem at that prior
itself. If there are no zero orders, all prediction probabilities
are constant after acquisition and one label is exact. The same
argument applies to an identically zero covariance. The endpoint
claim does not use a limiting interchange of a minimization over
encoders with $t\to0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Phase exponents, their inverse, and realization]
\label{cor:v19-memory-phases}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-analytic-memory}, put
$M_\gamma(t)=\lceil t^{-\gamma}\rceil$ for $\gamma\ge0$ and
$0<t<1$. For either checkpoint criterion,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-phase}
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{\log R_{M_\gamma(t),1}(t)}{\log t}
 =\min_{1\le\ell\le r}\frac{2(A_\ell+\gamma)}{\ell}.
\end{equation}
The successive transition exponents are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-transitions}
 \beta_\ell=\ell\alpha_{\ell+1}-A_\ell,
       \qquad 1\le\ell<r.
\end{equation}
They are nonnegative and nondecreasing. On
$\beta_{\ell-1}\le\gamma\le\beta_\ell$ the $\ell$th branch
is minimal, with $\beta_0=0$ and $\beta_r=+\infty$;
repeated transition values give intervals of length zero.

For the full checkpoint curve $R_t(M)$ and $1\le\ell\le r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-valuation-inverse}
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}
   \frac{\log\mathcal I_\ell(R_t)}{\log t}=A_\ell,
 \qquad \mathcal I_\ell(R)=\inf_{M\in\N,\,M\ge1}MR(M)^{\ell/2}.
\end{equation}
For $\ell>r$ the transform is zero. Thus its orders determine the
$\alpha_j$ by successive differences.
Every nondecreasing list of nonnegative integers of length
$r\le\min(b,q)$ occurs in \eqref{eq:v19-phase} inside the single
fixed experiment of Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-covariance-realization},
with the same two-sided density envelope.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The integer ceiling satisfies $t^{-\gamma}\le M_\gamma(t)
\le2t^{-\gamma}$. Therefore \eqref{eq:v19-joint-law} is comparable
to the maximum of the finitely many powers
$t^{2(A_\ell+\gamma)/\ell}$. The smallest exponent gives this
maximum up to a factor at most $r$. Dividing its logarithm by
$\log t$, whose magnitude tends to infinity, proves
\eqref{eq:v19-phase}; all fixed comparison constants disappear.
Adjacent affine functions of $\gamma$ intersect at
\eqref{eq:v19-transitions}. These values are nonnegative since
$A_\ell\le\ell\alpha_{\ell+1}$, and
\[
 \beta_{\ell+1}-\beta_\ell
       =(\ell+1)(\alpha_{\ell+2}-\alpha_{\ell+1})\ge0.
\]
Between two successive transitions, comparisons with all earlier
branches follow by chaining adjacent comparisons on their right,
and comparisons with later branches by chaining them on their left.
This proves the phase assertion, including ties.

Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:v18-affine-invariant}, equivalently the
integer-envelope argument in
Theorem~\ref{thm:operational-reconstruction}, to each full curve.
The uniform affine constants give
$\mathcal I_\ell(R_t)\asymp\prod_{j\le\ell}s_j(C(t))
\asymp t^{A_\ell}$ for $\ell\le r$, and zero for larger $\ell$.
This proves \eqref{eq:v19-valuation-inverse}. For realization choose
$A(t)$ to be a rectangular diagonal matrix with entries
$\varepsilon t^{\alpha_1},\ldots,\varepsilon t^{\alpha_r}$,
and all other entries zero. Taking $0<\varepsilon\le1/(2r)$
on $|t|\le1$ meets the entrywise bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-covariance-realization}. Its nonzero constant
covariance factor does not change any minor order. That theorem
supplies fixed physical likelihoods, fixed query weights, and priors
between $\mu_0/2$ and $3\mu_0/2$, completing the realization.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{An interior two-scale degeneration}
For instance, in the construction with $b=q=2$, take
\[
 A(t)=\frac1{16}
 \begin{pmatrix}t&t^2\\t^2&t^3+t^5\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]
Its entrywise norm is at most $5/16$, its first minor order is one,
and its determinant is $t^6/256$. Thus $\alpha=(1,5)$ and
\[
 R_{M,1}(t)\asymp\max\{t^2M^{-2},\ t^6M^{-1}\}.
\]
The changeover occurs at $M\asymp t^{-4}$ although every prior
remains uniformly equivalent to the same full-support probability.
This is an actual physical family, not merely a matrix spectrum
postulated after the risk law. More generally the realization theorem
and \eqref{eq:v19-phase} classify all the permitted one-parameter
orders in arbitrary finite covariance dimensions. The algebraic
normal form is classical; the conclusion here couples it to uniform
attainable mass, whole-image approximation, and the physical memory
resource. It is a one-acquisition-step conclusion. The distinct
multi-step collision and circular theorems, with their causal
recurrences, retain their full force and their separate hypotheses.

The inverse in \eqref{eq:v19-valuation-inverse} uses the entire
unbounded integer-budget curve at each $t>0$. It does not infer
orders from finitely many noisy errors, from a maximum over
checkpoints, or from a risk with a positive uncertainty floor.
Nor does the analytic-arc theorem assert a simultaneous monomial
normal form in several parameters.
