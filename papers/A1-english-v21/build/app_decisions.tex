\subsection{The observation algebra and asymmetric profiles}\label{sec:algebra}
For integral exponents define the standard graded algebra
\[
 \mathcal R_A=\R[z t^a:a\in A]\subset\R[z,t],\qquad \deg(zt^a)=1.
\]
Its degree-$m$ component has basis $\{z^m t^s:s\in mA\}$, so its
Hilbert function is exactly $h_A(m)$. The general sumset--Hilbert-function
connection is classical \cite{EliahouMazumdar,Elias}. Its role here is
operational: $h_A(m)-1$ is the number of independent future observable
tests, and Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} proves when the attainable past can
excite all of them. The algebra alone would not prove that transversality.

\begin{proposition}[Three-cell classification]\label{prop:three}
Let $A=\{0,a,b\}$ with integers $0<a<b$, put
$g=\gcd(a,b)$, $a'=a/g$ and $B=b/g$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hilbert-three}
 h_A(m)=\binom{m+2}{2}
       -\one_{\{m\ge B\}}\binom{m-B+2}{2}.
\end{equation}
The homogeneous relation is generated by
$x_1^B-x_0^{B-a'}x_2^{a'}$ under the map
$(x_0,x_1,x_2)\mapsto(z,zt^a,zt^b)$. In particular,
\[
 d_A(n,m)=\min\left\{2n,
 \binom{m+2}{2}-\one_{\{m\ge B\}}\binom{m-B+2}{2}-1\right\}.
\]
For nonnegative real exponents $0<a<b$ with $a/b$ irrational,
$h_A(m)=\binom{m+2}{2}$ for every $m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A monomial $x_0^kx_1^ix_2^j$ of degree $m$ maps to $z^m t^{ai+bj}$.
Two exponent triples of the same degree have the same image exactly when
their difference is an integer multiple of
$(-(B-a'),B,-a')$: this follows by dividing
$a(i-i')+b(j-j')=0$ by $g$ and using coprimality of $a',B$.
After cancelling a common monomial, each corresponding binomial is a
multiple of $x_1^B-x_0^{B-a'}x_2^{a'}$, by the difference-of-powers
identity. The kernel is spanned by these binomials, because monomials
with distinct images are linearly independent and each fiber's coefficient
sum must vanish. Thus this one relation generates the kernel.

The degree-$m$ polynomials in three variables have dimension
$\binom{m+2}{2}$. Multiplication by the displayed nonzero degree-$B$
relation is injective in the polynomial ring, so the degree-$m$ part of
the ideal has dimension zero for $m<B$ and
$\binom{m-B+2}{2}$ otherwise. Subtraction proves
\eqref{eq:hilbert-three}. If $a/b$ is irrational,
$a(i-i')+b(j-j')=0$ forces both differences to vanish; all
$\binom{m+2}{2}$ possible pairs are distinct. The operational formula
now follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The sparse space that separates rank from future dimension]\label{cor:sparse-example}
For $A=\{0,2,5\}$, the first values of $h_A(m)$, starting at $m=0$,
are $1,3,6,10,15,20,25$. At $n=3,m=2$ the exact information dimension
is five, for every full-support prior. For a total budget of six the
minimal causal dimensions, starting at $n=0$, are
\[
 0,\ 2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 5,\ 2,\ 0.
\]
Thus the peak need not be the peak of a rank-based symmetric tent.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:hilbert-three} with $B=5$ and then
\eqref{eq:main-dimension}. In particular
$2A=\{0,2,4,5,7,10\}$, while the binomial witness at $n=3$ has
$B_{A,3}=\{0,2,5,7,10,12,15\}$. The mixed matrix has rank six
and normalization leaves five. This proves the example uniformly over
full-support priors without relying on a single large rational determinant.
The uniform-prior three-cell example and its numerical witness first
appeared in the source-pinned report \cite{A1review}; the present
corollary is an application of the general theorem, not a new attribution
of that example.
\end{proof}

There is also an exact finite computation for any integral $A$: start
with $S_0=\{0\}$, form $S_{m+1}=\{s+a:s\in S_m,a\in A\}$, and
count distinct exponents. This handles all additive collisions, not just
a nominal degree range. For real exponents the formula is equally exact
as mathematics, but an effective implementation requires exact equality
information about the supplied exponents. Neither fixed rank nor arbitrarily
accurate floating-point exponents substitute for that information.


\subsection{A uniform streaming law through an additive collision}\label{sec:uniform}
We apply the confluent law to a family in which exact dimension changes
at a fixed horizon and at a fixed positivity margin. In this family all
repeated quantization errors can be controlled in the physical prediction
metric, uniformly at the collision.

\subsubsection{One fixed five-trial protocol}
Let $0\le\theta\le1/2$, $A_\theta=\{0,1,2+\theta\}$, and let $\mu$
be any fixed full-support probability on $[0,1]$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-collision-cells}
 \begin{aligned}
 k_1^\theta&=\tfrac13+\tfrac1{12}t,&
 k_2^\theta&=\tfrac13+\tfrac1{12}t^{2+\theta},\\
 k_0^\theta&=\tfrac13-\tfrac1{12}(t+t^{2+\theta}).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These cells are at least $1/6$, sum to one, and span $W_{A_\theta}$.
Use the fixed comparator cube $[1/4,3/4]^3$; every report factor is at
least $\kappa=1/24$. The three fixed probe commands have failure factors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-near-probes}
 F_j^\theta=\tfrac12+\tfrac18 k_j^\theta,\qquad j=0,1,2.
\end{equation}
The command accepts cell $j$ with probability $3/8$ and the other cells
with probability $1/2$. Their sum is $13/8$, so these factors span
$W_{A_\theta}$. At a checkpoint $n$ use the $3^{5-n}$ ordered product
queries of length $5-n$. Exactly one checkpoint and one query are used
in a run; all five trials are counted.

For the unconditional experiment the first three commands are independent
uniform vectors in the comparator cube. After their reports have been
compressed, reveal the independent uniform two-step query. The controller
knows $\theta$ and $\mu$ as read-only calibration, but has access to past
commands and reports only through its persistent index. The input law and
all query labels are independent of $\theta$.

For a history write $M_s=\mu(P_h t^s)$ and use the five coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:five-physical-coordinates}
 w=(M_1,M_2,M_{3+\theta},M_{4+2\theta},M_{2+\theta}-M_2).
\end{equation}
They determine every length-two future law. They are ordinary posterior
expectations of physical tests and their differences. For analysis put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:five-desingularized}
 z=(w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4,w_5/\theta)\quad(\theta>0),
 \qquad z_5\big|_{\theta=0}=\mu(P_h t^2\log t).
\end{equation}
This fifth coordinate at zero is an analytic continuation, not an extra
observable supplied to the filter. The zero-calibration future law uses
only the first four coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp two-parameter streaming order]\label{thm:uniform-streaming}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:five-resolution}
 \Psi_\theta(M)=\max\{M^{-1/2},\theta^{2/5}M^{-2/5}\},
 \qquad M\ge1,
\end{equation}
where the second term is zero at $\theta=0$. There are constants
$0<c\le C<\infty$, depending on the fixed prior and protocol but not on
$M$ or $\theta\in[0,1/2]$, with the following properties.

There is a deterministic $M$-state filter, updated after every report,
whose conditional prediction regret at every checkpoint and every
admitted history is at most $C\Psi_\theta(M)$. In the prescribed
three-trial exploration experiment, every $M$-state filter has
unconditional regret at least $c\Psi_\theta(M)$. The same lower bound
therefore holds for the minimax criterion over histories and checkpoints.
The lower assertion permits the independent randomization of
Definition~\ref{def:finite-state}.
\end{theorem}

In particular the two regimes are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-regimes}
 \overline{\mathcal R}^{\theta,\mathrm{stream}}_{M,5}\asymp
 \begin{cases}
 M^{-1/2},&1\le M\le\theta^{-4},\\
 \theta^{2/5}M^{-2/5},&M\ge\theta^{-4},
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with uniform comparison constants. This is an order-sharp crossover, not
a claim of an exact transition budget or a leading distortion constant.
The family and its base-model perturbation estimate were introduced in
the source-pinned technical note \cite{A1v6note}. The conclusion here is
the matching, uniform two-parameter law and its online implementation.

\subsubsection{Attainment of the fifth desingularized direction}
\begin{lemma}[A nondegenerate limiting patch]\label{lem:five-patch}
At a common interior three-command tuple the map to $z$ in
\eqref{eq:five-desingularized} has rank five for every $\theta\in[0,1/2]$.
Its all-failure subprobability law dominates a fixed positive multiple of
uniform probability on a translated five-dimensional cube of fixed side
length, uniformly in $\theta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The formal two-fold sum pairs give exponents
\[
 0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 2+\theta,\ 3+\theta,\ 4+2\theta.
\]
There is just one double cluster, at exponent two. Its divided difference
is $(t^{2+\theta}-t^2)/\theta$. The nonconstant orders are
$(0,0,0,0,1)$. The condition in \eqref{eq:full-future-condition} is
$5\le3(3-1)=6$.

For an explicit common attainable tuple take
$c_1=1/64$, $c_2=2/64$, $c_3=3/64$ and failure factors
$(1+c_it^{2+\theta})/2$. They are realized by rejection probabilities
\[
 (1-g_0,1-g_1,1-g_2)
       =(1/2-2c_i,1/2-2c_i,1/2+4c_i),
\]
strictly inside $[1/4,3/4]^3$. At zero their product tangent is the
entire polynomial space of degree at most six. The future confluent
space has basis
\[
 1,\quad t,\quad t^2,\quad t^2\log t,\quad t^3,\quad t^4.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:confluent-positive} makes its mixed pairing with that
tangent surjective. Normalization removes one dimension, not two.
For example, for the uniform prior, the minor with rows $t^i$,
$0\le i\le5$, is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:confluent-exact-minor}
 \det\left[
 \frac1{i+1},\frac1{i+2},\frac1{i+3},
 -\frac1{(i+3)^2},\frac1{i+4},\frac1{i+5}
       \right]_{i=0}^{5}
       =\frac1{338751673344000000}>0.
\end{equation}
This rational identity is only a diagnostic of the preceding all-prior
argument.

For every positive $\theta\le1/2$ the six displayed exponents are distinct
and the binomial tangent has seven distinct exponents. Strict mixed-moment
positivity gives surjectivity throughout this interval. All derivatives
in the desingularized coordinates extend continuously to zero, as proved
in Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-scales}. Compactness and the uniform inverse
construction in Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-patch} therefore apply on the
\emph{whole} interval $[0,1/2]$, not only on an unspecified punctured
neighborhood. The exact change of order in \eqref{eq:five-desingularized}
is a fixed permutation of the general jet coordinates and changes no
rank or minorization conclusion.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Uniform causal quantization}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-streaming}]
At times one and two retain, exactly for the moment, the chronological
normalized factors $f_i/\mu f_i$, with two coefficient coordinates per
factor. At time three change to $w$ in
\eqref{eq:five-physical-coordinates}. At time four retain
$(M_1,M_{2+\theta})$, and at time five use the unique terminal state.
These states are sufficient for the remaining observable future law.
All their reachable sets are compact.

The first two factor sets lie in fixed boxes of dimensions two and four,
uniformly in $\theta$. Indeed report coefficients are uniformly bounded,
$\mu f\ge\kappa$, and the coefficient hyperplane $\mu f=1$ can be
coordinatized by the two nonconstant coefficients, its constant coefficient
then being $1-\sum_{a>0}f_a\mu(t^a)$. The required prior moments are
fixed read-only data for each known calibration. Uniform boundedness of
these affine formulas on $[0,1/2]$ is immediate.

At time three the first four coordinates of $w$ belong to $[0,1]$.
For $0<t\le1$, the fundamental theorem of calculus in the exponent gives
\[
 |t^{2+\theta}-t^2|
   \le\theta t^2|\log t|\le\theta.
\]
This extends to zero. Consequently $-\theta\le w_5\le0$ for every
posterior, with no evidence denominator in this bound. At time four
both retained moments lie in $[0,1]$. Lemma~\ref{lem:rectangle}, with
reachable representatives in nonempty cells, now gives at most $M$
representatives at each time and squared covering errors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stage-errors}
 \delta_1^2\le C_1M^{-1},\quad
 \delta_2^2\le C_2M^{-1/2},\quad
 \delta_3^2\le C_3\Psi_\theta(M),\quad
 \delta_4^2\le C_4M^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The $C_i$ are independent of $\theta$. At zero simply omit the last,
constant coordinate of the third state. Every bound in
\eqref{eq:stage-errors} is at most $C_0\Psi_\theta(M)$.

It remains to verify that repeated updates do not divide by $\theta$.
The factor-to-factor update appends the current normalized factor. The
factor-to-$w$ update uses the product of the two retained factors and the
new report factor. It is a ratio of polynomial coefficient expressions
and bounded prior integrals. Along line segments between factor tuples
every interpolated normalized factor is at least $\kappa$, so the evidence
denominators and derivatives have positive uniform bounds. Each physical
test in $w$ is uniformly bounded; differentiating in factor coordinates
therefore gives a uniform Lipschitz constant for this changeover.

For the only nontrivial shrinking update, write the new report factor as
$f=a+bt+ct^{2+\theta}$. In terms of $w$ set
\[
 Z_f=a+bw_1+c(w_2+w_5).
\]
The next two moments are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-update}
 M'_1=\frac{aw_1+bw_2+cw_3}{Z_f},\qquad
 M'_{2+\theta}=\frac{a(w_2+w_5)+bw_3+cw_4}{Z_f}.
\end{equation}
Coefficients are uniformly bounded and $Z_f\ge\kappa$. A line segment
between reachable $w$ vectors represents a posterior mixture, so the
same lower bound holds along that segment. The quotient rule proves a
uniform Lipschitz estimate in the \emph{physical} $w$ coordinates.
There is no divided difference and no factor $1/\theta$ in
\eqref{eq:uniform-update}. The next, terminal update is constant.

At each time the filter stores only a representative index and applies
the exact transition to that representative and the current input, then
requantizes. A reachable representative followed by any admitted report
has a reachable image, so every quantizer is used inside its domain.
If $e_n$ is the state error in the appropriate Euclidean physical
coordinates, the preceding bounds give
\[
 e_0=0,\qquad e_{n+1}\le L_ne_n+C_0^{1/2}\Psi_\theta(M)^{1/2},
\]
with $L_n$ independent of $\theta$. Iterate this recurrence through the
five times. At times one and two each query prediction is a normalized
polynomial expression in retained factors; at times three and four it
is a fixed linear expression in the retained physical moments. These
prediction maps have uniform Lipschitz constants, again by the positive
evidence bounds. Squaring the resulting prediction error proves the
conditional upper bound at every checkpoint. The representatives and
transition functions are supplied existentially from the exact model,
not synthesized from finite-precision exponent data. Only their index is
persistent mutable memory.

For the lower bound at time three apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:five-patch} and the physical-metric argument in the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:confluent-law}, now to the nine common queries in
\eqref{eq:common-near-probes}. Their coefficient change from any other
fixed spanning basis is invertible before taking products, so its full
product expansion has constant full column rank. Four physical directions
have scale one and the last has scale $\theta$. Lemma~\ref{lem:rectangle}
gives the maximum of the four-dimensional lower bound $cM^{-1/2}$ and
the five-dimensional lower bound $c\theta^{2/5}M^{-2/5}$. The constants
are uniform on $[0,1/2]$. Every streaming filter is an $M$-message encoder
at this time, and the retained all-failure evidence is at least $(1/4)^3$.
The randomization argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:confluent-law} applies
unchanged. Finally, minimax regret is at least the unconditional regret
in this one prescribed experiment. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform bit law and the lower-constant scale]\label{cor:uniform-bits}
For sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, the least persistent bits for
unconditional regret at most $\varepsilon$ in the five-trial protocol,
and likewise for its checkpoint-uniform minimax criterion, satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-bits}
 B_*(\varepsilon,\theta)
   =\log_2\max\{\varepsilon^{-2},\theta\varepsilon^{-5/2}\}+O(1),
\end{equation}
where the $O(1)$ is uniform in $\theta\in[0,1/2]$. If
\[
 c_{\rm opt}(\theta)=\inf_{M\in\N,\,M\ge1}
           M^{2/5}\overline{\mathcal R}_{M,5}^{\theta,\mathrm{stream}},
\]
then $c_{\rm opt}(\theta)\asymp\theta^{2/5}$ for $0<\theta\le1/2$,
with constants independent of $\theta$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Solve the two necessary and sufficient inequalities from
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-streaming} for $M$, obtaining, up to constant
factors, $M\ge\varepsilon^{-2}$ and $M\ge\theta\varepsilon^{-5/2}$.
Rounding to $M=2^B$ changes $B$ by a bounded amount. The same bounds
apply to the minimax criterion. The lower bound for $c_{\rm opt}$ follows
from the five-dimensional term. For its upper bound let $M$ tend to
infinity in the matching upper estimate, so
$M^{-1/10}$ disappears. No limiting leading quantization constant is
needed.
\end{proof}

The exact profiles remain $(0,2,4,4,2,0)$ at zero and
$(0,2,4,5,2,0)$ at positive $\theta$. The new assertion is that the extra
direction is resolved at distortion scale $\theta^2$ and state-budget
scale $\theta^{-4}$, with matching estimates on both sides. It sharpens
the base-model bound $C(M^{-1/2}+\theta^2)$ without contradicting it.


\subsection{The value of a resolved direction in one sequential decision}\label{sec:resolved-value}
We next use the same exploration histories, the same two-step query menu
and the same eventual Bernoulli event as in Section~\ref{sec:uniform}.
Only the terminal decision is changed. This gives a common-payoff
comparison between keeping and erasing the newly resolved direction,
rather than comparing two separately normalized prediction tasks.

\subsubsection{An independently priced observable ticket}
After the time-three state has been formed, reveal the query $J$ and a
price $U$, independent and uniform on $[0,1]$. The controller chooses a
binary action $A\in\{0,1\}$. Execute the two query trials and let $Y$
be the same all-failure indicator as before. The payoff is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ticket-payoff}
 A(Y-U).
\end{equation}
The price and the query are new inputs, not historical storage. The
payoff belongs to $[-1,1]$ and depends only on observable apparatus
outcomes and the chosen action. Accepting the ticket changes the payoff,
not the detector law or the five-trial budget. The exploration command
program is prescribed; it is not optimized by the controller.

Let $p_j(h)=\Prob(Y=1\mid h,J=j)$. Write $V_*^\theta$ for the
full-history optimal expected payoff and $V_M^\theta$ for the supremum
over all $M$-state streaming policies, including randomized transitions
and terminal actions. At the terminal decision the policy reads only its
index, the new query and the new price.

\begin{proposition}[Exact decision--prediction identity]\label{prop:ticket-identity}
For every fixed streaming encoder with index $I$, its optimal terminal
rule accepts exactly when $U\le\E[p_J(h)\mid I,J]$. Its value gap is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ticket-identity}
 V_*^\theta-V^\theta(I)
   =\frac1{2s}\sum_{j=1}^s
        \E\left[(p_j(h)-\E[p_j(h)\mid I])^2\right],\qquad s=9.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ticket-streaming-law}
 c\Psi_\theta(M)\le V_*^\theta-V_M^\theta\le C\Psi_\theta(M)
\end{equation}
with positive constants independent of $M$ and $\theta\in[0,1/2]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on the full history and $J=j,U=u$, action one has expected
payoff $p_j-u$ and action zero has payoff zero. The optimum is the
threshold rule and its average over price is
$\int_0^{p_j}(p_j-u)\dd u=p_j^2/2$. Given only an index, replace
$p_j$ by $\bar p_{I,j}=\E[p_j\mid I]$, since price and query are
independent of the prefix and encoder. A randomized action is a convex
combination of the two payoffs and cannot improve the threshold optimum.
Thus
\[
 V_*^\theta=\frac1{2s}\sum_j\E p_j^2,
 \qquad V^\theta(I)=\frac1{2s}\sum_j\E\bar p_{I,j}^2.
\]
Conditional-variance decomposition gives \eqref{eq:ticket-identity}.
Conditional expectation is also the optimal squared-loss decoder for
the same encoder. Taking the infimum over the same streaming encoders
therefore makes the optimal value gap exactly one half the optimal
prediction regret. Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-streaming} proves
\eqref{eq:ticket-streaming-law}. The upper implementation may also
threshold the representative's prediction directly: conditional on a
history, integrating the mistaken decisions over the interval between
that prediction and $p_j$ gives half their squared difference.
\end{proof}

This proposition is a decision identity, not a new general comparison
principle. Its use here is to retain the entire resolved streaming law
in a bounded, binary-action task and then compare a specific erased
observation in that \emph{same} task.

\subsubsection{Erasing the weak coordinate}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jet-erasure}
 T_\theta(h)=(M_1,M_2,M_{3+\theta},M_{4+2\theta})
\end{equation}
be the first four coordinates of \eqref{eq:five-physical-coordinates}.
This is a deterministic, calibration-dependent postprocessing of the
history. It is not a coarsening of a raw one-step detector symbol, and it
is not claimed to be the best possible four-dimensional encoding.
Let $V_T^\theta$ be the optimal payoff of a terminal policy given the
\emph{entire exact} statistic $T_\theta$, with no memory restriction,
and then the same $J,U$. This comparison gives the erased controller
more computational and storage power than any $M$-message compression
of $T_\theta$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform value of the weak direction]\label{thm:resolved-value}
There are constants $c_e,C_e,K>0$, depending on the fixed prior and
protocol but independent of $0<\theta\le1/2$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:erasure-value}
 c_e\theta^2\le V_*^\theta-V_T^\theta\le C_e\theta^2.
\end{equation}
Moreover, whenever $M\ge K\theta^{-4}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-state-advantage}
 V_M^\theta-V_T^\theta\ge\tfrac12c_e\theta^2>0.
\end{equation}
In particular an $M$-state streaming policy beats every policy whose only
historical input is an $M$-message compression of $T_\theta$, even though
$V_T^\theta$ allows that comparator uncompressed access to the four
coordinates. Both values concern the same actual calibration, exploration
law, query, price, target, payoff and five-trial budget. At $\theta=0$ the
statistic $T_0$ is sufficient and $V_*^0=V_T^0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $Z_\theta=w_5/\theta$ for positive $\theta$, with its continuous
value at zero as in \eqref{eq:five-desingularized}. The common product
query expansion has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:query-linear-weak}
 p_j(h)=a_j+\ell_j(T_\theta(h))+\theta b_jZ_\theta(h),
\end{equation}
where $a_j,b_j$ and the linear forms $\ell_j$ are independent of
$\theta$. They are obtained by expanding the two factors in
\eqref{eq:common-near-probes} into the six formal monomials and replacing
$M_{2+\theta}$ by $M_2+\theta Z_\theta$. The full coefficient matrix
has rank six, as proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:observable-scales}. Hence
$\sum_jb_j^2>0$; otherwise its weak column would be zero.

Apply the conditional-variance identity from
Proposition~\ref{prop:ticket-identity} to the sigma-algebra generated by
$T_\theta$. With $b_*=(2s)^{-1}\sum_jb_j^2>0$ one obtains the exact
formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:erasure-exact}
 V_*^\theta-V_T^\theta
       =b_*\theta^2\E\Var(Z_\theta\mid T_\theta).
\end{equation}
The random variable $Z_\theta$ is bounded uniformly, since
$|(t^{2+\theta}-t^2)/\theta|\le t^2|\log t|\le1$ before taking
any posterior expectation. Thus the conditional variance is uniformly
bounded above.

For the lower bound, Lemma~\ref{lem:five-patch} gives a fixed positive
subprobability multiple $\beta$ of uniform measure on a translated cube
of side $2\rho$ in the coordinates $(T_\theta,Z_\theta)$. For every
Borel function $v$ of the first four coordinates,
\[
 \E|Z_\theta-v(T_\theta)|^2
 \ge\beta\int|z-v(t)|^2\dd\operatorname{Unif}_{\text{cube}}(t,z)
 \ge\beta\rho^2/3.
\]
The last inequality integrates the variance of the independent uniform
last coordinate; it is unaffected by the translation. Taking the infimum
over $v$ proves
$\E\Var(Z_\theta\mid T_\theta)\ge\beta\rho^2/3$.
This argument uses domination of an actual distribution, not an assumed
uniform posterior on a formal tangent space. Equation
\eqref{eq:erasure-exact} proves \eqref{eq:erasure-value} with uniform
positive constants.

By \eqref{eq:ticket-streaming-law},
\[
 V_M^\theta-V_T^\theta
 \ge c_e\theta^2-C\max\{M^{-1/2},\theta^{2/5}M^{-2/5}\}.
\]
For $M\ge K\theta^{-4}$ the maximum on the right is at most
$\theta^2\max\{K^{-1/2},K^{-2/5}\}$. Choose $K\ge1$ large enough
that $C\max\{K^{-1/2},K^{-2/5}\}\le c_e/2$. This proves
\eqref{eq:finite-state-advantage}. A policy observing only a compression
of $T_\theta$ cannot outperform the one observing all of $T_\theta$:
conditional on that statistic its randomization is independent of the
latent history, and averaging produces an admissible randomized
$T_\theta$-policy. Finally at zero the weak column in
\eqref{eq:query-linear-weak} vanishes, proving sufficiency and equality
of the full and erased values.
\end{proof}

The absolute advantage vanishes quadratically as the exponents collide.
What remains nondegenerate is the advantage after normalization by the
actual weak-direction scale $\theta^2$, once the state budget resolves
that scale. The theorem is not a lower bound for arbitrary exploration
objectives. It is an optimal-policy comparison in one prescribed
sequential acquisition experiment, with no change of Bayes baseline and
no substitution of the separate mechanical sign experiment.


\begin{corollary}[Necessary and sufficient budget order]\label{cor:necessary-budget}
Define
\[
 M_{\rm beat}(\theta)=\min\{M\in\N:V_M^\theta>V_T^\theta\}.
\]
For $0<\theta\le1/2$ this minimum exists and
$M_{\rm beat}(\theta)\asymp\theta^{-4}$, with uniform constants.
More explicitly, for some $0<k<K<\infty$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 1\le M\le k\theta^{-4}&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 V_M^\theta-V_T^\theta\le-C_e\theta^2<0,\\
 M\ge K\theta^{-4}&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 V_M^\theta-V_T^\theta\ge\tfrac12c_e\theta^2>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first assertion is vacuous if its integer range is empty.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write $E_\theta=V_*^\theta-V_T^\theta$ and
$L_M^\theta=V_*^\theta-V_M^\theta$. Theorems already proved give
$E_\theta\le C_e\theta^2$ and $L_M^\theta\ge c_sM^{-1/2}$.
Their exact difference uses the same experiment and baseline, hence
\[
 V_M^\theta-V_T^\theta=E_\theta-L_M^\theta
             \le C_e\theta^2-c_sM^{-1/2}.
\]
Choose $k=(c_s/(2C_e))^2$, decreasing it if necessary so that $k<K$.
The first inequality follows. The second is
Theorem~\ref{thm:resolved-value}, with $K$ increased if needed.
The admissible filter classes are nested as $M$ increases because extra
states can be unused. Thus the first-crossing budget exists; the two
bounds and integer rounding give its stated order.
\end{proof}

This consequence was extracted in the source-pinned v7 referee technical
note \cite{A1v7note}. It concerns beating the exact, uncompressed
$T_\theta$ comparator. It does not locate an exact integer transition,
and it does not assert equality of the full and erased experiments at
smaller \emph{equal compressed} budgets. At zero $T_0$ is sufficient,
so no finite-state policy has value exceeding $V_T^0=V_*^0$.


\subsection{A collision bit at a common finite-memory budget}\label{sec:common-risk}
This section compares total risks for one fixed target, not regrets against
two different Bayes forecasters. It concerns an uncensored raw detector,
so that erasure is literal postprocessing. It does not identify this operation
with erasure \emph{before} a commanded comparator.

\subsubsection{The counted mechanical realization}
Let the triangular lattice have fundamental area
$\mathfrak a=\sqrt3/2$, let $I_R=[9/20,47/100]$, and put a reflecting
disk of radius $R$ at each lattice point. In each cartridge prepare a
uniform position in the ambient torus, a uniform velocity direction,
and independent uniform apparatus coordinates. A guarded insertion fails
when the position is inside a disk. Otherwise a unit-speed particle is
released. Every attempted insertion consumes one cartridge.

Fix $0<T<3/50$ and $\epsilon>0$ with
$\epsilon(47/100)^2<1$. Distinct lifted disks have separation at least
$3/50$; an outgoing reflected ray cannot reenter the same convex disk
before another collision. Thus each released particle has at most one
collision by time $T$. At a collision let $z$ be its impact coordinate
relative to the disk center. The fixed detector reports $+$ when its
prepared uniform coordinate is at most
$1/2+\epsilon|z|^2/\pi$, and otherwise reports $-$. The controller
never receives $z$ or the uniform seed. This is an internally exact-coordinate
apparatus, not a lower bound on the complexity of physical measurement.

For completeness, incoming one-collision states are parameterized by
\[
 q=Rn_\alpha-sv^-,\quad
 v^-=-\cos\phi\,n_\alpha+\sin\phi\,t_\alpha,
 \quad 0<s<T,\quad |\phi|<\pi/2.
\]
The backward segment remains outside its own disk by convexity and cannot
reach a distinct disk within $T$ by separation. The forward reflected
segment has the same exclusion. Every regular colliding initial state
has its unique impact triple. At fixed incoming laboratory angle the
position Jacobian is $R\cos\phi$, and the angular coordinate change
has determinant of absolute value one. Integration against ambient
preparation $\dd q\,\dd\vartheta/(2\pi\mathfrak a)$ gives collision
mass
\[
 \frac{R}{2\pi\mathfrak a}
      \int_0^T\int_0^{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}
                       \cos\phi\,\dd\phi\,\dd\alpha\,\dd s
 =\frac{2TR}{\mathfrak a}.
\]
Tangencies are null, not an excluded positive-mass neighborhood.
Since $|z|=R$ at impact, the four uncensored raw probabilities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mechanical-cells}
 \begin{split}
 k_\partial&=\pi R^2/\mathfrak a,\qquad
 k_0=1-\pi R^2/\mathfrak a-2TR/\mathfrak a,\\
 k_\pm&=TR/\mathfrak a\ \pm 2T\epsilon R^3/(\pi\mathfrak a).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
They are positive on $I_R$ and sum to one. For $k_0$, positivity follows
already at $R=47/100$, $T=3/50$, using $\mathfrak a>0.86$ and
$\pi<22/7$; the other inequalities follow from the stated bounds.
Their coefficient matrix in degrees zero through three is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&1&0&0\\
 0&-2T/\mathfrak a&T/\mathfrak a&T/\mathfrak a\\
 \pi/\mathfrak a&-\pi/\mathfrak a&0&0\\
 0&0&2T\epsilon/(\pi\mathfrak a)&-2T\epsilon/(\pi\mathfrak a)
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
with determinant of absolute value $4T^2\epsilon/\mathfrak a^3$.
A bounded lookup comparator therefore realizes $A=\{0,1,2,3\}$.
Merging the signs gives the three probabilities
$k_\partial,k_0,k_++k_-$, which span $A=\{0,1,2\}$.
This supplies the dense special cases of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from
one counted apparatus. The stronger complete-record response, positive
filter, boundary-control and surrogate derivations are preserved in the
source companion \cite{A1v5}; the collision calculation above uses their
whole-preparation normalization rather than conditioning away insertion
failures.

\subsubsection{One target and all state budgets}
Consume two cartridges without censoring. Let $X\in\{\partial,0,+,-\}$
be the first raw report, and let $Y$ be the placement-failure indicator
on the second cartridge. After the first report the controller stores
one of $M$ states, then announces its forecast $a\in[0,1]$ and incurs
$(a-Y)^2$. The erased experiment observes
$e(X)\in\{\partial,0,H\}$ with $e(+)=e(-)=H$.
Both experiments use the same prior, both ambient attempts, and the same
second-cartridge variable $Y$. There is no acceptance charge or saved
cartridge. This is a separate uncensored experiment; it is not subject
to the lower acceptance margin of the streaming theorem.

Put $h(R)=\pi R^2/\mathfrak a$ and define
\[
 p_x=\mu k_x>0,\quad z_x=\mu(k_xh),\quad b_x=z_x/p_x,
 \qquad p_B=\sum_{x\in B}p_x,\quad z_B=\sum_{x\in B}z_x.
\]
For a nonempty subset $B$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:merge-cost}
 \delta(B)=\sum_{x\in B}\frac{z_x^2}{p_x}-\frac{z_B^2}{p_B}
 =\frac1{p_B}\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset B}p_xp_y(b_x-b_y)^2.
\end{equation}
All these quantities are finite combinations of prior moments through
order five and the exact physical constants.

\begin{theorem}[Complete common-risk finite-state comparison]\label{thm:finite-bit-value}
For either alphabet $\mathcal X$ (four raw symbols or three erased tags),
its optimal $M$-state risk, allowing independent randomization, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition-risk}
 R_M(\mathcal X)=\mu h-
  \max_{\substack{\mathcal Q\text{ partition of }\mathcal X\\|\mathcal Q|\le M}}
             \sum_{B\in\mathcal Q}\frac{z_B^2}{p_B}.
\end{equation}
A maximizing partition, its block index and block forecasts $z_B/p_B$
give an attained finite-state implementation.

Let $R_M^+$ and $R_M^0$ denote the raw and erased risks and
$\Gamma_M=R_M^0-R_M^+$. Then $\Gamma_M\ge0$ for every $M$,
$\Gamma_1=0$, and, with $\Delta=\delta(\{+,-\})$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-budgets}
 \Gamma_3=\Delta-\min_{x\ne y}\delta(\{x,y\}),\qquad
 \Gamma_M=\Delta\quad(M\ge4).
\end{equation}
The case $M=2$ is exactly \eqref{eq:partition-risk}: it compares the seven
two-block raw partitions and the three two-block erased partitions
(one-block partitions are dominated). Equivalently, if
$D_M^+$ and $D_M^0$ are the minimum sums of merge costs in the two
alphabets, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-decomposition}
 \Gamma_M=\Delta+D_M^0-D_M^+.
\end{equation}
These are common \emph{total} Bayes risks, not two separately normalized
memory regrets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on a block message, the optimal forecast is $z_B/p_B$.
Expanding squared loss gives block contribution
$z_B-z_B^2/p_B$; summing proves the risk of every deterministic partition.
For arbitrary random encoders and decoders, first replace a randomized
forecast at each message by its conditional mean. Holding these at most
$M$ forecasts fixed, each raw symbol can be assigned deterministically to
a forecast minimizing its conditional squared loss. This cannot increase
risk. Reoptimizing each resulting block forecast also cannot increase risk.
Thus a deterministic partition is optimal, and the finite maximum is
attained. A fresh public seed independent of $X,Y$ merely averages such
risks and does not improve the optimum.

The identity \eqref{eq:merge-cost} is obtained by expanding the weighted
variance. The lossless raw risk is $\mu h-\sum_x z_x^2/p_x$; the
lossless erased risk exceeds it by $\Delta$. Adding the optimal merging
distortion in each alphabet gives \eqref{eq:common-decomposition}.
Any erased code is a raw code after the deterministic map $e$, proving
$\Gamma_M\ge0$ directly. With one state both forecasts equal $\mu h$.
With at least four states both alphabets are losslessly encoded. With
three states the erased alphabet is lossless, and an optimal raw partition
can be refined to exactly three blocks without increasing risk. Such a
partition merges exactly one pair. This proves \eqref{eq:all-budgets}.
The partition counts for $M=2$ are the numbers of nontrivial subsets
modulo complementation, namely $(2^4-2)/2=7$ and $(2^3-2)/2=3$.
\end{proof}

Write $\mu_j=\mu(R^j)$, let
$\mu_H(\dd R)=R\mu(\dd R)/\mu_1$, and put
\[
 v=\Var_{\mu_H}(R^2),\qquad
 \bar c=1/2+(\epsilon/\pi)\mu_H(R^2).
\]
The exact lossless gain retained from \cite{A1v5} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical-gain}
 \Delta=\frac{2T\mu_1}{\mathfrak a}
    \frac{(\epsilon v/\mathfrak a)^2}{\bar c(1-\bar c)}>0.
\end{equation}
Indeed conditional on a first hit the sign probability is
$c(R)=1/2+\epsilon R^2/\pi$, and
$\Cov_{\mu_H}(h,c)=\epsilon v/\mathfrak a$.
The two conditional-mean shifts are respectively this covariance divided
by $\bar c$ and its negative divided by $1-\bar c$.
Their weighted squared sum is the covariance squared over
$\bar c(1-\bar c)$. Multiplication by the hit probability proves
\eqref{eq:physical-gain}, which is also precisely the pair cost
$\delta(\{+,-\})$. Full support makes $v>0$. This is an explicit
conditional-variance calculation, not a new general comparison-of-experiments
principle \cite{Blackwell}.

\begin{corollary}[A genuine finite-resolution threshold]\label{cor:threshold}
Fix $T>0$ and a full-support prior on $I_R$. There exists
$\epsilon_*>0$ such that, for $0<\epsilon<\epsilon_*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold}
 \Gamma_M=0\quad(M=1,2,3),\qquad
 \Gamma_M=\Delta>0\quad(M\ge4).
\end{equation}
Thus a four-state, two-bit controller realizes the strict gain, whereas
all smaller state budgets have exactly zero gain in this amplitude range.
This does not identify exact algebraic dimension with useful accuracy.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At $\epsilon=0$, $b_+=b_-=b_H$. The three distinct tag means satisfy
\[
 b_0<\mu h<b_H<b_\partial.
\]
The first strict inequality follows because $k_0$ is strictly decreasing
and $h$ strictly increasing, so $\Cov_\mu(k_0,h)<0$.
For example this sign follows by integrating
$(k_0(R)-k_0(R'))(h(R)-h(R'))/2$ against two independent prior draws.
The second inequality uses the positive covariance of $R$ and $h$.
The last follows in the hit-tilted prior from the same positive covariance
of $R$ and $h$, since the placement posterior is its additional $R$-tilt.
Full support makes all these covariances strict.

For three states the unique zero-distortion merging pattern at amplitude
zero is to keep the three distinct tag means separate and merge the two
signs. Every other pair has positive merge cost. For two states, no optimal
partition at amplitude zero splits the equal-mean signs between its two
blocks. To prove this, use the optimal block means as centers. Equal-mean
points assigned to different centers must be equidistant from them;
otherwise reassignment to the closer center strictly improves risk.
If the centers are distinct, $b_H$ is their midpoint. Each block must
then contain a different extreme tag to place its mean strictly on the
corresponding side of $b_H$. Move one sign into the other block, holding
centers fixed; the cost is unchanged. The vacated-sign block still contains
its extreme tag, so reoptimizing its center strictly reduces the cost,
a contradiction. If the two centers agree, the achieved risk is the
one-state risk, strictly worse than separating one extreme tag. Thus an
optimal two-state partition never splits the signs.

There are finitely many partitions. At amplitude zero every partition
that splits the signs and uses at most two states has a strictly positive
risk gap to the optimum, as does every non-sign-merging three-state
partition. All their risks depend continuously on $\epsilon$ because
the cell masses stay positive. Therefore, for a sufficiently small common
positive $\epsilon_*$, none of these partitions is optimal for $M=2,3$.
Every remaining partition keeps the signs together and has exactly the
risk of the corresponding erased partition, because both $p_++p_-$
and $z_++z_-$ are independent of $\epsilon$. This proves equality of
risks for $M=2,3$; $M=1$ and $M\ge4$ follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-bit-value} and \eqref{eq:physical-gain}.
\end{proof}

The threshold is checkable from finitely many moment expressions: enumerate
the partitions in \eqref{eq:partition-risk}, find their positive gaps at
zero, and bound their continuous rational derivatives while cell masses
stay bounded away from zero. No prior-uniform or horizon-uniform threshold
is claimed. In particular \eqref{eq:physical-gain} can be extremely small
for a concentrated prior. The threshold theorem gives an exact resource
comparison, not a promise of a practically large gain.
