# Theorem-level literature comparison — Revision 74

Primary sources checked on 4 October 2026. This is a targeted author-side comparison, not exhaustive independent priority clearance. Earlier comparisons remain in `predecessor-v73-audit/LITERATURE_AUDIT.md` and the inherited audit directories.

## Sedlak–Ziman: the directly relevant noisy single-shot antecedent

M. Sedlak and M. Ziman, *Optimal single-shot strategies for discrimination of quantum measurements*, Physical Review A 90 (2014), 052312, arXiv:1408.0934; https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.0934.

Section VI, Example 4, equation (37), explicitly considers projective qubit measurements mixed with white noise, with two possibly different noise parameters. Its reduction optimizes a single-shot measurement-discrimination problem through state discrimination, including the fixed-failure formulation. It is therefore an antecedent for unequal visibility, not merely for equal-visibility circles. This revision adds a finite-N joint metric comparison and family-covering/contact theorem; it does not claim discovery of the noisy single-shot family or its state-discrimination reduction. A single-shot reduction is not, without further proof, one global processor and seizer for all N adaptive uses.

## Puchala–Pawela–Krawiec–Kukulski: single-shot projective distance

Z. Puchala, L. Pawela, A. Krawiec and R. Kukulski, *Strategies for optimal single-shot discrimination of quantum measurements*, Physical Review A 98 (2018), 042103, arXiv:1804.05856; https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05856.

Theorem 1, equation (19), identifies the diamond distance of two von Neumann measurements with an optimization over diagonal phases of a unitary-channel distance. It is a single-shot theorem for projective measurements. The v72 multiple-shot result cited separately from Quantum 5 (2021), 425 has different quantifiers. Neither is invoked as an exact formula for the noisy ball. The new joint theorem gives a uniform comparison, not an exact adaptive optimization formula.

## Established ingredients and new assembly

Classical simulation/programme contraction, noisy coherent amplification, trace-distance/fidelity comparison, binomial likelihood-ratio monotonicity, metric nets, Stieltjes integration and stereographic rational coordinates are established tools. Their earlier primary references remain in the active bibliography.

The new inference chain is:

    exact radial programme equality + finite support-cutoff product estimate
      + inherited uniform angular modulus + monotone binomial test
    -> cancellation-free joint finite-use metric on the unbiased measurement ball
    -> small operational balls with boundary-dependent Euclidean radii
    -> weighted covering criterion on regular identifiable subsets
    -> contact trichotomy, critical logarithm, and joint disk/ball payloads.

The constructive addition accepts rational Cartesian vectors whose length need not be rational. An exact sign-before-square comparator and rational radial/axis charts produce one codebook charging all target coordinates. No optimal-code novelty is attributed to the chart formula itself.
