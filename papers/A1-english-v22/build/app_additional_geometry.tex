\subsection{A circular experiment with vanishing contrast}
\label{sec:circular}
We now give a second application in which the one-step detector itself
loses contrast. The hidden parameter lies on a circle, the posterior
pairing is Fourier rather than ordered Chebyshev, and acquisition and
future observation each contribute a small factor. Consequently its
resolution scales are not obtained by attaching a new observation
matrix to a fixed, nondegenerate attainable posterior cube.

\subsubsection{The physical experiment}
Write $w=e^{i\theta}$, $0\le\theta<2\pi$, and take normalized Haar
measure $d\theta/(2\pi)$ as prior. Fix $0<\eta<1/2$. The known
contrast is $0\le\tau\le1/2$. Four detector cells are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-cells}
 k_{1,\pm}(\theta)=\tfrac14(1\pm\tau\cos\theta),\qquad
 k_{2,\pm}(\theta)=\tfrac14(1\pm\tau\sin\theta).
\end{equation}
The controller specifies four real acceptance probabilities varying
continuously over $[\eta,1-\eta]^4$. Thus the command alphabet is
continuous, although the detector and report alphabets are finite. Reports and failures are as in
\eqref{eq:channel}. All five report likelihoods are bounded below by
$\eta/8$. The prior and calibration in this section are known exactly;
no prior-uncertainty assertion is included in the result.

Every report factor has a unique representation, for $\tau>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-factor}
 f(\theta)=a\{1+\tau(z w+\bar z w^{-1})\},
 \qquad a>0,\quad |z|\le\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
The coefficients $a,z$ extend continuously to $\tau=0$ using the
specified command and report. For accepted cells, $z$ is respectively
$1/2,-1/2,-i/2,i/2$. For failures, with rejection probabilities $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-command-coordinates}
 a=\tfrac14\sum_j u_j,\qquad
 z=\frac{u_{1,+}-u_{1,-}-i(u_{2,+}-u_{2,-})}{8a}.
\end{equation}
The bound on $|z|$ follows by the triangle inequality and $u_j\ge0$.
Near the central command, $z$ ranges over a fixed complex neighborhood
of zero, independently of $\tau$. More explicitly, the rejection vector
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-right-inverse}
 (u_{1,+},u_{1,-},u_{2,+},u_{2,-})
 =\bigl(\tfrac12+2\Re z,\tfrac12-2\Re z,
        \tfrac12-2\Im z,\tfrac12+2\Im z\bigr)
\end{equation}
realizes $a=1/2$ whenever all entries are in the command cube.
Thus the construction uses a fixed finite interface, not a gate that
reads the hidden angle.

Fix $0<\rho<(1/2-\eta)/8$. For an $m$-trial query put
$L_m=2m+1$ and $\varphi_l=2\pi l/L_m$, $0\le l<L_m$.
Query $l$ repeats the attainable failure factor with
$a=1/2$, $z=\rho e^{-i\varphi_l}$ exactly $m$ times, and asks for
the probability of all failures. Only the selected query is executed;
it is selected uniformly and independently after the memory index has
been formed. Independent uniform acquisition commands define the
unconditional experiment. Worst-history and causal losses have the same
meaning as in Definition~\ref{def:finite-state}, with this query menu.

\begin{theorem}[Harmonic resolution from acquisition and observation]
\label{thm:circle-resolution}
Fix $N\ge2$ and the interface above. There is $\tau_*>0$, depending
only on $N,\eta,\rho$, such that the following holds uniformly for
$0\le\tau\le\tau_*$ and all integers $M\ge1$. At a checkpoint
$n+m\le N$, $n,m\ge1$, put $k=\min(n,m)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-profile}
 \mathcal H_{n,m}(M,\tau)
 =\max_{1\le j\le k}\tau^{2(j+1)}M^{-1/j}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-checkpoint-law}
 c\mathcal H_{n,m}\le\overline R^{\tau}_{M;n,m}
 \le R^{\tau,\max}_{M;n,m}\le C\mathcal H_{n,m}.
\end{equation}
For horizon $N$, one deterministic, stage-compatible $M$-state transducer
has worst-history loss at every checkpoint bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-causal-profile}
 C\max_{1\le j\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor}
          \tau^{2(j+1)}M^{-1/j}.
\end{equation}
Every such transducer has maximum unconditional checkpoint loss at least
a fixed positive multiple of the same expression. The lower laws permit
independent coding randomization. For $\tau>0$ in this interval the
minimal continuous sufficient checkpoint dimension is $2\min(n,m)$;
for $\tau=0$ it is zero.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Information convention and comparison.}
The Fourier representation used below is the established Bayesian
representation discussed in Section~\ref{sec:fourier-filtering-comparison}.
The resource restriction is instead a persistent index in an alphabet
of size at most $M$. The prior, contrast, horizon, and interface may
enter a read-only program; a transition receives only its preceding
index and the current command and report. In particular, an exact
Fourier vector is not charged as one label. The causal assertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-resolution} uses this convention, at fixed
$N$ and known contrast; it is not an effective numerical compiler.

For orientation, consider one past trial and one future trial. If the
past factor is $a\{1+\tau(zw+\bar z w^{-1})\}$, Haar normalization
gives $c_1=\tau z$. With the query parameter $\rho$ and phases
$\varphi_l$ defined above, direct multiplication gives
\[
 q_l(z)=\tfrac12+\rho\tau^2\Re(ze^{i\varphi_l}),\qquad
 \frac13\sum_{l=0}^{2}|q_l(z)-q_l(z')|^2
       =\tfrac12\rho^2\tau^4|z-z'|^2.
\]
The two contrast factors are already visible in this elementary case.
The higher-dimensional result requires more: the normalized flags must
be excited by a set of acquisition histories with uniformly positive
subprobability, the global image must admit a matching cover, and the
successive covers must be compatible with the weighted Bayesian update.
The next two lemmas and the proof of the theorem establish these
properties with the actual evidence retained. This computation is an
illustration of the theorem, not a separate improvement of its law.


\subsubsection{Acquisition: a symmetric-polynomial submersion}
Remove the positive scalar factors $a_i$, which cancel from the posterior,
and expand the Laurent polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-laurent}
 P(w)=\prod_{i=1}^n\{1+\tau(z_iw+\bar z_iw^{-1})\}
     =\sum_{j=-n}^{n}p_jw^j,
 \qquad p_{-j}=\bar p_j.
\end{equation}
Haar integration extracts $p_0$, and $p_0\ge(1-\tau)^n$.
For $j\ge0$, each term contributing to $p_j$ chooses $j+h$ positive
and $h$ negative powers from distinct factors. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-coefficient-factorization}
 p_j=\tau^j P_{n,j}(z,\bar z;\tau^2),\qquad
 P_{n,j}(z,\bar z;0)=e_j(z_1,\ldots,z_n),
\end{equation}
where $e_j$ is the elementary symmetric polynomial and $e_0=1$.
This is an exact polynomial identity. Define
\[
 S_j=\frac{P_{n,j}(z,\bar z;\tau^2)}{P_{n,0}(z,\bar z;\tau^2)},
 \qquad c_j=\frac{p_j}{p_0}=\tau^jS_j,
 \qquad Y_j=\tau^jc_j=\tau^{2j}S_j.
\]
Set these coordinates to zero for $j>n$. The $c_j$ are the posterior
Fourier coefficients, or equivalently the posterior expectations of
$w^{-j}$. The $S_j$ and their first two command derivatives are uniformly
bounded on the command cube and $0\le\tau\le1/2$, for $n\le N$.
Indeed the numerator polynomials have finitely many bounded coefficients,
while their common denominator is at least $2^{-N}$; also $a\ge\eta$
for failures. These observations justify the assertion even at zero
contrast without division by $\tau$.

\begin{lemma}[Attainment of the normalized harmonic flag]
\label{lem:circle-attainment}
There are $\tau_*>0$ and, for every $n\le N$, one interior
all-failure command tuple independent of $\tau$, at which
$(S_1,\ldots,S_k):\R^{4n}\to\C^k$ has real rank $2k$ for all
$k\le n$ and $0\le\tau\le\tau_*$. The least row singular values
are uniformly positive. Under independent uniform commands the
all-failure subprobability law of these $2k$ real coordinates minorizes
uniform probability on a translated cube of fixed side and fixed mass.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose distinct small positive real numbers $\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_N$
within the interior neighborhood in \eqref{eq:circle-right-inverse}.
Use the first $n$ as the $z_i$ at length $n$. At $\tau=0$ the map
$(S_1,\ldots,S_n)$ is $(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$. Its complex differential
is invertible at a tuple of distinct numbers. To verify this directly,
if all differential coefficients of $\prod_i(\lambda+z_i)$ vanish,
then
\[
 \sum_i \dot z_i\prod_{h\ne i}(\lambda+\zeta_h)=0.
\]
Evaluating at $\lambda=-\zeta_i$ gives $\dot z_i=0$ for every $i$.
The corresponding real differential on $\C^n$ is therefore invertible.
Every initial collection of $k$ complex rows has real rank $2k$.
The command map onto $z$ is a submersion at the implementing tuples by
\eqref{eq:circle-command-coordinates}--\eqref{eq:circle-right-inverse}.

Continuity in $s=\tau^2$ now preserves all these lower singular-value
bounds on one interval $[0,\tau_*]$: there are only finitely many
$n,k\le N$. For precision about this choice, let $\sigma_0$ be the
minimum of the finitely many positive central row singular values at
zero. The rational formulas give a finite $C_*$ with
$\|D S(\tau)-D S(0)\|\le C_*\tau^2$ at the chosen tuples.
One can take the minimum of $1/2$ and
$\sqrt{\sigma_0/(2\max\{C_*,1\})}$. No assertion of a large-contrast
uniform interval is needed for this small-contrast theorem.
The first two derivative bounds established above, the fixed interior
margin, and $f\ge\eta/8$ verify the quantitative hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:product-criterion}. Its change-of-variables
argument applies directly to the maps $S$, including their continuous
extension at zero, and proves the stated minorization. The actual
failure evidence is $p_0\prod_i a_i$; it has not been replaced by one.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Observation: a finite Fourier menu}
\begin{lemma}[The physical query metric]\label{lem:circle-query-metric}
For histories $h,h'$ at the same checkpoint, let $q_l(h)$ denote the
probability of query $l$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-parseval}
 \frac1{2m+1}\sum_{l=0}^{2m}|q_l(h)-q_l(h')|^2
 =2^{1-2m}\sum_{j=1}^{m} B_{m,j}(\tau^2)^2
                       |Y_j(h)-Y_j(h')|^2,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-B}
 B_{m,j}(s)=\sum_{h=0}^{\lfloor(m-j)/2\rfloor}
 \frac{m!\,\rho^{j+2h}s^h}{(j+h)!h!(m-j-2h)!},\qquad 0\le j\le m.
\end{equation}
For $j\ge1$ these factors are bounded above and away from zero uniformly
on $0\le\tau\le1/2$. Thus the physical loss metric is uniformly
equivalent to Euclidean distance in $(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expanding the repeated query factor, its coefficient of $w^j$ is
$2^{-m}\tau^jB_{m,j}(\tau^2)e^{-ij\varphi_l}$. Multiplication by
the posterior density and Haar integration therefore give
\[
 q_l(h)=2^{-m}B_{m,0}(\tau^2)+
       2^{1-m}\Re\sum_{j=1}^{m}B_{m,j}(\tau^2)Y_j(h)e^{ij\varphi_l}.
\]
The discrete identity
$(2m+1)^{-1}\sum_l e^{ia\varphi_l}=0$ for
$0<|a|\le2m$ makes the distinct sine and cosine columns orthogonal,
with squared norm $1/2$. This proves \eqref{eq:circle-parseval}.
All coefficients in \eqref{eq:circle-B} are nonnegative, and its
constant term is $\binom mj\rho^j>0$. Compactness gives the other
bound. Orthogonal projection of an arbitrary prediction vector onto
this affine Fourier span cannot increase loss, so the same metric
comparison is available for lower bounds with unrestricted decoders.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Proof of the resolution and causal laws}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-resolution}]
At a checkpoint only the first $k=\min(n,m)$ complex coordinates $Y_j$
can be nonzero. Their real and imaginary parts lie in a rectangle with
paired half-widths
\[
 C_N\tau^2,C_N\tau^2,C_N\tau^4,C_N\tau^4,
             \ldots,C_N\tau^{2k},C_N\tau^{2k}.
\]
For ordered half-widths $a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_d$, a rectangular grid
has covering number at most
$C_d\sum_{l=0}^d r^{-l}\prod_{i\le l}a_i$ at radius $r$.
Indeed the product of the $d$ one-dimensional counts expands into
subset products, each bounded by the corresponding initial product.
Put $r=A\max_{l\ge1}(M^{-1}\prod_{i\le l}a_i)^{1/l}$ with a
fixed large $A$. For $M\ge2C_d$, choose $A$ so the nonconstant
terms are at most $M/2$; the constant term is also at most $M/2$.
For the finitely many smaller budgets one point and a larger fixed
constant suffice, since $r\ge a_1/M$. Replace every grid point whose
ball meets the reachable set by a point of that set, doubling the radius.
This enforces at most $M$ reachable representatives for every integer
budget, not only asymptotically.

For the lower bound, Lemma~\ref{lem:circle-attainment} minorizes a
$2k$-cube in the normalized real coordinates. Its first $l$-coordinate
projection, scaled by the first $l$ half-widths without the harmless
constant $C_N$, is a rectangle with volume comparable to
$\prod_{i\le l}a_i$. The union of $M$ radius-$b$ balls covers at
most $C_lMb^l/\prod_{i\le l}a_i$ of this minorized probability.
Taking $b$ a small multiple of $(\prod_{i\le l}a_i/M)^{1/l}$
leaves a fixed positive mass and gives a squared-error lower bound of
order $b^2$. Lemma~\ref{lem:circle-query-metric} transfers it to the
actual query loss. Conditional averaging removes decoder randomization;
randomized label assignment cannot beat the nearest of its $M$ centers.
Independent public coding randomness may be fixed and then averaged.
The minorization retains the acquisition probability throughout.

For $l=2j$ the squared radius just obtained is
$\tau^{2(j+1)}M^{-1/j}$. No odd index increases their maximum.
For $l=1$ its term is at most the $l=2$ term since $M\ge1$.
For $l=2j-1$, $j\ge2$, its logarithm is a convex combination of
the logarithms for $l=2j-2$ and $l=2j$; this follows by inserting
the equal last two half-widths into
$2l^{-1}(\sum_{i\le l}\log a_i-\log M)$.
This proves \eqref{eq:circle-checkpoint-law} for $\tau>0$.
At $\tau=0$ every report is independent of the hidden angle and every
query probability is known; both sides of the asserted law are zero.

It remains to construct one causal filter. Use the raw weighted Fourier
moments $Y_j$, not $S_j$. Extend by $Y_0=1$ and
$Y_{-j}=\overline{Y_j}$. Multiplication by a new factor
\eqref{eq:circle-factor} gives, for $j\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-update}
 Y'_j=\frac{Y_j+\tau^2zY_{j-1}+\bar zY_{j+1}}
                 {1+z\overline{Y_1}+\bar zY_1}.
\end{equation}
Only $j\le m-1$ is needed after the report. The denominator is the
posterior expectation of $f/a$ and is at least $1-\tau\ge1/2$;
this also holds on the segment between any two reachable posterior
states. The numerator and denominator have uniformly bounded coefficients
and states have bounded norm. Direct subtraction of the two fractions
therefore gives a Lipschitz constant independent of $\tau$. In
particular no reciprocal contrast occurs.

At each stage take the reachable representative codebook constructed
above. Update its current representative by \eqref{eq:circle-update}
with the current command and report, then select a nearest representative
in the next codebook with a fixed tie rule. An updated representative is
reachable. If $e_n$ is its error from the true state, then
$e_{n+1}\le L e_n+r_{n+1}$, $e_0=0$, where $r_{n+1}$ is the
next covering radius. Finite iteration gives
$e_n\le C_N\max_s r_s$ and the asserted maximum-checkpoint upper
law by \eqref{eq:circle-parseval}. Missing harmonics above degree $n$
are exactly zero before the changeover; after it, the shrinking future
state contains every index used in \eqref{eq:circle-update}.
The stage is public, while the persistent state is one of $M$ labels.
The lower bounds use prefixes of the same independent uniform acquisition
law and so apply to this same causal resource model. The maximum of
$\min(n,N-n)$ is $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$, proving
\eqref{eq:circle-causal-profile} with matching lower order.

Finally, for positive contrast the local submersion and the invertible
physical Fourier menu give an open $2k$-dimensional prediction patch.
A continuous sufficient encoding restricted to a local section is
injective, so invariance of domain forbids fewer than $2k$ real
coordinates. Every $m$-trial continuation likelihood is a trigonometric polynomial
of order at most $m$, so these Fourier moments determine all such future
laws, not merely the displayed menu. The Fourier state with its identically
zero high harmonics uses exactly $2k$, and \eqref{eq:circle-update} realizes it causally.
This proves the exact dimension assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Resolved harmonic phases]\label{cor:circle-phases}
For $0<\tau<\min(\tau_*,1)$, adjacent terms $j,j+1$ in the circular
causal profile exchange dominance at
\[
 M=\tau^{-2j(j+1)}.
\]
Every harmonic pair occurs in this ordered sequence. For $N=4$ and $N=6$
the respective laws are
\[
 \max\{\tau^4M^{-1},\tau^6M^{-1/2}\},\qquad
 \max\{\tau^4M^{-1},\tau^6M^{-1/2},\tau^8M^{-1/3}\}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Equating the two displayed adjacent powers yields the asserted value of
$M$. These values increase strictly with $j$ for $\tau<1$. Moreover
the logarithms of the terms are lines in $\log M$ with strictly
increasing slopes $-1/j$. Each line is maximal between its crossings
with its two neighbors; successive comparison also excludes every
nonadjacent line on that interval. The two examples substitute
$\lfloor N/2\rfloor=2,3$.
\end{proof}

The exact dimension agrees with that of a saturated three-dimensional
polynomial detector, but the resolved law does not follow from that
agreement. Here each $j$th harmonic is $O(\tau^j)$ in the acquired
posterior and another $O(\tau^j)$ in the future query. The physical
width is therefore $\tau^{2j}$. Keeping only the future attenuation
would already give the wrong $\tau$ power at $n=m=1$. Haar orthogonality
and the symmetric-polynomial submersion are explicit hypotheses and
mechanisms of this application; an arbitrary prior on the circle is not
silently substituted. The original full-support-prior monomial theorem
retains its separate scope.


\subsection{Reconstruction of the attainable volume profile}
\label{sec:operational-reconstruction}
The preceding theorems determine a prediction law from an attainable
flag. There is a converse: the entire integer-budget regret curve
recovers every product in that flag, up to uniform constants. Thus the
truncated volume profile is also necessary for uniform comparison of
prediction laws. We first prove the elementary envelope identity on
which this converse rests. Its role is to extract the invariant from
the two experiment-specific classifications; it is not a replacement
for their attainment, global covering, or causal arguments.

\subsubsection{An envelope identity with integer budgets}
For an ordered list $s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_p\ge0$, put
\[
 V_0(s)=1,\qquad V_\ell(s)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}s_i,
 \qquad
 e_s(b)=\max_{1\le j\le p}\left(\frac{V_j(s)}b\right)^{1/j}
 \quad(b\ge1).
\]
Zero entries and their products are retained. The extension to real
$b$ is an analytic device; an actual state alphabet always has an
integer cardinality.

\begin{lemma}[Recovery of all initial products]
\label{lem:integer-envelope-duality}
For every $1\le\ell\le p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuous-envelope-recovery}
 V_\ell(s)=\inf_{b\ge1}b\,e_s(b)^\ell.
\end{equation}
For integer budgets one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer-envelope-recovery}
 V_\ell(s)\le
 \inf_{M\in\N,\,M\ge1}M e_s(M)^\ell
 \le2V_\ell(s).
\end{equation}
Both assertions include zero products. If $s_\ell>0$, equality in
\eqref{eq:continuous-envelope-recovery} holds at
$b_\ell=V_\ell(s)/s_\ell^\ell\ge1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The $\ell$th term in the maximum gives
$b e_s(b)^\ell\ge V_\ell(s)$. Suppose first that $s_\ell>0$.
For $j<\ell$, monotonicity of the list gives
\[
 \frac{V_\ell}{V_j}=\prod_{i=j+1}^{\ell}s_i
       \ge s_\ell^{\ell-j},
\]
and hence $V_j/b_\ell\le s_\ell^j$. For $j>\ell$, the reverse
product estimate $V_j/V_\ell\le s_\ell^{j-\ell}$ gives the same
inequality. The $\ell$th term is equal to $s_\ell$, so
$e_s(b_\ell)=s_\ell$. Taking $M_\ell=\lceil b_\ell\rceil$ and
using that $e_s$ is nonincreasing, we obtain
\[
 M_\ell e_s(M_\ell)^\ell
 \le\lceil b_\ell\rceil s_\ell^\ell
 \le2b_\ell s_\ell^\ell=2V_\ell.
\]
This proves both assertions for positive products without rounding a
real budget down to an inadmissible alphabet size.

Let $r$ be the number of positive entries. If $r=0$, every quantity
in the assertions is zero. If $0<r<\ell$, then for
$b\ge b_r=V_r/s_r^r$ the last positive term is maximal. Indeed,
with $t=(V_r/b)^{1/r}\le s_r$, the inequality
$V_r/V_j\ge s_r^{r-j}\ge t^{r-j}$ gives $V_j/b\le t^j$ for
$j<r$. Consequently
\[
 b e_s(b)^\ell=V_r^{\ell/r}b^{1-\ell/r}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The same limit holds along the integers. Since $V_\ell=0$, this
settles every remaining case.
\end{proof}

The equality uses the ordered-product structure. It would be incorrect
to apply it to an arbitrary list of coefficients in a maximum of power
laws: some such coefficients can lie strictly below the envelope. Here
$\log V_\ell$ is concave on the positive indices, and each product
has the supporting budget exhibited in the proof. Repeated scales may
share that budget; their products are still recovered.

\subsubsection{A complete invariant for uniform regret comparison}
For any nonnegative curve $R:\N\to[0,\infty)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operational-volume-transform}
 \mathcal I_0(R)=1,\qquad
 \mathcal I_\ell(R)=\inf_{M\in\N,\,M\ge1}
                       M R(M)^{\ell/2}\quad(\ell\ge1).
\end{equation}
The infimum refers to the whole optimal-regret curve, not to finitely
many observations of an implemented filter. No numerical inversion
procedure or finite-budget estimator is implicit in this definition.

\begin{theorem}[Operational reconstruction and comparison]
\label{thm:operational-reconstruction}
Suppose a family of checkpoint regret curves satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reconstruction-hypothesis}
 c e_s(M)^2\le R_s(M)\le C e_s(M)^2
 \qquad(M\in\N,\ M\ge1)
\end{equation}
with $c,C>0$ uniform in the family and ordered lists of a fixed
maximum length $p$, padded with zeros when necessary. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:risk-recovers-products}
 c^{\ell/2}V_\ell(s)\le\mathcal I_\ell(R_s)
             \le2C^{\ell/2}V_\ell(s),\qquad 1\le\ell\le p.
\end{equation}
In particular the positive products, the scale orders of their successive
ratios, and their uniform orders of degeneration are
invariants of the regret curve up to comparison constants.

For two such families, their curves are uniformly comparable for all
integer $M\ge1$ if and only if all their corresponding products
$V_\ell$ are uniformly comparable, including their zero sets.
The comparison concerns finite-resolution prediction laws, not an
isomorphism of experiments or a reconstruction of their hidden parameters.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Raise \eqref{eq:reconstruction-hypothesis} to the power $\ell/2$,
multiply by $M$, and take the infimum. Lemma~\ref{lem:integer-envelope-duality}
gives \eqref{eq:risk-recovers-products}. For a positive product the
ratio $\mathcal I_\ell/\mathcal I_{\ell-1}$ is comparable to
$V_\ell/V_{\ell-1}=s_\ell$, with $\mathcal I_0=V_0=1$.
The first zero product and all its successors are detected as exact
zeros of the transform; no quotient $0/0$ is formed.

Suppose $A^{-1}R_s(M)\le\widetilde R_t(M)\le A R_s(M)$ uniformly.
The transform preserves these inequalities with constants $A^{\ell/2}$.
Applying \eqref{eq:risk-recovers-products} to both curves proves uniform
comparison of their products. Conversely, if
$B^{-1}V_\ell(s)\le V_\ell(t)\le B V_\ell(s)$ for all $\ell$,
with $B\ge1$, then
\[
 B^{-2}e_s(M)^2\le e_t(M)^2\le B^2e_s(M)^2.
\]
Indeed each branch has comparison factor $B^{2/\ell}\le B^2$;
taking maxima preserves the bounds. The two instances of
\eqref{eq:reconstruction-hypothesis} give the asserted comparison of
curves. Every constant depends only on the stated uniform constants
and the fixed maximum length, not on a positive lower bound for any
individual scale.
\end{proof}

For positive rank $r$, the proof of the lemma also gives
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{\log R_s(M)}{\log M}=-\frac2r.
\]
Thus the usual dimension slope is one consequence of the curve; the
products recovered in \eqref{eq:risk-recovers-products} give its
additional finite-resolution information. Uniformly equivalent norms
on the same prediction vector change every loss, and hence every
optimal curve, by uniform constants. The recovered product orders are
therefore independent of a uniformly conditioned choice of prediction
coordinates. Parameter-dependent changes with unbounded condition
number are not covered by this observation.

\subsubsection{The collision and contrast invariants}
For a monomial checkpoint define the past-truncated products
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal V}_{n,m;\ell}(a)=
 \begin{cases}
  \mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a),&1\le\ell\le p_{n,m},\\
  0,&\ell>p_{n,m}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The truncation is part of the invariant, not a convention for suppressing
unattained directions.

\begin{corollary}[Recovery at every additive collision]
\label{cor:monomial-operational-recovery}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-checkpoint}, let
$R^a(M)$ be either $\overline R^a_{M;n,m}$ or
$R^{a,\max}_{M;n,m}$. For each fixed finite maximum index,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monomial-operational-recovery}
 \mathcal I_\ell(R^a)\asymp
       \widehat{\mathcal V}_{n,m;\ell}(a),\qquad \ell\ge1,
\end{equation}
uniformly on $K$, including all additive collision strata. Consequently
these truncated volumes are a complete invariant for uniform comparison
of the corresponding checkpoint regret curves.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Take $s_j=d_j$ for $1\le j\le p_{n,m}$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:leja-scales} and pad by zeros. The same lemma gives
$V_\ell(s)\le\mathcal V_{m,\ell}\le\ell!V_\ell(s)$ through the
truncation. These factors are uniform at fixed horizon, including when
both sides vanish. Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-checkpoint} therefore
verifies \eqref{eq:reconstruction-hypothesis} for either regret curve.
Theorem~\ref{thm:operational-reconstruction} proves the assertion,
including zero products beyond the truncation.
\end{proof}

The conclusion recovers the observable volume orders, not the complete
arrangement of the future exponents. In particular it does not recover
directions inaccessible to this past. The latter may still occur in the
prior-ambiguity body of Section~\ref{sec:directional-geometry}, whose
information convention is different.

\begin{corollary}[Recovery of paired acquisition--observation scales]
\label{cor:circular-operational-recovery}
For either checkpoint regret curve in
Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-resolution}, with $k=\min(n,m)$, uniformly on
its fixed-horizon contrast interval,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-operational-recovery}
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal I_{2j-1}(R^\tau)&\asymp\tau^{2j^2},\\
 \mathcal I_{2j}(R^\tau)&\asymp\tau^{2j(j+1)},
             \qquad 1\le j\le k.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The transforms vanish at every higher index. For $\tau>0$ the
successive nonzero ratios recover both copies of each scale
$\tau^{2j}$; at $\tau=0$ all positive-order transforms vanish.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the real list
$(\tau^2,\tau^2,\tau^4,\tau^4,\ldots,\tau^{2k},\tau^{2k})$.
Its products of lengths $2j-1$ and $2j$ have respectively the exponents
$2j^2$ and $2j(j+1)$. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-resolution} identifies its full envelope with
$\mathcal H_{n,m}$, including the odd products even though they add no
larger branch. Theorem~\ref{thm:operational-reconstruction} now applies.
Ratios of consecutive products have exponent $2j$ on both members of
the $j$th pair. Zero contrast and indices above $2k$ are the zero-product
cases of Lemma~\ref{lem:integer-envelope-duality}.
\end{proof}

These reconstructions concern checkpoint curves with the experiment
known. Taking the maximum over checkpoints produces the causal laws
already proved, but need not retain the individual checkpoint curves.
Nor is a curve with a positive common-name input-error floor an instance
of \eqref{eq:reconstruction-hypothesis}. The distinct uncertainty law in
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-common-moments} and all of its hypotheses remain
in force.


\subsection{The geometry of unresolved prior information}
\label{sec:directional-geometry}
The acquired-history image and uncertainty about the prior are different
geometric objects. The first has its dimension bounded by $n(r-1)$.
The second can occupy every future direction, even after a fixed history.
We describe the second object here. This also identifies the directions
which are suppressed when an uncertainty problem is summarized by a
single squared-error floor.

\subsubsection{The bounded-test principle}
The finite-radius mechanism in this section is independent of monomials.
We state it before its confluent specialization. This formulation and the
normalization below were isolated in the preceding mathematical audit
\cite{A1v14audit}; we retain that attribution. The result is a bounded
right-inverse principle, not a second positive-history attainment theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Relative prior perturbations under an observation map]
\label{prop:general-bounded-dual}
Let $\mu$ be a probability, $0<c\le\ell\le C<\infty$, and
$\nu=\ell\mu/(\mu\ell)$. Let $\phi=(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_q)$ be
bounded real tests with $\|\phi_j\|_\infty\le B$ and
$\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(\phi,\phi)\ge\lambda I_q$, $\lambda>0$.
For $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$, vary $\mu'$ over
\[
 \{(1+u)\mu:\mu u=0,\ \|u\|_\infty\le\varepsilon\},
 \qquad \nu'=\ell\mu'/(\mu'\ell).
\]
For every linear map $A:\R^q\to\R^d$ the displacement set satisfies
\[
 c_0\varepsilon A[-1,1]^q
 \ \subset\ \{A(\nu'\phi-\nu\phi)\}
 \ \subset\ C_0\varepsilon A[-1,1]^q,
\]
where $c_0,C_0>0$ depend only on $q,B,\lambda$, not on the singular
values or rank of $A$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $\mu'=(1+u)\mu$, the exact identity
\[
 \nu'\phi-\nu\phi
 =\frac{\nu\{u(\phi-\nu\phi)\}}{1+\nu u}
\]
bounds each coordinate by $4B\varepsilon$. For the other inclusion put
$\Gamma=\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(\phi,\phi)$ and, for
$v\in[-1,1]^q$, set
\[
 f_v=v^{\mathsf T}\Gamma^{-1}(\phi-\nu\phi),\qquad
 K=\max\{1,2qB/\lambda\},\qquad s=\varepsilon/(4K).
\]
Then $\nu f_v=0$, $\nu(\phi f_v)=v$, and $\|f_v\|_\infty\le K$.
Define a prior, rather than only a posterior perturbation, by
\[
 \frac{d\mu'}{d\mu}=\frac{1+s f_v}{1+s\mu f_v}.
\]
It has total mass one, is positive, and obeys
\[
 \left\|\frac{d\mu'}{d\mu}-1\right\|_\infty
 \le\frac{2sK}{1-sK}
 =\frac{\varepsilon/2}{1-\varepsilon/4}\le\varepsilon.
\]
The evidence is $\mu\ell/(1+s\mu f_v)$, so Bayes normalization gives
exactly $\nu'=(1+s f_v)\nu$ and hence displacement $sv$. This realizes
one common cube, not merely separate directional alternatives. Applying
$A$ to both inclusions completes the proof, also when $A$ has a kernel.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Complete confluent flags and all width orders}


Throughout this section the calibration is known. Write $q=q_m$ and use
one of the Leja orders in Lemma~\ref{lem:leja-scales}. Put
\[
 \phi_a=(g_1^a,\ldots,g_q^a),\qquad
 D_a=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_q),\qquad
 B_\infty^q=[-1,1]^q,
\]
and let $L_a$ be the matrix in \eqref{eq:leja-physical}. All repeated
formal labels are retained. The convention $d_{q+1}=0$ will be useful.
For a probability $\nu$, let
\[
 X_a(\nu)=(\nu(t^{Hx_i}))_{i=1}^q=L_aD_a\nu\phi_a.
\]
The norm of a difference of physical query vectors is equivalent to the
Euclidean norm of the corresponding difference of $X_a$, with the fixed
constants already established for $G_m$.

Fix a full-support prior $\mu$ and a history $h$ of length $n$, where
$n+m\le N$. Its likelihood is denoted by $\ell_h$, and its posterior
by $\nu_h=\ell_h\mu/\mu\ell_h$. For $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-prior-ball}
 \mathcal B_\varepsilon(\mu)=
 \{(1+u)\mu:\ \mu u=0,\ \|u\|_{L^\infty(\mu)}\le\varepsilon\}.
\end{equation}
Every member is a full-support probability. Let $\nu_h'$ denote the
posterior obtained from $\mu'\in\mathcal B_\varepsilon(\mu)$ using
\emph{the same} likelihood, and define the centered ambiguity set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ambiguity-body}
 \mathcal A_h(a,\varepsilon)=
 \{X_a(\nu_h')-X_a(\nu_h):\mu'\in\mathcal B_\varepsilon(\mu)\}.
\end{equation}
This is a set at a specified history; choosing a point in it does not
select a different prior after each realized history in a statistical
experiment. A separate common-prior decision statement is given below.

\begin{lemma}[A uniformly bounded dual for the complete test flag]
\label{lem:flag-covariance}
The covariance matrix
\[
 \Gamma_{a,h}=\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_h}(\phi_a,\phi_a)
\]
has least eigenvalue bounded below by a positive constant, uniformly in
$a\in K$, histories of length at most $N-m$, and all orders of the
formal nodes, including their repeated-node limits. Moreover there is
$C_0\ge1$ such that, for every $v\in B_\infty^q$, the function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-flag-dual}
 f_v(t)=v^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_{a,h}^{-1}
                 (\phi_a(t)-\nu_h\phi_a)
\end{equation}
satisfies $\nu_h f_v=0$, $\nu_h(\phi_a f_v)=v$, and
$\|f_v\|_\infty\le C_0$. The constants can be chosen uniformly for
centers in any fixed dominated prior family
$c_-\mu_0\le\mu\le c_+\mu_0$ with $c_->0$ and full-support $\mu_0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every ordered multiset of $q$ positive nodes, the functions
$1,g_1^a,\ldots,g_q^a$ are linearly independent. Indeed,
Lemma~\ref{lem:prefix-hermite} identifies their span with the constant
and complete positive-exponent Hermite blocks. Linear independence of
these exponential-polynomial functions on $(0,1)$ follows from the
confluent Chebyshev argument in Lemma~\ref{lem:confluent-positive}.
A nonzero linear combination of the $g_j^a$ cannot therefore be constant
on the support of a full-support measure. Consequently its variance is
positive, and the covariance matrix under $\mu_0$ is positive definite.

The uniform bounds and continuity in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:newton-attainment} apply to the full prefix of length
$q$, regardless of the number of distinct node values. They show that
this covariance matrix is continuous on $K$ for each fixed permutation.
Compactness, and the finite number of permutations, give
$\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_0}(\phi_a,\phi_a)\ge\lambda_0 I_q$
for some $\lambda_0>0$. This statement concerns the normalized functions,
not physically observable coordinates divided by a zero pivot.

Since $\kappa^n\le\ell_h\le1$, one has
$\nu_h\ge\kappa^n c_-\mu_0$. For any $w\in\R^q$, the variational
formula for variance gives
\[
 \inf_b\nu_h((w^{\mathsf T}\phi_a-b)^2)
 \ge\kappa^n c_-\inf_b\mu_0((w^{\mathsf T}\phi_a-b)^2)
 \ge\kappa^N c_-\lambda_0\|w\|^2.
\]
For a single fixed prior use $\mu_0=\mu$ and $c_-=c_+=1$.
This proves the eigenvalue assertion without any compactness assumption
on the family of priors. Formula~\eqref{eq:bounded-flag-dual} now has
zero mean, and multiplication by the centered test vector gives $v$.
The uniform bound on $\phi_a$ and the just obtained bound on
$\Gamma_{a,h}^{-1}$ give $C_0$, after increasing it to at least one.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[All collision scales of the prior ambiguity body]
\label{prop:ambiguity-ellipsoid}
There are constants $c,C>0$, with the uniformity of
Lemma~\ref{lem:flag-covariance}, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ambiguity-sandwich}
 c\varepsilon L_aD_aB_\infty^q
 \ \subset\ \mathcal A_h(a,\varepsilon)
 \ \subset\ C\varepsilon L_aD_aB_\infty^q.
\end{equation}
If
\[
 w_k(\mathcal A)=\inf_{\dim V\le k}\sup_{z\in\mathcal A}
                        \operatorname{dist}(z,V),\qquad 0\le k\le q,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ambiguity-widths}
 c\varepsilon d_{k+1}\le w_k(\mathcal A_h(a,\varepsilon))
                         \le C\varepsilon d_{k+1}.
\end{equation}
In particular all widths past the number of distinct positive future
exponents are zero. No past-dimension truncation occurs in this assertion.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $\nu=\nu_h$. For $\mu'=(1+u)\mu$ in
\eqref{eq:relative-prior-ball}, direct normalization yields
\[
 \nu'\phi_a-\nu\phi_a
 =\frac{\nu\bigl(u(\phi_a-\nu\phi_a)\bigr)}{1+\nu u}.
\]
If every $g_j^a$ is bounded by $B$, the right side has coordinatewise
absolute value at most $2B\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)\le4B\varepsilon$.
Multiplication by $L_aD_a$ proves the upper inclusion.

For the lower inclusion take any $v\in B_\infty^q$, let $f=f_v$ be
\eqref{eq:bounded-flag-dual}, and put $s=\varepsilon/(4C_0)$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback-posterior-tilt}
 d\mu' =\frac{1+s f}{1+s\mu f}\,d\mu.
\end{equation}
This is a positive probability, and
\[
 \left\|\frac{d\mu'}{d\mu}-1\right\|_\infty
 \le\frac{2s C_0}{1-s C_0}
 =\frac{\varepsilon/2}{1-\varepsilon/4}\le\varepsilon.
\]
Because $\nu f=0$, its posterior at the same history is exactly
$d\nu'=(1+s f)\,d\nu$. Hence
$\nu'\phi_a-\nu\phi_a=s v$, proving the lower inclusion with
$c=1/(4C_0)$. Formula~\eqref{eq:pullback-posterior-tilt} enforces prior
normalization; simply using $(1+s f)\mu$ here would not do so.

Let $s_a$ be the number of distinct positive future exponents. The active
$q$ by $s_a$ block of $L_a$ is bounded above and below as an operator,
by its leading triangular block in Lemma~\ref{lem:leja-scales}.
Thus the nonzero singular values of
$L_a\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_{s_a})$ are comparable, in order,
to $d_1,\ldots,d_{s_a}$. The comparison follows directly by applying
the min--max formula to the two quadratic forms, bounded above and below
by fixed multiples of $\sum_jd_j^2v_j^2$.
The Euclidean unit ball lies in the cube, which lies in the ball of
radius $\sqrt q$. An ellipsoid with ordered semiaxes $b_j$ has $k$th
linear width $b_{k+1}$: projection on its first $k$ principal axes gives
the upper bound, while any $k$-dimensional space has a nonzero
orthogonal direction in the span of its first $k+1$ axes, giving the
lower bound. Apply this fact to the two inclusions in
\eqref{eq:ambiguity-sandwich}. If $k\ge s_a$, all vectors lie in a
space of dimension $s_a$, and both sides of
\eqref{eq:ambiguity-widths} are zero.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Exact lower moments and a vanishing uncertainty direction}
The preceding proposition resolves every direction, rather than only
the longest one. An equivalent formulation specifies which moments have
already been supplied. Let $s_a$ again denote the number of distinct
positive nodes. For $0\le k\le q$, restrict
\eqref{eq:relative-prior-ball} by the finite conditions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix-advice}
 \nu_h'(t^{Hx_i})=\nu_h(t^{Hx_i}),
                 \qquad 1\le i\le\min\{k,s_a\},
\end{equation}
and call the restricted class $\mathcal B_{h,k}$. Denote by
$Q_m^a(\nu)$ the vector of probabilities of the ordered physical
$m$-trial queries, with its normalized Euclidean norm $\|\cdot\|_{\rm av}$.
Define its squared minimax radius by
\[
 \mathfrak a_{h,k}(a,\varepsilon)=
 \inf_z\sup_{\mu'\in\mathcal B_{h,k}}
       \|z-Q_m^a(\nu_h')\|_{\rm av}^2.
\]
The center $\mu$, the relative envelope, the calibration and the values
in \eqref{eq:prefix-advice} specify this local problem. These are exact
conditional-moment constraints at $h$, not the pre-acquisition,
absolute-error moment name of Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty-geometry}.

\begin{corollary}[Sharp residual uncertainty after a moment prefix]
\label{cor:prefix-ambiguity}
With uniform constants as above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix-ambiguity-risk}
 c\varepsilon^2d_{k+1}^2
 \le\mathfrak a_{h,k}(a,\varepsilon)
 \le C\varepsilon^2d_{k+1}^2.
\end{equation}
For a constant-failure history, the same order is realized as an
unconditional, event-weighted physical prediction problem with exact
prior-moment advice. In particular, for
$A_\theta=\{0,1,2+\theta\}$, $N=3$, $n=1$, $m=2$ and sufficiently
small $|\theta|$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invisible-memory-visible-ambiguity}
 \Xi_3(M,A_\theta)\asymp M^{-1},\qquad
 \mathfrak a_{h,4}(A_\theta,\varepsilon)
                            \asymp\varepsilon^2\theta^2.
\end{equation}
The latter is zero at $\theta=0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $k<s_a$, the first $k$ raw constraints in
\eqref{eq:prefix-advice} are equivalent to equality of the first $k$
normalized moments. This follows by triangular back substitution in
the leading block of $L_aD_a$, whose first $k$ pivots are nonzero.
This equivalence is an exact identity, not a stability assertion for
approximate constraints. The upper-inclusion proof in
Proposition~\ref{prop:ambiguity-ellipsoid} therefore has its first $k$
coordinates zero. Its remaining scaled coordinates are bounded by
$C\varepsilon d_{k+1}$. The central prediction gives the upper bound.
For the lower bound use the construction
\eqref{eq:pullback-posterior-tilt} with $v=e_{k+1}$ and $v=-e_{k+1}$.
Both priors satisfy the constraints and their query vectors differ by
at least $c\varepsilon d_{k+1}$. For any proposed output, one of its
two distances is at least half this separation. Query-norm equivalence
proves the lower bound. If $k\ge s_a$, all distinct raw future moments
are fixed, and Proposition~\ref{prop:tests} gives radius zero.

For the physical assertion issue $n$ constant commands whose failure
factor is $1/2$, and consider the event $E$ of $n$ failures. Its
likelihood is the constant $2^{-n}$, and its probability is $2^{-n}$
under every prior in the class. On $E$ the posterior is the prior, so
\eqref{eq:prefix-advice} is now exact \emph{prior}-moment advice fixed
before acquisition. Score the squared prediction loss for the selected
$m$-trial query on $E$, and assign zero loss off $E$. Every trial and
the probability of $E$ remain counted. A single rule has the same
prediction kernel on $E$ for all compatible priors. The preceding
two-point argument, multiplied by $2^{-n}$, gives the unconditional
lower bound; the central prediction gives the upper bound. This is a
specific event-weighted decision problem, not a claim about the risk
on all other report histories under a different payoff.

For the displayed family, the five nonconstant formal two-trial nodes,
before the fixed normalization by $H$, are
\[
 1,\quad 2,\quad 2+\theta,\quad 3+\theta,\quad4+2\theta.
\]
They form four uniformly separated groups, with the only merging pair
$2,2+\theta$. Thus $\mathcal V_{2,j}\asymp1$ for $1\le j\le4$ and
$\mathcal V_{2,5}\asymp|\theta|$. Lemma~\ref{lem:leja-scales}
gives $d_1,\ldots,d_4\asymp1$ and $d_5\asymp|\theta|$,
including $d_5=0$ at the collision. Formula~\eqref{eq:prefix-ambiguity-risk}
proves the second assertion. At checkpoint $(1,2)$ the attainable
truncation is $p_{1,2}=2$, so its regret profile is $\asymp M^{-1}$.
At checkpoint $(2,1)$ there are two uniformly separated positive
one-trial nodes, and the same profile results. Taking the maximum proves
the first assertion in \eqref{eq:invisible-memory-visible-ambiguity}.
\end{proof}

The example separates a collision which is invisible to the acquired
memory exponent at this horizon from a vanishing direction of unresolved
prior information. More generally, \eqref{eq:ambiguity-widths} uses
all future scales, whereas \eqref{eq:intrinsic-profile} uses only those
which the past can excite. The isotropic common-name law in the next
section allows uncertainty in a uniformly visible direction; its
$\delta^2$ term is therefore not, by itself, a classification of the
weaker directions. No calibration-only converse is inferred from the
prior perturbations constructed here.


\subsection{Sharp memory under a common moment name}
\label{sec:uncertainty-geometry}
We now determine the joint effect of persistent memory and uncertainty in
the finite moment data. The upper construction in
Corollary~\ref{cor:uncertain-monomial} serves every experiment consistent
with the same name. A converse requires the consistency class to contain
actual alternatives; an arbitrary boundary name may determine a singleton.
The following formulation makes the necessary interior condition explicit.
It applies at every additive collision stratum, without changing the
fixed-prior classification.

\subsubsection{The consistency class}
Fix the detector, $K$, $N\ge2$, and $r\ge2$ as in the introduction.
Choose the attainable failure basis to be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uncertainty-probe-basis}
 F_0=\tfrac12,\qquad F_j=\tfrac12+\beta t^{a_j},\quad 1\le j<r,
\end{equation}
where $\beta>0$ is fixed and sufficiently small. The right inverse in
Lemma~\ref{lem:interior} supplies one command for each formal coefficient
vector, independent of the calibration. Thus this is one of the fixed
physical bases already allowed in the intrinsic theorem, not an extra
observation channel. At future length $m$, use its ordered $m$-fold
products, selected uniformly after the checkpoint index is formed.

Let $\mu_0$ be a fixed full-support probability on $[0,1]$, and take
$0<c_-<1<c_+<\infty$. For a fixed
$0<\lambda<\min\{1-c_-,c_+-1\}$, set
\[
 \mathcal D=\{\nu:c_-\mu_0\le\nu\le c_+\mu_0\},\qquad
 \mathcal D_\lambda=
 \{\nu:(c_-+\lambda)\mu_0\le\nu\le(c_+-\lambda)\mu_0\}.
\]
All members are probability measures. These inequalities are inequalities
of measures; no Lebesgue density is assumed. Let $\mathbf m_N(a,\mu)$
denote the vector of all formal moments of degrees $0,\ldots,N$,
with repeated exponent labels retained. For a rational vector $y$ of the
same format and $\delta>0$, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-consistency-class}
 \mathcal U(y,\delta)=\{(a',\nu)\in K\times\mathcal D:
       \|\mathbf m_N(a',\nu)-y\|_\infty\le\delta\}.
\end{equation}
The detector is fixed; certified approximations to its coefficients may
be supplied separately. The vector $y$ need not itself be a moment vector.

A common-name program is chosen from $y,\delta$ and the fixed task data,
without consulting the unknown member of $\mathcal U(y,\delta)$.
It retains at most $M$ labels at every stage. Let
$\mathcal R_{M,N}^{\rm av}(y,\delta)$ be the infimum over such programs
of the supremum, over this class, of maximum unconditional checkpoint
regret under the common independent command exploration.
Define $\mathcal R_{M,N}^{\max}(y,\delta)$ by using worst-history
checkpoint regret instead. At each checkpoint the query is the fresh
random choice specified above. The admissible programs include the
integer/dyadic programs with memoryless command coding. The lower bounds
below hold even for randomized programs with unrestricted offline advice
that is the same in all members of the consistency class.

\begin{theorem}[Joint memory and moment-uncertainty law]
\label{thm:sharp-common-moments}
There are constants $c,C,\delta_0>0$, depending only on the fixed data
and $\lambda$, such that the following holds. Suppose
$(a,\mu)\in K\times\mathcal D_\lambda$, $M\ge1$ is an integer,
$0<\delta\le\delta_0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-moment-name}
 \|y-\mathbf m_N(a,\mu)\|_\infty\le\delta/2.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-common-moments}
 c\bigl(\Xi_N(M,a)+\delta^2\bigr)
 \le \mathcal R_{M,N}^{\rm av}(y,\delta)
 \le \mathcal R_{M,N}^{\max}(y,\delta)
 \le C\bigl(\Xi_N(M,a)+\delta^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
The upper bound is attained in order by one finite integer/dyadic
program constructed from $y$, not from $(a,\mu)$. Its selected numerical
scale is comparable to
$\min\{1,\sqrt{\Xi_N(M,a)}+\delta\}$.
The constants are uniform on every additive collision stratum of $K$
and across their intersections.
\end{theorem}

The interior point in this statement is used to describe the order of the
risk and to prove a lower bound. It is not supplied to the algorithm.
In particular the theorem does not replace the consistency class by
alternatives selected after the true model has been revealed. The
hypothesis \eqref{eq:interior-moment-name} leaves room for a two-sided
physical perturbation. Omitting this hypothesis, or replacing
$\mathcal D_\lambda$ by an arbitrary boundary point of $\mathcal D$,
would not be justified by the argument.

\subsubsection{Uniform ambiguity after positive histories}
The converse rests on the following elementary identity. It records why
acquisition does not resolve the local ambiguity at a fixed horizon.

\begin{lemma}[Posterior separation with overlapping history laws]
\label{lem:overlap-tilt}
Let $\mu$ be a probability, let $\mu f=0$ and $|f|\le1$, and put
$\mu_\pm=(1\pm\varepsilon f)\mu$ for $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$.
For a positive likelihood $\ell_w$, let
$\nu_w=\ell_w\mu/\mu\ell_w$. For every bounded query $q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilt-posterior-gap}
 \nu_{w,+}q-\nu_{w,-}q
 =\frac{2\varepsilon\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_w}(q,f)}
        {1-\varepsilon^2(\nu_w f)^2}.
\end{equation}
Under the same parameter-independent acquisition rule, the history laws
satisfy $P_\pm\ge(1-\varepsilon)P_0$. If one prediction kernel $Y$
is used in both experiments and $q_\pm(w)=\nu_{w,\pm}q$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap-squared-loss}
 \frac12\sum_{\sigma\in\{-,+\}}\E_{P_\sigma}(Y-q_\sigma)^2
 \ge\frac{1-\varepsilon}{4}
       \int(q_+(w)-q_-(w))^2\,P_0(\dd w).
\end{equation}
The expectation includes any common independent coding randomization.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both perturbations have mass one and are positive. Direct normalization
gives
\[
 \nu_{w,\pm}q=
 \frac{\nu_w q\pm\varepsilon\nu_w(qf)}{1\pm\varepsilon\nu_w f}.
\]
Subtract these fractions to obtain \eqref{eq:tilt-posterior-gap}.
The density of $P_\pm$ relative to $P_0$ is
$1\pm\varepsilon\nu_w f$, by integration of the same likelihood
against the two priors. It lies between $1-\varepsilon$ and
$1+\varepsilon$. Conditional on a given history, the prediction kernel
is identical because the data supplied to the program are identical.
For every real $Y$ and real $u,v$,
\[
 \tfrac12\{(Y-u)^2+(Y-v)^2\}
 =\bigl(Y-(u+v)/2\bigr)^2+(u-v)^2/4.
\]
Dominate both history measures by $(1-\varepsilon)P_0$, integrate
this identity, and discard its nonnegative first term. This proves
\eqref{eq:overlap-squared-loss}. The same calculation integrates
randomized predictions. No equality of the two history laws is required.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A uniform physical moment ambiguity]
\label{lem:uniform-collision-ambiguity}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-common-moments}, the
class $\mathcal U(y,\delta)$ contains two experiments with the same
calibration $a$ such that, at every checkpoint $1\le n<N$, every
common prediction rule has maximum unconditional regret at least
$c_0\delta^2$. The rule may retain the entire acquired history.
The constant $c_0>0$ is uniform in $(a,\mu),y,\delta$ in the stated
ranges. Both alternatives are positive experiments in $\mathcal D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $z_a(t)=t^{a_1}$ and
\[
 v_0=\min_{a\in K}\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_0}(z_a)>0.
\]
The variance is positive for each $a$: full support gives positive mass
to two separated intervals on which the strictly increasing function
$z_a$ has disjoint ranges. Its dependence on $a$ is continuous in the
uniform norm, including at $t=0$, because $a_1$ has a positive lower
bound on $K$. Compactness therefore gives the displayed positive minimum.

Set $f=z_a-\mu z_a$ and $\varepsilon=\delta/2$. Then $\mu f=0$
and $|f|\le1$. If $\varepsilon\le\lambda/c_+$, the measure bounds
for $\mu\in\mathcal D_\lambda$ imply
\[
 c_-\mu_0\le(1\pm\varepsilon f)\mu\le c_+\mu_0.
\]
Indeed, the absolute change of the measure is bounded by
$\varepsilon\mu\le\varepsilon c_+\mu_0\le\lambda\mu_0$.
For every formal label through degree $N$, its monomial $v$ lies in
$[0,1]$, and hence
\[
 |\mu_\pm v-\mu v|=\varepsilon|\mu(vf)|\le\varepsilon.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:interior-moment-name} this puts both
$(a,\mu_\pm)$ in \eqref{eq:moment-consistency-class}. This uses the
same $y$ in both experiments; none of its entries is changed.

Let $w$ be any length-$n$ history and $\nu_w$ its reference posterior.
Its likelihood satisfies $\kappa^n\le\ell_w\le1$, so
$\nu_w\ge\kappa^n\mu$. The variational formula for variance gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-posterior-variance}
 \operatorname{Var}_{\nu_w}(z_a)
 =\inf_{b\in\R}\nu_w((z_a-b)^2)
 \ge\kappa^n\operatorname{Var}_{\mu}(z_a)
 \ge\kappa^n c_- v_0.
\end{equation}
This bound is uniform over commands and report words, including failures.
Let $m=N-n$ and take the actually executable ordered product probe
$F_1F_0^{m-1}$. Its event function is
\[
 q(t)=2^{-m}+\beta 2^{-(m-1)}z_a(t).
\]
It is selected with probability $r^{-m}$ by the prescribed menu and
uses exactly its $m$ trials. Since subtracting the constant $\mu z_a$
from $z_a$ does not change covariance under $\nu_w$,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_w}(q,f)
 =\beta 2^{-(m-1)}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_w}(z_a).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:tilt-posterior-gap}, whose denominator is in
$(0,1]$, therefore bounds the two conditional query means apart by
\[
 2\varepsilon\beta 2^{-(m-1)}\kappa^n c_-v_0.
\]
Apply \eqref{eq:overlap-squared-loss} and multiply by $r^{-m}$.
Taking the minimum of the resulting positive constants over the finitely
many $n$ gives $c_0\delta^2$. For example, the coefficient for a given
$n$ may be taken as
$r^{-m}\beta^2 2^{-2(m-1)}\kappa^{2n}c_-^2v_0^2/8$.
Here $\varepsilon=\delta/2$ and $1-\varepsilon\ge1/2$ were used.
The argument applies to every rule on the complete history, and hence
also to every finite-label rule. It neither resolves nor divides by any
additive exponent difference.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-common-moments}]
For every $(a',\nu)\in\mathcal U(y,\delta)$, the same finite table
is $\delta$-accurate. Corollary~\ref{cor:uncertain-monomial} supplies
one program with worst-history regret at most
$C(\Xi_N(M,a')+\delta^2)$ in each such experiment. Its finite input
and prior-family hypotheses hold uniformly by
Lemma~\ref{lem:prior-envelope}. The moment vectors of $(a',\nu)$
and $(a,\mu)$ differ by at most $3\delta/2$. Consequently
Corollary~\ref{cor:profile-stability}, applied with this bound, gives
\[
 \Xi_N(M,a')\le C'(\Xi_N(M,a)+\delta^2).
\]
Increasing the fixed constant proves the upper inequality in
\eqref{eq:sharp-common-moments}; no point of the consistency class has
been selected in the construction. The stated numerical scale follows
by applying the same corollary to the center, which belongs to the class.
An unconditional risk is bounded by its worst-history counterpart.

For the memory lower bound, the center itself is an admissible member
of $\mathcal U(y,\delta)$. Every common-name $M$-label program is an
admissible $M$-label program for that fixed experiment. The intrinsic
causal lower theorem and Lemma~\ref{lem:prior-envelope} imply a
uniform lower bound $c_1\Xi_N(M,a)$, including independent coding
randomization. Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-collision-ambiguity} supplies
the independent lower bound $c_0\delta^2$ for every such program.
Use $\max\{u,v\}\ge(u+v)/2$ and take the infimum over programs.
Finally choose $\delta_0$ to satisfy the positive-evidence restrictions
in the upper construction, $\delta_0\le1$, and
$\delta_0/2\le\lambda/c_+$. All restrictions depend only on the fixed
data. This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

The two-point method in these lemmas is elementary. The collision-sensitive
content of \eqref{eq:sharp-common-moments} is the full attained profile
$\Xi_N$, in both its memory converse and its common-program realization.
The uncertainty converse is now valid uniformly at colliding calibrations
rather than only in a noncolliding example. It uses prior ambiguity;
it is not a calibration-only converse for a known exact prior, nor a
lower bound for arbitrary degenerate consistency classes.

\subsubsection{The anisotropic saturation threshold}
The full profile gives a more precise resource threshold than its
separated-chain floor. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:advice-saturation-index}
 \mathcal M_\delta(a)=
 \max_{\substack{1\le n<N\\1\le\ell\le p_{n,N-n}}}
       \mathcal V_{N-n,\ell}(a)\delta^{-\ell}.
\end{equation}
Terms with zero volume are zero, not omitted by a numerical rank decision.

\begin{corollary}[Collision-sensitive saturation]
\label{cor:advice-saturation}
For every $a\in K$, $0<\delta\le1$, and integer $M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-saturation}
 \Xi_N(M,a)\le\delta^2
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad M\ge\mathcal M_\delta(a).
\end{equation}
Thus the least integer satisfying the profile inequality is
$\lceil\mathcal M_\delta(a)\rceil$. Under the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-common-moments}, the minimax order is $\delta^2$
above this threshold and is $\Xi_N(M,a)$ below it, with the same fixed
comparison constants. This is an order statement about risk, not an
exact numerical value of the minimax risk.

Suppose along a positive one-parameter path $a(\theta)$ each nonzero
volume satisfies
$\mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a(\theta))\asymp\theta^{\beta_{m,\ell}}$,
with constants uniform for sufficiently small $\theta>0$.
Then, uniformly also in $0<\delta\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:valuation-saturation}
 \mathcal M_\delta(a(\theta))\asymp
 \max_{n,\ell}\theta^{\beta_{N-n,\ell}}\delta^{-\ell}.
\end{equation}
Identically zero volumes contribute no term. In particular all contact
orders in the collision-tree law enter the advice saturation threshold.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each term $(\mathcal V_{m,\ell}/M)^{2/\ell}$ is at most
$\delta^2$ if and only if $M\ge\mathcal V_{m,\ell}\delta^{-\ell}$.
Take the maximum over all checkpoints and active formal indices.
This proves \eqref{eq:exact-saturation}, including exact zero volumes.
The term $\ell=1$ gives $\mathcal M_\delta\ge\delta^{-1}$, so the
integer statement has no small-budget exception. On each side of the
threshold the sum $\Xi_N+\delta^2$ is between its larger term and
twice that term; apply \eqref{eq:sharp-common-moments}.
Finally there are only finitely many volume terms. Substitute their
uniform two-sided estimates into \eqref{eq:advice-saturation-index}
and take the smallest and largest of their positive constants.
This proves \eqref{eq:valuation-saturation}. The contact exponents
are those already determined by the collision-tree argument; the
corollary introduces no new equality test.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uncertain moments at intersecting collision strata]
\label{cor:uncertain-intersections}
Consider the fixed four-cell detector and square of calibrations in
Corollary~\ref{cor:two-parameter}. Put
$\rho=\max\{|u|,|v|\}$ and
$\tau=\min\{|u|,|v-u|,|v-2u|\}$, as in that corollary.
Under the prior-interior and common-name hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-common-moments}, both common-name minimax
criteria at $N=5$ have order
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noisy-intersection-risk}
 \max\left\{M^{-1/3},\ \rho^{1/2}M^{-1/4},\
              (\rho^2\tau)^{2/9}M^{-2/9},\ \delta^2\right\}.
\end{equation}
The profile saturation threshold satisfies, uniformly on the entire square,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noisy-intersection-threshold}
 \mathcal M_\delta(u,v)\asymp
 \max\{\delta^{-6},\ \rho^2\delta^{-8},\ \rho^2\tau\delta^{-9}\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, on $u=\theta$, $v=\theta+\theta^k$, $k\ge2$ fixed,
and for sufficiently small $\theta>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noisy-tangency-phases}
 \mathcal M_\delta\asymp
 \begin{cases}
   \delta^{-6},&\delta\ge\theta,\\
   \theta^2\delta^{-8},&\theta^k\le\delta\le\theta,\\
   \theta^{k+2}\delta^{-9},&0<\delta\le\theta^k.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
These are uniform comparison orders, including their boundary scales.
Thus a fixed moment accuracy can obscure one collision scale while
resolving another; the same common-name program attains the appropriate
risk order without being told the stratum or the contact order.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-common-moments} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:two-parameter} give
\eqref{eq:noisy-intersection-risk}, since the sum of a nonnegative
profile and $\delta^2$ is comparable to their maximum.
For the threshold, the determinant calculation in that corollary gives,
at $(n,m)=(3,2)$, volumes comparable to one through order six, to
$\rho$ at order seven, to $\rho^2$ at order eight, and to
$\rho^2\tau$ at order nine. Other checkpoints contribute at most
$C\delta^{-6}$ to \eqref{eq:advice-saturation-index}, since their
attainable dimensions are at most six and their normalized volumes are
bounded. The seven-dimensional term introduces no further order:
\[
 \rho\delta^{-7}
   =\sqrt{\delta^{-6}\,\rho^2\delta^{-8}}
   \le\max\{\delta^{-6},\rho^2\delta^{-8}\}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:noisy-intersection-threshold}, also when a volume
is exactly zero. Along the stated path, $\rho\asymp\theta$ and
$\tau\asymp\theta^k$. The ratios of the middle threshold term to
the first, and of the last to the middle, have orders
$\theta^2/\delta^2$ and $\theta^k/\delta$, respectively.
The ordering $\theta^k\le\theta$ gives the three cases in
\eqref{eq:noisy-tangency-phases}. The implementation assertion is the
common-program conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-common-moments},
not a separate procedure that decides which case holds.
\end{proof}
