\begin{abstract}
We determine joint horizon--precision coding laws for quantum
instruments with a varying projective readout and singular conditional
preparations. The readout outcome is retained, or recovered from fixed
orthogonal output supports. If $b=d^2-d$ is the ordered-flag dimension
and $V=\sum_x(\sum_y r_{xy}(2n-r_{xy})-1)$ is the conditional
rank dimension, the optimal reusable description length is
$(b+V/2)\log_2N+(b+V)\log_2(1/\delta)+O(1)$, uniformly for
$N\ge1$ and $0<\delta\le1/32$. Error is the unhalved trace norm
maximized over common adaptive testers with entangled references,
feedback and bounded stopping. The readout resolution is $N^{-1}$,
whereas conditional preparations have resolution $N^{-1/2}$.
An exact rational encoder selects bounded triangular flag coordinates
directly from projection matrices; it preserves all conditional zero
blocks and does not increase their ranks. A second theorem transfers
finite-use state covering numbers to uniformly environment-seizable
families, including unrestricted legal memoryless centres, and gives
full submaximal-error rank-state and Pauli-channel laws. The established
measurement-discrimination and environment-state reductions are credited
separately from the covering and coding arguments. All dimensions and
rank bounds are fixed. The target is supplied matrix data; description
payload, learning, workspace and physical implementation are distinct.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle
\noindent\textit{Operational interfaces.}
Description compilation returns a mathematical quantum instrument;
numerical streaming processes fully specified matrix data. Neither is a
physical classical device acting on an unknown quantum input, and
neither is merely a transcript-only simulation task. The new code lengths
charge the reusable description, with fixed public dimensions, rank bounds where applicable, horizon and error.
All joint-state errors
are unhalved trace norms; transcript total variation is half the classical
block trace norm.
