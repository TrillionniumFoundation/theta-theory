\begin{abstract}
We study finite-use discrimination of ordered quantum measurements
with classical output and no residual device system.  A normalized
effect-tuple covariance has a complete support-coordinate kernel.
For every fixed two-sided $C^2$ measurement curve with nonzero first
derivative $H$, the Hermitian matrix
$\Gamma_E(H)=\frac i2\sum_j[\operatorname{supp}E_j,H_j]$
determines the finite-use scale.  The distance is comparable to
$\min\{1,\sqrt N|t|\}$ when this matrix belongs to the support span,
and to $\min\{1,N|t|\}$ otherwise.  Constants and the two-sided
interval depend on the fixed curve.  Second-order rank openings
are allowed.  An analytic horizontal surrogate and a corrected
logical channel give direct finite-angle bounds; certified control
errors have a separate explicit accumulation bound.
The exponents and error-correction principle are established in
channel metrology; the formulation here uses effect coordinates
without assuming a smooth exact Kraus representation at the boundary.
The arbitrary-pair covariance theorem remains an upper bound.
Transported-weight regularization obeys classical data processing.
Balanced-interior description and learning results retain their
fixed-outcome-count optimal orders, with growing-outcome dependence
separately displayed.  Exact first-order decisions and affine
legalization are polynomial on rational input; general recovery and
dictionary synthesis are not inferred from them.  The principal
support and discrimination proofs are self-contained in this article;
complete binary auxiliary material is preserved in a current supplement.
\end{abstract}
