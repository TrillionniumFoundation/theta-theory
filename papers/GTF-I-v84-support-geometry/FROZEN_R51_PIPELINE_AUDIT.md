## 5. Detailed proof audit: endpoint calibration

### 5.1 Scale schedule and resources

Set `tau=1/N`. The calibration runs over dyadic regularizations `t_j` down to the cutoff scale. At each stage it uses

```text
h_d = d + 4 binom(d,2) = 2d^2-d
```

probe groups and a sample size of order

```text
d^2 t_j^(-1) log(h_d/epsilon_j).
```

Multiplication by the number of groups gives order `d^4` per inverse scale. Summing the dyadic scales is order `N`; the reverse-scale confidence logarithms have a summable geometric weight. Thus no extra `log N` factor appears.

Every branch of the procedure has the same prescribed call count. Failed concentration does not trigger unbudgeted retries.

### 5.2 Data-dependent eigenbasis

The current estimate has algebraic entries because empirical frequencies are rational and the update uses algebraic spectral operations. At repeated eigenvalues, the procedure orders eigenspaces by eigenvalue, projects a fixed public basis, and applies deterministic Gram--Schmidt. This gives a finite measurable rule and avoids an unspecified arbitrary eigenbasis.

State-preparation and algebraic classical arithmetic are not counted in the call budget. They remain separate resources.

### 5.3 Entry estimates

Diagonal probabilities are estimated directly. Real and imaginary off-diagonal entries use differences of positive and negative superposition probabilities. Pairing opposite superpositions controls the variance by the sum of the relevant diagonal probabilities.

Two simultaneous Bernstein estimates are retained:

- one in terms of `E_ii+E_jj`; and
- one in terms of `2-E_ii-E_jj`.

This is essential. A one-sided estimate would not calibrate both support endpoints.

### 5.4 Normalized perturbation

The preceding stage's Loewner invariant bounds the true diagonal probabilities by the current regularized eigenvalues. Since the new sample variance scale is proportional to `t_j`, every normalized entry of `B_j-E` is of order `1/d`. The Hilbert--Schmidt norm therefore controls the normalized operator norm.

The selected constant `K_d=(320d)^2` is large enough to make the raw perturbation a small relative form at both endpoints.

### 5.5 Legal update

The raw estimate is clipped to a slightly enlarged interval and then affinely moved into `[0,1]`. This makes the procedure legal on every record.

On the good event, the relative inequalities place the raw spectrum within the enlarged interval, so clipping is inactive. The affine move introduces a controlled endpoint perturbation and propagates the weaker factor-two invariant.

This argument does not use the false principle that arbitrary spectral clipping preserves relative Loewner order.

### 5.6 Operator error

The same entry concentration without endpoint weights gives an operator error of order `sqrt(t_j)`. The final stage therefore provides `||E-A||_op <= sqrt(tau)`, which is later squared in the compression leakage.

### 5.7 Confidence

All stage probabilities are conditional on the previous good record. Their deterministic allowances sum geometrically. Independence of stage success events is neither used nor needed.

The calibration lemma is coherent.

## 6. Detailed proof audit: variance comparison and compression

### 6.1 Harmonic identity

For a legal effect `G`, the regularized endpoint matrices commute. Direct scalar calculation gives

\[
R_G=((G+\tau I)^{-1}+(I-G+\tau I)^{-1})^{-1}
=\frac{G(I-G)+(\tau+\tau^2)I}{1+2\tau}.
\]

The two endpoint Loewner comparisons transfer through inversion, addition, and inversion again, giving constant-factor comparison of `R_E` and `R_A`. The displayed scalar identity then compares `E(I-E)+tau I` and `A(I-A)+tau I`.

Changing the regularization from `tau` to `tau/2` costs only a further constant.

### 6.2 Exact compression identity

Let `P` commute with `A` and `C=PEP` on the range of `P`. Expanding gives

\[
C(I-C)=PE(I-E)P+PE(I-P)EP.
\]

The second term is positive. Since `P A(I-P)=0`,

\[
PE(I-P)=P(E-A)(I-P).
\]

Hence the leakage is at most `||E-A||_op^2 P <= tau P`. The regularized calibrated variance already dominates `tau P/2`, so the leakage is absorbed by an absolute factor.

This establishes a uniform comparison for every projection commuting with the learned calibration, independent of support rank and spectral multiplicity.

### 6.3 Data dependence

The projection is chosen from the calibration record. All compressed-device samples are fresh. Conditional on the record, it is a fixed two-dimensional embedding and the observed channel is exactly the compressed binary effect. The qubit theorem can therefore be applied conditionally.

There is no use of an unknown true eigenbasis.

## 7. Detailed proof audit: pair learning, assembly, and legalization

### 7.1 Pairwise accuracy

There are `s=binom(d,2)` compressed qubit problems. Each uses accuracy `c_* delta/d` and confidence `eta/(2s)`. The qubit theorem has absolute constants and is uniform over the legal qubit body, including scalar and rank-deficient compressions.

The chosen `c_*` is decreased to lie inside both the qubit theorem's small-error regime and the local matrix-form regime.

### 7.2 From operational error to endpoint form

The dimension-two metric lower bound first controls the midpoint modulus. When the error is sufficiently small, the local midpoint/endpoint comparison converts that bound to the endpoint quadratic form of the true compression.

The calibrated compression variance then dominates the coordinate form built from the eigenvalues of `A`.

No global inverse of the metric comparison is asserted outside the local range.

### 7.3 Assembly multiplicities

Each off-diagonal coordinate is taken from its unique pair. Each diagonal coordinate is assigned to one incident pair. In the global form, every used coordinate contribution appears in at least one corresponding pair form.

The sum of all pair forms includes extra nonnegative duplicate diagonal contributions. Discarding them preserves the upper bound. No equality or consistency among duplicate estimates is needed.

### 7.4 Projection to the legal body

The norm induced by `A` is strictly positive because of the horizon regularization. The legal effect body is compact and convex, so the exact nearest point exists and is unique.

For a finite procedure, the proof constructs a legal rational net in the fixed public basis. An inward move creates a spectral margin; fine entrywise rounding preserves legality. Evaluating the known quadratic objective to sufficient finite precision selects a point with the required slack.

The unknown effect appears only as a feasible comparator in the proof. It is not queried by the algorithm.

### 7.5 Final transfer

The calibrated variance comparison gives

```text
g_(N,E)(F-E) <= constant * g_(N,A)(F-E).
```

Choosing the pair accuracy small enough makes the true endpoint form lie within the local radius. The endpoint-to-midpoint comparison and global metric upper theorem then give risk at most `delta`.

### 7.6 Calls

Calibration uses `O(d^4 N log(d/eta))` calls. Each pair learner uses

```text
O(N (d/delta)^2 log(d/eta))
```

calls, and there are order `d^2` pairs. The total is

```text
O(d^4 N delta^(-2) log(d/eta)).
```

All bounds hold on every record.

### 7.7 Lower bound

For scalar effects, the device's observation distribution is Bernoulli and independent of every input or retained reference. An adaptive protocol is therefore a common randomized processing of independent Bernoulli bits and parameter-independent resources.

The two scalar effects are chosen at separation `Delta` of order `delta/sqrt(N)`, so their future-`N` operational balls of radius `delta` are disjoint. A successful estimator gives a binary test with both errors at most `eta`. The product relative entropy is order `M Delta^2`, and the fidelity/testing inequality gives

```text
M >= c N delta^(-2) log(1/eta).
```

This lower bound is dimension independent. It does not establish optimal `d` dependence.

## 8. Detailed proof audit: exact matrix codec

### 8.1 Grid finiteness

For fixed `(d,N,delta)`, every independent Hermitian coordinate ranges over a finite integer interval divided by `K`. The legal subset is finite and lexicographically ordered.

The implementation's two-by-two minor filter removes only candidates violating necessary conditions. Full exact PSD tests remain decisive.

### 8.2 Positive-semidefinite testing

At a positive pivot, exact Schur complement preserves equivalence. At a zero diagonal pivot, a PSD matrix must have zero entries in that row and column; otherwise a two-dimensional principal restriction is indefinite. Recursion treats singular matrices correctly.

Both `G` and `I-G` are tested.

### 8.3 Exact modulus

The regularized Sylvester coefficient has eigenvalues bounded below by `1/N`, so it is invertible. Gaussian-rational input gives Gaussian-rational solution. The trace defining `Q_N^2` is a nonnegative rational real number.

The implementation checks reality, nonnegativity, and degeneracy at equality.

### 8.4 Greedy construction

The first legal grid point is retained. A later point is retained exactly when it is strictly farther than `eta` in `Q_N` from all retained centres. Every rejected point therefore has an earlier centre within the cutoff.

The strict inequality makes the dictionary deterministic at equality. The theorem never uses a triangle inequality for `Q_N`.

### 8.5 Target quantization

The inward displacement has spectral margin `s=2d/K`. Independent-coordinate rounding changes operator norm by at most `d/K`. The rounded matrix remains between `d/K` and `1-d/K` and is in the legal grid.

The target displacement is at most `3d/K`, giving the hybrid operational contribution `3delta/16`.

### 8.6 Greedy predecessor error

The retained predecessor has `Q_N<=delta/16`, so the metric upper contributes at most `delta/2`. Total operational error is `11delta/16`.

This holds at every rank and boundary point because the inward move precedes rounding.

### 8.7 Separation and codebook cardinality

Distinct retained centres satisfy `Q_N>delta/16`. The global metric lower converts this into actual operational separation of order `delta/d`. Choosing balls with radius below half that separation makes them disjoint.

The all-strata ball-volume lower bound applies to each centre. Summing their measures below the total weighted volume gives

```text
M <= C_d N^(d^2/2) log(N+2)^(floor(d/2)) delta^(-d^2).
```

This is a packing argument in `d_N`, not in `Q_N`.

### 8.8 Public decoder

Encoder and decoder perform the same complete enumeration and greedy retention from public parameters. The payload is the index padded to the reconstructed fixed length. Out-of-range words return `I/2`.

No centre coordinates are transmitted.

### 8.9 Real target interface

A Gaussian-rational target is encoded exactly. For a real target, a certified rational coordinate approximation at precision `O(1/K)` suffices before the inward move and grid rounding. The paper does not claim a finite representation for an arbitrary real oracle without such input data.

### 8.10 Complexity boundary

The ambient grid has up to

```text
(K+1)^d (2K+1)^(d(d-1))
```

coordinate tuples. Each legal candidate can require comparisons against many retained centres. Rational numerator and denominator sizes grow during exact elimination and Sylvester solves.

The theorem proves termination and payload order. It does not prove polynomial runtime, polynomial workspace, or practical reconstruction. This limitation is accurately stated.

### 8.11 Implementation behavior

`matrix_codec.py` constructs a codebook only after exhausting the legal stream. A candidate limit raises `IncompleteConstruction`. `audit-prefix` labels its output as a finite kernel audit and does not return a code or payload.

This correctly prevents a partial dictionary from being mistaken for a theorem codebook.

Zambrano--Ramos-Calderer--Kueng give dimension-optimal nonadaptive single-copy POVM tomography bounds in a one-use worst-input outcome metric. Current channel-learning lower-bound work further constrains the surrounding sample-complexity landscape.

The present learner differs through:

- the future experiment horizon `N`;
- an anisotropic loss whose boundary directions can scale as `1/N`;
- data-dependent entangled blocks of length at most `N`;
- simultaneous calibration of both effect endpoints; and
- fixed-dimensional optimality in `N`, `delta`, and `eta`.

These differences are real. They do not establish that no prior estimator, reanalysed under `d_N`, attains the same rate. Human specialist comparison remains necessary.

### 13.3 Coding antecedents

General compactness, rational density, and greedy nets are not new. The contribution is an exact public construction tied to this operational modulus, including support-boundary-safe rounding and actual-metric cardinality control.

The code is mathematically constructive but not algorithmically efficient.

### 13.4 Novelty status

The strongest plausible new theorem package is:

1. a closed-body finite-use comparison for ordered binary matrix effects;
2. its all-strata fixed-dimensional operational entropy with endpoint logarithms;
3. a common future-loss learner on the same body; and

This is sufficient for a strong specialist contribution. The evidence reviewed does not establish the broader transformative significance expected at the four leading general journals.
