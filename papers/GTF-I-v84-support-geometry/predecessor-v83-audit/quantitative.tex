\begin{abstract}
We study finite-use geometry and learning for ordered quantum measurements
with classical output and no residual device quantum system.  On balanced
interiors of noncommuting $k$-outcome measurements, the operational metric
is comparable to $\sqrt N$ times the maximum component operator norm.
We obtain the affine description entropy and a common learner with joint
query order $\Theta_k(N\delta^{-2}[d^2+d\log(1/\eta)])$ for fixed $k$.
The explicit upper bound has a $k^3$ factor; the lower allows arbitrary
coherent adaptive training.  All recordwise budgets and public stopping
conventions are retained.

On the complete measurement body we introduce a positive, concave
normalized-tuple covariance.  Its regularized inverse controls every
finite-use adaptive experiment, including changes of rank and zero
effects, and reduces exactly to the binary Sylvester variance.  An
energy-minimizing horizontal decomposition proves the bound.  The same
form yields a data-processing inequality and a sharp fixed-alphabet
noise--horizon crossover on a projective family.  Its rational upper
certificate has polynomial bit complexity.  A separate affine repair
map removes exhaustive search from balanced legalization while preserving
the learning and description orders.  Exact public dictionaries and
finite risk certificates remain available; general dictionary and
readout synthesis are not claimed efficient.

The complete binary boundary theory is supplied in the appendices.
Finite-outcome entropy assumes $0<\delta<1/128$; joint learning assumes
$0<\delta\le2^{-24}$ and $0<\eta\le1/8$.  The closed-body upper bound
holds for every integer $d,N\ge1$ and $k\ge2$.
\end{abstract}
