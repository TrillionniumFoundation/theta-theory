
\section{Round-fourteen positive closure: exact-number polymer coefficients and regular shells}
\label{sec:r14-b1}

The grand-canonical zero-particle atom is never Fourier inverted in the
continuous constraint variables.  Particle number is extracted first by a
Cauchy coefficient at its exact finite-volume saddle.  The remaining
canonical characteristic function is analyzed by a labelled trajectory
polymer expansion; no spatial independence at a growing propagation radius
is assumed.

\subsection{The coefficient-level polymer gas}

Let \(z\) be the activity and let \(H\) denote the complete bounded
particle-path and actual-contact source supplied by the grand-canonical part
of B2.  For a continuous constraint mark
\(Y\in\mathbb R^d\), put
\[
 \mathcal Z_\varepsilon(z,u;H)
 =
 \mathbb E_{\rm Pois}
 \left[
 z^{N_\varepsilon}
 e^{\mu_\varepsilon\langle H,\mathcal X_\varepsilon\rangle
   +iu\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{N_\varepsilon}Y_j}
 1_{\rm hc}
 \right].
\]
The connected trajectory expansion has the labelled exponential form
\[
 \mathcal Z_\varepsilon(z,u;H)
 =
 \sum_{N\ge0}{z^N\over N!}
 \sum_{\Pi\in\mathfrak P_N}
 \prod_{C\in\Pi}\zeta_{\varepsilon,H,u}(C),
\]
where \(C\) is a connected labelled trajectory polymer.  On the short-time
source chart, B2 proves the Koteck\'y--Preiss estimate
\[
 \sup_i\sum_{C\ni i}
 e^{a|C|}\,|\zeta_{\varepsilon,H,0}(C)|
 \le a_0<1 .
\]

\begin{lemma}[Many regular components or exponential penalty]
\label{lem:r14-b1-components}
There are \(m_0\), \(c_0,c_1>0\), and a finite collection of regular polymer
types \(\mathcal G\), each of size at most \(m_0\), such that in the
exact-\(N\) coefficient:
\[
 {1\over N!}[z^N]\mathcal Z_\varepsilon(z,0;H)
\]
the total absolute weight of partitions containing fewer than \(c_0N\)
members of \(\mathcal G\) is at most \(e^{-c_1N}\) times the total positive
coefficient.  On every \(G\in\mathcal G\), the summed mark map has a smooth
density whose characteristic function satisfies
\[
 |\widehat\nu_G(u)|\le C_s(1+|u|)^{-s}
\]
for every fixed \(s\), uniformly in the source chart.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Mark every polymer in \(\mathcal G\) by an auxiliary variable \(v\).  The
coefficient pressure
\[
 p_N(v)={1\over N}\log[z^N]\mathcal Z_\varepsilon(z,0;H;v)
\]
is analytic for \(|v-1|<\delta\), by the same polymer norm.  The singleton
and finitely many two-particle regular collision polymers give
\(\partial_{\log v}p_N(1)\ge2c_0\): their activities are uniformly positive
on fixed position and velocity patches, while the total activity of polymers
of size \(>m_0\) is made smaller than \(c_0\) by the exponential size tail.
Chernoff's inequality at \(v<1\) gives the exponential lower-tail estimate.

For a regular singleton, the velocity/position mark map has full-rank
Jacobian by the declared affine-span hypothesis.  When energy and momentum
make the singleton singular, use one of the finitely many regular
\(2,\ldots,m_0\)-particle polymers; the convolution map is a submersion on a
compact positive-activity patch.  Coarea gives a \(C^\infty\) density there,
and repeated integration by parts gives the Fourier estimate.  The finite
family covers every direction \(u/|u|\).
\end{proof}

This lemma is combinatorial and coefficient-level.  It neither partitions the
torus into spatially independent boxes nor discards high velocities.

\subsection{Exact particle-number extraction}

Let \(N_\varepsilon/\mu_\varepsilon\to n_0>0\).  Define
\[
 Z_{\varepsilon,N}(u;H)
 =[z^{N_\varepsilon}]\mathcal Z_\varepsilon(z,u;H).
\]
Let \(z_{\varepsilon,H,u}\) solve the finite-volume coefficient saddle
\[
 z\partial_z\log\mathcal Z_\varepsilon(z,u;H)=N_\varepsilon .
\]

\begin{theorem}[Uniform coefficient saddle]
\label{thm:r14-b1-saddle}
On a common complex neighborhood of \(u=0\) there is a unique saddle
\(z_{\varepsilon,H,u}\), analytic in \((H,u)\), and
\[
 {1\over\mu_\varepsilon}\log Z_{\varepsilon,N}(u;H)
 =
 {1\over\mu_\varepsilon}\log
 \mathcal Z_\varepsilon(z_{\varepsilon,H,u},u;H)
 -{N_\varepsilon\over\mu_\varepsilon}\log z_{\varepsilon,H,u}
 +o(1)
\]
locally uniformly with the first three derivatives.  The activity Hessian is
bounded above and below uniformly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the coefficient-positive real law,
\(z\partial_z\log\mathcal Z\) is the mean particle number and its derivative
is the variance.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r14-b1-components} and the positive
singleton activity give a variance between \(c\mu_\varepsilon\) and
\(C\mu_\varepsilon\).  The analytic implicit-function theorem gives the
saddle.  Cauchy's formula on \(|z|=|z_{\varepsilon,H,u}|\) has a Gaussian
central arc.  Outside that arc, the span-one particle count and the polymer
aperiodicity give an exponential gap.  Steepest descent yields the coefficient
formula and derivative convergence.
\end{proof}

After this extraction the particle number is fixed and is not included as a
Fourier coordinate.  Let
\[
 \varphi_{\varepsilon,N,H}(u)
 ={Z_{\varepsilon,N}(u;H)\over Z_{\varepsilon,N}(0;H)}
 e^{-iu\cdot\mathbb E_{N,H}\sum_jY_j}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Canonical full-frequency theorem]
\label{thm:r14-b1-fourier}
There are \(\delta<R<\infty\) and \(c>0\) such that:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
|u|\le\delta:&
 |\varphi_{\varepsilon,N,H}(u)|
 \le e^{-cN|u|^2},\\
\delta\le|u|\le R:&
 |\varphi_{\varepsilon,N,H}(u)|
 \le e^{-cN},\\
|u|>R:&
 |\varphi_{\varepsilon,N,H}(u)|
 \le e^{-cN}+C_s(1+|u|)^{-s-c_0N}.
\end{array}
\]
The estimates are uniform on compact source and target charts.  In
particular, for \(s>d+3\),
\[
 \int_{|u|>R}|\varphi_{\varepsilon,N,H}(u)|\,du
 =o(N^{-d/2}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The central estimate is the coefficient Hessian bound from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r14-b1-saddle}, including every cross term.  On the compact
annulus, equality in modulus would make \(u\cdot Y\) constant modulo
\(2\pi\) on every regular polymer type.  The affine-span assumption excludes
this; compactness and coefficient pressure analyticity give an exponential
gap.

For the tail, split coefficient partitions according to
Lemma~\ref{lem:r14-b1-components}.  The exceptional class has relative
weight \(e^{-cN}\).  Every remaining partition contains \(c_0N\) disjoint
regular components.  Condition on all other components and integrate their
marks; each contributes the smooth Fourier factor.  Using one integration
power per regular component and reserving \(s\) extra derivatives gives the
displayed exponent.  The exponential term is a coefficient weight, not a
frequency-independent integrand: on the exceptional class its continuous
mark law still has one fixed regular component, so it is multiplied by
\(C_s(1+|u|)^{-s}\).  Thus the actual bound is
\[
 C_s\left[e^{-cN}(1+|u|)^{-s}
 +(1+|u|)^{-s-c_0N}\right],
\]
whose integral has the stated order.  This also treats all singular
low-particle grand-canonical sectors, because they are absent after exact
\(N\)-extraction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Continuous constraints and the source-dependent saddle}

Let \(C\) denote momentum, energy, and the declared additional continuous
constraints, all expressed per particle.  For target \(a\) in the relative
interior of their affine support, let \(\lambda_{\varepsilon,H,a}\) solve
\[
 D_\lambda Q_{\varepsilon,N}(H,\lambda)=a,
 \qquad
 Q_{\varepsilon,N}
 ={1\over\mu_\varepsilon}\log
 Z_{\varepsilon,N}(0;H+C^*\lambda).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite source-dependent continuous saddle]
\label{thm:r14-b1-continuous}
The map \((H,a)\mapsto\lambda_{\varepsilon,H,a}\) exists uniquely on a common
regular chart and converges, with two derivatives, to
\(\lambda_{H,a}\).  Its Hessian is the full canonical covariance matrix,
including all cross terms, and
\[
 cI\le D_{\lambda\lambda}^2Q_{\varepsilon,N}\le CI .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The regular component family in
Lemma~\ref{lem:r14-b1-components} contains full-rank mark patches in every
constraint direction.  Since there are linearly many such components in the
coefficient law, their conditional covariance is at least \(cN\); the
polymer moment estimate gives the upper bound.  Properness on the relative
interior follows by sending a multiplier to the boundary of the convex mark
support.  Degree one and strict convexity yield a unique global saddle.
Derivative convergence follows from the coefficient pressure theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A quantified regular shell class}

Let \(G_{H,a}\) be the centered Gaussian with covariance equal to the Schur
complement after particle-number extraction.  A shell sequence
\(\mathcal W_\varepsilon\subset\mathbb R^d\) is regular when:
\[
 g_\varepsilon:=G_{H,a}(\mathcal W_\varepsilon)>0,\qquad
 \sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}\,\partial\mathcal W_\varepsilon
 \text{ has uniformly bounded Lipschitz charts},
\]
and smooth upper/lower approximations have Fourier error
\(r_\varepsilon=o(g_\varepsilon\mu_\varepsilon^{-d/2})\).
Centered expanding boxes with
\(\delta_\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and
\(\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}\delta_\varepsilon\to\infty\) are regular and have
\(g_\varepsilon\to1\).

\begin{theorem}[Mixed canonical shell coefficient]
\label{thm:r14-b1-shell}
Under the exact-\(N_\varepsilon\), source-\(H\) saddle law,
\[
 \mathbb P_{N,H}
 \left(
 \sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}(C-a)\in\mathcal W_\varepsilon
 \right)
 =
 g_\varepsilon(1+o(1)),
\]
and the unscaled coefficient equals
\[
 {e^{-\mu_\varepsilon I_{N,H}(a)}
  \over(2\pi\mu_\varepsilon)^{d/2}
        \sqrt{\det\Sigma_{N,H}}}
 \,G_{H,a}(\mathcal W_\varepsilon)\,(1+o(1)).
\]
The error is relative to the displayed Gaussian mass.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fourier inversion uses only the continuous variable \(u\).  On the central
ball, the coefficient pressure Taylor expansion gives the Gaussian.  The
compact annulus and high-frequency integrals are
\(o(g_\varepsilon\mu_\varepsilon^{-d/2})\) by
Theorem~\ref{thm:r14-b1-fourier} and the defining approximation error.
The saddle exponential gives \(I_{N,H}(a)\).  No grand-canonical atom remains.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Microcanonical transfer}

Let \(\mathcal S_{\varepsilon}(N,a,\mathcal W_\varepsilon)\) be the primitive
hard-core shell.  Put
\[
 Q^{\rm mc,a}_\varepsilon(H)
 ={1\over\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\mathbb E
 \left[
 e^{\mu_\varepsilon\langle H,\mathcal X_\varepsilon\rangle}
 \mid\mathcal S_\varepsilon
 \right].
\]

\begin{theorem}[Source-dependent microcanonical pressure]
\label{thm:r14-b1-main}
Uniformly on the complete B2 bounded-source chart,
\[
 Q^{\rm mc,a}_\varepsilon(H)\longrightarrow
 Q_N(H,\lambda_{H,a})-\lambda_{H,a}\cdot a
 -Q_N(0,\lambda_{0,a})+\lambda_{0,a}\cdot a .
\]
The initial fluctuation covariance is
\[
 \Sigma_{\rm mc}
 =\Sigma_{\rm can}
 -\Sigma_{\rm can}C^*
  (C\Sigma_{\rm can}C^*)^{-1}C\Sigma_{\rm can}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:r14-b1-shell} to the numerator at its own finite saddle
\(\lambda_{\varepsilon,H,a}\) and to the denominator at
\(\lambda_{\varepsilon,0,a}\).  The relative shell coefficients cancel at
exponential scale and remain controlled at polynomial scale.  Taking the
ratio gives the formula.  Two differentiations of the saddle equation give
the Schur complement.
\end{proof}
