\section{Round-seven reconstruction: sectorwise compound-Poisson smoothing and the correctly scaled shell}
\label{sec:r7-b1}

This section replaces the round-six controlling module.  The one-particle
momentum--energy mark is treated as a surface measure on its paraboloid.  Full
dimensional smoothing is obtained only after sufficiently many independent
particles.  Every smaller particle sector, including the empty sector, is
retained explicitly as an exponentially small error.  Normalized and extensive
constraint variables are kept distinct throughout.

\subsection{The singleton surface measure and its convolution rank}

Let
\[
 C(z)=(1,v,|v|^2/2,\chi_1(z),\ldots,\chi_d(z))
 \in\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^{4+d}
\]
be the number and continuous constraint mark.  Conditional on one particle,
the momentum--energy projection is supported on
$e=|p|^2/2$ and is therefore singular in four dimensions.  Denote the full
singleton measure by $\nu_1$.

For $n\ge1$ let
\[
 \Phi_n(z_1,\ldots,z_n)=\sum_{j=1}^n C'(z_j),
\]
where $C'$ is the continuous part.  Let $D_n\Phi_n$ be its differential on the
$n$-particle product.

\begin{lemma}[Finite convolution rank]
\label{lem:r7-b1-rank}
There is an integer $n_0\le 5+d$ and an open product set $U^{n_0}$ of positive
singleton activity on which $D_{n_0}\Phi_{n_0}$ has full rank $4+d$.
Consequently $\nu_1^{*n_0}$ has a $C^4$ density on an open set, and for every
$q$ there is $n(q)$ such that $\nu_1^{*n(q)}$ has a $C^q$ density with compactly
controlled derivatives on the finite saddle chart.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose four velocities near $ae_1,-ae_1,be_2,-be_2$ with $a\ne b$.
Variations of the four radial coordinates span the energy direction and three
independent momentum directions.  The remaining $d$ marks are separated by
the assumed activity patches for $\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_d$.  The resulting
Jacobian contains a nonzero $(4+d)$-minor.  The coarea formula gives an
absolutely continuous component of the $n_0$-fold convolution.  Convolving
with further copies differentiates under the integral and raises regularity;
compactness of the saddle chart makes all bounds uniform.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact Poisson-sector decomposition}

Let $a_{1,\varepsilon}$ be the total singleton activity and
$\widehat\nu_{1,\varepsilon}(t,u)$ its mixed lattice--continuous Fourier
transform.  The singleton exponential is exactly
\[
 \exp\{\mu_\varepsilon a_{1,\varepsilon}
       (\widehat\nu_{1,\varepsilon}(t,u)-1)\}
 =e^{-\mu_\varepsilon a_{1,\varepsilon}}
  \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(\mu_\varepsilon a_{1,\varepsilon})^n}{n!}
  \widehat\nu_{1,\varepsilon}(t,u)^n.
\]
Fix $n_*=n(M)$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-b1-rank}.  Split the sum into
$n<n_*$ and $n\ge n_*$.  The total probability of the first part is
\[
 e^{-\mu a_1}\sum_{n<n_*}\frac{(\mu a_1)^n}{n!}
 \le C_{n_*}\mu^{n_*-1}e^{-c\mu}.
\]
It includes the empty sector $e^{-\mu a_1}$.

Let $\mathfrak r_\varepsilon(t,u)$ be the connected cluster remainder.  The
B2-GC tree estimate gives, on the finite saddle chart,
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\mathfrak r_\varepsilon(t,u)
 \le C(T+\varepsilon),
 \qquad
 |\partial_u^j\mathfrak r_\varepsilon(t,u)|\le C_j(T+\varepsilon)
\]
for every fixed derivative order needed in the inversion.  Choose the local
preparation horizon so that this real-part loss is smaller than half the
positive-particle singleton gap.

\begin{theorem}[Correct mixed characteristic estimate]
\label{thm:r7-b1-characteristic}
For every $M$ there are $c,C_M>0$ such that uniformly on the exact finite saddle
chart,
\[
 |\varphi_\varepsilon(t,u)|
 \le C_M\mu_\varepsilon^{n_*-1}e^{-c\mu_\varepsilon}
 +C_M(1+\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}|u|)^{-M}
\]
for large continuous frequency, and
\[
 |\varphi_\varepsilon(t,u)|\le e^{-c\mu_\varepsilon}
\]
on every compact mixed minor arc away from the lattice origin.  No estimate
forces the characteristic function below the empty-sector atom.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The sectors $n<n_*$ are bounded by their total Poisson probability.  For
$n\ge n_*$, group the first $n_*$ marks and use the full-rank coarea density.
Repeated integration by parts gives arbitrary polynomial decay after enough
marks; the remaining marks have Fourier modulus at most one.  The derivatives
of $e^{\mu\mathfrak r_\varepsilon}$ are controlled by the connected bounds,
and its real part is dominated by the singleton gap.  On compact minor arcs,
the lattice aperiodicity and the continuous convolution rank give a fixed
strict modulus loss.  Summing the sectors proves both estimates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform covariance and the finite saddle}

Let
\[
 Q_\varepsilon(H,\lambda)
 =\mu_\varepsilon^{-1}
  \log\mathbb E\exp\{\mu_\varepsilon\langle H,X_\varepsilon\rangle
  +\lambda\cdot C_{\rm tot}\}.
\]
The normalized constraint mean is
\[
 a_\varepsilon(H,\lambda)=D_\lambda Q_\varepsilon(H,\lambda)
 =\mu_\varepsilon^{-1}
   \mathbb E_{H,\lambda}C_{\rm tot}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Compound-sector covariance]
\label{lem:r7-b1-covariance}
On every compact source and multiplier chart there is $c_0>0$ such that
\[
 D_{\lambda\lambda}^2Q_\varepsilon(H,\lambda)\ge c_0I
\]
for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.  The lower bound is supplied by the
positive-activity $n_0$-particle sector of
Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-b1-rank}; it does not assume a full-dimensional singleton
density.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition on a small product patch inside $U^{n_0}$.  Its Poisson probability
per unit activity is bounded below uniformly on the saddle chart.  The
full-rank Jacobian gives a strictly positive covariance matrix on that event.
Variance decomposition transfers this lower bound to the compound law.  The
connected correction has operator norm $O(T+\varepsilon)$ and is absorbed by
choosing the preparation chart within the B2 convergence radius.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-volume saddle and mean image]
\label{thm:r7-b1-saddle}
Let $A$ be a compact subset of the interior limiting mean image.  For every
bounded source $H$ and $a\in A$ there is a unique exact finite-volume
multiplier $\lambda_{H,a,\varepsilon}$ satisfying
\[
 D_\lambda Q_\varepsilon(H,\lambda_{H,a,\varepsilon})=a.
\]
The multipliers and all finite source derivatives converge uniformly to the
limiting saddle.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-b1-covariance} gives uniform local injectivity.  On the
boundary of a slightly larger multiplier box, the support function of the
compound mark points strictly outward because the activity patches span every
constraint direction.  Brouwer degree is therefore one for every target in
$A$.  Strict convexity gives uniqueness.  Uniform pressure convergence and the
implicit-function theorem give convergence of the saddle and its derivatives.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The correctly extensive shell}

Let $N_\varepsilon$ be the particle number and let $Y_\varepsilon$ be the
extensive continuous total.  For target
$a=(\rho,a')$ define
\[
 \mathcal S_\varepsilon(a)=
 \left\{N_\varepsilon=\lfloor\mu_\varepsilon\rho\rfloor,
 \quad
 Y_\varepsilon-\mu_\varepsilon a'
   \in\mu_\varepsilon\delta_\varepsilon B\right\},
\]
where
\[
 \delta_\varepsilon\downarrow0,
 \qquad
 \sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}\delta_\varepsilon\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Equivalently the normalized variable
$Y_\varepsilon/\mu_\varepsilon$ lies in
$a'+\delta_\varepsilon B$.  The shell therefore contains an increasing number
of central-limit standard deviations while shrinking macroscopically.

\begin{theorem}[Mixed lattice--continuous shell coefficient]
\label{thm:r7-b1-coefficient}
Uniformly for $a$ in compact subsets of the mean image and bounded sources
$H$,
\[
 \mathbb P_{H,\lambda_{H,a,\varepsilon}}
  (\mathcal S_\varepsilon(a))
 =\frac{1+o(1)}{
   \sqrt{2\pi\mu_\varepsilon\sigma_{N|Y}^2}}
 \int_{\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}\delta_\varepsilon B}
  \phi_{\Sigma_{Y|N}}(y)\,dy
 +O(\mu_\varepsilon^{n_*-1}e^{-c\mu_\varepsilon}).
\]
In particular the probability is subexponential and is bounded above and below
by positive polynomial multiples of $\mu_\varepsilon^{-1/2}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use Fourier inversion in the exact lattice coordinate and smooth upper and
lower approximations of the extensive continuous shell.  In the central box,
expand at the exact finite saddle from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-b1-saddle}; the lattice coefficient and continuous
Gaussian integral give the displayed Schur complements.  Compact minor arcs
and the large-frequency region are controlled by
Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-b1-characteristic}.  The atomic and small-particle sectors
remain as the explicit exponentially small term.  Boundary smoothing is
$o(\mu^{-1/2})$ because
$\sqrt\mu\delta_\varepsilon\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Source-dependent ensemble transfer}

Define the microcanonical normalized pressure
\[
 Q_\varepsilon^{\rm mc,a}(H)
 =\mu_\varepsilon^{-1}
  \log\mathbb E^{\rm gc}
  \left[e^{\mu_\varepsilon\langle H,X_\varepsilon\rangle}
  \mid\mathcal S_\varepsilon(a)\right].
\]

\begin{theorem}[Microcanonical pressure and covariance]
\label{thm:r7-b1-transfer}
Locally uniformly in $H$,
\[
 Q_\varepsilon^{\rm mc,a}(H)\longrightarrow
 \inf_\lambda\{Q(H,\lambda)-\lambda\cdot a\}
 -\inf_\lambda\{Q(0,\lambda)-\lambda\cdot a\}.
\]
Its Hessian at a regular source is the exact Schur complement
\[
 Q_{HH}-Q_{H\lambda}Q_{\lambda\lambda}^{-1}Q_{\lambda H}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Change measure in the numerator by the exact finite saddle
$\lambda_{H,a,\varepsilon}$.  On the extensive shell the multiplier term is
$-\mu_\varepsilon\lambda_{H,a,\varepsilon}\cdot a+o(\mu_\varepsilon)$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-b1-coefficient} makes the residual shell probability
subexponential, including all atomic sectors.  Repeat with $H=0$ for the
denominator.  The envelope theorem and
Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-b1-covariance} give the Hessian.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Prepared initial large deviations}

Use bounded time-zero cylinder tests and the conserved Maxwellian moment.  The
finite-dimensional lower bound follows from the exact saddle and pressure gap;
boundary mean points are reached by mixtures of interior saddles and admissible
constraint-preserving patches.

\begin{theorem}[Prepared empirical-measure LDP]
\label{thm:r7-b1-ldp}
Under the correctly scaled shell, the prepared initial empirical measure
satisfies a good LDP in the weak topology augmented by the conserved
Maxwellian Lyapunov moment.  Its rate is the grand-canonical entropy restricted
to the constraint surface and shifted by its constrained minimum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-b1-transfer} gives every finite projected pressure.
Exact finite-saddle tilts and strict projected covariance give local lower
bounds at interior means.  Convex mixtures with smooth constraint-preserving
patches approximate boundary finite-rate measures.  Conservation gives
exponential tightness.  Dawson--Gartner and the topology-upgrade lemma complete
the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-seven B1 closure]
\label{thm:r7-b1-main}
The physical one-particle paraboloid is treated as a singular surface measure;
smoothing begins in a full-rank compound sector; every atomic and small-sector
contribution is retained; the shell is centered at the extensive total
$\mu_\varepsilon a'$ with width $\mu_\varepsilon\delta_\varepsilon$; and the
source-dependent coefficient, covariance, ensemble transfer, and prepared LDP
follow at the exact finite saddle.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Lemmas~\ref{lem:r7-b1-rank} and
\ref{lem:r7-b1-covariance} with
Theorems~\ref{thm:r7-b1-characteristic},
\ref{thm:r7-b1-saddle},
\ref{thm:r7-b1-coefficient},
\ref{thm:r7-b1-transfer}, and
\ref{thm:r7-b1-ldp}.
\end{proof}
