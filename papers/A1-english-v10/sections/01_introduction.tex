\section{Introduction}
A finite experiment creates information at one rate and uses it at
another. The information that must persist is determined by the tests
that remain available, not by the full accumulated likelihood alone.
For positive sparse monomial experiments we compute this persistent
information, both exactly and at prescribed accuracy. We then determine
how the answer composes when several independent experiments share one
memory and their observations are interleaved.

There are two different sources of variation. Additive coincidences among
future exponents change which directions can be tested. Near a coincidence,
a direction can be exactly observable but have arbitrarily small
statistical amplitude. Independently, the order in which several
experiments are observed changes how many of their attained directions
must be retained at the same time. The first phenomenon requires a
collision-uniform resolution law. The second requires a joint coding
converse, not merely the construction of separate block quantizers.

\subsection{The scalar resolution law}
Let
\[
 A=\{0=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_{r-1}=D\},\quad r\ge2,
 \qquad W_A=\spanop\{t^a:a\in A\}.
\]
Write $mA$ for sums of exactly $m$ elements of $A$, including zero
padding, and $h_A(m)=|mA|$, with $0A=\{0\}$. A positive finite
detector has cells spanning $W_A$. Its commands are independent lookup
entries in a fixed cube $[\eta,1-\eta]^J$, $0<\eta<1/2$; the
accepted cell label or the failure symbol is reported. The full experiment
is defined in Section~\ref{sec:experiment}.

For the uniform assertion the hidden scalar lies in $[0,1]$ with one
fixed full-support prior $\mu$. The known nonzero exponents range over
a compact set $K$ inside $0<a_1<\cdots<a_{r-1}$. A fixed coefficient
matrix of column rank $r$ realizes the cells throughout $K$; their sum
is one and their common lower bound is positive. Fix a horizon $N$ and
$H\ge\max\{1,N\max_Ka_{r-1}\}$. At a checkpoint with $n$ past and
$m=N-n$ remaining trials, the positive formal homogeneous labels
$\alpha\in\N_0^r$, $|\alpha|=m$, $\alpha\ne me_0$, give nodes
$x_\alpha=H^{-1}\sum_i\alpha_i a_i$. Their number is $q_m$.
They are retained as formal labels when their values coincide. Put
\[
 \mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a)=
 \max_{|J|=\ell}\prod_{\{\alpha,\beta\}\subset J}|x_\alpha-x_\beta|,
 \qquad \mathcal V_{m,1}=1,
 \qquad p_{n,m}=\min\{n(r-1),q_m\},
\]
where $J$ ranges over positive formal labels. A volume is zero if fewer
than $\ell$ distinct sites remain. Define
\[
 \Psi_{n,m}(M,a)=\max_{1\le\ell\le p_{n,m}}
                  (\mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a)/M)^{2/\ell},
 \qquad \Xi_N(M,a)=\max_{1\le n<N}\Psi_{n,N-n}(M,a),
\]
with zero checkpoint profile when $n=0$ or $m=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic attainable resolution and causal memory]\label{thm:resolution-main}
Under these hypotheses there are constants $0<c\le C<\infty$,
depending only on the fixed experiment, $K$, $N$ and $\mu$, with the
following properties for every known calibration $a\in K$ and every
integer $M\ge1$.

At each checkpoint $n+m=N$, the optimal $M$-message squared-prediction
regret is between $c\Psi_{n,m}(M,a)$ and $C\Psi_{n,m}(M,a)$,
both unconditionally under independent uniform acquisition commands and
in the minimax sense over admitted histories. The query is sampled
independently after the index is formed, from the ordered products of
one fixed attainable failure-factor basis; all its $m$ trials are counted.

There exists one deterministic stage-compatible transducer, allowed to
depend on $a$ and $M$, storing at most $M$ persistent labels after
every report, whose regret at every checkpoint and on every history is
at most $C\Xi_N(M,a)$. Conversely every such transducer has maximum
unconditional checkpoint regret at least $c\Xi_N(M,a)$ under the
same common exploration law. Independent coding randomization does not
remove the lower bound. The clock and fixed real-valued program are
read-only as in Definition~\ref{def:finite-state}.

The constants are uniform across all additive collision strata of $K$.
No calibration-blind codebook or uniformity over all full-support priors
is part of the assertion.
\end{theorem}

The proof has three parts. At a common interior product of binomials,
the tangent pairs at full rank with every initial Newton--Hermite flag,
including at repeated nodes. Actual exploration histories therefore
minorize a uniformly sized resolved cube, with their acquisition evidence
retained. A bounded-format covering estimate controls the entire reachable
image, with the products of scales truncated at the past capacity.
Finally, raw moment updates have no inverse exponent gaps, so one
persistent index realizes these covers through all stages. These parts
are proved in that order in Sections~\ref{sec:rank}--\ref{sec:collision-geometry}.

The determinant profile has classical finite spectral content. If $W_m$
is the square ordinary Vandermonde matrix on the positive formal nodes,
then $\mathcal V_{m,\ell}\asymp\|\bigwedge^\ell W_m\|$,
uniformly even at coincidences; Proposition~\ref{prop:ordinary-spectrum}
records the precise comparison. It does not prove attainable posterior
geometry or compatibility with causal compression. The arbitrary-prefix
Hermite fact is likewise isolated as Lemma~\ref{lem:hermite-prefix}
before it is used in that geometry.

\subsection{Composition with one shared memory}
Take $B$ independent scalar experiments of this class, with possibly
different detectors, exponent sets, priors and horizons $N_b$. Their
joint hidden parameter is a vector with the product prior. A deterministic
schedule $\omega$ specifies which block is observed on each of
$T=\sum_bN_b$ trials. At time $s$ its count vector is $(n_b(s))$,
with $m_b(s)=N_b-n_b(s)$. A future query chooses a product probe in
every block and predicts the all-failure event of the resulting tensor
query. All $T-s$ remaining trials are executed. There is one shared
memory index, not an uncharged tuple of separate block memories.

At a block checkpoint put $V_{b,0}=1$ and
$V_{b,\ell}=\mathcal V_{b,m_b,\ell}(a^{(b)})$ for
$1\le\ell\le p_b=\min\{n_b(r_b-1),q_{b,m_b}\}$. When $n_b=0$
or $m_b=0$ put $p_b=0$. Define the real bit profile
\[
 b_b(n_b,\varepsilon,a^{(b)})=
 \max_{0\le\ell\le p_b}
 \left\{\frac{\ell}{2}\log_2\frac1\varepsilon+\log_2V_{b,\ell}\right\}.
\]
The zero-index term is zero and zero positive-index volumes are omitted.

\begin{theorem}[Shared-memory information law]\label{thm:composition-main}
For these fixed block experiments, the least integer persistent bit budget
for maximum-checkpoint squared-prediction regret at most $\varepsilon$
is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-shared-bits}
 B_\omega^*(\varepsilon,a)=
 \max_s\sum_b b_b(n_b(s),\varepsilon,a^{(b)})+O(1),
 \qquad 0<\varepsilon\le1.
\end{equation}
This holds both for maximum-history regret and for unconditional regret
under one common independent uniform exploration law. The bounded
remainder is uniform in accuracy, calibration and schedule. The lower
bound permits arbitrary joint randomized encoders; the upper bound is
realized by one stage-compatible index-only transducer.

Writing $L_b=\max_n b_b(n,\varepsilon,a^{(b)})$, the smallest
possible budget over schedules is $\max_bL_b+O(1)$, attained by every
serial schedule. The largest is $\sum_bL_b+O(1)$, attained in order
by a schedule that brings all blocks to their respective maximizing
counts. A fixed serial schedule is optimal in this comparison sense
simultaneously for every calibration and accuracy.
\end{theorem}

The all-budget regret form and complete proof are
Theorem~\ref{thm:shared-state}; its logarithmic inversion and scheduling
consequences are Theorems~\ref{thm:shared-bits} and
\ref{thm:schedule-extrema}. The joint attainable image is a product
embedded by a multilinear map, not a full tensor-dimensional body.
Its metric is recovered from the full tensor query menu by a bounded
linear map defined even on arbitrary decoded centers. A product of
blockwise dimension-truncated covers yields the upper bound, while a
product of attained subprobability rectangles yields a converse against
unrestricted joint coding. The raw updates then prove causal compatibility.
Thus \eqref{eq:intro-shared-bits} is not obtained by defining the optimum
to be a product code.

This gives an observation-order effect with a sharp change of exponent.
For $B$ independent two-exponent blocks, each with horizon $2h$, a serial
schedule has streaming regret $\asymp M^{-2/h}$; a schedule bringing
all blocks to their middle checkpoint has regret
$\asymp M^{-2/(Bh)}$. The number of raw trials is $2Bh$ in both
cases and the query protocol is the same full tensor construction
(Corollary~\ref{cor:concurrency-exponent}). The saving uses the specified
future-only task: it does not erase an externally requested past payoff.

\subsection{Exact sufficiency and the proof structure}
The exact scalar law also holds on a fixed interval $I=[l,u]$,
$0\le l<u<\infty$, with a full-support prior. Its role is to identify
the attained tangent before any finite-error comparison is made.

\begin{theorem}[Sparse finite-horizon information law]\label{thm:main}
Suppose the positive finite detector spans $W_A$ and every lookup entry
ranges over $[\eta,1-\eta]$, where $0<\eta<1/2$. For $n,m\ge0$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-dimension}
 d_A(n,m)=\min\{n(r-1),h_A(m)-1\}.
\end{equation}
Two attainable posteriors after $n$ reports have the same law under every
common future-only continuation of length $m$ if and only if their moments
at the exponents in $mA$ agree. The prediction map on all-failure command
histories has generic rank $d_A(n,m)$, and it has a smooth attainable patch
of this dimension with a continuous section to histories. The smallest
Euclidean dimension of a sufficient encoding continuous on each
known-report history stratum is exactly $d_A(n,m)$.

For a total budget $N$, these dimensions can be attained simultaneously
by a causal exact-real state. Its maximal size is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peak}
 D_A(N)=\max_{0\le n\le N}d_A(n,N-n).
\end{equation}
Only prior moments with exponents in $NA$ are needed for its updates.
\end{theorem}

For independent blocks the exact dimensions add at a checkpoint and their
sum is then maximized over the schedule
(Theorem~\ref{thm:product-dimension}). Continuous encodings here are
encodings of known-command histories on each report stratum. They need
not identify different factorizations of the same posterior, and no
global chart of a singular quotient is assumed.

The principal proof proceeds from the observation experiment and memory
model to the product tangent, complete Hermite flags, the global covering
estimate, the collision-uniform law, and finally the shared-state law.
The observation-algebra calculations and the full affine, five-trial,
seven-trial, control, decision and mechanical arguments are given in
Appendices~\ref{sec:algebra}--\ref{sec:tree-convexity}. Their distinct
operational hypotheses are not replaced by the product experiment.
In particular, the binary ticket identity transfers prediction loss to
one specified common payoff; it is not a universal optimal-control lower
bound. The separate control upper bound retains its weaker exponent.

\subsection{Relation to the classical ingredients}
Predictive state representations, approximate recursively updated
information states and squared-error quantization are established subjects
\cite{LittmanSutton,Subramanian,KaraYuksel,GrafLuschgy}. The mixed and
confluent determinant arguments use classical total positivity
\cite{Pinkus}. The sumset interpretation of a homogeneous observation
algebra is also classical \cite{EliahouMazumdar,Elias}. The Newton--Hermite
prefix principle is explicitly stated in \cite[Proposition 7, p.~48]{deBoor};
finite Leja orderings and their linear-algebraic implementations are
classical \cite{Leja,BosEtAl}. The real covering input is separated in
Lemma~\ref{lem:tame-rectangle}, using
\cite{YomdinComte,ComteHalupczok,ZhangKileel}.

Clustered Fourier Vandermonde spectra are studied in
\cite[Theorems 2.2--2.3]{BatenkovEtAl}; the matrix comparison used here
concerns an ordinary finite real Vandermonde matrix and is proved directly.
Neither comparison supplies the attained history distribution or the
persistent-index converse. Functional product quantization
\cite[Sections 1.1--1.2 and 5.5]{GuoKaragodinStepanov} optimizes output
error when input components are separately quantized. In the present
problem the histories are generated sequentially, the future menu spans
a tensor observation algebra, and the converse permits an arbitrary
joint $M$-message code. Its uniform constants include exponent
coincidences, while its one-index upper bound accumulates all preceding
compression errors. The elementary multiplication of covering estimates
is used as such, rather than presented as a new general quantization
principle.

The finite spectral comparison and the convex allocation argument in
Appendix~\ref{sec:tree-convexity} were made explicit in the source-pinned
technical note \cite{A1v9note}; they are not reassigned as independent
contributions of the composition theorem. The specific attainable
pairing, the global image bound, the joint coding converse and their
causal compatibility are the mathematical assertions proved here.
The full earlier mechanical and likelihood derivations remain separate
sources \cite{A1v5,A1v6,A1round33}; no long-time conclusion is imported
from their historical labels into a fixed-horizon proof.

All uniform constants in the resolved theorems are for fixed priors,
detectors, command cubes and finite horizons. Each known calibration and
budget may have its own read-only exact-real program. The product theorem
requires a product prior. Neither prior-uniform conditioning, effective
codebook synthesis, calibration-blind filtering nor arbitrary hidden-
parameter terminal losses are implicit in these statements.
