\section{A global dimension-truncated covering estimate}\label{sec:global-cover}
We first record the real-geometric input with its anisotropic consequence.
A bounded semialgebraic format means a bound on the number of variables,
polynomial relations and their degrees in a defining formula; arbitrary
real coefficients are allowed. Quantified formulas of fixed format can
also be used, since real quantifier elimination gives a bound depending
only on this format. Neither a coefficient norm nor a lower bound on a
nonzero coefficient is part of the definition.

\begin{lemma}[Bounded-format sets in a thin rectangle]\label{lem:tame-rectangle}
Let $S$ be a nonempty compact semialgebraic subset of
$\prod_{i=1}^{q}[-a_i,a_i]$, where $a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_q\ge0$.
Suppose its format is bounded and its dimension is at most $p$, with
$1\le p\le q$. There is a constant $C$ depending only on the format
and $q$ such that, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tame-cover-count}
 \mathcal N(S,\varepsilon)
 \le C\sum_{j=0}^{p}\varepsilon^{-j}\prod_{i=1}^{j}a_i.
\end{equation}
The empty product is one. Consequently $S$ has a covering by at most
$M$ representatives belonging to $S$, of radius at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tame-cover-radius}
 C'\max_{1\le j\le p}
           \left(M^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{j}a_i\right)^{1/j}.
\end{equation}
If all $a_i$ vanish, one representative suffices with radius zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We use two classical real-geometric facts. First, intersections of a
bounded-format semialgebraic set with arbitrary affine subspaces have a
uniform bound on their number of connected components. The bound is
independent of the coefficients; one formulation is the regularity
statement in \cite[Lemma 2.18]{ZhangKileel}, together with real quantifier
elimination. Second, the real Vitushkin--Ivanov entropy inequality is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vitushkin-input}
 \mathcal N(S,\varepsilon)\le C_q\sum_{j=0}^q
       \varepsilon^{-j}V_j(S),\qquad
 V_j(S)=c_{q,j}\int b_0(S\cap L)\,\dd L.
\end{equation}
For $j>0$ the integral is over affine subspaces of codimension $j$,
with normalized rotational measure and Lebesgue translation measure;
$V_0(S)=b_0(S)$. This is the classical real inequality in
\cite[Theorem 3.5]{YomdinComte}, also recalled as equations (4)--(5)
in \cite{ComteHalupczok}. No nonarchimedean result is used here.

For $j>p$, almost every such affine section is empty: its translation
parameter lies outside a projection of dimension at most $p<j$.
Thus $V_j(S)=0$. For $1\le j\le p$, write an affine section as
$L=u+E^\perp$, $u\in E$, for a $j$-plane $E$. If it meets $S$,
then $u$ belongs to the projection onto $E$ of the containing rectangle.
The component bound, denoted $B$, therefore gives
\[
 V_j(S)\le c_{q,j}B
      \int_{G(q,j)}\operatorname{vol}_j
       \left(\operatorname{proj}_E\prod_i[-a_i,a_i]\right)\dd E.
\]
This projected rectangle is a zonotope. Its volume is the sum, over
$j$-element subsets of the coordinate directions, of their parallelepiped
volumes. Each corresponding orthogonal-projection minor has absolute
value at most one. Hence its volume is at most
$2^j\binom qj a_1\cdots a_j$. Also $V_0(S)\le B$.
Substitution into \eqref{eq:vitushkin-input} proves
\eqref{eq:tame-cover-count}, including zero-width axes. This argument
explains why the products stop at the dimension of $S$, not at the
number of sides of its ambient rectangle.

To respect the integer budget, let $e$ be the maximum in
\eqref{eq:tame-cover-radius} without its constant. If $a_1>0$, then
$e>0$ and $a_1\cdots a_j\le Me^j$ for every $j\le p$.
Choose $L\ge2$ with $C\sum_{j=1}^p L^{-j}\le1/2$. For
$M\ge2C$, \eqref{eq:tame-cover-count} at radius $Le$ is at most
$C+M/2\le M$. Discard empty balls and replace each center by a point
of $S$ in its ball; the radius at most doubles. For $M<2C$, a single
point of $S$ covers it at radius $2\sqrt q\,a_1$, while
$e\ge a_1/M\ge a_1/(2C)$. Increasing the constant proves the assertion
for these budgets as well. A nearest-representative map, with a fixed
tie convention, is Borel. If $a_1=0$, the set is a singleton.
\end{proof}

The bounded-format hypothesis is essential to a uniform statement of
this kind. An unrestricted smooth image could develop arbitrarily many
sheets. In the present experiment this hypothesis is a consequence of
the finite product likelihood, not an extra regularity assumption on the
prior or on an unknown attainable variety.
