\section{Shared memory for independent experiments}\label{sec:shared-memory}
The scalar classification is stable under taking independent products,
although the corresponding future query space is a tensor product. The
persistent resource is one joint index. In particular, the converse below
does not assume a separate encoder, a product codebook, or a separate
memory budget for each experiment.

\subsection{The product experiment}
Fix $B\ge1$. For each $b\in\{1,\ldots,B\}$ take an experiment satisfying
Section~\ref{sec:collision-geometry}, with exponent set $A_b$, number of
one-step exponents $r_b$, compact calibration set $K_b$, coefficient
matrix $C_b$, command cube, full-support prior $\mu_b$, and horizon
$N_b\ge2$. These data, including $B$, are fixed. The hidden parameter is
$t=(t_1,\ldots,t_B)\in[0,1]^B$ with prior
$\mu=\bigotimes_{b=1}^B\mu_b$. Conditional on $t$, all raw trials are
independent. A trial assigned to block $b$ uses only that block's detector
and current command. The known calibration is
$a=(a^{(1)},\ldots,a^{(B)})\in\prod_bK_b$.

A deterministic schedule $\omega$ is a word of length
$T=\sum_bN_b$ containing $N_b$ copies of $b$. After $s$ acquisition
trials let $n_b(s)$ be the number assigned to $b$ and put
$m_b(s)=N_b-n_b(s)$. At that checkpoint sample independently and uniformly
one member of each block's ordered $m_b$-fold product probe menu. A block
with $m_b=0$ contributes the constant factor one. The selected probe
executes all $T-s$ remaining trials, in the residual schedule order. Its
target $Y$ is the indicator that every one of those reports is a failure.
All trials are consumed, including after a nonfailure. Thus $s$ acquisition
trials and the selected query together always use exactly $T$ trials.
There is one selected checkpoint per run, not simultaneous execution of
all the counterfactual queries.

We use the squared-loss excess relative to the full-history Bayes
predictor. The decoder receives the query tuple only after the acquisition
index has been formed. Its persistent memory has at most $M$ values in
total, with the resource convention of Definition~\ref{def:finite-state}.
The schedule and clock are read-only; previous commands and reports are
not. For the unconditional criterion all acquisition commands, in every
block and at every time, are independent and uniform on their respective
cubes, independently of the hidden parameter and coding randomization.
This is a single acquisition law whose prefixes serve all checkpoints.

Write $\overline R^a_{M;s,\omega}$ and $R^{a,\max}_{M;s,\omega}$ for
the optimal unconditional and maximum-history checkpoint regrets.
Write $\mathfrak R^{a,\mathrm{av}}_{M,\omega}$ and
$\mathfrak R^{a,\max}_{M,\omega}$ for the infima over one stage-compatible
filter of, respectively, its maximum unconditional checkpoint regret and
its maximum regret over checkpoints and histories. A checkpoint encoder
may inspect its full prefix once; a streaming transition may not.

For $n,m>0$, let $q_{b,m}$ and $\mathcal V_{b,m,\ell}$ be the positive
formal label count and determinant volumes of block $b$. Put
\[
 p_b(n,m)=\min\{n(r_b-1),q_{b,m}\}.
\]
Set $p_b(n,m)=0$ when $n=0$ or $m=0$. At a fixed checkpoint abbreviate
$p_b=p_b(n_b,m_b)$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-volumes}
 V_{b,0}=1,\qquad
 V_{b,\ell}=\mathcal V_{b,m_b,\ell}(a^{(b)})
       \quad(1\le\ell\le p_b),\qquad P_s=\sum_b p_b.
\end{equation}
A block not yet observed, as well as a completed block, has one reachable
future state and contributes only $V_{b,0}$. Formal positive labels are
not discarded at collisions; zero volumes are allowed. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composition-volumes}
 \begin{split}
 A_{s,k}(a)&=
 \max_{\substack{0\le\ell_b\le p_b\\\sum_b\ell_b=k}}
              \prod_{b=1}^B V_{b,\ell_b},\qquad A_{s,0}=1,\\
 \Theta_{s,\omega}(M,a)&=
       \max_{1\le k\le P_s}(A_{s,k}(a)/M)^{2/k},\\
 \Theta_\omega(M,a)&=\max_{0\le s\le T}\Theta_{s,\omega}(M,a).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
An empty maximum for the checkpoint profile is zero. The dependence of
$A_{s,k}$ on the schedule is through the count vector $(n_b(s))$.
The operation in the first line is associative: grouping several blocks
only groups the same finite maximization over allocations. We shall need
its full finite-error form, not just its largest nonzero index.

\subsection{Tensor queries retain the component metric}
For block $b$, let $v_b$ be its formal raw moment vector indexed by
$\Lambda_{b,m_b}$, including the constant entry $v_{b,m_be_0}=1$.
For $m_b=0$ this vector is $(1)$. Let $\mathcal S_b$ be its complete
reachable image at the checkpoint. If $n_b=0$, it is the singleton given
by the known prior. All its entries lie in $[0,1]$.

\begin{lemma}[Product attainment and the tensor metric]\label{lem:product-metric}
The joint posterior after an admitted history is the product of its block
posteriors, and the set of attainable raw tuples is exactly
$\prod_b\mathcal S_b$. If $Q(v)$ is the vector of probabilities of the
full tensor query menu, then uniformly in calibration,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor-metric}
 c\sum_b\|v_b-\widetilde v_b\|^2
 \le \|Q(v)-Q(\widetilde v)\|^2
 \le C\sum_b\|v_b-\widetilde v_b\|^2.
\end{equation}
There is, moreover, a bounded linear map on the entire query-coordinate
space that takes $Q(v)$ to the concatenated vector $(v_b)_b$.
The bound for this map is independent of exponent gaps.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Group the likelihood factors of a realized history by block, writing
$F_b$ for their product. Its evidence is
$\int\prod_bF_b(t_b)\,\bigotimes_b\mu_b(\dd t_b)
=\prod_b\mu_bF_b$. Bayes normalization consequently gives the product
posterior. Every tuple of admitted block histories can be interleaved in
the prescribed schedule; every report word has positive evidence.
This proves the statement about the whole attainable image, not only a
local family. The argument also applies to commands chosen using past
reports: for each fixed realized history the chosen likelihood factors
are known. The lower law, however, uses the independent commands already
specified, not an adaptive command law.

Let $G_b$ be the fixed, full-column-rank formal coefficient matrix for the
block's product probes, and take $G_b=(1)$ for $m_b=0$. Independence gives
\[
 Q(v)=G\bigotimes_bv_b,\qquad G=\bigotimes_bG_b.
\]
Each $G_b$ is independent of calibration, hence $G$ has a bounded fixed
left inverse $G^\dagger$. In the tensor $\bigotimes_bv_b$, selecting
entries in which only the $b$th index varies and every other index is the
constant label gives precisely $v_b$. Let $E$ be the concatenation of
these coordinate-selection maps. Then
$E G^\dagger Q(v)=(v_b)_b$, proving the last assertion and the lower
inequality. The repeated constant entries cause no difficulty.
For the upper inequality expand the difference of the two tensor products
as a telescoping sum, changing one factor at a time. Every other factor
has norm bounded by the square root of its fixed coordinate count.
Cauchy--Schwarz and the fixed bound on $G$ give the upper inequality.
All constants are finite-dimensional; none contains a reciprocal
exponent separation.
\end{proof}

Although $Q(\prod_b\mathcal S_b)$ lies in a nonlinear tensor-product
image, its metric is thus the component product metric. In particular,
its tensor ambient dimension is not the attainable information dimension.
The existence of the linear map on arbitrary query centers will also be
needed for a converse against unrestricted joint encoders.

\begin{theorem}[Exact information dimension under composition]\label{thm:product-dimension}
At checkpoint $s$, the smallest Euclidean dimension of a sufficient
history encoding continuous on each report stratum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-dimension}
 d_\omega(s,a)=\sum_{b=1}^B
       \min\{n_b(s)(r_b-1),\ |m_b(s)A_b|-1\}.
\end{equation}
Sufficiency is for every common future-only continuation in the prescribed
remaining block schedule. These dimensions are attained simultaneously
by a causal exact-real state. Its least possible peak size is
$D_\omega(a)=\max_s d_\omega(s,a)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each realized future word has a likelihood in the tensor product of the
block future observation spaces. Equality of the block future moments
therefore implies equality of every such word probability under a common
feedback continuation; integrating independent feedback randomization
preserves that assertion. Conversely equality for the tensor probe menu
recovers each block future vector by Lemma~\ref{lem:product-metric}.
Thus the quotient on attainable histories is the product of the scalar
operational quotients.

For each active block, Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} supplies a smooth attainable
patch of dimension $d_b=\min\{n_b(r_b-1),|m_bA_b|-1\}$ and a continuous
section to all-failure histories. The product of these sections, interleaved
by $\omega$, is a continuous section of a $\sum_b d_b$-dimensional patch.
A sufficient continuous encoding is injective on this patch. Invariance
of domain, applied after embedding a smaller target Euclidean space in
$\R^{\sum_b d_b}$, excludes a smaller dimension. Singleton factors
contribute zero.

For the upper bound run the exact causal construction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:causal} separately in each block at its own local clock,
concatenating its coordinates in the single state. On a trial of block
$b$, update only that component. Before its first observation its state
is known, and after its last trial it has no remaining future task.
Each component changes from normalized factors to future moments at
most once. Its coordinate count at every checkpoint is exactly $d_b$,
so the concatenated count is \eqref{eq:product-dimension}. All needed
block prior moments have exponents in $N_bA_b$. The lower bound holds
at every checkpoint of any exact causal encoder, and hence also at the
checkpoint maximizing the sum. This proves the peak assertion. As in
the scalar theorem, an encoding of histories is not asserted to be a
global coordinate chart of a singular quotient.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A joint converse and a globally attainable cover}
\begin{theorem}[Shared-state composition law]\label{thm:shared-state}
For the fixed block experiments there exist $0<c\le C<\infty$ such
that, for every calibration $a\in\prod_bK_b$, every deterministic
schedule $\omega$, every checkpoint $s$ and integer $M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shared-checkpoint}
 c\Theta_{s,\omega}(M,a)
 \le\overline R^a_{M;s,\omega}
 \le R^{a,\max}_{M;s,\omega}
 \le C\Theta_{s,\omega}(M,a).
\end{equation}
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shared-streaming}
 c\Theta_\omega(M,a)
 \le\mathfrak R^{a,\mathrm{av}}_{M,\omega}
 \le\mathfrak R^{a,\max}_{M,\omega}
 \le C\Theta_\omega(M,a).
\end{equation}
The upper bound is attained by one deterministic transducer with at most
$M$ joint labels after every report. The lower bound permits arbitrary
joint encoding and decoding, including the independent coding randomness
of Definition~\ref{def:finite-state}. Constants may depend on the fixed
block data and horizons, but not on $a$, $M$ or $\omega$. The filter
may depend on all three. No product-code restriction is imposed on the
optimum, and no extra persistent block indices are supplied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a checkpoint with at least one active block. For each active $b$,
write $d_{b,1}\ge\cdots\ge d_{b,q_{b,m_b}}\ge0$ for its Leja
scales, and put
\[
 D_{b,0}=1,\qquad D_{b,\ell}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}d_{b,j}
       \quad(1\le\ell\le p_b).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:leja-scales} gives
$D_{b,\ell}\le V_{b,\ell}\le\ell!D_{b,\ell}$, including zero
values. Every active block has $D_{b,1}=1$.

\emph{Upper bound on the entire product image.}
The bounded-format image and dimension argument in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-checkpoint}, followed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tame-rectangle}, gives at every radius $e>0$ a cover
of the block raw image with at most
\[
 C_b\sum_{\ell=0}^{p_b}D_{b,\ell}e^{-\ell}
\]
balls of radius $e$, after changing a uniform constant for the raw metric.
A singleton block needs only one ball. Taking products of these covers
at radius $e/\sqrt B$ gives, in the concatenated metric, a cover bounded
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-cover-polynomial}
 \mathcal N\left(\prod_b\mathcal S_b,e\right)
 \le C_0\sum_{k=0}^{P_s} A_{s,k}(a)e^{-k}.
\end{equation}
Indeed expansion of the product sums over all bounded allocations
$(\ell_b)$; there are only finitely many of them, and each product is
bounded by its maximizing allocation in \eqref{eq:composition-volumes}.
The factors $B^{k/2}$ and the factorial comparisons are absorbed into
$C_0$. This argument keeps \emph{each} past cap $p_b$. Applying a
single total-dimensional rectangle estimate to the concatenated ambient
box instead could wrongly allocate more than $p_b$ axes to a block.

Set $e_* =\max_{1\le k\le P_s}(A_{s,k}/M)^{1/k}$.
For $M\ge2C_0$, choose a constant $L$ so large that
$C_0\sum_{k=1}^{P_s}L^{-k}\le1/2$. Substituting $Le_*$ in
\eqref{eq:product-cover-polynomial} yields at most $M$ balls.
Discard empty balls and replace each remaining center by a reachable
tuple in that ball, increasing radii by at most two. For $M<2C_0$,
a single reachable tuple covers the bounded product image at a fixed
radius; since $A_{s,1}=1$, $e_*\ge1/M\ge1/(2C_0)$, so a larger
uniform constant suffices. The finite code thus respects the integer
budget for every $M$. Lemma~\ref{lem:product-metric} transfers the
cover to all tensor query predictions. Squaring its radius and dividing
by the fixed menu size gives the checkpoint upper bound. A fixed
nearest-center tie rule makes the encoder Borel.

\emph{Lower bound against joint centers.}
Take any allocation $(\ell_b)$ with $k=\sum_b\ell_b\ge1$ and
$\prod_bV_{b,\ell_b}>0$. Under the common exploration law the block
histories are independent. For each block with $\ell_b>0$, project the
minorized $p_b$-cube of Lemma~\ref{lem:newton-attainment} onto its first
$\ell_b$ coordinates and scale them by
$d_{b,1},\ldots,d_{b,\ell_b}$. The product of the corresponding
all-failure subprobability laws dominates a fixed positive multiple of
uniform probability on a translated $k$-rectangle of volume
\[
 c_1\prod_bD_{b,\ell_b}.
\]
Blocks with $\ell_b=0$ are integrated out; no density assumption on any
$\mu_b$ is used. The positive multiplier includes the actual acquisition
evidence in all selected blocks. Constants can be chosen uniformly over
all allocations and count vectors because these are finite in number.

For an arbitrary decoder, average its private randomness conditional on
the index and query. This leaves at most $M$ centers in the entire query
vector space and cannot increase squared loss. Apply to those centers
the linear map $E G^\dagger$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:product-metric}, then
the inverse of each active Leja block and the selected-coordinate
projections. These maps recover exactly the scaled Newton coordinates
of a true query vector, and have bounded operator norms uniformly in
calibration. They are defined on arbitrary centers, not only on product
posteriors or reachable representatives. In particular this reduction
does not assume a factored decoder.

At most a constant times
$M u^k/\prod_bD_{b,\ell_b}$ of the minorized uniform rectangle can be
covered by $M$ balls of radius $u$. Taking
$u=c_2(M^{-1}\prod_bD_{b,\ell_b})^{1/k}$ with $c_2$ small enough
leaves at least half of that uniform mass uncovered. Mean squared
prediction regret is therefore at least
$c_3(M^{-1}\prod_bD_{b,\ell_b})^{2/k}$.
Randomized assignment to centers cannot improve upon the closest center.
Independent public coding randomness may be fixed first and averaged
last, since it does not generate the acquisition history. Maximizing
first over allocations and then over $k$ proves the checkpoint lower
bound by the factorial comparisons. Zero-volume allocations contribute
zero and require no division. The unconditional optimum cannot exceed
the maximum-history optimum. When no block is active, the true query
vector is known in advance and all three regrets are zero.

\emph{One persistent index through the whole schedule.}
At every stage choose the preceding reachable joint codebook with at most
$M$ representatives. The initial codebook is the exact product prior.
A representative is a tuple of block raw vectors stored in the read-only
program; its index is the entire persistent state. When the next trial
belongs to $b$, apply \eqref{eq:formal-multiindex-update} only to its
component, leave all other components unchanged, and quantize the updated
tuple into the next joint codebook. The denominator in that block update
is at least its report margin on a segment of posterior mixtures.
Consequently the joint raw update is uniformly Lipschitz, including at
all exponent collisions. An updated representative is reachable, so it
is always inside the domain of the next covering quantizer. A previously
unobserved block starts from its known exact prior, and a completed block
is replaced by $(1)$.

If $e_s$ is the concatenated raw error, then
\[
 e_0=0,\qquad e_{s+1}\le L_s e_s+
       C_s\Theta_{s+1,\omega}(M,a)^{1/2}.
\]
There are $T$ stages and finitely many block updates, so iteration gives
$e_s\le C_T\Theta_\omega(M,a)^{1/2}$ for every $s$.
This recurrence includes all errors from earlier joint re-encodings,
including changes in inactive components. It never resets them by
rereading a prefix. Lemma~\ref{lem:product-metric} yields the upper
bound in \eqref{eq:shared-streaming}.

Conversely, the index of any streaming filter is an $M$-message encoding
at each checkpoint. All the checkpoint lower laws just proved are
prefixes of the same uniform exploration experiment. Every filter
therefore satisfies each such lower bound before an infimum is taken.
Taking their maximum proves the streaming converse. Finally there are
only finitely many count vectors and schedules for the fixed horizons;
taking the largest upper and smallest positive lower constants makes
all comparisons uniform in $\omega$ as asserted.
\end{proof}

The tensor query algebra, the attainable posterior image, and the charged
memory thus have different composition rules. The first is a tensor
product, the second is a product embedded by a multilinear map, and the
last is controlled by the maximum over allocations in
\eqref{eq:composition-volumes}. Only after that allocation is resolved
can one take the maximum over causal checkpoints.
