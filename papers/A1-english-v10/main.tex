\begin{abstract}
We determine persistent finite-state memory for positive sparse monomial
experiments, uniformly through arbitrary additive exponent collisions,
and realize the sharp order by finite numerical programs. At a checkpoint,
the regret is the maximum of fractional powers of maximal Vandermonde
products, truncated by the attainable past dimension. Its maximum over
checkpoints is attained by one causal filter. For effectively supplied
finite prior-moment data, we construct integer transition tables and
dyadic output tables with the same order of regret. Numerical precision
of $\log_2(M+1)+O(1)$ binary places suffices for $M$ persistent states,
independently of the smallest nonzero exponent gap. The proof separates
classical finite interpolation from attainable posterior geometry, and
then applies a finite compilation theorem for compact Lipschitz state
systems. The compiler uses rational approximations to feasible histories;
no online posterior vector, exact-real program or historical tape is
retained. The original arbitrary-prior classification remains valid
without computability assumptions. Complete affine, decision and
mechanical specializations are included with their separate hypotheses.
\end{abstract}
