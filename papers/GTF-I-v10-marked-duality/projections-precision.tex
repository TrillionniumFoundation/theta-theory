\section{Marked projections and finite numerical acquisition}
\label{sec:v10-projections}

The common object behind the decision problems is the attainable
family of joint laws under one admissible encoder, with its task
controllers and its actual target marks. Evaluating a bounded loss is
a linear projection of this family. A risk body for one selected task
family is therefore a projection, not by itself a classification of
the whole experiment. We record the exact links to the quantitative
regimes used later in the article.

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic and metric projections]\label{prop:v10-projections}
For an uncontrolled standard Borel history $H$ and a square-integrable
Hilbert-valued target $U$, write $m(H)=\E[U\mid H]$. Any statistic $I$
formed from $H$ with independent randomization has optimal quadratic
excess
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v10-variance}
 \E\norm{m(H)-\E[m(H)\mid I]}^2.
\end{equation}
For finitely many such tasks the common-encoder weighted support
problem is the conditional variance of the direct sum of their
posterior means multiplied by the square roots of their task weights.
On finite histories this is precisely the compatible-partition
functional in Corollary~\ref{cor:v9-quadratic}. On the predictive trees
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v7-tree}, its within-cell variances are comparable
to the retained tree-energy sums under the hypotheses of that theorem.

For a revealed law $\pi\in\mathcal P([0,1])$, take as actual target
$\pi$ and as output a realized probability law $\widehat\pi$.
The loss $W_1(\pi,\widehat\pi)$ yields exactly the law-presentation
profile of Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-measureentropy}. Replacing the output by
a sample, and testing only its marginal, is a different task and does
not preserve this profile.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on $(H,I)$ the target mean is $m(H)$. Orthogonal
projection gives
\[
 \E\norm{U-a(I)}^2=
 \E\norm{U-m(H)}^2+\E\norm{m(H)-a(I)}^2.
\]
The second term is minimized by $a(I)=\E[m(H)\mid I]$, proving
\eqref{eq:v10-variance}. Weighted sums are squared norms in the
Hilbert direct sum. For a deterministic finite partition, conditioning
on $I$ is averaging over its cell, giving the stated finite functional.
The lower and upper comparisons with tree energy are exactly the
separation and diameter estimates of Theorem~\ref{thm:v7-tree}; no
new equality between energy and variance is asserted. The continuous
Gaussian volume argument is another evaluation of
\eqref{eq:v10-variance}, not an invocation of the finite-history
partition theorem on a continuous history space.

For the second assertion, the joint output loss of an encoder and
its random decoder is, by definition,
$\E W_1(\pi,D(Q(\pi)))$. The decoder must output a law on each run.
This is the profile in \eqref{eq:v8-lawprofile}, whose minimax order
is $1/\log(S+1)$ under its stated common-code convention. The
single-marginal probe in Proposition~\ref{prop:v8-probe} has a
different output and a different rate. Integrating out the realized
law before applying the distance changes the loss, so does not
commute with this projection.
\end{proof}

The revealed-law family is generally nondominated. It belongs to the
marked-spectrum definition, but is not being smuggled into the
product-dominated compactness theorem. Conversely the latter has a
proved closed attainable body, whereas a general infinite family of
tasks need not have one without hypotheses. Terminal statistical
deficiency uses the decision-complete task family and compactness
specified in Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-duality}; a finite selection of task
supports is not sufficient to infer Blackwell equivalence. These
distinctions allow the tree, Wasserstein, finite Gaussian and count
bounds to be used together without equating their output spaces.

\subsection{A finite table for the delayed Gaussian problem}
Retain the notation and information pattern of
Definition~\ref{def:v10-gaussian-signature}. The following bound
separates acquisition, numerical evaluation and persistent storage.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-precision Gaussian implementation]
\label{prop:v10-gaussian-precision}
Let $L=\lfloor S^{1/d}\rfloor\ge2$. An acquisition interface returns
one of the interior bins of a grid of width at most $h$ on $[-R,R]$
and two tail symbols, where $R>1$. Let the stored table approximate
$m(x)$ at each interior bin midpoint to error at most $\varepsilon$,
with values clipped to $[-1,1]$. Quantize those approximations into
$L$ equal intervals and store the resulting labels in the base-$L$
register. Then the squared excess in every coordinate is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v10-gaussian-precision}
 \left(L^{-1}+\frac{h}{2\sigma^2}+\varepsilon\right)^2
 +8\exp\left(-\frac{(R-1)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right).
\end{equation}
For fixed computable $\sigma>0$, there are rational uniform-grid
choices with $1/(2L)\le h\le1/L$, $\varepsilon\le1/L$, and
$1+\sigma\sqrt{6\log L}\le R\le2+2\sigma\sqrt{6\log L}$
that give excess $O_{\sigma}(L^{-2})$.
The number of acquisition symbols is $O_\sigma(L\sqrt{\log L})$,
the table has $O_\sigma(L(\log L)^{3/2})$ bits, and each scalar
acquisition uses $O_\sigma(\log L)$ report bits. For rational
$\sigma^2$ the table is computable by a terminating certified
rational procedure. Its offline evaluation cost is separate from
the bit-serial lookup and register-update costs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:v9-posteriorderivative} and $|\theta|\le1$ imply
$0<m'(x)\le\sigma^{-2}$. An interior-bin midpoint differs from $X$
by at most $h/2$, so its posterior mean differs by at most
$h/(2\sigma^2)$. Table evaluation and the final equal-interval
quantization add at most $\varepsilon$ and $1/L$. On a tail event,
any clipped output differs from $m(X)$ by at most two. Since
$X=\theta+\sigma Z$ and $|\theta|\le1$,
\[
 \mathbb P(|X|>R)\le
 2\exp(-(R-1)^2/(2\sigma^2)).
\]
The conditional-variance identity subtracts the same full-observation
baseline and gives \eqref{eq:v10-gaussian-precision}. This estimate
holds coordinatewise; no union bound over all coordinates is needed
for a single queried coordinate. The pre-query and post-query
register updates are exactly the base-$L$ construction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v9-delayed}.

There are $O(R/h)$ interior bins. Only their final labels in $[L]$
need be stored after certified preprocessing; thus the table uses
$O((R/h)\log L)$ bits. One may include the rational grid endpoints
and the two prescribed tail labels in the fixed program. Choosing
rational upper and lower approximations with constant-factor slack
gives the asserted bounds without requiring an exact logarithm or
square root in the online machine. A sequential lookup scans the
finite table; binary arithmetic then appends or extracts a base-$L$
digit. It uses $O(d\log L+\log(R/h))$ transient bits besides the
read-only program and polynomial time in the table length and the
register bit length. The physical analog-to-bin acquisition is an
explicit interface operation, not claimed to be implemented for free
by the register machine.

For completeness, certified values of $m$ are available without an
exact real oracle when $\sigma^2$ and the grid are rational. They are
ratios of the integrals
\[
 \int_{-1}^1 u e^{-(x-u)^2/(2\sigma^2)}du,
 \qquad
 \int_{-1}^1 e^{-(x-u)^2/(2\sigma^2)}du.
\]
The denominator is at least
$2e^{-(R+1)^2/(2\sigma^2)}>0$ for $|x|\le R$. Rational Taylor bounds
for the exponential, combined with Riemann-sum error bounds from
uniform derivative bounds on the compact rectangle, give rational
intervals for both integrals of arbitrarily small width. Refine until
the quotient interval has width at most $\varepsilon$, and choose
its rational midpoint. If it lies near an equal-interval threshold,
either adjacent label still has error at most
$1/L+\varepsilon$ after enlarging the certified tolerance by a fixed
factor, or the rational midpoint may simply be compared exactly to
the rational thresholds. Termination follows from the denominator
lower bound. The same construction for fixed computable $\sigma$
uses certified intervals for it. No polynomial bound in the
unspecified description of a general computable real is asserted.
\end{proof}

The cardinality converse remains valid for this finite numerical
signature because its implementations form a subclass of the
unrestricted measurable machines used in that converse. Finite
acquisition therefore preserves the delayed-query exponent when
its error parameters are scaled as above, while displaying the
additional resources that the cardinality theorem alone does not
price.
