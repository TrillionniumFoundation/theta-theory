\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:v10-introduction}

A statistic may preserve every parameter likelihood and yet fail to
preserve the target of a decision. A compressor may be optimal for
each task separately and yet fail when its update rule must be chosen
before the task. Both distinctions become structural when information
arrives sequentially and retained memory is bounded. We study them
through the attainable risks of an executable causal experiment,
with its target marks and information pattern specified together.

The principal object is the set of risk functions obtained by one
common encoder and a family of task controllers. The encoder may use
the current report, the current control and its retained label, but
neither the task index nor the unknown parameter is an additional
input. A task controller may depend on its task and retained label;
it must work across all parameter rows of that task. Randomness is
also part of the specification. Fresh private draws do not provide
a free retained random variable, whereas an independent shared seed
may correlate all stages of an execution. Expectations over that seed
are taken before the worst decision row is selected.

\subsection{The main results}
Fix finite controls, decisions and a horizon $T$, but allow standard
Borel report spaces and a compact parameter set. For a width vector
${\bf S}=(S_0,\ldots,S_T)$ let $K_{\bf S}$ be the set of all private
common-encoder designs and let $R(k)\in C(J)$ be their risk functions
on a compact set $J$ of task--parameter rows. Under the dominated
loss-effect condition of Section~\ref{sec:v10-compact},
Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-compact} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v10-intro-dual}
 \min_{\lambda\in\mathcal P(K_{\bf S})}\max_{j\in J}
       \{\E_\lambda R_j-b_0(j)\}
 =\max_{w\in\mathcal P(J)}
       \left\{\min_{k\in K_{\bf S}}\int R_j(k)w(dj)
                        -\int b_0(j)w(dj)\right\}.
\end{equation}
The private attainable risk set is compact, and its shared-seed
barycenter set is compact and convex. Positive measures on $J$
characterize the closed upper risk body. These conclusions concern
all measurable finite-state updates, not just a compact family
postulated in advance or encoders enumerated on a finite report tree.

The proof uses a product of weak star simplex spaces. Its main analytic
step is a tensor-testing lemma: convergence of one bounded encoder
factor in each report coordinate can be tested against an arbitrary
$L^1$ path effect. Compact families of effects give uniform convergence
of the risk functions. A separate representative lemma makes public
mixtures of weak star classes jointly measurable and executable.
Thus compactness is proved within the common-encoder information
pattern. Corollary~\ref{cor:v10-countable} treats countably many tasks
by compactness and finite-subproblem duality; in that setting the dual
is a supremum and no uniform finite seed is asserted.

The finite observation theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-approximation},
compares the actual joint report--mark laws by causal cell conversion
and within-cell reconstruction. Its error tends to zero uniformly
in the memory width, task and controller. Finite experiments are
therefore an approximating family for the dominated theory, not its
sole domain. Corollary~\ref{cor:v10-seeds} gives the price of replacing
a public mixture by a finite privately retained index.

Our second main result concerns the state carried by a marked
experiment. For a finite instrument, two histories have the same
continuation state when their conditional laws of the parameter,
future reports and terminal mark agree under every future control
word. Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-minimal} derives this state from decision
risks: among all source-consuming randomized causal statistics, it
has the least number of labels preserving every restart prediction
risk. No prescribed posterior coordinates appear in that operational
definition. The proof uses a strict conditional-variance identity for
binary-event prediction. Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-reconstruction} then
constructs parameter-independent causal report channels preserving
the actual terminal mark. An explicit binary example shows why
parameter sufficiency alone does not give this conclusion.

For rational finite instruments, Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-synthesis}
computes these classes by backward continuation spaces and forward
exploration of distinct positive states. Its cost is polynomial in
the input bit length and the number of output states, rather than in
the entire expanded history tree. The dimension of a linear
representation is used to test equivalence; it is not confused with
the number of positive retained states. A binary source with linear
dimension two and $t+1$ distinct states at time $t$ illustrates the
difference.

Two quantitative examples make the information pattern visible.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-membership} has three individually lossless
two-label tasks, but common-encoder worst risk $1/2$ with private
randomness and $2/9$ with shared randomness. All nine decision rows
receive positive mass in an optimal dual witness. The delayed-query
Gaussian theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:v9-delayed}, has pre-query error
of order $S^{-2/d}$ while its query-aware checkpoint has order
$S^{-2}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:v10-gaussian-precision} gives a finite
acquisition and table implementation retaining the causal exponent.

\subsection{Related work and the distinction of scope}
Weak star policies and existence in stochastic teams are developed by
Saldi~\cite{Saldi2017Topology}; strategic measures, public mixtures and
failures of weak closure are treated by Y\"uksel and
Saldi~\cite{YukselSaldi2017}. In particular, convexification by common
randomness and compactness methods are not new principles here. Our
result specifies a bounded retained register shared by several tasks,
proves continuity for its coupled encoder factors, and obtains the
risk-function body and its width-uniform marked approximations.
Nonconvexity of the private set is consistent with the classical fact
that randomization cannot improve a single fixed linear team objective:
our strict example concerns a worst-row objective.

Causal source coding and common-information control provide the
closest information-structure antecedents. See
Witsenhausen~\cite{Witsenhausen1979},
Y\"uksel~\cite{YukselCausal2010}, and
Nayyar, Mahajan and Teneketzis~\cite{NayyarMahajanTeneketzis2013}.
An offline occupancy or belief recursion does not by itself constitute
an online finite-memory implementation; we keep that distinction in
the finite support recursion and the compiler. Comparison of filtered
experiments, as in Norberg~\cite{Norberg2002}, is likewise part of the
background of causal comparison, rather than a novelty claimed for
the word ``causal''.

Continuation equivalence is related to automata and predictive-state
constructions~\cite{Nerode1958,ShaliziCrutchfield2001}. The linear
span computation uses the weighted-automaton equivalence method
presented by Kiefer~\cite{Kiefer2020}; neither rational elimination nor
linear automaton minimization is asserted as new. The operational
minimality here is for the declared marked continuation and for
source-consuming statistics under all restart preparations. It is not
minimality among independent simulators with unpriced retained
memory. The target mark and this comparison category cannot be
omitted from the statement.

The positive-model applications include established mechanisms:
positive-matrix filter contraction, the logarithmic control-semigroup
construction of Nisio~\cite{Nisio1976}, and the risk-sensitive
information-state augmentation of B\"auerle and
Rieder~\cite{BauerleRieder2017}. We prove the needed estimates here
and specify how their actual marked effects enter the common-encoder
theorem. No priority claim is made for these individual mechanisms.

\subsection{Consequences and organization}
The marked decision spectrum of Section~\ref{sec:v8-spectrum} fixes
the experiment, the task family, the information pattern and the
resource budget before optimization. The compact theorem and the
finite common-encoder theorem give exact variational descriptions
in their stated classes. Quadratic targets give conditional variance;
complete terminal loss families give statistical comparison; tree
geometry gives weighted distortion estimates; and presenting an
actual probability measure gives a Wasserstein law task. These are
typed consequences of one risk construction, not an assertion that
one scalar rate or one domination assumption describes every model.
In particular, presenting a realized measure and sampling a single
marginal probe are different tasks.

The following sections develop the compact theorem, marked quotient,
compiler and common-task example, followed by the finite variational
and saturation theory. The Gaussian temporal example and numerical
acquisition result precede three model applications: positive hidden
Markov observations and history reduction, nonlinear finite-state
semigroups, and hidden-phase exponential moments. The remaining
sections supply the predictive-tree, measure, terminal Gaussian and
count developments with full proofs. The separate complete-development
edition also includes the earlier mathematical bodies and their
historical introductions. None of their local hypotheses is removed
when the results are used here.
