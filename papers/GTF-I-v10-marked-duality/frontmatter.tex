\author{Qian Qi}

\begin{abstract}
We study causal experiments in which one finite-state encoder must serve
several decision tasks without access to the unknown parameter. For
standard Borel observations with dominated, continuously parameterized
loss effects, we prove compactness of the attainable private risk set
and an exact shared-randomness minimax duality over a compact family
of decision rows. All measurable encoder kernels are admitted. The
proof constructs executable representatives of weak star policy
classes and retains the common encoder throughout the compactness
argument. Finite observation approximations converge uniformly over
all memory widths. For finite marked instruments we characterize the
least retained state preserving every restart prediction risk, including
randomized statistics, and give exact causal reconstruction of the
joint report--target law. Rational continuation spaces yield an
output-sensitive synthesis algorithm. A three-task example separates
taskwise, private and shared compression, and a delayed-query Gaussian
experiment exhibits a temporal penalty of order $S^{-2/d}$. Positive
hidden Markov models, nonlinear finite-state semigroups and latent-phase
moment problems provide explicit consequences. Predictive-tree,
measure-valued, Gaussian and count regimes are developed with their
respective information and numerical resource specifications.
\end{abstract}
