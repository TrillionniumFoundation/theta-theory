\section{Compact duality for dominated causal experiments}
\label{sec:v10-compact}

We begin with a nonfinite experiment. The observation spaces in this
section need not be locally compact, and the encoder kernels need not
be continuous in the observation. The hypothesis used in place of such
regularity is domination of the complete marked observation law.

\subsection{The experiment and its information pattern}
Fix a horizon $T$, finite control sets $A_t$, nonempty finite registers
$I_t=[S_t]$, and a finite task set $D$. Task $d$ has a finite decision
set $B_d$. Let $Y_t$ and $W$ be standard Borel spaces, with probability
reference measures $\mu_t$ and $\nu$, and write
$\mu=\bigotimes_{t=1}^T\mu_t$. The mark $W$ is the actual terminal
quantity on the source probability space; it is not a reconstructed
sample with the same marginal distribution.

A causal experiment is specified by a consistent family of laws of
$(Y_1,\ldots,Y_T,W)$ under open-loop action words
$a=(a_0,\ldots,a_{T-1})$, indexed by a parameter $\theta\in\Theta$.
Consistency means that the marginal law of $Y_{1:t}$ is independent of
$a_t,\ldots,a_{T-1}$, and that feedback laws are obtained by inserting
their conditional action probabilities in the open-loop laws. Suppose
that these laws have densities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v10-density}
 p_{\theta,a}(y,w)\quad\hbox{with respect to }\mu(dy)\nu(dw).
\end{equation}
This hypothesis is satisfied by dominated hidden Markov instruments,
including continuous observation models. It excludes, for example, an
unnoised repeated real observation supported on a diagonal of product
Lebesgue measure. We do not replace domination by an unproved closure
claim for arbitrary strategic measures.

The encoder is a single family
\[
 q_0\in\mathcal P(I_0),\qquad
 q_t(i'\mid i,a,y)\in\mathcal P(I_{t+1}),\quad 0\le t<T,
\]
where $y\in Y_{t+1}$. It is common to every task and every parameter.
The task controller and terminal rule are
$g_{td}(a\mid i)$ and $v_d(b\mid i)$; neither depends on $\theta$.
The task is known to the controller, not supplied as an additional
argument to $q_t$. The current control and observation are transient
inputs. Between updates, the only private retained state is $i\in I_t$.
Time and the prescribed tables are part of the fixed program.
All private draws are fresh and conditionally independent given their
arguments. A retained independent public seed, when allowed, is a
separate resource. These conventions rule out both a hidden controller
transcript and an uncharged retained random tape.

Let $J=D\times\Theta$ be compact metrizable, with $D$ discrete and
$\Theta$ compact metrizable. More generally the argument works for a
compact row set with a continuous map into the finite task set. For
$j=(d,\theta)$ and $b\in B_d$, let $0\le\ell_j(w,b)\le1$ be measurable.
Our regularity condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v10-effect}
 f_{j,a,b}(y):=\int\ell_j(w,b)p_{\theta,a}(y,w)\nu(dw),
 \qquad j\longmapsto f_{j,a,b}\ \hbox{continuous in }L^1(\mu)
\end{equation}
on each task component, for every $a,b$. A continuous baseline
$b_0\in C(J)$ is fixed. A full-history minimax baseline for task $d$
is constant on its parameter rows, and is one permitted choice; a
parameterwise oracle is a different baseline and is not substituted
for it.

Write $K_{\bf S}$ for the space of all such private designs
$k=(q_0,q,g,v)$. The risk of a design is
\begin{align}\label{eq:v10-risk}
 R_j(k)=\sum_{i_0,\ldots,i_T}\sum_{a,b}
 q_0(i_0)v_d(b\mid i_T)
 \prod_{t=0}^{T-1}g_{td}(a_t\mid i_t)
 \int f_{j,a,b}(y)
 \prod_{t=0}^{T-1}q_t(i_{t+1}\mid i_t,a_t,y_{t+1})\mu(dy).
\end{align}
The sums over $b$ are in $B_d$. This expression follows by first fixing
the control and register paths in the causal law. In particular it does
not optimize the parameter rows separately.

\subsection{The topology and an executable realization}
Each encoder row is a simplex-valued element of a finite product of
$L^\infty(\mu_{t+1})$, endowed with its weak star topology. Positivity
and the constraint that its coordinates sum to one are weak star
closed: test them against nonnegative $L^1$ functions. Banach--Alaoglu
therefore makes the row space compact. Since the probability space is
standard Borel, $L^1$ is separable, and this compact space is metrizable.
Give the finite controller, initialization and decoder simplices their
usual topologies, and $K_{\bf S}$ the product topology.

\begin{lemma}[Tensor testing]\label{lem:v10-tensor}
Suppose $u_{tn},u_t\in L^\infty(\mu_t)$ have absolute value at most one
and $u_{tn}\to u_t$ weak star, for each $t$. Then
\[
 \int F(y)\prod_t u_{tn}(y_t)\mu(dy)
 \longrightarrow \int F(y)\prod_t u_t(y_t)\mu(dy)
\]
for every $F\in L^1(\mu)$. Convergence is uniform over every compact
subset of $L^1(\mu)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $F(y)=\prod_t F_t(y_t)$ with $F_t\in L^1(\mu_t)$ the integrals
factor, and each factor converges by the definition of the topology.
Finite sums of these tensors are dense in $L^1(\mu)$: rectangular
simple functions generate the product sigma field, and a monotone
class argument followed by truncation gives the density statement.
An $L^1$ error of $\epsilon$ in the test function changes either
integral by at most $\epsilon$. This proves convergence for one $F$.
A finite $\epsilon$-net of a compact family proves the uniform assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Jointly measurable representatives]\label{lem:v10-representatives}
The encoder row space has a choice of representatives $(q,y)\mapsto
\widehat q(y)$ that is jointly Borel, is simplex valued at every $y$,
and represents each equivalence class $q$ almost everywhere. Consequently
a Borel probability on $K_{\bf S}$ can be sampled as one independent
public seed and implemented by measurable causal update rules.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose increasing finite measurable partitions generating the report
sigma field modulo completion. The coordinates of
$\E[q\mid\mathcal F_n](y)$ are finite sums of functions of the form
\[
 \1_C(y)\frac{\int_C q\,d\mu_t}{\mu_t(C)},\qquad \mu_t(C)>0.
\]
On each zero-measure partition cell use one fixed probability vector.
Their coefficients are weak star continuous in $q$, so these functions
are jointly Borel. For fixed $q$, the bounded martingale convergence
theorem gives coordinatewise convergence to a representative of $q$
almost everywhere. On the jointly Borel set where all coordinates
converge use their limit, and elsewhere use one fixed probability
vector. Every finite-stage vector is in the simplex, so is the limit.
There are only finitely many rows. A sampled $k$ is thus executable
by inverse transforms with fresh independent uniform variables.
Domination ensures that exceptional report sets have zero probability
for every actual action path and parameter. Compact metrizability also
ensures that every probability on $K_{\bf S}$ is the law of a Borel
function of a uniform seed. The sampled design is retained publicly;
this last retention is not free private memory.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nonfinite common-encoder duality]\label{thm:v10-compact}
Under \eqref{eq:v10-density}--\eqref{eq:v10-effect}, the map
$R:K_{\bf S}\to C(J)$ is continuous in the uniform norm. The attainable
shared-seed risk set and its upper extension are
\[
 \mathcal A_{\bf S}=
 \left\{\int R(k)\lambda(dk):\lambda\in\mathcal P(K_{\bf S})\right\},
 \qquad \mathcal U_{\bf S}=\mathcal A_{\bf S}+C(J)_+.
\]
The first is compact and convex and the second is closed and convex.
Set
\[
 h_{\bf S}(w)=\min_{k\in K_{\bf S}}\int_J R_j(k)w(dj),
 \qquad w\in\mathcal P(J).
\]
Then $u\in\mathcal U_{\bf S}$ if and only if
$\int u\,dw\ge h_{\bf S}(w)$ for every $w\in\mathcal P(J)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v10-duality}
 \min_{\lambda\in\mathcal P(K_{\bf S})}
 \max_{j\in J}\left\{\int R_j(k)\lambda(dk)-b_0(j)\right\}
 =\max_{w\in\mathcal P(J)}
 \left\{h_{\bf S}(w)-\int b_0\,dw\right\}.
\end{equation}
Both extrema are attained. The private minimax optimum is also
attained, but its attainable risk set is $R(K_{\bf S})$, not its
convex hull.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compactness of $K_{\bf S}$ was proved above. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:v10-tensor} to each term of \eqref{eq:v10-risk}.
The effect functions form finitely many compact subsets of $L^1$,
by \eqref{eq:v10-effect}. Thus convergence of designs gives uniform
convergence of all row risks. This proves the first assertion without
enumerating deterministic encoders.

For completeness, the barycenter map is continuous from weak convergence
of $\lambda$ to norm convergence in $C(J)$. Approximate the continuous
map $R$ uniformly on compact $K_{\bf S}$ by a finite-valued continuous
partition-of-unity interpolation. Integrals of the finitely many
continuous coefficients converge; the uniform approximation controls
the remainder. The image of compact $\mathcal P(K_{\bf S})$ is therefore
compact. Lemma~\ref{lem:v10-representatives} identifies this image with
executable public mixtures, rather than only a formal relaxed set.
Convexity follows by mixing the public seed.

If $u_n=a_n+c_n\to u$, $a_n\in\mathcal A_{\bf S}$ and $c_n\ge0$,
pass to a convergent subsequence of $a_n$. Its limit $a$ satisfies
$u-a\ge0$, proving closedness of $\mathcal U_{\bf S}$. A separating
continuous functional for a point outside this closed convex upper
set must be nonnegative: otherwise translating an attainable risk
in a nonnegative direction contradicts its lower separating bound.
By the Riesz representation theorem this functional is integration
against a finite positive measure, which may be normalized to a
probability. Minimizing it over the upper set gives $h_{\bf S}$.
This proves the support characterization.

The bilinear payoff
\[
 F(\lambda,w)=\int_J\int_{K_{\bf S}}R_j(k)\lambda(dk)w(dj)
             -\int_J b_0(j)w(dj)
\]
is continuous separately on the two compact convex probability spaces.
The classical convex minimax theorem gives equality of the two extrema.
For fixed $w$, a linear functional on probabilities has minimum at a
Dirac probability, which gives the inner minimum in
\eqref{eq:v10-duality}. The left objective is continuous and the right
objective, as an infimum of continuous functions of $w$, is upper
semicontinuous. Compactness gives attainment. Finally the private
objective is continuous on compact $K_{\bf S}$. No convexification
was used for that assertion.
\end{proof}

The use of policy topologies and static reduction belongs to stochastic
team theory; see Saldi~\cite{Saldi2017Topology}, especially the weak star
construction and continuity/existence results, and
Y\"uksel--Saldi~\cite{YukselSaldi2017}, who explain why conditional
independence is not generally closed under weak convergence. Here the
product domination and finite action-path expansion supply the needed
continuity, while the same encoder coordinates remain coupled across
all tasks and unknown-parameter rows. Convex separation and minimax
are classical tools, not additional novelty claims.

\begin{corollary}[Effect formulation with arbitrary marks]\label{cor:v10-effects}
Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-compact} still holds when there is no common
reference measure for $W$, provided each open-loop report marginal
is dominated by $\mu$ and the signed effect measures
\[
 C\longmapsto\int_{C\times W}\ell_j(w,b)\,P_{\theta,a}(dy,dw)
\]
have $L^1(\mu)$ densities satisfying \eqref{eq:v10-effect}.
The actual joint marked laws, not arbitrarily selected couplings,
are used to define these effects.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Boundedness of the loss and report domination give absolute
continuity of each effect measure. Disintegration of the actual
marked law, or simply integration first in that measure, gives
\eqref{eq:v10-risk}. All compactness and separation arguments in the
theorem use only these effects. Report domination still makes the
jointly measurable representatives executable. No common measure
on $W$ is needed in any of these steps. By contrast, the marked
total-variation approximation theorem below retains its stronger
joint-density hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Countably many tasks]\label{cor:v10-countable}
Suppose that the task-row set is countable, every row has the dominated
representation above, and every task has finitely many controller and
decoder entries. No uniform continuity across rows is required. For a
bounded baseline sequence, the shared optimum is attained and
\[
 \min_\lambda\sup_j\{\E_\lambda R_j-b_0(j)\}
 =\sup_{w\in\mathcal P_f(J)}
   \left\{\min_k\sum_jw_jR_j(k)-\sum_jw_jb_0(j)\right\},
\]
where $\mathcal P_f(J)$ denotes the finitely supported probabilities.
The right side is a supremum, not an asserted maximum.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The countable product of the task-controller and decoder simplices
with the common encoder space is compact metrizable. Each risk is
continuous by the tensor lemma; the representative construction is
unchanged. On $\mathcal P(K_{\bf S})$, the objective is a supremum of
continuous functions, hence lower semicontinuous and attains its
minimum. If every finite subset of row constraints
$\E R_j-b_0(j)\le r$ is feasible, compactness and the finite intersection
property make all constraints feasible simultaneously. Finite-row
convex minimax therefore gives the displayed identity, by taking
$r$ strictly above its right side and then decreasing $r$ to the
infimum. This argument makes no uniform finite-seed assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The price of a finite mixture]\label{cor:v10-seeds}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-compact}, put
\[
 \omega(\delta)=\sup_{k,\ d_J(j,j')\le\delta}
                   |R_j(k)-R_{j'}(k)|.
\]
Then $\omega(\delta)\to0$. If $J$ has a $\delta$-net of size $N$,
every attainable shared risk can be approximated within $2\omega(\delta)$
in uniform norm by a mixture of at most $N+1$ private designs.
Such a mixture has a private implementation with registers
$(N+1)S_t$, including initialization. If its weights are approximated
by a probability vector with denominator $2^p$, an additional error
at most $(N+1)2^{-p}$ suffices.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The compact subset $R(K_{\bf S})$ of $C(J)$ is equicontinuous.
Carath\'eodory's theorem applied to its evaluations on the net gives
$N+1$ designs whose mixture agrees there with the prescribed risk.
Move an arbitrary row to the net; each of the two risks changes by at
most $\omega(\delta)$. Store the chosen design index with the register
label for the private implementation. Approximate weights by rounding
down the first $L-1$ coordinates and putting the remainder in the last,
where $L\le N+1$. Their total variation distance is at most $L2^{-p}$,
which bounds the change of every $[0,1]$-valued risk. This approximation
can be sampled with $p$ fair bits; exact non-dyadic weights do not have
an asserted fixed worst-case fair-bit cost.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite observation approximations, uniformly in memory}
Let $\mathcal F_{tn}$ be increasing finite partitions generating $Y_t$,
and retain the actual mark $W$. The finite-report experiment $E_n$
reports only the cell of each observation. Its reverse report channel
samples independently from $\mu_t$ conditioned on that cell. Cells of
zero reference measure are assigned arbitrary outputs and have zero
probability in the experiment. Write
\[
 p^{(n)}_{\theta,a}
 =\E_{\mu\otimes\nu}
   [p_{\theta,a}\mid\bigotimes_t\mathcal F_{tn}\otimes\mathcal B(W)],
 \quad
 \delta_n=\frac12\sum_a\sup_\theta
             \norm{p_{\theta,a}-p^{(n)}_{\theta,a}}_1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Width-uniform approximation]\label{thm:v10-approximation}
If each family $\{p_{\theta,a}:\theta\in\Theta\}$ is compact in
$L^1(\mu\otimes\nu)$, then $\delta_n\to0$. For every width vector,
private or shared randomness, and every task family with losses in
$[0,1]$, the raw minimax risks of $E$ and $E_n$ differ by at most
$\delta_n$. The same comparison of excess risks costs at most
$2\delta_n$ when the full-history baselines are recomputed. The
simulations neither add persistent states nor reveal the mark.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conditional expectations on the generating partitions converge in
$L^1$ for each density. Their norms are at most one as operators on
$L^1$, so a finite-net argument gives uniform convergence on each
compact family. There are finitely many action words.

Reporting the cell is an exact causal channel from $E$ to $E_n$.
After the reverse channel, the open-loop joint density of synthetic
reports and the actual $W$ is $p^{(n)}_{\theta,a}$. Under an arbitrary
adaptive policy and encoder, expand the joint law by its action and
register paths. The conditional rule multiplying each density is
between zero and one. Summing the absolute differences over action
words, and integrating over the register randomization, bounds the
total variation of the marked output laws by $\delta_n$. This bound
is uniform over policies, tasks, seeds and widths. Composition of
any code with either report channel needs only the original register:
cell conversion or within-cell sampling is a current-report operation.
Thus every code transfers with the stated risk error in the reverse
direction, and with zero error in the forward direction. Taking
minima and worst rows proves the raw comparison. The same argument
without a width bound compares full-history baselines, and the triangle
inequality gives the excess-risk bound.
\end{proof}
