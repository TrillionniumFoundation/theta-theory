\section{The exact two-preparation marked saddle}
\label{sec:two-saddle}
The one-preparation saddle has an exact successor. A new zero coefficient
of the least-favorable predictive measure makes it necessary to randomize
at the two extreme training counts. This randomization changes the value,
but does not enlarge the alphabet of frozen validation events.

Let $b(p,q)$ and $Q_*$ be as in Section~\ref{sec:physical}. Let $r$ be the
unique root in $(1/3,7/20)$ of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-cubic}
                     2r^3+25r^2-3=0,
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-constants}
 \tau=\frac{6(-r^2+4r-1)}{r(3r+25)},\qquad
 v_2=\frac{-r^3+6r^2-3r+14}{32},\qquad
 p_\pm=\frac{1\pm\sqrt r}{2}.
\end{equation}
The polynomial in \eqref{eq:r-cubic} is strictly increasing on the positive
half-line and has opposite signs at the indicated endpoints. These
observations prove the asserted existence, uniqueness and isolating
interval. That interval also gives $0<\tau<1$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact two-preparation value]\label{thm:two-saddle}
For the full marked-word response class of \eqref{eq:selector},
\[
                   U_2(b,Q_*)=v_2.
\]
A least-favorable prior gives mass $1/2$ to each of $(p_-,p_-)$ and
$(p_+,p_+)$. An optimal response discards the two training marks, retains
the total number $S$ of ones in the four training report bits, and uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-response}
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 S&000&001&110&111\\\hline
 0&\tau&0&1&1\\
 1&1&0&1&1\\
 2&1&1&1&1\\
 3&1&1&0&1\\
 4&1&1&0&\tau
\end{array}
\end{equation}
with zero response at off-diagonal validation atoms. Numerically,
$r=0.3417694155\ldots$, $\tau=0.1688236447\ldots$ and
$v_2=0.4261127787\ldots$. The exact value is determined by
\eqref{eq:r-cubic}--\eqref{eq:two-constants}, not by these decimals.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $a=(1-p)(1-q)$, $c=pq$, $u=(p+q-1)^2$ and $v=(p-q)^2$.
Both $u$ and $v$ belong to $[0,1]$. The score of the deterministic
five-event selector is the polynomial $H(u,v)$ in \eqref{eq:poly}.
Only counts zero and four are changed by \eqref{eq:two-response}.
Their probabilities are $a^2$ and $c^2$. Consequently the new score is
\[
 G(u,v)=H(u,v)+\tau J(u,v),\qquad
 J=a^2(5/16-a/2)+c^2(5/16-c/2).
\]
Expansion gives the polynomial identity
\begin{align}\label{eq:J-two}
 J(u,v)={}&-\frac{u^3}{64}+\frac{3u^2v}{64}-\frac{25u^2}{128}
       -\frac{3uv^2}{64}+\frac{13uv}{64}\notag\\
 &+\frac{v^3}{64}-\frac{v^2}{128}-\frac v{32}+\frac3{128}.
\end{align}
Thus
\[
 H(u,0)=\frac{-u^3+6u^2-3u+14}{32},\qquad
 J(u,0)=\frac{3-25u^2-2u^3}{128}.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:r-cubic} says $J(r,0)=0$, and the choice of $\tau$
says $\partial_uG(r,0)=0$. Direct factorization, using these two
identities, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-square}
 G(u,0)-v_2=
 \frac{(u-r)^2\{75+12r+6u-37r^2-74ru\}}
      {64r(3r+25)}.
\end{equation}
The expression in braces is strictly positive for $0\le u\le1$:
it is at least $75-37(7/20)^2-74(7/20)>0$.
Moreover,
\[
 \partial_vH=\frac{3(u-v)^2-8u+4v+15}{32}\ge\frac7{32},\qquad
 \partial_vJ=\frac{3(u-v)^2+13u-v-2}{64}\ge-\frac3{64}.
\]
Because $0<\tau<1$, $\partial_vG\ge11/64$ on the unit square.
It follows that $G(u,v)\ge G(u,0)\ge v_2$, with equality precisely
when $v=0$ and $u=r$. Both equality points are feasible and are the two
parameters in the asserted prior. This proves the global lower bound.

It remains to prove an upper bound against every response, not only
those depending on $S$. Under the asserted prior the four training
report bits are independent Bernoulli variables with common parameter
$p_-$ or $p_+$. Conditional on $S=s$, their arrangements and the two
independent training marks have parameter-independent distributions.
The signed predictive coefficients can therefore be grouped by $S$
without restricting any response coordinate. For the four displayed
validation atoms put
\[
 C_{s,j}=\frac12\sum_{p\in\{p_-,p_+\}}
 {4\choose s}p^s(1-p)^{4-s}\{Q_{*,j}-b_j(p,p)\}.
\]
The first three rows of $256C$ are
\begin{align*}
 s=0:
 &\quad\bigl(-(2r^3+25r^2-3),\;-(2r^3+27r^2+12r-1),\bigr.\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{40mm}\bigl.-(2r^3-5r^2-20r-1),\;-(2r^3-7r^2-32r-3)\bigr),\\
 s=1:
 &\quad4(r-1)\bigl(2r^2+7r-3,\;2r^2+9r-1,\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{43mm}2r^2-7r-1,\;2r^2-9r-3\bigr),\\
 s=2:
 &\quad-6(r-1)^2(2r-3,\;2r-1,\;2r-1,\;2r-3).
\end{align*}
Rows three and four are the reversals of rows one and zero, respectively.
Their signs, using $1/3<r<7/20$, are
\[
 (0,-,+,+),\quad(+,-,+,+),\quad(+,+,+,+),\quad
 (+,+,-,+),\quad(+,+,-,0).
\]
Every off-diagonal validation coefficient is nonpositive because its
target mass is zero. Hence \eqref{eq:two-response} maximizes every signed
coefficient: it is one at positive coefficients, zero at negative ones,
and arbitrary at the two zeros. The lower-bound equality at both prior
points shows that this Bayes maximum is exactly $v_2$.
The posterior-predictive dual, Theorem~\ref{thm:dual}, now gives the
matching upper bound for the full response class.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact physical score on the peak-twelve segment]
\label{cor:two-physical-exact}
Use the atomic stochastic-row convention and the original marked
interface of Definition~\ref{def:physical-class}. At two candidate
training preparations, every peak budget $W\ge12$ has exact ideal
worst-case score $v_2$. For a target $Q$ with $\TV(Q,Q_*)\le\beta$,
the corresponding value lies in $[v_2-\beta,v_2+\beta]$. In particular,
with $u_{\rm ex}=(85-7\sqrt{73})/64$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical-v26}
 N_c^{\rm phys}(W,Q)=2\quad\text{if}\quad
 W\ge12,\qquad u_{\rm ex}+\beta\le c<v_2-\beta.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $h_0,\ldots,h_4$ be the five deterministic events in
\eqref{eq:events}. At count zero select $h_1$ with probability $\tau$
and $h_0$ otherwise; at count four select $h_3$ with probability $\tau$
and $h_4$ otherwise. At the other counts retain the indicated event.
Discard the count and retain the selected event for both validations.
This realizes \eqref{eq:two-response}; the event alphabet remains five.
The full charged profile, now including the separate event-selection
update, is
\[
           (1,2,3,3,4,5,5,5,10,12,10,10,7,3).
\]
The common residual-function compiler in Lemma~\ref{lem:profile} applies
unchanged. In particular, no persistent random selector is outside the
register. The all-on law for every private candidate is $b(p,q)$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-saddle} proves the constructive lower bound.
For the converse restrict to the admissible common-garbling product
subfamily and apply Theorem~\ref{thm:outer} followed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-saddle}. This bounds all architectures, including
those using feedback, stopping and more memory. Target perturbation
changes each frozen-event expectation by at most $\beta$. Finally the
exact one-preparation theorem excludes a strict score above $c$ at
$N\le1$, whereas the displayed machine achieves it at $N=2$ under
\eqref{eq:physical-v26}.
\end{proof}

The root $r$ is larger than $2-\sqrt3$. The old diagonal polynomial
$H(u,0)$ is strictly increasing between these points, whence $v_2>m_2$.
The deterministic theorem, including its zero-random-bit implementation,
remains valid; the new exact score uses an additional stochastic row.

\begin{proposition}[Finite precision for the new row]\label{prop:tau-precision}
Replacing $\tau$ by a dyadic $\tau_b=k/2^b\in[0,1]$ with
$|\tau_b-\tau|\le2^{-b}$ loses at most $(5/16)2^{-b}$ in uniform score.
A clocked fair-bit implementation uses $b$ extra random bits and at most
$\max\{12,6(b+1)+5\}$ persistent states, including their phase, in
addition to the unchanged preparation count.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At each affected training word the response changes at a single atom;
$|5/16-a/2|$ and $|5/16-c/2|$ are at most $5/16$.
The two affected word probabilities sum to at most one. This gives the
score estimate. To implement the dyadic coin, compare $b$ fresh fair
bits lexicographically with the fixed $b$-bit integer $k$. Retain the
origin (count zero or four), one of the three comparison flags, and a
phase in $\{0,\ldots,b\}$. Once the comparison ends retain its chosen
one of five events. Unaffected counts can wait in their event states.
These states total at most $6(b+1)+5$; the other cuts have peak twelve.
The cases $k=0,2^b$ are deterministic. Exact atomic randomization and a
finite fair-bit implementation are therefore distinct resource claims.
\end{proof}
