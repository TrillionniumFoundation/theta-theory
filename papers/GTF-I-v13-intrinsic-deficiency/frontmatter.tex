\begin{abstract}
We define the least marked simulation error of a causal experiment at a
prescribed persistent-state and seed budget. For finite experiments the
private problem is an attained polynomial optimization, while hidden
convexification has an exact causal-testing dual and a finite realization
priced by the affine dimension of attainable behavior. Revealing the same
selector changes both the variational problem and its zero set. A delayed
channel identifies the private zero set with nonnegative rank and separates
the hidden and visible theories at fixed width. The deficiency spectrum
has a multiplicative state-cost composition law and transports all common
finite-register decision problems. We characterize exact compression for
endogenously controlled tasks by reachable compatible covers with one
shared encoder, and give a finite positive witness outside the exact face.
A regenerative theorem treats nonsummable average risks without domination
of the physical transition kernels. Finally, velocity interventions in a
hard-sphere system yield an active microscopic realization: graph-core
approximations converge in marked comparison, uniformly over consumer
widths, both at fixed horizons and for regenerative average risks.
\end{abstract}
