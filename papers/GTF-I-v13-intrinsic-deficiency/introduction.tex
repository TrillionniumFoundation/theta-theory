\section{Introduction}\label{sec:v13-intro}
An exact simulation of a statistical experiment need not preserve the
memory budget of a decision maker. Conversely, convexifying a collection
of finite-state simulations may introduce a persistent choice whose
storage was absent from each member of the collection. The distinction
is particularly sharp when the choice is revealed to the decision maker:
a cancellation in a mixture of report laws then need not be a cancellation
in the joint law of the choice and the reports. These observations suggest
that the error, the retained state, and the visibility of randomization
should be coordinates of a single comparison object.

We make this object explicit. Given marked controlled experiments
$E,F$, a width profile $K=(K_0,\ldots,K_T)$, and a seed signature, the
\emph{intrinsic causal deficiency} is the infimum of the marked feedback
simulation error over all simulators with that signature. The mark is
an actual hidden variable of the experiment, not a newly drawn variable
having a convenient marginal. A simulator is independent of the unknown
parameter and of the eventual decision task. Controls are passed through
at the declared time, and the simulator can retain only its specified
state. These restrictions are part of the object being compared.

\subsection{The main theorem}
For finite experiments, write $\cK_K$ for the product of the simulator's
stochastic row simplexes and $b(q)$ for the vector of all marked transcript
probabilities against deterministic feedback trees. The map $q\mapsto
b(q)$ is polynomial, but its image need not be convex. Put
\[
 a_K=\dim\aff\{b(q):q\in\cK_K\}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main} proves the following statement.
\emph{The hidden-mixture deficiency has a complete dual in weighted causal
tests and an exact realization using at most $a_K+1$ selector values;
this realization costs at most $(a_K+1)K$ private states. In contrast,
arbitrary visible selection has zero deficiency exactly when a private
$K$-state simulator has zero deficiency. All three signatures have
multiplicative state-cost composition and uniform decision transport.}
The private optimization and every fixed finite selector problem are
attained. Thus the theorem compares optimal costs, rather than merely
composing a simulator supplied in advance.

The state price in this statement is substantive. In a two-checkpoint
experiment, with the input consumed before the output is produced,
zero private deficiency at width $K$ is equivalent to nonnegative rank
at most $K$ of the required stochastic channel
(Proposition~\ref{prop:v13-rank}). A three-row channel has positive private
two-state deficiency but zero hidden-selector deficiency at the same
within-mode width. The selector can be stored, but it cannot be discarded.
Consequently a convex decision dual cannot silently be interpreted as a
same-width private inverse randomization theorem. Our converse gives the
implementation and its cost together.

\subsection{Endogenous decisions and nonsummable risks}
A common encoder must also be shared by tasks that choose different
controls. The set of histories relevant to a task then depends on the
controller being synthesized. Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-endogenous} characterizes
zero excess relative to the full-history Bellman value using task-indexed
reachable cells, task-dependent actions, and one task-independent update
map. Closure is required only under the chosen actions and actual
supported successors. Randomization leaves this exact feasibility set
unchanged. An occupation identity gives quantitative upper bounds for
approximate covers and a positive finite-table lower bound for infeasible
profiles. The latter is a certificate for a fixed experiment, not a
uniform margin over perturbed models.

The average-risk theorem uses an explicit physical regeneration. A reset
refreshes the physical state and clears the within-policy register. Between
resets the controlled physical kernel may be singular or deterministic.
Uniform regeneration yields a compact risk image for stationary common
finite-register policies, an attained minimax theorem after priced
selection, and a vanishing-discount limit
(Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-average}). It is the reset of the joint physical and
retained state, not an unproved assertion about the mixing of a hidden
plant, that gives the required uniformity. This result treats a
nonsummable criterion; it is different from the dominated finite-prefix,
summable infinite-horizon theory retained in the complete development.

\subsection{Active microscopic realization}
Our physical example is a finite hard-sphere system. At observation times
an action applies an orthogonal velocity intervention, followed by the
hard-sphere flow. The action therefore changes the subsequent trajectory.
The interventions are bounded composition operators and need not preserve
the generator domain. A graph-core Galerkin approximation of the unbounded
Koopman generator, followed by projected interventions, approximates each
finite controlled word. Gaussian acquisition converts the resulting
$L^2$ errors into two-sided marked comparison bounds before optimizing
any finite-register decision rule
(Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-active}). A sum over action words is essential:
feedback conditioning does not preserve a uniform posterior density
bound. After physical regeneration, the same comparison extends to
average risks by a geometrically weighted cycle argument, even though
no rate uniform in the word length is assumed.

The physical system, noise levels, and finite action alphabet are fixed.
The approximation dimension is a continuous physical state dimension,
not a bound on the cardinality of a decision maker's register. We do not
identify this active, regenerative microscopic theorem with a
Boltzmann--Grad limit or with a nonlinear kinetic action theorem.

\subsection{Relation to earlier theories}
Blackwell comparison and Le Cam deficiency supply the classical
statistical background; filtered experiments add the timing of
information and decisions \cite{Blackwell1953,LeCam1964,Norberg2002}.
The finite minimax step, separation, and Carath\'eodory's theorem are
classical. What is retained here through these operations is the
simulator's \emph{persistent-state and visibility signature}. The
nonnegative-factorization example makes the difference from unconstrained
randomization concrete. A full proof-level comparison with Norberg's
original filtered-experiment theorem is not asserted: the original full
text was not available in the present source audit. In particular, we do
not claim novelty for filtered deficiency as such.

Finite-memory control and the common-information method organize
controllers through information states and prescriptions
\cite{Witsenhausen1979,NayyarMahajanTeneketzis2013}. Our cover theorem
addresses a different finite question: exact Bellman optimality for
several endogenous tasks with one retained encoder. Compatible covers
in incomplete machine minimization are classical, and the cover device
itself is not claimed as new. We give its decision-regret interpretation,
its shared-update constraint, and its quantitative connection to the
cost-indexed comparison theorem. The corresponding source-audit
limitations are recorded outside the article.

The operator ingredients are likewise not presented as a new
Mori--Zwanzig identity or a new abstract semigroup approximation theorem.
Trotter's approximation theorem \cite[Theorem~5.2]{Trotter1958} is the
classical antecedent of our graph-core argument. Recent domain-conscious
projection results include \cite{WidderZimmerSchilling2025,WidderSchilling2026}.
The new physical conclusion concerns feedback intervention and marked
finite-register comparison, including its regenerative average-risk
limit. The microscopic flow input is the standard almost-everywhere
hard-sphere flow theorem \cite{GallagherSaintRaymondTexier}.

\subsection{Organization}
Section~\ref{sec:v13-deficiency} defines and proves the intrinsic
comparison theorem, including its nonconvex and exposed-seed boundaries.
Section~\ref{sec:v13-controlled} connects it to endogenous exact and lossy
common decisions. Section~\ref{sec:v13-regeneration} treats average risks,
and Section~\ref{sec:v13-active} proves the active microscopic realization.
The canonical article is self-contained relative to the expressly cited
classical inputs. The accompanying complete development retains every
predecessor mathematical body; those additional results are not silently
made hypotheses of the present main theorem. The research program is a
typed dependency graph, not a chain of implications inferred from paper
names or repository chronology.
