\begin{abstract}
We study finite-use discrimination of ordered quantum measurements.
At a fixed measurement $E$, nonzero one-sided tangent $H$ and
remainder allowance $\Lambda$, consider every legal $F$ with
$\sum_j\|F_j-E_j-sH_j\|_{\rm op}\le\Lambda s^2$.
For independent complete probe--reference groups of at most $b$
calls, the trace separation has orders $\min\{1,\sqrt N s\}$
on the covariance range, $\min\{1,\sqrt{Nb}\,s\}$ on coherent
tangents, and $\min\{1,Ns\}$ on first-order support openings.
The same orders hold for adaptive quantum processing inside blocks
and classical feedback between blocks, with unrestricted receiver
quantum memory, provided every new complete acquisition is prepared
independently of that memory conditional on the classical history.
A conditional fidelity inequality controls the worst-path sum of
squared block lengths without assuming product outputs after feedback.
The constants and scale interval depend on $E,H,\Lambda$, but are
uniform in $N,b,F$ for all $1\le b\le N$.
Arbitrary entangled parallel and fully adaptive strategies consequently
have matching local orders, not necessarily equal distances.
The proofs retain finite quadratic remainders and use lower readouts
independent of the unknown remainder.  A complete support-coordinate
criterion distinguishes the cases, and an explicit sufficient
normalization makes the neighborhoods nonempty.
Further results include a global covariance upper, its complete
support kernel, controlled higher-order approaches, and
balanced-interior description and common learning laws.
Exact classical certificates, quantum-control synthesis and
physical execution are separate resources.
\end{abstract}
