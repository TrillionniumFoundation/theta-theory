## 3. Operational interface and resource classes

The unknown device is an ordered measurement

```text
E=(E_1,...,E_k),
E_j>=0,
sum_j E_j=I,
```

with classical-output channel

```text
M_E(rho)=sum_j tr(E_j rho)|j><j|.
```

No residual post-measurement device system is accessible.

The new exact game compares three two-call classes.

### 3.1 Complete-measurement classical adaptation

The first complete call is fully measured in the experimenter's strategy; only a classical record is retained to choose a freshly prepared second tester. In the two-call tester representation, decision effects lie in the closed cone generated by positive products across the two complete call slots.

This is the class compared to Ohst et al.'s complete-measurement/classically adaptive testers. It is smaller than the unit-reset class.

### 3.2 Unit reset with retained receiver

After the first call, an arbitrary receiver instrument may preserve unmeasured quantum memory and a classical history. The entire second acquisition—probe, reference and within-block working memory—is freshly prepared by one common rule and is tensor-independent of the old receiver conditional on that history. The old receiver is not coherently imported into the second acquisition. At the end, old and new receiver systems may be measured jointly.

Receiver dimension is unrestricted. Classical feedback and public randomization are allowed. The theorem optimizes over this whole class.

### 3.3 All adaptive two-call protocols

The third class allows the old quantum memory to participate coherently in the second acquisition. It is the full deterministic two-call tester class for the stated classical-output interface.

### 3.4 Resource distinction

The exact hierarchy distinguishes:

```text
complete measurement and classical representation,
retained receiver quantum storage behind a fresh-preparation cut,
coherent cross-call acquisition.
```

This is not a hierarchy indexed by ancillary Hilbert-space dimension. The inherited hard costs `N,V,Q` count unknown-device-call allocation, not receiver memory dimension.

## 4. Finite simultaneous second moments

Let `P_y^b` be the `y`th rank-one projection of ordered basis `b`, with weights `w_b`.

The required identities are

```text
M_yy = sum_b w_b P_y^b tensor P_y^b
     = (I+S)/(d(d+1)),

M_yz = sum_b w_b P_y^b tensor P_z^b
     = (dI-S)/(d(d^2-1))    for y!=z.
```

### 4.1 Haar calculation

For one Haar column, invariance and symmetric support give

```text
E[P tensor P]=2 Pi_+/(d(d+1)).
```

For two distinct columns, invariance gives `aI+bS`. The trace is one and the swap contraction is zero by orthogonality. Solving gives the second identity.

Partial trace gives the first moment

```text
sum_b w_b P_y^b=I/d.
```

### 4.2 Finite support

Collecting all `d^2` Hermitian second moments gives a point in a real vector space of dimension at most

```text
d^2 * (d^2)^2 = d^6.
```

The Haar average lies in the convex hull of a compact set. Carathéodory gives a convex combination of at most `d^6+1` ordered bases. Zero weights can be discarded.

This proves finite existence. It does not prove rational weights, efficient construction, minimal support or polynomial bit complexity.

### 4.3 Qubit family

For `d=2`, the six ordered eigenbases of the three Pauli matrices, with both outcome orders and equal weights, satisfy the identities by the Pauli expansion of swap.

## 5. The finite ensemble and first-moment obstruction

Define

```text
C_y=I/d,
E_y^b(t)=(1-t)I/d+tP_y^b,
0<=t<=1.
```

Put

```text
L=2(d+1)-t^2,
p_C=(d+1-t^2)/L,
p_E=(d+1)/L.
```

The alternative basis index `b` is selected once with law `w_b` and is fixed for both calls.

### 5.1 Priors

One has

```text
p_C+p_E=1,
p_E-p_C=t^2/L>=0.
```

Thus the best no-information decision is the alternative, with score `p_E`.

### 5.2 One-call equality

The first moments give

```text
sum_b w_b E_y^b(t)=C_y
```

for every label. Therefore the averaged alternative one-call channel equals the scalar channel. This remains true on inputs entangled with arbitrary references because it is a channel identity.

### 5.3 Shared versus redrawn index

For two uses of one selected device, the alternative second moment is

```text
sum_b w_b E_y^b(t) tensor E_z^b(t).
```

If the basis were independently redrawn, it would instead be

```text
(sum_b w_b E_y^b(t)) tensor
(sum_c w_c E_z^c(t))
=C_y tensor C_z,
```

and the signal would vanish. The theorem is about the shared latent device.

## 8. Filtered-swap lemma

For `A,B>=0`, define

```text
K=(sqrt A tensor sqrt B)S(sqrt A tensor sqrt B).
```

### 8.1 Spectrum

For positive definite factors, `K` is similar to an operator of the form

```text
(sqrt A B sqrt A tensor I)S.
```

Let `lambda_1,...,lambda_d` be the nonnegative eigenvalues of `sqrt A B sqrt A`. On the diagonal basis vectors the eigenvalues are `lambda_i`; on each two-dimensional subspace generated by `(i,j)` and `(j,i)`, they are

```text
+sqrt(lambda_i lambda_j),
-sqrt(lambda_i lambda_j).
```

Therefore

```text
tr(-K)_+=sum_{i<j}sqrt(lambda_i lambda_j)
        =1/2[(sum_i sqrt(lambda_i))^2-sum_i lambda_i].
```

Equivalently,

```text
tr(-K)_+
=1/2[(tr sqrt(sqrt A B sqrt A))^2-tr(AB)].
```

Singular factors follow by continuity, or directly after restricting to support and adding zero eigenvalues.

### 8.2 Upper bound

Let

```text
f=||sqrt A sqrt B||_1
 =tr sqrt(sqrt A B sqrt A).
```

Cauchy–Schwarz on the eigenvalues gives

```text
tr(AB)>=f^2/d.
```

Fidelity Cauchy–Schwarz gives

```text
f^2<=tr(A)tr(B).
```

Hence

```text
tr(-K)_+
<=1/2(1-1/d)f^2
<= (d-1)tr(A)tr(B)/(2d).
```

### 8.3 Equality

For nonzero factors, equality in the final bound forces equality in both steps.

- Equality `f^2=tr(A)tr(B)` says the normalized positive operators `A/trA` and `B/trB` have fidelity one, hence are equal.
- Equality `tr(AB)=f^2/d` says the eigenvalues of `sqrt A B sqrt A` are all equal.

Combining the two conditions forces

```text
A=(tr A)I/d,
B=(tr B)I/d.
```

The scalar factors attain equality.

### 8.4 Rectangular maps

If `U^*U=A` and `V^*V=B`, the nonzero spectrum of

```text
(U tensor V)S(U tensor V)^*
```

is the same as that of `K`; larger receiving spaces add zero eigenvalues. This is the form required by arbitrary receiver dilations.

The lemma is sound. The submitted proof should expose the equality logic more explicitly, because the present polar-decomposition sentence is shorter than the argument it encodes.

## 9. Unit-reset normal form

This is the most delicate reduction.

### 9.1 First acquisition

Purify the initial input-reference state. In a fixed input basis write the vector through a coefficient map `C_0` with

```text
tr(C_0^*C_0)=1.
```

After outcome `y`, the unnormalized receiver/reference operator is congruent to

```text
C_0 E_y^T C_0^*.
```

### 9.2 Intervening receiver instrument

Let `h` include all retained classical outcomes, public random seeds and stopping/control records. Retain the Stinespring environment of the branch map as an upper relaxation. The complete branch output can be represented by one rectangular map `C_yh`:

```text
R_yh(E)=C_yh E_y^T C_yh^*.
```

Set

```text
A_yh=C_yh^*C_yh.
```

Completeness of the instrument gives

```text
sum_h A_yh=C_0^*C_0
```

for each first label `y`. For countably many branches, the sum is a monotone trace-class sum; a finite truncation can retain a remainder branch.

### 9.3 Fresh second acquisition

Conditional on `(y,h)`, unit reset requires the complete new acquisition to be tensor-independent of the old receiver. Purify that fresh state and controls with a rectangular coefficient map `D_yh`. Put

```text
B_yh=D_yh^*D_yh,
tr B_yh=1.
```

Mixed preparations are included by purifying into the fresh reference. Any fresh public random seed can be appended to `h`.

### 9.4 Final receiver effect

The equal-label payoff operator at the receiver is a congruence of `-kS`. For any Hermitian `X`,

```text
sup_{0<=F<=I} tr(FX)=tr(X_+).
```

Therefore a branch contributes at most the positive mass of

```text
-k(C_yh tensor D_yh)S(C_yh tensor D_yh)^*.
```

The filtered-swap lemma applies with `A_yh,B_yh`.

### 9.5 Null histories and endpoint

At `t=1`, some formal histories may be null under an alternative. The resource rule and fresh preparation remain defined on every formal history. The proof uses unnormalized branch operators and never divides by branch probabilities.

### 9.6 Audit conclusion

The reduction covers arbitrary receiver dimension and arbitrary common CP instruments. I find it correct in substance. The paper should state it as a named normal-form lemma with explicit domain/codomain spaces to remove the present dependence on compressed prose.

## 10. Unit-reset upper and attainment

For an equal-label branch, the filtered-swap lemma gives

```text
branch gain
<= k(d-1) tr(A_yh)tr(B_yh)/(2d)
 = k(d-1)tr(A_yh)/(2d).
```

Summing over branches and labels:

```text
sum_yh tr(A_yh)
=sum_y tr(C_0^*C_0)
=d.
```

Thus

```text
P_reset-p_E
<= k(d-1)/2
 = t^2(d-1)/(2dL).
```

### 10.1 Attainment

Prepare two independent states `|Omega>/sqrt(d)`, one for each input-reference pair. After both measurements, declare the scalar hypothesis exactly on the event:

```text
same classical label,
reference pair in Pi_-.
```

Under the scalar device the event probability is

```text
a=(d-1)/(2d^2).
```

Under every basis device it is

```text
(1-t^2)a.
```

The Bayes gain is

```text
a[p_C-p_E(1-t^2)]
=t^2(d-1)/(2dL).
```

Hence

```text
P_reset=(d+1)/L+t^2(d-1)/(2dL).
```

### 10.2 Equality information

If a nonrandomized protocol attains the upper in the selected purified normal form, every nonzero branch must saturate the filtered-swap bound:

```text
A_yh=(tr A_yh)I/d,
B_yh=I/d.
```

Summing gives `C_0^*C_0=I/d`. This is a consequence of equality, not an assumption on competitors. It does not imply uniqueness of dilation or final readout.

## 11. Full adaptive tester upper

### 11.1 Physical derivation of normalization

Purify the initial state as `|psi>` on `I_1 tensor R`. After first label `y`, let `A_y` be the common trace-preserving intervening map from `R` to `I_2 tensor R'`.

Define

```text
X_y=(Id tensor A_y)(|psi><psi|),
Xi_y=tr_{R'} X_y,
rho=tr_R |psi><psi|.
```

Then

```text
Xi_y>=0,
tr_{I_2}Xi_y=rho,
tr rho=1,
tr Xi_y=1.
```

Let `F_yz` be the final receiver effect for the scalar decision. The tester block `T_{0,yz}` is obtained by the corresponding positive congruence and partial trace. Since `0<=F_yz<=I`,

```text
0<=T_{0,yz}<=Xi_y.
```

The complementary tester is positive. This establishes the deterministic two-call tester normalization without a normalized-Choi dimension factor.

### 11.2 Upper

The off-diagonal payoffs are nonpositive. Since

```text
-kS<=kI,
```

one has

```text
P_all-p_E
<=k sum_y tr Xi_y
=kd
=t^2/L.
```

### 11.3 Attainment

Prepare a normalized pure state in the antisymmetric subspace of the two device inputs. Declare the scalar hypothesis when the classical labels agree.

Under `C`, the equal-label probability is `1/d`. Under every `E^b(t)`, it is `(1-t^2)/d`, because the double rank-one projector annihilates the antisymmetric subspace.

The gain is `t^2/L`, so

```text
P_all=(d+1+t^2)/L.
```

This is a parallel strategy, hence also an admissible adaptive strategy.

## 13. Robust score separation

Suppose each specified measurement channel, including the scalar device and every basis device, is replaced by a channel within unhalved diamond distance `delta`, while priors and the latent-index law remain fixed.

A two-call hybrid changes each normalized output by at most `2delta` in unhalved trace norm. Any event probability changes by at most `delta`. Taking suprema over a fixed class gives

```text
|P_class^perturbed-P_class|<=delta.
```

If the displayed reset implementation has hard preparation defect at most `epsilon`, its output changes in unhalved trace norm by at most `epsilon`, and its score changes by at most `epsilon/2`.

Subtracting the perturbed classical upper from the reset lower gives the sufficient condition

```text
2delta+epsilon/2
<t^2(d-1)/(2dL).
```

The factors are consistent. This is conditional norm stability, not physical calibration.





Moreover `M<=R<=M^2`, so the inherited quadratic-budget theorem applies. Expected costs and fractional-duration models are different and are not covered.

### 15.5 Scope

## 20. Literature and priority boundary

### 20.1 Zonnios–Binder

Their coherent-memory hierarchy concerns autonomous repeated-instrument machines for multi-time quantum processes, indexed by coherent ancillary dimension, with finite-time completeness. The present reset model permits time-dependent classical histories and unrestricted receiver storage but imposes a fresh-preparation cut. Hard `Q` is a call-allocation resource, not an ancillary dimension. Neither theorem implies the other.

### 20.2 Ohst–Zhang–Nguyen–Plávala–Quintino

Their constrained-separability framework treats limited auxiliary dimensions and classical/quantum memories in adaptive channel discrimination. Their complete-measurement class is contained in the unit-reset class. The v90 middle upper is over the larger retained-receiver class and cannot be obtained by applying a separable-effect upper to all reset testers.

### 20.3 Measurement comparison

Antisymmetric comparison of unknown measurements and reductions between measurement and state discrimination are established mechanisms. Revision 90 does not claim those mechanisms as new. Its specific assertion is the simultaneous exact optimization of three resource classes on the same finite classical-output ensemble.

### 20.4 Second-moment designs

Finite unitary/projective design theory is established. The paper's Carathéodory lemma is a direct existence reduction, not a design-construction novelty.

### 20.5 Audit conclusion

The active comparison is substantially better than in v89. No equivalent exact triple-value theorem was located in the targeted directly relevant sources inspected for this review. This does not establish firstness. Independent human priority clearance remains absent and is correctly marked false.
