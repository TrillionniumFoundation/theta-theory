\begin{abstract}
We study finite-use discrimination of ordered quantum measurements.
At a fixed measurement $E$ and nonzero one-sided tangent $H$, we
consider every legal $F$ satisfying
$\sum_j\|F_j-E_j-sH_j\|_{\rm op}\le\Lambda s^2$.
Inputs may be entangled within independent groups of at most $b$
calls, with arbitrary references and a collective final readout.
Uniformly over $1\le b\le N$ and the whole stated remainder
neighborhood, the trace separation has orders
$\min\{1,\sqrt N s\}$ on the covariance range,
$\min\{1,\sqrt{Nb}\,s\}$ on coherent tangential directions,
and $\min\{1,Ns\}$ on first-order support openings.
Consequently arbitrary entangled parallel acquisition attains the
fully adaptive local order, while independent acquisition need not.
A complete support-coordinate criterion distinguishes the three cases.
We give an explicit sufficient allowance making the neighborhood
nonempty and a polynomial exact certificate on rational input.
The proof combines horizontal surrogates, finite block-fidelity
bounds, and corrected GHZ blocks with an explicit finite remainder.
The constants and the small parameter interval depend on
$E,H,\Lambda$, but not on $N,b$ or the allowed remainder.
Further results include a global regularized covariance upper,
a complete support-kernel formula, controlled higher-order approaches,
and balanced-interior description and common learning laws.
The latter have fixed-outcome sharpness and separately stated
trusted-control and reconstruction resources.
\end{abstract}
