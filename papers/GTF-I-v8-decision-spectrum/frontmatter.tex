\begin{abstract}
We associate a marked decision spectrum to a causal experiment: one
encoder is fixed before a family of decision problems, and every
persistent resource of its realization is specified. We prove transport
under typed causal simulations, representation of the terminal
all-decision slice by statistical deficiency, and a likelihood-saturated
bridge between Bayesian predictive coordinates and parameter-uniform
experiment comparison. For finite observation alphabets, a
successor-compatible partition formula characterizes optimal causal
quadratic distortion exactly. A delayed-query example separates this
quantity exponentially from checkpoint cardinality. We construct
near-optimal predictive trees using certified rational energy bounds,
with exact suffix closure, logarithmic acquisition depth and a finite
bit description. We also establish the sharp inverse-logarithmic
presentation order for a nonlinear all-priors posterior class, distinguish
it from single-probe simulation, and obtain the finite-register
$S^{-2/(nr)}$ decision-complete law for sequential Gaussian innovations.
The full predictive-tree, filtering and physical count-to-contact
arguments used in these consequences are included.
\end{abstract}
