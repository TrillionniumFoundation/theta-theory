\section{A prior-independent experiment behind predictive coordinates}
\label{sec:v8-sufficiency}
A future-test quotient relative to one Bayesian preparation need not
preserve a parameterized experiment. The additional requirement is
\emph{likelihood saturation}. This section gives both a terminal
construction and the extra recursion needed for a causal reconstruction.
All assertions about measurable coordinates are modulo the stated common
null sets.

\begin{theorem}[A saturated posterior quotient]\label{thm:v8-sufficient}
Let $K$ be compact metric, let $X$ be standard Borel, and let
$E=\{P_\theta:\theta\in K\}$ be continuous in total variation.
Choose a dense sequence $(\theta_j)$ and weights $w_j>0$ with
$\sum_jw_j=1$. Put $m=\sum_jw_jP_{\theta_j}$ and define
\begin{equation}
 T(x)=\left(w_j\frac{dP_{\theta_j}}{dm}(x)\right)_{j\ge1}
       \in[0,1]^{\N}.                                \label{eq:v8-posteriorcoordinate}
\end{equation}
There is one parameter-independent kernel $R$ such that
\begin{equation}
                      R(T_\#P_\theta)=P_\theta
                      \quad(\theta\in K).            \label{eq:v8-sufficientreconstruction}
\end{equation}
Thus $E$ and $TE$ are statistically equivalent. The completed
$\sigma$-field generated by $T$ is minimal among sufficient statistics.
A different dense sequence and strictly positive weights give the same
minimal sufficient $\sigma$-field modulo $m$ and the same experiment
equivalence class, not necessarily the same coordinates.
For the terminal cardinality signature, their marked decision spectra
are identical for every budget and every family of bounded terminal
parameter-decision marks, whose losses depend on the parameter and the
action. An additional latent-target mark requires its own joint-law
sufficiency condition. No computability or finite recursive representation of $T$ is
asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $m(B)=0$, then $P_{\theta_j}(B)=0$ for all $j$; continuity and density
imply $P_\theta(B)=0$ for every $\theta$. Choose Borel versions of the
countably many densities. They give $\sum_jT_j=1$ $m$-almost surely;
modify them on one common null set if necessary. Disintegrate $m$ given
$T$ to obtain the standard Borel conditional kernel $R(t,dx)$.
For every $j$, the density of $P_{\theta_j}$ is $T_j/w_j$, so
\[
 P_{\theta_j}(B)=\int \frac{t_j}{w_j}R(t,B)\,T_\#m(dt)
               =R(T_\#P_{\theta_j})(B).
\]
For arbitrary $\theta$, choose $\theta_{j_k}\to\theta$. Both applying
$T$ and applying $R$ contract total variation. Taking limits proves
\eqref{eq:v8-sufficientreconstruction}.

For minimality, let $S$ be sufficient, in the conditional-law sense that
one kernel $R_S$ is a conditional law of $X$ given $S$ under every
$P_\theta$. Mixing shows that it is also the conditional law under $m$.
The factorization
\[
 \frac{dP_{\theta_j}}{dm}(x)
 =\frac{d(S_\#P_{\theta_j})}{d(S_\#m)}(S(x))
\]
follows by integrating both sides on arbitrary measurable sets using
$R_S$. Thus every coordinate of $T$ is $\sigma(S)$-measurable.
Conversely the preceding disintegration proves sufficiency of $T$.
Mixtures obtained from different dense sequences are mutually absolutely
continuous: each dominates every member of $E$, hence also the other
mixture. Minimality then proves equality of the completed sufficient
$\sigma$-fields.

Finally, any terminal encoder from $TE$ is an encoder from $E$ by
composition with $T$. Conversely, an $S$-label encoder $Q$ from $E$ is
implemented from $TE$ by $QR$, with exactly the same label laws for all
parameters. This uses only transient reconstruction randomization and
at most $S$ terminal labels. It is a kernel assertion, not an algorithm
that stores a sampled infinite object in a finite register. The two
sets of attainable terminal risks, and hence their spectra, coincide.
\end{proof}

The theorem makes explicit a classical dominated-sufficiency mechanism
\cite{LeCam1964,Torgersen1991}. Its role here is to identify the precise
terminal slice on which the prior-relative and parameter-uniform notions
have the same resource profile. A predictive-test quotient has this
property when its generated $\sigma$-field contains the likelihood
coordinates \eqref{eq:v8-posteriorcoordinate}. Without that inclusion the
conclusion is false, even for a two-point parameter set: observe
$Y=\theta\in\{0,1\}$ once, and allow only constant future tests. Their
quotient is a point although the observed experiment distinguishes both
parameters exactly. Predictive equivalence always refers to a declared
family of future questions.

\subsection{The causal factorization}
We give a finite-horizon formulation to avoid any assertion that a
terminal sufficient statistic is automatically a causal sufficient state.
Let $H_t=(A_0,Y_1,\ldots,A_{t-1},Y_t)$ be the observed history.
All policies have the same parameter-independent action kernels. Under
a reference prior on $\theta$, write $\overline P$ for the mixture law.
Suppose Borel coordinates $S_t=S_t(H_t)$ satisfy the following
\emph{saturation conditions}, with versions fixed for every admissible
history and action where they are used:
\begin{align}
 S_t&=F_t(S_{t-1},A_{t-1},Y_t),                         \label{eq:v8-saturation-update}\\
 \overline P(dy\mid H_{t-1},a)&=k_t(dy\mid S_{t-1},a),  \label{eq:v8-saturation-predict}\\
 \frac{dP_\theta|_{H_t}}{d\overline P|_{H_t}}
     &=\ell_{\theta,t}(S_t).                           \label{eq:v8-saturation-likelihood}
\end{align}
The likelihood identity is required for each parameter, initially on a
dense reference family and then for all parameters by finite-horizon
variation continuity. These conditions hold uniformly over the policies
under consideration. Alternatively one may assume domination and the
identity directly for every parameter. The reference prior is not
allowed to change with the policy during a reconstruction.

\begin{theorem}[Causal reconstruction of a saturated quotient]
\label{thm:v8-causal-sufficiency}
Under \eqref{eq:v8-saturation-update}--\eqref{eq:v8-saturation-likelihood},
there are parameter-independent kernels
$L_t(dy\mid s,a,s')$ which reconstruct the observation stream from its
successive quotient states, with the correct joint law under every
admissible policy. Hence the original and quotient instruments have
mutually exact causal simulations. If $S_t$ has at most $K_0$ values
at each time, both simulations may be implemented with $K_0$ internal
states and the cardinality spectra obey the budget dilations of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-spectrum}. If the quotient is not finite, the theorem
asserts statistical and causal reconstruction only, with the storage of
$S_{t-1}$ explicitly charged; it does not assert a finite-register
resource equivalence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $H_t^0(ds'\mid s,a)$ be the pushforward of $k_t(\cdot\mid s,a)$ by
$F_t(s,a,\cdot)$. Disintegration, including the Borel variables $(s,a)$,
gives
\begin{equation}
 k_t(dy\mid s,a)
   =\int L_t(dy\mid s,a,s')H_t^0(ds'\mid s,a),         \label{eq:v8-causaldisintegration}
\end{equation}
with $F_t(s,a,y)=s'$ under the resulting joint kernel. On histories
with $\ell_{\theta,t-1}(s)>0$, change of measure gives the actual joint
conditional law of $(Y_t,S_t)$ as
\[
 L_t(dy\mid s,a,s')
 \frac{\ell_{\theta,t}(s')}{\ell_{\theta,t-1}(s)}
 H_t^0(ds'\mid s,a).
\]
In particular, conditioning further on $s'$ removes the likelihood
factor. On zero-likelihood histories the kernel may be chosen
arbitrarily; such histories are not visited under that parameter.
For parameters outside a countable reference support, equality of the
constructed finite-horizon laws extends by total variation, or follows
directly from the assumed domination identity.

Start at the declared initial state. Given a target policy on the
reconstructed reports, feed its chosen action into the quotient
instrument; on receiving $s'$, draw $y$ with $L_t(\cdot\mid s,a,s')$,
then replace $s$ by $s'$. Induction proves equality of every finite
parameter-indexed report/action law. This induction is essential: it reconstructs
responses after the same actions, not after actions selected with hidden
parameter information. In the forward direction, apply $F_t$ recursively
to the real reports. Both executions retain only the previous quotient
state in addition to the target encoder's own register. This costs
$K_0S$ states if that state has $K_0$ possible values. The general
standard Borel version has no such finite bound, exactly as stated.
\end{proof}

A likelihood statistic alone supplies \eqref{eq:v8-saturation-likelihood},
not \eqref{eq:v8-saturation-predict}. Conversely, a Bayesian predictive
state supplies future report predictions, not necessarily all parameter
likelihoods. The conjunction is the bridge. Sequential comparison of
filtered experiments already has a substantial decision-theoretic
history \cite{Norberg2002}; the present statement fixes the additional
register cost of the reconstruction rather than claiming that sequential
comparison itself is new.

For marks involving an additional latent target rather than the parameter, its conditional kernel must also be included in the predictive saturation condition. The causal reconstruction theorem does not infer equality of latent-target laws from equality of report laws alone.
