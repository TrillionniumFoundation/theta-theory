[4] The realization algebra has a wider scope than the collision theorem.
[5] It is useful to state it at that scope before returning to the monomial
[6] geometry. The static coefficient set is a finite-output postprocessing
[7] region in the sense of Blackwell \cite{Blackwell1953}. Its iteration
[8] records the joint law of the retained label and the latent parameter.
[9] Neither this postprocessing interpretation nor polynomial propagation
[10] asserts the attainable rank or the collision profile proved above.

[12] Let $\Theta$ be a standard Borel space, let $\mu$ be any probability
[13] on $\Theta$, and let $k_1^a,\ldots,k_J^a:\Theta\to[0,1]$ be Borel
[14] functions with $\sum_k k_k^a=1$. Here $a$ denotes a fixed known
[15] specification of the cells. No monomial structure, density of $\mu$,
[16] or strict positivity of the cells is required in this section until
[17] we explicitly return to collision limits. Conditional on $t\in\Theta$,
[18] the raw cells on successive trials are independent with law $k^a(t)$.
[19] The command lies
[20] in $G=[\eta,1-\eta]^J$, where $0<\eta<1/2$; raw cell $k$ is
[21] reported as $k$ with probability $g_k$ and otherwise as $\dagger$.
[22] The command at time $n$ has prescribed law $\nu_n$, independently of
[23] the latent parameter, all previous variables and the fresh private coins.
[24] The controller reads only its preceding label, current command and
[25] current report, and retains one of $M$ labels after each report.
[26] 
[27] At checkpoint $n<N$ there is a finite query menu with fixed
[28] nonnegative weights $\omega_{nq}$ summing to one. Each future
[29] likelihood $H^a_{nq}\in[0,1]$ is the product of at most $N-n$
[30] cell-linear likelihood factors. In particular it is a polynomial of
[31] degree at most $N-n$ in the cells. The query is selected independently
[32] after retention. Mean regret is the squared difference from the
[33] full-history prediction, averaged over this prescribed experiment,
[34] and the common-controller criterion is the maximum of these means.
[35] The notation of Definition~\ref{def:monomial-risks-v24} is extended
[36] to this finite-cell model with exactly those quantifiers. The positive
[37] monomial model and its uniform command laws are a specialization.
[38] A history of zero evidence contributes zero to every integral; its
[39] posterior prediction can be assigned any value in $[0,1]$.

[41] \subsection{The implementability body}
[42] For a command law $\nu$, put $m_k=\int_G g_k\,\nu(\mathrm dg)$ and define
[43] \begin{equation}\label{eq:v33-allocation}
[44]  \mathscr Z_M(\nu)=\left\{z\in\R^{J\times M}:
[45]  z_{kj}=\int_G(1-g_k)u_j(g)\,\nu(\mathrm dg),\quad
[46]  u:G\longrightarrow\Delta_M\ \hbox{Borel}\right\},
[47] \end{equation}
[48] where $\Delta_M$ is the probability simplex. Define the one-state
[49] implementability body by
[50] \begin{equation}\label{eq:v33-body}
[51]  \mathscr A_M(\nu)=
[52]  \left\{ A\in\R^{J\times M}: A_{kj}=m_k r_{kj}+z_{kj},\quad
[53]        r_k\in\Delta_M\ (1\le k\le J),\quad z\in\mathscr Z_M(\nu)\right\}.
[54] \end{equation}
[55] Every row of $A$ sums to one. The shared array $z$ is essential: all
[56] raw cells that produce failure must use the same failure update.
[57] 
[58] 
[59] \begin{proposition}[The finite-output Blackwell region]
[60] \label{prop:v34-blackwell}
[61] On $\Omega=G\times\{1,\ldots,J,\dagger\}$ define the experiment
[62] with auxiliary index $k\in\{1,\ldots,J\}$ by
[63] \begin{equation}\label{eq:v34-experiment}
[64]  \mathsf E_k(\mathrm dg,\mathrm dx)=\nu(\mathrm dg)
[65]        \{g_k\delta_k(\mathrm dx)+(1-g_k)\delta_\dagger(\mathrm dx)\}.
[66] \end{equation}
[67] Then, exactly,
[68] \begin{equation}\label{eq:v34-blackwell}
[69]  \mathscr A_M(\nu)=\{\mathsf E U:
[70]        U\text{ is a Borel Markov kernel from }\Omega\text{ to }[M]\}.
[71] \end{equation}
[72] The actual observation law given $t$ is the mixture
[73] $\sum_k k_k^a(t)\mathsf E_k$. The index of this auxiliary experiment
[74] is not an additional observation supplied to the controller.
[75] 
[76] For a prior $\pi$ on the auxiliary indices and a reward table
[77] $r(k,j)$, its optimal finite-action Bayes reward is the support function
[78] of $\mathscr A_M(\nu)$ at $C_{kj}=\pi_k r(k,j)$. Moreover, for
[79] $k\ne l$ the measure
[80] \[
[81]  \lambda_{kl}(\mathrm dg,\mathrm dx)
[82]  =\min(1-g_k,1-g_l)\nu(\mathrm dg)\delta_\dagger(\mathrm dx)
[83] \]
[84] is a common submeasure of $\mathsf E_k$ and $\mathsf E_l$.
[85] Its mass is preserved by every postprocessing, and hence
[86] \begin{equation}\label{eq:v34-blackwell-overlap}
[87]  \operatorname{TV}(A_k,A_l)
[88]  \le 1-\lambda_{kl}(\Omega)\qquad(A\in\mathscr A_M(\nu)),
[89] \end{equation}
[90] where total variation between probability rows is half their
[91] $\ell^1$ distance.
[92] \end{proposition}
[93] \begin{proof}
[94] Direct integration gives
[95] \[
[96]  (\mathsf E U)_{kj}
[97]    =\int_G\{g_k U(j\mid g,k)
[98]                   +(1-g_k)U(j\mid g,\dagger)\}\,\mathrm d\nu.
[99] \]
[100] The accepted term is $m_k r_{kj}$ for the probability row obtained
[101] by $g_k$-weighted averaging of the accepted kernel. The failure terms
[102] all use the same simplex-valued function $U(\cdot\mid g,\dagger)$,
[103] so belong to $\mathscr Z_M(\nu)$. Conversely, realize any
[104] $m_k r_{kj}+z_{kj}$ by the constant accepted kernel $r_k$ and a
[105] common failure kernel representing $z$. This proves
[106] \eqref{eq:v34-blackwell}. Mixing the conditional raw-cell laws gives
[107] the asserted physical observation law.
[108] 
[109] For any postprocessing the Bayes reward is
[110] $\sum_{k,j}\pi_k r(k,j)(\mathsf E U)_{kj}$; taking the supremum
[111] proves the support interpretation. Finally $\lambda_{kl}\le\mathsf E_k$
[112] and $\lambda_{kl}\le\mathsf E_l$, so the row vector $\lambda_{kl}U$
[113] is dominated by both output rows. Its entries sum to
[114] $\lambda_{kl}(\Omega)$ because $U$ is a Markov kernel. Thus
[115] $\sum_j\min(A_{kj},A_{lj})\ge\lambda_{kl}(\Omega)$, which is
[116] \eqref{eq:v34-blackwell-overlap}.
[117] \end{proof}

[119] The proposition identifies the postprocessing region. The next lemma
[120] supplies its compactness and explicit support function; the corollary
[121] then excludes arbitrary stochastic rows from the controller domain.
[122] 
[123] \begin{lemma}[One-step implementability]\label{lem:v33-body}
[124] The set $\mathscr A_M(\nu)$ is compact and convex. A matrix $A$ belongs
[125] to it if and only if there are Borel transition probabilities
[126] $U(j\mid g,x)$ such that
[127] \begin{equation}\label{eq:v33-realize-row}
[128]  A_{kj}=\int_G\bigl[g_k U(j\mid g,k)
[129]                  +(1-g_k)U(j\mid g,\dagger)\bigr]\,\nu(\mathrm dg).
[130] \end{equation}
