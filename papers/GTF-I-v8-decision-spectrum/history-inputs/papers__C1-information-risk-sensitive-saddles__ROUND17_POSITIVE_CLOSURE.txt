[11] \subsection{The global belief state and regular observation charts}
[12] 
[13] Let $E$ be the microscopic or path state and $Y$ the observation space.  A
[14] belief is a probability $\pi\in\mathcal P_W(E)$ with finite Lyapunov moment.
[15] For a control $a$ let
[16] \[
[17]  \Lambda^a_\pi(dx,dy)=\pi(dx)K^a(x,dy)
[18] \]
[19] be the unnormalized one-step joint kernel.  The exact next belief is a
[20] Rokhlin conditional law of $x'$ given the observed $y$ under the predicted
[21] joint law.  The global state stores the unnormalized coefficient measure and,
[22] when its total mass is zero, a projective direction in the positive cone.
[23] 
[24] Fix a reference observation measure $\nu$.  A regular chart
[25] $\mathfrak B_{M,c,C,s}$ consists of beliefs and controls for which the
[26] predicted observation kernel has a density $k_\pi^a(x,y)$ satisfying
[27] \[
[28]  c\le k_\pi^a(x,y)\le C,
[29]  \qquad
[30]  \|k_\pi^a\|_{W^{s,1}_y(C^1_{W,x})}\le M,
[31]  \qquad s>\dim Y.
[32] \]
[33] The chart metric is the weighted weak belief metric plus the
[34] $W^{s,1}_y(L^1_W)$ distance of unnormalized kernels.  By Rellich compactness,
[35] its bounded subsets are compact in the corresponding $L^1$ kernel topology.
[36] 
[37] \begin{theorem}[Model-derived regular filter chart]
[38] \label{thm:r17-c1-chart}
[39] For the finite-cylinder Sinai observations and the regular hard-sphere
[40] particle/contact observations used in this series, every compact source and
[41] control set has a chart $\mathfrak B_{M,c,C,s}$ on a fixed positive-evidence
[42] region.  The microscopic joint kernels enter that chart after an exponentially
[43] good smoothing at a scale tending to zero, and the smoothing error is
[44] exponentially negligible at the governing LDP speed.
[45] \end{theorem}
[46] 
[47] \begin{proof}
[48] For Sinai observations, the unsmoothed vector--return--roof coefficient theorem
[49] of A2 gives a density with uniform derivatives on compact interior
[50] observation windows.  Convolution only regularizes the bounded local path
[51] coordinates and its error is controlled by the singularity shield of A3.  For
[52] hard spheres, B1's exact-number coefficient theorem and B2's positive
[53] actual-contact source expansion give the same bounds on compact regular
[54] windows.  In both cases the denominator has a uniform Gaussian-scale lower
[55] bound, while Sobolev derivatives follow by differentiating the common source
[56] kernel before inversion.  The complement of the regular event has an
[57] arbitrarily large exponential cost after the chart is enlarged.  Atomic
[58] finite-volume empirical observations are therefore represented by an
[59] exponentially good regularized kernel, not asserted to lie literally in an
[60] $L^1$ density topology.
[61] \end{proof}

[63] \subsection{Integrated filter stability}
[64] 
[65] If $J(dx,dy)=j(x,y)m(dx)\nu(dy)$ is in the chart, put
[66] $e_J(y)=\int j(x,y)m(dx)$ and
[67] \[
[68]  \Pi_J(y)(dx)={j(x,y)m(dx)\over e_J(y)}.
[69] \]
[70] 
[71] \begin{theorem}[Dominated disintegration estimate]
[72] \label{thm:r17-c1-stability}
[73] For two joint kernels $J,\widetilde J$ in one regular chart,
[74] \[
[75]  \int W_1^W(\Pi_J(y),\Pi_{\widetilde J}(y))
[76]       \bar e(y)\nu(dy)
[77]  \le C_\mathfrak B
[78]  \|j-\tilde j\|_{L^1_y(L^1_W)},
[79] \]
[80] where $\bar e=e_J\wedge e_{\widetilde J}$.  Consequently prediction
[81] followed by observation is Feller on the regular chart.  The theorem makes no
[82] continuity claim for arbitrary weakly convergent joint laws.
[83] \end{theorem}
[84] 
[85] \begin{proof}
[86] For every weighted Lipschitz test $\varphi$,
[87] \[
[88]  \left|{\int\varphi j\,dm\over e_J}
[89]       -{\int\varphi\tilde j\,d\tilde m\over e_{\tilde J}}\right|
[90]  \le {C\over c}
[91]  \int W(x)|j(x,y)-\tilde j(x,y)|\,dm_0(x),
[92] \]
[93] after embedding both kernels in the fixed dominating measure $m_0$ of the
[94] chart.  Take the supremum over the weighted Lipschitz unit ball and integrate
[95] in $y$.  The positive lower bound on evidence and the fixed dominating
[96] measure are precisely the hypotheses absent from weak disintegration.  The
[97] prediction kernel is continuous in the chart norm by
[98] Theorem~\ref{thm:r17-c1-chart}; composition proves Feller continuity.
[99] \end{proof}

[101] \subsection{Exact belief Markov state and dynamic programming}
[102] 
[103] Let $\mathcal Z$ be the disjoint union of the regular belief charts and the
[104] blown-up zero/singular-evidence boundary.  The state records the unnormalized
[105] positive coefficient measure, its mass, and a projective cone direction when
[106] the mass is zero.
[107] 
[108] \begin{theorem}[Belief transition and posterior DPP]
[109] \label{thm:r17-c1-dpp}
[110] For each predictable control pair there is a Borel transition kernel on
[111] $\mathcal Z$ obtained by prediction and Rokhlin disintegration.  On regular
[112] charts it is Feller; on the full state it is universally measurable.  Compact
[113] relaxed controls admit measurable selectors, and the finite-horizon
[114] risk-sensitive value satisfies the exact posterior dynamic-programming
[115] principle.  One-time preparation, reward-only feedback, and adaptive
[116] likelihood control remain three distinct games.
[117] \end{theorem}
[118] 
[119] \begin{proof}
[120] The microscopic transition and observation kernels are Borel on standard
[121] Borel spaces.  Rokhlin disintegration can be selected jointly measurably in
[122] the joint law on a countably generated observation sigma-field.  Adjoin the
[123] unnormalized mass and the projective zero-mass direction before normalization;
[124] this makes the update Borel also at zero evidence.  The regular-chart Feller
[125] property is Theorem~\ref{thm:r17-c1-stability}.  The compact relaxed-control
[126] space and lower semicontinuity of the transition action give a measurable
[127] minimum/maximum selector by the measurable maximum theorem.  Conditioning the
[128] exact path likelihood at the next observation time and applying the tower
[129] property yields the DPP.  Since the phase/preparation variable is fixed once
[130] in the first game but the adaptive source is selected blockwise in the third,
[131] their timing is not conflated.
[132] \end{proof}

[134] \subsection{Finite determining coordinates without a fictitious rate}
[135] 
[136] Let $K\subset\mathcal P_W(E)$ be a compact regular belief set.  For every
[137] $\eta>0$, choose a finite family
[138] $\Phi_\eta=(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_{m(\eta)})$ from the
[139] weighted bounded-Lipschitz unit ball that separates an $\eta$-net of $K$.
[140] Let $R_\eta$ map the coordinate vector to a nearest point of the net.
[141] 
[142] \begin{proposition}[Tolerance-dependent belief reconstruction]
[143] \label{prop:r17-c1-finite}
[144] The families may be chosen measurably so that
[145] \[
[146]  \sup_{\pi\in K}
[147]  W_1^W\bigl(\pi,
[148]  R_\eta(\pi(\Phi_\eta))\bigr)
[149]  \le3\eta.
[150] \]
[151] The finite-coordinate controlled values converge uniformly to the full belief
[152] value as $\eta\downarrow0$.  No claim is made that the first $K$ members
[153] of an arbitrary dense enumeration have a prescribed resolution rate.
[154] \end{proposition}
[155] 
[156] \begin{proof}
[157] Compactness gives a finite $\eta$-net.  For each pair of distinct net
[158] points, Kantorovich duality supplies a bounded Lipschitz test separating them
[159] by at least their distance minus $\eta$; collect finitely many such tests.
[160] Nearest-net-point reconstruction is Borel after fixing a deterministic tie
[161] rule and gives the metric estimate.  The regular filter kernel and reward are
[162] uniformly continuous on $K$, so backward induction and the contraction of the
[163] risk-sensitive Bellman operator give uniform convergence of values.
[164] \end{proof}
