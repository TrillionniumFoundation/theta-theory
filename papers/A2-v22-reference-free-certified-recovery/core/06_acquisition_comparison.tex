\section{Acquisition, comparison and retained conclusions}\label{sec:acquisition}
\subsection{A sufficient producer and earlier stopping}
The completeness certificate does not require an exhaustive scanner.
Nevertheless, the previously proved scanner gives one finite sufficient
producer of genuine records. We record its scope and the geometric
reason for eventual acceptance; its complete original proof is retained
in Supplement P.

Let $\rho_* =\sup_x\dist(x,\mathcal O)$ and choose $R>2\rho_*$.
The lifted graph joining clear shortest bridges of gap less than $R$
is connected. Indeed choose $\rho_*<b<R/2$. The open $b$-neighborhoods
of the bodies cover the plane, so their intersection graph is connected.
Intersecting neighborhoods have body gap less than $R$. If a shortest
segment of such a pair meets a third body, the two resulting gaps are
strictly shorter. The set of gap lengths below $R$ modulo periods is
finite. Induction over these lengths replaces every obstructed pair by
a clear path. The clear graph is therefore connected. Path lifting from
a root copy to each of its translates shows that the cycle translations
of its finite quotient generate the full period lattice.

Suppose $r\le r_0$, body diameters are at most $D_0$, and
$\rho_*\le\rho_0$. Set
\[
 L_0=\rho_0+D_0+(r_0-1)(R+2D_0),\qquad B=L_0+\xi,\quad\xi>0.
\]
A tree lift of the quotient graph places one point in every representative
within $(r_0-1)(R+2D_0)$ of the root. Every point in the plane is
within $\rho_0+D_0$ of one of their translates. Hence $L_0$ bounds
the covering radius of the lattice. Every bridge-midpoint coset has a
representative in the aperture $B_B(0)$. Scanning all clear shortest
bridges of gap less than $R$ with midpoints there therefore includes
a covering saturated set. The new certificate must accept by the end
of this finite scan, and may justify stopping earlier.

Only bodies meeting the disk of radius $B+R/2$ can be endpoints or
blockers; their complete shapes lie in the padded radius $B+R/2+D_0$.
For a fixed periodic table there are finitely many. A uniform count
requires a further quantitative prior: if all distinct bodies are
$d_0$-separated, packing gives at most
$(1+2(B+R/2+D_0)/d_0)^2$ bodies to test. The aperture bounds alone
are not a uniform bound on the number of copies or pair tests.

This producer uses geometric access to bodies, shortest pairs and blockers.
We do not obtain those primitives from unmarked trajectories. In contrast,
the stopping decision uses only the local probability records already
produced and known uniform bounds. It never asks the producer whether
all channels, all types or a named witness were retained. The distinction
between a sufficient acquisition mechanism and a posterior certificate
is part of the theorem, not a change of sensor.

\subsection{Relation to inverse geometry and arithmetic}
Stefanov--Uhlmann--Vasy~\cite[Theorems 1.1 and 1.3]{SUV} recover a
Riemannian metric in dimension at least three from local boundary distances
or global lens data under their convexity hypotheses, modulo boundary-fixing
diffeomorphisms. Here the ambient metric is Euclidean and the unknowns are
two reflecting arcs, a periodic obstacle family and its period group.
Proposition~\ref{prop:lens} explicitly identifies the local information:
two physical densities are equivalent to a generating action and its
absolute phase normalization. No claim that travel-time differentiation
or the affine quotient is a new abstract lens-rigidity principle is needed.

Gurfinkel--Noakes--Stoyanov~\cite[Theorems 1 and 2]{GNS} use exterior
travelling times to recover the non-trapping flow and, under non-positive
curvature, strictly convex obstacles. Their boundary measurement set is
not a finite list of selected local three-impact records. The present
completion identity addresses the missing-obstacle and period-index
problem for that finite list; it does not replace their exterior inverse.
The local direction identities are credited in Section~\ref{sec:local}.
Santal\'o-type relations between flow measure and geometry provide a
separate volume perspective \cite{Santalo}. Here the specific input is
the absolute cell normalization in \eqref{eq:density}, and the new use
is the nonnegative completion defect \eqref{eq:defect}.

The obstruction-descent argument has a proximity-graph antecedent in
relative-neighborhood constructions \cite{Toussaint,JT}. Gaps between
bodies need not satisfy the triangle inequality, but the strict shortening
along a blocked shortest segment suffices for the finite induction.
Visibility complexes and free-bitangent constructions \cite{PV} concern
different geometric primitives from normal dispersing returns. None of
their algorithmic complexity results is transferred to the unknown-body
physical acquisition problem. Likewise Hermite/Smith algorithms
\cite{KB,Cohen} apply to the integer stage once rational coordinates
are separated; they do not make analytic-body fitting polynomial-time.

The analytic-continuation loss remains class-dependent \cite{Trefethen}.
The new statistical result eliminates a supplied reference witness,
not the analytic strip or quantitative shape and symmetry assumptions.
We do not assert an exhaustive priority search. The claimed additions
are the missing-area completion identity, its reference-free finite-error
certification and the precisely scoped physical area-testing theorem.

\subsection{What remains available without alteration}
The complete preceding manuscript and all its original inputs remain
Supplement P. This includes the finite-index classification for incomplete
but covering collections; the all-cycle gcd formula and primitive-generation
example without a primitive pair; complete short-channel catalogues;
finite apertures; sparse saturated witnesses; and persistence while unused
channels cross the cutoff or lose visibility. A reference-based stability
theorem remains valid under its original hypotheses; it is not retroactively
claimed to have proved the new reference-free certificate.

Supplement R retains the moment-compressed all-parity inverse, the
registered theorem for symmetric or repeated shapes, smooth finite-jet
realization and count-fiber arguments, as well as both earlier regularization
routes. Supplement S retains the complete smooth relative-law, Volterra,
Abel and auxiliary-experiment development. The present primary is organized
around one completion question rather than reprinting the entire programme,
but no earlier theorem is deleted, silently weakened or declared published
elsewhere.

For the moment sensor, reconstruction order can increase while finite-jet
conditioning deteriorates. For the full endpoint sensor, the fixed
$C^2$ density estimate gives actual arcs before continuation. These are
different experiments, and their error exponents cannot be compared as
minimax rates for one observation. Theorem~\ref{thm:area-testing} adds
a sharp decision scale only in its stated fixed-window family. None of
the full-law results proves exact analytic count-only rigidity or
nonrigidity. Equality of formal or smooth Taylor data is still distinct
from equality of exact analytic count germs. Symmetric and repeated-shape
tables retain their registered or finite-ambiguity statements, not the
unregistered uniqueness claimed for the asymmetric shape-separated class.
