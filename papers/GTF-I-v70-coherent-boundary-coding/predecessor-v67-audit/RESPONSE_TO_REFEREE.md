## Principal mathematical response

The report distinguishes the correctness of the v66 arithmetic upper theorems from the absence of a matching general description theory. Sections 38–39 now supply two precise converses and constructive encodings. They do not change the old definition of width or claim that a description converse is a trajectory-space lower bound.

For all m-outcome instruments M_d -> M_n the affine dimension is s=d^2(mn^2-1). A diamond ball of radius 1/(dmn) fits inside the legal Choi body; a finite-dimensional volume argument gives the sharp high-resolution coefficient s. An exact codec removes the d^2 marginal coordinates, solves them from the partial-trace identity, applies the proven integer buffer and packs the remaining s signed digits into one mixed-radix integer. A particular supplied rational instrument is processed with exact arithmetic and polynomial input/output complexity, without net enumeration.

For the fixed-margin body J_y>=aI and a fixed final error delta<1, reusable descriptions for N calls have optimal length (s/2)log_2 N+O(1). The upper proof expresses each nearby pair as two different classical mixtures of the same two genuine quantum instruments. The programme method is classical and explicitly credited. Product relative entropy and a common-processor contraction give a finite square-root-N bound for arbitrary common adaptive testers, including entangled memories and bounded public stopping. Repeated Choi tests and a full-dimensional packing give the matching description converse. The rational codec realizes the corresponding precision by an exact integer square-root grid.

These are matching general description laws in stated asymptotic regimes. They are not an optimal simultaneous dimension/error theorem, an unknown-channel learning theorem, a general disturbing-instrument workspace classification or physical classical channel simulation. A unitary boundary example explains why the positive margin cannot be silently removed. The v63 multiplicative width gap remains a separate statement.
