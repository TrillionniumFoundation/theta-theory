\begin{abstract}
We determine joint description laws for a boundary family of quantum
instruments that retains coherent data and appends a rank-bounded preparation
state. With $u=d^2-1$ unitary directions and
$v=\sum_y r_y(2n-r_y)-1$ preparation directions, the optimal reusable
length is $(u+v/2)\log_2N+(u+v)\log_2(1/\delta)+O(1)$, uniformly for
$N\ge1$ and $0<\delta\le1/32$. Dimensions and rank bounds are fixed.
Error is the unhalved final-state trace distance against arbitrary common
bounded adaptive quantum testers. Pair-dependent unitary amplification and
product-state testing give the converse even for centres outside the family.
A rational unitary atlas and a singular factor code give legal centres with
rank nonincrease and exact zero-outcome preservation; an explicit qubit
codec uses only integer and rational arithmetic. An exact retraction also
shows that general memoryless centres do not improve preparation-family
coverings. We retain the joint interior law of coefficient $s/2$, where
$s=d^2(mn^2-1)$, and the margin-free preparation law of coefficient $v/2$.
The coherent boundary law is not a classification of all disturbing
instruments. The encoder receives supplied matrix data; payload length,
encoder workspace, learning and physical classical simulation are distinct
resources. No dimension-uniform constants are asserted.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle
\noindent\textit{Operational interfaces.}
Description compilation returns a mathematical quantum instrument;
numerical streaming processes fully specified matrix data. Neither is a
physical classical device acting on an unknown quantum input, and
neither is merely a transcript-only simulation task. The new code lengths
charge the reusable description, with fixed public dimensions, rank bounds where applicable, horizon and error.
All joint-state errors
are unhalved trace norms; transcript total variation is half the classical
block trace norm.
