\section{Observation densities and coupled process versions}
\label{sec:v20-versions}
We record three measure-theoretic facts used in the global transport
argument. Their purpose is to make explicit the passage from a tilted
physical law to an observation law and from two marginal processes to
one joint realization.

\begin{lemma}[Observation-level return from a bounded tilt]
\label{lem:v20-tilt}
Let $dP^g/dP=g(Z)/c$, where $c=\E_Pg$ and
$e^{-\gamma}\le g\le e^\gamma$. For any observation sub-$\sigma$-field
$\mathcal F$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-tilt}
 \frac{d(P|_{\mathcal F})}{d(P^g|_{\mathcal F})}
 =\E_{P^g}\left[\frac{c}{g}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F\right]
 =\frac{c}{\E_P[g\mid\mathcal F]}.
\end{equation}
Both this density and its reciprocal lie in
$[e^{-2\gamma},e^{2\gamma}]$. In particular, for any nonnegative
$\mathcal F$-measurable $V$,
$\E_P V\le e^{2\gamma}\E_{P^g}V$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $A\in\mathcal F$, integrate $c/g$ over $A$ under $P^g$ to obtain
$P(A)$. Conditioning proves the first identity. In the other direction,
$d(P^g|_{\mathcal F})/d(P|_{\mathcal F})=\E_P[g\mid\mathcal F]/c$;
equivalence and inversion prove the second. The bounds follow from the
bounds on $g$, its conditional mean, and $c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Domination of the common-path measure]
\label{lem:v20-diagonal}
Let $(Y',Y)$ be a coupling on a standard Borel path space, with marginal
laws $P',P$, and let $E=\{Y'=Y\}$. The subprobability measure
$\kappa(A)=\Pr(E,Y\in A)$ is dominated by both $P$ and $P'$.
Consequently, for any nonnegative measurable path functional $V$,
\[
 \E[\1_E V(Y)]\le\E_P V,
 \qquad \E[\1_E V(Y')]\le\E_{P'} V.
\]
The assertion also holds on a common latent--observation space when the
latent coordinate is shared by the coupling.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The event defining $\kappa(A)$ is contained in $\{Y\in A\}$ and,
on $E$, in $\{Y'\in A\}$. This proves domination on sets. Integration
of simple functions followed by monotone convergence proves the rest.
Apply the same argument to the product coordinate for the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Marginal stochastic integrals on an arbitrary coupling]
\label{lem:v20-integrals}
On a canonical standard Borel observation path space, suppose that under
each of two marginal laws there are a continuous adapted process $U$,
a predictable square-integrable process $Z$, and an innovation $I$ such
that, in that marginal's own filtration,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-integralidentity}
 \int_0^t Z_s\,dI_s
   =U_t-U_0+\frac\gamma2\int_0^t|Z_s|^2\,ds,
 \qquad 0\le t\le T.
\end{equation}
Assume $Z$ is bounded, as in the entropic model here. There are measurable
versions of the two continuous integral processes which can be pulled
back to any coupling of the observation laws. Each pulled-back version
has the correct marginal law and satisfies its own identity
\eqref{eq:v20-integralidentity}. Neither integral thereby acquires a
martingale or It\^o-integral interpretation in the enlarged joint
filtration.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A continuous process is determined by its values at rational times.
Completed-measurable random variables on the canonical space have Borel
versions. Choose such versions at the countably many rational times and
extend by continuity on the measurable set where a continuous extension
exists, using the zero path elsewhere. This gives a Borel
$C([0,T])$-valued version of $U$. Likewise take a jointly measurable
version of $Z$ modulo $dt\otimes P$; its bounded time integral has a
continuous path, and its rational-time values give a measurable
continuous version by the same construction. The right side of
\eqref{eq:v20-integralidentity} is consequently a measurable continuous
path functional. Marginal equality at all rational times, and continuity
of the stochastic integral, make it indistinguishable from that integral.
Repeat for the other marginal. Pullback along the two coordinate maps of
any coupling preserves their respective almost-sure identities and laws.
No enlargement theorem for innovations is used.
\end{proof}

In Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-global}, Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-tilt} is applied with
$\mathcal F=\mathcal F_T$ to the integrated squared coefficient
comparison. Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-diagonal} justifies restricting a same-path
estimate to the equality event of the latent-preserving coupling.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-integrals} fixes the meaning of the coupled backward
martingale integrals before their difference is estimated. Thus the
coupled notation retains the marginal observation filtrations throughout.
