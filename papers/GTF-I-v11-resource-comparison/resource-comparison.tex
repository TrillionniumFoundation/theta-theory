\section{State-costed comparison and complete-state fusion}
\label{sec:v11-comparison}

An experiment is not identified with an implementation of its observation
channel. Two equivalent experiments can expose the same information at
different times. The cost of carrying that information between those times
is the subject of this section. All losses in this section take values in
$[0,1]$; a bound $L$ multiplies every error term by $L$.

\subsection{The information interface and priced randomness}
Fix a finite horizon $T$, finite control and terminal decision alphabets,
a parameter set, a common marked target $W$, and a family $J$ of decision
rows. A row specifies a parameter and a task. The task is available to its
controller and terminal decision rule, but not to the common encoder.
The parameter is unavailable to all three. The timing is the timing of
Section~\ref{sec:v8-spectrum}: a report is consumed once, and only the
retained register is passed to the next checkpoint. Any current side
observation supplied afresh by the instrument is part of the interface and
is specified separately. It is not silently included in the register.

Write $S=(S_0,\ldots,S_T)$ for the private register bounds. A \emph{public
seed of size $q$} is an independent random variable with at most $q$ values,
retained and readable by the encoder and the controllers. Its law is chosen
as part of the design. Fresh private randomization remains available.
The notation $q=\infty$ permits one independent uniform random variable
on $[0,1]$, with no bound on its information content. A seed is never
allowed to depend on the source or parameter. Let
\[
 \mathcal R_E(S,q)=\{(r_j(\pi))_{j\in J}:\pi
      \text{ is an admissible common-encoder design on }E\}.
\]
For continuous risk functions on compact $J$, put
\[
 \mathcal U_E(S,q)=\mathcal R_E(S,q)+C(J)_+,
 \qquad
 h_E(S,q;w)=\inf_{r\in\mathcal R_E(S,q)}\int_Jr_j\,w(dj),
 \quad w\in\mathcal P(J).
\]
The lower support value $h$ does not assert convexity of the private risk
set. In particular, $q=1$ is not replaced by $q=\infty$ in this notation.

\begin{definition}[Primitive marked simulator]
\label{def:v11-simulator}
A simulator $\Gamma:E\longrightarrow F$ consumes the reports of $E$ and
produces the reports of $F$ with the same timing and control interface.
At checkpoint $t$ it retains $J_t\in\{1,\ldots,K_t\}$, and it uses a public
seed with at most $r$ values. Its update and report kernels depend only on
its old state, the current input, the currently supplied side observations,
and the issued control. They do not depend on the task or parameter.
The simulator has error $\varepsilon$ when, for every parameter and every
admissible randomized feedback strategy at the output interface, the total
variation distance between the simulated and true joint laws of the
output transcript and the \emph{actual} mark $W$ is at most $\varepsilon$.
The same requirement applies after adjoining independent randomization
used by the output strategy. The simulator neither observes an unavailable
mark nor replaces it by an independent draw.
\end{definition}

This definition is a constraint on a causal channel, not a claim that a
channel exists whenever certain decision inequalities hold. It is often
verified by a parameter-uniform coupling or by factoring the actual marked
law. The abstract simulation hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-spectrum}
is thereby supplied with an explicit machine and a charged state space.

\begin{theorem}[Resource-costed comparison]
\label{thm:v11-comparison}
Suppose $\Gamma:E\longrightarrow F$ is a primitive simulator of cost
$(K,r)$ and error $\varepsilon$. Then, for every target width $S$ and seed
bound $q$, every target design has a source implementation retaining only
reachable pairs $(J_t,I_t)$. In particular its width is at most $K_tS_t$
and its public seed has at most $rq$ values. Its risk differs from the
target risk by at most $\varepsilon$ in every row. Consequently
\begin{align}
 \mathcal U_F(S,q)+\varepsilon\1
       &\subseteq\mathcal U_E(KS,rq), \label{eq:v11-upper}\\
 h_E(KS,rq;w)&\le h_F(S,q;w)+\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:v11-support}
\end{align}
Products involving $\infty$ have their natural meaning.
Two composable simulators of costs $(K,r)$ and $(L,s)$ and errors
$\varepsilon,\eta$ have a composite of cost $(KL,rs)$ and error at most
$\min(1,\varepsilon+\eta)$. Identity simulators have cost $(\1,1)$ and
error zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Keep $(J_{t-1},I_{t-1})$ and the two independent seed indices. The target
controller uses $I_{t-1}$ to issue the next control. The simulator consumes
the source input, draws its synthetic report and new internal state, and
passes that report to the target encoder. The encoder draws $I_t$ and
then discards the report. This is one transition on the finite product
state space; no earlier input is retained. The common encoder remains
common because the simulator does not use the task and the only task
information entering its transition is the legitimately issued control.
Unreachable pairs may be removed, which proves the stated sharper
implementation bound as well as the product bound.

Definition~\ref{def:v11-simulator} applies to this feedback strategy.
Applying a bounded terminal loss, or a bounded measurable loss of the
specified marked transcript, changes its expectation by at most total
variation. The same simulated design works for all rows; it is not chosen
after the row is known. This proves the risk assertion. If $r_E$ and $r_F$
are the resulting risks, then $r_F+\varepsilon\1-r_E\ge0$, which gives
\eqref{eq:v11-upper}; integration and an infimum give
\eqref{eq:v11-support}.

For composition retain both simulator states and both seed indices.
Compare the composite first with the second simulator fed by the exact
intermediate experiment. Data processing does not increase the first
error. The second comparison costs at most $\eta$. This argument is valid
under the feedback of the final consumer, since that consumer and the
second simulator together are an admissible feedback strategy at the
intermediate interface. The triangle inequality proves the error bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Local error certificates]
\label{rem:v11-local}
If the two conditional transition laws can be coupled at step $t$ with
failure probability at most $e_t$, conditional on every previously matched
history, and the terminal conditional mark can be coupled with failure
probability at most $e_W$, then the simulator error is at most
$1-(1-e_W)\prod_t(1-e_t)$. Indeed the probability that all couplings succeed
is at least the product. A bound only for uncontrolled one-step marginals
is not such a certificate: the conditional and feedback quantifiers are
necessary.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Deficiency frontiers and spectra}
Define $\delta_{K,r}(E,F)$ as the infimum of the errors of the simulators in
Definition~\ref{def:v11-simulator} with the indicated cost bounds. Set the
value to $+\infty$ when there is no such simulator. No attainment is
presupposed.

\begin{corollary}[Composition and decision witnesses]
\label{cor:v11-frontier}
For compatible interfaces,
\[
 \delta_{KL,rs}(E,G)
 \le \delta_{K,r}(E,F)+\delta_{L,s}(F,G).
\]
Moreover each choice of $S,q,w$ gives the lower bound
\[
 \delta_{K,r}(E,F)\ge
 \bigl[h_E(KS,rq;w)-h_F(S,q;w)\bigr]_+.
\]
If the two upper risk sets under consideration are closed and convex,
\eqref{eq:v11-upper} is equivalent to \eqref{eq:v11-support} for every
$w\in\mathcal P(J)$. This last equivalence concerns risk sets, and is not
an inverse theorem constructing a simulator.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use simulators within an arbitrary positive tolerance of each finite
infimum, apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-comparison}, and let the tolerance
decrease to zero. For the risk-set assertion, separate a point outside
the closed convex upper set from that set. Any separating continuous
linear functional must be nonnegative on $C(J)_+$, since the set is upper.
It is therefore integration against a finite positive measure. Normalize
its mass to one. The resulting separation contradicts the asserted lower
support inequalities. The other implication is immediate.
\end{proof}

For a fixed bounded loss family, let $b_{E,j}$ be the full-history Bayes
risk with the same observation timing and parameter-blind decision rules,
and define the centered minimax spectrum
\[
 \Theta_E(S,q)=\inf_{r\in\mathcal R_E(S,q)}\sup_{j\in J}(r_j-b_{E,j}).
\]
When the unrestricted baseline is defined using a prior rather than a
single parameter row, the same argument applies to that prior throughout.
If exact full-history simulation is possible in both directions, these
baselines agree. More generally, forward and reverse simulation errors
$\varepsilon,\eta$ imply $b_{F,j}-b_{E,j}\le\eta$, and hence
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_E(KS,rq)\le\Theta_F(S,q)+\varepsilon+\eta.
 \label{eq:v11-centered}
\end{equation}
Here the baseline inequality follows by simulating any source
full-history rule on the target and taking its infimum. Thus neither a
parameter oracle nor a change of timing enters the centering.
For a fixed horizon and two exact simulations with fixed finite costs,
a scalar budget $B$ controlling all $S_t$ and the charged finite seed
index is changed by at most constant factors. Whenever a positive
spectrum has a power exponent, the inequalities in both directions
preserve that exponent. A growing horizon with growing $K_t$ is not covered
by this constant-factor assertion.

\begin{proposition}[Public-to-private conversion and finite seed cost]
\label{prop:v11-seed}
A design with a $q$-valued public seed has an exactly equivalent privately
randomized implementation of width $qS_t$: store the sampled index with
the private state. Suppose, in addition, that a family of risks satisfies
$|r_j(\pi)-r_{j'}(\pi)|\le\omega(d(j,j'))$ for all private designs, where
$J$ is compact. For an ideal public design and any $h,\zeta>0$, there is a
public mixture of at most $N(J,h)+1$ private designs whose uniform risk
error is at most $\zeta+2\omega(h)$. Charging this index gives private
width at most $(N(J,h)+1)S_t$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The exact conversion samples the seed before observing the source and
makes its index part of every register. Since the controller reads that
register, all the original kernels can be executed. This is not a claim
that $qS_t$ is always necessary.

Choose an $h$-net of $N=N(J,h)$ rows. At these rows the risk vector of the
ideal design is an expectation of vectors in $[0,1]^N$, and hence belongs
to the closure of their convex hull. Approximate it within $\zeta$ by a
finite convex combination. Caratheodory's theorem reduces this combination
to at most $N+1$ actual private designs without changing those $N$
coordinates. Move from an arbitrary row to a net row in the target and
the approximation; each move costs at most $\omega(h)$. This proves the
claim without assuming that the private risk set itself is closed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The marked quotient as a zero-overhead object}
For this subsection use the finite marked instrument of
Section~\ref{sec:v10-marked}. Let $c_t(h)$ be its marked continuation class,
$K_t$ the number of supported classes, and $Q(E)$ the class experiment of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-reconstruction}. It has class update
$F_t(c,a,y)$ and the parameter-independent reverse report kernel
$L_t(y\mid c,a,c')$ constructed there.

\begin{definition}[Complete retained design]
\label{def:v11-complete}
A design is complete when, at every checkpoint, there is a map
$\phi_t$ such that
$c_t(H_t)=\phi_t(U,I_t)$ almost surely for every supported preparation
and open-loop control word. With a finite instrument the same maps can be
chosen outside one seed-null set, simultaneously for all supported
histories. On $Q(E)$ the corresponding condition is
$C_t=\phi_t(U,I_t)$. Let $\mathcal R^{\mathrm c}$ denote the risk body
restricted to these designs.
\end{definition}

Completeness is equivalent to preserving all the restart Brier risks of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-minimal}. It is not imposed on an arbitrary finite
list of decision tasks.

\begin{theorem}[Complete-state fusion]
\label{thm:v11-fusion}
For the finite marked instrument, every width vector and seed signature
satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_E^{\mathrm c}(S,q)=
 \mathcal R_{Q(E)}^{\mathrm c}(S,q).
 \label{eq:v11-fusion}
\end{equation}
Both sides are empty if $S_t<K_t$ at any required checkpoint. For general,
not necessarily complete, designs the two exact simulations give instead
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal U_{Q(E)}(S,q)&\subseteq\mathcal U_E(KS,q),\\
 \mathcal U_E(S,q)&\subseteq\mathcal U_{Q(E)}(KS,q).
\end{align*}
Thus exact marked equivalence is resource-neutral on complete retained
designs, while on arbitrary task designs it has an explicitly charged
state distortion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider first a complete design on $E$ to be run on $Q(E)$. At the start
of a step recover the old class $c$ from $(U,I_{t-1})$. When the class
instrument supplies $c'$, sample $y$ from $L_t(\cdot\mid c,a,c')$ and
perform the original encoder update with $y$. The factorization in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-reconstruction} shows that this report has the correct
conditional law, jointly with the actual future marked experiment.
In particular every positive-probability updated register symbol has
class $c'$, by completeness of the original design. The new register
therefore again recovers the new class. The sampled report and the old
class need not be retained separately. Induction gives an implementation
with precisely the original private labels and the original seed.

Conversely, start with a complete design on $Q(E)$ and an actual report
$y$ from $E$. Recover $c$ from the old register, compute
$c'=F_t(c,a,y)$, and apply the class encoder with that input. Its new
register recovers $c'$. The class update and terminal marked channel are
exactly those of the class experiment, so induction again proves equality
of all marked risks. At the initial checkpoint use the stipulated initial
class (or the same class construction after the initial report). Neither
construction inserts an additional persistent state.

The lower bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-minimal}, applied conditionally on
the independent seed; the finite supported-history set permits a common
null set. Finally, without completeness one retains $c$ separately.
The forward class tracker and the reverse report transducer each need at
most $K_t$ states, and Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-comparison} gives the last two
inclusions.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A sharp temporal state charge]
\label{ex:v11-repetition}
Let $X$ be uniform on $\{1,\ldots,k\}$ and let $W=X$. Experiment $E$
reports $X$ at time one and a constant at time two; experiment $F$ reports
$X$ at both times. These marked experiments simulate each other exactly.
The forward simulator $E\longrightarrow F$ stores $X$ between reports;
the reverse one is stateless. At widths $(S_1,S_2)=(s,k)$, the terminal
error for identifying $W$ is zero on $F$, whereas on $E$ it is at least
$1-s/k$ when $s\le k$, with equality attainable.
Indeed, for a private message channel $Q(m\mid x)$, its maximal guessing
success is
\[
 \frac1k\sum_m\max_x Q(m\mid x)\le\frac{s}{k}.
\]
Independent public randomization averages this bound and cannot improve
it. Partitioning the $k$ inputs into $s$ nonempty cells attains it.
The same argument applied to a simulator which must reproduce the second
report shows that its exact intermediate state count is at least $k$.
The marked continuation quotient has $k$ states. A complete consumer
already carries them, and Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-fusion} eliminates the
additional factor for exactly that reason.
\end{example}

Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-comparison}, the lower support characterization in
Corollary~\ref{cor:v11-frontier}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-fusion} are the
resource-aware comparison statement of this article. The compactness
results below supply cases in which its dual risk invariant is attained;
the task-completion results identify which exact state requirement a
particular decision family imposes.
