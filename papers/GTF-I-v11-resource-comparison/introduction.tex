\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:v11-introduction}

Statistical equivalence does not specify the memory needed to use an
experiment. A report supplied at two different times and the same report
supplied only once contain the same full-history information, but they
need not support the same finite-register decisions. Nor does the state
needed for a finite list of optimal decisions necessarily determine the
state needed to preserve a whole marked continuation experiment. We study
these distinctions through causal comparison with an explicit persistent
state cost.

The object is a marked causal experiment together with an information
interface. A single parameter-blind encoder must serve all declared tasks.
A task controller may use its own task index but cannot reveal it as an
additional uncharged encoder input. A consumed report is discarded; only
the specified register survives. Independent public randomness is a
separate resource. In particular a public variable taking an arbitrary
point of a compact policy space is not identified with a finite index
stored in the register.

\subsection{The structural result}
Let $\mathcal R_E(S,q)$ be the risk functions attainable on $E$ with
private widths $S=(S_t)$ and a retained independent public seed of at most
$q$ values. The value $q=\infty$ denotes an ideal uniform seed. The central
comparison statement has three parts.

First, a causal marked simulator with internal widths $K=(K_t)$, public
seed bound $r$, and feedback-uniform error $\varepsilon$ transfers every
target design to a source design with widths at most $KS$, seed bound
$rq$, and row-risk error at most $\varepsilon$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-comparison}). The actual implementation uses the
reachable pairs of the simulator and consumer states. Composed simulators
multiply these costs and add their errors. Lower support values of the
common-encoder risk bodies give witnesses against an insufficient
simulation budget (Corollary~\ref{cor:v11-frontier}). These are concrete
implementations of the abstract spectrum transport statement retained in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-spectrum}, not a new name for that statement.

Second, for a finite marked instrument let $Q(E)$ be the quotient by
conditional laws of the parameter, future reports, and actual target mark.
A design is complete if its retained register, together with its independent
seed, determines the current continuation class. Then
\[
 \mathcal R_E^{\mathrm c}(S,q)=
                 \mathcal R_{Q(E)}^{\mathrm c}(S,q).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-fusion} proves this equality by fusing the simulator's
class register into the consumer's already complete register. Without
completeness the same simulation can require $K_tS_t$ labels. The repeated
report example gives a sharp temporal cost: exact experiment equivalence
can coexist with error $1-s/k$ at an intermediate width $s$, while the
other presentation has zero error. Thus the cost distortion and its
zero-overhead subcategory are both part of the comparison invariant.

Third, completeness has an operational decision meaning. For a finite
family of restart decisions, the least exact width is a compatible-cover
number (Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-taskcover}). It is generally not the number
of equivalence classes of a pairwise relation. For Brier prediction tasks
the unique optimal predictions turn that cover into equality of conditional
probability features. Under positive reports their causal closure is the
least right congruence refining those features
(Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-causal-tasks}). Completion under all marked event
predictions recovers the universal quotient, and hence the complete-state
fusion theorem.

\subsection{Analytic realization and computational distinctions}
The lower support invariant is attained in two nonfinite observation
classes. The product-dominated finite-horizon theorem retained from the
preceding development proves compactness of the private risk set and
compact convexity of its ideal-public body for standard Borel reports
with an $L^1$-continuous family of complete loss effects
(Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-compact}). Its tensor-testing and measurable
representative arguments remain essential: merely naming a standard Borel
carrier would not supply compactness.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-conditional} adds a singular displayed-covariate
class. A continuous physical observation is supplied afresh at every
checkpoint, while the innovations satisfy a conditional product-effect
hypothesis. The full observation path can be supported on a nonatomic
diagonal and have no product dominating measure. A compact space of
conditional probability measures on private innovation policies gives an
exact executable ideal-public risk body. This is a conditional Young
measure argument with the private state still bounded, not an assumption
that arbitrary information constraints are weakly closed. Finite stored
randomness is recovered at the explicit row-covering cost of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v11-seed}.

The exact continuation compiler uses classical weighted-automaton linear
algebra to test equality, but outputs positive normalized states. We prove
a polynomial count-vector bound for homogeneous conditionally independent
controlled reports and an exponential $K_t=2^t$ example at continuation
rank two. We also realize graph coloring as the exact zero-excess
common-task width. Thus linear equivalence testing, positive-state
realization, and task-optimal compression are distinct computational
questions, not three descriptions of one polynomial algorithm.

\subsection{A physical consequence and the place of the other results}
The microscopic hard-sphere application makes the comparison theorem
operative on an unbounded generator. Orbit regularization constructs a
collision-compatible weighted $L^p$ graph core, with an explicit single
approximation sequence (Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-core}). For noisy finite
acquisitions of actual physical observables, its approximation error gives
stateless marked simulators in both directions. Their decision bounds are
uniform over all memory widths and feedback rules
(Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-physical-transfer}). This is a microscopic domain and
acquisition result; it does not assert the separate action-sublevel BBGKY
corrector or the nonlinear kinetic limit. The physical model and its
operator topology are not replaced by a finite positive hidden-state
model to obtain this conclusion.

The common-membership separation, delayed-query Gaussian exponent,
positive filtering, finite nonlinear semigroups, hidden-phase moments,
predictive trees, probability-measure geometry, and finite statistical
comparison are retained with their full proofs and local hypotheses.
They give distinct ways to evaluate or test the same risk construction.
The canonical article contains this development; the complete-development
edition additionally retains the earlier mathematical bodies and historical
introductions. Earlier results are not deleted or declared consequences
of stronger hypotheses they do not satisfy.

\subsection{Antecedents and the claim of distinction}
Blackwell--Le Cam comparison and positive convex separation underlie the
risk-set viewpoint \cite{Blackwell1953,LeCam1964,Torgersen1991}.
Filtered-experiment comparison is already present in
Norberg~\cite{Norberg2002}. Weak star team-policy topology, existence, and
strategic-measure convexification are established methods
\cite{Saldi2017Topology,YukselSaldi2017}. Theorems~6, 7, and~9 of
\cite{Saldi2017Topology} provide the relevant continuity, existence, and
static-reduction antecedents. We do not claim any of these mechanisms as
new, or infer unrestricted filtered compactness from the conditional
extension proved here.

The common-information dynamic program of
\cite{NayyarMahajanTeneketzis2013}, Theorem~3 and Corollary~5, and causal
coding reductions such as \cite{YukselCausal2010} identify useful designer
states and policy structures. An offline belief can be an excellent
optimization variable without being free online memory. Our costed
simulators count the state needed to execute the comparison and identify
exactly when it is already carried by a complete consumer.

Automaton continuation and predictive equivalence have their own long
history \cite{Nerode1958,ShaliziCrutchfield2001,Tzeng1992,Kiefer2020}.
Proposition~2.1 and Theorem~2.3 of \cite{Kiefer2020} describe the linear
span engine used here. Graph coloring in zero-error information is also
classical \cite{Witsenhausen1976}. The new statements are therefore
submitted as resource-sensitive refinements and their operational
connection, not as the invention of minimax, causal comparison, state
minimization, or graph coloring. The distinction sought is the interaction between executable state cost,
the task family, and exact marked reconstruction. No claim of priority
for unrestricted filtered comparison is made.

A model becomes a consumer of the comparison theory only after its
information interface and analytic hypotheses have been verified. An
acquisition adapter does not, by itself, derive that model's independent
geometric or operator-theoretic assertions. This distinction is especially
important for microscopic limits, where the topology and the scale of
approximation form part of the theorem.
