\begin{abstract}
We study marked causal experiments used by one common finite-register
encoder across several decision tasks. A simulator's persistent state
and independent public randomness are charged explicitly. We prove risk-body
and spectrum comparison under composition, and an exact zero-overhead
fusion theorem when the consumer already determines the marked continuation
class. For a finite task family the least exact checkpoint width is a
compatible-cover number; completion under marked prediction losses yields
the universal continuation quotient. Compact common-encoder duality holds
for product-dominated finite-horizon loss effects and, by conditional policy
randomization, for a singular class with repeatedly displayed continuous
covariates. Finite stored seeds have an explicit row-covering cost. Positive
state synthesis has polynomial structured families but can require
exponentially many states at linear rank two; task-optimal zero-excess
synthesis contains graph coloring. A collision-compatible graph core for
hard-sphere transport gives width-uniform marked comparison for noisy
physical acquisitions. Common-task separation, temporal Gaussian exponents,
filtering, semigroup, predictive-tree and measure-valued consequences are
developed with their respective information and resource specifications.
\end{abstract}
