\section{A microscopic graph core and width-uniform acquisition transfer}
\label{sec:v11-hard-sphere}

The comparison theorem can be used before a kinetic limit is taken.
Here the underlying trajectories are actual hard-sphere trajectories,
not a finite hidden-state surrogate. We construct a collision-compatible
core for their unbounded transport generator and show that replacing
observables by this core preserves every bounded-register decision problem
under noisy acquisition. The topology of the core is specified explicitly:
it is a weighted $L^p$ graph topology, not local uniform convergence on
kinetic action sublevels.

\subsection{The microscopic flow and invariant spaces}
Let $\Omega_{N,\epsilon}$ be the nonoverlap phase space of $N$ identical
hard spheres of diameter $0<\epsilon<1/4$ in the unit flat torus, with unbounded
velocities. At a binary nongrazing collision the incoming and outgoing
velocities are related by elastic specular reflection. We use the
classical almost-everywhere hard-sphere flow $\Phi_t^{N,\epsilon}$,
defined for all real times outside the invariant singular null set.
The requisite existence statement excludes multiple collisions, grazing
pathologies, and finite-time accumulation for almost every initial state;
see the hard-sphere flow construction in \cite{GallagherSaintRaymondTexier}.
This classical flow existence theorem, not a kinetic-limit theorem, is
an explicit input here. The cited construction is stated in Euclidean
space. Its local exceptional-set argument applies to the periodic system
as follows. On a bounded-energy set and a bounded time interval there are
only finitely many periodic images that can enter a collision chart.
Near a contact, $\epsilon<1/4$ gives an unambiguous local lift to a
Euclidean binary-collision surface. The volume estimate for states with
two collisions in an interval of length $\delta$ is therefore the same
$O(\delta^2)$ estimate, with a finite sum of chart constants; this is the
estimate in Lemma~4.1.1 of the cited construction. A union over
$O(T/\delta)$ time intervals has volume $O(T\delta)$. Letting
$\delta$ decrease to zero excludes collision accumulation, together
with the multiple and grazing exceptional sets. Exhaust bounded energies
and time intervals countably. This supplies the periodic version used
here; no uniform-in-$N$ collision estimate is inferred from it.

The normalized equilibrium measure
\[
 d\mu_{N,\epsilon,\beta}
 =Z_{N,\epsilon,\beta}^{-1}\1_{\Omega_{N,\epsilon}}
             \exp(-\beta |V_N|^2/2)\,dX_N\,dV_N
\]
is invariant. Free flight preserves phase volume, elastic reflection has
unit absolute Jacobian and preserves kinetic energy, and their
concatenation gives invariance on the regular flow. Null sets are
completed in the usual way. We restrict to permutation-symmetric
observables.

One may use a fixed nonempty system, or a countable disjoint family of
nonempty systems $(N,\epsilon_N)$ with positive summable weights $a_N$.
In the latter case put $\bar\mu=\sum_N a_N\mu_{N,\epsilon_N,\beta}$,
normalized to mass one. This is a family of particle sectors, not a claim
that infinitely many positive-radius spheres fit into a fixed torus.
Additional particle-number or energy weights may be absorbed into the
invariant measure whenever their total mass is finite. Fix $1\le p<\infty$
and let $\mathcal B_p=L^p_{\rm sym}(\bar\mu)$.

\begin{lemma}[Strong transport and a dense observable class]
\label{lem:v11-flow}
The operators $U_t f=f\circ\Phi_t$ form a strongly continuous isometric
group on $\mathcal B_p$. Let $A$ be its generator. The symmetric smooth
observables with finite sector support and compact support in the
interior phase space are dense in $\mathcal B_p$. The generator is the
closed transport operator associated with the physical collision flow;
in the interior it acts as $\sum_i v_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Invariance gives $\|U_tf\|_p=\|f\|_p$ and the flow law gives the group
property. First truncate the sector sum, then truncate velocities and
remove a neighborhood of the collision boundary. The discarded mass
tends to zero. Ordinary smooth approximation on the remaining open
phase space, followed by permutation averaging, proves density.
For a bounded continuous interior test, $f(\Phi_tz)\to f(z)$ for almost
every initial state as $t\to0$: before its first collision an interior
trajectory is a free flight. Dominated convergence gives strong
continuity on the dense test class; isometry extends it to every $f$.
The generator of a strongly continuous group is closed. Testing against
interior smooth functions and differentiating free flight identifies its
interior distributional action. The collision identification is inherited
from the flow, rather than prescribed by an arbitrary boundary extension.
\end{proof}

Choose a nonnegative $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ with integral one and
write $\varphi_\delta(s)=\delta^{-1}\varphi(s/\delta)$. Define the orbit
regularization
\begin{equation}
 B_\delta f=\int_\R\varphi_\delta(s)U_sf\,ds.
 \label{eq:v11-orbit}
\end{equation}
The integral is a Bochner integral in $\mathcal B_p$.

\begin{theorem}[Collision-compatible graph core]
\label{thm:v11-core}
For every $f\in\mathcal B_p$, $B_\delta f\in\bigcap_{m\ge1}D(A^m)$,
and
\begin{align}
 A^m B_\delta f&=(-1)^m\int\varphi_\delta^{(m)}(s)U_sf\,ds,
 \label{eq:v11-core-derivative}\\
 \|A^mB_\delta f\|_p&\le
       \delta^{-m}\|\varphi^{(m)}\|_1\|f\|_p.\nonumber
\end{align}
The linear span of $B_\delta f$, with $f$ in the dense class of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v11-flow} and $\delta>0$, is a graph core for $A$.
There is one diagonal sequence of such observables converging to any
$g\in D(A)$ in the graph norm. These observables have equal incoming
and outgoing traces at regular specular collisions, in the local
collision-flux almost-everywhere sense.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A change of variables gives
\[
 U_h B_\delta f=\int\varphi_\delta(s-h)U_sf\,ds.
\]
Difference quotients of the scalar compactly supported smooth function
converge in $L^1(ds)$. Isometry therefore permits passage to the limit in
$\mathcal B_p$, proving \eqref{eq:v11-core-derivative} for $m=1$ and its
norm estimate. Iterate to get every $m$. Strong continuity and the
approximate-identity property give $B_\delta f\to f$ in $\mathcal B_p$.
If $g\in D(A)$, differentiation under the Bochner integral gives
$AB_\delta g=B_\delta Ag$, so $B_\delta g\to g$ also in graph norm.

To obtain a core with the stated dense tests, choose $\delta_n\downarrow0$
and a smooth finite-sector $f_n$ with $\|f_n-g\|_p\le\delta_n^2$.
Then
\[
 \|B_{\delta_n}(f_n-g)\|_p\le\delta_n^2,
 \qquad
 \|AB_{\delta_n}(f_n-g)\|_p
       \le\|\varphi'\|_1\delta_n.
\]
Together with graph convergence of $B_{\delta_n}g$ this proves the single
sequence assertion. There is no passage from an iterated limit to a
uniform kinetic estimate in this argument.

It remains to verify the boundary assertion, which is why orbit
regularization is used instead of convolution in particle coordinates.
For almost every regular orbit, $s\mapsto f(\Phi_sz)$ is locally
integrable. Equation~\eqref{eq:v11-orbit}, evaluated on that orbit,
is convolution in its time coordinate; it is a smooth function of time.
At a nongrazing binary collision, the incoming point and its reflected
outgoing point are the same orbit at the same collision time. The two
one-sided values of this smooth time function therefore agree. To pass
from interior almost-everywhere statements to collision traces, use a
local free-flight tube transverse to a regular collision surface. Its
phase-volume element is the collision surface element times the absolute
normal relative velocity times $ds$. Fubini's theorem transfers the
orbit statement to collision-flux almost every boundary point. Exhaust
bounded-velocity, nongrazing, single-collision tubes by countably many
such charts. Their complement consists of the excluded singular or
grazing sets. This gives the asserted trace convention and no stronger
claim of global smoothness across grazing configurations.
\end{proof}

The theorem solves an exact microscopic domain problem in a stated
weighted norm. It does not assume a bounded finite-state generator.
It also does not identify the transport generator with a nonlinear
collision Hamiltonian: the deterministic chain rule remains the
transport chain rule.

\subsection{Noisy acquisitions of physical observables}
Let the initial microscopic state $Z$ have density $\rho_\theta$ with
respect to $\bar\mu$. Assume
\begin{equation}
 \sup_\theta\|\rho_\theta\|_{p'}\le M,
 \qquad 1/p+1/p'=1.
 \label{eq:v11-density-bound}
\end{equation}
A finite acquisition protocol chooses, by feedback, an index
$a\in\mathcal A_t$ before its $t$th report. Its scalar report is
\[
 Y_t=f_{t,a}(\Phi_{t_t}Z)+\sigma_t\xi_t,
 \qquad \xi_t\sim N(0,1),\quad\sigma_t>0,
\]
where the $\xi_t$ are mutually independent and independent of $Z$.
Each $f_{t,a}$ is a bounded symmetric observable. An actual mark $W$ is
sampled from a fixed kernel given $Z$, independently of acquisition noise;
in particular it may be any measurable functional of the entire
microscopic trajectory. The acquisition choice does not alter the
hard-sphere dynamics. Denote this marked experiment by $E_f$.

\begin{theorem}[Graph-core acquisition comparison]
\label{thm:v11-physical-transfer}
For another finite observable family $g_{t,a}$, the identity report
transducers in both directions between $E_f$ and $E_g$ have one internal
state, no public seed, and marked feedback error at most
\begin{equation}
 e(f,g)=\min\left\{1,
    \frac{M}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_t\frac1{\sigma_t}
           \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_t}\|f_{t,a}-g_{t,a}\|_p\right\}.
 \label{eq:v11-physical-error}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every width profile, every seed bound and every bounded
common-task family,
\begin{align*}
 |h_{E_f}(S,q;w)-h_{E_g}(S,q;w)|&\le e(f,g),\\
 |\Theta_{E_f}(S,q)-\Theta_{E_g}(S,q)|&\le2e(f,g).
\end{align*}
Taking $g_{t,a}=B_\delta f_{t,a}$ makes these bounds tend to zero
uniformly over all register widths and feedback designs, while all
approximating observables belong to the microscopic graph domain and
satisfy its collision compatibility.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple the two experiments with the same $Z$, the same mark conditional
on $Z$, and the same private and public randomization of the feedback
design. Conditional on a matched report history and on $Z$, the issued
control is the same. The total variation distance between the two next
Gaussian report laws is
\[
 2\Phi\!\left(\frac{|f_{t,a}(\Phi_{t_t}Z)
                       -g_{t,a}(\Phi_{t_t}Z)|}{2\sigma_t}\right)-1
 \le \frac{|f_{t,a}(\Phi_{t_t}Z)
                       -g_{t,a}(\Phi_{t_t}Z)|}{\sigma_t\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]
Use a maximal coupling at each step. The probability of a first mismatch
is bounded by the expectation of the sum of these bounds on previously
matched histories. Bound the selected absolute difference by the sum
over all possible $a$; this removes the selection's dependence on $Z$
and on earlier noises. By H\"older's inequality and invariance,
\[
 \int \rho_\theta(z)|f-g|(\Phi_{t_t}z)\,d\bar\mu(z)
 \le M\|U_{t_t}(f-g)\|_p=M\|f-g\|_p.
\]
Summing proves \eqref{eq:v11-physical-error}. The mark has been coupled
identically throughout. This proves the required uniform marked feedback
bound, not just convergence of open-loop marginal observations.
The identity channel retains no state. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-comparison} in both directions and
\eqref{eq:v11-centered} to get the two decision bounds. Finally strong
convergence of $B_\delta$ applies to the finitely many observables;
Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-core} supplies the domain and boundary assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A physical consumer of compact duality]
\label{cor:v11-physical-duality}
Suppose the parameter space is compact, $\theta\mapsto\rho_\theta$ is
$L^1(\bar\mu)$ continuous, and the mark kernel is parameter independent.
Let the bounded loss family be uniformly continuous in its compact row
index in supremum norm. Then $E_f$ satisfies the dominated loss-effect
hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-compact}. Its ideal-public risk body
is compact, its common-encoder minimax duality is attained, and its
graph-core approximations obey Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-physical-transfer}
under \eqref{eq:v11-density-bound}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed open-loop control word, condition on $Z$ and multiply the
Gaussian report densities. The mixture is absolutely continuous with
respect to Lebesgue measure, and hence to a product of standard Gaussian
reference probabilities, whose density is everywhere positive. Integrate
the actual conditional marked loss before taking this density. A change
of preparation by $\|\rho_\theta-\rho_{\theta'}\|_1$ changes the
$L^1$ loss effect by at most that amount times the loss bound. A change
of loss costs at most its supremum norm. The row-effect map is therefore
$L^1$ continuous. This verifies the hypothesis rather than presuming it
from smoothness of the microscopic dynamics. The stated conclusions now
follow from the two cited theorems.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The precise historical implication}
The B4 microscopic program requires both exact generator-domain objects
and a model-specific identification of their kinetic limit. The preceding
results supply a boundary-compatible weighted $L^p$ graph core and a
width-uniform decision transfer for noisy physical acquisitions. They
neither prove the BBGKY recollision estimates nor turn $L^p$ graph
convergence into convergence locally uniformly on action sublevels.
The latter requires additional uniform integrability and model-specific
corrector estimates. In particular the full historical B4 target is not
an automatic consequence of the present consumer.

There is a second distinction worth recording. A nonlinear resolvent
$J_\lambda=(I-\lambda H)^{-1}$, when it exists uniquely, satisfies
\begin{equation}
 J_\lambda f=
 J_\mu\left(\frac\mu\lambda f+
                  \left(1-\frac\mu\lambda\right)J_\lambda f\right).
 \label{eq:v11-nonlinear-resolvent}
\end{equation}
Indeed, put $u=J_\lambda f$ and substitute $Hu=(u-f)/\lambda$ in
$u-\mu Hu$. A linear difference/product resolvent identity cannot simply
be carried over to $H$. Exponential resolvents and the required domain
extension have an established theory; see \cite{Kraaij2019}, Theorem~3.4
and Proposition~3.6. Equation~\eqref{eq:v11-nonlinear-resolvent} is the
identity to use in a nonlinear continuation of the microscopic graph
argument, not a proof of full range from the existence of the linear
Koopman group.
