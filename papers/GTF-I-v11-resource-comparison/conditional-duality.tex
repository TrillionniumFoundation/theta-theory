\section{A singular observation class and conditional compact duality}
\label{sec:v11-conditional}

Product domination is not invariant under repeatedly displaying a
continuous observation. A physical instrument may display the same
covariate at every checkpoint, without asking the private register to
store it. We prove compact duality for this non-product observation class.
The distinction between a repeatedly supplied observation and a privately
remembered one is essential, as Example~\ref{ex:v11-repetition} shows.

\subsection{Conditional innovation models}
Let $X$ take values in a standard Borel space $\mathsf X$ and be supplied
\emph{currently} to the encoder, controller, and terminal decision rule at
each of their operations. The distribution of $X$ may depend on the
parameter; $X$ is not a public random seed. The successive innovation
reports belong to standard Borel spaces $\mathsf Z_t$. At every fixed
$x$, all private registers, controls, and terminal action sets are finite.
Let $\nu$ and $\mu_t$ be reference probabilities, and set
$\mu=\bigotimes_{t=1}^T\mu_t$. As in Section~\ref{sec:v10-compact}, for
each open-loop control word $a$, terminal action $b$, and row $j$, define
the nonnegative loss effect $f_{j,a,b}(x,z)$. It is the density with respect
to $\nu\otimes\mu$ of the measure obtained by integrating the bounded
loss against the \emph{actual} open-loop joint law of $(X,Z,W)$.
We require
\begin{equation}
 j\longmapsto f_{j,a,b}\quad\hbox{continuous in }
 L^1(\nu\otimes\mu),\qquad J\hbox{ compact metric}.
 \label{eq:v11-effects}
\end{equation}
The finite collection of these effects includes the initial probabilities
and all parameter-dependent weights. It need not factor in $x,z$, and
there is no hypothesis of domination by a product measure on the full
\emph{displayed} report path $(X,(X,Z_1),\ldots,(X,Z_T))$.

Let $\mathcal K_S$ be the compact metrizable space of private innovation
policies at a fixed value of $x$, using the weak star simplex topology of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v10-compact}. This includes initial-state, controller, and
terminal decision simplices and the common encoder kernels. A private
policy with the displayed covariate is a measurable section
$x\mapsto k_x\in\mathcal K_S$. A public policy is a measurable
$(x,u)\mapsto k_{x,u}$, where $u$ is uniform and independent of the source.
The jointly measurable representatives constructed in the dominated
section turn these sections into actual kernels on $(x,u,z_t)$.

For a fixed $x$, let $R_j(x,k)$ be the finite sum over control, register,
and terminal paths of their policy factors integrated against
$f_{j,a,b}(x,\cdot)$. It is an unnormalized conditional contribution:
\[
 r_j(k_\cdot)=\int_{\mathsf X}R_j(x,k_x)\,\nu(dx).
\]
This notation avoids dividing by a possibly vanishing density of $X$.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional risk integrands]
\label{lem:v11-integrands}
Each $R_j$ represents an element of
$\mathcal B=L^1(\nu;C(\mathcal K_S))$, and the map $j\mapsto R_j$ is
continuous into $\mathcal B$. After choosing measurable versions, the
integrands are measurable in $x$ and continuous in $k$ for almost every
$x$ (for each fixed integrand). Every measurable section of
$\mathcal K_S$ has an executable common-encoder representative.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For almost every $x$, the slices $f_{j,a,b}(x,\cdot)$ are integrable.
The tensor-testing lemma in the dominated section implies continuity in
$k$ of each path integral. Its absolute value is bounded by the sum of
the $L^1(\mu)$ norms of its finitely many sliced effects, multiplied by
the finite number of state paths. This is an integrable envelope.
For two rows, the same estimate applied to the difference gives
\[
 \|R_j-R_{j'}\|_{\mathcal B}
 \le C_S\sum_{a,b}\|f_{j,a,b}-f_{j',a,b}\|_{L^1(\nu\otimes\mu)}.
\]
Measurability first follows on the countable dense family of simple
effects and policies and then by the displayed bound and tensor testing.
Since $C(\mathcal K_S)$ is separable, this gives strong measurability in
$\mathcal B$. Continuity follows from \eqref{eq:v11-effects}.

For executability, use the Borel representative of a weak star simplex
coordinate furnished by successive conditional expectations on finite
partitions, as in the dominated section. This representative is jointly
Borel in the coordinate and the innovation. Composing it with the
measurable map $x\mapsto k_x$, or $(x,u)\mapsto k_{x,u}$, gives measurable
encoder kernels. The finite-dimensional policy coordinates require no
additional choice. A section measurable for the completion of $\nu$ has
a Borel version outside a $\nu$-null set, since the target is standard
Borel; the reference domination makes this replacement harmless for
every parameter. Conversely, a measurable implemented kernel gives a
measurable section by integration against a countable dense family of
$L^1(\mu_t)$ tests. Thus this parametrization neither loses policies nor
adds nonexecutable ones.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact conditional randomization]
\label{lem:v11-young}
The set $\mathcal Y$ of probability kernels
$\Lambda_x(dk)$ from $(\mathsf X,\nu)$ to $\mathcal K_S$, modulo
$\nu$-null sets, is compact metrizable for integration against
$\mathcal B$. Its elements are precisely the positive continuous linear
functionals $L$ on $\mathcal B$ satisfying
\[
 L(g(x)\1(k))=\int g\,d\nu\qquad(g\in L^1(\nu)).
\]
Every such kernel has a realization $k=F(x,U)$ with $F$ measurable and
$U$ uniform independent of the source.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Positivity and normalization imply
$|L(V)|\le\int\|V(x,\cdot)\|_\infty\,\nu(dx)$, so all such functionals
lie in the closed unit ball of $\mathcal B^*$. Their defining equalities
and inequalities are weak star closed. Banach--Alaoglu therefore gives
compactness; separability of $\mathcal B$ gives metrizability on this ball.

For completeness, the representation does not assume a density in the
policy coordinate. For each $\varphi\in C(\mathcal K_S)$, the bounded
functional $g\mapsto L(g\varphi)$ on $L^1(\nu)$ has a Radon--Nikodym
representative $v_\varphi\in L^\infty(\nu)$. Choose these representatives
on a countable dense rational vector lattice of continuous functions
containing constants. Outside a single null set, they are linear,
positive, normalized, and bounded by $\|\varphi\|_\infty$. At each
remaining $x$ extend by continuity to $C(\mathcal K_S)$. The Riesz
representation theorem gives a unique probability $\Lambda_x$.
The integrals of a countable determining family are measurable, hence
$x\mapsto\Lambda_x$ is a measurable probability kernel. Simple
$C(\mathcal K_S)$-valued functions and $L^1$ approximation now prove
$L(V)=\int V(x,k)\Lambda_x(dk)\nu(dx)$ for every $V\in\mathcal B$.

Finally a probability kernel to a standard Borel space is realized by a
measurable function of its input and one independent uniform variable
(the kernel randomization lemma; see \cite{Kallenberg2021}). One may also
construct it by successively splitting $[0,1]$ along refining countable
partitions of the compact policy space. This uses an independent seed;
it does not treat $X$ as independent of the source.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Singular conditional common-encoder duality]
\label{thm:v11-conditional}
Under \eqref{eq:v11-effects} and the displayed-covariate interface, the
ideal-public attainable risk body is the compact convex subset of $C(J)$
\[
 \mathcal A_S=
 \left\{\left(\int R_j(x,k)\Lambda_x(dk)\nu(dx)\right)_{j\in J}:
                  \Lambda\in\mathcal Y\right\}.
\]
Every point is executed with the stated finite private register and one
independent public seed. Its upper set is closed, and, for
$w\in\mathcal P(J)$, its lower support value is
\begin{equation}
 h_S(w)=\int_{\mathsf X}\min_{k\in\mathcal K_S}
          \left\{\int_JR_j(x,k)\,w(dj)\right\}\nu(dx).
 \label{eq:v11-fiber-support}
\end{equation}
In particular
\[
 \min_{r\in\mathcal A_S}\max_{j\in J}r_j
       =\max_{w\in\mathcal P(J)}h_S(w),
\]
and both extrema are attained. For each fixed $w$ the support value is
attained by one measurable private section. Finite-seed approximation and
its state charge are those of Proposition~\ref{prop:v11-seed}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:v11-integrands}, the image of $J$ in $\mathcal B$ is
compact. If $\Lambda^n\to\Lambda$ in the topology of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v11-young}, the risk integrals converge on each fixed
integrand. Approximate the compact set of row integrands by a finite
$\mathcal B$-net and use the uniform norm-one bound on the functionals.
Convergence is then uniform in $j$. Thus the risk map is continuous into
$C(J)$, and its image is compact and convex. The kernel realization and
Lemma~\ref{lem:v11-integrands} prove executability. A sum of a compact set
and the closed positive cone is closed.

The Bochner integral $V_w=\int_JR_j\,w(dj)$ belongs to $\mathcal B$.
It has a Caratheodory version $V_w(x,k)$. The measurable minimum theorem
on a compact metric space gives a measurable minimizing section $k_x$;
more explicitly, its nonempty compact argmin correspondence is measurable,
as can be tested by infima over a countable dense set of policies, and
admits a measurable selector. Its minimum is integrable by the envelope
bound. Any kernel averages values no smaller than that minimum, while
the Dirac kernel at $k_x$ attains it. This proves
\eqref{eq:v11-fiber-support}. The choice depends on $x$ and $w$, not on
the realized task or parameter; all task-dependent controls remain
coupled to a single encoder inside $k_x$.

The payoff $(r,w)\mapsto\int r\,dw$ is continuous and affine on two
compact convex sets. The compact minimax theorem therefore proves the
identity and attainment; equivalently use separation of the compact
upper risk body. The common modulus needed for finite seeds is supplied
by the compact family of integrands and its $\mathcal B$-continuity.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A genuinely non-product report law]
\label{ex:v11-singular}
Take a nonatomic $X$ on $[0,1]$, display it at times zero and one, and
adjoin any finite-horizon innovation model satisfying
\eqref{eq:v11-effects}. The full displayed law is concentrated on the
diagonal $x_0=x_1$. It is not dominated by any product of probability
reference measures on the two display coordinates. Indeed a product
measure charges the diagonal only at common atoms; there are at most
countably many such atoms. Outside those atoms it assigns zero mass to
the diagonal, whereas the nonatomic displayed law assigns mass one.
The same conclusion holds for sigma-finite product references, by
restriction to countably many finite-measure sets. Nevertheless
Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-conditional} applies with a single reference for $X$.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[What has and has not been compactified]
The theorem treats arbitrary measurable dependence on the repeated
covariate, not a finite quantization of it. It does not assert weak
closure of all strategic measures with arbitrary information structures.
Nor does it assert compactness of the entire private risk body in this
conditional model. The conditional Young kernels are exactly the
ideal-public relaxation, and finite retained randomness is charged by
Proposition~\ref{prop:v11-seed}. If the instrument ceases to display $X$,
a policy that still accesses $X$ is no longer admissible. These are
information-interface distinctions, not exceptional null-set conventions.
\end{remark}
