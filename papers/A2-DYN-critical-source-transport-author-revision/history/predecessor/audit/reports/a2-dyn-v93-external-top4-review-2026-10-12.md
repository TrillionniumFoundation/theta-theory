
Writing

```text

```

the historical positive scalar decomposition is

```text
P-G = m^2 b^{epsilon(B)} + e_B,

b^{epsilon(B)} >= 0,

    <= C_M B^(-1/192).
```

Revision 93 addresses that source directly. It neither divides by `P`, nor replaces the source by a bounded reciprocal, nor invokes a finite-norm embedding into `L-infinity`. This is the correct direction after the revision-92 report.

## 6. Audit of module 220: positive-continuant cancellation

The optical recursion is

```text
y_1 = ell_0,
F_0 = 1,
F_i = F_{i-1} + V_i y_i,
y_{i+1} = y_i + ell_i F_i,
V_i = 2/(R c_i),
omega = R c_0 c_m / y_m.
```

All variables in the recurrence are nonnegative. Hence `y_j` is increasing. For an internal contact `1<=i<m`,

```text
y_m >= y_{i+1}
    >= ell_i V_i y_i
    >= 2 ell_i ell_0 /(R c_i)
    >= 2 tau_-^2 /(R c_i).
```

The two displayed consequences are therefore

```text
omega <= R_+/tau_-,

omega/c_i <= R_+^2/(2 tau_-^2).
```

These constants and inequalities are correct. The endpoint incidence factors `c_0` and `c_m` are already present in the numerator of `omega`; an internal incidence factor is cancelled by the lower bound on the positive continuant.

Since each positive depth occurs at most twice,

```text
sum_{i=1}^{m-1} q_0^{d_i} <= 2 q_0/(1-q_0),
```

and therefore

```text
omega I_m
 <= R_+/tau_-
    + [2 q_0/(1-q_0)] R_+^2/(2 tau_-^2).
```

I find no exponent or indexing error here. The estimate is genuinely collision-count independent and does not impose an incidence corridor.

The manuscript also states the correct limitation: this is an estimate for the absolute density. It does not bound `d log omega` near grazing and does not make the logarithmic optical sensitivity uniformly regular.

## 7. Audit of module 220: complete-sheet traces

On one maximal physical segment,

```text
A_sigma = omega/(4 pi c_* chi),

f_sigma^epsilon = A_sigma W^epsilon.
```

The inherited estimates give

```text
|A_sigma'| <= C A_sigma I_m/z,

|(W^epsilon)'| <= C epsilon^(-1)/z.
```

The new cancellation gives uniform bounds for `A_sigma` and `A_sigma I_m`. The product rule therefore yields

```text
|(f_sigma^epsilon)'|
 <= C(1+epsilon^(-1))/z.
```

This passage is correct. In particular the proof does not delete the angular Jacobian `chi`, set an inverse twist equal to one, or replace the physical density by unweighted transverse measure.

Radial growth gives, after sending the earlier roof to the backward endpoint,

```text
z(s)^2 >= 2 lambda_0 (s-a_sigma).
```

Consequently

```text
int_u^v ds/z(s)
 <= sqrt(2/lambda_0)
    [sqrt(v-a_sigma)-sqrt(u-a_sigma)]
 <= C sqrt(v-u).
```

Thus

```text
|f_sigma^epsilon(v)-f_sigma^epsilon(u)|
 <= C(1+epsilon^(-1)) sqrt(v-u).
```

The one-half exponent and constant dependence are correct.

At an endpoint where an internal incidence `c_i` tends to zero, the bound `omega<=C c_i` forces the trace to zero. Endpoint incidences are also killed by the explicit numerator `c_0 c_m`. A backward critical endpoint need not have zero trace; the manuscript retains it.

This is a useful new regularity statement. It is, however, a statement on each physical sheet. It does not control the projected sum over sheets, because no collision-uniform bound for their transverse multiplicity or total entrance capacity is proved.

## 9. Audit of module 221: the original union is an initial prefix

Along a maximal segment:

- the total residence `ell=b_sigma-a_sigma` is constant;
- `z` is strictly increasing in the forward roof coordinate.

Hence the original selector

```text
z<zeta or ell<delta
```

is exactly one initial prefix:

- if `ell<delta`, the entire segment is selected;
- otherwise the selected set is the initial interval on which `z<zeta`;
- the interval may be empty or full.

The Borel endpoint `c_sigma` is therefore well defined. The source

```text
R_c^epsilon(t)
 = int 1_{a_sigma<t<c_sigma} f_sigma^epsilon(t) d sigma
```

is the original weighted union, not the sum of two restrictions which double counts their intersection.

This is an important structural observation. It uses the original source and original coarea kernels; no collar, common-survivor restriction, transported surrogate, or new source normalization is introduced.

## 10. Audit of module 221: exact signed balance

On a single sheet, integration by parts gives

```text
D_t [1_{(a,c)} f]
 = f(a+) delta_a
   + 1_{(a,c)} f' dt
   - f(c-) delta_c.
```

After decomposing `f'` into positive and negative parts and summing the fixed-count finite measures, module 221 obtains

```text
D_t R_c^epsilon
 = I_c^epsilon + G_c^{epsilon,+}
   - D_c^epsilon - G_c^{epsilon,-}.
```

The signs are correct. Every interior prefix cutoff is an upper endpoint and therefore has the negative sign. A prefix cut coinciding with a physical forward end remains negative in the derivative of the roof source. Positive atoms occur only at backward entrances.

The proof also correctly distinguishes:

- the positive measures obtained by splitting each sheet before projection; and
- the Jordan positive part of the projected derivative.

Those objects need not agree, because cancellations may occur after projection. Revision 93 does not claim equality.

The common domination

```text
D_t R_c^epsilon <= I^epsilon + G^{epsilon,+}
```

is therefore a valid sufficient upper bound in the sense of signed measures. It may overcount the true projected positive variation, and the manuscript treats it as an upper mechanism rather than an exact Jordan decomposition.

Grazing entrance traces vanish. Backward critical directional traces remain and may contribute atoms even though the critical set has zero original area. The manuscript's model example, the pushforward of planar area by `F(q)=|q|^2/2`, correctly illustrates this distinction.

## 11. Audit of module 221: target-ancestral pointwise comparison

For a target roof `t`, module 221 keeps only sheets satisfying

```text
a_sigma < t < c_sigma.
```

These are the selected ancestors of the target. The ramp

```text
k_{r,t}(u) = (u-t+r)/r
```

on `[t-r,t]` gives the exact sheet identity

```text
f_sigma(t)
 = r^(-1) int_{t-r}^t 1_{s>a_sigma} f_sigma(s) ds
   + 1_{a_sigma>t-r} k_{r,t}(a_sigma) f_sigma(a_sigma+)
   + int_{max(a_sigma,t-r)}^t
       k_{r,t}(u) f_sigma'(u) du.
```

The formula is correct. It follows by integration by parts of `k f`, with the zero extension to the left of `a_sigma`. The entrance coefficient has the right sign and lies in `[0,1]`.

Summing only over target ancestors is also correct. At an earlier roof `s`, the surviving target ancestors form a subfamily of the complete earlier prefix source. Thus their averaged contribution is bounded by `R_c^epsilon(s)` without adding ended prefixes or later re-entries.

Replacing the final derivative by its positive part gives

```text
R_c^epsilon(t)
 <= r^(-1) int_{t-r}^t R_c^epsilon(s) ds
    + B_{c,r}^epsilon(t)
    + G_{c,r}^{epsilon,+}(t).
```

This is a genuine pointwise inequality at the original roof. The radius `r` is prescribed before the collision limit. No integral in a starting-roof variable appears on the left.

I find this theorem internally consistent, subject to the common-kernel and fixed-count variation inputs.

## 12. Audit of the upper-spike persistence dichotomy

Removing the ramp gives the sharper budgets

```text
B_{c,r}^{epsilon,sharp},
G_{c,r}^{epsilon,+,sharp}.
```

For almost every `s in (t-r,t)`, the sheetwise fundamental theorem yields

```text
R_c^epsilon(s)
 >= R_c^epsilon(t)
    - B_{c,r}^{epsilon,sharp}(t)
    - G_{c,r}^{epsilon,+,sharp}(t).
```

The inequality is correct, including sheets born between `s` and `t`: their entire target contribution is paid by the entrance trace plus subsequent positive growth. Negative growth can only improve the lower bound at the earlier roof.

Therefore, if

```text
m^2 R_c^epsilon(t) >= lambda
```

and the complete ancestral positive budget is at most `lambda/2`, then

```text
m^2 R_c^epsilon(s) >= lambda/2
```

for almost every `s in (t-r,t)`.

This is stronger than an integrated crossing estimate. It gives persistence on the full prescribed interval.

It is nevertheless a conditional dichotomy. The revision does not prove that the ancestral positive budget is small at every potential spike roof. A spike may still be created by a concentration of entrance traces or critical positive growth. Thus request 18.1 of the preceding report is advanced substantially but not fully closed.

## 13. Audit of module 222: complete noncritical positive gain

Before applying the absolute sheet estimate, module 222 keeps the physical density in the product rule:

```text
[(f_sigma^epsilon)'(u)]_+
 <= C(1+epsilon^(-1))
    A_sigma(u) I_m(u)/z(u).
```

This inequality is valid. The `A'` term has the inherited bound `C A I_m/z`; the guard term is bounded by `C epsilon^(-1) A/z`, and `I_m>=1`.

On the region `z>=rho`, target ancestors form a Borel subfamily of the complete exact fiber. The maximal-segment parametrization counts each intermediate physical state once. Therefore

```text
G_{c,r}^{epsilon,>=rho,sharp}(t)
 <= C(1+epsilon^(-1))
    int_{t-r}^t v^rho(u) du,
```

where

```text
v^rho du
 = F_#(I_m z^(-1) 1_{z>=rho} d bar nu).
```

No word-count factor is introduced, and no surface trace is equated to unweighted volume flux.

The retained positive-cutoff theorem gives, for every fixed `rho,r>0`,

```text
limsup_m sup_{R,n,k}
sup_{|J|=r} m^2 int_J v^rho
 <= C_0 r,
```

with a leading constant independent of the previously fixed positive `rho`. Hence

```text
limsup_m sup_{R,n,k,c}
m^2 ||G_{c,r}^{epsilon,>=rho,sharp}||_infinity
 <= C(1+epsilon^(-1)) r.
```

This deduction is correct. The finite-count remainder of the cutoff theorem contains a negative power of `rho`, so `rho` must remain fixed before the collision limit. Revision 93 respects that order and does not set `rho=rho(m)`.

The theorem does not hold at `rho=0` on the basis of the cited input. The critical part is retained separately, as it must be.

## 15. What the two remaining forcing terms contain

The first term,

```text

```

contains weighted target-ancestral entrance traces born within the prescribed backward window. It may include:

- noncritical true physical entrances;
- section-decision entrances;
- competing-hit and clearance entrances;
- endpoint grazing entrances, whose trace vanishes;
- internal grazing entrances, whose trace vanishes; and
- backward critical directional entrances, whose trace need not vanish.

The second term,

```text

```

is the cofinal collision-normalized essential height of positive density growth at intermediate states with `z<rho`, after the collision limit and essential supremum are taken.

Neither term is controlled by the single-sheet one-half modulus alone. A large number or large weighted capacity of sheets can concentrate at one roof. Fixed-count definable finiteness gives existence of the sums, but no normalized estimate.

Their decay is therefore a sufficient condition which may be stronger than the original pointwise theorem. That is not a logical error, but it means the new reduction is not yet an equivalence comparable to the earlier exact original-source charge criterion.

A successful next revision must either prove decay of these stronger positive quantities or recover enough signed cancellation to formulate a smaller, still pointwise-controlling forcing measure.

## 19. What a successful next revision must prove

The next revision should remain focused on the two positive forcing mechanisms. Further reciprocal, posterior, entropy, or finite-`p` consequences would not close the scalar theorem.

### 19.1. A quantitative ancestral entrance theorem

The manuscript needs an estimate of the form

```text

 <= C_M epsilon^(-q) r^beta
```

for some finite `q` and positive `beta`, or another bound which vanishes along a legal outer choice `r=r(B)`.

The proof should decompose entrance types rather than appeal to a total component count. At minimum it should treat separately:

- nongrazing physical and section-decision entrances;
- competing-hit and clearance entrances;
