## 9.2 — Direct support recovery and the new scalar output

Section 5.1 expressly states that the prior exact spatial first-impact support gives the relevant physical boundary union. It is not presented as an indirect travel-time or spectral inverse.

The new observation instead records only whether an attempted free launch collides by one fixed time; failed solid starts and misses both contribute zero. For displacement a, the pointwise identity is

`B_a(q) - B_-a(q+a) = chi(q+a) - chi(q)`.

Integrating against a commanded density f gives the difference of two observable probabilities, the reverse command being f shifted by +a. A segment entering and leaving a body between two free endpoints contributes one in both directions and cancels. The identity remains true for multiple segment intersections and does not take a small-time derivative.

With separated bounded components, every sufficiently long but finite t-spaced chain contains a free point. Theorem 1.2 recovers chi as the negative minimum of its measured partial difference sums, without knowing which point is free. Lemma 1.3 extends the zero witness to mollified occupation, so finite scalar means reconstruct occupation averages with scale-independent error. Theorem 4.1 then propagates a finite grid to complete bodies and the full period lattice.

The preparation contract is conspicuous in the abstract, theorem and resource remark: spatial masks and collimated opposed directions are actively controlled. We do not identify this with uniformly prepared count-only data or claim it resolves the previously isolated analytic count-germ question. The new theorem removes output localization while retaining and quantifying input localization; it is not a claim of universally weaker total information.
