\begin{abstract}
We study marked causal experiments with a fixed persistent register and
separately charged hidden or visible randomization. We characterize finite
registers by compatible continuation polytopes and determine an exact
memory--error law at an erasure cut. On the original nonconvex behavior image,
a finite reset-audit game has an attained minimax representation and error
at most $\sqrt{D/n}$, where $D$ counts independent observable probabilities.
A binary family gives a matching uniform sampling exponent. We then prove
transport of these certificates together with likelihoods, posterior
processes, and bounded entropic backward equations under Gaussian observation
of changing microscopic trajectories. For two hard spheres this yields one
explicit finite-data certificate, uniform on a contact-diameter interval,
separating private and visible width one from a hidden two-valued selector.
The collision creates the observed signal from a prepared impact variable;
removing the collision makes all three deficiencies zero. Varying the
contact diameter changes the collision time and the microscopic path, while
the certified resource gap and the conditional-law consequences persist.
The effective constructions retain the original resource constraints.
\end{abstract}
