\section{The canonical theorem chain}
\label{sec:v18-dependencies}
The diagram records dependencies within this article. A solid arrow means
that the conclusion at its tail is used in a proof at its head. The
continuation characterization identifies the original finite-resource
object; the exact cut theorem supplies its independent sharp obstruction.
The two testing constructions have different information conventions.

In detail, the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-microscopic}(b) uses the
audit theorem and the exact marked gap, while part (c) uses the
moving-collision lemma, the TV-to-posterior estimate, the likelihood theorem
and its conditional consumers. Part (d) consumes the chart and physical
effective-presentation constructions. No testing theorem is an input to
the elementary elastic-scattering calculation.

The comparison theorem, strict polynomial testing, revealed-behavior
hierarchy, exact marked gap, scattering bounds, and common-latent
TV-to-posterior estimate are inherited mathematical inputs with complete
proofs reproduced here. The continuation normalization, exact cut spectrum,
reset-audit game and lower bound, formal chart algorithm, changing-trajectory
likelihood estimates, entropic consumer, and uniform moving-collision
composition are additions of the present revision. The positive-cone,
minimax, two-point testing, polynomial positivity, and stochastic-calculus
mechanisms are classical inputs, not priority claims.

The parameter and the actual mark remain unavailable to the online
simulator. The reset auditor sees completed marked trials through its
separate interface. A revealed-behavior test sees an exact proposed law.
The maximal coupling of observation paths may use their entire future and
is not an executable transducer. These three information privileges are
never substituted for one another.
