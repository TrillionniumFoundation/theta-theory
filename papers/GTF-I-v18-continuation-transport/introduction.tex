\section{Introduction}
A causal reduction consumes observations, emits reports, and carries some
information into the future. The size and visibility of that carried
information are mathematical data. In particular, a persistent hidden
selector is not equivalent to fresh private randomization, and revealing
the selector can change a comparison even when the retained register is
unchanged. A theory of reduction must therefore compare marked laws before
convexifying its feasible set.

There are two further requirements. A separating test should specify what
it observes about a proposed reduction; an exact probability law is a much
stronger observation than finitely many independent runs. Moreover, a
certificate obtained in a microscopic model should survive an approximation
of the dynamics, not only an approximation of a formula written after the
dynamics have been fixed. This paper joins these requirements.

Our basic object is the resource spectrum of a marked causal experiment.
A point of that spectrum records a register-width profile, an error, and,
where applicable, a hidden or visible selector alphabet. The mark is an
actual system variable. It is not available to the online encoder. The
comparison tests include its terminal value. Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-main}
gives the elementary compactness, factorization and costed composition
properties. The subsequent argument does not replace the private spectrum
by its convex hull.

\subsection{The principal mechanism}
Let $B$ be the compact behavior image of a fixed resource signature. A
behavior $b=(b_j)_{j=1}^J$ lists the laws of finitely many marked causal
transcripts. Fix target laws $Q_j$ and put
\[
 F(b)=\max_{j,A}\{Q_j(A)-b_j(A)\},\qquad
 \delta(B,Q)=\min_{b\in B}F(b).
\]
Complements of events supply both signs. An auditor is allowed $n$
independent, reset runs of each row, followed by independent validation.
The candidate is held fixed during the whole audit, but its realized
private randomness is reset between runs. The auditor sees completed marked
transcripts, not the candidate's probabilities or its retained states.
This information pattern is different both from an ordinary single-run
comparison and from a revealed-law verification game.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-resource certification and microscopic transport]
\label{thm:v18-spine}
The following assertions hold with the conventions just described.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(i)} Finite causal kernels have a register realization with a
prescribed width profile exactly when their normalized continuation laws
admit compatible finitely generated polytopes of those widths. At an
erasure cut carrying a latent letter of law $\pi$, the optimal error with
$K$ retained states is
\[
 1-\sum_{i=1}^{\min(K,N)}\pi_{(i)},
 \qquad \pi_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge\pi_{(N)}.
\]
Independent hidden or visible selectors do not improve this cut value.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(ii)} The reset-audit value is attained, has a finite-dimensional
moment minimax representation on the original $B$, and satisfies
\[
 0\le\delta(B,Q)-\vaudit_n(B,Q)\le\sqrt{D/n},
 \qquad D=\sum_j(|\Omega_j|-1).
\]
The exponent $1/2$ cannot be improved uniformly over compact candidate
classes. This assertion is about reset samples, not about the minimal
degree of unrestricted polynomial tests.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(iii)} Suppose two prepared microscopic observation models have a
common preparation variable, bounded signals $H,H'$ on $[0,T]$, and the
same positive Gaussian noise level $\sigma$. Set
\[
 \eta^2=\E\int_0^T|H-H'|^2dt,\qquad
 \varepsilon=\min\{1,\eta/(2\sigma)\}.
\]
Their joint marked observation laws differ by at most $\varepsilon$.
This estimate transports resource spectra at their stated budgets and
transports each fixed finite audit with its explicitly charged reset cost.
It also gives whole-process posterior convergence. The unprojected and
projected likelihoods converge in the supremum $L^2$ norm under the common
Wiener reference when $\eta\to0$. For bounded latent payoffs, the associated
entropic backward equations converge in value, and their integrands
converge after the stated denominator localization.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(iv)} In the two-sphere model of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v18-microscopic}, one rationally specified audit with
$2{,}800$ training runs has expected discrepancy strictly greater than
$2/5$ against every private width-one simulator, uniformly for
$9/10\le d\le11/10$ and $0<\sigma\le1/24$. A hidden two-valued selector
has error less than $13/100$; every visible-selector width-one error exceeds
$9/20$. For $d_n\to d$ the microscopic paths, likelihoods and posterior
processes converge in the precise senses of that theorem, and the same
audit remains valid. For $d\le2/5$ all three deficiencies vanish.
\end{theorem}

Part (iv) is a composition, not a juxtaposition: the scattering calculation
provides a marked gap, the reset-audit theorem turns it into one finite-data
certificate on the nonconvex private image, and microscopic transport
carries this certificate together with a conditional-law consumer through
a changing collision geometry. The proofs are given in
Theorems~\ref{thm:v18-continuation}, \ref{thm:v18-cut},
\ref{thm:v18-audit}, \ref{thm:v18-auditlower},
\ref{thm:v18-gaussian}, \ref{thm:v18-entropic}, and
\ref{thm:v18-microscopic}.

\subsection{What the information restrictions do}
The continuation criterion is a geometric realization criterion, not a
claim that ordinary Hankel rank equals nonnegative state complexity. Its
generators must be stable under all one-step input--output shifts. The
cut theorem gives a sharp obstruction even when all other checkpoints
have unrestricted memory. In particular, $m$ erased unbiased bits require
at least $(1-\epsilon)2^m$ retained states to attain error $\epsilon$.

The reset-audit dual randomizes over a candidate chosen once for an entire
audit. Its moment measure cannot be replaced by the barycenter behavior:
the replicated samples are conditionally independent given that candidate,
not after forgetting it. This is why minimax on the auditor's finite
coefficient simplex does not supply free persistent randomness to the
simulator. The auditor's count register, within-run buffer, validation
choice, and reset interface are all explicit. No sample-complexity statement
here is a polynomial-time statement about minimizing on a nonconvex image.

We also retain revealed-behavior testing and its behavior-dimensional
Bernstein bound. The infinite-dimensional representation is a general
partition-of-unity identity. Its finite polynomial hierarchy and its small
exact nonlinear witness remain useful, but they have different information
requirements from the reset audit. Proposition~\ref{prop:v18-chart} gives
an exact input--output algorithm for the rational observable quotient and
chart used by that hierarchy.

\subsection{Relation to earlier comparison and realization theory}
Blackwell and Le Cam supply the classical comparison viewpoint
\cite{Blackwell1953,LeCam1964,Torgersen1991}. Filtered comparison has a
substantial earlier literature, including Norberg and Weisshaupt
\cite{Norberg2002,Weisshaupt2006}. We do not assert that inserting a
filtration into an experiment is new. The extra data here are fixed
persistent width, the visibility of separately charged selectors, and the
finite reset interface of the testing problem. An original-text
proof-by-proof crosswalk with Norberg is not established in this revision;
no claim of priority over an uninspected theorem is used in a proof.

Positive realization by stable finitely generated cones is classical.
Fliess's Theorem 3.1 and the stochastic-process specialization in
Theorem 5.4 and its second corollary make this particularly clear
\cite{Fliess1975}. Our continuation statement is a finite-horizon,
input-indexed normalization of that mechanism, with a width at each cut.
The new use is its connection to the marked resource spectrum and to an
exact cut obstruction; the existence of a positive-cone realization is
not claimed as a discovery.

The minimax exchange is classical \cite{Sion1958}. The statistical lower
bound is a two-point testing argument, and the empirical upper bound uses
multinomial second moments. Bernstein and positivity methods are also
classical \cite{deKlerkLaurent2010,LaurentSlot2023,deKlerkVera2026}.
What is proved here is the compatibility of these constructions with the
unconvexified resource object, including their information costs and their
transport through an interacting microscopic limit. Bayes' formula,
Gaussian change of measure, martingale representation, and maximal
inequalities are the analytic inputs to the transport step; the
moving-collision estimate verifies that step without replacing the
microscopic trajectory by a fixed flow.

\subsection{Scope and organization}
The physical theorem is at fixed particle number and fixed positive noise.
The $B$--$W$ correlation belongs to the preparation. Collision transduces
$B$ into the measured transverse signal; it does not create that correlation.
The feedback is an acquisition gate, not a mechanical force. Changing the
contact diameter changes a genuine microscopic path, but is not a
Boltzmann--Grad or particle-number limit.

The foundations developed here remain applicable to the larger dynamical
program. A concrete downstream consequence is the bounded Gaussian
changing-path likelihood, posterior and entropic-value theorem proved in
Section~\ref{sec:v18-transport}; it replaces a finite-dimensional-to-tightness
shortcut by uniform estimates. Algebraic geometry, kinetic action
compactness and particle-uniform hierarchy recovery retain their own
hypotheses. No independence of an algebraic-geometric theorem is converted
into a dependency merely by the order of papers.

The canonical proof chain follows the diagram in
Appendix~\ref{sec:v18-dependencies}. The full preceding comparison,
physical-domain, task, and historical development is preserved in the
companion volume. It is not necessary to reconstruct that archive to read
the proofs in this article.
