\begin{abstract}
We extend the fully observed collision game of Paper III in two independent
directions: partial observation of a noncompact hidden coordinate and
pure-strategy Isaacs structure on noncompact control sets.  These extensions
are downstream of the base physical-time HJB theorem and are not required for
its validity.

The hidden state is a refresh--autoregressive Markov process realized by a
deterministic product shift.  A common refresh component gives strict
total-variation contraction, the autoregressive component gives a quadratic
Lyapunov drift, and bounded nondegenerate observations preserve an explicit
posterior moment ball.  Sufficient prediction time between observations makes
the nonlinear filter contract uniformly over strategy trees.  A vanishing
initial layer removes the initial belief from the macroscopic value.

We also prove an information-order no-go theorem: exact pre-action observation
of a nonzero fast payoff cannot in general collapse to the fully observed-base
Hamiltonian with the fast variable removed.  The positive soft-observation
theorem and the exact-observation obstruction are therefore stated as distinct
branches.

For pure games, quadratic actuator energies compensate adverse control
curvature.  The resulting saddle variational inequality is strongly monotone,
has a unique global pure saddle, and admits a Lipschitz selector on closed
convex, possibly noncompact control sets.  Coupled with the collision
coefficients of Paper III, this yields a partially observed physical-time
Isaacs limit on the product platform
\texttt{OB3-MG-v1}$\times$\texttt{AR-FILTER-v1}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Paper III already gives an exact microscopic DPP and physical-time HJB when
the slow state and current obstacle label are observed.  This paper does not
re-prove that base theorem.  It asks what changes when an additional hidden
state affects drift, reward, or observation, and when the local lower and
upper game operators must be shown to have a common pure saddle.

Filtering stability and Isaacs equality are separate questions.  A filter can
be stable while a pure game has no value; a game can have a value while exact
observations prevent belief collapse.  We therefore keep the information and
game branches typed separately.

We use standard nonlinear filtering references
\cite{BainCrisan2009,AtarZeitouni1997,vanHandel2009}, stochastic-game
viscosity theory \cite{FlemingSouganidis1989,FlemingSoner2006}, and monotone
operator methods.

\section{The product platform}\label{sec:platform}

Let $(I_k)$ be the obstacle-label process of Papers II--III.  Independently,
let $(Y_k)$ be a hidden Markov state with kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 P_Y(y,\cdot)
 =
 \delta\gamma+(1-\delta)N(ry,1-r^2),
 \qquad
 0<\delta<1,\quad \abs r<1,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma=N(0,1)$.  The kernel is realized by a deterministic shift with
a Bernoulli refresh coordinate and a Gaussian innovation coordinate.  The
full fast platform is the deterministic product of that shift with the coded
open billiard.

Let $W(y)=1+y^2$.  The observation alphabet is $\{-1,1\}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:likelihood}
 g_o(y)=\frac{1+o\epsilon_o\tanh y}{2},
 \qquad0<\epsilon_o<1.
\end{equation}
Thus
\[
 g_-=rac{1-\epsilon_o}{2},\qquad
 g_+=\frac{1+\epsilon_o}{2}.
\]

\section{Prediction and Bayes operators}\label{sec:filter}

For a belief $\pi$, define
\[
 \mathsf{Pred}(\pi)=\pi P_Y,\qquad
 \mathsf B_o(\pi)(dy)
 =
 \frac{g_o(y)\pi(dy)}{\pi(g_o)}.
\]
Use
\[
 \norm{\mu-\nu}_{\rm TV}
 =\sup_{\abs f\le1}\abs{\mu(f)-\nu(f)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Bayes expansion]\label{lem:bayes}
If $0<g_-\le g\le g_+$, then
\[
 \norm{\mathsf B_g(\mu)-\mathsf B_g(\nu)}_{\rm TV}
 \le
 C_B\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\rm TV},
 \qquad
 C_B=\frac{2g_+}{g_-}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\abs f\le1$, add and subtract $\nu(gf)/\mu(g)$.  Bound the numerator
differences by $g_+\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\rm TV}$ and both denominators by $g_-$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Refresh contraction and drift]\label{lem:refresh}
For all probability measures $\mu,\nu$,
\[
 \norm{\mu P_Y-\nu P_Y}_{\rm TV}
 \le(1-\delta)\norm{\mu-\nu}_{\rm TV}.
\]
With $\alpha=(1-\delta)r^2<1$,
\[
 P_YW(y)=\alpha W(y)+2(1-\alpha).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The common refresh part cancels in the signed difference.  The remaining
kernel is a contraction.  The second identity follows from the conditional
second moment of \eqref{eq:kernel}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Noncompact soft-filter contraction]\label{thm:filter}
Choose an integer $m_*$ such that
\[
 q=C_B(1-\delta)^{m_*}<1,\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{g_+}{g_-}\alpha^{m_*}<1.
\]
Then one block of $m_*$ predictions followed by one observation:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item contracts total variation by $q$;
\item preserves every moment ball $\{\pi:\pi(W)\le R\}$ with
\[
 R\ge\frac{2g_+/g_-}{1-\kappa};
\]
\item contracts uniformly along every common observation and strategy tree;
\item remains stable under compact slow/control perturbations preserving the
two strict inequalities.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:refresh} $m_*$ times and then
\cref{lem:bayes}.  The drift iteration gives
\[
 P_Y^{m_*}W\le\alpha^{m_*}W+2.
\]
A Bayes update multiplies the moment by at most $g_+/g_-$.  This gives the
affine moment recursion $\kappa M+2g_+/g_-$ and the stated invariant ball.
\end{proof}

\section{Initial-layer belief collapse}\label{sec:initial-layer}

Let $\ell_\varepsilon$ filter blocks occur before the first macroscopic reward,
during physical time $\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}$.

\begin{theorem}[Value-level prior removal]\label{thm:prior}
Assume
\[
 \ell_\varepsilon\to\infty,\qquad
 \delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}\to0,
\]
and that the running reward in the initial layer is bounded by
$C\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}$.  Then values started from beliefs
$\pi,\pi'$ in the invariant moment ball satisfy
\[
 \abs{V^\varepsilon(\pi)-V^\varepsilon(\pi')}
 \le
 C_Tq^{\ell_\varepsilon}
 \norm{\pi-\pi'}_{\rm TV}
 +C\delta_\varepsilon^{\rm init}
 +\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon.
\]
Here $\operatorname{Err}_\varepsilon$ is the geometric convolution of the
slow-freezing moduli.  If it vanishes, the macroscopic value is independent of
the initial hidden belief.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Couple both filters along the same observations and strategy tree.
\cref{thm:filter} gives the first term at the first macroscopic reward.
Only the initial layer sees the uncontracted prior.  Subsequent slow
perturbations satisfy an affine recursion with factor $q$, whose iteration is
the displayed convolution.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact observation is a different problem}\label{sec:no-go}

The next theorem prevents an illegitimate upgrade from soft observations to
exact pre-action information.

\begin{theorem}[Exact-observation no-go]\label{thm:exact-no-go}
Let a centered nonzero fast payoff $g$ be revealed exactly before the control
is chosen, and let the control set contain $\{-1,1\}$.  For reward
$r(u,z)=ug(z)$,
\[
 \int\sup_{u\in\{-1,1\}}r(u,z)\,d\mu(z)
 =
 \int\abs{g(z)}\,d\mu(z)>0,
\]
whereas
\[
 \sup_{u\in\{-1,1\}}\int r(u,z)\,d\mu(z)=0.
\]
Therefore a high-rate exact pre-action observation cannot, without an
additional sufficient statistic, collapse to the blind macroscopic
Hamiltonian of the unobserved model.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first controller chooses $u(z)=\operatorname{sign}g(z)$.  A controller
who acts before seeing $z$ receives $u\int g\,d\mu=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem does not exclude an exact belief-state DPP.  It excludes silently
reusing the soft-filter collapse theorem in an exact-information model.
\end{remark}

\section{Curvature compensation}\label{sec:curvature}

Let $U\subset\R^m$ and $V\subset\R^n$ be closed convex sets.  Let
$F_0(z,u,v)$ be $C^2$ in $(u,v)$ with
\[
 D_{uu}^2F_0\le L_uI,\qquad
 D_{vv}^2F_0\ge-L_vI.
\]
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:payoff}
 F(z,u,v)
 =
 F_0(z,u,v)-\frac\mu2\abs u^2+\frac\nu2\abs v^2,
 \qquad \mu>L_u,\quad\nu>L_v.
\end{equation}
Let
\[
 G_z(u,v)=(-D_uF(z,u,v),D_vF(z,u,v)).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Pure-saddle theorem]\label{thm:saddle}
Assume $G_z+N_{U\times V}$ is coercive.  Then it is strongly maximal monotone
with constant
\[
 \lambda=\min\{\mu-L_u,\nu-L_v\}>0.
\]
There is a unique solution $(u_*(z),v_*(z))$ of
\[
 0\in G_z(u,v)+N_{U\times V}(u,v),
\]
and it is the unique global pure saddle:
\[
 F(z,u,v_*)\le F(z,u_*,v_*)\le F(z,u_*,v).
\]
If $G_z$ is Lipschitz in $z$, the selector is Lipschitz.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The symmetric quadratic form of $DG_z$ in an increment
$(\dot u,\dot v)$ is
\[
 -\dot u^TD_{uu}^2F\dot u+\dot v^TD_{vv}^2F\dot v.
\]
The mixed Hessian terms cancel.  Equation \eqref{eq:payoff} bounds this below
by $\lambda(\abs{\dot u}^2+\abs{\dot v}^2)$.  Strong monotonicity, maximality
of the normal cone, and coercivity give existence and uniqueness.  The
variational inequalities plus strong concavity/convexity give the saddle
inequalities.  Comparing two inclusions gives Lipschitz dependence.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Energy-generated nonconvexity]\label{ex:energy}
For
\[
 F(p;u,v)
 =
 p(u+v)-\frac\mu2u^2+\frac\nu2v^2
\]
on $\R^2$, the saddle is
\[
 u_*=p/\mu,\qquad v_*=-p/\nu,
\]
and
\[
 H(p)=\frac12(\mu^{-1}-\nu^{-1})p^2.
\]
It is concave when $\mu>\nu$.
\end{example}

\section{Belief-state collision DPP}\label{sec:belief-dpp}

Let the slow state be $x$, current obstacle label $i$, and hidden belief
$\pi$.  Over one decision block, choose $(u,v)$, evolve the open-billiard
collision state and the hidden prediction, observe $o$, and update by Bayes.
The exact lower recursion is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal V_k^\varepsilon(x,i,\pi)
 =
 \max_u\min_v
 \E\Big[
 &\varepsilon^2 c_i^{uv}(x,Y_k)\\
 &+\mathcal V_{k+1}^\varepsilon
 (X_{k+1},I_{k+1},\mathsf B_o(\pi P_Y^{m_*}))
 \Big].
\end{aligned}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exactness and monotonicity]\label{prop:belief-dpp}
The recursion is the exact finite-horizon DPP on the sufficient state
$(x,i,\pi)$.  It is monotone and stable on the invariant moment ball.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Condition successively on actions, hidden prediction, observation, and the
next collision label.  The Bayes posterior is the conditional law of the
hidden state, so $(x,i,\pi)$ is Markov.  Positivity of expectation and
max--min preserve order; bounded rewards give stability.
\end{proof}

\section{The partially observed Isaacs limit}\label{sec:limit}

Assume the slow coefficients obtained by averaging over the posterior are
uniformly Lipschitz on the invariant moment ball and that the base diffusion
coefficient is the physical coefficient of Paper III:
\[
 D_{\rm HJB}(a)=\frac1{14\bar\tau_a}I_2.
\]
Assume the local game payoff satisfies \cref{thm:saddle} uniformly.

\begin{theorem}[Filtered physical-time Isaacs limit]\label{thm:filtered-limit}
Under the filter gates of \cref{thm:filter}, the initial-layer scaling of
\cref{thm:prior}, and the uniform pure-saddle conditions, the lower and upper
belief-state schemes converge to the same viscosity solution.  After the
initial layer the limiting value is independent of the initial belief, while
the effective coefficients retain the declared posterior-averaged information
structure.  The local lower and upper Hamiltonians agree and are attained by
the unique pure Lipschitz saddle selector.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The filter contraction gives compactness in the value-relevant belief
direction and removes the initial prior.  The base collision scheme supplies
monotonicity, physical clock, and uniform ellipticity.  The saddle theorem
identifies the lower and upper local operators and provides continuous
selectors.  Perturbed-test consistency and viscosity comparison then give the
common limit.
\end{proof}
