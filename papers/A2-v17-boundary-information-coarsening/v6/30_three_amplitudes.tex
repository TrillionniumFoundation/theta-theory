\section{Three amplitudes in the constrained physical family}
\label{sec:v6-three}
The four-amplitude theorem permits an independent area normalizer. The
physical family \eqref{eq:g-isogap-family} has a constraint on that
normalizer, and this constraint can reduce the required number of
amplitudes. We prove the reduction near $R=1/4$. The four-amplitude
result remains available on its full positive parameter range and
with an independent area.

Put $x_i=r_i-R$ for the three contact radii and let $e_k$ be their
elementary symmetric functions. The three values in
\eqref{eq:g-contact-curvatures} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v6-area-coordinates}
 \alpha=\frac{e_1}{108},\qquad
 \beta^2+\zeta^2=\frac{e_1^2}{2916}-\frac{e_2}{972}.
\end{equation}
Substituting in \eqref{eq:v3-area} yields the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v6-area-constraint}
 A(R,e)=A_0(R)-\frac{\pi R}{54}e_1
             +\frac{41\pi}{7776}e_1^2-\frac{5\pi}{432}e_2,
 \qquad A_0(R)=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}-\pi R^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the sum of the three $x_i$ is $108\alpha$, while their squared
sum is $3888\alpha^2+1944(\beta^2+\zeta^2)$. The identity
$2e_2=e_1^2-\sum_i x_i^2$ proves \eqref{eq:v6-area-coordinates}, and
collecting its terms in the area formula proves
\eqref{eq:v6-area-constraint}.

The contact radii in this physical family are actual radii at both ends
of each channel: central symmetry makes the two facing curvatures equal.
This is additional structure not required by the general forward law.
Without equality of the facing curvatures, the scalar count coefficient
retains only
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v6-effective-curvature}
 \kappa_e^{\mathrm{eff}}=
 \frac{\sqrt{(1+g\kappa_{e,0})(1+g\kappa_{e,1})}-1}{g}.
\end{equation}
The ambient scalar inverse then recovers these effective parameters,
not the two actual endpoint curvatures. The conditional variances in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-tomography} recover the latter without this symmetry.

\begin{theorem}[Physical three-amplitude reconstruction]
\label{thm:v6-three}
There is a neighborhood of $R=1/4$, $\alpha=\beta=\zeta=0$ in
\eqref{eq:g-isogap-family} such that the spacing $g=1-2R$ and the
first three unlabelled leading amplitudes determine the unordered
contact-curvature triple, including multiplicities, and the area.
For two members of a sufficiently small such neighborhood, put
\[
 \Delta=|g-\widetilde g|+
                   \max_{1\le j\le3}|C_j-\widetilde C_j|.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v6-three-bound}
 |R-\widetilde R|+|A-\widetilde A|
       +\sum_{k=1}^3|e_k-\widetilde e_k|\le C\Delta,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{dist}_{\rm match}(\kappa,\widetilde\kappa)
                                      \le C\Delta^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
The pairwise exponent $1/3$ is sharp even at fixed $R=1/4$ and known
$g$. Thus no fourth amplitude is required in this constrained family
near that reference point. The assertion does not impose a minimum
separation between distinct curvatures.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $R$, set $g=1-2R$, and use the analytic contour sums
$M_j(R,e)$ of \eqref{eq:v5-contour}. They depend analytically also on
$R$ in a small real neighborhood of $1/4$, with a common complex
contour and branches. On a full coefficient neighborhood, define
\[
 \Psi_3(R,e)=\left(\frac{M_j(R,e)}{A(R,e)}\right)_{j=1}^3.
\]
The area stays positive there. At $e=0$, the derivative with respect
to the three coefficient coordinates has rows
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v6-three-jacobian}
 \frac1{A_0}\left(
 \Phi_j'(R)+\frac{\pi R}{18A_0}\Phi_j(R),\quad
 -\Phi_j''(R)+\frac{5\pi}{144A_0}\Phi_j(R),\quad
 \frac12\Phi_j'''(R)\right).
\end{equation}
Derivatives on $\Phi_j$ here keep $g$ fixed. The area terms follow
from $M_j(R,0)=3\Phi_j(R)$ and
$A_{e_1}=-\pi R/54$, $A_{e_2}=-5\pi/432$, $A_{e_3}=0$.

We verify nondegeneracy without assuming it numerically. At $R=1/4$
and $g=1/2$, the entries $\Phi_j^{(k)}(R)/\sqrt2$ are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 j&k=0&k=1&k=2&k=3\\ \hline
 1&1/4&3/4&-5/4&21/4\\
 2&1/24&17/72&35/216&-491/216\\
 3&1/140&297/4900&36243/171500&-4458537/6002500
 \end{array}
\]
These values follow by differentiating
$\Phi_1(r)=r/\sqrt{g(g+2r)}$,
$\Phi_2=\Phi_1/[2(1+g/r)]$ and
$\Phi_3=\Phi_1/[4(1+g/r)^2-1]$.
Substitution into \eqref{eq:v6-three-jacobian} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v6-three-determinant}
 \det D_e\Psi_3(1/4,0)=
 -\frac{2\sqrt2(15804720 A_0+64253\pi)}{72930375 A_0^4}\ne0,
 \qquad A_0=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}-\frac{\pi}{16}>0.
\end{equation}
To include changes in the spacing, apply the analytic inverse-function
theorem to
\[
 (R,e)\longmapsto(1-2R,\Psi_3(R,e)).
\]
Its first row is $(-2,0,0,0)$, and the determinant in the other three
columns is \eqref{eq:v6-three-determinant}. Hence it is locally
invertible in a full four-dimensional coefficient neighborhood, before
restriction to real roots. Choose a convex data ball on which the
inverse has bounded derivative and then a smaller parameter neighborhood
whose image lies in that ball. This gives the coefficient and $R$
estimates in \eqref{eq:v6-three-bound}. Analyticity of
\eqref{eq:v6-area-constraint} gives the area estimate.

The root-matching proof in Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-pairwise} gives
$\operatorname{dist}_{\rm match}(x,\widetilde x)
\le C\Delta^{1/3}$. Adding the change of $R$ and applying the
Lipschitz map $r\mapsto r^{-1}$ on a compact positive interval proves
the curvature estimate, after decreasing the neighborhood so
$\Delta\le1$. This retains all repeated-root strata. On the physical
path $\alpha=\zeta=0$, $\beta=s$, compared with $-s$, the gap stays
fixed and Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-sharpness} gives amplitude discrepancy
$O(|s|^3)$ and matching curvature distance comparable to $|s|$.
The nonzero first-amplitude cubic coefficient gives the reverse
amplitude bound for the first three amplitudes as well. An exponent
larger than $1/3$ would contradict this path.
\end{proof}

With deterministic error at most $\delta$ in the observed spacing and
these three amplitudes, choose an admissible member minimizing their
maximum discrepancy on a sufficiently small compact physical parameter
set containing the truth. Its exact data differ from the true data by
at most $2\delta$. The theorem therefore gives area error $C\delta$
and matching curvature error $C\delta^{1/3}$. This existence argument
neither requires a unique minimizer nor asserts a particular efficient
optimization algorithm. More importantly, it concerns errors in
amplitudes, not yet errors in individual collision counts. We next
account for the experiment that estimates those amplitudes.
