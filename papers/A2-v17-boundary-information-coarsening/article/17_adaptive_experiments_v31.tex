\section{Adaptive observation and stopped experiments}
\label{sec:v16-adaptive}
The preceding comparison concerns a fixed list of preparations. In an
observation procedure the next window may instead depend on the records
already obtained, and the procedure may stop after enough successful
records. We prove a comparison for the entire stopped transcript. In
particular, the failure symbols and the stopping time are not removed
from the experiment.

We use the unscaled physical endpoint and residual-time coordinates
at the end of Section~\ref{sec:v6-transfer}. A design $a$ specifies a
labelled channel, a flight number $j(a)$ and a physical observation time
$t(a)$. At geometric parameter $\xi$ its excess is
$d_\xi(a)=t(a)-j(a)g_\xi$. Write $P_{\xi,a}$ for the raw coarsened
physical law and $Q_{\xi,a}$ for the corresponding boundary law. The
success probability is $p_\xi(a)=P_{\xi,a}(\text{success})$.
The chosen designs must lie in the common physical collar:
$0<d_\xi(a)\le d_*$. An unknown excess is used only in proving the
comparison; it is not supplied to the observation rule.

The design and record spaces are standard Borel spaces. A policy consists
of measurable rules for choosing a design or stopping, using the observed
past and auxiliary randomness independent of $\xi$. The same rules are
used in the two experiments. Conditional on the past and the chosen
design, a fresh preparation has law $P_{\xi,a}$ or $Q_{\xi,a}$,
respectively. The policy stops after $T\le N$ preparations, where $N$ is
a prescribed deterministic cap. The transcript includes every design,
every success or failure record, and $T$. These conventions include
randomized pilot procedures followed by data-dependent designs.

\begin{lemma}[A stopped comparison for observation kernels]
\label{lem:v16-kernel-comparison}
Let $P_{\xi,a}$ and $Q_{\xi,a}$ be jointly measurable probability
kernels on a common record space, and suppose that their total variation
distance is at most a nonnegative measurable function $e_\xi(a)$.
Assume that a measurable maximal coupling is available for each pair of
kernels. For any policy as above, let $\mathsf P_\xi^\pi$ and
$\mathsf Q_\xi^\pi$ be the laws of its stopped transcript. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v16-kernel-comparison}
 \|\mathsf P_\xi^\pi-\mathsf Q_\xi^\pi\|_{\rm TV}
 \le \min\left\{1,
       \mathbb E_{\mathsf P_\xi^\pi}
          \sum_{i=1}^{T}e_\xi(a_i)\right\}.
\end{equation}
One may interchange $P$ and $Q$ in the expectation. The conclusion is
unchanged by recording the independent random seed or by applying a
common randomized observation map to the transcript.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the same auxiliary randomness for both policies while their pasts
agree. They then choose the same design, or both stop. At a common active
past, couple the next two records maximally. After their first disagreement
continue each marginal according to its own policy and observation
kernel. This constructs a coupling of the required transcript laws.
Let $D_i$ be the event that their first disagreement is at preparation
$i$. Given a common past and its next design $a_i$, its conditional
probability is at most $e_\xi(a_i)$. Therefore
\[
 \Pr(D_i)\le
 \mathbb E\bigl[\one_{\{\text{pasts agree before }i,\ T_P\ge i\}}
                         e_\xi(a_i^P)\bigr]
 \le \mathbb E_{\mathsf P_\xi^\pi}
                 [\one_{\{T\ge i\}}e_\xi(a_i)].
\]
Here $T_P$ and $a_i^P$ refer to the physical marginal; no independence
of the random designs is used. The events $D_i$ are disjoint. If none
occurs, the two policies also stop together and their complete transcripts
agree. For any measurable set $B$, a coupling satisfies
$|\Pr(X\in B)-\Pr(Y\in B)|\le\Pr(X\ne Y)$.
Summing the last display proves \eqref{eq:v16-kernel-comparison}.
Interchanging the marginals gives the other expectation. Coupling the
randomized output conditionally in the same way proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The coupling inequality is classical; see, for example,
\cite[Proposition 4.7]{LPW}. For the physical kernels, a common scalar dominating measure suffices;
their planar pushforwards need not have ambient densities.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive physical transfer with a success-weighted budget]
\label{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer}
For the compact physical families of Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-transfer},
let $\pi$ be a policy whose designs are in the common collar on every
reachable history. There are constants $C<\infty$ and $0<\tau<1$,
independent of $\pi$ and $N$, such that
\begin{align}
 \|\mathsf P_\xi^\pi-\mathsf Q_\xi^\pi\|_{\rm TV}
 &\le \min\{1,C\mathcal B_\xi(\pi)\},
                                      \label{eq:v16-adaptive-transfer}\\
 \mathcal B_\xi(\pi)
 &:=\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_\xi^\pi}
       \sum_{i=1}^{T}p_\xi(a_i)\tau^{j(a_i)}
   =\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_\xi^\pi}
       \sum_{i=1}^{T}Y_i\tau^{j(a_i)},
                                      \label{eq:v16-success-budget}
\end{align}
where $Y_i$ is the indicator of a successful preparation. Hence replacing
the physical experiment by the boundary experiment changes any risk for
a loss in $[0,1]$ by at most $\min\{1,C\mathcal B_\xi(\pi)\}$.
The bounds are uniform in $\xi$ and survive any common coarsening.

In particular, if $j(a_i)\ge J$ and the policy stops no later than
its $k$th success, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v16-success-stop}
 \|\mathsf P_\xi^\pi-\mathsf Q_\xi^\pi\|_{\rm TV}
                         \le\min\{1,Ck\tau^J\}.
\end{equation}
The physical cost remains the number $T$ of raw preparations, including
all failures; it is not replaced by the number of successes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-transfer}, transported to the common unscaled
physical coordinates, supplies
$e_\xi(a)=Cp_\xi(a)\tau^{j(a)}$. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:v16-kernel-comparison}. The event $\{T\ge i\}$ and
the design $a_i$ are known before the $i$th fresh observation. Its
conditional success probability is $p_\xi(a_i)$. Conditional expectation
therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\one_{\{T\ge i\}}Y_i\tau^{j(a_i)}]
 =\mathbb E[\one_{\{T\ge i\}}p_\xi(a_i)\tau^{j(a_i)}].
\]
Summing the finite list proves \eqref{eq:v16-success-budget}. Under
the stopping assumption the realized sum is at most $k\tau^J$.
For a loss $0\le L\le1$, write
$L=\int_0^1\one_{\{L>s\}}\,\dd s$ and apply the total variation
bound. This proves both the risk assertion and the stated uniformities.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Pilot observations and growing adaptive experiments]
\label{cor:v16-pilot-transfer}
The same bound applies when a charged pilot is kept exact in both
experiments and only the subsequent preparations are replaced by their
boundary laws. In that case the sum in
\eqref{eq:v16-success-budget} runs over the replaced preparations only,
whereas the total cost includes the pilot. If the pilot certifies the
common collar on an event of probability at least $1-\eta_0$, the bound
becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v16-pilot-transfer}
 \|\mathsf P_\xi^\pi-\mathsf Q_\xi^\pi\|_{\rm TV}
 \le \eta_0+C\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_\xi^\pi}
       \left[\one_{\{\text{good pilot}\}}
         \sum_{i\in I_{\rm post}}Y_i\tau^{j(a_i)}\right].
\end{equation}
Here, on bad pilot histories, the comparison experiment is completed by
any specified probability kernels on the same transcript space; no
boundary formula outside its collar is assumed. For a sequence of policies,
if $\eta_0\to0$ and the displayed success-weighted expectations tend to
zero uniformly on the physical family, every bounded transcript risk
has a vanishing comparison error.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Couple the exact pilot identically, including its stopping decision.
It has the same law in both experiments. Condition on the entire pilot
transcript. On a good transcript apply the preceding proof to the
post-pilot policy, and on a bad one bound disagreement by one. Integration
over this common pilot law proves \eqref{eq:v16-pilot-transfer}. The
conditional-expectation identity for successes holds with the good-pilot
indicator, which is measurable before every post-pilot observation.
The final assertion follows by taking a uniform bound.
\end{proof}

For example, any sequence of policies capped after at most $k_n$ post-pilot
successes and using flight numbers at least $J_n$ has vanishing error if
$k_n\tau^{J_n}\to0$ and $\eta_{0,n}\to0$. This includes adaptive
selection of positive physical offsets and early stopping. Neither this
criterion nor the exact pilot removes a preparation charge. It is a
consequence of the relative physical theorem for complete experiments;
it does not replace the separate full-profile regularization argument,
or assert an optimal rate for choosing $J_n$.
