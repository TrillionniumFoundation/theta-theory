\section{A finite classification across channel ranks}
\label{sec:family-v96}
The boundary family admits a classification which includes both channel
ranks and the weight constraint. We fix $D>0$, $0<\alpha_*<1/2$,
and an integer $\ell\ge7$. Let $\mathfrak F$ consist of the parameters
\[
 \theta=(r,s,\alpha,U,V,T,w),\qquad
 0<s<r<D,\quad \alpha_*<\alpha<1-\alpha_*,
\]
where $U=(u_1,u_2)$ and $V=(v_1,v_2)$ are strictly positive
column-stochastic $2\times2$ matrices, $\det V\ne0$,
$D<T_1<\cdots<T_\ell$, and $w_j>0$, $\sum_jw_j=1$.
No rank assumption is imposed on $U$. Write $\gamma=1-\alpha$ and set
\begin{align}
 f(z)&=z(z-r)^2,&g(z)&=(z-s)^3,\nonumber\\
 K(z)&=\alpha f(z)u_1v_1^{\mathsf T}
           +\gamma g(z)u_2v_2^{\mathsf T},&
 q(z)&=\alpha f(z)+\gamma g(z),\label{eq:family-model-v96}\\
 P_{\theta,j}&=K(T_j)/q(T_j).&&\nonumber
\end{align}
Competitors always range over the entire closed binary degree-three
model: both channels may be singular or have zero entries, the weights
are at least $\alpha_*$, and all six roots belong to $[0,D]$.
They use the same $T,w$ as the centre. The target is the unordered
multiset of six roots. Denote its diameter over the radius-$t$
observation ball by $\omega_\theta(t)$. Our convention is
\[
 h_w(P,Q)^2=\sum_j w_j\sum_e(\sqrt{P_{je}}-\sqrt{Q_{je}})^2.
\]
Coefficient norms are Euclidean; matrix coefficients are stacked with
Frobenius norm. The metric on real root multisets is the maximum
discrepancy between their increasing orderings.

Introduce the following two polynomials in the family parameters:
\begin{align}
 A(r,s)&=(r+3s)(2r-3s)^2,\nonumber\\
 B(r,s)&=(3D-r)s-2rD,\nonumber\\
 E(r,s,\alpha)&=(1-\alpha)A(r,s)-4\alpha_*r^3.\label{eq:phase-polynomials-v96}
\end{align}
They involve neither an observation-ball optimization nor a selected
parameter tangent space.

\begin{theorem}[Classification of the family]
\label{thm:family-classification-v96}
The exact spectral fibre of a datum in $\mathfrak F$ is a singleton
if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identifiable-region-v96}
 \theta\in\mathfrak I
 :=\{\det U\ne0\}\ \cup\ \{B<0\}\ \cup\ \{E<0\}.
\end{equation}
In particular $\mathfrak I$ is an open semialgebraic subset of
$\mathfrak F$, containing identifiable rank-one and rank-two data.
On its complement put
\[
 z_*={rs\over3s-2r},\qquad c_*={4r^3\over A(r,s)},\qquad
 R(z)={z(z-r)^2\over(z-s)^3},\qquad c_D={R(D)\over R(D)-1}.
\]
Besides $(f,g)$, all exact component-polynomial pairs, up to
permutation, are precisely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-interval-v96}
 (f,(1-c)f+cg),\qquad
 c_*\le c\le\min\{c_D,\gamma/\alpha_*\}.
\end{equation}
This interval is nonempty, including at equality in either boundary
condition. Every pair in it is realized by positive stochastic
channels and admissible weights.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first record the normalization argument in a form which is independent
of the rank of $U$. If $e$ has degree below three and a matrix polynomial
$H$ of degree at most three satisfies $H(T_j)=e(T_j)P_{\theta,j}$,
then seven-point interpolation gives $qH=eK$. Its second marginal is
\[
 qH^{\mathsf T}\one=e\,V\diag(\alpha,\gamma)(f,g)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Multiplication by $\diag(\alpha,\gamma)^{-1}V^{-1}$ shows that
$q\mid ef$ and $q\mid eg$. Since $\gcd(f,g)=1$, Bezout's identity
implies $q\mid e$, and consequently $e=0$. Thus the monic normalizer
and the matrix polynomial $K$ are determined by the observations.
The same argument proves injectivity of the finite normalization
operator, not merely uniqueness within the displayed family.

When $\det U\ne0$, the leading coefficient $K_3$ is invertible and
\[
 {\det K(z)\over\det K_3}=f(z)g(z).
\]
Every exact closed-model competitor has this same $K$ and therefore
this same monic spectral polynomial. This proves identification on the
first set in \eqref{eq:identifiable-region-v96}.

Suppose next that $\det U=0$. Column stochasticity gives $u_1=u_2=u$.
The coefficient matrix of the second marginal
$V\diag(\alpha,\gamma)(f,g)^{\mathsf T}$ has rank two. For any exact
competitor its second channel is therefore invertible and its two
monic component polynomials belong to the affine pencil
$h_c=(1-c)f+cg$. Direct calculation gives
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{disc}(h_c)
 &=c(c-1)^2(r-s)^3\{A(r,s)c-4r^3\},\\
 h_c(0)&=-cs^3,\qquad
 {R'(z)\over R(z)}={(2r-3s)z+rs\over z(z-r)(z-s)}.
\end{align*}
Nonnegative roots exclude $c<0$; the discriminant excludes $0<c<1$.
For $c>1$ there is one root in $(s,r)$. The other two must exceed $r$
and solve $R(z)=c/(c-1)>1$. They exist only when $s>2r/3$.
In this case $R$ increases to its maximum at $z_*$ and then decreases
to one. The double-root value is $c_*$, and the larger root increases
from $z_*$ to $D$ as $c$ increases from $c_*$ to $c_D$.
Thus the complete admissible pencil is $\{0,1\}$, together with
$[c_*,c_D]$ exactly when $z_*\le D$. At $z_*=D$ this interval is a
singleton. Notice that $A=4r^3-27s^2(r-s)<4r^3$, so $c_*>1$.

If the two pencil parameters are $c_1,c_2$, the monic normalizer gives
$\alpha'_1c_1+\alpha'_2c_2=\gamma\in(0,1)$. Hence one is zero and
the other is either one or a $c$ in the additional interval. Its
weight is $\gamma/c$, admissible exactly when $c\le\gamma/\alpha_*$;
the other weight is $1-\gamma/c\ge\alpha>\alpha_*$. Conversely set
\[
 \beta'_f=\alpha v_1+\gamma(1-1/c)v_2,\qquad
 \beta'_h=\gamma v_2/c.
\]
These vectors are positive, have the stated weights as entry sums,
and satisfy $\beta'_ff+\beta'_hh_c=\alpha v_1f+\gamma v_2g$.
Normalizing them and keeping the first channel $(u,u)$ realizes the
whole interval. Since $h_c\ne g$ for $c>1$, each is a second spectral
value. Finally $z_*\le D$ is equivalent to $B\ge0$; this already
implies $s>2r/3$ and $A>0$. On this set $c_*\le\gamma/\alpha_*$
is equivalent to $E\ge0$. This proves all assertions, including the
strict inequalities describing the open identifiable set.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform coefficient inverse on identifiable compacta]
\label{lem:uniform-inverse-v96}
Let $H\Subset\mathfrak I$ be compact. There exist $M,\delta_0>0$
such that, for every $\theta\in H$ and every closed-model competitor
$\xi$ with $\delta=\max_j\|F_T(\xi)_j-P_{\theta,j}\|_F<\delta_0$,
one component permutation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-inverse-v96}
 \|f'_1-f\|_{\rm coef}+\|f'_2-g\|_{\rm coef}
 +|\alpha'_1-\alpha|+\|U'-U\|_F+\|V'-V\|_F\le M\delta.
\end{equation}
The constants are uniform even when $H$ meets both channel ranks.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Injectivity of the normalization operator in the preceding proof,
subtraction of its linear equations, and the uniform boundedness of
all competitor normalizer coefficients imply
\[
 \|q'-q\|_{\rm coef}+\|K'-K\|_{\rm coef}
       +\|K'^{\mathsf T}\one-K^{\mathsf T}\one\|_{\rm coef}\le C\delta.
\]
For completeness, the normalization equation projects the array
$(q(T_j)P_j)_j$ onto the orthogonal complement of the degree-three
Vandermonde column space. Subtracting the two equations gives the
fixed injective operator at $P_\theta$, applied to the degree-below-three
polynomial $q'-q$, with right side bounded by $C\delta$. A bounded
left inverse gives the first estimate; interpolation gives the others.
All these bounds are locally uniform in the centre.

At a rank-two centre choose two clocks $T_i,T_j$ for which
$f(T_j)/f(T_i)\ne g(T_j)/g(T_i)$. Such clocks exist: otherwise the
nonzero degree-at-most-three polynomial $g(T_i)f-f(T_i)g$ would
vanish at all seven clocks. The matrix $K(T_j)K(T_i)^{-1}$ has distinct
real eigenvalues and eigenvectors $u_1,u_2$. Normalize each eigenvector
to have entry sum one. The simple-eigenvalue inverse is analytic near
the centre. Applying $U^{-1}$ to $K(z)$, and taking row sums, then
recovers $\alpha f$ and $\gamma g$; leading coefficients and normalized
rows recover weights and $V$. This gives \eqref{eq:uniform-inverse-v96}
for arbitrary nearby competitors, since $K'(T_i)$ remains invertible.

At an identifiable rank-one centre, Theorem~\ref{thm:family-classification-v96}
and compactness first confine all sufficiently close competitors to
small coefficient neighbourhoods of $(f,g)$, after permutation. There
is no hidden non-root fibre here: fixed functionals dual to $f,g$
recover their coefficient matrices, whose entry sums and normalized
marginals recover the weights and both channels uniquely.
Put $B_{\rm coef}=(K^{\mathsf T}\one)_{\rm coef}$ and
$\beta=\sigma_2(B_{\rm coef})>0$. In
$B'_{\rm coef}=V'\diag(\alpha')F'_{\rm coef}$, compactness supplies
$M_0\ge\|\diag(\alpha')F'_{\rm coef}\|$. Thus
\[
 \beta/2\le\sigma_2(B'_{\rm coef})
       \le\sigma_{\min}(V')M_0,\qquad
 \|\diag(\alpha')^{-1}V'^{-1}\|\le {2M_0\over\alpha_*\beta}.
\]
Multiplication by this bounded inverse and monicity yield
$f'_b=h_{c_b}+O_{\rm coef}(\delta)$, with bounded $c_b$ respectively
near zero and one. Only \emph{local} pencil isolation at these two
points is now needed; additional remote pencil polynomials have
already been excluded by whole-model fibre compactness.

Near zero, $f'_b(0)\le0$ gives $c_b\ge-C\delta$, and the discriminant
formula, whose derivative at zero is $-4r^3(r-s)^3<0$, gives
$c_b\le C\delta$. Near one put $v=1-c_b$ and depress $f'_b$ using
its own root mean. Its depressed coefficients satisfy
\[
 a=f'(s)v+O(v^2+\delta),\qquad b=f(s)v+O(v^2+\delta).
\]
Real-rootedness implies $a\le0$ and
$|b|\le2(-a)^{3/2}/(3\sqrt3)$. Since $f(s)>0$, these inequalities
give $|v|\le C\delta+C v^2+C(|v|+\delta)^{3/2}$, and the last
two terms are absorbed locally. The component coefficients are
therefore recovered with error $O(\delta)$.

Choose coefficient functionals dual to $f,g$. Their values on the
nearby components form $I+O(\delta)$. Applying them to $K'$ recovers
$\alpha'_bu'_b(v'_b)^{\mathsf T}$ with error $O(\delta)$.
Entry sums give weights; normalized row and column sums give channels.
This proves the desired estimate at rank one, including cases isolated
by the weight constraint rather than by the root interval alone.

All confinement and displayed estimates hold on a neighbourhood of
each such centre. To see the first assertion uniformly, a contrary
sequence of centres tending to it and competitors with observation
error tending to zero would have a limiting exact competitor, which
has just been classified. Positivity, coefficient independence and
the local pencil inequalities have strict margins on a smaller
neighbourhood. A finite cover of $H$ completes the proof.
\end{proof}
