\begin{abstract}
We study polynomial spectra observed through stochastic channels and
an unknown scalar normalization. Real monomialization of the
observation--spectral graph gives finite valuative data for the
intrinsic root exponent; real weighted specialization retains the
joint leading spectral set and its inequality constraints. For the
binary boundary cubic family with an interior double root and an
interior triple root, we classify the entire exact spectral fibre
across both ranks of the first channel. The identifiable set is an
explicit open semialgebraic set, including regions isolated by the
weight constraint. On each of its compact subsets the whole-model
modulus has the uniform expansion $C(\theta)\sqrt t+O(t)$.
A finite active-set calculation gives a semialgebraic partition on
which $C^4$ is rational, and constructs extremizing stochastic
alternatives. The constant is continuous across identified rank
changes. The same expansion yields uniform squared-risk bounds on
specified shrinking oracle neighbourhoods. Additive normalization,
clock geometry, quotient recovery, and structured singular perturbation
results provide the complementary global theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A singular observation map can identify an unordered polynomial
spectrum even when it fails to identify unrestricted coefficient
directions. Stochastic inequalities distinguish these two assertions:
an ambient invisible direction need not have an admissible nonconstant
representative. The intrinsic spectral law must consequently be
computed on the real observation--spectral graph. This article passes
from finite algebraic data for that graph to an explicit family
classification, a uniform normal form, and a finite formula for its
leading constant.

For a compact semialgebraic model with observation map $F$ and monic
target polynomial $A_x$, fix a datum $P$ whose exact fibre has a single
spectral value. Let $c_{r,a}$ be the coefficient of degree $a$ in the
monic cluster at a root $r$ of multiplicity $m_r$. On a compact real
lift put $G=h_w(F(x),P)^2$ and $H_{r,a}=|c_{r,a}|^2$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-newton-v95} computes the nonrigid exponent as
\[
 \alpha_P=\min_{r,a,i}\frac{b_{r,a,i}}{a_i(m_r-a)},
\]
where $a_i,b_{r,a,i}$ are integer orders on accessible real divisors
of a simultaneous monomialization. The input is the defining graph,
not optimized coefficient envelopes. Proposition~\ref{prop:effective-fibre-v95}
retains the simultaneous weighted coefficient specialization and
outputs its exact variational diameter. This construction includes
singular observation charts and positive-dimensional parameter fibres.

The principal family result is more explicit. Take
$f=z(z-r)^2$, $g=(z-s)^3$, with $0<s<r<D$, strict weights and positive
binary channels, the second channel invertible and the first of either
rank. Define
\[
 B=(3D-r)s-2rD,\qquad
 E=(1-\alpha)(r+3s)(2r-3s)^2-4\alpha_*r^3.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:family-classification-v96} shows that the entire
closed-model spectral fibre is a singleton exactly on
\[
 \mathfrak I=\{\det U\ne0\}\cup\{B<0\}\cup\{E<0\}.
\]
It describes all additional component polynomials on the complement.
Thus channel rank, the active root boundary and the weight floor enter
one necessary-and-sufficient classification. The earlier open root
region $s<2rD/(3D-r)$ is only one part of $\mathfrak I$; the region
$B\ge0,E<0$ is identified by the weight constraint instead.

On every compact $H\Subset\mathfrak I$, Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-normal-v96}
identifies the complete leading spectral set and proves
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in H}
  |\omega_\theta(t)-C(\theta)\sqrt t|\le C_Ht.
\]
Here competitors range over the whole closed stochastic model, not
merely over the displayed family. The proof gives a quantitative
Hausdorff comparison with the cluster polynomials
\[
 z,\qquad z^2-X,\qquad z^3-Yz-Z,
 \quad X,Y\ge0,\quad J_\theta(X,Y)\le4,\quad27Z^2\le4Y^3.
\]
The constrained Fisher cost $J_\theta$ retains all seven free nuisance
directions and the one-sided endpoint motion. Necessity follows from
a uniform coefficient inverse; sufficiency follows from actual root
arcs with a controlled radial correction. Neither part is inferred
from a pointwise Puiseux expansion. In particular the error remains
uniform when the first channel changes rank inside $H$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:phase-partition-v96} eliminates the two remaining
nonnegative least-squares problems by four explicit support tests
apiece. On a finite semialgebraic partition, the fourth power of the
leading constant is the rational function
\[
 C^4=\max\{4/\kappa_x,\,64/(9\kappa_y)\},
\]
with the switching sign $9\kappa_y-16\kappa_x$. The constant is
continuous across the partition and analytic on a finite refinement.
This is a family-level formula, rather than an existence statement
for the coefficient-envelope orders of a single datum.

\subsection*{Relation to classical results}
Uniformization and resolution are classical constructions
\cite{v95BM88,v95BM97}; so is the calculation of a
\L{}ojasiewicz order from ratios of divisor orders
\cite{v95BE09}. Semialgebraic elimination and stratification
\cite{BCR98,BPR06}, and general definable metric regularity
\cite{v95LP20}, provide the appropriate setting. The broader
filtration perspective in \cite{v95Ha26} is contextual rather than a
required technical input. The contributions specific to the present
experiment are the real probability graph with its joint cluster
weights, the exact stochastic fibre classification, and the uniform
spectral normal form with the finite Fisher phase calculation.
The active-set calculation itself is elementary convex quadratic
minimization; its role is to make the complete leading invariant
explicit in the original statistical coordinates. We do not claim a
new resolution algorithm, a universal ordinary-jet cutoff, or a
classification of unrelated root-multiplicity patterns.

\subsection*{Structure and scope}
The main text proceeds from the experiment to finite real-valuative
data, then to the family classification, uniform normal form and
finite phase formulas. Direct rank-one calculations are preserved as
complementary proofs, followed by the global additive and clock
results, general intrinsic moduli, quotient and inference theory,
and structured perturbation results. These are organized by
mathematical dependence rather than by the dates of their derivation.
Every previously active mathematical and bibliography module remains
in the complete manuscript. Fixed-datum constants, uniform constants
on compact identifiable families, and boundary-uniform global bounds
have distinct quantifiers throughout. The risk statements concern
specified shrinking oracle neighbourhoods, not an unknown-centre
adaptive procedure.

\paragraph{Coefficient representatives.}
The quotient statements use monic-coefficient coordinates, or roots
ordered within each component. The internal root-permutation quotient
has already been taken. Raw root labels add internal permutations,
not new coefficient representatives; on the internally simple
stratum the remaining component action is the stated free
$\mathfrak S_k$ action. In the immersed determinant calculation an
invertible leading matrix fixes the determinant degree locally and
excludes roots escaping to infinity.
