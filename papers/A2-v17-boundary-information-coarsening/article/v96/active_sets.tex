\section{Finite active-set formulas and the spectral phase partition}
\label{sec:active-v96}
The leading constant can be evaluated without a numerical optimizer.
Index the rows and columns of $Q_\theta$ by $0,x,y$. For $i=x$ or $y$
let $M_i=\{0,x,y\}\setminus\{i\}$. For a subset $A\subseteq M_i$
with $\det Q_{AA}>0$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:candidate-v96}
 v_A=-Q_{AA}^{-1}Q_{Ai},\qquad
 k_{i,A}=Q_{ii}-Q_{iA}Q_{AA}^{-1}Q_{Ai}.
\end{equation}
For $A=\varnothing$ use $k_{i,\varnothing}=Q_{ii}$. Say that a
candidate is admissible when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KKT-v96}
 v_A\ge0,\qquad
 Q_{ji}+Q_{jA}v_A\ge0\quad(j\in M_i\setminus A).
\end{equation}
For the empty set only the second inequalities are imposed.
All these tests are explicit signs of rational functions in the original
root, weight, channel and design parameters. There are four candidates
for each of $\kappa_x$ and $\kappa_y$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite formulas and a stratified law]
\label{thm:phase-partition-v96}
At every $\theta\in\mathfrak I$ at least one of the four candidates
is admissible, and every admissible candidate has value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:active-value-v96}
 \kappa_i(\theta)=k_{i,A}(\theta).
\end{equation}
Together with \eqref{eq:identifiable-region-v96}, these tests give a
finite semialgebraic partition of the entire named family into exact
fibre classes and, on its identifiable part, leading-constant classes.
On each identifiable class, $C(\theta)^4$ is one explicit rational
function. More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourth-power-v96}
 C(\theta)^4=
 \begin{cases}
 4/\kappa_x(\theta),&9\kappa_y(\theta)\ge16\kappa_x(\theta),\\
 64/(9\kappa_y(\theta)),&9\kappa_y(\theta)\le16\kappa_x(\theta).
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The two formulas agree on equality. The partition has a finite
semialgebraic real-analytic stratification on which $C$ is analytic.
Its formula extends continuously across active-set boundaries and
across channel-rank changes contained in $\mathfrak I$. The uniform
remainder in Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-normal-v96} holds on every compact
subset of $\mathfrak I$, not just on a compact subset of one stratum.
For real algebraic input the tests produce algebraic $\kappa_i$ and a
polynomial and isolating interval for $C$, using finite exact arithmetic
and real-root isolation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write the three projected score columns as $p_0,p_x,p_y$ in the
Fisher inner product, so $Q_{ij}=\langle p_i,p_j\rangle$.
For fixed $i$ the required problem is
\[
 \min_{v\in\R_+^{M_i}}
       \left\|p_i+\sum_{j\in M_i}v_jp_j\right\|^2.
\]
A minimizer exists by Lemma~\ref{lem:coercive-v96}. Choose one with
minimal positive support. Its supported columns are linearly
independent: a nonzero linear dependence permits a signed variation
of its positive coefficients, preserving the represented vector,
until one coefficient becomes zero. This contradicts minimality.
Its supported Gram matrix is consequently positive definite.
The derivative vanishes on its support and is nonnegative on inactive
coordinates. These are exactly \eqref{eq:candidate-v96} and
\eqref{eq:KKT-v96}. Conversely these conditions imply optimality:
expanding the quadratic at the candidate gives a nonnegative linear
term on every feasible displacement and a nonnegative quadratic term.
The value is \eqref{eq:active-value-v96}. Singular two-column Gram
matrices cause no loss of candidates; an independent minimal support
still exists. Zero active coefficients and ties are also allowed.

Each entry of the score is rational in $\theta$, because the clocks
are above $D$ and every base probability is positive. The Fisher
weights are $w_j/P_{\theta,je}$, not their square roots. The seven-column
Gram matrix is invertible by Lemma~\ref{lem:coercive-v96}. Thus
\eqref{eq:Q-v96} is rational throughout $\mathfrak I$. Select, for
example, the first admissible support in a fixed ordering of the four
subsets. This yields a finite semialgebraic partition with a rational
formula for each $\kappa_i$. Taking fourth powers in
\eqref{eq:C-v96} gives \eqref{eq:fourth-power-v96}; the switching test
is $9\kappa_y-16\kappa_x$, with no fractional-power comparison.
Refine these finitely many sign conditions, the rank condition, and
the identification signs into a semialgebraic analytic stratification
\cite{BCR98}. The positive fourth root of the selected positive
rational function is analytic on every such stratum. Continuity at
boundaries follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:coercive-v96}, not from an
assumption that the support remains fixed.

With algebraic input, all matrix inversions, sign tests and selected
rational operations take place in a real algebraic number field.
The positive solution of $z^4=C(\theta)^4$ is then isolated in the
usual real algebraic representation. No maximization over shrinking
observation balls, no fitted Puiseux exponent, and no inversion of a
singular ambient Fisher matrix enters this calculation.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope of the classification.}
The family has one boundary simple root, one interior double root and
one interior triple root, with $0<s<r<D$, an invertible second channel,
and an arbitrary positive first channel. It includes the full
identifiable portion of that family, including the weight-isolated
rank-one region $B\ge0$, $E<0$. It is not a classification of unrelated
multiplicity patterns. Within its stated scope, the exact fibre is
classified by three polynomial signs, the leading constant by eight
finite support tests and one comparison, and its approximation error
is uniform with order $t$ on identifiable compacta. The real-valuative
construction supplies the general singular-germ framework; the results
of these sections give a finite parameter-family computation inside
that framework.

\begin{example}[A rational datum and an exact constant]
\label{ex:exact-constant-v96}
Take $D=1$, $\alpha_*=1/10$, $r=3/5$, $s=1/5$, $\alpha=2/5$,
\[
 u_1=u_2=(1/3,2/3)^{\mathsf T},\quad
 v_1=(1/4,3/4)^{\mathsf T},\quad v_2=(3/4,1/4)^{\mathsf T},
\]
with clocks $(6/5,7/5,8/5,9/5,2,5/2,3)$ and weights $1/7$.
Here $B=-18/25<0$. The projected Gram matrix has rank two; the empty
support is admissible for both programs, and $9\kappa_y>16\kappa_x$.
Exact substitution in \eqref{eq:Q-v96}--\eqref{eq:fourth-power-v96} gives
\[
 C^4=
 \frac{78550423766145732273576408862601811503774391446}
      {385279214348552940073664077156875},
 \qquad 3778.707<C<3778.708.
\]
Replacing only $u_2$ by $(2/5,3/5)^{\mathsf T}$ gives a rank-two datum,
a rank-three projected Gram matrix, and the triple-dominant phase, with
$59.970<C<59.971$. Both intervals are rational isolating intervals,
verified by fourth powers, not error bars from floating-point fitting.
The ancillary exact-arithmetic evaluator records the rational Gram
matrices, support inequalities and fourth powers for both data.
These examples also show why positivity and fixed multiplicities alone
do not supply a useful universal leading constant.
\end{example}
