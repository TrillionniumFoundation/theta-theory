\subsection{A nonpolynomial positive-kernel family}
\begin{proposition}[Exponential log-detectors]\label{prop:v83-exponential}
Fix a \emph{known} $\lambda>0$ and clocks $T_j=j$, $1\le j\le4$.
Replace the polynomial in \eqref{eq:v83-model} by
$\beta_b e^{\lambda T_j}$, where $\beta_b$ is unknown. If the
$(W_b,\beta_b)$ are distinct, $W_b<1$, and the actions are not all
equal, four projective matrices determine the actions, channels,
relative weights and relative $\beta_b$. Four are necessary in the
worst case. The local enumerations give the intrinsic component
covering. This does not assert recovery of an unknown shape parameter
$\lambda$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $a=e^\lambda>1$ and $B_0=3+2/(a-1)>3$. The annihilators of
$\operatorname{span}\{\one,(a,a^2,a^3,a^4)\}$
\[
 c^0=(a,-a-1,1,0),\qquad c^1=(0,a,-a-1,1)
\]
have kernels
\[
 k_0(s)=\frac{(a-1)(B_0-s)}{(1-s)(2-s)(3-s)}>0,\qquad
 \frac{k_1}{k_0}(s)=\frac{(1-s)(B_0+1-s)}{(4-s)(B_0-s)}.
\]
For $s<1$, the logarithmic derivative of the latter ratio is
\[
 -\frac{3}{(1-s)(4-s)}+
       \frac{1}{(B_0-s)(B_0+1-s)}<0.
\]
The second term is smaller than $1/((3-s)(4-s))$, which is smaller
than the magnitude of the first. The positive-kernel theorem identifies
an unequal pair and its nuisance coefficients. With one action known,
the first positive contrast identifies every other action, even an
equal one.

Spectral separation must also be checked. For
$f(T)=\alpha+\beta e^{\lambda T}+L_x-L_y$, with $x\ne y$,
\[
 e^{-\lambda T}f'(T)=\lambda\beta+
 (x-y)\frac{e^{-\lambda T}}{(T-x)(T-y)}.
\]
The nonconstant term is strictly monotone on $T>\max(x,y)$; hence
$f'$ has at most one zero. Three equal values of $f$ are impossible.
For $x=y$, distinct $\beta$ gives the same separation conclusion.
The two reduced operators at clocks two and three therefore have
distinct joint vectors. The matrix calculation in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v83-components}, scalar inversion and centring recover
the full model and its covering.

For three clocks, the columns $1,e^{\lambda T},-(T-x)^{-1}$ are
independent. The derivative of a putative linear combination, multiplied
by $e^{-\lambda T}$, is a constant plus a multiple of
$e^{-\lambda T}(T-x)^{-2}$, a strictly decreasing positive function.
Three zeros would give two derivative zeros and force all coefficients
to vanish. The implicit function theorem solves for
$(\alpha,\beta,x)$ as a nearby $y$ varies. Fixed positive full-rank
two-component channels turn this scalar level set into identical
projective matrices, proving the lower bound.
\end{proof}
Known positive affine changes of the clock coordinate give the same
example on four equally spaced clocks. A small positive baseline keeps
these detector functions valid retention probabilities on the finite
observation interval.
