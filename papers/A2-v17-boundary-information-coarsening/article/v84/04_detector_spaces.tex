\section{Detector function spaces and stable finite designs}
\label{sec:v84-spaces}
A criterion on one sampled subspace does not by itself decide whether a
function space admits a useful design. We separate these questions.
Let $K_T=[\tau_0,\tau_1]$, with $\tau_0<\tau_1$, be a clock interval,
and let $I\subset(-\infty,\tau_0)$ be an open action interval.
Let $\cF\subset C(K_T)$ be a finite-dimensional real space containing
constants, with any fixed coefficient norm. Set
\[
 L_x(T)=\log(T-x),\qquad
 \mathcal A=\{(x,y)\in I^2:x\ne y\},\qquad
 \Gamma(x,y)=L_x-L_y.
\]
The exact functional observation is $e+\Gamma(x,y)$, $e\in\cF$.
Its image in $C(K_T)/\cF$ is a secant of the logarithmic action curve.

\begin{definition}\label{def:v84-regular}
The detector space is \emph{regularly separating on $I$} if
\begin{align}
 \Gamma(a)-\Gamma(a')\in\cF&\ \Longrightarrow\ a=a'
                  &&(a,a'\in\mathcal A),\label{eq:v84-secants}\\
 -\frac{u}{T-x}+\frac{v}{T-y}\in\cF&\ \Longrightarrow\ u=v=0
                  &&((x,y)\in\mathcal A).\label{eq:v84-tangents}
\end{align}
The second condition is a regularity condition, not part of set-theoretic
identification. It excludes a vanishing first-order signal in the quotient.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Function-space criterion and finite deadline design]
\label{thm:v84-design}
If $\cF$ is regularly separating, then for every compact
$K\subset\mathcal A$ there are finitely many distinct deadlines in
$(\tau_0,\tau_1)$ and a constant $c_K>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v84-design}
 \max_j |(e-e')(T_j)+\Gamma(a)(T_j)-\Gamma(a')(T_j)|
 \ge c_K\bigl(\|e-e'\|_{\cF}+|a-a'|\bigr)
\end{equation}
for all $a,a'\in K$ and all $e,e'\in\cF$. Evaluation is injective on
$\cF$. The finite observation map is an immersion near each point of
$K$ and has a locally smooth left inverse, including a smooth local
extension from ambient sampled data.

Conversely, functional identification implies
\eqref{eq:v84-secants}. A differentiable local left inverse at every
admissible pair, from any collection of point evaluations, implies
\eqref{eq:v84-tangents}. Thus these conditions characterize regular
identification and the existence of stable finite designs on compact
classes, rather than merely restating a criterion for one chosen design.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Consider every nonzero difference $e+\Gamma(a)-\Gamma(a')$, normalized
by $t=\|e\|_{\cF}+|a-a'|$. We show that the closure of this family
in $C(K_T)$ is compact and does not contain zero. Given a sequence,
pass to subsequences so that $a,a'$ converge in $K$ and the normalized
coefficient vector $e/t$ converges. If $t\to\infty$, the logarithmic
part divided by $t$ converges to zero, whereas $\|e/t\|_{\cF}\to1$.
If $t\to t_0\in(0,\infty)$, the limit is a normalized secant
difference plus a member of $\cF$. Its vanishing would force $a=a'$
by \eqref{eq:v84-secants}, then $e=0$, contradicting $t_0>0$.
If $t\to0$, the actions have a common limit $a=(x,y)$ and
$(a-a')/t$ has a convergent subsequence with limit $(u,v)$. Taylor's
formula, uniformly on $K_T$, gives the limit
\[
 e_0-\frac{u}{T-x}+\frac{v}{T-y},\qquad
       \|e_0\|_{\cF}+|(u,v)|=1.
\]
It cannot vanish by \eqref{eq:v84-tangents} and the independence of a
basis of $\cF$. These alternatives also prove sequential compactness;
the logarithm has uniformly bounded parameter derivatives on the
compact action and clock sets. In the case of a bounded sequence of
$t$ with no limit, take a further convergent subsequence first.

Let $\kappa>0$ be the minimum supremum norm on this compact closure.
For each member choose an interior deadline where its absolute value
exceeds $\kappa/2$. Continuity in the function and compactness give
finitely many such evaluations detecting every member by at least
$\kappa/4$. Repeated deadlines may be removed. This proves
\eqref{eq:v84-design}. To secure the immersion simultaneously, include
the compact normalized tangent family
\[
 \left\{e-\frac{u}{T-x}+\frac{v}{T-y}:
 a\in K,\ \|e\|_{\cF}+|(u,v)|=1\right\}
\]
in the same finite-subcover construction. Condition
\eqref{eq:v84-tangents} excludes zero from it. The sampled derivative
then has full column rank. A nonsingular coordinate minor and the
inverse function theorem give the stated smooth local left inverse.
Taking $a=a'$ in \eqref{eq:v84-design} proves injective evaluation on
$\cF$.

If \eqref{eq:v84-secants} fails, the corresponding nuisance difference
makes even the full functions identical. If
\eqref{eq:v84-tangents} fails, a nonzero tangent vector, supplemented
by its nuisance coefficients, lies in the kernel of every sampled
derivative. The chain rule contradicts a differentiable left inverse.
\end{proof}
The proof distinguishes two kinds of separation. A nonsingular tangent
map need not separate distant secants; exact injectivity alone need
not provide a differentiable inverse. The clock set and $c_K$ depend
on the function space and the compact action class, not just on its
dimension. The theorem does not assert that an arbitrary prescribed
set of that many clocks works. It allows annihilators of any dimension
and contains no assumption on the sign of their Cauchy kernels.

For a proposed enlargement $\cF\subset\widetilde\cF$, the exact test
is particularly transparent: no nontrivial secant difference in
\eqref{eq:v84-secants}, and no nonzero tangent combination in
\eqref{eq:v84-tangents}, may enter $\widetilde\cF$. This gives the
boundary of \emph{regular} admissible enlargements. It is not a
claim that there is one largest admissible detector space.

\subsection{A uniform generic clock bound for analytic spaces}
The preceding compactness construction does not bound the number of
clocks by the dimension. Analyticity supplies such a bound, and permits
one clock set to work on the entire admissible action interval.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-order zeros and generic evaluations]
\label{lem:v84-evaluations}
Let $\mathcal P$ be an open subset of $\R^p$, $J_0$ an open interval,
and $H:\mathcal P\times J_0\to\R$ smooth. Suppose every function
$H(\theta,\cdot)$ is nonzero and real analytic. If $N>p$, then for
almost every $(T_1,\ldots,T_N)\in J_0^N$ there is no $\theta$ for
which $H(\theta,T_j)=0$ for all $j$. The same assertion holds on
countably many parameter charts of dimension at most $p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At an incidence point where all the sampled values vanish, let $r_j$
be the finite order of the zero at $T_j$. For the subset of incidence
points with this same order vector, all equations
$\partial_T^{r_j-1}H(\theta,T_j)=0$ hold. Their derivative matrix
with respect to the $N$ clock variables is diagonal with nonzero
entries $\partial_T^{r_j}H(\theta,T_j)$. The implicit function theorem
therefore places this subset locally in a graph
$(T_1,\ldots,T_N)=\varphi(\theta)$. There are countably many order
vectors, and second countability gives countably many such local
graphs covering each subset. A smooth image of a $p$-dimensional
coordinate domain in $\R^N$, $p<N$, has $N$-dimensional Lebesgue
measure zero: restrict to countably many compact coordinate boxes
where the map is Lipschitz and cover each box by small cubes.
The countable union of these images is null. Analyticity is used to
exclude an infinite-order zero of a nonzero function, not to assume
that all zeros are simple. Countably many parameter charts do not
change the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Generic analytic deadline design]
\label{thm:v84-generic}
Suppose $\dim\cF=d$, every member of $\cF$ is real analytic on an
open connected clock interval above $I$, and
\eqref{eq:v84-secants}--\eqref{eq:v84-tangents} hold there. Then almost
every ordered set of $d+5$ distinct clocks in any nonempty open
subinterval identifies every $e\in\cF$ and every $(x,y)\in\mathcal A$
globally, with a locally smooth inverse. For each such clock set,
\eqref{eq:v84-design} holds on every compact $K\subset\mathcal A$.
Thus the exceptional clock set is independent of the unknown action
pair and nuisance coefficients. The count is a uniform sufficient
bound, not a sharp count for each analytic space. Smooth inversion here
is local in parameter space; the uniform estimates are on compact
action classes. No properness at the ends of $I$ is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a basis of $\cF$ and consider
\[
 H(e,a,a';T)=e(T)+\Gamma(a)(T)-\Gamma(a')(T).
\]
Outside the trivial set $e=0,a=a'$, the functional secant condition
makes this a nonzero analytic function of $T$. Its parameter space
has dimension $d+4$ and is covered by countably many open coordinate
charts. Lemma~\ref{lem:v84-evaluations} with $N=d+5$ shows that almost
every clock tuple excludes every nontrivial scalar collision. This
includes nuisance-only differences and hence full-rank evaluation on
$\cF$.

For the derivative consider
$H(e,x,y,u,v;T)=e(T)-u/(T-x)+v/(T-y)$, with nonzero joint coefficient
vector $(e,u,v)$. Normalize one nonzero coefficient to one; the
finitely many such charts have dimension $d+3$, including $x,y$.
The tangent condition makes these functions nonzero. The lemma again
excludes their simultaneous sampled zeros for almost every clock
tuple (already $d+4$ clocks suffice for this derivative step).
Removing the null sets from both arguments gives injectivity and
full derivative rank at every admissible point. Distinctness and
ordering only discard diagonals and restrict to an open chamber.
The local inverse theorem and the normalized-secant compactness
argument prove the final statements. No parameter-dependent exceptional
set is exchanged with an uncountable union: the incidence argument
has already included all parameters before projecting to clock space.
\end{proof}
The clock hierarchy is now explicit: $d+5$ generic clocks for regularly
separating analytic spaces, $d+4$ arbitrary clocks for the protected-pole
class below, and the sharp $d+2=q+3$ for polynomial log-detectors.
These are different design conclusions, not interchangeable dimension
counts. The analytic theorem is a scalar design theorem; latent
spectral separation must still be verified, as it is in the applications.

\subsection{Logarithmic detectors with protected singularities}
Let $a_1,\ldots,a_k<\tau_0$ be distinct known numbers outside $I$.
For $q\ge0$ consider the function space
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v84-logspace}
 \cF_{q,A}=\cP_q+\operatorname{span}\{\log(T-a_1),\ldots,
                                  \log(T-a_k)\}.
\end{equation}
The locations $a_i$ are fixed features of the detector family, not
unknown actions. Polynomial differentiation and residues show that
this sum has dimension $q+k+1$.

\begin{theorem}[Protected-pole rigidity]\label{thm:v84-poles}
For \eqref{eq:v84-logspace}, any $q+k+5$ distinct deadlines in $K_T$
determine $x\ne y$ in $I$ and $e\in\cF_{q,A}$ from
$e(T_j)+\log(T_j-x)-\log(T_j-y)$. The inverse is locally smooth and
is uniformly Lipschitz on every compact separated action class, with
unbounded nuisance differences allowed as in \eqref{eq:v84-design}.

Replace the polynomial log-detectors in \eqref{eq:v83-model} by members
of this space. If each pair of components differs in its action or
nonconstant detector coefficients, and the actions are not all equal,
the same $q+k+5$ clocks determine the visible count, both channels,
absolute actions and detector coefficients modulo a common member of
$\cF_{q,A}$. The component covering is intrinsic as before. The bound
is sufficient; no optimality for this enlarged family is claimed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The difference of two competing scalar observations is
\[
 H(T)=P(T)+\sum_i\beta_i\log(T-a_i)
             +L_x-L_y-L_{x'}+L_{y'}.
\]
Its derivative is rational plus $P'$. Multiply by the product of its
distinct pole factors, of degree at most $k+4$. The resulting
polynomial has degree at most $q+k+3$ (also when $q=0$). If $H$
vanishes at $q+k+5$ clocks, Rolle's theorem gives at least $q+k+4$
zeros of $H'$ above every pole. Thus that numerator is identically
zero. At each protected pole the residue is $\beta_i$, so all
$\beta_i$ vanish. At the moving poles the remaining identity is
\[
                  \delta_x-\delta_y=\delta_{x'}-\delta_{y'}.
\]
Since both intervals are nondegenerate, equality of these signed
point masses gives $x=x'$ and $y=y'$. Finally $P'=0$ and the sampled
zeros give $P=0$.

For the derivative of the scalar parameter map, a putative dependence
has the form
\[
 H(T)=P(T)+\sum_i\beta_i\log(T-a_i)
                         -u/(T-x)+v/(T-y).
\]
After differentiation, the two moving poles are at most double; the
common denominator still has degree at most $k+4$. The same degree
bound and Rolle argument apply. The double-pole coefficients give
$u=v=0$, then the simple protected residues and the constant give
$\beta_i=0$ and $P=0$. The sampled Jacobian has full column rank.
The normalized-secant compactness argument in the preceding proof,
now for this fixed sampled map, gives the claimed compact-class
Lipschitz bound. The same residue arguments establish
\eqref{eq:v84-secants}--\eqref{eq:v84-tangents} at the function level.

For latent separation, a difference of two log-weight trajectories
minus a constant has only two moving poles. Its differentiated
numerator has degree at most $q+k+1$. Already $q+k+3$ equal sampled
values force it to be constant identically. Protected and moving
residues then force equal actions and equal nonconstant coefficients,
contrary to the hypothesis. Thus the joint spectral trajectories are
distinct at our clocks, and Lemma~\ref{lem:v83-components} applies
with all the available reduced operators.
A pair with nonzero projection modulo the sampled detector space has
unequal actions; a zero projection for unequal actions is excluded by
the same two-pole argument. Recover one unequal pair by the scalar
inverse. With one action fixed, the corresponding one-action map is
injective and has a nonvanishing derivative modulo the detector space,
again by its protected poles, even when the new action equals the
anchor. Recover all remaining actions, interpolate the nuisance
coefficients, and centre them fibrewise. The spectral columns provide
the same covering and matching construction as before.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Identification with no positive annihilator]
\label{prop:v84-no-positive}
Let $a<b$, $I=(a,b)$, and
\[
 \cF=\operatorname{span}\{1,h\},\qquad
                 h(T)=\log\frac{T-a}{T-b},\quad T>b.
\]
Any six distinct clocks above $b$ identify ordered unequal actions in
$I$ and both nuisance coefficients, with a locally smooth inverse.
The associated latent model is also identifiable under the distinct
$(W_b,\beta_b)$ and unequal-action hypotheses. For \emph{every}
finite set of clocks, every nonzero sampled annihilator kernel changes
sign on $(a,b)$. Thus no two-dimensional subspace of that annihilator
satisfies the positive-kernel hypothesis.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a scalar collision, $H'=\beta h'+(L_x-L_y-L_{x'}+L_{y'})'$
has at most six simple poles. Its total residue is zero, so it is
$O(T^{-2})$ at infinity. Its numerator after multiplication by the
six-factor denominator has degree at most four. Six sampled zeros of
$H$ force five zeros of $H'$, hence $H'=0$. The residues at $a,b$
give $\beta=0$, and the moving residues determine the ordered pair.
The constant is fixed by one sample. For the parameter derivative,
$H'=\beta h'+u/(T-x)^2-v/(T-y)^2$ has a denominator of degree six
and is again $O(T^{-2})$; the same argument proves full derivative rank.
For spectral separation, the corresponding two-moving-pole derivative
has numerator degree at most two, so four equal clock values already
force identical action--coefficient pairs. The remaining latent
reconstruction follows the preceding theorem.

If $c$ annihilates $1$ and the sampled $h$, then its Cauchy kernel
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v84-zero-integral}
 \int_a^b\sum_j\frac{c_j}{T_j-s}\dd s
             =\sum_jc_j\log\frac{T_j-a}{T_j-b}=0.
\end{equation}
A nonzero $c$ has a nonzero rational kernel, by its distinct pole
residues. This kernel is continuous on $[a,b]$, so zero integral forces
both signs. In particular it cannot be positive throughout $(a,b)$.
\end{proof}
This example belongs to a fixed two-dimensional function space, not
to a detector space chosen after observing the data. Its larger
annihilator does useful work that no positive-kernel reduction can do.

\subsection{Several action thresholds and the common-divisor gauge}
The same invariant has a higher-dimensional form. Replace $T-W_b$ in
the model by a monic polynomial
\[
 D_b(T)=\prod_{\ell=1}^n(T-W_{b,\ell}),\qquad W_{b,\ell}\in I,
\]
with known degree $n\ge1$, and take detector logarithms in
$\cF_{q,A}$. Repeated roots are allowed for the exact statement.

\begin{theorem}[Action divisors]\label{thm:v84-divisors}
Suppose the pairs consisting of $D_b$ and the nonconstant detector
coefficients are distinct. With $q+k+4n+1$ distinct clocks, the
projective matrices determine the channels, the relative detector
coefficients and the reduced polynomial family
\[
 E_b=D_b/G,\qquad G=\gcd(D_1,\ldots,D_B),
\]
where all polynomials are monic. They also determine $\deg G$.
The complete polynomial ambiguity is to replace $G$ by any monic
polynomial $\widetilde G$ of the same degree with roots in $I$.
Consequently all action multisets are absolutely identifiable whenever
$G=1$. In addition there is the common additive detector gauge.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For two proposed log-ratios the moving logarithmic derivatives have
at most $4n$ distinct poles, with integer residues. Together with the
$k$ protected poles, the differentiated numerator has degree at most
$q+k+4n-1$. The stated samples force it to vanish identically. Its
protected residues recover the relative detector coefficients, and its
moving residues recover the reduced rational function $D_b/D_c$.
Monicity fixes the rational multiplicative constant. The same argument
with at most $2n$ moving poles proves joint spectral separation; equal
spectral trajectories would force equal polynomial and nonconstant
detector data. The component matrices are therefore recovered first.

Fix component $1$ and write the recovered ratios in reduced form.
Let $H$ be the monic least common multiple of their denominators.
At any root $w$, writing $m_b$ for its multiplicity in $D_b$, the
multiplicity in $H$ is
$\max_b(m_1-m_b)_+=m_1-\min_bm_b$. Hence $H=D_1/G$ and
$(D_b/D_1)H=D_b/G=E_b$. Since $n$ is known, $\deg G=n-\deg E_1$.
Every alternative family with the same ratios has these same reduced
polynomials, and so is $\widetilde G E_b$ with a common monic factor
of that degree. Conversely this replacement multiplies every branch
weight at each clock by $\widetilde G(T)/G(T)>0$ and leaves the
projective matrices unchanged. This proves completeness of the gauge.
\end{proof}
The theorem identifies unordered roots through their divisor, not a
smooth enumeration through a multiple-root collision. Smooth rootwise
stability requires the corresponding root-separation and fixed
multiplicity assumptions. For $n=1$ the condition $G=1$ is exactly
that not all actions coincide. The sharp $q+3$ polynomial clock count
uses the additional interval structure and improves the general bound.
