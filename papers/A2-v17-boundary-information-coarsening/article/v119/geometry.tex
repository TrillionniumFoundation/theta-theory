\begin{abstract}
We determine multiplication failure through its coherent cokernel, retaining nonreduced scheme structure. If a unital subbundle of a finite locally free algebra has codimension $r$, its products stabilize in degree $r+1$, over arbitrary $\mathbb Q$-algebras. In codimension two this gives equations for the entire failure scheme in degree three. Off the subalgebra locus, that scheme is an open relative Grassmannian over the rank-two quotient-algebra scheme. A proper incidence extends across the subalgebra locus, where its fibres are governed by rank-three conductor quotients and quadratic-algebra towers. For the connected algebras $\C[x,y]/(x^2,xy,y^h)$, $h\ge5$, we obtain local rings of the full multigenerator failure scheme with an explicit embedded primary component and nilradical index $2h-3$. These are non-Cartier schemes whose higher contact coefficients enter the defining ideal. For every zero-dimensional $Z\subset\mathbb P^e$, a regularity-controlled conductor theorem identifies the global and finite multiplication cokernels when $n\ge2\operatorname{reg}I_Z-1$, including finite flat families and arbitrary base change. We also retain the sharp curve comparison, the complete contact-hyperplane and pencil results, the determinant-power theorem for truncated local algebras, and the reduced three-plane diagonal theorem. Primary decompositions are asserted on their specified parameter spaces; they are not inferred for arbitrary specializations.
\end{abstract}
