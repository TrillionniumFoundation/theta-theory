\section{Three generators on a reduced contact divisor}
\label{sec:three-generators}
This theorem realizes Haiman's diagonal ideal as the stable Fitting
ideal of an actual multiplication map. The all-powers identity is
Haiman's input, not a theorem deduced here from multiplication.
The resulting global-section failure schemes and their coherent
cokernels are obtained using the conductor comparison.



\begin{theorem}[The complete stable three-plane ideal]
\label{thm:three-plane-primary}
Let $D=p_1+\cdots+p_d$ be reduced, $d\ge4$, and let $A$ vary on
the entire generating open $\Gr(3,H^0(\mathcal O_D))^\circ$.
On the smooth unit-frame cover write $A=\langle a,ax,ay\rangle$;
thus the matrix with rows $(1,x_i,y_i)$ has rank three. For $m\ge d-1$,
the zeroth Fitting ideal $J_m$ is independent of $m$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-plane-primary}
 J_m=\bigcap_{i<j}P_{ij},\qquad P_{ij}=(x_i-x_j,y_i-y_j).
\end{equation}
For every $q\ge1$ one has the stronger identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-plane-powers}
 J_m^q=\bigcap_{i<j}P_{ij}^q.
\end{equation}
These are complete primary decompositions, including all component
intersections. Thus $J_m$ is radical, all its components are smooth
of codimension two on this cover and on the fixed-$D$ Grassmannian,
and neither it nor any of its powers has embedded associated points.
The corank is $d$ minus the number of distinct pairs $(x_i,y_i)$.
For global binary subseries the same statements hold for $n\ge2d-3$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After dividing the target by $a^m$, the multiplication matrix has
as columns all monomials in $x,y$ of total degree at most $m$,
evaluated at the $d$ labelled points. Lemma~\ref{lem:polarized-truncation}
identifies, over the entire coefficient ring, the ideal of its
$d$-minors for $m\ge d-1$ with the ideal generated by determinants
of arbitrary monomial columns. Those determinants span the
alternating polynomials in the $d$ pairs of variables: antisymmetrize
a monomial and use linearity. Haiman's theorem
\cite[Corollary 3.8.3]{Haiman} states that the ideal generated by these
alternants has every power equal to the intersection of the
corresponding powers of the pair-diagonal ideals. It gives
\eqref{eq:three-plane-primary} and \eqref{eq:three-plane-powers}
before any localization; hence they hold on the rank-three open.
In that corollary Haiman's $n$ is our number $d$ of labelled points,
and Haiman's exponent $d$ is our power $q$. These two indices are
independent; no restriction on $q$ is imposed.
This use of the diagonal-ideal theorem is essential.

Each $P_{ij}$ is a smooth prime ideal of codimension two; its powers
are primary (use two normal coordinates in a regular local ring).
The intersections therefore have exactly the indicated associated
primes. Smooth faithful flat descent gives the fixed-$D$ assertions.
Polynomial functions separate any finite set of distinct affine
points, so their image dimension is the number of distinct pairs.
The truncation lemma gives the stated corank in this finite degree.
Finally apply Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-conductor} with $k=3$.
\end{proof}

The stable moving scheme over the reduced-divisor stratum is integral,
though it need not be smooth or unibranched. Its normalization marks
one equal pair and is smooth and finite. Indeed the ordered
configuration cover is etale and the permutation group is transitive
on pairs. Upstairs the reduced scheme is the union in
\eqref{eq:three-plane-primary}; its normalization is the disjoint
union of the smooth pair diagonals. This construction descends.
A point is smooth precisely when only one pair coincides; two or
more failed pairs give distinct local branches after etale base
change. No assertion about nonreduced divisors is inferred from this
etale calculation; those have their own quotient-algebra structure
in Section~\ref{sec:quotient-hilbert} when $k=d-1$.
