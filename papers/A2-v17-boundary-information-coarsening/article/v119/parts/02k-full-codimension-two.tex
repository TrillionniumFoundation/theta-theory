\section{The full codimension-two failure scheme}
\label{sec:full-codimension-two}
Fix a finite locally free commutative algebra $B/S$ of rank $d\ge4$
over a locally Noetherian $\C$-scheme. Let
\[
 X=\Gr_S(d-3,B/\mathcal O\cdot1),
\]
viewed as the Grassmannian of rank-$(d-2)$ subbundles $W\subset B$
containing $1$. This is a unital parameter space, not an open subset
of the Grassmannian of all generating planes. We explain the passage
to the latter at the end of the section.

Write $\mu_j:\Sym^j W\to B_X$, and let $D$ denote the stable
zeroth-Fitting failure scheme. Let $T\subset X$ denote the unital
subalgebra scheme. In general $T$ is a proper closed subscheme of
$D$; its ideal is the first, rather than the zeroth, Fitting ideal.

\begin{theorem}[Equations and the corank-one incidence]
\label{thm:full-codimension-two}
The stable scheme $D$ is defined already by $\mu_3$. On a splitting
$B_X=W\oplus M$, with $\rank M=2$, use the block notation of
\eqref{eq:compressed-commutator}. Let $\beta$ be the matrix with
columns $\beta_{w_i}(w_j)$, for a local basis $(w_i)$ of $W$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codim-two-matrix}
 \mathcal K=[\,\beta\mid C_{w_1}\beta\mid\cdots\mid C_{w_{d-2}}\beta\,].
\end{equation}
For every $m\ge3$ there are equalities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-codim-two-ideals}
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{coker}\mu_m&\simeq\operatorname{coker}\mathcal K,\\
 \mathcal I_D&=I_2(\mathcal K),\\
 \mathcal I_T&=I_1(\beta)=I_1(\mathcal K)
       =\operatorname{Fitt}_1\operatorname{coker}\mu_m.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The cokernel isomorphism uses the chosen splitting; the ideals glue
canonically. These are equations for the full schemes, not just
for their radicals or their exact-rank strata.

Let $H=\operatorname{Hilb}^2_S(B)$ be the scheme of locally free
rank-two quotient algebras. Over $H$, let $E_H\subset B_H$ be the
preimage of the scalar subalgebra in the universal quotient. Put
\[
 Z=\Gr_H(d-3,E_H/\mathcal O\cdot1),\qquad
 Z^\circ=\{W:W^2\longrightarrow E_H\text{ is onto}\}.
\]
Then the forgetful morphism induces an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codim-two-incidence}
 Z^\circ\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ D\setminus|T|.
\end{equation}
It holds on arbitrary test schemes, including nonreduced ones.
On this open the stable generated algebra is the hyperplane $E_H$,
and the normalized multiplication cokernel is its quotient line
$B/E_H$. The proper morphism $Z\to X$ factors through $D$ and is
surjective on geometric points of $D$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:codimension-stabilization} gives $W^3=\mathcal O[W]$.
By \eqref{eq:residual-filtration}, its residual image in $M$ is
$N_1=\operatorname{im}\mathcal K$. The identity columns supplied by
$W\subset W^3$ may be removed from a presentation of $B/W^3$.
This proves the first two assertions in
\eqref{eq:full-codim-two-ideals}. Every entry of $C_{w_i}\beta$ is
a linear combination of entries of $\beta$, so their entry ideals
are equal. Vanishing of these entries is exactly $W^2\subset W$,
which is the subalgebra functor. This proves the last line.

Work now where a column $v$ of $\beta$ is unimodular in $M$.
Its lift is a quadratic product modulo $W$, and
$E=W+\mathcal O v$ is a hyperplane subbundle of $B$. Pivot on the
unit coordinate of $v$. The two by two minors of $\mathcal K$
then say precisely that all its columns belong to $\mathcal O v$.
In the quotient ring by those minors, $W^2=E$ and $WE\subset E$.
Moreover $v\in W^2$, so multiplication by $v$ preserves $E$:
write $v$ as a sum of products of elements of $W$ and use $WE\subset E$
twice. Thus $E$ is a unital subalgebra. Equivalently its equality
with the stable image follows directly from
Theorem~\ref{thm:codimension-stabilization}.

The conductor equivalence of Lemma~\ref{lem:hyperplane-subalgebra}
associates to $E$ a unique rank-two quotient of $B$. It recovers a
point of $Z^\circ$. Conversely, a point of $Z^\circ$ has stable
image $E_H$, since $W^2=E_H$ and $E_H$ is an algebra. Its residual
multiplication rank is exactly one, so it factors through
$D\setminus|T|$. The two constructions are inverse on every test
scheme and are defined by split pivots and the hyperplane conductor.
They therefore glue to \eqref{eq:codim-two-incidence}, including
its nilpotent structure. The same description gives the cokernel line.

The relative Hilbert scheme is projective: it is the closed
ideal-quotient subscheme of the Grassmannian of rank-two module
quotients of $B$. Hence $Z\to X$ is proper. Every multiplication
image on $Z$ lies in the hyperplane algebra $E_H$, so its maximal
minors vanish and the map factors through $D$. Surjectivity away
from $T$ is already proved. Surjectivity on geometric points of
$T$ follows from the two fibre descriptions in
Proposition~\ref{prop:codim-two-incidence-fibres} below.
\end{proof}

The theorem gives both an equation for $D$ along $T$ and a complete
functorial normal form off $T$. In particular, it is not obtained
by replacing the ideal $I_2(\mathcal K)$ with the entry ideal
$I_1(\beta)$. Nor does it assert that the corank-one open is dense
in every component: for a particular algebra a component may be
contained in $T$.

\begin{proposition}[Fibres over the conductor strata]
\label{prop:codim-two-incidence-fibres}
Restrict $T$ to an exact-action-rank stratum and write
$J=\operatorname{cond}_B(W)$, $C=W/J$, and $Q=B/J$.
The fibre product $Z\times_X T$ has the following descriptions,
with arbitrary base change within the indicated stratum.

If the action rank is one, $C=\mathcal O$ and $Q$ has rank three.
The fibre product is $\operatorname{Hilb}^2(Q)$. More explicitly,
on $\Pj(Q/\mathcal O)$, in the convention of lines, it is the zero
scheme of the canonical binary cubic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic-extension}
 \ell^{\otimes3}\longrightarrow\bigwedge^2(Q/\mathcal O),
 \qquad v^{\otimes3}\longmapsto \bar v\wedge\overline{v^2}.
\end{equation}
The cubic may vanish identically; no relative degree-three flatness
is asserted without a nonvanishing hypothesis.

If the action rank is two, $C$ has rank two and $Q$ is a rank-two
$C$-algebra. The fibre product is canonically $\operatorname{Spec} C$,
a finite locally free double cover of the stratum, possibly nonreduced.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A point of the fibre product is a hyperplane algebra $E\subset B$
containing $W$. Since $J$ is an ideal of $B$ contained in $W$, it
is contained in the conductor of $E$. Thus the problem is exactly
to choose a unital hyperplane subalgebra of $Q$ containing $C$.
Conversely its inverse image supplies $E$.

In the first case this is the unital-hyperplane functor of a
rank-three algebra, hence $\operatorname{Hilb}^2(Q)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:hyperplane-subalgebra}. A line $\ell\subset Q/\mathcal O$
has an algebra as its preimage if and only if its square remains
in that preimage. This is exactly \eqref{eq:cubic-extension}.
Changing a lift $v$ by a scalar changes $v^2$ by a scalar and a
multiple of $v$, so the displayed wedge is independent of the lift.
This also proves its scheme-theoretic meaning over a nonreduced base.

In the second case put $M=Q/C$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:relative-cyclic-vector}, $M$ is locally an invertible
$C$-module. A hyperplane extension gives a $C$-stable subbundle
$\ell\subset M$ of rank one over the original base. The induced
action on the quotient line $M/\ell$ is a character
$C\to\mathcal O$. Conversely such a character, with kernel $I$,
gives $\ell=IM$; it is a subbundle and the quotient is a line.
These constructions are inverse, as can be checked after trivializing
$M$ over $C$. They are the functor of $\operatorname{Spec} C$.
There is no additional multiplication equation. Indeed locally
write $Q=C\oplus Cz$, with $z^2=\alpha+\gamma z$, $\alpha,\gamma\in C$.
The extension is $C\oplus Iz$, which is an algebra because $I$ is
an ideal of $C$. This proves the assertion over every test scheme.

For completeness both fibres are nonempty over an algebraically
closed field. A rank-three commutative algebra has a quotient of
length two, by an ideal composition series; a rank-two algebra has
a character. This proves the geometric surjectivity used above.
\end{proof}

For example, the rank-three algebra $\C^3$ gives three points in
\eqref{eq:cubic-extension}, whereas $\C[u,v]/(u,v)^2$ gives the
whole projective line. Mere invariance under the scalar action
would incorrectly give a projective line in both cases. The square
condition in \eqref{eq:cubic-extension} is essential. In contrast,
on the rank-two action stratum it follows from the quadratic-algebra
tower. The conductor stratification thus controls the exceptional
fibres of one proper incidence over the full failure scheme.
We do not identify this morphism with a normalization or a blow-up.

\begin{remark}[Unit covers and unrestricted generating planes]
\label{rem:codim-two-unit-cover}
Let $G=\Gr_S(d-2,B)^\circ$ be the full generating Grassmannian,
and let $\widetilde G$ parametrize pairs $(A,a)$ with $a\in A\cap B^\times$.
The morphism $\widetilde G\to G$ is smooth and surjective, and
\[
 \widetilde G\simeq B^\times\times_S X,
 \qquad (A,a)\longmapsto(a,a^{-1}A).
\]
Multiplication by $a^{-m}$ identifies the pulled-back cokernel with
the unital one. All the ideal identities above therefore give the
full failure scheme on this smooth cover of $G$ and descend.
Over $\C$, local primary structures discussed below may equally be
read on this cover; the assertion is not that a special linear
slice of $G$ determines its ambient scheme structure.
\end{remark}
