\section{Generating hyperplanes and length-two quotients}
\label{sec:quotient-hilbert}
We now solve a multigenerator problem without assuming that the finite
algebra is monogenic, reduced, or constant. If $B$ is a finite locally
free commutative algebra of rank $d$ over a scheme $S$, let
$\operatorname{Hilb}^2(B/S)$ denote the functor of quotient algebras
$B_T\twoheadrightarrow Q$ that are locally free of rank two over $T$.
It is the relative Hilbert scheme of two points of $\operatorname{Spec}_S B$.
A generating line in $Q$ means a line subbundle whose $Q$-span is $Q$.

\begin{lemma}[The conductor of a unital hyperplane]\label{lem:hyperplane-subalgebra}
Unital subalgebras $S_0\subset B_T$ which are hyperplane subbundles
are naturally equivalent to rank-two quotient algebras of $B_T$.
The maps are
\[
 S_0\longmapsto B_T/J,\quad J=\{b\in B_T:bB_T\subset S_0\},
 \qquad
 (B_T\to Q)\longmapsto\operatorname{preimage}(\mathcal O_T\cdot1).
\]
The conductor $J$ is an ideal subbundle of rank $d-2$, and its
formation commutes with arbitrary base change.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work locally with $T=\operatorname{Spec}R$, trivialize $B/S_0$, and
write it as the target of $\phi:B\to R$. Choose $e$ with $\phi(e)=1$.
Since $1\in S_0$, the bilinear form $\beta(b,c)=\phi(bc)$ has
$\beta|_{S_0\times S_0}=0$ and $\beta(1,e)=1$.
An element of its radical first lies in $S_0$, by pairing with $1$;
it then lies in the kernel of the split surjection
\[
 S_0\longrightarrow R,\qquad s\longmapsto\phi(se),
\]
which sends $1$ to $1$. Thus the radical has rank $d-2$, and
$B/J$ has basis the classes of $1,e$. Associativity shows that the
radical is an ideal: $\beta(jb,c)=\beta(j,bc)=0$. It is precisely
the displayed conductor. This split-kernel description proves base
change and gives $S_0/J=R\cdot1$.

Conversely, in a rank-two locally free algebra $Q$, the unit is a
unimodular vector, so $Q/R\cdot1$ is a line bundle. The inverse
image of the scalars is a unital hyperplane subalgebra. No nonzero
ideal of $Q$ can be contained in $R\cdot1$: in a basis $1,e$, if
$c\cdot1$ belongs to such an ideal, then $ce$ must be scalar, so
$c=0$. Its conductor is therefore exactly the original kernel.
The two constructions are inverse and are independent of all
trivializations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The universal hyperplane failure scheme]\label{thm:universal-hyperplane}
Let $B/S$ be finite locally free of rank $d\ge3$ over a
$\C$-scheme. On $X=\Gr_S(d-1,B)^\circ$, the generating open,
let $\mathcal A$ be the tautological hyperplane and put
\[
 \mathcal C_m=\operatorname{coker}(\Sym^m\mathcal A\to B),
 \qquad Y_m=V(\operatorname{Fitt}_0\mathcal C_m).
\]
For every $m\ge2$ the ideal of $Y_m$ is the same. The common scheme
$Y$ is naturally isomorphic to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-hyperplane-incidence}
 \{(B\twoheadrightarrow Q,\ell):\ \rank Q=2,
                 \ \ell\subset Q\text{ a generating line}\}.
\end{equation}
The map to $X$ sends $(Q,\ell)$ to the inverse image of $\ell$.
If $i:Y\hookrightarrow X$ and $B_Y\twoheadrightarrow Q\supset\ell$
is the universal quotient with its generating line, the canonical formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyperplane-canonical-line}
 \mathcal C_m\simeq i_*(Q/\ell^{(m)}),\qquad
 \ell^{(m)}=\operatorname{im}(\ell^{\otimes m}\longrightarrow Q).
\end{equation}
Both $\ell^{(m)}$ and its quotient are line bundles on $Y$.
In particular all positive-index Fitting ideals are the unit ideal. The isomorphism of schemes and all these assertions commute
with arbitrary base change. The same statement holds for an
invertible $B$-module in place of $B$, with target its $m$th tensor
power.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Locally for the smooth topology, choose a unit $a\in\mathcal A$.
Such choices cover $X$: on each geometric fibre avoid the finitely
many evaluation hyperplanes. Replace $\mathcal A$ by
$A'=a^{-1}\mathcal A$, and the target by multiplication by $a^{-m}$.
Now $1\in A'$. Split $B=A'\oplus Re$ locally. Since $(A')^m$
contains $A'$, elementary row and column operations present the
cokernel by a single row. For $m=2$ its entries are exactly the
coefficients of the products $ss'$ in $B/A'$, for $s,s'\in A'$.
Call their ideal $I$. Thus $Y_2$ represents the condition that $A'$
is a subalgebra, with its full scheme structure, not just its support.
Modulo $I$ all higher products lie in $A'$. Conversely every
quadratic product is an $m$-fold product by inserting $1$'s.
Hence the residual ideals equal $I$ for every $m\ge2$, and the
cokernel is locally $R/I$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:hyperplane-subalgebra} supplies the unique rank-two
quotient on $Y$. Its kernel $J$ also lies in $\mathcal A=aA'$,
since $J$ is an ideal and $aJ=J$. The image of $\mathcal A$ is a
generating line in $Q$. The construction does not depend on $a$.
Indeed another unit $b\in\mathcal A$ gives $b/a\in A'$, and its
inverse belongs to $A'$ by Cayley--Hamilton in $B$ (the constant
coefficient is a unit). Thus $(b/a)^{-1}A'=A'$ and the conductor
is unchanged. This also proves descent from the smooth cover.
Conversely the inverse image of a generating line in a rank-two
quotient becomes the inverse image of the scalars after division
by a unit, so it satisfies precisely the residual equations.
These constructions are inverse on every test scheme, proving the
scheme isomorphism. On $Y$, the normalized subalgebra $A'$ has conductor $J$, and
$J\subset a^m A'=(\mathcal A)^m$ since $a$ is a unit and $J$ an ideal.
Passing to $Q$ identifies $(\mathcal A)^m/J$ with $\ell^{(m)}$.
This gives the canonical map in \eqref{eq:hyperplane-canonical-line};
the local cyclic presentations identify it as an isomorphism.
Multiplication by a local unit spanning $\ell$ makes $\ell^{(m)}$
a direct-summand line, so the quotient is indeed a line bundle.
For an invertible $B$-module $P$, put $P_Q=P\otimes_B Q$; the same
formula is $i_*(P_Q^{\otimes_Q m}/\ell^{(m)})$, where now
$\ell\subset P_Q$. Trivialization of $P$ gives the local proof and
this intrinsic expression supplies its gluing.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Every fixed multiplicity partition}
For $r\ge2$ define polynomials
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grass-recurrence}
 F_0=0,\quad F_1=1,\quad F_2=u,\qquad
 F_{j+1}=uF_j+vF_{j-1},\qquad
 G_r=\C[u,v]/(F_r,F_{r+1}).
\end{equation}
For positive $r,s$ put $H_{r,s}=\C[x,y]/(x^r,y^s)$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete primary schemes for contact hyperplanes]
\label{thm:contact-hyperplane-primary}
Let $D=\sum_i d_i p_i$ have length $d\ge3$. Consider the entire
generating open $\Gr(d-1,H^0(\mathcal O_D))^\circ$.
For every $m\ge2$, its multiplication failure scheme is the disjoint
union
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyperplane-primary-disjoint}
 Y_D\simeq
 \coprod_{i:d_i\ge2}\bigl(\mathbb A^1\times\operatorname{Spec}G_{d_i}\bigr)
 \amalg\coprod_{i<j}\bigl(\mathbb G_m\times\operatorname{Spec}H_{d_i,d_j}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The identifications use local coordinates on $D$ and trivializations
of the restricted line bundle. Each displayed summand is a primary
component on this fixed-divisor parameter space. There are no
embedded associated points. All reduced components are smooth and
pairwise disjoint. Their transverse lengths and exact nilradical
indices are, respectively,
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{length-two support}&\text{length}&\text{nilpotency index}\\ \hline
 2p_i&\binom{d_i}{2}&2d_i-3\\
 p_i+p_j&d_i d_j&d_i+d_j-1.
 \end{array}
\]
The convention for a reduced component is index one. Every failure
fibre has cokernel dimension one. On binary inverse-image subseries
these are exactly the multiplication failure schemes whenever
$n\ge d+1$; on curves they are the same schemes under
Theorem~\ref{thm:curve-conductor}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A degree-two quotient of the product algebra
$\prod_i\C[z_i]/(z_i^{d_i})$ decomposes according to the ranks of its
idempotent factors. These ranks are locally constant even on a
nonreduced base. Its possibilities are rank two at a single factor,
or rank one at each of two distinct factors. This is a decomposition
into open and closed Hilbert subschemes, not merely a decomposition
of closed points.

For rank two in $\C[z]/(z^r)$, the elements $1,z$ form a basis in
every geometric quotient, hence on every family by Nakayama. Write
$z^2=uz+v$. Then
\[
 z^j=F_j(u,v)z+vF_{j-1}(u,v).
\]
The relation $z^r=0$ is equivalent to
$F_r=vF_{r-1}=0$, equivalently $F_r=F_{r+1}=0$. Thus this Hilbert
scheme is $\operatorname{Spec}G_r$. A rank-one quotient at a factor
of length $r$ is $\operatorname{Spec}\C[x]/(x^r)$, so the two-factor
case is $\operatorname{Spec}H_{r,s}$. A generating line in the first
rank-two algebra is uniquely spanned by $1+\tau z$; in the split
two-factor algebra it is uniquely spanned by $(1,t)$, with $t$ a
unit. Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-hyperplane} now proves
\eqref{eq:hyperplane-primary-disjoint} over arbitrary test schemes.

The algebra $G_r$ is Artin local: if $v\ne0$, two consecutive zero
values in the recurrence force $F_1=0$, and if $v=0$ they force
$u=0$. The two equations form a regular sequence in $\C[u,v]$.
With $\deg u=1$, $\deg v=2$, its Hilbert series is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grass-hilbert}
 \frac{(1-t^{r-1})(1-t^r)}{(1-t)(1-t^2)}.
\end{equation}
It has length $\binom r2$ and weighted socle degree $2r-4$.
To see the exact ordinary nilpotency, identify $u$ and $-v$ with
the elementary symmetric functions in two variables. Then
$F_j=h_{j-1}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grass-ring}
 G_r\simeq H^{2*}(\Gr(2,r),\C),
\end{equation}
where the cohomological degree is divided by two. This is the
classical presentation by $h_{r-1},h_r$, not a new presentation of
Grassmannian cohomology; the rectangular Schur basis is recalled in
\cite[Theorem 2.7 and the following discussion]{Grinberg}.
Successive multiplication by $u=s_{(1)}$ adds one box. The only
partition of size $2r-4$ in the $2$ by $r-2$ rectangle is
$(r-2,r-2)$; the ballot-path count of its standard tableaux is
$\frac1{r-1}\binom{2r-4}{r-2}$. Consequently
\[
 u^{2r-4}=\frac1{r-1}\binom{2r-4}{r-2}(-v)^{r-2}\ne0.
\]
Every monomial of ordinary degree $2r-3$ has weighted degree at
least $2r-3$ and vanishes. This proves the index $2r-3$, including
$r=2$. In $H_{r,s}$ the top monomial is $x^{r-1}y^{s-1}$, giving
length $rs$ and index $r+s-1$.

Each summand is a complete intersection with reduced support a
smooth integral curve, so it is primary and has no embedded points.
Equivalently, over a local Artin coefficient algebra, a polynomial
with nonzero reduction is a nonzerodivisor, as one checks on the
successive powers of the maximal ideal. The same holds after
inverting $t$. This proves the primary assertions also globally on
the indicated affine curves. The cokernel assertion follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-hyperplane}. The two transports follow
from Theorems~\ref{thm:sharp-conductor} and~\ref{thm:curve-conductor}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The smooth moving scheme]\label{thm:moving-hyperplane}
Let $d\ge3$. On the relative generating Grassmannian of $(d-1)$-planes over
$\operatorname{Hilb}^d(C)$, for a smooth connected projective curve $C$, the finite-contact
failure scheme is a generating open of a projective-line bundle over
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}^2 C\times\operatorname{Sym}^{d-2} C,
 \qquad (Z,Q)\longmapsto D=Z+Q.
\]
It is smooth, irreducible, and of dimension $d+1$. Its projection
to $\operatorname{Sym}^d C$ is flat of relative dimension one.
The finite map of divisor spaces has degree $\binom d2$; the primary
lengths in Theorem~\ref{thm:contact-hyperplane-primary} sum to this
degree on every fixed fibre. Under either conductor theorem, these
are statements about the original global multiplication scheme.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A nested pair $Z\le D$ of effective divisors on a smooth curve is
uniquely $D=Z+Q$. This holds in families: a finite flat subscheme
of a smooth relative curve is a relative Cartier divisor, and
inclusions of their invertible ideals give the residual divisor.
The incidence in Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-hyperplane} is therefore
exactly the following canonical open projective bundle.
On $T=\operatorname{Sym}^2 C\times\operatorname{Sym}^{d-2} C$
let $\mathcal Z\subset C\times T$ be the universal degree-two divisor
and set $F=(\pi_{\mathcal Z})_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z}$.
Use the projectivization $\Pj_T^{\rm lines}(F)$ parametrizing line
subbundles of $F$, and retain the open where the tautological line
$\ell$ satisfies $F\ell=F$. This is the claimed bundle, not an
unspecified local form. For a restricted line bundle replace $F$
by $(\pi_{\mathcal Z})_*(L|_{\mathcal Z})$ and use generation as an
$F$-module. The open is nonempty on every
fibre of its smooth irreducible base, and hence is smooth and
irreducible of dimension $d+1$.

The map of divisor spaces is finite (proper with finite geometric
fibres). Both spaces are smooth of dimension $d$; the finite source
is Cohen--Macaulay and the target regular, so the map is flat.
Over a reduced divisor it has $\binom d2$ points. This proves the
degree and flatness assertions; alternatively, the fibre lengths
computed above give $\sum_i\binom{d_i}2+\sum_{i<j}d_id_j=\binom d2$.
The open projective bundle is smooth of relative dimension one,
so its composite projection is flat of that relative dimension.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A direct residual presentation}
For comparison with the multiplication matrix, normalize a unit and
write, on the unital hyperplane chart in $\C[z]/(z^r)$,
\[
 A'=\langle1,z^i-c_i z:2\le i\le r-1\rangle.
\]
Put $c_1=1$ and $c_j=0$ for $j\ge r$. After eliminating the pivot
columns, the remaining entries of the quadratic matrix are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyperplane-residual-generators}
 c_{i+j}-c_i c_{j+1}-c_j c_{i+1}+c_2c_ic_j\quad(2\le i\le j\le r-1).
\end{equation}
For $r\ge4$, the relations with $i=2$ give
$c_{j+2}=u c_{j+1}+v c_j$, with $u=c_2$, $v=c_3-c_2^2$.
They eliminate $c_4,\ldots,c_{r-1}$ and leave $F_r,F_{r+1}$.
Every other relation follows from the recurrence identity
\[
 F_{i+j}=F_iF_{j+1}+F_jF_{i+1}-uF_iF_j,
\]
proved by induction in $j$. For $r=3$ the single residual entry is
$c_2^3$, agreeing with $G_3\simeq\C[u]/(u^3)$.
For $r=4$ one obtains
\[
 G_4=\C[u,v]/(u^3+2uv,\ u^2v+v^2),
 \qquad \operatorname{length}G_4=6,\quad\mathfrak m^5=0\ne\mathfrak m^4.
\]
Thus the multigenerator transverse scheme is not a powered smooth
divisor, even though its length equals the corresponding pencil
weight. The smooth moving scheme has these nonreduced fibres;
nonreducedness of a fibre is not an embedded component of the total
scheme. Nor does this determine the embedded primary ideal along
the evaluation curve in the unrestricted, non-generating hyperplane
problem retained in the complementary material.

\subsection{A noncurvilinear algebra and an embedded component}
The general finite-algebra theorem is not restricted to the primary
patterns arising from divisors on a smooth curve.

\begin{proposition}[An exact embedded chart]\label{prop:noncurvilinear-embedded}
Let $B=\C[x,y]/(x^2,y^2)$. On the unit-normalized hyperplane chart
\[
 A'=\langle1,y-a x,xy-bx\rangle\subset B\otimes\C[a,b],
\]
the multiplication failure ideal, for every $m\ge2$, is
\[
 I=(ab,b^2)=(b)\cap(a,b)^2.
\]
Its associated primes are $(b)$ and $(a,b)$. Its nilradical is a
one-dimensional square-zero module supported at the embedded point.
These equations give the pullback of the full failure scheme on
the generating Grassmannian, not only its reduced support.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the quotient functional $\phi(1)=0$, $\phi(x)=1$,
$\phi(y)=a$, $\phi(xy)=b$. Products involving $1$ are pivots.
The other quadratic products have residual values $-2ab,-b^2,0$.
This gives $I$; Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-hyperplane} gives every
higher degree. The displayed primary decomposition follows by
intersecting the monomial ideals. The nilradical is generated by
$b$, whose annihilator modulo $I$ is $(a,b)$. The normalization by
a unit is the same smooth-cover calculation used in that theorem.
In particular adding the freely chosen unit does not remove the
embedded associated prime.
\end{proof}

Thus the quotient description distinguishes smooth moving contact
schemes from finite-algebra families with embedded structure. The
absence of embedded points in Theorem~\ref{thm:contact-hyperplane-primary}
is a consequence of the curvilinear contact algebra and its explicit
Hilbert schemes, not a general assertion about finite algebras.
