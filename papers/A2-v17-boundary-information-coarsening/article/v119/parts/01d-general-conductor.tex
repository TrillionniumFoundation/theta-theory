\section{Conductor transport for arbitrary finite projective schemes}
\label{sec:general-conductor}
The finite-algebra results apply to arbitrary zero-dimensional
projective schemes once the conductor kernel is filled. This can
be done by a normal-layer argument in which Castelnuovo--Mumford
regularity replaces the monomial calculation for a homogeneous fat
point. We distinguish the regularity of a homogeneous ideal from
that of its associated sheaf; the distinction is essential for its
ordinary square.

\begin{theorem}[Regularity-controlled conductor transport]
\label{thm:regularity-conductor}
Let $Z\subset\Pj^e_\C$, $e\ge1$, be a nonempty zero-dimensional
closed subscheme, and let $I\subset R=\C[X_0,\ldots,X_e]$ be its
saturated homogeneous ideal. Put
\[
 r=\operatorname{reg} I,\qquad s=\operatorname{reg}(I^2),
\]
where $I^2$ is the ordinary homogeneous ideal square. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-conductor-bound}
 n\ge\max\{r,s-1\},
\end{equation}
then every generating subspace $A\subset H^0(Z,\mathcal O_Z(n))$,
with $U=\operatorname{res}_Z^{-1}A$ and
$K_t=H^0(\Pj^e,\mathcal I_Z(t))$, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-kernel-fill}
 K_n U^{m-1}=K_{mn}\qquad(m\ge2).
\end{equation}
Consequently restriction induces a canonical isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-conductor-cokernel}
 \operatorname{coker}\bigl(\Sym^m\mathcal U\to
               H^0(\Pj^e,\mathcal O(mn))\otimes\mathcal O\bigr)
 \simeq
 \operatorname{coker}\bigl(\Sym^m\mathcal A\to
               H^0(Z,\mathcal O_Z(mn))\otimes\mathcal O\bigr)
\end{equation}
on the entire generating Grassmannian, for every $m\ge2$.
In particular all Fitting ideals agree.

The simpler sufficient range
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twice-regularity}
 n\ge2\operatorname{reg}I-1
\end{equation}
always implies \eqref{eq:general-conductor-bound}. It has no
restriction on the local embedding dimension, the support partition,
or a monomial presentation of $Z$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We use the following classical regularity facts. An ideal of
regularity $r$ is generated in degrees at most $r$. If an ideal
has regularity $s$, then it agrees with its saturation in degrees
at least $s$, and the first cohomology of its associated sheaf
vanishes in degrees at least $s-1$; see
\cite[Theorem 1.3]{Sidman}. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sidman-square}
 \operatorname{reg}(I^2)\le2\operatorname{reg}I
\end{equation}
for a zero-dimensional projective scheme, by
\cite[Theorem 1.8]{Sidman}. This is a bound for the ordinary ideal
square, not merely its sheafification. It proves the implication
from \eqref{eq:twice-regularity}. We prove the sharper assertion
under \eqref{eq:general-conductor-bound}.

Choose $g\in U$ whose restriction generates $\mathcal O_Z(n)$
as an invertible $\mathcal O_Z$-module. Such a section exists by
Lemma~\ref{lem:generating-open}. Let $t\ge n$. Since $t\ge s-1$,
the conormal sequence gives a surjection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conormal-surjection}
 K_t\longrightarrow
 H^0\bigl(Z,(\mathcal I_Z/\mathcal I_Z^2)(t)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Multiplication by $g$ is an isomorphism between the conormal twists
$t$ and $t+n$, since its restriction is a unit after trivializing
$\mathcal O_Z(n)$. Thus, for any $f\in K_{t+n}$, one can choose
$q\in K_t$ for which $f-gq$ lies in
$H^0(\Pj^e,\mathcal I_Z^2(t+n))$.

The ideal $I$ is generated in degrees at most $r$, and $n,t\ge r$.
Factoring coefficient monomials between two chosen ideal generators
therefore gives the elementary homogeneous equality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinary-square-components}
 (I^2)_{t+n}=I_n I_t.
\end{equation}
Indeed for generators of degrees $a,b\le r$, a coefficient monomial
of degree $t+n-a-b$ can be split into factors of degrees $n-a$ and
$t-b$. This proves both containments. Since $s\ge2$ and
$n\ge s-1$, one has $t+n\ge s$. Agreement with saturation in that
degree identifies the preceding sheaf section space with
$(I^2)_{t+n}$. Also $K_t=I_t$, because $I$ is saturated. We have proved
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-layer-fill}
 K_{t+n}=gK_t+K_nK_t=UK_t.
\end{equation}
The first equality means equality of the sum with the whole kernel;
the last follows from $g,K_n\subset U$, with the reverse inclusion
clear from vanishing on $Z$. Iterating for $t=n,2n,\ldots$ proves
\eqref{eq:general-kernel-fill}.

Restriction in degrees $n$ and $mn$ is surjective by regularity.
The restriction sequence $0\to K_n\to U\to A\to0$ is locally
split on the parameter Grassmannian. The kernel on symmetric powers
is generated by $K_n\otimes\Sym^{m-1}U$, whose multiplication image
is the full target restriction kernel by
\eqref{eq:general-kernel-fill}. Taking cokernels of the restriction
square proves \eqref{eq:general-conductor-cokernel}. Its map is
restriction itself, so the local choices of $g$ disappear from the
result. The relative version below supplies, in particular, the
asserted scheme-level statement over this whole Grassmannian.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Relative finite schemes and base change]
\label{thm:relative-regularity-conductor}
Let $S$ be a locally Noetherian $\C$-scheme and
$\mathcal Z\subset\Pj^e_S$ a finite locally free family. Suppose the
saturated homogeneous ideal $I_z$ of every geometric fibre has
regularity at most $r$. For $n\ge2r-1$, the kernel identity and
cokernel comparison of Theorem~\ref{thm:regularity-conductor} hold
on each relative generating Grassmannian over $S$ and commute with
arbitrary base change. The same conclusion holds under uniform
bounds $\operatorname{reg}I_z\le r$, $\operatorname{reg}(I_z^2)\le s$
and $n\ge\max\{r,s-1\}$.

No hypothesis that formation of a conductor ideal or of a primary
decomposition commutes with specialization is used. The assertion
is about the actual coherent multiplication cokernels.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $p:\Pj^e_S\to S$. For $t\ge n$, regularity on geometric fibres
and cohomology and base change give split exact sequences locally
on $S$,
\[
 0\longrightarrow p_*\mathcal I_{\mathcal Z}(t)
 \longrightarrow p_*\mathcal O(t)
 \longrightarrow p_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z}(t)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Their terms are vector bundles and their formation commutes with
base change. One may also obtain this directly from fibrewise
surjectivity of restriction, the local freeness of its two outer
bundles, and Nakayama's lemma. The resulting kernel has the required
fibre by the exact sequence on each fibre.

Over the relative Grassmannian, form the multiplication morphism
\[
 p_*\mathcal I_{\mathcal Z}(n)\otimes\Sym^{m-1}\mathcal U
       \longrightarrow p_*\mathcal I_{\mathcal Z}(mn).
\]
It is surjective on every geometric fibre by the preceding theorem,
using \eqref{eq:sidman-square} if only the bound $r$ is specified.
Its coherent cokernel therefore vanishes by Nakayama's lemma.
The same symmetric-power restriction square proves the relative
cokernel isomorphism. The restriction bundles and their split
sequences pull back, and a surjective morphism remains surjective
after every pullback. Hence the construction proves arbitrary
base change, not just flat base change. Fitting ideals commute with
base change for finitely presented modules. This argument does not
require flatness of the first infinitesimal neighbourhood of
$\mathcal Z$ or base change for a selected primary component.
\end{proof}

The bound recovers the fat-point range $n\ge2h-1$, since the
homogeneous ideal of an order-$h$ fat point has regularity $h$.
The direct triangular proof in Theorem~\ref{thm:fat-conductor} is
retained, as is its sharp boundary example. The present proof also
applies to nonmonomial local schemes, unions with unequal local
lengths, and finite flat deformations with a uniform regularity
bound. A uniform bound is available on a fixed projective Hilbert
scheme by boundedness; sharpness of $2r-1$ is not asserted for each
individual finite scheme or each generating rank.

\begin{corollary}[Global embedded multiplication failure]
\label{cor:global-embedded-multigenerator}
Let $h\ge5$ and let $Z_h\subset\Pj^2$ have saturated ideal
\[
 I=(X^2,XY,Y^h)\subset\C[T,X,Y].
\]
For every $n\ge2h-1$, take all inverse-image series of generating
$(h-1)$-planes on $Z_h$. In every degree $m\ge3$, their global
multiplication failure scheme has, on a smooth unit cover near the
plane \eqref{eq:fork-plane}, the local primary description
\eqref{eq:fork-local-ring}--\eqref{eq:fork-primary}. It has the
embedded component and exact nilradical index stated there.
The coherent cokernel comparison persists in any finite flat
family with the stated regularity bound.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The variable $T$ is a nonzerodivisor on $\C[T,X,Y]/I$, so $I$ is
saturated and the scheme is supported at $[1:0:0]$. Its affine
algebra is $B_h$. The standard monomials in $\C[X,Y]/I$ are
$1,X,Y,\ldots,Y^{h-1}$, and its top degree is $h-1$; thus
$\operatorname{reg}I=h$. For comparison, the minimal monomial
generators of $I^2$ are
\[
 X^4,\ X^3Y,\ X^2Y^2,\ XY^{h+1},\ Y^{2h},
\]
and its top quotient degree is $2h-1$, so
$\operatorname{reg}(I^2)=2h$. The conductor theorem applies at
$n\ge2h-1$. Trivialize $\mathcal O_{Z_h}(n)$ by $T^n$ and apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:embedded-multigenerator} and the unit-cover
construction. The equality of coherent cokernels gives the same
Fitting scheme globally. The final assertion is the relative
theorem, not a claim that the displayed embedded primary ideal
persists unchanged under every deformation.
\end{proof}
