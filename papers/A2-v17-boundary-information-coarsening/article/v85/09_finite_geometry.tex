\section{Finite selective records determine a metric with a certificate}
\label{sec:v85-finite-geometry}
We now join the statistical experiment to the geometric inverse. The
observations are finitely many categorical readout pairs, not exact
boundary distances at a continuum of endpoints. The reference delay
may vary with the endpoint pair. Neither its values nor the detector
coefficients nor either readout channel are calibrated.

\subsection{The geometric stability class}
Fix a compact $d$-dimensional manifold $M$, $d\ge2$, with identified
smooth boundary and a fixed coordinate atlas. We use the following
precise geometric input from Stefanov--Uhlmann~\cite[Theorem 5]{V85SU}.
For every $0<\mu<1$ there is an integer $k$ such that, near a simple
metric $g_0$ with s-injective geodesic ray transform, metrics with a
fixed $C^k$ bound satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-SU}
 \|g'-\psi^*g\|_{C^2(M)}
       \le C_{\rm geom}\|d_g-d_{g'}\|_{C^0(\partial M\times\partial M)}^\mu
\end{equation}
for some diffeomorphism $\psi$ fixing the boundary. Here proximity and
the constant have exactly the meaning of that theorem: $g,g'$ are in
its $C^0$ neighbourhood of $g_0$ and have a prescribed $C^k$ bound.
S-injectivity means that the kernel of the ray transform on symmetric
two-tensors consists of symmetric differentials of vector fields
vanishing at the boundary. Analytic simple metrics are examples by
\cite[Theorem 1]{V85SU}.

Fix a compact subset $\mathcal G$ of this neighbourhood in the $C^k$
topology, consisting of simple metrics with the same bounds. One may,
for example, take the $C^k$ closure of a sufficiently small smooth
$C^{k+1}$-bounded family near $g_0$, with uniform ellipticity and a
smaller $C^k$ neighbourhood ensuring simplicity. Fix a smooth boundary
metric with distance $\rho_\partial$ and use the product distance
\[
 \rho((x,y),(x',y'))=\rho_\partial(x,x')+\rho_\partial(y,y').
\]
Choose $L,D_0$ such that for every $g\in\mathcal G$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-distance-bounds}
 \sup d_g<D_0,\qquad
 |d_g(z)-d_g(z')|\le L\rho(z,z').
\end{equation}
Such constants follow from a uniform upper bound on the boundary
metric and the triangle inequality for $d_g$. These are geometric
prior bounds, not estimates of unobserved detector derivatives.
The pullback in \eqref{eq:v85-SU} is indispensable; we do not identify
different coordinate representatives as equal tensors.

Let $\Lambda_h\subset\partial M$ be a finite $h/2$-net. Acquire
records at all ordered distinct pairs in $\Lambda_h^2$, denoted
$z_1,\ldots,z_{m_h}$. At equal pairs the boundary distance is exactly
zero and requires no acquisition. These pairs together form an $h$-net
for the product distance. At each acquired pair use the two-arm model
\eqref{eq:v85-forward}, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-geometric-arms}
                x_i=d_g(z_i),\qquad \tau_i\in[\tau_-,\tau_+].
\end{equation}
The remaining parameters are arbitrary members of their factors in
\eqref{eq:v85-compact}. In particular no continuity of the unknown
channels, detector coefficients, or reference delays between acquired
pairs is required. The ordering and bounds in
\eqref{eq:v85-apparatus} remain known architectural information.

\begin{theorem}[Finite noisy geometric rigidity]
\label{thm:v85-geometric}
In this experiment, form the action intervals of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v85-confidence}, or its finite-grid implementation,
with $m=m_h$. Let $w_h$ be their largest width, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-metric-set}
 \widehat{\mathcal G}=
   \{g'\in\mathcal G:a_i^-\le d_{g'}(z_i)\le a_i^+,
                                                \ 1\le i\le m_h\}.
\end{equation}
With probability at least $1-\zeta$, this is a nonempty compact set
containing the true metric. Every two of its members satisfy, for
some boundary-fixing diffeomorphism $\psi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-metric-certificate}
           \|g'-\psi^*g\|_{C^2(M)}
                  \le C_{\rm geom}(w_h+2Lh)^\mu.
\end{equation}
The number $w_h$ is obtained from the records; no inverse Lipschitz
constant or channel signal floor is needed to produce it.

If the true channels at every acquired pair belong to one regular
class \eqref{eq:v85-regular-class}, then, for sufficiently large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-metric-rate}
 \|\widehat g-\psi^*g\|_{C^2(M)}
 \le C\left\{h+
       \left(\frac{\log(2m_hJuv/\zeta)}{n}\right)^{1/2}\right\}^{\mu}
\end{equation}
for every $\widehat g\in\widehat{\mathcal G}$ on the same event.
For finite-grid action intervals assume $\delta\le\eta_n$.
The constants may depend on $\mathcal G,\mu$, the apparatus bounds,
clocks, and the true regular class; the confidence procedure does not
need the last dependence as input.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Simultaneous action coverage puts the true $g$ in
\eqref{eq:v85-metric-set}. The map $g\mapsto d_g$ is continuous on
the uniformly elliptic compact class; this also follows by comparing
lengths when two metrics are uniformly close. Consequently the
inequalities defining \eqref{eq:v85-metric-set} are closed, and the
set is compact.

For two feasible metrics the boundary distances differ by at most
$w_h$ at each observed pair, and agree at every diagonal pair.
For arbitrary $z\in\partial M\times\partial M$, choose
$z_0\in\Lambda_h^2$ with $\rho(z,z_0)\le h$. Twice using
\eqref{eq:v85-distance-bounds} gives
\[
 |d_g(z)-d_{g'}(z)|
 \le |d_g(z)-d_g(z_0)|+|d_g(z_0)-d_{g'}(z_0)|
                       +|d_{g'}(z_0)-d_{g'}(z)|
 \le w_h+2Lh.
\]
This reasoning includes the case in which $z_0$ is on the diagonal.
Apply the stated geometric theorem \eqref{eq:v85-SU} to obtain
\eqref{eq:v85-metric-certificate}. Finally
Theorem~\ref{thm:v85-confidence} bounds $w_h$ by $C_\kappa\eta_n$,
or Proposition~\ref{prop:v85-grid} bounds it by
$C_\kappa(\eta_n+\delta)$. Substitution proves
\eqref{eq:v85-metric-rate}.
\end{proof}
No component covering or global enumeration is used in this theorem.
The lower action at an individual acquisition site is the propagation
distance by the apparatus ordering. The interpolation step uses only
the regularity of boundary distance, not a spatial interpolation of
unknown nuisance parameters. Thus rough or independently changed
readout channels do not enter its spatial bias term.

\begin{corollary}[A finite geometric dictionary]\label{cor:v85-dictionary}
Suppose a finite set $\mathcal G_\rho\subset\mathcal G$ is supplied
whose boundary distances form a $\rho$-net for those of $\mathcal G$
in the uniform norm. Retain its metrics satisfying
\[
                    a_i^- -\rho\le d_{g'}(z_i)\le a_i^+ +\rho
                                      \quad\hbox{for every }i.
\]
On the coverage event this finite set is nonempty. Every retained
metric differs from the truth, after a boundary-fixing pullback, by
at most $C_{\rm geom}(w_h+\rho+2Lh)^\mu$. The corresponding bound
between two retained metrics uses $w_h+2\rho+2Lh$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A dictionary metric within boundary-distance error $\rho$ of the
truth passes every padded interval. At observed pairs a retained
metric and the truth differ by at most $w_h+\rho$. Repeat the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v85-geometric}. For two retained metrics the
padding is used twice.
\end{proof}
Compactness guarantees the existence of finite dictionaries. Constructing
one with certified distance errors is additional numerical work for a
given prior; it is not silently supplied by an infinite-dimensional
optimization oracle. With a supplied dictionary and a forward-parameter
grid, both searches are finite. The exact compact-set statement does
not claim an efficient general-purpose metric reconstruction algorithm.

\begin{corollary}[Total acquisition budget]\label{cor:v85-budget-rate}
For regular quasiuniform boundary nets on a $d$-dimensional manifold,
$m_h\asymp h^{-2(d-1)}$. With $N=Jm_hn$ total records, the choice
$h\asymp(\log(N/\zeta)/N)^{1/(2d)}$, and comparable allocation to
all cells, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-total-rate}
             \|\widehat g-\psi^*g\|_{C^2(M)}
                \le C\left(\frac{\log(N/\zeta)}{N}\right)^{\mu/(2d)}
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-\zeta$, whenever the regular-class
bounds hold uniformly along the sequence of experiments. For surfaces
the exponent is $\mu/4$. This is an upper rate for the stated design;
no nonparametric minimax assertion for metrics is inferred from it.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $n=N/(Jm_h)$ into \eqref{eq:v85-metric-rate} and balance
$h$ with $\{h^{-2(d-1)}\log(N/\zeta)/N\}^{1/2}$. Integer rounding
changes only constants for sufficiently large budgets.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What is used from geometric rigidity}
Theorem~\ref{thm:v84-surface} remains an exact continuum theorem for
all simple surfaces, with a constant reference arm and observations at
every ordered distinct boundary pair. Its last step is the global
boundary-distance theorem of Pestov--Uhlmann~\cite{V84PU}. The present
finite-record theorem instead uses the precise local stability class
of \cite{V85SU}, allows an endpoint-dependent reference delay, and
ends with a quantitative $C^2$ pullback error from finitely many noisy
records. Neither theorem replaces the other's hypotheses.

The geometric estimate \eqref{eq:v85-SU} is imported explicitly, not
reproved by the clock inversion. The new statement is the complete
finite-observation implication with a data-derived action certificate
and its statistical rate. Inverse problems with finite measurements
as in \cite{V85AS} provide important methodological precedent; here
the measured variables are uncalibrated categorical records, and the
spatial bias is controlled even without interstitial nuisance
regularity. A shrinking confidence diameter requires regularity of the
true channels, whereas validity of the certificate does not.
