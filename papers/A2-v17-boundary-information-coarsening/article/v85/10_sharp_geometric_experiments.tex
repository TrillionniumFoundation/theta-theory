\section{Sharp geometric experiments}
\label{sec:v85-sharp-geometry}
The preceding spatial rate does not assert nonparametric optimality.
For finite-dimensional geometric priors one can give matching upper
and lower statistical bounds. One can also transfer the exact clock
fibre into a genuine metric ambiguity. These statements distinguish
loss on a geometrically realizable parameter from loss on an arbitrary
action field.

\subsection{Finite boundary designs for geometric priors}
Let $\theta\mapsto g_\theta$ be a $C^2$ map from a neighbourhood of
$0\in\R^p$ into smooth simple metrics, with $g_0$ having s-injective
geodesic ray transform. Suppose the tangent tensors
$h_\ell=\partial_{\theta_\ell}g_\theta|_0$ are linearly independent
modulo symmetric differentials of vector fields vanishing on the
boundary. The parameter is a chosen coordinate on this geometric
family, not an arbitrary coordinate representative modulo an
unspecified diffeomorphism.

\begin{lemma}[A finite geometric design]\label{lem:v85-geometric-design}
There are $p$ ordered distinct boundary pairs $z_1,\ldots,z_p$ and
a sufficiently small closed Euclidean ball $B\subset\R^p$ about zero
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-parametric-inverse}
 |\theta-\theta'|\le C_D
       \left|\bigl(d_{g_\theta}(z_i)-d_{g_{\theta'}}(z_i)\bigr)_{i=1}^p\right|
                                     \quad(\theta,\theta'\in B).
\end{equation}
In particular these finitely many distances distinguish all metrics in
the family on $B$, including against boundary-fixing isometries.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed distinct boundary pair, first variation of the length of
its unique geodesic gives
\[
 \left.\partial_{\theta_\ell}d_{g_\theta}(z)\right|_0
       =\frac12\int_{\gamma_{0,z}}
                   h_\ell(\dot\gamma_{0,z},\dot\gamma_{0,z})\dd t.
\]
The variation of the geodesic itself contributes zero by stationarity
with fixed endpoints. Simplicity gives smooth dependence near a fixed
nondegenerate pair. If a linear combination of these $p$ functions
vanished at every boundary pair, s-injectivity would make the
corresponding combination of the $h_\ell$ a potential tensor,
contrary to the hypothesis. Their evaluation row vectors therefore
span $\R^p$: otherwise a nonzero vector orthogonal to their span
would give just such a vanishing combination. Choose $p$ rows forming
an invertible matrix $A_0$.

Write $D(\theta)=(d_{g_\theta}(z_i))_i$ and
$s_0=\sigma_{\min}(A_0)>0$. Shrink a convex ball about zero until
$\|D'(\theta)-A_0\|_{\rm op}\le s_0/2$ there. Integrating on the
line segment from $\theta'$ to $\theta$ gives
\[
 |D(\theta)-D(\theta')|
 \ge |A_0(\theta-\theta')|-(s_0/2)|\theta-\theta'|
 \ge(s_0/2)|\theta-\theta'|.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:v85-parametric-inverse}. Boundary-fixing
isometries preserve all the selected distances, so the last assertion
follows as well.
\end{proof}
Finite measurement selection from a finite-dimensional prior is a
classical principle; see \cite{V85AS}. The lemma identifies its
geometric derivative explicitly. Its role here is to combine the
geometric prior with uncalibrated finite readout records, rather than
to claim a new abstract finite-dimensional inverse theorem.

Choose apparatus bounds covering all the distances of this family
on $B$. At the $p$ selected pairs use $J=q+3$ clocks, and take $n$
independent pairs per cell, now independently across all cells. The
reference delay, detector coefficients and both channels may differ
at every site. Define a reconstruction by minimizing the maximum
matrix residual over $\theta\in B$ and all nuisance parameters in
the compact factors of \eqref{eq:v85-compact}, with
$x_i=d_{g_\theta}(z_i)$. This compact feasible model is nonempty and
a minimizer exists. Fix a deterministic tie-breaking rule.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp parametric geometric risk]\label{thm:v85-parametric-risk}
Suppose the true nuisance parameters belong to a fixed nonempty
regular class \eqref{eq:v85-regular-class}. With $N=pJn$ total
records, the estimator just defined satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-parametric-upper}
                    \sup\E|\widehat\theta-\theta|^2\le C/N.
\end{equation}
The supremum is over $\theta\in B$ and all such nuisance parameters.
The estimator does not require the value of their rank lower bound.

If the class contains an interior parameter point, strictly positive
full-rank channels and interior detector and delay values, then for
all sufficiently large $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-parametric-minimax}
       c/N\le\inf_{\widetilde\theta}\sup
                 \E|\widetilde\theta-\theta|^2\le C/N.
\end{equation}
The constants depend on the fixed geometric family and acquisition
design. This is a minimax theorem for that geometrically realizable
finite-dimensional prior, not for unrestricted simple metrics.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $e=\max_{i,j}\|\widehat P_{ij}-P_{ij}\|_F$. The minimizing
model has residual no larger than $e$, so its exact observation tuple
differs from the true one by at most $2e$ at each site. For $e$ below
a fixed threshold, the regular-compact argument in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v85-confidence} gives a distance error at most
$C_\kappa e$ at every selected pair, even though the minimization
allowed singular candidate channels. Equation
\eqref{eq:v85-parametric-inverse} gives
$|\widehat\theta-\theta|\le C e$.
For $e$ above that threshold, the same inequality holds after enlarging
$C$, since $B$ has bounded diameter. For a categorical empirical matrix,
\[
 \E\|\widehat P_{ij}-P_{ij}\|_F^2
       =n^{-1}\sum_{a,b}P_{ij,ab}(1-P_{ij,ab})\le n^{-1}.
\]
Bounding the maximum by a sum yields $\E e^2\le pJ/n$. Since $p,J$
are fixed and $N=pJn$, this proves \eqref{eq:v85-parametric-upper}.
Measurability is immediate on the finite sample space with fixed
choices of minimizers.

For the lower bound fix the nuisance parameters at one of the strict
interior points specified in the statement. Move the metric parameter
from an interior $\theta_0$ to $\theta_0+t v$ along a unit vector.
All categorical probabilities are bounded below by a positive constant
and depend smoothly on $t$, at the finitely many fixed cells. Therefore
\[
  \mathrm{KL}(P_{ij,t},P_{ij,0})
       \le\sum_{a,b}\frac{(P_{ij,t,ab}-P_{ij,0,ab})^2}{P_{ij,0,ab}}
       \le C t^2.
\]
Independence gives relative entropy at most $CNt^2$ for all records.
Taking $t=a/\sqrt N$ with a fixed sufficiently small $a>0$ makes
the total variation distance between the two record laws at most
$1/2$. For any estimator, the test choosing the nearer parameter errs
only when its squared error is at least $t^2/4$. The sum of the two
testing errors is at least one minus the total variation distance.
Thus the larger of its two squared risks is at least $t^2/16$.
This proves the lower bound; the upper bound is already established.
\end{proof}
The usual information inequalities used in this two-point argument
are stated, for example, in \cite{V85Tsybakov}. Unlike the earlier
weighted-action risk theorem, the alternatives here are actual
metrics in a fixed geometric family. There is no transfer of an
arbitrary action perturbation into geometry without a realizability
argument.

\subsection{The geometric meaning of the missing clock}
The endpoint-dependent reference class admits an exact clock threshold,
not only a sufficient count. It is important to state the reference
class in the lower bound: the following ambiguity need not preserve a
constant reference delay.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp clock threshold for geometric recovery]
\label{thm:v85-geometric-clock}
For the ordered two-arm class with arbitrary unknown endpoint-dependent
reference delay in \eqref{eq:v85-apparatus}, $q+3$ clocks suffice for
the geometric conclusions of Theorems~\ref{thm:v85-geometric} and
\ref{thm:v85-parametric-risk}. With $q+2$ fixed clocks there are
non-boundary-isometric simple metrics, with strictly positive full-rank
readout channels and strict apparatus margins, giving identical
projective matrices at every boundary pair. They therefore give the
same law of any number of records. Such alternatives can be chosen
arbitrarily close to an analytic simple metric, and within a
one-dimensional smooth geometric family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sufficiency has been proved. For necessity put $J=q+2$ and choose an
analytic simple metric $g_0$ with $\sup d_{g_0}<D_0$. Let
$g_t=(1+t)^2g_0$, for $t$ in a sufficiently small interval about zero;
then $x_t(z)=d_{g_t}(z)=(1+t)d_{g_0}(z)$. These metrics are simple,
and distinct values of $t$ have different boundary distances, hence
are not related by a boundary-fixing isometry.

Let $w(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{q+2}(T_j-s)^{-1}$ and choose $F'=w$ on
$(-\infty,T_1)$. Fix an interior constant delay
$\tau_0\in(\tau_-,\tau_+)$ and an interior relative polynomial
$P_0$ (one may take $P_0=0$). Define, at every boundary pair,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-geometric-fibre}
 \tau_t(z)=F^{-1}\bigl(F(x_t(z))-F(x_0(z))+F(\tau_0)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For uniformly small $|t|$ this is well defined and belongs to
$(\tau_-,\tau_+)$, by compactness of the range of $x_0$ and positivity
of $F'$ on the relevant compact intervals. Moreover
\[
           F(x_t(z))-F(\tau_t(z))=F(x_0(z))-F(\tau_0).
\]
The complete-fibre theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:v84-fibre}, now supplies
a unique degree-at-most-$q$ polynomial $P_{t,z}$ satisfying, at all
$q+2$ clocks,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-identical-geometric-ratios}
 P_{t,z}(T_j)+\log(T_j-x_t(z))-\log(T_j-\tau_t(z))
       =P_0(T_j)+\log(T_j-x_0(z))-\log(T_j-\tau_0).
\end{equation}
For completeness, the difference of the two logarithmic sample
vectors has zero degree-$q$ annihilator by the preceding identity.
It therefore lies in the polynomial evaluation subspace and has a
unique interpolating polynomial. The fixed interpolation map and
compact residual margins imply $P_{t,z}\to P_0$ uniformly in its
coefficient vector, over every boundary pair. Thus the fixed strict
coefficient bounds persist. All these quantities are smooth away
from the boundary diagonal; no diagonal observations are needed.

Fix strictly positive full-rank probability channels, the same for
all $t$ and $z$. Equation \eqref{eq:v85-identical-geometric-ratios}
keeps the normalized component weights, and hence every projective
matrix, exactly fixed. Positive throughputs realizing $P_{t,z}$ are
available; decreasing a common retention baseline preserves strict
retention bounds without changing any projective record. All apparatus
inequalities have uniform strict room. The construction is a small
scaling family near $g_0$, so it lies in the local geometric stability
class after shrinking its parameter interval. This proves the claimed
geometric, not merely scalar, ambiguity.
\end{proof}
The theorem exhibits precisely the ambiguity removed by the additional
clock. It does not assert the same necessity for a constant-reference
subclass, where cross-endpoint information can constrain the scalar
fibres. At $q+3$ clocks even an independently varying bounded reference
is recoverable pointwise; at $q+2$ the same scalar fibre can conceal a
change of metric. This is the common observation mechanism behind the
sharp scalar inverse, the confidence certificates, and the geometric
experiment.
