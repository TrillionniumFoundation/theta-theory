\section{Finite-record action certificates without a supplied signal floor}
\label{sec:v85-certificates}
A uniform inverse constant is useful for a rate theorem but need not be
an input to a confidence procedure. We distinguish a compact forward
model, on which confidence is valid even at singular channels, from
regular subsets, on which its diameter contracts. The distinction will
allow finite records to yield a geometric error certificate.

\subsection{A closed two-arm model}
Fix $q\ge0$, $J=q+3$ distinct clocks, $u,v\ge2$, and apparatus bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-apparatus}
        0<D_0<\tau_-<\tau_+<T_1,\qquad A>0.
\end{equation}
Let $\Delta_{r-1}$ be the closed probability simplex in $\R^r$.
Our parameter set is the explicitly closed compact polytope
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-compact}
 \mathfrak K=(\Delta_{u-1})^2\times(\Delta_{v-1})^2
          \times[0,D_0]\times[\tau_-,\tau_+]
          \times[-A,A]^{q+1}.
\end{equation}
Write its elements as $\omega=(U,V,x,\tau,c)$, and define
\begin{align}
 f_j(\omega)&=\sum_{\nu=0}^q c_\nu T_j^\nu
                 +\log(T_j-x)-\log(T_j-\tau),\nonumber\\
 p_j(\omega)&=\frac{\exp f_j(\omega)}{1+\exp f_j(\omega)},\qquad
 \Phi_j(\omega)=U\diag(p_j,1-p_j)V^{\mathsf T}.
                                               \label{eq:v85-forward}
\end{align}
Here $c$ is the relative detector polynomial, including relative
throughput. The common detector gauge has disappeared. The first arm
is labelled by the known ordering in \eqref{eq:v85-apparatus}, not by
an observed channel label. The second action is unknown and may vary
independently from one acquisition site to another. No rank, entrywise
positivity, spectral gap, or channel-separation lower bound is imposed
in \eqref{eq:v85-compact}.

Use the ambient Euclidean norm on $\mathfrak K$ and
$\|\Phi-\Phi'\|_{\rm obs}=\max_j\|\Phi_j-\Phi'_j\|_F$.
A forward Lipschitz constant is known from the apparatus bounds. For
example, with $r_0=T_1-\tau_+$ one may take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-forward-constant}
 L_\Phi=2+\frac12\max_j
        \left(\sum_{\nu=0}^qT_j^{2\nu}+2r_0^{-2}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $p'(f)=p(1-p)\le1/4$, channel columns have norm at most one,
and the two channel-matrix differentials have norms at most
$\sqrt2\|\dd U\|_F$ and $\sqrt2\|\dd V\|_F$. The coefficient and
action differential contributes at most half the displayed gradient
norm. Cauchy--Schwarz and integration on the convex set
$\mathfrak K$ give \eqref{eq:v85-forward-constant}. This is a
\emph{forward} constant, which does not diverge when the channels lose
rank.

\subsection{Simultaneous confidence intervals}
At sites $z_1,\ldots,z_m$, observe $n$ independent readout pairs at
each clock. The law in cell $(i,j)$ is $\Phi_j(\omega_i)$ for some
$\omega_i\in\mathfrak K$. Independence is required within each
cell; the different cells need not be mutually independent. Let
$\widehat P_{ij}$ be the empirical probability matrix, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-radius}
 \eta_n=\left\{\frac{uv}{2n}
               \log\frac{2mJuv}{\zeta}\right\}^{1/2},
                    \qquad 0<\zeta<1.
\end{equation}
Define compact confidence sets and their action projections by
\begin{align}
 \mathfrak C_i&=\left\{\omega\in\mathfrak K:
        \max_j\|\Phi_j(\omega)-\widehat P_{ij}\|_F\le\eta_n\right\},
                                                   \label{eq:v85-confidence}\\
 a_i^-&=\min_{\mathfrak C_i}x,\qquad
 a_i^+=\max_{\mathfrak C_i}x.\nonumber
\end{align}
An empty set is reported as model inconsistency; no endpoint is then
silently assigned. For nonempty sets the extrema are attained.
Because the sample spaces are finite for fixed $n$, fixed choices
among minimizers and all reported endpoints are measurable.

\begin{theorem}[Observable action certificates]\label{thm:v85-confidence}
Uniformly over all $\omega_i\in\mathfrak K$, including rank-deficient
channels, with probability at least $1-\zeta$ every
$\mathfrak C_i$ is nonempty and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-coverage}
                         x_i\in[a_i^-,a_i^+]
                              \quad(1\le i\le m).
\end{equation}
The confidence procedure uses only the finite records and the forward
bounds \eqref{eq:v85-apparatus}; in particular it does not use an
inverse constant or a supplied channel signal floor.

For $\kappa>0$ define the regular compact subset
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-regular-class}
 \mathfrak K_\kappa=\{\omega\in\mathfrak K:
                  \sigma_2(U),\sigma_2(V)\ge\kappa\}.
\end{equation}
There are $\epsilon_\kappa>0$ and $C_\kappa<\infty$, depending only
on this subset and the forward class, such that if every true
$\omega_i\in\mathfrak K_\kappa$ and $\eta_n<\epsilon_\kappa$, then,
on the same event,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v85-contraction}
              \max_i(a_i^+-a_i^-)\le C_\kappa\eta_n.
\end{equation}
The value of $\kappa$ is not an input to the procedure. It indexes
subsets on which its observed certificate contracts at the stated rate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the bounded-indicator exponential inequality to every entry of
every empirical matrix. A union bound over $mJuv$ entries gives
\[
       \max_{i,j}\|\widehat P_{ij}-\Phi_j(\omega_i)\|_F
                                                    \le\eta_n
\]
outside an event of probability at most $\zeta$. This argument uses
no independence between cells. On this event the true parameters
belong to their confidence sets, proving nonemptiness and coverage.

For contraction, compactness and \eqref{eq:v85-apparatus} give a
number $p_0>0$ such that $p_j,1-p_j\ge p_0$ on all of
$\mathfrak K$. At a true parameter in $\mathfrak K_\kappa$,
\[
       \sigma_2(\Phi_1)\ge p_0\kappa^2.
\]
Every feasible candidate differs from this exact tuple by at most
$2\eta_n$. If $2\eta_n<p_0\kappa^2/2$, its first matrix has second
singular value at least $p_0\kappa^2/2$. Since probability-column
matrices have operator norm at most $\sqrt2$, the inequalities
\[
 \sigma_2(\Phi_1)\le\sqrt2\sigma_2(U),\qquad
 \sigma_2(\Phi_1)\le\sqrt2\sigma_2(V)
\]
force both candidate channels to have full rank with a uniform lower
bound. Thus all feasible candidates and the truth lie in one fixed
regular compact subset of $\mathfrak K$.

On that subset Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-main} gives injectivity after
ordering the two actions. The smooth ambient extensions of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v83-local} give local inverse Lipschitz bounds. A finite
subcover gives such a bound for nearby pairs of parameters. Pairs
outside these neighbourhoods have a positive minimum observed
separation by compactness and injectivity, so the bound extends to
all pairs in that compact subset. Denote it by $C'\!_\kappa$.
Every feasible action is therefore within $2C'\!_\kappa\eta_n$ of
the true action. Taking both extrema proves
\eqref{eq:v85-contraction}, after enlarging the constant. This use of
an inverse modulus proves a rate; it does not insert that modulus
into \eqref{eq:v85-confidence}.
\end{proof}
The guarantee at singular channels is coverage, not a uniform shrinking
radius. A wide interval is an observable lack of precision. This avoids
confusing a finite-record certificate with model-order identification
at arbitrarily weak signal. The two-arm architecture remains known.

\begin{proposition}[Finite implementation]\label{prop:v85-grid}
Let $\mathfrak K_\delta$ be a finite Euclidean $\delta$-net in
$\mathfrak K$. At site $i$, retain its points whose residual is at
most $\eta_n+L_\Phi\delta$. Take the smallest and largest retained
$x$ coordinates, pad the resulting interval by $\delta$ at both
ends, and intersect it with $[0,D_0]$. These finite intervals have the
simultaneous coverage of Theorem~\ref{thm:v85-confidence}.
On its regular subsets their widths are at most
$C_\kappa(\eta_n+\delta)$ when $\eta_n+\delta$ is sufficiently small.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A net point within $\delta$ of the truth has residual at most
$\eta_n+L_\Phi\delta$ and an $x$ coordinate within $\delta$ of the
true one. Conversely every retained point has observed distance at
most $2\eta_n+L_\Phi\delta$ from the truth. The compact regular
inverse estimate in the preceding proof applies once this number is
small. The two padding terms add $2\delta$ to the width.
\end{proof}
The simplex and interval factors in \eqref{eq:v85-compact} admit
explicit rational grids. Hence the procedure terminates after a finite
search, rather than only specifying a continuous minimizer. Its size
grows as a power of $\delta^{-1}$ with exponent equal to the fixed
parameter dimension; no dimension-uniform polynomial-time guarantee
is claimed. Taking $\delta\le\eta_n$ preserves the statistical rate.

\subsection{Relation to calibration and synchronization}
Blind bilinear calibration can sometimes be reduced to linear least
squares, with efficient algorithms under the hypotheses of
Ling--Strohmer~\cite{V85LS}. Our forward map instead has two unknown
readout channels and a nonlinear action factor under polynomial log
selection. The theorem supplies a finite exhaustive certificate for
that compact model, not a replacement for those efficient calibration
algorithms. Spectral permutation synchronization from noisy pairwise
permutations, including the sharp statistical analysis in
Nguyen--Zhang~\cite{V85NZ}, is likewise a different observation
experiment. Here scalar action intervals require no spatial
permutation synchronization. For component coverings, the
nearest-column and face-cocycle reconstruction remains the separate
statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:v84-mesh}.
