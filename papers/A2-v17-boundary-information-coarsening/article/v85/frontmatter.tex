\begin{abstract}
We study action and geometric rigidity for projective matrix observations
with unknown selective detectors and two unknown conditionally independent
readouts. For finite-dimensional detector spaces with rational derivatives,
regular identification is equivalent to finitely many residue and
double-pole tests. The classification gives maximal admissible spaces
under principal-part closure and a uniform clock budget from pole data
and order at infinity. Polynomial log-detectors of degree at most $q$
have the sharp threshold $q+3$. Finite readout records yield action
confidence intervals without a supplied channel signal floor. In a
two-arm boundary experiment, the reference delay and both channels may
vary independently with the endpoint pair. Combining the observed
interval widths with boundary-distance stability gives a quantitative
$C^2$ metric certificate, modulo boundary-fixing diffeomorphisms, from
finitely many noisy acquisitions near a simple metric with s-injective
ray transform. Raw attempts, including failures, are charged separately.
For finite-dimensional geometric priors we prove matching parametric
risk bounds. The missing-clock fibre is realized by distinct simple
metrics, establishing a sharp geometric clock threshold for the
variable-reference class. The exact theory also determines component
coverings, action divisors and compatible return lifts on their observed
domains.
\end{abstract}
