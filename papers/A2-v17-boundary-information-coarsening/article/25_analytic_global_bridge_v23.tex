\section{From noisy finite jets to global analytic table loss}
\label{sec:v23-analytic-global-bridge}

The exact analytic rigidity theorem uses all contact jets, whereas every
finite-sample information theorem is first proved in a fixed finite jet model.
We now give a precise bridge between those two levels.  The result is a
compactness/sieve theorem: it does not postulate a uniform-in-order condition
number, and it does not perform unstable numerical analytic continuation.
Instead it uses injectivity of the intrinsic lifted-channel data map on a
compact analytic class to show that sufficiently many accurately recovered
finite coordinates resolve the global table uniformly.

Fix the marked abstract Euclidean lattice, obstacle-orbit labels and a
connected measured lifted-channel graph.  Let \(\mathfrak K\) be a compact
family of labelled periodic billiard tables, modulo one simultaneous common
element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\), with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every obstacle boundary is connected, real analytic and strictly convex,
and the family has uniform positive curvature, separation and channel-collar
bounds.
\item The chosen lifted channels persist throughout \(\mathfrak K\) with the
same labels, deck displacements and orientations.
\item The periodic intrinsic gluing space of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-intrinsic-table-rigidity} is a singleton throughout
\(\mathfrak K\).  A sufficient model is a fixed signature-rigid spanning tree
together with a fixed signature-rigid lattice-anchoring cycle; alternatively
one may work with a known oriented ambient lattice frame and a
signature-rigid spanning tree.
\item The topology of \(\mathfrak K\) is strong enough that analytic boundary
images and the corresponding half-line actions vary continuously with all
fixed finite derivatives.  A uniformly bounded holomorphic extension to a
fixed complex collar is a sufficient model.
\end{enumerate}
For \(q\ge0\), let \(d_q\) be any metric inducing the labelled
\(C^q\)-topology on this quotient class.

For a table \(T\in\mathfrak K\) and an integer \(M\ge2\), define its finite
intrinsic action data
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-finite-data-map}
 \mathcal D_M(T)
 =\left(
     g_e,
     S_{e,0}^{(m)}(0),S_{e,1}^{(m)}(0):
     e\in E,\ 2\le m\le M
   \right).
\end{equation}
The fixed lifted-channel labels \((a_e,b_e,\ell_e)\) are part of the
experimental design and are suppressed from this numerical vector.  The full
sequence \(\mathcal D_\infty\), together with those labels, is determined by
the signed endpoint support germs, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-intrinsic-table-rigidity} makes it injective on
\(\mathfrak K\).

\begin{theorem}[Uniform finite-coordinate resolution of a compact analytic
class]
\label{thm:v23-finite-coordinate-resolution}
For every \(q\ge0\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exist an integer
\(M=M(q,\varepsilon)\) and a number \(\eta=\eta(q,\varepsilon)>0\) such that
for all \(T,T'\in\mathfrak K\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-finite-resolution}
 \|\mathcal D_M(T)-\mathcal D_M(T')\|<\eta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d_q(T,T')<\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
Thus global analytic table loss can be controlled by finitely many intrinsic
channel action jets, with the required order allowed to depend on the desired
global accuracy.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the assertion fails for some \(q\) and \(\varepsilon>0\).  For each
integer \(m\ge2\) there then exist \(T_m,T'_m\in\mathfrak K\) such that
\[
 d_q(T_m,T'_m)\ge\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \|\mathcal D_m(T_m)-\mathcal D_m(T'_m)\|<m^{-1}.
\]
By compactness, after passing to a subsequence,
\[
                         T_m\to T,\qquad T'_m\to T'
\]
in \(\mathfrak K\).  Fix any finite order \(M\).  For all sufficiently large
\(m\ge M\), continuity of \(\mathcal D_M\) and the preceding estimate give
\[
                         \mathcal D_M(T)=\mathcal D_M(T').
\]
Since \(M\) is arbitrary,
\(\mathcal D_\infty(T)=\mathcal D_\infty(T')\).  The all-order inverse and the
singleton periodic gluing hypothesis imply that \(T=T'\) in the
\(\mathrm{SE}(2)\)-quotient.  Continuity of \(d_q\) then gives
\(d_q(T_m,T'_m)\to0\), contradicting the fixed lower bound
\(\varepsilon\).
\end{proof}

This theorem is the stability statement needed to avoid treating analytic
continuation of noisy Taylor series as an algorithm.  It says that on a fixed
compact analytic class there are no globally separated tables whose intrinsic
action data remain arbitrarily close through arbitrarily high finite order.

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal growing-order reconstruction principle]
\label{thm:v23-diagonal-global-reconstruction}
Let \(\mathsf E_n=\{P_{n,T}:T\in\mathfrak K\}\) be any sequence of physical or
boundary experiments.  Assume that for every fixed \(M\) there exists an
estimator \(\widehat{\mathcal D}_{n,M}\) of the finite data
\(\mathcal D_M(T)\) such that, for every \(\eta>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-fixed-M-consistency}
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}
 P_{n,T}\!\left(
   \|\widehat{\mathcal D}_{n,M}-\mathcal D_M(T)\|>\eta
 \right)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Then there exist a deterministic nondecreasing sequence \(M_n\to\infty\) and
estimators \(\widehat T_n\in\mathfrak K\) such that for every fixed
\(q\ge0\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-global-consistency}
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}
 P_{n,T}\{d_q(\widehat T_n,T)>\varepsilon\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
One may choose \(M_n\) to increase arbitrarily slowly.  No uniform-in-\(M\)
conditioning of the determinant-one recursion is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(m=1,2,\ldots\), apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-finite-coordinate-resolution} with
\((q,m^{-1})\) to obtain \(M_m\) and \(\eta_m\).  Increase \(M_m\) if
necessary so that it is strictly increasing.  By
\eqref{eq:v23-fixed-M-consistency}, choose \(n_m\) so large that, for
\(n\ge n_m\),
\[
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}
 P_{n,T}\!\left(
   \|\widehat{\mathcal D}_{n,M_m}-\mathcal D_{M_m}(T)\|>\eta_m/3
 \right)<2^{-m}.
\]
For \(n_m\le n<n_{m+1}\), set \(M_n=M_m\).  Let \(\widehat T_n\) be a
measurable \(\eta_m/3\)-approximate minimizer over \(\mathfrak K\) of
\[
 T'\longmapsto
 \|\mathcal D_{M_m}(T')-
        \widehat{\mathcal D}_{n,M_m}\|.
\]
Compactness gives such approximate minimizers.  On the event that the finite
data estimate is within \(\eta_m/3\) of the truth, the triangle inequality
gives
\[
 \|\mathcal D_{M_m}(\widehat T_n)-
        \mathcal D_{M_m}(T)\|<\eta_m.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-finite-coordinate-resolution} therefore gives
\(d_q(\widehat T_n,T)<m^{-1}\).  The exceptional probability is at most
\(2^{-m}\), proving \eqref{eq:v23-global-consistency}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[How the physical finite-order theorems feed the global
bridge]
\label{cor:v23-physical-global-bridge}
Whenever a compact analytic class \(\mathfrak K\) admits a common finite
acquisition atlas for which every fixed finite action-jet vector
\(\mathcal D_M\) is uniformly consistently estimable, the actual finite-bridge
experiments of this paper admit a growing-order global table reconstruction in
any fixed \(C^q\) loss by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-diagonal-global-reconstruction}.

The fixed-order ingredients used to construct such local estimators are the
signed-jet tangent isomorphism, finite positive physical designs, the
endpoint--time Poisson experiment (or its endpoint Gaussian coarsening), and
the stopped finite-to-boundary transfer.  The present theorem supplies the
missing \(M_n\to\infty\) and global-loss passage.  Sharp rates in \(M_n\) are
not claimed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[What has and has not been proved]
\label{rem:v23-global-bridge-scope}
The bridge is stronger than simply placing an exact analytic theorem beside a
fixed-dimensional statistical theorem: it gives a genuine growing-order
sieve statement and a global table loss.  It is deliberately weaker than a
sharp analytic minimax theorem.  Obtaining an explicit optimal relation among
analytic radius, jet order, physical preparation cost and global \(C^q\) risk
would require uniform quantitative control of the finite-order inverse as
\(M\to\infty\).  No such bound is inserted by assumption here.
\end{remark}
