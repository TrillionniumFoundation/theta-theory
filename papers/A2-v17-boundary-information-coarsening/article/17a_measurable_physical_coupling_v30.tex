\begin{lemma}[Measurable coupling in physical observation coordinates]
\label{lem:v30-physical-coupling}
Let $K$ be a fixed bounded scalar endpoint--time box, and equip
$K_\partial=K\sqcup\{\partial\}$ with the measure
$\nu=\operatorname{Leb}_K+\delta_\partial$.  Suppose
$f_{\xi,a},g_{\xi,a}$ are jointly measurable probability densities with
respect to $\nu$.  Let $Y$ be a standard Borel physical record space and
let $\iota_{\xi,a}:K_\partial\to Y$ be jointly measurable and, for
each $(\xi,a)$, a Borel isomorphism onto a Borel subset of $Y$.
Assume that it sends $\partial$ to the common failure symbol.
Then the probability kernels
\[
 P_{\xi,a}=(\iota_{\xi,a})_*(f_{\xi,a}\nu),\qquad
 Q_{\xi,a}=(\iota_{\xi,a})_*(g_{\xi,a}\nu)
\]
admit a jointly measurable maximal coupling.  In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v30-physical-coupling-tv}
 \|P_{\xi,a}-Q_{\xi,a}\|_{\rm TV}
   =1-\int_{K_\partial}\min(f_{\xi,a},g_{\xi,a})\,d\nu.
\end{equation}
No domination of $P_{\xi,a}$ and $Q_{\xi,a}$ by ambient Lebesgue measure
on $Y$, or domination across different geometric parameters, is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppress $(\xi,a)$ and put $h=\min(f,g)$ and $m=\int h\,d\nu$.
For a measurable set $D\subset Y\times Y$ define
\begin{align*}
 C(D)={}&\int_{K_\partial}
       \one_D(\iota(x),\iota(x))h(x)\,d\nu(x)\\
 &+\frac1{1-m}\int_{K_\partial^2}
       \one_D(\iota(x),\iota(y))
       (f(x)-h(x))(g(y)-h(y))\,d\nu(x)d\nu(y),
\end{align*}
where the second term is defined to be zero when $m=1$.
Both residual measures have mass $1-m$, so $C$ is a probability measure
with marginals $P$ and $Q$.  Its dependence on $(\xi,a)$ is measurable
by integration of jointly measurable functions; on $\{m=1\}$ the
specified zero branch is measurable as well.

The residual measures are supported, up to null sets, on the disjoint
sets $\{f>g\}$ and $\{g>f\}$.  Injectivity of $\iota$ makes their images
disjoint.  Thus the second term puts no mass on the diagonal and the
coupling disagrees with probability $1-m$.  Conversely, for fixed $(\xi,a)$ the assumed Borel inverse makes the
image of $\{f>g\}$ measurable,
and the difference of its $P$ and $Q$ probabilities is
\[
 \int_{\{f>g\}}(f-g)\,d\nu=1-m.
\]
The coupling bound and this measurable set prove
\eqref{eq:v30-physical-coupling-tv} and maximality.
\end{proof}

For the billiard kernels, $K$ is the common unscaled scalar contact box
for $(u,v,r)$.  The finite and boundary densities, including the failure
atom, are given by the physical flux formula and its two-boundary limit.
The maps $\iota_{\xi,a}$ insert the two boundary positions in the plane
and retain the residual time and the chosen labels.  The supplied contact
graphs make these maps jointly smooth on each channel component.
Their inverses on the physical images are the transverse projections in
the corresponding contact frames, together with the unchanged time and
labels.  Thus they are Borel isomorphisms onto their Borel images.
A finite or countable disjoint union
handles the flight numbers and channel labels.  The preceding lemma
therefore supplies the measurable maximal coupling used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v16-kernel-comparison} for the actual physical records.

When only scalar endpoint coordinates and residual time are observed,
$\iota_{\xi,a}$ is the identity and the same construction applies directly.
For planar endpoint positions the successful law is instead supported on
an embedded boundary surface; it is not an ambient Lebesgue density.
The parameter-dependent embedding is used only to prove a comparison at
one fixed table.  It is neither an extra observation nor a parameter-free
simulator for an unknown table, and does not compare densities on the
different boundary surfaces of two distinct tables.
