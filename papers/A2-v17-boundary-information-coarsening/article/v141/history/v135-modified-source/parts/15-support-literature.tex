\section{Subspace varieties and the support calculation}
\label{sec:support-literature}

The support argument has two distinct ingredients.  The description
of tensors by contraction flattenings, the tangent formula for a
Grassmannian, and the Schur decomposition are classical.  The
additional calculation here is the kernel of
\[
 \kappa_q:\det V\otimes\Sym^4V\longrightarrow
                     \bigwedge^3\Sym^2V
\]
for every nonzero symmetric bilinear form \(q\), followed by its
application to the two component lines recovered from the intrinsic
normal cone.  We give a geometric formulation that distinguishes this
calculation from general rank or orbit questions.

For a vector space \(W\) define the exterior subspace variety
\[
 \operatorname{Sub}^{\wedge}_r(W)
   =\{[z]\in\Pj(\bigwedge^4W):
          z\in\bigwedge^4S\text{ for some }S\subset W,\ \dim S\le r\}.
\]
It is the reduced closed set defined by rank at most \(r\) of
\(W^*\to\bigwedge^3W\), \(q\mapsto\iota_qz\).
Indeed the support-annihilator identity
\eqref{eq:exterior-support-annihilator} identifies that rank with
the smallest support dimension.  Thus the minors give its support;
we make no assertion here that their ideal is radical.  Similarly,
\(\operatorname{Sub}^{\mathrm{sym}}_d(V)\subset\Pj(\Sym^4V)\)
is the reduced closed locus of quartics in at most \(d\) essential
variables.  It is the first-catalecticant rank locus by
\cite[Proposition~1]{Carlini}.  The spaces being measured are different:
\(V\) for essential variables and \(W=\Sym^2V\) for exterior support.

\begin{proposition}[Exact reduced pullbacks of support varieties]
\label{prop:schur-subspace-pullbacks}
Let \(\jmath:\Pj(\Sym^4V)\hookrightarrow
\Pj(\bigwedge^4\Sym^2V)\) be the projectivization of \(j\);
the one-dimensional determinant twist does not affect this map.
Let \(\mathcal Q=\{[h^2]:0\ne h\in\Sym^2V\}\), the closed
quadratic-square locus.  As reduced closed subvarieties,
\begin{align}
 \bigl(\jmath^{-1}\operatorname{Sub}^{\wedge}_4(W)\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}
       &=\operatorname{Sub}^{\mathrm{sym}}_1(V),\label{eq:pullback-support-four}\\
 \bigl(\jmath^{-1}\operatorname{Sub}^{\wedge}_7(W)\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}
 =\bigl(\jmath^{-1}\operatorname{Sub}^{\wedge}_8(W)\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}
       &=\operatorname{Sub}^{\mathrm{sym}}_2(V),\label{eq:pullback-support-eight}\\
 \bigl(\jmath^{-1}\operatorname{Sub}^{\wedge}_9(W)\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}
       &=\operatorname{Sub}^{\mathrm{sym}}_3(V)\cup\mathcal Q.
       \label{eq:pullback-support-nine}
\end{align}
In particular a quartic with at least three essential variables has
image outside the second secant variety
\(\sigma_2(\Gr(4,W))\), including its limiting secants.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The support values in Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-schur-support} give
the first two equalities on complex points.  They also give the third:
its four-essential-variable part consists exactly of squares of
nondegenerate quadratics.  A degenerate quadratic and its square have
the same essential-variable space, since
\(\partial(h^2)=2h\partial h\) in a polynomial domain.
Their squares therefore already belong to
\(\operatorname{Sub}^{\mathrm{sym}}_3(V)\).
The locus \(\mathcal Q\) is closed as the image of the projective
morphism \([h]\mapsto[h^2]\), which has no base points.
All the displayed loci are closed and have the specified reduced
structures, so equality on complex points proves the assertions.

A sum of two decomposable four-vectors lies in
\(\operatorname{Sub}^{\wedge}_8(W)\).  That rank locus is closed,
so it contains the closure of all such secant lines.  Equation
\eqref{eq:pullback-support-eight} then proves the last assertion.
This is an exclusion statement; it does not identify the entire
intersection of the binary locus with \(\sigma_2(\Gr(4,W))\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The classical inputs and the additional assertion}

The tangent-space identity in \cite[Lemma~2.1]{Boralevi} is precisely
\(\widehat T_{w_R}\Gr(4,W)=\bigwedge^3R\wedge W\).
Its eight-dimensional support bound is the immediate linear-algebra
consequence used in Lemma~\ref{lem:secant-tangent-support}.
We do not claim either formula, or the general study of Grassmannian
secant dimensions, as new.  The issue here is the position of one
specified Schur subspace relative to those secants, not the dimension
of a general secant variety.

Landsberg--Weyman's subspace varieties are defined for products of
tensor factors; their Theorem~3.1 describes their equations and
singularities \cite{LandsbergWeyman}.  That framework explains the
use of flattening ranks as support conditions.  It is not a theorem
about the contraction of our determinant-twisted quartic submodule,
nor does it identify the reduced pullbacks
\eqref{eq:pullback-support-four}--\eqref{eq:pullback-support-nine}.
The exterior rank identity needed here was proved directly, so no
scheme-theoretic conclusion for alternating tensors is imported from
the tensor-product statement.

The occurrence of \(\mathbb S_{(5,1,1,1)}V\) is a plethysm
calculation, not a novelty claim.  Its multiplicity is one in
\(\bigwedge^4\Sym^2V\), whose two highest weights are
\((4,3,1,0)\) and \((5,1,1,1)\).  For example its character
identity is
\[
 [t^4]\prod_{i\le j}(1+t x_ix_j)
       =s_{(4,3,1,0)}(x)+s_{(5,1,1,1)}(x).
\]
The highest-weight and exterior-duality verification is given in
Lemma~\ref{lem:universal-schur-readout}; polarization fixes the
normalization of its unique quartic embedding.  The skew Cauchy and
\(SL_2\) character calculations used for the scalar kernel are likewise
standard representation operations, expanded in
Lemma~\ref{lem:orthogonal-exterior-cube} rather than cited as a
new decomposition method.

The precise additional assertions proved here are the all-ranks
contraction kernels of Lemma~\ref{lem:schur-contraction-kernel},
the resulting exact support table, and the reconstruction consequence
of combining that table with the intrinsic common-coordinate
readout.  Exterior support is neither the least number of decomposable
summands nor a classification of \(GL(W)\)-orbits in
\(\bigwedge^4W\).  The reduced support pullbacks above, and the
containment of the ambient reconstruction obstruction over binary
quartics, must not be mistaken for either such classification.
The primary comparisons just made establish which cited tools are
classical; they are not an exhaustive historical priority certificate.
In particular the six-way theorem comparison with the unread
Ballico source \cite{Ballico93} remains separately unresolved.
