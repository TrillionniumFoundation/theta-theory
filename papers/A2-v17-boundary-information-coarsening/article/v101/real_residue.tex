\section{Real residues and filtered changes of observation}\label{sec:v101-residue}
We compare the weighted relation with signed valuation geometry before
imposing its observation metric. Throughout, positive-real refers to
positive values of the degeneration parameter. It does not restrict
the coordinates of a real leading coefficient to the positive orthant.

Let $\mathbb K=\R\langle t\rangle$ be the real closed field of real
algebraic Puiseux series, ordered by positive leading coefficient, with
$t$ positive infinitesimal. Write $\operatorname{val}$ for its rational
valuation, $\mathcal O$ for the valuation ring, and
$\operatorname{res}:\mathcal O\to\R$ for its residue map. We use the
convention $\operatorname{val}(0)=+\infty$. A semialgebraic germ and
its defining inequalities are interpreted over $\mathbb K$ by the
same real-closed-field formulas.

Let $S\subset\R^n$ be the actual semialgebraic observation--coefficient
relation germ at zero and let $w\in\Q_{>0}^n$. Put
\[
 \mathcal B_w(S)=\{y\in S(\mathbb K):
                          \operatorname{val}(y_i)\ge w_i\text{ for all }i\}.
\]
Only the germ matters, since every point of this set is infinitesimal.
The coefficientwise residue below is taken after multiplication by
$t^{-w_i}$.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted real residue comparison]\label{thm:v101-residue}
The positive-real weighted tangent relation satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v101-residue}
 T_w^+S=\operatorname{res}\bigl(t^{-w}\mathcal B_w(S)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For every sign vector $\sigma\in\{-,+\}^n$, its part with all
coordinates nonzero has the exact comparison
\begin{align}\label{eq:v101-signed-fibre}
 &\exists v\in T_w^+S\cap(\R^*)^n,
                         \quad\sgn v=\sigma\nonumber\\
 &\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists y\in S(\mathbb K),\quad
       \operatorname{val}y=w,\quad\sgn y=\sigma.
\end{align}
Thus forgetting the nonzero residue magnitudes gives the fixed-weight
fibre of the coordinate signed valuation map. Imposing $S\subset
\R_{>0}^n$ gives its positive-orthant specialization. Zero coordinates
on the left of \eqref{eq:v101-residue} also retain lifts of higher
valuation; they must not be discarded by requiring equality of every
coordinate valuation.

For the observation and coefficient blocks of Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical},
the manuscript's joint object is consequently the following operation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v101-comparison-schematic}
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal B_w(S)\ \xrightarrow{\;\operatorname{res}(t^{-w}\,\cdot\,)\;}
 T_w^+S\ \xrightarrow{\;\|U\|\le1\;}
 \mathcal I_0\ \xrightarrow{\;\text{target projection and roots}\;}
 \mathcal L_\theta .\quad}
\end{equation}
The observation-unit section and spectral decoder in this formula are
additional metric data, not part of the coordinate signed valuation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A point on the right of \eqref{eq:v101-residue} is represented by
algebraic Puiseux series. After a common positive ramification these
series converge on a small positive interval. Transfer of the finite
semialgebraic defining formula, or equivalently the eventual signs of
its convergent algebraic functions, makes the resulting real points
belong to $S$. Their rescaled limit is their displayed residue, proving
one inclusion.

Conversely let $(0,v)$ lie in the closure of
$\{(\epsilon,\epsilon^{-w}y):\epsilon>0,\ y\in S\}$. This is a
semialgebraic set after clearing the weight denominators. Real curve
selection gives a semialgebraic arc with positive first coordinate
approaching $(0,v)$. Its first coordinate is nonconstant and eventually
strictly monotone. Reparametrize by that coordinate. Each component is
an algebraic Puiseux germ in $\epsilon$, so it gives a point of
$\mathbb K$ after renaming $\epsilon$ as $t$. Boundedness of the
rescaled arc says $\operatorname{val}y_i\ge w_i$, and its limit is
$\operatorname{res}(t^{-w}y)$. This proves equality.
A rescaled coordinate has nonzero residue precisely when its valuation
is exactly its prescribed weight; its sign is then the residue sign.
This proves \eqref{eq:v101-signed-fibre}. The observation-unit identity
in \eqref{eq:v101-comparison-schematic} is
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical}, including its boundary rescaling argument
at $\|U\|=1$; it is not an unproved interchange of closure and
intersection with a closed ball.
\end{proof}

Jell--Scheiderer--Yu \cite{A2JSY22} develop real tropicalization for
semialgebraic sets over an ordered nonarchimedean real closed field and
identify real analytification with an inverse limit of such
real tropicalizations. Under the normalization $|t|=e^{-1}$, the
right side of \eqref{eq:v101-signed-fibre} is the fibre of their
\emph{coordinate} signed valuation map at
$(\sigma_i e^{-w_i})_i$. Formula~\eqref{eq:v101-residue} retains the
full residues before that projection. We make no claim that the full
inverse-limit real analytification loses the same information as one
coordinate tropicalization. The fixed observation norm and root decoder
are specified here independently of that inverse-limit construction.

Positive tropical geometry asks for positive lifts, rather than all real
points of a complex initial variety. The initial-ideal and lifting
criteria studied by Rose--Telek \cite{A2RT26} are directly relevant
in that setting. In the present setting positivity applies to the
scale; coordinate orthants and all feasibility inequalities must be
kept separately. The following familiar nonsingular criterion records
exactly when an initial-point calculation alone suffices locally.

\begin{proposition}[A sufficient real lifting test]\label{prop:v101-lift}
After clearing weight denominators, suppose a local presentation of the
weighted deformation is
\[
 F(t,z)=0,\qquad g_1(t,z)>0,\ldots,g_s(t,z)>0,
\]
with Nash functions, and suppose these are the complete local equations
and inequalities of the actual deformed relation. If $F(0,v)=0$,
$D_zF(0,v)$ has full row rank, and $g_i(0,v)>0$ for every $i$, then
$v\in T_w^+S$. This is a sufficient test, not an equivalence at a
singular initial point or at an active inequality.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose as many $z$ coordinates as there are equations so that the
corresponding Jacobian minor is invertible, and keep the remaining
coordinates fixed at their values in $v$. The Nash implicit-function
theorem gives $z(t)$ with $z(0)=v$ and $F(t,z(t))=0$.
The strict inequalities persist for small positive $t$. Undoing the
weighted substitution gives a real lift in the actual relation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Naturality from finite weighted jets}
We next derive the convergence needed for naturality from a condition
on the presentation, instead of assuming that convergence. For
$w\in\Q_{>0}^n$ and $w'\in\Q_{>0}^n$, call a Nash isomorphism germ
$h:(\R^n,0)\to(\R^n,0)$ \emph{bi-filtered} if every nonzero Taylor
monomial $y^\alpha$ of $h_j$ has $w\cdot\alpha\ge w'_j$, and the
inverse satisfies the corresponding condition with $w,w'$ reversed.
Only finitely many monomials have weight below any $w'_j$; the
vanishing conditions are therefore conditions on finite jets.
Let $h_{[w,w']}$ consist of the terms with equality of weights.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-jet filtered naturality]\label{thm:v101-filtered}
A bi-filtered Nash isomorphism satisfies, locally uniformly on bounded
sets,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v101-filtered-limit}
 D_{t,w'}^{-1}hD_{t,w}=h_{[w,w']}+O(t^\eta),\qquad \eta>0,
\end{equation}
where $D_{t,w}=\diag(t^{w_i})$. The analogous statement holds for
$h^{-1}$, and its weighted initial map is the inverse polynomial map
of $h_{[w,w']}$. Consequently
\[
 T_{w'}^+(h(S))=h_{[w,w']}(T_w^+S).
\]
Weighted initial maps preserve composition. They commute with the
residue comparison \eqref{eq:v101-residue}. A positive ramification
$t=s^q$ replaces $w$ by $qw$ and leaves the residue set unchanged.
When the observation-unit section and spectral decoder are transported,
so is the resulting metric fibre. Equality of untransported bottleneck
diameters requires that the transported target map be an isometry.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a common denominator for all the weights. In a finite Taylor
polynomial, every term of weight strictly larger than its output weight
has a gap bounded below by a positive reciprocal integer. Such terms
are $O(t^\eta)$ on bounded rescaled sets. Choose the Taylor order $N$
so large that $(N+1)\min_i w_i>\max_j w'_j+\eta$.
The analytic Taylor remainder is $O(\|D_{t,w}z\|^{N+1})$ uniformly
for bounded $z$, and hence has the same bound after division by the
output weights. This proves \eqref{eq:v101-filtered-limit}. The same
argument applies to the inverse. Substitution in the identities
$h^{-1}h=hh^{-1}=\mathrm{id}$ shows that their limiting polynomial
maps are mutually inverse; boundedness needed for substitution follows
from the uniform estimates just proved. The same argument proves
preservation of composition.

Apply these limits to any rescaled arc in $S$ and then apply the inverse
to obtain both inclusions of the tangent-set identity. Alternatively,
valuation compatibility of the Taylor series and reduction modulo the
positive-valuation ideal give
\[
 \operatorname{res}(t^{-w'}h(y))=
 h_{[w,w']}\bigl(\operatorname{res}(t^{-w}y)\bigr),
\]
which proves commutation with \eqref{eq:v101-residue}. Ramification
follows by the substitution $t=s^q$. Finally a transported section and
decoder have the same feasible decoded points by their definitions;
the metric is preserved exactly when its transport is respected.
\end{proof}

For example, at weights $(1,2)$ the map
$h(y_1,y_2)=(y_1,y_2+y_1^2)$ is bi-filtered and its initial map is
nonlinear. The theorem thus includes weighted changes beyond ordinary
linear similarities, while stating precisely which metric data must be
transported. Neither the residue comparison nor the implicit-function
lifting test is presented as a new tropical fundamental theorem. The
observation-specific finite determination is
Theorem~\ref{thm:v101-finite}; the comparison specifies the real
object to which that theorem applies.
