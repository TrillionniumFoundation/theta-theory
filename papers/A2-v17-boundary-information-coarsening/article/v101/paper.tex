\begin{abstract}
We study unordered polynomial spectra observed through unknown stochastic
channels and a scalar normalization. For the full-rank coprime binary
model of fixed degree, we prove that a finite collection of scalar
constrained observation costs determines the complete leading spectral
fibre, with all channel and mixture parameters free. The costs recover
a positive definite quotient form, and the multiplicity pattern decodes
its cone section into a joint root set. At degree $d$, at most
$d(2d+1)$ scalar calibrations suffice; perturbations of those values give
explicit Hausdorff and diameter bounds. For an isolated Nash remote
observation germ, we identify the entrance exponent as the maximal
residual-to-time order ratio over accessible real divisors and identify
its leading coefficient by a resolved metric-unit maximum. A Puiseux
residue theorem relates the real weighted fibre to coordinate real
tropicalization, while finite weighted jets imply filtered naturality
without an assumed convergence of conjugates. The complete multiplicity
law, rank-deficient cubic fibre, inverse through channel-rank loss,
weight wall, endpoint wall, and their exact critical-shell calculations
are retained. The general semialgebraic atlas and entrance asymptotics
remain available beyond the explicit binary normal forms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A normalized polynomial experiment can be locally identifying and yet
poorly resolve its roots. It can also admit a remote exact representative
which enters a small observation ball while the local inverse remains
coercive. These are different phenomena. The first depends on the joint
geometry of root splitting and the observation metric; the second
depends on contact between a moving observation and a remote image.
This article determines both for stochastic polynomial observations.

Our concrete experiment is
\[
 K(z)=\sum_{a=1}^2\alpha_a f_a(z)u_av_a^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 P_j=K(T_j)/\bigl(\one^{\mathsf T}K(T_j)\one\bigr),
\]
with monic degree-$d$ real-rooted components, roots in $[0,D]$, strictly
positive centre weights, and unknown binary stochastic channels. The
target is the unordered root multiset of $f_1f_2$. The observation
radius $t$ is weighted Hellinger distance; the spectral modulus is the
\emph{diameter of the entire feasible set} in the bottleneck metric,
not only its maximal distance from the central spectrum.

\subsection*{Finite information determining a joint root fibre}
For coprime components, invertible strictly positive channels and at
least $2d+1$ exterior clocks, normalizer recovery and a matrix-pencil
inverse control all coefficient and stochastic errors by observation
error. Real-root cluster geometry then leaves only two leading regimes:
an interior repeated root splits at order $\sqrt t$, while simple and
one-sided boundary roots move at order $t$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v101-finite} identifies a finite scalar datum which
retains the joint metric information lost by scalar orders. On each
marked multiplicity pattern there are $k\le2d$ retained sum or
variance coordinates. For $v$ in their feasible cone, constrain those
coordinates to equal $tv$, minimize over the remaining roots and all
stochastic parameters, and set
\[
 c_\theta(v)=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac4{t^2}
      \min h_w(F_T(\xi),P_\theta)^2.
\]
These limits are not formal probes of an arbitrary compact set. They
are costs of actual polynomial-root competitors in the full model.
We prove that $c_\theta(v)=v^{\mathsf T}Q_\theta v$, with
$Q_\theta>0$, and hence that the $k(k+1)/2$ values
\[
 c_\theta(e_i),\qquad c_\theta(e_i+e_j)\quad(i<j)
\]
recover the entire quotient form. The multiplicity pattern decodes
$\{v:v^{\mathsf T}Q_\theta v\le4\}$ into the complete joint leading
root set. Corollary~\ref{cor:v101-stability} gives quantitative metric
stability from errors in these finitely many scalars. This finite
sufficiency result is the structural starting point of the article.

The underlying multiplicity law is preserved in full. If an interior
cluster is repeated, its sharp whole-model modulus is
\[
 \omega_\theta(t)=C(\theta)\sqrt t+O_H(t),\qquad
 C(\theta)=\max_j2\sqrt{(m_j-1)/m_j}\,\kappa_j^{-1/4}.
\]
Otherwise the modulus is linear. The complete root sets, endpoint
constraints, optimal coefficient orders and compact-uniform errors are
in Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-atlas}. The calibration theorem strengthens
their information content without replacing the root-expansion proof
by a general definability argument.

\subsection*{Remote geometry and the two cubic walls}
The rank-deficient cubic family $f=z(z-r)^2$, $g=(z-s)^3$ has an exact
fibre controlled by
\[
 B=(3D-r)s-2rD,\qquad
 E=(1-\alpha)(r+3s)(2r-3s)^2-4\pi r^3.
\]
Its identifying locus is
$\{\det U\ne0\}\cup\{B<0\}\cup\{E<0\}$.
The complete classification, inverse through rank loss, oblique local
Fisher geometry and both identification walls are given by the direct
pencil and feasible-chart arguments below. The weight wall and endpoint
wall have different nonidentified-side openings; neither is removed by
passing to an abstract tangent relation. The exact equality-shell and
signed second-order calculations are retained.

For a more general isolated remote germ, let
$R(x,\delta)=f(x)-p(\delta)$ and
$d(\delta)=\min_x\|R(x,\delta)\|$. In the nonexact Nash case,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v101-divisorial} identifies
\[
 \rho=\max_{E\ \mathrm{real\ accessible}}
              \frac{\operatorname{ord}_E(R_1,\ldots,R_M)}
                   {\operatorname{ord}_E\delta},\qquad
 d(\delta)=M^{-1}\delta^\rho+o(\delta^\rho).
\]
Here only divisors with positive time order enter the maximum, and
$M$ is the maximum of the continuous resolved extension of
$\delta^\rho/\|R\|$ on the accessible zero-time preimage.
The exponent is thus a relative real contact invariant of a residual
ideal and a time ideal, not an invariant of the remote image alone.
The coefficient requires their observation-metric units. The formula
recovers the singular contact $n/m$ and its sharp coefficient in the
existing polynomial probability example, and recovers order one and
the cone-projection coefficient in the regular regime.
The continuous-semialgebraic family theorem, its rational order lists,
its uniform remainders and its exact threshold decisions remain in
Theorem~\ref{thm:singular-entrances}.

\subsection*{Which real weighted object is being measured?}
The general weighted relation retains real feasibility before imposing
a norm and decoding roots. Theorem~\ref{thm:v101-residue} identifies
it with the residue of its bounded weighted points over the real
algebraic Puiseux field:
\[
 T_w^+S=\operatorname{res}\{t^{-w}y:y\in S(\R\langle t\rangle),
                                      \operatorname{val}y\ge w\}.
\]
Its nonzero coordinate signs and valuations give the corresponding
fibre of coordinate real tropicalization \cite{A2JSY22}; the residue
magnitudes, observation-unit section and spectral decoder are retained
before that projection. Positive real scale is distinguished from
positive coordinate orthant, and the precise sufficient smooth lifting
test is stated alongside the comparison with positive tropical
lifting \cite{A2RT26}. Theorem~\ref{thm:v101-filtered} derives the
required conjugate limits from finite weighted jets of a Nash
isomorphism and its inverse.

The earlier algebraic initial-fibre comparison, scalar distance-function
reconstruction, sharp-marginal separation and full binary valuative
separation are all retained. The distance-function identity is a general
metric fact; it is not the finite model-specific theorem above.
Likewise, ordinary valuation data discard positive metric units; the
binary separation example makes that mechanism explicit rather than
claiming that the mechanism itself is new. The residue comparison
positions the object against real tropical geometry without claiming
a new tropical fundamental theorem.

\subsection*{Organization and dependencies}
The model inverse, multiplicity law, finite calibration, cubic fibre and
two walls come first. Their proofs are direct. The relative real atlas,
weighted comparisons and singular entrance theory follow, with the
complete foundational arguments in the appendix. Definition
\ref{def:v101-format} specifies the finite complexity datum underlying
all fixed-format assertions. Common monomial presentations concern a
finite chosen diagram, not every scalar polynomial simultaneously.
Appendix~\ref{app:v101-format} states exactly which concrete proofs do
and do not require the general atlas. All proofs and finite calculations
from the preceding article are retained; its full text and historical
archive also remain in the separate archival volume.
