\section{Format, finite diagrams, and theorem dependencies}\label{app:v101-format}
We make explicit two conventions used in the general semialgebraic
statements. Neither convention alters the whole-model quantifiers in
the concrete binary theorems.

\begin{definition}[Geometric and arithmetic format]\label{def:v101-format}
A fixed-format family is specified by a fixed finite list of formulas
for the feasible-set graph, observation graph, monic target-coefficient
graph, centre-path graph, and any isolating neighbourhoods or threshold
functions used in the assertion. Its geometric format is
\[
 \mathcal F=(n_\Theta,n_X,n_{\mathrm{aux}},M,d_A,s,D,L).
\]
Here the first three numbers bound the parameter, source and auxiliary
variables in the entire list; $M$ is the observation dimension; $d_A$
is the target degree; $s$ bounds the number of polynomial atoms;
$D$ bounds their total degrees; and $L$ bounds the length of their
Boolean and quantifier syntax trees, with the quantifier blocks included.
Rational functions are encoded by graph equations and denominator sign
conditions. A Nash branch is encoded by its polynomial equations and
real branch inequalities. Square roots, root constraints, path
ramifications and neighbourhood choices are included as auxiliary
graph data, not treated as zero-cost primitive operations.

Coefficients may range over real parameters for geometric finiteness.
For effective algebraic input, each fixed coefficient is specified by
a square-free integer polynomial and a rational isolating interval,
with a consistent common real algebraic embedding for joint arithmetic.
Bounds on their degrees and binary encoding lengths may be appended
as the arithmetic format. No bit-complexity estimate is claimed here.
A new threshold or a selected scalar polynomial must be included in
the finite list before a uniform bound is asserted for it.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[An explicit meaning of the finite order list]\label{prop:v101-format}
For the format in Definition~\ref{def:v101-format}, there is a
computable integer $B(\mathcal F)$ such that every nonzero exponent
in Theorem~\ref{thm:singular-entrances} belongs to
\[
 \left\{p/q:1\le p,q\le B(\mathcal F)\right\}.
\]
When the centre path is $C^1$, only the members at least one occur.
The integer can be obtained from a real quantifier-elimination degree
bound applied to the actual minimum graph formula; it is not an
unquantified appeal to a notion of bounded format.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The graph formula for the attained minimum uses one copy of the
source variables for an attaining point, another for comparison with
all competitors, the already specified graph formulas, and an equation
for the squared norm. Its numbers of variables, atoms and degrees are
bounded by computable functions of $\mathcal F$. Apply real quantifier
elimination \cite{BPR06} and let $B$ bound the degrees of all output
polynomials, increasing it if necessary to a positive integer.
Fix a parameter. On a final positive interval the graph of a nonzero
minimum lies on the zero set of a nonzero specialized output polynomial
$P(\delta,z)$: otherwise a sign cell containing the graph would have
interior in the $(\delta,z)$ plane, contrary to its being a graph.
There are only finitely many cells and polynomials, so one final branch
and a nonzero polynomial can be selected. Identically zero specialized
polynomials are discarded first.

Substitute $z=a\delta^\rho+o(\delta^\rho)$, $a>0$, using the
one-variable semialgebraic Puiseux theorem. At least two distinct
monomials $\delta^i z^j$ have the lowest order; a unique lowest term
could not cancel. They must have different $j$, since otherwise equality
of orders would force equal $i$. Thus
$\rho=(i-i')/(j'-j)$, with nonzero integer denominator and numerator
of absolute value at most $B$. Positivity of $\rho$ gives the asserted
finite list after reduction. Vanishing coefficients only remove
monomials and do not enlarge this bound. The $C^1$ lower bound follows
from the fixed exact competitor as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:singular-entrances}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite scalar families, not one modification for all polynomials}
A common monomial presentation is asserted for every \emph{finite
selected} family of functions, ideals and inequalities, after a common
refinement depending on that family. It is not asserted that one fixed
modification simultaneously monomializes the infinite ring of all
polynomial scalar functions of the target coefficients. This convention
applies in particular to the divisorial-order interpretation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-separation}. The theorem's comparison along
every analytic arc and for every scalar polynomial remains valid
pointwise for each polynomial; it does not require a simultaneous
principalization for all of them.

There is a simple reason for the distinction. In a plane, the infinitely
many germs $x-cy$, $c\in\R$, have distinct strict transforms. No fixed
finite normal-crossings divisor can contain the zero divisors of all
of them. A finite chosen list can be included in a resolution diagram;
its size and degrees then enter $\mathcal F$. The contact formula in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v101-divisorial} uses only the finite residual/time
pair and feasibility inequalities. The finite calibration theorem uses
exactly the probes listed in \eqref{eq:v101-finite-datum}.

\subsection{Dependency of the concrete results}
The normalizer recovery and full-rank coefficient inverse are direct
interpolation and matrix-pencil arguments. The multiplicity and
boundary laws use these inverses, real-root cluster expansions and
finite Fisher projection. The finite calibration theorem uses the same
inverse to prove the convergence of \emph{actual} constrained minima
and their whole-model recovery; its stability bound is finite-dimensional.
None of these proofs needs the relative resolution atlas. The
semialgebraic refinement of their algebraic output functions is the
separate organizing use of real algebraic geometry.

The exact cubic fibre and the inverse through rank loss are direct
affine-pencil and second-marginal arguments. The weight and endpoint
walls require their different exact feasible charts and the regular
conic expansion. Those proofs, including equality-shell membership and
the signed second correction, do not depend on a general singular
resolution theorem. They have therefore been placed before the general
weighted framework in the principal article.

The general atlas is essential to the uniform weighted output theorem
for arbitrary specified semialgebraic observation relations, beyond the
explicit binary normal forms. Real curve selection and algebraic
Puiseux germs give the residue comparison. Simultaneous principalization
of the residual/time pair supplies the divisorial entrance formula;
the scalar semialgebraic entrance theorem supplies the broader
continuous-semialgebraic statement and uniform family refinements.
These are distinct inputs and conclusions. We do not claim that the
existence of a general atlas is necessary for a computation already
proved by the concrete pencil or Fisher arguments.
