\section{Two-sided deficiency for the moving-ceiling experiment}
\label{sec:v24-endpoint-time-deficiency}

We give parameter-independent kernels in both directions for the local
endpoint--time experiment.  Besides convergence of the ceiling and its trace,
the argument uses a uniform first-order relative density bound on the common
bulk.  We state this bound explicitly: convergence of a trace in \(C^0\)
alone would not imply the required product Hellinger estimate.

For one design write \(k=k_n\), \(w=w_\ell\), \(\rho=\rho_\ell\), and
\(U=U_\ell\).  Let \(K\) be a fixed compact local parameter set containing
zero.  On a fixed bounded endpoint box \(V\), the exact successful density
has the form
\[
 q_{n,z}(u,v,r)=\rho_{n,z}(u,v,r)
                  \mathbf1_{\{0<r<w_{n,z}(u,v)\}}.
\]
All density factors extend to a common bounded residual-time box, with
uniform upper and positive lower bounds.  Put \(D=\{w>0\}\), whose boundary
is regular in the endpoint box, and assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-deficiency-remainder}
 \varepsilon_{n,K}:=\sup_{z\in K}
 \left(k\|w_{n,z}-w+k^{-1}Uz\|_{C^1}
                      +\|\rho_{n,z}-\rho\|_{C^0}\right)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Here \(\rho=\rho(u,v)\) is independent of \(r\) and
\(\int_D\rho w\,du\,dv=1\).  In addition suppose that on the common bulk
specified below,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-bulk-relative-hypothesis}
 \sup_{z\in K}\left\|\frac{\rho_{n,z}}{\rho_{n,0}}-1\right\|_\infty
                                                     \le C_K/k.
\end{equation}
A bounded expansion \(1+k^{-1}a_{n,z}+o_K(k^{-1})\) is sufficient, but
not necessary, for this bound.

These hypotheses hold for the anchored fixed-window boundary model of
Section~\ref{sec:v23-full-endpoint-time}.  Its parameter displacement is
\(O_K(k^{-1})\), its normalized smooth density factor is independent of
\(r\), and its reference factor is bounded away from zero.  Smooth
dependence of that factor and its positive normalizer gives
\eqref{eq:v25-bulk-relative-hypothesis} uniformly on the box.
Equation~\eqref{eq:v23-et-ceiling-expansion} gives the support estimate;
the residual gap term is \(O((j_nk_n)^{-1})=o(k_n^{-1})\).  Thus the
additional displayed hypothesis is a verified dependency of the physical
application, not a deduction from bare trace convergence.

Choose
\[
 R>2+\sup_{z\in K,(u,v)\in\overline V}|U(u,v)z|
\]
and define the reference layer
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-reference-layer}
                 L_{n,R}=\{|k(w(u,v)-r)|\le R\}.
\end{equation}
It is independent of \(z\).  For large \(n\), the support outside this
layer is the common set \(0<r<w-R/k\); the alternative ceilings cannot
reach the other side \(r>w+R/k\).  This is the bulk in
\eqref{eq:v25-bulk-relative-hypothesis}.

Let \(p_{n,z}=Q_{n,z}(L_{n,R})\), let \(A_{n,z}\) be its conditional
layer law after \(y=k(w-r)\), and let \(B_{n,z}\) be the conditional bulk
law.  The target intensity is the finite measure on
\(D\times[-R,R]\) with density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-Lambda-R}
 d\Lambda_z^R=\rho(u,v)
  \mathbf1_{\{y>U(u,v)z\}}\mathbf1_{\{|y|\le R\}}\,du\,dv\,dy.
\end{equation}
The factor in this formula is the previously defined density \(\rho\).

\begin{lemma}[Uniform layer and bulk bounds]
\label{lem:v24-layer-bulk-bounds}
Uniformly for \(z\in K\),
\begin{align}
 p_{n,z}&=O(k^{-1}),\label{eq:v24-layer-p}\\
 \left|k\,Q_{n,z}\circ(u,v,r\mapsto u,v,k(w-r))^{-1}
                    \big|_{L_{n,R}}-\Lambda_z^R\right|(V\times[-R,R])
 &\le C_K(\varepsilon_{n,K}+k^{-1}),\label{eq:v24-layer-measure}\\
 H^2(B_{n,z},B_{n,0})&\le C_Kk^{-2}.\label{eq:v24-bulk-H}
\end{align}
The notation in the second line means that the layer submeasure is
restricted before pushforward, and that the target intensity is extended
by zero from \(D\) to \(V\).  The vertical bars denote total variation
mass of a finite signed measure.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The layer has residual thickness \(2R/k\) over a bounded endpoint set,
which gives \eqref{eq:v24-layer-p}.  Regularity of \(\{w=0\}\) implies
\(\operatorname{Leb}\{|w|\le C/k\}=O(k^{-1})\).  On this endpoint strip
any physical residual interval involved has length \(O(k^{-1})\), so its
one-record mass is \(O(k^{-2})\).  The part of the target intensity on
\(0<w\le C/k\) is \(O(k^{-1})\) as well.  These estimates handle both
the intersection with the fixed face \(r=0\) and any alternative support
created just outside \(D\).

Off that strip, \(r>0\) imposes no additional restriction in the reference
layer.  The change of variable has Jacobian \(k^{-1}\), and the ceiling
condition becomes
\[
                         y>U(u,v)z+e_{n,z}(u,v),
             \qquad \|e_{n,z}\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_{n,K}.
\]
Fiber integration bounds the symmetric difference by the endpoint area
times \(\varepsilon_{n,K}\), while the trace-density difference is
bounded by the second term of \eqref{eq:v24-deficiency-remainder}.  Adding
the corner error proves \eqref{eq:v24-layer-measure}.  In particular, the
unscaled mass attributable to the support remainder is at most
\(C_K\varepsilon_{n,K}/k\).

On the common bulk write \(\rho_{n,z}/\rho_{n,0}=1+e_{n,z}^B\), with
\(\|e_{n,z}^B\|_\infty\le C_K/k\).  Dividing by the bulk normalizers
gives exactly
\[
 \frac{dB_{n,z}}{dB_{n,0}}=
       \frac{1+e_{n,z}^B}{1+B_{n,0}e_{n,z}^B}=1+O_K(k^{-1}).
\]
The inequality \((\sqrt{1+x}-1)^2\le Cx^2\) for small \(x\) proves
\eqref{eq:v24-bulk-H}.  This proof identifies the relative-density
hypothesis actually used in the bulk and controls the normalized conditional
law, not merely a marginal fluctuation.
\end{proof}

Let \(\mathsf E_{n,K}^R=\{Q_{n,z}^{\otimes k}:z\in K\}\) and
\(\mathsf P_K^R=\{\operatorname{PPP}(\Lambda_z^R):z\in K\}\).

\begin{theorem}[Explicit two-sided deficiency]
\label{thm:v24-two-sided-deficiency}
Under the displayed support, positivity and relative-density hypotheses,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-LeCam-bound}
 \Delta(\mathsf E_{n,K}^R,\mathsf P_K^R)
          \le C_K(\varepsilon_{n,K}+k^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
Both deficiencies admit Markov kernels independent of \(z\).  For finitely
many independent batches the bounds add.  In particular this proves the
two-sided equivalence claimed in Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-et-poisson}; it is
stronger than convergence of the extracted point process alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The forward kernel retains records in \(L_{n,R}\), replaces \(r\) by
\(y=k(w-r)\), and discards points outside \(D\).  All operations use
reference quantities only.  Before the last discard, the retained sample
is a binomial point process.  Its distance from the Poisson process with
the same intensity is at most \(Ckp_{n,z}^2=O(k^{-1})\).  Poisson coupling
and \eqref{eq:v24-layer-measure} bound the further intensity error by
\(C_K(\varepsilon_{n,K}+k^{-1})\).  Discarding exterior points has already
been accounted for in the corner estimate and cannot increase distance.
This proves the forward deficiency bound.

For the reverse kernel, let \(m\) be the number of input Poisson points.
If \(m\le k\), map each point to \((u,v,r=w-y/k)\), draw \(k-m\)
independent records from the reference bulk law \(B_{n,0}\), and interleave
the two collections by a uniformly random permutation.  If a reconstructed
point has \(r\le0\), replace it by one fixed reference record.  Such a
point has \(0<w\le R/k\), so the probability that this convention is
used is \(O(k^{-1})\).  If \(m>k\), output one fixed \(k\)-sample.
The Poisson means are uniformly bounded, hence
\(\sup_{z\in K}\Pr_z\{m>k\}=o(k^{-A})\) for every fixed \(A\).
This defines a valid parameter-independent kernel on every configuration,
including corner and count exceptions.

For the original iid sample, conditional on its layer count, the layer
records and bulk records have respective iid laws \(A_{n,z}\) and
\(B_{n,z}\), are independent of each other, and are uniformly interleaved.
The layer count and locations can be coupled with the Poisson input with
the forward error just proved.  Replacing the conditional bulk by its
reference law costs, uniformly in \(m\le k\),
\[
 H^2(B_{n,z}^{\otimes(k-m)},B_{n,0}^{\otimes(k-m)})
       \le(k-m)H^2(B_{n,z},B_{n,0})\le C_K/k.
\]
Its total variation cost is therefore \(O_K(k^{-1/2})\).  The common
permutation does not increase distance.  Adding the corner and count tails
gives \eqref{eq:v24-LeCam-bound}.  Independent-product coupling proves
tensorization over finitely many batches.  For the proportions in
\eqref{eq:v23-et-design-proportions}, scale by total \(k_n\); the intensity
in a batch is multiplied by \(k_{n,\ell}/k_n\), converging to
\(\alpha_\ell\).  The resulting additional intensity error tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The projective Poisson experiment]
\label{rem:v24-projective-poisson}
On \(D\times\mathbb R\), the intensity
\(\rho(u,v)\mathbf1_{\{y>U(u,v)z\}}du\,dv\,dy\) is locally finite.
Restriction from a larger finite strip to a smaller one is a common Markov
kernel.  Once \(R\) exceeds every displacement on the compact parameter
set, the added positive tail has parameter-independent intensity and the
negative tail is empty.  It can thus be adjoined as an ancillary factor.
This precise projective interpretation does not require an informal limit
in the reverse reconstruction kernel.
\end{remark}
