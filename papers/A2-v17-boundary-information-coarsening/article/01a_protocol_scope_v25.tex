\subsection{From relative laws to physical observation protocols}
\label{subsec:v25-protocol-scope}

The relative comparison retains the probability of obtaining a selected
record.  In Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-transfer}, the finite and boundary
subprobability laws are compared at each table in common endpoint and
residual-time coordinates, with their missing-event atom included.  In
unscaled physical positions the same boundary embeddings are used for the
two laws at that table.  This proves a bound between statistical families;
it does not supply a sampling kernel that knows the unknown table's
contact center or tangent.

For predetermined physical windows \(t_i\), the excess
\(d_i(T)=t_i-j_i g_e(T)\) is used only in the proof.  The acquisition need
not observe the gap.  For adaptive designs and stopped procedures,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer} compares the complete declared
transcript, with a bound of the form
\[
 \|\mathsf P_T^\pi-\mathsf Q_T^\pi\|_{\rm TV}
       \le C\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_T^\pi}
                    \sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal T}Y_i\tau^{j(a_i)}.
\]
Here \(\mathcal T\) is the stopping time, \(a_i\) the chosen design and
\(Y_i\) its success indicator.  A deterministically capped batch stopping
no later than its \(k\)-th success has error at most \(Ck\tau^J\) when
all its flight numbers are at least \(J\).  No failed preparation is
removed from the cost.

There are two different realizations of endpoint coordinates in this
article.  The local information theorems in
Section~\ref{sec:v22-raw-physical} concern an anchored registered family:
the reported scalar transverse coordinates are literal laboratory
coordinates fixed throughout that family, and the programmed windows are
\(t_{j,\ell}=jg_0+d_\ell\).  The local gap parameter is part of the
likelihood, not supplied to the acquisition.  The following information
hierarchy concerns this declared scalar local record and its coarsenings.
It does not assert that the local Poisson or Gaussian limit exhausts all
information in a two-dimensional position sensor.

The global unknown-contact experiment is specified separately in
Section~\ref{sec:v25-common-observables}.  Each channel sensor records both
components of its first and last endpoint positions before any transverse
projection.  A single sensor frame is shared by the two types of that
channel, while different channel frames are not registered.  The charged
pilot of Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-observable-calibration} estimates the contact
positions, tangent and onset.  The subsequent transverse normalization is
then a measurable function of those estimates and a fresh physical record.
Proposition~\ref{prop:v25-test-implementation} bounds its error for the
finite bounded Lipschitz tests used by the global estimator.  The
parameter-dependent ideal normalization appears only in this error bound,
not among the observations.

Thus the same-table physical-to-boundary comparison, the anchored local
information experiment, the observable within-channel calibration, and the
intrinsic inter-channel gluing have different roles.  Their hypotheses are
not exchanged.  The earlier centered-window and fixed-table coarsening
results remain in the auxiliary compendium with their stated preparation
costs and calibration assumptions.
