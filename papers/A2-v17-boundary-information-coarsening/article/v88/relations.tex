\section{Relation to the other observation laws}
\label{sec:relations}
In degree one, normalizing raw matrices
$c(T)U\diag(a_b(T-\xi_b))V^{\mathsf T}$, with an unknown positive
scalar detector $c(T)$, gives \eqref{eq:model} with
$\alpha_b=a_b/\sum_ca_c$. In particular the logarithmic action law
is unchanged:
\[
 \log\frac{p_b(T)}{p_c(T)}
   =\log\frac{\alpha_b}{\alpha_c}+\log(T-\xi_b)-\log(T-\xi_c).
\]
For $k=m_1=m_2=2$, stochastic columns imply
\[
 \frac{|\det U|}{\sqrt2}\le\sigma_2(U)\le|\det U|,
 \qquad
 \frac{|\det V|}{\sqrt2}\le\sigma_2(V)\le|\det V|.
\]
Indeed the largest singular value lies between $1$ and $\sqrt2$,
and the product of the two singular values is the absolute determinant.
On an action class with $\xi_2-\xi_1\ge\Delta>0$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:factors} therefore gives the single inverse
power of $|\det U\det V|$. The identity
$\alpha_1=(\xi_2-h)/(\xi_2-\xi_1)$ also recovers the relative
throughput with a constant depending on this fixed gap.

The shared two-clock spatial experiment has a different nuisance
space: the clock factors are common across sites. Spatial calibration
can replace a third clock there. Its exact identification and
one-clock geometric alternatives, and its local correctly-labelled
calibration estimate, are separate from the pointwise projective
experiment treated here. The local estimate is not a global bound
through collisions of spatial profiles.

The polynomial, rational and compact-surface detector quotients in
the historical derivations retain their respective secant and tangent
tests, divisor counts, and branched-clock forms of the logarithmic law.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} concerns the specific simultaneous polynomial
numerator and its scalar normalization. Its normalization-rank
criterion and sharp worst-case $2d+1$ count do not declare every
ambient meromorphic pole-budget bound optimal. In particular,
common-factor cancellation in \eqref{eq:kernel} is a property of this
projective observation, not an identification of all those detector
quotients with one another.

Finite-record geometric certificates, component coverings,
return-lift constructions, and raw-acquisition laws remain logically
separate. Where the recovered actions determine boundary-distance data,
metric rigidity is supplied by the stated classical geometric
rigidity hypotheses in those derivations. The present results concern
the upstream observation inverse. All proofs of the principal results
above are contained in this article and require no geometric appendix;
the other regimes are retained as an independent archive rather than
added as hypotheses to this theorem.
