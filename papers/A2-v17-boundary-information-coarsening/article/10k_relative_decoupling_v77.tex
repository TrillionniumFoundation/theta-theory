\subsection{A dimension-independent two-end comparison}
\label{sec:v76-relative-principle}

The determinant argument admits a formulation that separates the size of
an experiment from the interaction of its two ends.  We state it for
finite matrices of arbitrary size; padding a matrix by zero does not
change any of the quantities below.  The trace and Hilbert--Schmidt norms
are denoted by $\|\cdot\|_1$ and $\|\cdot\|_2$, respectively.
For a parameter domain $U$, a $C^k(U;\mathfrak S_p)$ bound means the
maximum of these norms over all parameter derivatives of order at most
$k$.  Operator norms will be written without a subscript.

\begin{proposition}[Separated perturbation determinants]
\label{prop:v76-determinant}
Fix $k\ge0$ and $q<1$.  Relative to two orthogonal end spaces, write
\[
 T_N=\mathsf B_N+E_N,\qquad \mathsf B_N=\diag(T_{N,-},T_{N,+}),\qquad
 E_N=\begin{pmatrix}0&E_{N,-+}\\E_{N,+-}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here $\mathsf B_N$ is a block operator, not the scalar reference twist
$D_{N,b}$.  Suppose, uniformly on $U$ and in $N$, that
\[
 \|\mathsf B_N+tE_N\|\le q\quad(0\le t\le1),\qquad
 \|\mathsf B_N\|_{C^k(\mathfrak S_1)}\le M_k,\qquad
 \|E_N\|_{C^k(\mathfrak S_2)}\le\varepsilon_N\le1.
\]
Suppose also that there are functions $\ell_-,\ell_+$ with bounded
$C^k$ norms such that
\[
 \|\log\det(I+T_{N,\pm})-\ell_\pm\|_{C^k(U)}
 \le\delta_N.
\]
All logarithms are defined by the power series at zero.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v76-two-end-determinant}
 \|\log\det(I+T_N)-\ell_--\ell_+\|_{C^k(U)}
 \le C_{k,q,M_k}(\delta_N+\varepsilon_N^2).
\end{equation}
The constant is independent of the dimensions of both end spaces.
If $T_N$ is first replaced by $\widetilde T_N$ with
$C^k(\mathfrak S_1)$ error $\eta_N$, with the same operator margin
and bounded $C^k(\mathfrak S_1)$ norms along the intervening segment,
an additional $C\eta_N$ suffices on the right.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $\|T\|<1$ put
\[
 \mathcal L(T)=\sum_{h\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{h+1}}h\tr(T^h).
\]
This is the indicated logarithm, whether or not the matrix is symmetric.
Along $T(t)=D+tE$, let $R(t)=(I+D+tE)^{-1}$.  Differentiating the
convergent series, and then using cyclicity of trace, gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\mathcal L(D+tE)=\tr(R(t)E),\qquad
 \frac{d^2}{dt^2}\mathcal L(D+tE)=-\tr(R(t)ER(t)E).
\]
The inverse $R(0)$ is block diagonal and $E$ is off diagonal.
Consequently $\tr(R(0)E)=0$.  Taylor's formula is therefore the exact
identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v76-offdiagonal-identity}
 \mathcal L(D+E)-\mathcal L(D)
 =-\int_0^1(1-t)\tr(R(t)ER(t)E)\,dt.
\end{equation}
Since $\|R(t)\|\le(1-q)^{-1}$ and
$|\tr(AB)|\le\|A\|_2\|B\|_2$, the undifferentiated absolute
value is at most $\|E\|_2^2/[2(1-q)^2]$.
For parameter derivatives use
$\partial R=-R\partial(D+tE)R$ repeatedly.  Each differentiated
integrand in \eqref{eq:v76-offdiagonal-identity} still contains the two
displayed $E$ factors, possibly differentiated.  Place these in
$\mathfrak S_2$ and all other factors in operator norm.  The bounds
on $D$, $E$ and $R$ yield $C_{k,q,M_k}\varepsilon_N^2$.
The number of product-rule terms depends on $k$, not on dimension.
The block-diagonal determinant is the product of its two determinants;
adding their stipulated errors proves \eqref{eq:v76-two-end-determinant}.

For the last assertion write $T(t)=T+t(\widetilde T-T)$ and integrate
$\tr((I+T(t))^{-1}(\widetilde T-T))$.  In every parameter derivative
one factor is a derivative of $\widetilde T-T$ in trace norm; all
remaining factors have uniformly bounded operator norm.  This gives
$C\eta_N$.  These arguments also justify each stated fixed derivative:
all the finite-dimensional identities have bounds uniform in dimension.
\end{proof}

A convenient sufficient condition for the diagonal hypothesis is
$C^k(\mathfrak S_1)$ convergence of the padded diagonal blocks to
trace-class half-line operators of norm below $q$.  The same path
argument gives convergence of their logarithms.  Thus no determinant
estimate proportional to the number of interior collisions is needed.
The quadratic off-diagonal term expresses the fact that a closed trace
must cross from one end to the other and back.  A first-order trace-norm
estimate remains sufficient when this refinement is not needed.

\begin{corollary}[Relative comparison before conditioning]
\label{cor:v76-relative-conditioning}
Let $Q$ be a fixed compact gate and let the unconditioned endpoint
density be exactly
\[
 w_N=c\,d_N\,\beta_N r_N,\qquad c>0,\quad d_N>0.
\]
Assume uniform positive lower and upper bounds for $\beta_N,r_N$ and
their limits $\beta,r$, uniform $C^k$ bounds, and
\[
 \|\log\beta_N-\log\beta\|_{C^k(Q)}
       +\|r_N-r\|_{C^k(Q)}\le e_N.
\]
If $Z_N=\int_Q\beta_Nr_N$ and $Z=\int_Q\beta r$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v76-relative-conditioning}
 \left\|\frac{w_N}{\int_Qw_N}-\frac{\beta r}{Z}\right\|_{C^k(Q)}
 +\left|\frac{\int_Qw_N}{c\,d_N}-Z\right|\le C e_N.
\end{equation}
The constant is independent of $d_N$, which may tend to zero at any
rate.  In particular, a scalar cofactor identity
$\log\beta_N=b_N-\log\det(I+T_N)$, with a separated edge sum
$b_N$ and the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:v76-determinant},
gives such a comparison with the corresponding end errors.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Exponentiation is locally Lipschitz on bounded $C^k$ sets.  Products
therefore compare $\beta_Nr_N$ with $\beta r$ with error $Ce_N$.
Integration compares $Z_N$ with $Z$, and the fixed positive gate gives
a positive lower bound for both integrals.  Division by these integrals
proves the first estimate.  The second uses the exact equality
$\int_Qw_N=c\,d_NZ_N$.  Neither step divides an approximation error
by $d_N$.
\end{proof}

For the billiard operator the end spaces are the first and last
$\lfloor N/3\rfloor$ sites after the middle perturbation has been
removed.  The Green and gluing bounds in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v64-periodic-relative} give the trace-norm deletion
error, the two diagonal limits and the Hilbert--Schmidt off-diagonal
bound, all exponentially small up to fixed polynomial factors in $N$.
Shrinking the collar gives the operator margin along the entire segment.
Consequently the proposition applies before the cofactor is converted
to a probability law.  In Corollary~\ref{cor:v76-relative-conditioning}
one takes $d_N=D_{N,b}$, $c=(2\pi\mathcal A)^{-1}$, and
$r_N=d-E_{N,b}+p_bu-p_bv$.  The geometry is responsible for these
hypotheses and for the exact physical clock; the operator comparison
itself does not use periodicity, a billiard reflection rule, or a lower
bound on the total success mass.
