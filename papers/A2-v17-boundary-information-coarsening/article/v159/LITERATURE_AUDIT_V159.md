# Primary-source comparison for A2 revision 159

This record separates an inspected theorem or construction from bibliographic access. It does not purport to establish exhaustive historical priority. The new modular statements are proved in the submitted source, not inferred from failure to find a matching title.

## Symmetric invariant ideals and multipliers

I. Henriques and M. Varbaro, *Test, multiplier and invariant ideals*, Advances in Mathematics 287 (2016), 704–732, DOI `10.1016/j.aim.2015.09.028`, arXiv `1407.4324`.

The arXiv full text and publisher's theorem text were inspected. Section 2.4, Theorems 2.6–2.8 recall the symmetric invariant-ideal and determinantal-product machinery; Theorem 4.8 gives the symmetric multiplier formula. Paper II, Section 2, now makes this a direct comparison: its coefficient identification realizes a particular multiplication map by an ordinary balanced minor product. It is not a new classification of invariant ideals. The multiplier corollary is a substitution into the established formula. The original Abeasis article is not represented as newly obtained merely because its statements are recalled in this primary source.

Accessed primary sources: `https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.4324` and `https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2015.09.028`.

## Complete-quadric graph and blow-up constructions

A. Massarenti, *On the birational geometry of spaces of complete forms I: collineations and quadrics*, arXiv `1803.09161`.

Remark 2.5 identifies the symmetric exterior-power graph and Construction 2.6 gives the successive symmetric rank blow-ups. The exposition explicitly attributes the scheme construction and boundary smoothness to Vainsencher's Theorem 6.3. These passages were inspected in the full primary exposition. A separate complete copy of Vainsencher's original paper was not obtained in this audit; the citation to its theorem is transparently mediated by this exact construction, not claimed as a new direct inspection of the original proof.

Accessed primary source: `https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.09161`.

A. Casarotti, E. Corniani and A. Massarenti, *Complete singular collineations and quadrics*, International Mathematics Research Notices 2023, no. 18, 15370–15407, DOI `10.1093/imrn/rnac271`, arXiv `2111.02940`.

Definition 2.5, Remark 2.6 and Theorem 2.14 were inspected for the singular-rank version, smoothness and orbit structure. The all-rank power theorem uses the classical variety: its assertion is exact recovery of the image plane and the full graph scheme from multiplication in a fixed algebra. Classical orbit strata are not counted as new classifications.

Accessed primary sources: `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02940` and the publisher's IMRN entry for the DOI above.

## Symmetric pencil congruence

R. C. Thompson, *Pencils of complex and real symmetric and skew matrices*, Linear Algebra and its Applications 147 (1991), 323–371, DOI `10.1016/0024-3795(91)90238-R`.

The publisher's primary entry confirms the congruence-canonical-form subject and bibliographic data. The paper invokes complex symmetric Kronecker congruence as a classical input, not an original classification. The needed DVR congruence argument is supplied directly in Paper II, Lemma 7.1. The nullity inversion and the contact/image-degree identity are then proved from the displayed blocks and bundle sequences. Bibliographic/abstract inspection is not presented as a fresh line-by-line audit of all 49 pages of Thompson's proof.

Accessed primary source: `https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/002437959190238R`.

## Scope of the boundary contribution

The classical graph construction alone does not compute the Hilbert fibre of a pencil family. Paper II, Theorem 11.3, evaluates the ordinary ideals on the total local surface, identifies its graph modification, and proves flatness, reducedness, multidegrees and all tail coefficient systems. Theorem 11.4 constructs actual flat families of distinct embedded limits over a fixed semisimple pencil, proves smoothing realizability and obtains an incidence-fibre dimension bound. The general Hilbert-functor universal property is credited as standard; the asserted contribution is the specified boundary family and its multiplication data. No exhaustive claim about the absence of related constructions elsewhere is made.
