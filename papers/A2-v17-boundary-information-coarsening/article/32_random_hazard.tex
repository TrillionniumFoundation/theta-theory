\subsection{Random accumulated hazard}
The deterministic limit in Corollary~\ref{cor:v16-adaptive-critical-limit}
has the following distributional form. This clarification is recorded
in the independent memorandum~\cite[Section 4.4]{A2ReviewV16}.

\begin{corollary}[Random-hazard mixture]
\label{cor:v17-random-hazard}
Use the same capped policies, common-history laws and physical
approximation budgets as in Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-critical}.
Suppose, under $\mathsf R^{\pi_n}$,
\[
 H_n:=\sum_{i\le T_n}h(a_i)\ \Longrightarrow\ B,
 \qquad M_n:=\max_{i\le T_n}h(a_i)\longrightarrow0
                  \quad\hbox{in probability},
\]
where $B$ is a finite nonnegative random variable. If
$\varepsilon_{\pi_n}\to0$, then
\[
 \|\mathsf P^{\pi_n}-\mathsf Q^{\pi_n}\|_{\rm TV}
       \longrightarrow 1-\mathbb E e^{-B},
 \qquad \inf\{\text{equal-prior testing error}\}
       \longrightarrow \tfrac12\mathbb E e^{-B}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The convergence in distribution makes $H_n$ tight. On $M_n<1$, the
logarithmic inequality in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:v16-adaptive-critical-limit} gives
\[
 0\le-\log\prod_{i\le T_n}(1-h(a_i))-H_n
                   \le\frac{M_nH_n}{2(1-M_n)}.
\]
The right side tends to zero in probability. Consequently the product
converges in distribution to $e^{-B}$. It takes values in $[0,1]$,
so its expectation tends to $\mathbb E e^{-B}$ by bounded continuous
convergence. Apply \eqref{eq:v16-overlap-functional},
\eqref{eq:v16-adaptive-critical-physical} and the exact binary testing
identity. No averaging under the physical history law is substituted
for averaging under $\mathsf R^{\pi_n}$.
\end{proof}

In particular the limit is not determined by the mean of $B$. If
$\mathbb EB<\infty$, strict convexity gives
$\mathbb E e^{-B}\ge e^{-\mathbb EB}$, with equality only when $B$
is almost surely constant. This observation does not assert optimality
of an adaptive policy or make failed preparations free.
