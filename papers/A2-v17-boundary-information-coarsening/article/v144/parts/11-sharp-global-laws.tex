\section{Sharp global depth and geometric boundary laws}
\label{sec:sharp-global-laws}

The preceding calculations have a global consequence stronger than the
uniform exponent five for arbitrary quadratic quotients.  The relevant
distinction is between the full coefficient space and the open of
basepoint-free webs with smooth Jacobian quartic.  We first make the
Pieri projection quantitative, then use its polynomial nature to pass
between all projection ranks.  We subsequently identify two global
minimal supports and construct an explicit cross-corank incidence
family.  None of these arguments replaces a primary decomposition by a
list of Hilbert functions.

\subsection{A normalized determinant projection}

\begin{lemma}[Fischer coefficient for the determinant-four line]
\label{lem:fischer-determinant-projection}
Let \(X=(x_{ij})\) be a generic \(4\times4\) matrix, put
\(D=(\det X)^4\), and let
\(p=[123\mid123]^4x_{44}^4\).  The Cauchy-isotypic projection of
\(p\) onto the line \(\C D\) is
\[
 \operatorname{pr}_{(4,4,4,4)}(p)=\frac1{35}D.
\]
In particular the coordinate-ring product of the \((4,4,4,0)\)
and \((4)\) Cauchy components has nonzero determinant-four projection.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the polynomial ring use the Fischer Hermitian form
\(\langle x^\alpha,x^\beta\rangle=\alpha!\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}\).
It is invariant under the unitary groups acting on the two matrix
factors.  Inequivalent Cauchy summands are therefore orthogonal.
The monomials of \(D\) which use only the northwest \(3\times3\)
block and \(x_{44}\) are exactly the monomials of \(p\), with the
same coefficients.  Indeed each of the four determinant factors must
then use its \(x_{44}\) entry.  Hence
\[
 \langle p,D\rangle=\langle p,p\rangle>0.
\]
Successive application of the classical Cayley identity gives
\[
 \|\det(X_n)^s\|^2
 =\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(s+j)!}{j!};
\]
see \cite{CSSCayley}.  The northwest variables and \(x_{44}\) are
disjoint, so
\[
 \|p\|^2=24\prod_{j=0}^{2}\frac{(4+j)!}{j!}=24883200,
 \qquad
 \|D\|^2=\prod_{j=0}^{3}\frac{(4+j)!}{j!}=870912000.
\]
Their ratio is \(1/35\).  This calculates an isotypic projection;
it does not assert a coefficient-one identity in an unspecified
straightening normalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp global exponent on the Jacobian open]
\label{thm:sharp-global-jacobian-depth}
For every \(R\in G_4^\circ\), the residual ideal on every Schur
chart satisfies
\[
 d^4\in J.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal N}_R^{\,5}=0,
 \qquad W_j=\varnothing\quad(j\ge5).
\]
At every projection-corank-three or projection-corank-four point the
local nilpotency index is exactly five.  Thus the global exponent five
for the nilradical is sharp on \(G_4^\circ\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:corank-four-jacobian}, with
Lemma~\ref{lem:fischer-determinant-projection} supplying its explicit
nonvanishing, gives a polynomial identity
\[
 (\det X)^4\in I_6(\gamma_R\Sym^2X)
 \quad\text{in }\C[x_{ij}:1\le i,j\le4].
\]
It is an identity in the coordinate ring of the entire matrix space,
not an identity valid only in the local ring of its origin.  On a Schur
chart substitute \(X=I_H\oplus T\).  The maximal minors specialize
to those of
\([A_H,B_H(1_H\otimes T),C_H\Sym^2T]\), and \(\det X\) specializes
to \(d=\det T\).  Ideal membership is preserved by this ring map;
no flatness hypothesis is involved.  Hence \(d^4\in J\) on every
chart.  Since the failure ideal is \(dJ\), the asserted nilpotence
and vanishing of the fifth and subsequent graded layers follow.

The corank-three Schur-symbol exclusion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-d3-corank3} gives \(d^3\notin J\).
The corank-four exclusion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:corank-four-jacobian} gives the same nonmembership.
Together with \(d^4\in J\) these prove exact local index five.
The corank-four stratum is nonempty: \(S_R\) has dimension six and
contains four-planes, whose projections to \(V\) are zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Admissibility of the ambient rank tables]
\label{prop:smooth-web-rank-admissibility}
At projection corank two for \(R\in G_4^\circ\), the ranks
\(a=\rank A_H\) and \(b=\rank\overline B_H\) necessarily satisfy
\[
 a\ge2,\qquad a+b\ge5.
\]
Thus the possible pairs on this open are contained in
\[
 \{(3,3),(3,2),(2,4),(2,3)\}.
\]
The other rows of the coefficient-space tables remain valid statements
about surjective quadratic quotients; they are not strata of the
smooth, basepoint-free Jacobian open.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The dimension of \(R\cap\Sym^2H\) is \(3-a\).  If \(a\le1\),
the web contains two independent quadrics in the two \(H\)-variables.
Along the line \(\Pj(H^\perp)\), their gradients vanish.  The
Jacobian matrix therefore has rank at most two.  Its determinant and
all its first derivatives vanish there, contradicting smoothness of
\(Y_R\).

The dimension of
\(R\cap(\Sym^2H+H\otimes(V/H))\) is \((3-a)+(4-b)\).
Consequently the restriction of the web to the line
\(\Pj((V/H)^*)\) has dimension \(a+b-3\).  A vector space of
binary quadrics of dimension at most one has a common zero over
\(\C\), contrary to basepoint freeness.  Hence \(a+b-3\ge2\).
The upper bounds on \(b\) are respectively three and four when
\(a=3\) and \(a=2\), giving the displayed list.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two global minimal supports and a resolution}

Regard \(Y_R\) as a subvariety of \(\Gr(3,V)=\Pj(V^*)\).  Let
\(\mathcal H\) be the tautological rank-three bundle on it.  Define
\[
 \widetilde Z_R=\operatorname{Tot}\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathcal H)
 =\{(H,T):H\in Y_R,\ \operatorname{im}T\subset H\},
 \qquad \pi:\widetilde Z_R\longrightarrow\End(V).
\]
Let \(Z_R\) be its reduced image and let
\(D_2\subset\End(V)\) be the determinantal variety of matrices of
rank at most two.  These constructions refer to the fixed quotient
\(\gamma_R\), not to a choice of presentation of its colons.

\begin{theorem}[Global minimal supports of the first two layers]
\label{thm:global-minimal-supports}
Let \(R\in G_4^\circ\), and on the full matrix space put
\(J=I_6(\gamma_R\Sym^2T)\), \(d=\det T\).  Then
\[
 \sqrt{(d,J)}=I(Z_R),
 \qquad
 \sqrt{(d,J:d)}=I(D_2)=I_3(T).
\]
The variety \(Z_R\) is irreducible of dimension fourteen, and
\(\pi\) is a proper birational morphism from a smooth variety.
The first layer has generic length one along its unique minimal
support \(Z_R\).  For \(R\in G_4^\dagger\), the second layer has
generic length one along its unique minimal support \(D_2\).
These assertions determine minimal supports and their generic lengths;
they do not exclude additional embedded primes of either layer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a matrix of rank three with image \(H\), the image of
\(\Sym^2T\) is \(\Sym^2H\).  The restriction of \(\gamma_R\)
to this six-dimensional space has nontrivial kernel exactly when
\(R\cap\Sym^2H\ne0\).  If \(\alpha\) defines \(H\), contraction
\(\Sym^2V\to V\) by \(\alpha\) has kernel \(\Sym^2H\).
The determinant of its restriction to \(R\) is, up to the fixed
nonzero normalization, the Jacobian quartic evaluated at \(\alpha\).
Thus the rank-three part of \(V(J)\) is exactly the image of the
rank-three part of \(\widetilde Z_R\).

Every matrix of rank at most two belongs to \(V(J)\), since the
symmetric square of its image has dimension at most three.  It also
belongs to \(Z_R\): the projective space of hyperplanes containing
its image has positive dimension and meets the quartic \(Y_R\).
Once such a hyperplane is chosen, the matrix is a limit of rank-three
maps into that hyperplane.  Invertible matrices do not belong to
\(V(J)\), since \(\gamma_R\) is surjective.  This proves the first
radical equality.

The total space \(\widetilde Z_R\) is a rank-twelve vector bundle
over the smooth irreducible surface \(Y_R\).  It is smooth and
irreducible of dimension fourteen.  Its inclusion in
\(Y_R\times\End(V)\) is closed, so its projection to matrix space
is proper.  A rank-three matrix determines its image hyperplane
uniquely; on this dense open the inverse is the regular image map to
the Grassmannian.  Hence \(\pi\) is birational.

We record the local scheme calculation at rank three, since it also
controls the second layer.  Write a Schur chart as \(I_H\oplus t\).
Smoothness of the Jacobian implies that \(A_H\) has rank five at a
point of \(Y_R\); rank at most four would make every cofactor, and
therefore every first derivative of its determinant, vanish.  Eliminate
an invertible five-minor.  The residual ideal is generated by a local
equation \(h\) of \(Y_R\), the mixed entries \(t b_i\), and a
quadratic entry divisible by \(t^2\).  At least one \(b_i\) is a
unit.  Otherwise a nonzero functional on the target cokernel,
viewed as a quadratic form on \(V\) annihilating \(R\), would
vanish on \(\Sym^2H+H\otimes(V/H)\), and would be proportional to
\(\alpha^2\).  Then every quadric of \(R\) would vanish at
\([\alpha]\), contradicting basepoint freeness.  It follows that
\[
 J_{\mathrm{loc}}=(t,h).
\]
Thus the first layer is generically reduced on \(Z_R\), whereas
\((J:t)\) is the unit ideal at every rank-three point of \(Z_R\).
It is also the unit ideal off \(Z_R\) in rank three.

At a point of rank at most two, use the homogeneous transverse Schur
coordinates.  At corank two every residual minor has degree at least
three, whereas \(d\) has degree two.  At corank three the corresponding
lower bounds are seven and three; at corank four they are twelve and
four.  Thus \(d\notin J\) even after localization at the transverse
origin.  If \(J:d\) contained a unit there, multiplication by its
nonzero constant term would contradict this degree bound.  Consequently
the support of the second layer is exactly \(D_2\), proving the
second radical equality.  On the dense simple-contact, mixed-regular
corank-two open supplied by \(G_4^\dagger\), the inherited colon
calculation gives the reduced transverse vertex for \(W_2\).
Its generic length on the irreducible variety \(D_2\) is therefore
one.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fibres of the boundary resolution]
\label{cor:resolution-fibres}
For a matrix \(T\) of rank \(r\le2\), the scheme-theoretic fibre is
\[
 \pi^{-1}(T)=Y_R\cap\Pj((V/\operatorname{im}T)^*).
\]
For general matrices of rank two it consists of four reduced points;
for general matrices of rank one it is a smooth plane quartic of
genus three; over the zero matrix it is the quartic K3 surface \(Y_R\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The incidence equation is \(\alpha T=0\), which gives the displayed
linear section scheme-theoretically.  A general line meets the smooth
quartic transversely in four points, and a general hyperplane section
is a smooth plane quartic.  Adjunction gives genus three in the latter
case.  At \(T=0\) the incidence equation imposes no condition.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A cross-corank family with explicit components}

\begin{theorem}[Contact points, plane quartics, and the K3 fibre]
\label{thm:cross-corank-incidence}
Let \(R\in G_4^\dagger\).  Choose a general mixed-regular plane
\(H=\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\), with a general basis, and extend it
to a basis of \(V\).  On \(B=\mathbb A^2_{u,v}\) set
\[
 M(u,v)=\operatorname{diag}(u,v,0,0).
\]
There is a family of four-planes in \(V\oplus S_R\) with this
projection matrix.  Its projection coranks are two on \(uv\ne0\),
three on either punctured axis, and four at the origin.

Write \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_4\) for the dual homogeneous
coordinates.  The pullback of the resolution \(\pi\) is
\[
 \widetilde Z_B=V(u\alpha_1,v\alpha_2)\subset B\times Y_R.
\]
It has seven reduced irreducible components, no embedded components,
and the following primary decomposition of ideal sheaves:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross-corank-primary}
 (u\alpha_1,v\alpha_2)
 =(u,v)\cap(u,\alpha_2)\cap(\alpha_1,v)
       \cap(\alpha_1,\alpha_2).
\end{equation}
The last factor splits as the intersection of the ideals of the four
constant contact-point sections.  The other components are
\(\{0\}\times Y_R\), the \(u=0\) axis times
\(C_2=Y_R\cap V(\alpha_2)\), and the \(v=0\) axis times
\(C_1=Y_R\cap V(\alpha_1)\).  The fibre Hilbert polynomials with
respect to \(\mathcal O_{Y_R}(1)\) are respectively
\[
 4\quad(uv\ne0),\qquad
 4n-2\quad(\text{a punctured axis}),\qquad
 2n^2+2\quad((u,v)=(0,0)).
\]
The family is flat on each of these strata, but not across them.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a four-dimensional subspace \(E\subset S_R\) and an
isomorphism \(\iota:V\to E\).  The graphs
\[
 \mathcal K_{u,v}=\{(M(u,v)w,\iota(w)):w\in V\}
\]
are four-planes for every \((u,v)\), since their second component
is injective.  They define a morphism to the Grassmannian with the
stated ranks.  The resolution pulls back by the equations
\(\alpha M(u,v)=0\), namely \(u\alpha_1=v\alpha_2=0\).

Generality of \(H\) makes the four points in
\(Y_R\cap V(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\) distinct, and the general basis
makes \(C_1,C_2\) smooth plane quartics.  They meet transversely at
these four points.  Locally at each of them, \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\)
are regular parameters on the smooth surface.  Equation
\eqref{eq:cross-corank-primary} is then the squarefree monomial
identity for \((ux,vy)\).  Away from the four points at least one
of \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\) is a unit, and the same equality reduces
to the elementary two-factor intersection.  Thus the equality holds
as ideal sheaves.  The three surface components and four constant
sections are distinct reduced irreducible components.  The local
squarefree description shows that there is no embedded component.

On the torus both linear equations remain; on a punctured axis only
one remains; at the origin neither remains.  This identifies the
fibres.  Their Hilbert polynomials are those of four points, a plane
quartic of genus three, and a degree-four K3 surface.  The restrictions
to the displayed strata are products, proving their flatness.  The
change of Hilbert polynomial proves failure of flatness across them.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Intrinsic graded fibre algebra across coranks]
\label{prop:cross-corank-graded-fibres}
Pull back \(\operatorname{gr}_{\widehat{\mathcal N}_R}
\mathcal O_{\widehat D_R}\) along the preceding family of reduced
points.  After a local trivialization of the conormal line, its algebra
on a geometric fibre is
\[
 \kappa[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^3)\quad(uv\ne0),
 \qquad
 \kappa[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^5)\quad(uv=0),
 \qquad \deg\epsilon=1.
\]
Thus degrees three and four appear in an actual family passing from
projection corank two to three to four.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every positive graded piece is a cyclic quotient of the reduced local
ring, with the conormal-line twist, and its products are the canonical
quotient products of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-all-corank-depth}.
Its residue-field fibre is therefore either zero or one-dimensional.
The mixed-regular corank-two calculation gives local nilpotency index
three on the torus.  Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-global-jacobian-depth}
gives index five on the punctured axes and at the origin.  The
nonvanishing pieces are consecutive, and quotient multiplication takes
the chosen generator to its powers, giving the stated algebras.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two different base-change operations]
The preceding proposition pulls back an already formed intrinsic graded
algebra.  It does not form the nilradical after pulling back the failure
scheme to \(B\).  In fact \(\det M(u,v)=0\) identically, so the
latter pullback loses its transverse nilpotent information.  Likewise,
the primary decomposition in \eqref{eq:cross-corank-primary} is the
primary decomposition of the incidence-resolution family, not a claim
that all transverse primary components of \(W_3\) and \(W_4\) have
been classified.  This distinction is essential in a cross-stratum
specialization, where kernel formation need not commute with pullback.
\end{remark}

\subsection{An explicit infinite family of proper quotient maps}

The next result gives an exact primary law and a sharp exponent in all
symmetric degrees for quotients induced by a proper quotient of the
underlying vector space.  Its maximal-minor identity is classical:
we use, and explicitly credit, Bruns--Vasconcelos
\cite[Theorem 1(2)]{BrunsVasconcelos}.  The point here is to compute the
resulting failure ideal, residual tower, and canonical embedded length
in the notation of the present paper.

\begin{theorem}[Primary law for quotient-induced symmetric powers]
\label{thm:quotient-induced-primary-law}
Let \(1\le k<e\), \(r\ge1\), and let \(\rho:V\twoheadrightarrow W\)
be a surjection with \(\dim V=e\), \(\dim W=k\).  Set
\[
 \gamma=\Sym^r\rho,\quad
 A=\C[\End(V)],\quad d=\det T,\quad P=I_k(\rho T),
 \quad a=\binom{k+r-1}{r-1},\quad h=e-k+1.
\]
If \(s=\dim\Sym^rW\) and
\(J=I_s(\gamma\Sym^rT)\), then
\[
 J=P^a=P^{(a)},\qquad
 (J:d^q)=P^{\max(a-q,0)},\qquad
 \min\{q:d^q\in J\}=a.
\]
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-induced-decomposition}
 dJ=(d)\cap P^{a+1}
\end{equation}
is an irredundant primary decomposition.  The nilradical of
\(A/dJ\) has exact index \(a+1\).  Its reduced determinant component
has generic length one.  Its only embedded associated prime is \(P\),
and the canonical local torsion length there is
\[
 \mu_P(A/dJ)=\binom{a+h-1}{h}.
\]
For \(1\le j\le a\), the \(j\)-th positive graded layer is
\[
 A/(d,P^{a-j+1})(-je),
\]
and its length at \(P\) is
\(\binom{a-j+h-1}{h-1}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose bases so that \(\rho T\) consists of the first \(k\) rows
of a generic \(e\times e\) matrix.  The maximal-minor identity of
Bruns--Vasconcelos gives \(J=P^a\) in characteristic zero.  We recall
why the powers of this maximal-minor ideal are symbolic, so that the
primary assertions and colon calculation are explicit.

In the coordinate ring of a generic \(k\times e\) matrix, the Cauchy
summands are indexed by partitions \(\lambda\) of length at most
\(k\).  A highest vector is the product of principal minors
\[
 v_\lambda=\prod_{j=1}^k\Delta_j^{\lambda_j-\lambda_{j+1}},
 \qquad \lambda_{k+1}=0.
\]
At the generic point of \(P\), the minors \(\Delta_j\), \(j<k\),
are units, whereas \(\Delta_k\) has order one.  This follows also
by eliminating an invertible \((k-1)\)-minor: the remaining maximal
minors are the parameters of a regular local ring of dimension
\(h=e-k+1\).  Thus \(v_\lambda\) has order \(\lambda_k\).
The symbolic power is stable under the two general linear groups, so
complete reducibility shows that it consists exactly of the summands
with \(\lambda_k\ge a\).  Their highest vectors are divisible by
\(\Delta_k^a\), and hence belong to \(P^a\).  The reverse
containment is automatic.  This proves \(P^{(a)}=P^a\), and the
same proof applies to every positive power.  Adjoining the remaining
rows is a polynomial extension and preserves these identities and
primaryness.

In \(A_P\), the full determinant \(d\) is a parameter: after the
same elimination its linear term in the remaining maximal-minor
parameters is nonzero, since the remaining generic rows can complete
a rank-\((k-1)\) matrix to rank \(e-1\).  Hence
\[
 (P^aA_P:d^q)=P^{\max(a-q,0)}A_P.
\]
This also follows from the polynomial associated graded ring of the
regular local ring.  Contracting to \(A\) proves the asserted colons
and exact exponent.  In particular
\((d)\cap P^{a+1}=d(P^{a+1}:d)=dP^a\).
The determinant ideal is prime, \(P^{a+1}\) is \(P\)-primary,
and their distinct radicals satisfy \((d)\subsetneq P\) because
\(h\ge2\).  Neither component can be removed, so the decomposition
is irredundant and determines its associated primes.

Locally at \(P\), write \(\mathfrak m=PA_P\).  Since \(d\) is a
parameter and \(h-1\ge1\), the quotient \(A_P/(d)\) has no
finite-length torsion.  Therefore the local torsion of
\(A_P/(d\mathfrak m^a)\) is
\[
 (d)/(d\mathfrak m^a)\simeq A_P/\mathfrak m^a,
\]
of length \(\binom{a+h-1}{h}\).  Localizing at \((d)\) makes
\(P\) the unit ideal, giving generic determinant length one.
The residual-colon formula gives the displayed graded layers.
Modulo the parameter \(d\), their lengths are the numbers of
monomials of degree less than \(a-j+1\) in \(h-1\) variables,
namely \(\binom{a-j+h-1}{h-1}\).  Their sum is the preceding
embedded length.  This identifies the canonical embedded torsion,
not the noncanonical size of an arbitrarily chosen embedded primary
component.
\end{proof}
