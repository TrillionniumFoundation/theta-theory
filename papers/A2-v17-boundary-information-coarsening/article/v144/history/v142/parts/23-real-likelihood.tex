\section{A totally real reciprocal likelihood fibre}
\label{sec:totally-real-likelihood}

The spectral data also lead to a question independent of reconstruction:
can every critical point of reciprocal likelihood on a definite real
pencil be made real by a choice of real data?  The assertion was
formulated as Conjecture~4.5 in
\cite{FevolaMandelshtamSturmfels}.  We give an explicit construction
valid for every number of distinct eigenvalues and every
multiplicity pattern.  Its proof counts the critical points directly;
it does not assume the complex likelihood-degree formula.

A real symmetric pencil containing a positive definite matrix can be
written, after a real congruence and a choice of generators, as
\(\langle I,\diag(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\rangle\).  Indeed, make the
definite matrix the identity and orthogonally diagonalize the other
symmetric matrix.  Group its distinct eigenvalues as
\(\alpha_1<\cdots<\alpha_r\), with multiplicities
\(m_1,\ldots,m_r>0\) and \(n=\sum_i m_i\).  A two-dimensional
pencil has \(r\ge2\).  If \(\sigma_i\) is the sum of the data
entries in the \(i\)-th eigenspace, the reciprocal score equations
are the critical equations of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grouped-reciprocal-likelihood}
 \widetilde\ell(x,y)
 =-\sum_{i=1}^r m_i\log(\alpha_i x+y)
       -\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{\sigma_i}{\alpha_i x+y}
\end{equation}
on the complement of \(\prod_i(\alpha_i x+y)=0\).
The derivatives are rational and independent of the local branches
of the logarithms.  On the real complement one can equivalently
use \(\log|\alpha_i x+y|\).

\begin{theorem}[Explicit totally real likelihood data]
\label{thm:totally-real-reciprocal-likelihood}
Choose any real numbers
\[
 \alpha_r<\beta_1<\cdots<\beta_{r-1},
 \qquad
 D(t)=\prod_{i=1}^r(t-\alpha_i),\quad
 Q(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}(t-\beta_j),
\]
and prescribe
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-real-likelihood-data}
                       \sigma_i=\frac{Q(\alpha_i)}{D'(\alpha_i)}.
\end{equation}
Then \eqref{eq:grouped-reciprocal-likelihood} has exactly
\(2r-3\) critical points on its complex domain.  Every one is
real and nondegenerate.  There is one critical point with
\(t=-y/x\) in each interval
\((\alpha_i,\alpha_{i+1})\), and one in each interval
\((\beta_j,\beta_{j+1})\); there are no others.
The same conclusion holds on a nonempty Euclidean open subset of
the real data space.  Thus the assertion of Conjecture~4.5 in
\cite{FevolaMandelshtamSturmfels} holds for all definite real pencils.
\end{theorem}

The data here are unrestricted real data, exactly as in that
assertion.  Neither positivity of the data matrix nor the assertion
that all the critical points lie in the positive definite cone is
part of the theorem.  For example, the residues in
\eqref{eq:explicit-real-likelihood-data} alternate in sign.

\begin{lemma}[Two separated strings of roots]
\label{lem:separated-root-score}
With the notation of the theorem, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-score-polynomial}
 H(t)=\sum_{i=1}^r(n-m_i)\frac{D(t)}{t-\alpha_i},
 \qquad F(t)=nD(t)Q'(t)-H(t)Q(t).
\end{equation}
The polynomial \(F\) has degree exactly \(2r-3\).  It has a
simple root in each of the intervals in the theorem, has no other
root over \(\C\), and is relatively prime to \(DQ\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since \(\sum_i(n-m_i)=n(r-1)\), the terms of degree
\(2r-2\) in \(F\) cancel.  At a zero of \(D\),
\[
 F(\alpha_i)=-(n-m_i)D'(\alpha_i)Q(\alpha_i).
\]
The factors \(n-m_i\) are positive, all the values
\(Q(\alpha_i)\) have the same sign, and consecutive values
\(D'(\alpha_i)\) have opposite signs.  Thus \(F\) changes
sign in each of the \(r-1\) intervals between the \(\alpha_i\).
At a zero of \(Q\),
\[
                        F(\beta_j)=nD(\beta_j)Q'(\beta_j).
\]
Here \(D(\beta_j)>0\), and consecutive values of \(Q'\) have
opposite signs.  There are therefore a further \(r-2\) roots in
the intervals between the \(\beta_j\).  These intervals are
pairwise disjoint and are disjoint from all the former intervals.
The upper bound \(2r-3\) on the degree forces exactly one root
in each interval, all simple, and excludes any remaining real or
complex root.  The displayed endpoint values are nonzero, proving
coprimality.  This argument includes \(r=2\), when the second
list of intervals is empty.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:totally-real-reciprocal-likelihood}]
Partial fractions give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-score-partial-fractions}
 \frac{Q(t)}{D(t)}=\sum_i\frac{\sigma_i}{t-\alpha_i},
 \qquad S(t):=\sum_i\frac{\sigma_i}{\alpha_i-t}
                         =-\frac{Q(t)}{D(t)}.
\end{equation}
On the chart \(x\ne0\), put \(y=-xt\).  Locally, up to a
constant, the function becomes
\[
 -n\log x-\sum_i m_i\log(\alpha_i-t)-\frac{S(t)}x.
\]
The first critical equation gives \(x=S(t)/n\).  If \(S(t)=0\)
it cannot be satisfied on this chart.  When \(S(t)\ne0\), the
remaining critical equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-score-elimination}
 \left.\frac{\partial\widetilde\ell}{\partial t}\right|_{x=S(t)/n}
 =-\sum_i\frac{m_i}{t-\alpha_i}-n\frac{S'(t)}{S(t)}
 =-\frac{F(t)}{D(t)Q(t)}=0.
\end{equation}
The lemma now gives exactly \(2r-3\) solutions in this chart,
with the real coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-critical-coordinate-formula}
                  x=-\frac{Q(t)}{nD(t)},\qquad y=-xt.
\end{equation}
At each of these points \(x\ne0\) and
\(\alpha_i x+y\ne0\), so none is an extraneous solution
introduced by clearing denominators.

We must also exclude the omitted chart \(x=0\).  Comparing the
coefficients of \(t^{-1}\) and \(t^{-2}\) in
\eqref{eq:real-score-partial-fractions} gives
\[
 \sum_i\sigma_i=1,
 \qquad
 \sum_i\alpha_i\sigma_i=\sum_i\alpha_i-\sum_j\beta_j.
\]
At \(x=0\), the \(y\)-equation would imply \(y=1/n\).
The \(x\)-equation would then require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:excluded-score-chart}
                 \sum_i(n-m_i)\alpha_i=n\sum_j\beta_j.
\end{equation}
But the left side is strictly smaller than
\(n(r-1)\alpha_r\), whereas the right side is strictly larger.
Thus there is no critical point on \(x=0\).  This proves the
assertion about the entire complex domain, not just the chosen
coordinate chart.

For nondegeneracy, at a critical point in \((x,t)\)-coordinates
one has \(\widetilde\ell_{xx}=-n/x^2\ne0\).  The derivative of
the eliminated equation \eqref{eq:real-score-elimination} is
\(-F'(t)/(D(t)Q(t))\ne0\).  It is the Schur complement of
\(\widetilde\ell_{xx}\) in the Hessian.  Hence the Hessian is
nonsingular.  The coordinate change \((x,t)\mapsto(x,-xt)\)
has nonzero Jacobian at these points, so nondegeneracy also holds
in the original coordinates.

Finally allow \(Q\) to have any positive leading coefficient and
\(r-1\) distinct real roots to the right of \(\alpha_r\).
These polynomials form a nonempty Euclidean open set in their
\(r\)-dimensional coefficient space.  The linear map
\(Q\mapsto(Q(\alpha_i)/D'(\alpha_i))_{i=1}^r\) is an
isomorphism, by polynomial interpolation.  Scaling \(Q\) does
not change its critical \(t\)-values and scales \((x,y)\) by
the same nonzero factor.  Thus its image is the required open set
of grouped data.  The map from the original \(n\) data entries
to their \(r\) group sums is surjective, so its inverse image is
open and nonempty as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:real-likelihood-three-eigenvalues}
For \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)=(1,2,3)\),
\(m_1=m_2=m_3=1\), and \((\beta_1,\beta_2)=(4,5)\),
the data are \((6,-6,1)\) and
\[
                    F(t)=15t^3-130t^2+345t-278.
\]
Its three roots lie respectively in \((1,2)\), \((2,3)\),
and \((4,5)\).  The theorem proves the same assertion for
arbitrary \(r\), not just for examples reached by root finding.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the intrinsic invariant]
\label{rem:real-likelihood-reconstruction-scope}
The finite failure neighbourhood recovers the complex projective
pencil and its spectral sheaf.  To apply the real theorem one
specifies a real structure and a definite member; these are not
asserted to be recoverable from an unmarked complex scheme.
After that specification, the construction uses only the ordered
spectral points, their multiplicities, and the freely chosen
\(\beta_j\).  The result is an independently stated consequence
in reciprocal likelihood geometry, rather than a new name for
Weierstrass--Segre classification.  It proves the cited conjectural
assertion without claiming that every real critical point is a
statistically admissible maximum.
\end{remark}
