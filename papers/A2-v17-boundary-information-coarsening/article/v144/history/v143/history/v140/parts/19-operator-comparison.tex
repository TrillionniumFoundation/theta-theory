\subsection{The restricted contraction versus classical operators}
\label{sec:operator-signatures}

The relation with classical invariant theory is more precise than the
shared occurrence of a representation.  Howe's
\cite[Theorem~9 and \S4(a), pp.~555--557]{HoweClassical} gives harmonic
irreducibility and the orthogonal decomposition through the commuting
quadratic multiplication and Laplacian operators.  Thus the passage
to harmonic summands in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-contraction} is classical, including its
full-orthogonal-group interpretation.  The additional step is the
restriction of the particular exterior contraction to each summand,
not a new harmonic decomposition.

The different operators can be compared without terminology.  With a
volume fixed, the Jacobian map in two variables is
\[
 C_2(a\wedge b)=a_{x_1}b_{x_2}-a_{x_2}b_{x_1}.
\]
It is the first binary transvectant, with the normalization for
quadratic inputs in \cite[\S2.2, Definition~2.6 and Remark~2.7]{OliveGordan}.
Thus neither that covariant nor its polarization is new.  Moreover,
when \(n=2\), \(j_2\) is an isomorphism onto
\(\bigwedge^2\Sym^2V\), so for every nonzero \(q\)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary-kernel-classical}
 \ker\kappa_{2,q}
  =j_2^{-1}\bigl(\bigwedge^2\ker[q:\Sym^2V\to\C]\bigr).
\end{equation}
This one-dimensional kernel is elementary exterior algebra.

For general \(n\), a quadratic apolar differential operator has the
signature
\(q(\partial):\Sym^nV\to\Sym^{n-2}V\).  In orthonormal
coordinates its kernel is \(\mathcal H_n\), of dimension
\(\binom{2n-1}{n}-\binom{2n-3}{n-2}\).  In contrast the
operator studied here has signature
\[
 \kappa_{n,q}:\det V\otimes\Sym^nV
       \longrightarrow\bigwedge^{n-1}\Sym^2V,
       \qquad \kappa_{n,q}=\iota_qj_n.
\]
For nondegenerate \(q\) its kernel is zero in odd dimension and
the quadratic-power line in even dimension.  These are not the
apolar harmonic kernels.  For singular \(q\), the proof uses its
unipotent radical shears as well as its nondegenerate orthogonal
factor; a full-rank harmonic branching statement alone cannot supply
that argument.  The normalization, nonzero harmonic coefficients,
and singular-radical calculation are supplied in
Section~\ref{sec:uniform-support}.

Finally, the universal coefficient map
\eqref{eq:general-coefficient-map} is classical matrix-coefficient
representation theory.  What is proved geometrically is that its
specified left subspaces can be recovered from an abstract failure
scheme, with one common projective transformation.  In the natural
pencil family there is only one such subspace, and exterior duality
recovers the full pencil.  The classical Weierstrass--Segre
classification and the pencil--hyperelliptic correspondence classify
objects after a pencil has been supplied; they do not enter the proof
that the abstract scheme supplies that pencil.  These comparisons
identify the exact additional arguments relative to the inspected
statements, without claiming exhaustive historical nonanticipation.
