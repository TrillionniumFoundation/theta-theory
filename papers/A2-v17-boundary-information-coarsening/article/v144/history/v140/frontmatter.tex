\title[Intrinsic reconstruction from failure schemes]{Intrinsic reconstruction from nonreduced failure schemes}
\author{Qian Qi}
\date{September 23, 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{14M12, 14J28, 13C40, 14B05, 14D20, 05E05}
\keywords{Intrinsic reconstruction, pencils and webs of quadrics, Fitting schemes, Schur functors, exterior support, hyperelliptic curves, nilpotent filtration}
\hypersetup{pdftitle={Intrinsic reconstruction from nonreduced failure schemes, revision 140},pdfauthor={Qian Qi}}
\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct quadratic relation spaces from abstract nonreduced
multiplication-failure schemes of cube-zero algebras.  For every
basepoint-free four-dimensional web with smooth Jacobian quartic,
the failure scheme determines the web up to projective equivalence,
without a further genericity hypothesis.  The same conclusion holds
for every pencil of quadrics in every dimension at least three,
including singular pencils.  In both families the reduced failure
scheme is a fixed Schubert divisor.  The proof recovers the deepest
normal cone, its oriented rank-one ruling, and a residual coefficient
module.  We prove that this coefficient extraction is automatic for
a full Grassmannian family of quadratic relation spaces.  For webs,
an all-ranks contraction-kernel formula gives the exterior-support
gap needed to recombine the coefficients.  For pencils, a single
irreducible module recovers the entire Pluecker line.  The pencil
invariant distinguishes classical Jordan data lost by the full
discriminant and yields fixed-reduction families parametrized by
hyperelliptic curve classes.  The pencil is already determined by the
\((n^2+2n-4)\)-th neighbourhood of the socle Grassmannian,
whereas every lower-order neighbourhood is independent of it.
In fixed tensor coordinates the first relation spaces give a
closed immersion of the full pencil parameter scheme, compatible
with nonreduced base change.  A flat specialization with fixed discriminant and fixed reduced rank loci
changes the full spectral torsion sheaf and, in dimension four, the
reciprocal curve from a conic to a line.  Polar-restriction schemes provide a
further coefficient construction in arbitrary degree.
\end{abstract}
