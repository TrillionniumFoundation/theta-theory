\section{Uniform physical estimability and global analytic reconstruction}
\label{sec:v24-uniform-physical-global}

We now close the physical-to-global step.  The point is not to assume a
uniform estimator for each finite jet vector, but to construct one from the
actual finite-bridge experiment.  Since only consistency is at issue, the
argument uses compactness twice: first to replace the continuum of local
positive designs by a finite common acquisition atlas at each fixed order,
and then to replace the continuum of separated parameter pairs by finitely
many bounded statistical separators.  The charged onset pilot and the stopped
physical comparison of Section~\ref{sec:v16-adaptive} make that atlas
implementable without knowing the table.

Throughout this section \(\mathfrak K\) is a uniformly nondegenerate compact
analytic class in the sense of
Section~\ref{sec:v24-quantitative-gluing}.  Its measured graph, oriented deck
labels and transverse sign conventions are fixed.  The two rank-two anchoring
cycles and the rooted signature-rigid spanning tree are also fixed.  In
particular, the channel labels are acquisition labels in the marked cover;
their Euclidean deck vectors are not supplied and are recovered by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-lattice-gram-recovery}.

For a fixed finite order \(M\), let
\[
 \mathcal D_M(T)=
 \left(g_e,S_{e,0}^{(m)}(0),S_{e,1}^{(m)}(0):
       e\in E,\ 2\le m\le M\right)
\]
with the normalized finite-coordinate norm of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-global-modulus}.  All constants below may depend on
\(M\) and \(\mathfrak K\); no uniformity as \(M\to\infty\) is assumed.

\subsection{A common finite design at fixed order}

For a table \(T\), an edge \(e\), endpoint type \(b\) and positive offset
\(d\) in the common collar, let \(\mathcal K_{T,e,b,d}^{(M)}\) denote the
endpoint information quadratic form of
\eqref{eq:v22-information-matrix}, transported to the fixed labelled
finite-jet tangent coordinates at \(T\).  Give this finite-dimensional tangent
bundle any continuous norm; compactness makes all such choices uniformly
equivalent.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform finite positive design]
\label{lem:v24-uniform-positive-design}
For every fixed \(M\) there are finitely many labelled designs
\[
 \mathscr A_M=\{(e_r,b_r,d_r):1\le r\le L_M\},
 \qquad d_r>0,
\]
positive weights \(\alpha_r\), and a constant \(c_M>0\), all independent of
\(T\in\mathfrak K\), such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-uniform-information}
 \sum_{r=1}^{L_M}\alpha_r
   \mathcal K_{T,e_r,b_r,d_r}^{(M)}[z,z]
 \ge c_M\|z\|^2
\end{equation}
for every unit finite-jet tangent vector \(z\) at every
\(T\in\mathfrak K\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At a fixed pair \((T,z)\), the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-finite-positive-design} shows that some labelled channel,
endpoint type and positive offset detects \(z\); otherwise all corresponding
action variations would vanish and the finite tangent-jet isomorphism would
force \(z=0\).  Detection is an open condition in \((T,z,d)\), because the
action, amplitude and information form vary continuously on the common
collar.  The unit tangent bundle over the compact finite-dimensional model on
\(\mathfrak K\) is compact.  A finite subcover therefore supplies finitely
many designs that detect every unit vector.  The sum of their information
forms is continuous and positive on that compact unit bundle, hence has a
positive minimum.  Renormalizing positive weights gives
\eqref{eq:v24-uniform-information}.
\end{proof}

This lemma supplies a common local atlas.  Uniform global consistency can be
proved without estimating an information matrix, by a finite-separation
argument which is also useful because it is insensitive to the choice of
finite-dimensional coordinates.

For a centered boundary acquisition at labelled design
\(a=(e,b,d)\), let \(Q_{T,a}^{\infty}\) denote the limiting successful
endpoint--time law in its channel-local signed coordinates.  Equality of the
collection \(\{Q_{T,a}^{\infty}:a\}\) for all sufficiently small positive
offsets determines the signed support germs and hence the complete intrinsic
action data.

\begin{lemma}[Finite statistical separators]
\label{lem:v24-finite-separators}
Fix \(M\) and \(\eta>0\).  There are a finite list of centered labelled
designs \(a_1,\ldots,a_R\), bounded measurable functions
\(\phi_r\), \(|\phi_r|\le1\), and \(c=c(M,\eta)>0\) such that for every
\(T,T'\in\mathfrak K\) with
\(\|\mathcal D_M(T)-\mathcal D_M(T')\|_M\ge\eta\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-separator}
 \max_{1\le r\le R}
 \left|
   Q_{T,a_r}^{\infty}\phi_r-Q_{T',a_r}^{\infty}\phi_r
 \right|\ge4c.
\end{equation}
The offsets in this finite list are strictly positive; hence their minimum is
positive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The separated-pair set
\[
 \mathcal P_{M,\eta}=
 \{(T,T')\in\mathfrak K^2:
   \|\mathcal D_M(T)-\mathcal D_M(T')\|_M\ge\eta\}
\]
is compact.  For each pair in it, equality of every centered signed boundary
law at every positive offset would imply equality of both action germs and
therefore equality of \(\mathcal D_M\), a contradiction.  Thus some design
\(a\) gives distinct probability measures.  By the dual characterization of
total variation there is a bounded measurable \(\phi\), \(|\phi|\le1\),
whose expectations differ.  The boundary laws vary continuously in the table
on the common collar; after reducing the separation constant, the same pair
\((a,\phi)\) separates a neighborhood of the chosen parameter pair.  A finite
subcover of \(\mathcal P_{M,\eta}\) gives the asserted list, and the minimum of
the finitely many positive separation constants gives \(4c\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{A charged global centering pilot}

The centered designs in Lemma~\ref{lem:v24-finite-separators} do not presume
knowledge of the gaps.  They are implemented after a charged pilot which is
retained in the transcript.  We record the global version needed here.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform finite-bridge centering pilot]
\label{lem:v24-global-centering-pilot}
Fix a finite list of persistent labelled channels.  For every
\(\epsilon>0\) and \(0<\delta<1\) there is a finite, deterministically capped
physical pilot, using only predetermined channel labels, flight numbers and
laboratory times together with its own previously observed records, which
returns gap estimates \(\widehat g_e\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-pilot-uniform}
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}
 \Pr_T\left\{
  \max_e j_e|\widehat g_e-g_e(T)|>\epsilon
       \right\}<\delta.
\end{equation}
The pilot has a finite deterministic preparation cap depending on
\((\epsilon,\delta,\mathfrak K)\), and every subsequent programmed time
\(j_e\widehat g_e+d\), with \(d\) in a prescribed finite positive-offset
list, lies in the common physical collar on the event in
\eqref{eq:v24-pilot-uniform} once \(\epsilon\) is chosen below the collar
margin.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Compactness gives a common finite interval \([g_-,g_+]\) containing every
designated channel gap, a common positive threshold collar, and uniform upper
and lower constants in the threshold law.  For a chosen flight number \(j\),
scan a deterministic grid of candidate onsets in \([g_-,g_+]\) with mesh
smaller than \(h/(8j)\).  At each grid point use a fixed small positive
programmed excess and independent selected-channel preparations.  Before the
selected onset the designated event is impossible, while at a grid point
whose true excess lies in a fixed subinterval of the threshold collar its
success probability is bounded below by
\(c h^2e^{-j\gamma_+}\), with \(\gamma_+<\infty\) uniform on
\(\mathfrak K\).  Binomial exponential bounds and a union bound over the
finite grid therefore locate, with arbitrarily high uniform probability, a
coarse center \(g_0\) satisfying the bracket
\(|j(g-g_0)|\le h/4\).

Conditional on this event, apply the onset-zero interpolation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v5-calibration} using fresh preparations.  The proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration} is uniform on compact geometric
families and gives
\(|j(\widehat g-g)|\le C_mh^{m+1}\).  First choose \(m,h\) so that this is at
most \(\epsilon\), and then choose the success counts and deterministic
waiting-time caps so that the total failure probability, including the coarse
scan, is below \(\delta\).  There are finitely many channels, so another union
bound proves \eqref{eq:v24-pilot-uniform}.  The pilot is exact physical data;
it is not replaced by a boundary experiment and its cost is not discarded.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform fixed-order estimation from actual finite bridges}

\begin{theorem}[Physical uniform estimability of every fixed action-jet vector]
\label{thm:v24-fixed-M-physical-estimability}
For every fixed \(M\), every \(\eta>0\) and every \(0<\delta<1\), there is a
single finite, deterministically capped, implementable finite-bridge
acquisition policy \(\pi_{M,\eta,\delta}\) and a measurable estimator
\(\widehat{\mathcal D}_M\) based on its complete transcript such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-fixed-M-uniform}
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}
 \Pr_T^{\pi_{M,\eta,\delta}}
 \left\{
  \|\widehat{\mathcal D}_M-\mathcal D_M(T)\|_M>\eta
 \right\}<\delta.
\end{equation}
The policy is parameter independent in the statistical sense: its labelled
channels, pilot grid, finite offset lists, stopping targets and deterministic
caps are fixed from \((M,\eta,\delta,\mathfrak K)\); only subsequent design
choices are measurable functions of the already observed pilot transcript.
No unknown gap, local chart or table parameter is supplied to the policy.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:v24-finite-separators} with separation \(\eta\), reducing
\(\eta\) by a harmless fixed factor when passing between the normalized data
norm and a minimum-distance reconstruction below.  Let
\((a_r,\phi_r)_{r=1}^R\) and \(c>0\) be the resulting finite separator list.
Continuity of the centered boundary laws gives a centering tolerance
\(\epsilon>0\) such that replacing every true centered time \(jg_e+d_r\) by
\(j\widehat g_e+d_r\) changes all separator expectations by less than
\(c/4\) whenever \(j|\widehat g_e-g_e|<\epsilon\).

Run Lemma~\ref{lem:v24-global-centering-pilot} with failure probability at
most \(\delta/8\).  Conditional on a good pilot, use fresh preparations at
the finite separator designs and stop each batch after \(k\) successful
records.  Choose \(k\) so large that Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound
give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-empirical-separators}
 \Pr\left\{
  \max_r|\widehat\mu_r-Q_{T,a_r}^{\infty}\phi_r|>c/2
       \right\}<\delta/8
\end{equation}
for the ideal centered boundary experiment, after allowing the preceding
\(c/4\) centering error and a further \(c/4\) finite-flight relative-law
error.

We now choose the common even flight number \(J\).  The relative boundary law
is uniform on \(\mathfrak K\), so take \(J\) large enough that the finite-
flight boundary separator expectations differ from their limiting values by
less than \(c/4\).  Increase \(J\) further until the stopped physical transfer
budget satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-transfer-budget}
                         C R k\tau^J<\delta/8.
\end{equation}
This is always possible because \(0<\tau<1\).  The charged pilot is kept
exactly.  Corollary~\ref{cor:v16-pilot-transfer} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer} then compare the entire post-pilot
stopped physical transcript with its boundary counterpart by at most the
right side of \eqref{eq:v24-transfer-budget}, on the good-pilot event.

For this fixed \(J\) and finite positive-offset list, compactness and the
uniform threshold law give a strictly positive lower bound for the success
probability in each post-pilot batch on the good event.  Therefore one can
choose deterministic preparation caps so that failure to obtain the requested
\(k\) successes contributes less than \(\delta/8\), uniformly on
\(\mathfrak K\).  On a bad pilot history the policy uses any fixed safe
completion rule and stops at its deterministic cap, exactly as in
Corollary~\ref{cor:v16-pilot-transfer}.

It remains to reconstruct the finite data.  For \(T'\in\mathfrak K\) put
\[
 \mu(T')=(Q_{T',a_1}^{\infty}\phi_1,\ldots,
          Q_{T',a_R}^{\infty}\phi_R)
\]
and choose a measurable approximate minimum-distance estimator
\(\widehat T\) from a fixed countable dense subset of \(\mathfrak K\):
\[
 \|\mu(\widehat T)-\widehat\mu\|_\infty
 \le\inf_{T'\in\mathfrak K}
       \|\mu(T')-\widehat\mu\|_\infty+c/4.
\]
Whenever all errors above are within their allocated bounds, the true table
has criterion less than \(c\).  If
\(\|\mathcal D_M(\widehat T)-\mathcal D_M(T)\|_M\ge\eta\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:v24-finite-separators} supplies a coordinate whose two ideal
expectations differ by at least \(4c\), contradicting the two criterion
bounds.  Thus
\(\|\mathcal D_M(\widehat T)-\mathcal D_M(T)\|_M<\eta\).
Set \(\widehat{\mathcal D}_M=\mathcal D_M(\widehat T)\).  Summing the pilot,
empirical, finite-flight, transfer and cap errors proves
\eqref{eq:v24-fixed-M-uniform}.  No local asymptotic theorem has been promoted
to global consistency by fiat: the finite physical policy has been explicitly
constructed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One growing-order physical reconstruction sequence}

We can now eliminate the hypothesis in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-diagonal-global-reconstruction} for the actual
finite-bridge model.

\begin{theorem}[Global reconstruction from the actual finite-bridge experiment]
\label{thm:v24-physical-global-reconstruction}
There exists a deterministic sequence of finite, deterministically capped,
implementable finite-bridge policies \(\pi_s\), finite jet orders
\(M_s\uparrow\infty\), and measurable table estimators
\(\widehat T_s\in\mathfrak K\) such that for every fixed integer \(q\ge0\)
and every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-physical-global-consistency}
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}
 \Pr_T^{\pi_s}\{d_q(\widehat T_s,T)>\varepsilon\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The policy sequence uses the actual finite-bridge transcript, including all
charged pilots, failures, waiting counts, selected endpoint--time records and
stopping times.  The number \(L_s\) of design points, their offsets, the
success allocations, flight numbers and deterministic caps are all specified
at stage \(s\); they may grow with \(s\).  They can be chosen so that the total
finite-to-boundary comparison error satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-growing-transfer}
                         C L_s k_s\tau^{J_s}\le2^{-s}.
\end{equation}
No uniform-in-order inverse condition number and no sharp minimax rate are
assumed or claimed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each \(s\), use Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-global-modulus}.  Choose
\(M_s\ge s\), increasing in \(s\), and \(\eta_s>0\) so small that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-diagonal-modulus-choice}
 \omega_q(C\eta_s+r_{M_s})<s^{-1}
 \qquad\text{for every }0\le q\le s.
\end{equation}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-fixed-M-physical-estimability} with
\((M,\eta,\delta)=(M_s,\eta_s,2^{-s})\).  In its construction, after the
finite separator list and success count \(k_s\) are known, choose the common
flight number \(J_s\) large enough that both the finite-flight approximation
error required there and \eqref{eq:v24-growing-transfer} hold.  This explicitly
solves the growing-design allocation problem: the order \(M_s\) is chosen
first, compactness supplies a finite design list of size \(L_s\), the empirical
accuracy fixes a finite success target \(k_s\), and exponential transfer then
allows a finite \(J_s\).  The uniform threshold lower bound at that finite
\(J_s\) gives finite deterministic preparation caps.  No circular dependence
is present.

Let \(\widehat{\mathcal D}_{M_s}\) be the resulting physical estimator and
choose \(\widehat T_s\) by the same measurable minimum-distance rule used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-fixed-M-physical-estimability}.  With probability at least
\(1-2^{-s}\), its finite data differ from those of the true table by at most a
constant times \(\eta_s\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:v24-finite-modulus} and the choice
\eqref{eq:v24-diagonal-modulus-choice} imply simultaneously
\[
                         d_q(\widehat T_s,T)<s^{-1},
                         \qquad 0\le q\le s.
\]
Fix \(q\) and \(\varepsilon>0\).  For all sufficiently large \(s\),
\(q\le s\) and \(s^{-1}<\varepsilon\), while the exceptional probability is
at most \(2^{-s}\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:v24-physical-global-consistency}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope and cost]
\label{rem:v24-global-cost-scope}
The theorem is a global consistency theorem for the declared finite-bridge
observation architecture.  It is not a statement about the full growing
collision array.  Its construction is intentionally rate-agnostic: the
finite separator number \(L_s\), success allocation \(k_s\), flight number
\(J_s\) and pilot cost may grow very rapidly.  The achievement here is that
all of them are finite, parameter independent or implementably adaptive, and
chosen from proved uniform bounds, so the deterministic all-order inverse and
the physical experiment meet in one unconditional theorem.  Optimizing this
cost is a separate minimax problem.
\end{remark}
