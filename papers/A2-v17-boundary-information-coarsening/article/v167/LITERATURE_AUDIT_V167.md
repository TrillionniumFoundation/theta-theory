## Hilbert regularity and graph blow-ups

Gerd Gotzmann, *Eine Bedingung für die Flachheit und das Hilbertpolynom eines graduierten Ringes*, Math. Z. 158 (1978), 61–70, DOI 10.1007/BF01214566. The publisher's page and EuDML record were checked:

- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01214566
- https://eudml.org/doc/172619

The contemporary primary research exposition by Patience Ablett, *Gotzmann's persistence theorem for smooth projective toric varieties*, arXiv:2405.02275v2, Section 2.1 and its introduction, was read for the standard-graded theorem and the distinction from multigraded generalizations:

- https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02275v2

The present proof uses the ordinary projective Hilbert scheme after the Segre embedding, computes the Gotzmann representation of `(a+1)l+1`, and restricts the degree-m quotient to `H^0(P1 x P2,O(m,m))`. It does not invoke an unproved multigraded regularity bound. The original Grothendieck Hilbert representability source already appears in the inherited bibliography.

The Stacks Project, Section 31.33, Tag 01OF, was read for the Rees definition, affine blow-up charts, flat base change, universal property, and blow-up of product ideals:

- https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01OF

The graph-closure equals Rees blow-up argument is reproduced in the proof. Its classical nature is explicitly acknowledged. The new explicit input is the matrix `M_a`; on the nonsymmetric slice the row-by-row computation gives the actual ideal `b^10(b,c)^4(b,c^2)^6`.

## Full flattening versus a horizontal Hilbert main component

The Stacks Project, Section 38.21, Tag 052F, was read:

- https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/052F

Its full-pullback flatness functor is the comparison target. The manuscript gives the natural inclusion from full-flat admissible tests into horizontal-flat tests, and the plane blow-up restricted to a line is a scheme-theoretic counterexample to equating them. Classical flattening by blow-up concerns pure transforms, so it is also distinguished from full tensor-pullback flatness. The revision does not claim a new general representability mechanism or a replacement for Raynaud–Gruson flattening.

Raynaud–Gruson, *Critères de platitude et de projectivité. Techniques de «platification» d'un module*, Invent. Math. 13 (1971), 1–89, DOI 10.1007/BF01390094: publisher/EuDML bibliographic records were checked. No full-paper proof audit is claimed from those records.

## State polytopes and coefficient-state chambers

David Bayer and Ian Morrison, *Standard bases and geometric invariant theory. I. Initial ideals and state polytopes*, J. Symbolic Comput. 6 (1988), no. 2–3, 209–217, DOI 10.1016/S0747-7171(88)80043-9. Bibliography was checked against the author's publication list and publisher listing:

- https://www.math.columbia.edu/~bayer/papers/
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747717188800439

The publisher full-text fetch was not successful. No exhaustive theorem-level nonanticipation certificate is claimed. Classical initial ideals/state polytopes are credited in the manuscript. The new proof independently constructs a finite hyperplane arrangement on the **coefficient supports of the explicit minors**, then partitions residue parameters by every possible cancellation. It makes no claim that a change in vocabulary, or coefficient weights instead of target-coordinate weights, alone establishes originality.

## Other compactification frameworks

The revision supplies a fixed-target embedded Hilbert graph, strict horizontal comparison, and a computed smooth two-wall parameter surface. It does not establish an equivalence with a relative Quot compactification, logarithmic moduli stack, expanded degeneration stack, or stable-map compactification. The inherited references and target distinctions remain, but a complete theorem/proof comparison across all these frameworks is not represented as finished.
