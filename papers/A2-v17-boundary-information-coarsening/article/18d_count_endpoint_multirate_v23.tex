\section{Waiting counts and endpoint information after residual-time coarsening}
\label{sec:v23-count-endpoint}

Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-complete-et} identifies the fastest local experiment
when residual time is retained.  We now return to the coarsening which removes
residual time but \emph{keeps the waiting counts}.  At that record level the
positive-density ceiling has been integrated out.  Two slower information
mechanisms remain: the rare-event probability detects the hyperbolic exponent,
while the endpoint mark detects motion of the linearly vanishing support.
They operate on different local scales.

Fix one anchored labelled channel and a finite contact-jet model of order
\(M\ge2\).  Let \(\vartheta\in\mathbb R^{r_M}\) be the anchored shape
coordinate at fixed gap and let
\[
                         \gamma(g,\vartheta)
\]
be the channel hyperbolic exponent.  At the reference point \((g_0,0)\) put
\[
 \lambda=D_\vartheta\gamma(g_0,0)\in(\mathbb R^{r_M})^*.
\]
Because the finite jet coordinate contains the two contact curvatures and
\(\gamma\) depends nontrivially on them, \(\lambda\ne0\).  Choose
\(v_\gamma\in\mathbb R^{r_M}\) with
\(\lambda(v_\gamma)=1\), and let
\[
                         H_\gamma=\ker\lambda.
\]
Thus \(v_\gamma\) is one fast hyperbolic direction and \(H_\gamma\) is the
iso-hyperbolic tangent space to first order.  The choice of
\(v_\gamma\) is not canonical, but different choices give equivalent local
coordinates.

Let \(k_{n,\ell}\) and \(k_n\) satisfy
\eqref{eq:v23-et-design-proportions}.  Choose \(\delta_n\downarrow0\) with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-count-endpoint-critical}
             k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1.
\end{equation}
For bounded \((b,a,h)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\times H_\gamma\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-count-endpoint-parameter}
 \eta_n=\frac1{j_n\sqrt{k_n}},\qquad
 g_n(a)=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a,
 \qquad
 \vartheta_n(b,h)=\eta_nb\,v_\gamma+\delta_nh.
\end{equation}
The fast coordinate \(b\) changes the exponent by
\(b/(j_n\sqrt{k_n})\) to first order, whereas the slow shape coordinate is
tangent to the level set of \(\gamma\).  The gap remains on the v22 endpoint
scale \(\delta_n/j_n\).

For each design let \(T_{n,\ell}\) be the number of preparations required to
obtain \(k_{n,\ell}\) successes before the deterministic cap, and retain the
successful endpoint pairs.  This is a coarsening of the stopped
endpoint--time transcript: residual times are discarded, but failures and
stopping counts are not.

\begin{lemma}[Negative-binomial hyperbolic score]
\label{lem:v23-negative-binomial-score}
Assume
\[
 j_n\delta_n\to0.
\]
Then, uniformly on compact \((b,a,h)\)-sets,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-success-log-expansion}
 \log\frac{p^\partial_{n,\ell}(b,a,h)}
          {p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0,0,0)}
 =-\frac{b}{\sqrt{k_n}}+o(k_n^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
The slow coordinates \((a,h)\) contribute \(o(k_n^{-1/2})\) to this log
ratio.

If
\[
 S_{n,\ell}
 =\frac{k_{n,\ell}-p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)T_{n,\ell}}
        {1-p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)},
 \qquad
 Z_n=-\frac1{\sqrt{k_n}}\sum_{\ell=1}^LS_{n,\ell},
\]
then under the reference law
\[
                         Z_n\Longrightarrow N(0,1),
\]
and the waiting-count log likelihood satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-count-lan}
 \log\frac{d\mathsf W_{n,b,a,h}}
          {d\mathsf W_{n,0,0,0}}
 =bZ_n-\frac12b^2+o_P(1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The uniform threshold formula has the form
\[
 p^\partial_{n,\ell}
 =C_{n,\ell}(g,\vartheta)
   [d_\ell+O(\delta_n)]^2
   \exp\{-j_n\gamma(g,\vartheta)\},
\]
with uniformly bounded derivatives of \(\log C_{n,\ell}\).  Along
\eqref{eq:v23-count-endpoint-parameter},
\[
 \gamma(g_n(a),\vartheta_n(b,h))-\gamma(g_0,0)
 =\frac{b}{j_n\sqrt{k_n}}
   +O_K(\delta_n/j_n)+O_K(\delta_n^2)+o_K((j_n\sqrt{k_n})^{-1}).
\]
The first-order \(\delta_nh\) term vanishes because \(h\in H_\gamma\).
After multiplication by \(j_n\), the gap contribution is \(O_K(\delta_n)\)
and the quadratic slow contribution is \(O_K(j_n\delta_n^2)\).  Both are
\(o(k_n^{-1/2})\) after multiplication by \(\sqrt{k_n}\): indeed
\(\sqrt{k_n}\delta_n=O(\log(1/\delta_n)^{-1/2})\), while
\(j_n\delta_n\to0\) makes
\(\sqrt{k_n}j_n\delta_n^2=o(1)\).  Smooth prefactors contribute only
\(O_K(\delta_n)+O_K(\eta_n)\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:v23-success-log-expansion}.

For a negative-binomial variable stopped at \(k\) successes, the score for
\(\phi=\log p\) is
\[
                         \frac{k-pT}{1-p}
\]
and its variance is \(k/(1-p)\).  Since
\(p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)\to0\) and
\(k_{n,\ell}/k_n\to\alpha_\ell\), the independent batch scores satisfy the
Lindeberg central limit theorem and have total normalized variance tending to
one.  Taylor expansion of the negative-binomial log likelihood in
\(\phi\), together with \eqref{eq:v23-success-log-expansion}, gives
\eqref{eq:v23-count-lan}.
\end{proof}

Let \(\mathcal K_{\rm design}\) be the positive-definite v22 endpoint
information matrix on the full coordinate \((a,h_{\rm full})\).  Denote by
\(\mathcal K_{H}\) its restriction to
\[
                         \mathbb R\times H_\gamma.
\]
It is positive definite on that subspace.

\begin{theorem}[Two-speed count--endpoint local experiment]
\label{thm:v23-count-endpoint-joint}
Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-count-endpoint-conditions}
 \begin{gathered}
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1,
 \qquad j_n\delta_n\to0,\\
 \frac{\log(j_n\sqrt{k_n})}{j_n^2}\to0,
 \qquad k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0,
 \qquad j_n\in2\mathbb N.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Use the local parameter \eqref{eq:v23-count-endpoint-parameter} and the
reference-based caps.  Then the boundary experiment which retains the waiting
counts and the successful endpoint pairs, but not residual times, has the
following joint local limit uniformly on compact parameter sets:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-count-endpoint-limit}
 \mathcal G^{\rm count}_b\otimes
 \mathcal G^{\rm end}_{(a,h)},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{itemize}
\item \(\mathcal G^{\rm count}_b\) is the one-dimensional Gaussian shift
with information one;
\item \(\mathcal G^{\rm end}_{(a,h)}\) is the Gaussian endpoint shift on
\(\mathbb R\times H_\gamma\) with information
\(\mathcal K_H\);
\item the two Gaussian factors are independent.
\end{itemize}
Equivalently, in the multirate coordinate \((b,a,h)\) the limiting information
is block diagonal,
\[
                         1\oplus\mathcal K_H.
\]
The fast hyperbolic direction is visible at shape scale
\((j_n\sqrt{k_n})^{-1}\); iso-hyperbolic shape directions remain at the
moving-endpoint scale \(\delta_n\), and gap remains at
\(\delta_n/j_n\).

The actual finite-bridge count--endpoint experiment has the same limit.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The waiting-count factor is Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-negative-binomial-score}.
Conditional on the waiting counts, the successful endpoint marks are iid and
independent of those counts.  For the slow coordinates \((a,h)\), the fixed
window reduction and Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-fixed-window-gaussian} apply exactly
as in v22, with the information quadratic form restricted to
\(\mathbb R\times H_\gamma\).

It remains to show that the fast perturbation
\(\eta_nb v_\gamma\) is invisible to the endpoint marks at the
\(\delta_n\) experiment.  A linearly vanishing moving boundary displaced by
\(O(\eta_n)\) has one-record squared Hellinger distance
\[
 O_K\!\left(\eta_n^2\log\frac1{\eta_n}\right).
\]
Therefore its contribution over \(k_n\) successful records is
\[
 O_K\!\left(
 k_n\eta_n^2\log\frac1{\eta_n}\right)
 =O_K\!\left(
 \frac{\log(j_n\sqrt{k_n})}{j_n^2}
 \right)=o(1).
\]
The smooth amplitude contribution is smaller.  Thus the endpoint factor sees
only \((a,h)\) in the limit.

Conversely, Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-negative-binomial-score} shows that the slow
coordinates are invisible in the count likelihood.  Exact factorization of a
stopped iid Bernoulli-mark experiment makes the two central sequences
independent.  The cap changes the law by exponentially small total variation.
Finally Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-stopped-local-transfer} transfers the whole
count--endpoint coarsening from the boundary model to the finite bridges with
error \(O_K(k_n\tau^{j_n})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A three-level information hierarchy]
\label{rem:v23-three-level-information}
For the declared physical acquisition process there are now three explicit
levels.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Retaining residual time gives the non-Gaussian Poisson boundary
experiment of Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-complete-et}, with shape scale
\(k_n^{-1}\) and gap scale \((j_nk_n)^{-1}\).
\item Removing residual time but retaining waiting counts gives the two-speed
Gaussian experiment of Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-count-endpoint-joint}: a fast
hyperbolic count direction at \((j_n\sqrt{k_n})^{-1}\) and the slower endpoint
boundary directions at \(\delta_n\).
\item Removing both residual time and waiting counts gives the v22
endpoint-output Gaussian experiment.
\end{enumerate}
These are genuinely different experiments produced by common Markov
coarsenings; none is relabelled as another.
\end{remark}
