\section{A remote branch at the stochastic weight wall}\label{sec:wall}
Fix $r,s,D$ with $B>0$, fix positive $u,v_1,v_2$ with $v_1\ne v_2$,
and set $U=(u,u)$, $V=(v_1,v_2)$. Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall-centre}
 \gamma_0=\pi c_*,\qquad \alpha_0=1-\pi c_*,\qquad
                   \pi<\alpha_0<1-\pi.
\end{equation}
The design is fixed. Write $\theta_\delta$ for the original $(f,g)$
centre with weight $\alpha=\alpha_0+\delta$. Then $E=-A\delta$,
so $\delta>0$ is the identified side. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstar}
 a=\frac{4rs}{r+3s},\qquad b=\frac{rs}{3s-2r},\qquad
 h_*=(1-c_*)f+c_*g=(z-a)(z-b)^2.
\end{equation}
Direct substitution gives $s<a<r<b<D$. At $\delta=0$ the exact
component pairs are precisely $(f,g)$ and $(f,h_*)$. The remote pair
has weight $\pi$ on $h_*$ and second-channel columns
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remote-channel}
 v_f^*=\frac{\alpha_0v_1+(\gamma_0-\pi)v_2}{1-\pi},\qquad v_h^*=v_2.
\end{equation}
Let $\xi_*$ denote this parameter point. Its observed array equals
$P_0=P_{\theta_0}$. The sorted base and remote spectra are
\[
 (0,s,s,s,r,r),\qquad (0,a,r,r,b,b),
\]
and their distance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dstar}
 D_* =\max(r-s,b-r)>0.
\end{equation}
In particular, a zero-length additional pencil interval at the wall
already produces a positive exact-fibre diameter.

\subsection{The constrained entrance program}
At $\xi_*$ the full first-order feasible coefficient and stochastic
cone is described by
\begin{align}
 p_f&=-a_0(z-r)^2-b_0z(z-r)-Xz,\nonumber\\
 p_h&=-c_0(z-b)^2-d_0(z-a)(z-b)-Y(z-a),                 \label{eq:remote-jets}\\
 &a_0,X,Y,k\ge0.\nonumber
\end{align}
Here $k$ is the derivative of the weight on $h_*$; $b_0,c_0,d_0$ and
four channel increments are free. Denote the score by $S_*$, with
weights $(1-\pi,\pi)$ and columns \eqref{eq:remote-channel}, and put
$B_0=\left.\frac{d}{d\delta}P_{\theta_\delta}\right|_{\delta=0}$.
The norm is Fisher at $P_0$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu}
 \mu_E=\frac12\min_{v\in\mathcal T_*}\|S_*v-B_0\|_{I,0},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T_*$ is the cone \eqref{eq:remote-jets} with its free
variables. It is a finite-dimensional cone, not a closure of unspecified
observation-ball optimizers.

\begin{lemma}[Positive entrance separation]\label{lem:mu-positive}
The score has no nonzero zero on $\mathcal T_*$, and $B_0$ is not in
its image. Thus $\mu_E>0$ and its minimum is attained. After eliminating
seven free variables, $\mu_E^2$ is given by sixteen independent-support
KKT tests on four nonnegative variables. For algebraic input this is
an exact real algebraic number.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $S_*v=B_0$. Normalizer injectivity
(Lemma~\ref{lem:normalizer}, or its differentiated linear equations)
gives $\dot q=f-g$ and $\dot K=\dot K_{\theta_\delta}|_0$.
The second marginal of this derivative lies in $\operatorname{span}(f,g)$.
Since the coefficient vectors of the remote two components and their
second-channel weights are independent, differentiating their marginal
factorization gives $p_f,p_h\in\operatorname{span}(f,g)$. Both have
degree below three, hence are multiples of $g-f$.

At the $f$ endpoint of the pencil, the endpoint and discriminant
inequalities in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:local-cubic} force the
first-order pencil displacement to be zero. Thus $p_f=0$. At $h_*$,
\eqref{eq:disc} has positive derivative with respect to $c$ at $c_*$,
so feasible $p_h=\dot c(g-f)$ requires $\dot c\ge0$. Differentiating
the monic normalizer now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall-obstruction}
 \pi\dot c+c_*k=-1,
\end{equation}
which is impossible because $k,\dot c\ge0$.
For a zero score the same argument gives right-hand side zero, so
$k=\dot c=0$. All component jets vanish, and dual coefficient
functionals then force the four channel derivatives to vanish.
The basis in \eqref{eq:remote-jets} is independent within each component,
so all variables are zero. Compactness of the intersection of
$\mathcal T_*$ with the unit sphere gives coercivity. It implies
boundedness of minimizing sequences in \eqref{eq:mu}, hence attainment
and strict positivity.

For an explicit program, let $\mathsf L_*$ be the seven free columns,
$\mathsf D_*$ the four nonnegative columns, and let $\mathcal P$ be the
Fisher-orthogonal projection off $\operatorname{im}\mathsf L_*$. Put
\[
 Q_*=\mathsf D_*^{\mathsf T}W\mathcal P\mathsf D_*,\quad
 g_*=\mathsf D_*^{\mathsf T}W\mathcal P B_0,\quad
 b_*=B_0^{\mathsf T}W\mathcal P B_0.
\]
The already proved coercivity implies that $\mathsf L_*$ has full rank. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entrance-QP}
 4\mu_E^2=\min_{v\ge0}\{v^{\mathsf T}Q_*v-2g_*^{\mathsf T}v+b_*\}.
\end{equation}
For each of the sixteen supports $A$, with $Q_{*,AA}$ positive definite,
set $v_A=Q_{*,AA}^{-1}g_{*,A}$, require $v_A\ge0$ and
$(Q_*v-g_*)_j\ge0$ off $A$, and take value
$b_*-g_{*,A}^{\mathsf T}Q_{*,AA}^{-1}g_{*,A}$. The empty support is
included. The minimal-positive-support argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic-Q} proves completeness even if $Q_*$ is
singular. All entries are rational in the wall coordinates; selecting
the positive square root gives the exact algebraic output.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local coefficient control at the remote point]\label{lem:remote-control}
There is a coefficient and stochastic neighbourhood of $\xi_*$ in the
closed model on which
\[
 d_{\rm par}(\xi,\xi_*)\le C\|F_T(\xi)-P_0\|.
\]
Every competitor at coefficient distance $O(\delta)$ has bounded
coordinates of \eqref{eq:remote-jets} at scale $\delta$, with observation
expansion $P_0+\delta S_*v+O(\delta^{3/2})$. Conversely each bounded
cone vector has a realizing stochastic root arc with that expansion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient tangent cone is exactly \eqref{eq:remote-jets}.
Indeed, at the endpoint $0$ a simple root has nonnegative displacement;
at either interior double root its centred squared separation is
nonnegative; the two simple/mean coordinates are unrestricted. The
weight on $h_*$ is at its lower floor, so its derivative is nonnegative.
All channels and the other weight are interior. Analytic factorization
into the separated simple and double clusters gives these coordinates
for normalized coefficient secants. Conversely the root arcs with
simple and mean shifts of order $\delta$, double splits
$\pm\sqrt{\delta X}$ and $\pm\sqrt{\delta Y}$, and weight
$\pi+\delta k$, realize every cone vector.

If the asserted inverse failed, normalize a sequence of coefficient
and stochastic displacements by their norm. A subsequential limit is
a nonzero vector of this tangent cone with zero score, contrary to
Lemma~\ref{lem:mu-positive}. For the stronger expansion, the coefficient
bound controls simple and mean shifts by $O(\delta)$ and the squared
double separations by $O(\delta)$. Extract them as exact coordinates.
Expansion of their cubic products gives \eqref{eq:remote-jets} and a
bounded $O(\delta^{3/2})$ remainder. The rational observation map and
the square-root probabilities have bounded derivatives in this
neighbourhood. The converse follows from the displayed realizing arcs;
all roots remain in $[0,D]$ because $a,r,b$ are strictly interior.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two-scale law}
Choose disjoint small closed coefficient and parameter neighbourhoods
$N_0,N_*$ of the two exact representatives at the wall, modulo component
permutation, with $\xi_*$ in the relative interior of $N_*$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drem}
 d_{\rm rem}(\delta)=\min_{\xi\in N_*}h_w(F_T(\xi),P_{\theta_\delta}).
\end{equation}
Different sufficiently small choices give the same minimum for all
sufficiently small $\delta>0$; their boundaries have a positive
observation separation from $P_0$.

\begin{theorem}[Weight-wall transition]\label{thm:wall}
Under \eqref{eq:wall-centre} and $B>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entrance-law}
 d_{\rm rem}(\delta)=\mu_E\delta+O(\delta^{3/2})\quad(\delta\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
The local constant $C_{\loc}(\theta_\delta)$ has the finite positive
limit $C_{\loc}(\theta_0)$; neither $\kappa_x$ nor $\kappa_y$ vanishes
at this wall. For a fixed $\lambda>0$ the whole-model modulus has the
following two distinct regimes:
\begin{align}
 0<\lambda<\mu_E:\quad
 \omega_{\theta_\delta}(\lambda\delta)
   &=C_{\loc}(\theta_0)\sqrt{\lambda\delta}+O_\lambda(\delta),
                                                        \label{eq:below-wall}\\
 \lambda>\mu_E:\quad
 \omega_{\theta_\delta}(\lambda\delta)
   &=D_*+O_\lambda(\sqrt\delta).                         \label{eq:above-wall}
\end{align}
The exact entrance curve, including equality for the closed observation
ball, is $t=d_{\rm rem}(\delta)$. The transition layer left unresolved
by the first-order threshold alone has width $O(\delta^{3/2})$ in $t$.

On the nonidentified side, for $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonident-opening}
 \omega_{\theta_{-\varepsilon}}(0)
 =\max\left\{r-s,\ b-r+\sqrt{\frac{\chi\varepsilon}{\pi}}
                         +O(\varepsilon)\right\},\qquad
 \chi=\frac{f(b)-g(b)}{b-a}>0.
\end{equation}
If $b-r<r-s$, the exact diameter remains equal to $r-s$ initially;
if $b-r\ge r-s$, it opens above $D_*$ at the displayed square-root rate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At $\delta=0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-fibre} gives exactly the two
representatives and no others. Compactness gives a positive observation
gap outside $N_0\cup N_*$. Since $\xi_*$ itself has distance $O(\delta)$
from $P_{\theta_\delta}$, a minimizer in \eqref{eq:drem} has observation
$O(\delta)$ from $P_0$, lies away from the boundary of $N_*$, and has
coefficient distance $O(\delta)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:remote-control}.
Its exact scaled root and stochastic coordinates are bounded in
$\mathcal T_*$. Taylor expansion at the common positive probability
array gives, uniformly on these bounded coordinates,
\[
 h_w(F_T(\xi),P_{\theta_\delta})
   =\frac\delta2\|S_*v-B_0\|_{I,0}+O(\delta^{3/2}).
\]
This is the lower bound in \eqref{eq:entrance-law}. A minimizing cone
vector in \eqref{eq:mu}, realized by the arcs of
Lemma~\ref{lem:remote-control}, gives the upper bound. This also proves
independence from the selected neighbourhood for small $\delta$.

Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic-Q} and Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-local} apply
on a compact path segment including $\theta_0$; the base pair stays
locally coercive there. Hence $C_{\loc}$ is positive and locally
Lipschitz through the wall. If $\lambda<\mu_E$, the remote neighbourhood
is excluded from the radius-$\lambda\delta$ ball for small $\delta$.
The positive exterior gap excludes every other competitor. The entire
ball is therefore the base local ball, and \eqref{eq:cubic-C} gives
\eqref{eq:below-wall}. Replacing $C_{\loc}(\theta_\delta)$ by
$C_{\loc}(\theta_0)$ changes the expression by only $O(\delta^{3/2})$.

If $\lambda>\mu_E$, a remote minimizer belongs to the ball, as does
the base centre. Every competitor lies in one of the two neighbourhoods.
The respective coefficient inverses put its roots within $O_\lambda(\sqrt
\delta)$ of the corresponding exact spectrum at the wall. The triangle
inequality for bottleneck distance then gives both bounds in
\eqref{eq:above-wall}. Compact attainment makes remote membership
exactly the inequality $t\ge d_{\rm rem}(\delta)$, including equality.
The stated layer width follows from \eqref{eq:entrance-law}; no universal
answer at $t=\mu_E\delta$ is inferred from a first-order expansion.

For $\theta_{-\varepsilon}$, the extra exact pencil interval is
$[c_*,c_*+\varepsilon/\pi]$, since $c_D>c_*$ is fixed. At
$c=c_*+\sigma$, expand the polynomial at $z=b+u$:
\[
 h_c(b+u)=(b-a)u^2+u^3+\sigma(g(b)-f(b))+O(\sigma|u|).
\]
Since $R(b)>1$, the coefficient $\chi$ is positive, and the two
roots near $b$ are $b\pm\sqrt{\chi\sigma}+O(\sigma)$. The third
root stays in $(s,r)$. The larger root increases with $c$, by the
monotonic branch of $R$ above its maximum. Comparing the increasing
root lists shows that the distance from the base spectrum is exactly
$\max(r-s,b_+(c)-r)$. Distances between two remote spectra are
$O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$ and hence smaller than $D_*$ for sufficiently
small $\varepsilon$. The whole exact-fibre diameter is therefore
attained against the base at the upper pencil endpoint. This proves
\eqref{eq:nonident-opening} and the last two assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The critical ray and effective higher orders]
At $t=\mu_E\delta$, remote membership is decided by the sign of
$d_{\rm rem}(\delta)-\mu_E\delta$, not by choosing either strict regime
by convention. For algebraic input, \eqref{eq:drem} has a compact
first-order minimum formula. Elimination and one-variable real algebraic
branch isolation determine its Puiseux expansion. If the difference is
not identically zero, its first nonzero coefficient decides membership
for all small positive $\delta$; if it is identically zero the remote
branch belongs to the closed ball. This gives an effective determination
of the critical ray while keeping the universal first-order statement
honest about its transition layer.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The geometric wall]
At $B=0$ with $E>0$, the remote double root is $b=D$ and the additional
pencil interval again consists of one point with a positive spectral
distance from the base. From the nonidentified side, put $\ell_0=D-b$.
Taylor expansion at the strict maximum of $R$ gives
\[
 c_D-c_*=
 \frac{-R''(b)}{2(R(b)-1)^2}\,\ell_0^2+O(\ell_0^3),\qquad
 \ell_0=\frac{B}{3s-2r}.
\]
The coefficient is positive and the weight floor is inactive near this
wall. Thus this pencil interval is born with quadratic length in the
geometric clearance, unlike the linear length at the weight wall.
The local constants in \eqref{eq:cubic-C} remain finite on compact
subsets of $\mathfrak F$ here as well. A two-scale entrance program at
the endpoint has different one-sided root coordinates from
\eqref{eq:remote-jets}; the explicit entrance theorem above concerns
the weight wall, not that different cone.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[An exact wall datum]\label{ex:wall-exact}
Take $D=1$, $r=3/5$, $s=11/20$, $\pi=1/10$, the columns and clocks
of the rank-one rational example in Section~\ref{sec:cubic}, and equal
design weights. Then
\[
 \alpha_0=\frac{547}{675},\quad c_*=\frac{256}{135},\quad
 a=\frac{44}{75},\quad b=\frac{11}{15},\quad
 B=\frac3{25},\quad D_*=\frac2{15},\quad\chi=\frac3{64},
\]
and $v_f^*=(1457,3403)^{\mathsf T}/4860$. Exact rational evaluation
of the two base programs and the remote program gives
\begin{align*}
 5975.429249985072&<C_{\loc}(\theta_0)<5975.429249985073,\\
 2.131024914\cdot10^{-6}&<\mu_E<2.131024915\cdot10^{-6}.
\end{align*}
In both base programs, and in the four-variable remote program, the
empty positive support is admissible. The ancillary exact record
contains the rational Gram matrix, positive squared entrance constant,
fourth power of the local constant, and rational isolating intervals.
The decimal displays are consequences of rational power comparisons,
not optimization tolerances. They illustrate a well-defined but poorly
conditioned local law and a distinct, very small remote entrance scale.
They do not certify the universal quantifiers in the preceding theorems.
\end{example}

\subsection*{Three stability questions}
Root multiplicity and one-sided boundary constraints determine the
valuation weights in Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-atlas}. Exact identification
in the cubic family is determined instead by \eqref{eq:I}. Conditioning
of a fixed local law is measured by the positive Fisher programs.
The weight-wall theorem shows that the second can fail while the third
stays finite and the local square-root exponent persists. In contrast,
Example~\ref{ex:crossover} changes the exponent itself and makes the
linear constant diverge. These are different strata and different
limiting operations in the real spectral atlas.
