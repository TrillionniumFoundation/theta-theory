\begin{abstract}
We study the diameter of polynomial spectra compatible with a small
neighbourhood of a normalized stochastic observation. A joint real weighted
specialization, rather than separate coefficient bounds, gives the complete
leading spectral set. We specify its effective construction and a finite
parameter stratification retaining exact fibres, accessible vanishing orders,
cluster weights, and leading diameters. For binary polynomial experiments of
arbitrary fixed degree with invertible channels, the resulting atlas is
explicit across all coprime root-multiplicity and boundary patterns: the
exponent is one half precisely when an interior root is repeated, and is one
otherwise. The leading constants are finite constrained Fisher programs;
for an interior cluster of multiplicity $m$ its diameter factor is
$2\sqrt{(m-1)/m}$. A binary cubic family adds an explicit rank-deficient
stratum. At its stochastic weight wall, identification is lost by the entry
of a remote spectral branch while the local Fisher constant stays finite.
We prove the linear entrance law for that branch, the resulting two-scale
transition from a vanishing modulus to a positive diameter, and the
square-root opening of the nonidentified exact fibre. These results separate
root-multiplicity scaling, exact identification, and local conditioning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An observation may determine a spectrum without determining unrestricted
coefficient directions. Conversely, a locally well-conditioned spectral
inverse need not remain globally identifying. The first distinction is
caused by real feasibility; the second can be caused by a remote component
of an exact fibre. A theory of singular spectral observations must keep
both distinctions, as well as the relations between different root clusters.

Let $F:X\to\mathcal P$ be a continuous semialgebraic observation on a compact
model, and let $A_x$ be a monic target polynomial. We write
\[
 \omega_P(t)=\diam\{Z(A_x):h_w(F(x),P)\le t\},
\]
where $Z$ counts roots with multiplicity and the metric is bottleneck
matching. The principal object is the simultaneous weighted specialization
of the real observation--coefficient graph. If the exact fibre has one
spectral value, Theorem~\ref{thm:joint} expresses the leading constant as the
diameter of its root image. Proposition~\ref{prop:real-cover} specifies the
real-accessible monomial data used to compute the exponent, including
components entirely above the exact observation fibre. Theorem~\ref{thm:atlas}
places these objects in a finite semialgebraic parameter atlas. The scalar
valuation formula is a classical ingredient, not the novelty claim of the
construction.

The explicit part of the article begins with a class larger than a fixed
cubic singularity. In the binary degree-$d$ model, take coprime component
polynomials and positive invertible channels. Roots may have arbitrary
multiplicities and may lie at either endpoint of the root interval. There
are finitely many such patterns at fixed $d$. Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-atlas}
gives the full leading set for every pattern. An interior cluster has
coefficient orders $1,k/2$ in weights $1,k\ge2$, whereas a boundary cluster
has orders $k$. Thus
\[
 \alpha=\begin{cases}1/2,&\text{some interior root is repeated},\\
 1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
In the first case the exact constant is
\[
 C=\max_{j:m_j\ge2,\ r_j\in(0,D)}
       2\sqrt{\frac{m_j-1}{m_j}}\,\kappa_j^{-1/4},
\]
with $\kappa_j$ given by a finite nonnegative least-squares problem. This
formula includes every interior multiplicity, not just two and three.
The proof is a whole-model Hausdorff comparison with realizing root arcs.
It does not replace the joint fibre by a product of marginal bounds.

The rank-deficient cubic class has an additional global phenomenon.
For $f=z(z-r)^2$, $g=(z-s)^3$, the exact spectral fibre is classified by
three polynomial signs (Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-fibre}). The local coefficient
inverse extends through the identification walls, although its
\emph{whole-model exclusion radius} need not do so. At a transverse
weight-wall crossing, put $\delta=\alpha-\alpha_0$. Theorem~\ref{thm:wall}
proves that the distance of the remote branch from the observed centre is
\[
 d_{\rm rem}(\delta)=\mu_E\delta+O(\delta^{3/2}),\qquad \mu_E>0.
\]
For $t=\lambda\delta$, the modulus is of order $\sqrt\delta$ when
$0<\lambda<\mu_E$, but tends to a positive exact-fibre diameter when
$\lambda>\mu_E$. In particular, a blow-up of the local Fisher constant is
not necessary for loss of identification. The constant $\mu_E$ is another
finite constrained Fisher calculation, now at the remote branch. An exact
rational example evaluates both constants.

For clarity about the comparison with \cite{Ha26}, its finite-test-set
hypotheses concern integral-closure tests and positive asymptotic
valuations. Its family theorem assumes a common toric model, constant
Newton polyhedra and uniform nondegeneracy, with piecewise-linear weights.
Neither hypothesis package is identified here with a real stochastic
feasibility condition. A real-valued valuation is also not the same
notion as an admissible real transversal. Granting the scalar finite-max
results leaves the joint specialization, its root image, and the
stochastic wall calculation to be established.
