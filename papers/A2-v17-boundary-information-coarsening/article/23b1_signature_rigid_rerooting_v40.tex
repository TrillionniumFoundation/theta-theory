\subsection{Transporting the root to an anchoring cycle}
\label{subsec:v40-rerooting}

The root of a signature-rigid spanning tree need not be prescribed at the
starting frame of an anchoring cycle.  The next lemma shows that these two
choices are compatible under the existing signature hypotheses.  Thus the
propagation of obstacle placements requires no additional registration or
root-incidence assumption.

\begin{lemma}[Rerooting a signature-rigid spanning tree]
\label{lem:v40-signature-rigid-rerooting}
Let a rooted signature-rigid spanning tree join a finite collection of
connected real-analytic strictly convex obstacle boundaries.  Suppose one
vertex $r$ has a framed boundary point at which the complete oriented
curvature signature occurs uniquely.  Then the same undirected tree,
rooted instead at $r$, is signature-rigid.  In particular, an obstacle
orbit met by a signature-rigid anchoring cycle may be chosen as the root.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First, uniqueness of the complete signature at one framed point of a
boundary $C$ implies
\[
                         \mathrm{Sym}^{+}(C)=\{\mathrm{id}\}.
\]
Indeed, every symmetry carries that framed point to one with the same
signature, by Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-signature-symmetry}.  Uniqueness makes it
fix the point and its oriented unit tangent.  An orientation-preserving
Euclidean motion fixing such a frame is the identity.  The same lemma
then shows that the complete signature occurs uniquely at every framed
point of $C$.  Thus uniqueness is a property of this boundary, rather
than of a specially chosen incidence on it.

Let $r_0$ be the original root.  Every vertex which has a child in the
original rooted tree has a unique-signature contact on an outgoing edge,
by Definition~\ref{def:v23-signature-rigid-tree}.  The preceding argument
makes all its framed boundary signatures unique.  Write the unique tree
path from $r_0$ to $r$ as
\[
                    r_0=v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_k=r.
\]
Rerooting reverses precisely the edges of this path.  On the reversed
edge from $v_i$ to $v_{i-1}$, with $1\le i<k$, the new parent $v_i$ was
the old parent of $v_{i+1}$ and therefore has unique signatures at every
framed point.  For $i=k$, the same conclusion follows from the hypothesis
on $r$.  Every reversed edge consequently has a unique-signature contact
on its new parent.  Off the path, the parent--child relations are
unchanged and retain their original property.  This proves the assertion;
when $k=0$, including the one-vertex empty tree, nothing is changed.

Every required incidence transition on a signature-rigid cycle has a
unique framed signature by its definition.  A vertex met by that cycle
therefore satisfies the hypothesis on $r$, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

For the rank-two recovery of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-uncalibrated-periodic-rigidity}, fix the common
starting channel frame of the anchoring pair and choose a root obstacle
met there.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v40-signature-rigid-rerooting} transports any
supplied rooted signature-rigid spanning tree to that obstacle.  The two
cycle holonomies determine the lattice in this frame, and induction along
the rerooted tree then places every obstacle orbit.  The same argument
applies to the single-offset reconstruction in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-single-offset-global}.  The tree is only reoriented;
no edge, obstacle label, deck displacement, or observation is added or
removed.
