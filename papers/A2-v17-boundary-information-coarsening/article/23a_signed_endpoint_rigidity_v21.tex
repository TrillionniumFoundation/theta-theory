\section{Signed endpoint rigidity without contact symmetry}
\label{sec:v19-signed-rigidity}

The scalar onset laws of Section~\ref{sec:v9-compatibility} deliberately
integrate out the signs of the two endpoint coordinates.  Their natural inverse
is therefore a symmetrized energy profile.  The physical endpoint record is
strictly richer.  Once the two endpoint signs and the two contact labels are
retained, the support itself records the two half-line actions before any
symmetrization.  We prove below that these actions determine arbitrary labelled
contact jets, odd and even.  The proof is given directly in the weighted
half-line sequence space; in particular the determinant-one all-order block and
its differential inverse are not inferred from nonlinear injectivity.

Fix once and for all oriented transverse coordinates in the two labelled
contact frames.  In these coordinates the facing graphs are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-signed-graphs}
 (-\psi_0(u),u),\qquad (g+\psi_1(v),v),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-general-contact-jets}
 \psi_b(y)=\frac{\kappa_b}{2}y^2+
       \sum_{n\ge3}\frac{q_{b,n}}{n!}y^n,
 \qquad b\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
For smooth graphs this is notation for the jet.  Reversing one transverse axis
changes the corresponding odd jets and also changes the recorded endpoint
sign.  Thus the odd coefficients below are invariants of the \emph{registered
oriented} channel, not unoriented Euclidean scalars.

For $b\in\{0,1\}$ use an even number of flights and the orientation whose first
and last impacts have type $b$.  Let
$\Pi^{\mathrm{end},\infty}_{bb,d}$ be the limiting conditional law of the two
signed endpoint coordinates after residual time has been integrated out.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-main-relative}, its density is positive in the interior of
its support and is proportional to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-signed-endpoint-density}
 (d-S_b(u)-S_b(v))_+B_b(u)B_b(v).
\end{equation}

\subsection{The weighted half-line equations}

Choose once and for all a number $\rho\in(0,1)$ satisfying
$e^{-\gamma_-}<\rho<1$ on the compact positive geometric family under
consideration and put
\[
 \mathcal X_\rho=
 \left\{x=(x_i)_{i\ge1}:\|x\|_\rho:=
       \sup_{i\ge1}\rho^{-i}|x_i|<\infty\right\}.
\]
For a starting label $b$, set $x_0=u$ and write the stationary half-line
equations as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-halfline-equations}
 \mathcal F^{(b)}_i(x,u)=
 \partial_2\ell_{(b+i-1)\bmod2}(x_{i-1},x_i)
 +\partial_1\ell_{(b+i)\bmod2}(x_i,x_{i+1})=0,
 \qquad i\ge1.
\end{equation}
At $(x,u)=(0,0)$ the derivative in $x$ is the positive quadratic Jacobi
operator $\mathcal J_b$.  Its inverse is the Green operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-halfline-green}
 (\mathcal J_b^{-1})_{ik}
 =\frac{g\sigma_i^{(b)}\sigma_k^{(b)}}{2c\sinh\gamma}
 \left(e^{-\gamma|i-k|}-e^{-\gamma(i+k)}\right),
 \qquad i,k\ge1,
\end{equation}
from \eqref{eq:v4-half-green}.  In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-green-bound}
 \|\mathcal J_b^{-1}y\|_\rho\le C\|y\|_\rho
\end{equation}
uniformly on compact positive geometric sets, and the same estimate holds after
any fixed number of parameter derivatives after leaving a strict exponential
margin.

\begin{lemma}[All-order weighted differentiation]
\label{lem:v21-all-order-halfline}
On a common contact neighborhood the solution
$x^{(b)}(u)=(x_i^{(b)}(u))_{i\ge0}$ of
\eqref{eq:v21-halfline-equations} is the unique small element of
$\mathcal X_\rho$ with $x_0=u$ and $x_i\to0$.  For every fixed $N$, the jet
\[
 \{\partial_u^k x_i^{(b)}(0):0\le k\le N,\ i\ge0\}
\]
depends only on the leading geometry and the graph jets
$q_{r,n}$ with $3\le n\le N$, and satisfies uniform weighted bounds.
At each differentiation order the highest unknown orbit derivative is
multiplied by the same invertible operator
$D_x\mathcal F^{(b)}(x^{(b)}(u),u)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The contraction proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:v4-halfline} gives the solution and
its weighted decay.  We record the derivative mechanism because it is the
point needed for the all-order inverse.  On a sufficiently small common box,
\[
 D_x\mathcal F^{(b)}(x^{(b)}(u),u)
 =\mathcal J_b+\mathcal R_b(u),
 \qquad
 \|\mathcal J_b^{-1}\mathcal R_b(u)\|_{\mathcal X_\rho\to\mathcal X_\rho}
 \le\frac12.
\]
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-nonlinear-jacobi-inverse}
 [D_x\mathcal F^{(b)}]^{-1}
 =(I+\mathcal J_b^{-1}\mathcal R_b)^{-1}\mathcal J_b^{-1}
\end{equation}
has a uniformly bounded inverse on $\mathcal X_\rho$.

Differentiate \eqref{eq:v21-halfline-equations} $N$ times in $u$.  The only
term containing the $N$th orbit derivative is
$D_x\mathcal F^{(b)}\,\partial_u^N x^{(b)}$.  Every other term is a finite
sum of products of lower orbit derivatives and derivatives of the local flight
functions.  A derivative of the latter of total endpoint degree at most $N$
uses no graph derivative above order $N$.  The local chain rule, bounded ratios
of adjacent weights and \eqref{eq:v21-nonlinear-jacobi-inverse} therefore give
an induction in $N$.  This proves both finite-jet dependence and the uniform
weighted bounds.  No analyticity of the graphs is used.
\end{proof}

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-linear-halfline-orbit}
 t_i^{(b)}=
 \begin{cases}
 e^{-i\gamma},&i\ \text{even},\\
 \mathfrak r_b e^{-i\gamma},&i\ \text{odd},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \mathfrak r_b=\sqrt{c_b/c_{1-b}}.
\end{equation}
Then
$x_i^{(b)}(u)=t_i^{(b)}u+O(u^2\rho^i)$, with all fixed differentiated
versions uniformly summable.

\begin{proposition}[First appearance of an arbitrary graph jet]
\label{prop:v21-first-appearance}
Let
\[
 s_n=(S_0^{(n)}(0),S_1^{(n)}(0))^t,
 \qquad q_n=(q_{0,n},q_{1,n})^t.
\]
For every $n\ge3$, $s_n$ depends only on the leading geometry and
$q_3,\ldots,q_n$, is affine in $q_n$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-general-jet-block}
 D_{q_n}s_n=\mathcal M_n:=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-det-one}
 \det\mathcal M_n=1.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-all-order-halfline} proves the finite-jet dependence.  It
remains to identify exactly the first occurrence of $q_{r,n}$.

Keep the leading geometry and all graph jets below order $n$ fixed and vary
only $q_{r,n}$.  In a one-flight action, the first varying homogeneous term is
\[
 \frac{q_{r,n}}{n!}
 \left(\mathbf 1_{\{\text{left endpoint has type }r\}}y_i^n
      +\mathbf 1_{\{\text{right endpoint has type }r\}}y_{i+1}^n\right).
\]
All other terms involving this variation have endpoint degree at least
$n+1$.  Differentiate the stationary half-line action with respect to
$q_{r,n}$.  The derivative of the stationary orbit makes no first-order
contribution: every interior orbit variation is multiplied by the stationary
Euler--Lagrange equation, and the remote boundary term vanishes by weighted
exponential decay.  This is the infinite-dimensional envelope identity,
justified by first truncating the half-line, differentiating the finite sum,
and using the uniform weighted bounds to pass to the limit.

To extract endpoint degree $n$, the nonlinear orbit can be replaced by its
linear part \eqref{eq:v21-linear-halfline-orbit}.  Indeed the replacement
error is $O(u^{n+1}\rho^i)$ after summation.  A boundary site occurs once in
the half-line action and every interior site twice.  For an orbit beginning at
type $b$, variation of the type-$b$ graph therefore gives
\begin{align}
 \partial_{q_{b,n}}S_b^{(n)}(0)
 &=1+2\sum_{k\ge1}e^{-2nk\gamma}
   =\frac{1+e^{-2n\gamma}}{1-e^{-2n\gamma}}
   =\coth(n\gamma),
                                      \label{eq:v19-own-action-variation}
\end{align}
and variation of the opposite graph gives
\begin{align}
 \partial_{q_{1-b,n}}S_b^{(n)}(0)
 &=2\mathfrak r_b^n\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-n(2k+1)\gamma}
   =\mathfrak r_b^n\csch(n\gamma).
                                      \label{eq:v19-other-action-variation}
\end{align}
The sums are absolutely convergent with every fixed geometric derivative.
Since the first varying term is homogeneous of degree $n$, lower graph jets
and the corresponding nonlinear orbit corrections cannot contaminate these
coefficients.  Reversing the labels gives \eqref{eq:v19-general-jet-block}.
Finally $\mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1=1$, hence
\[
 \det\mathcal M_n
 =\coth^2(n\gamma)-\csch^2(n\gamma)=1.
\]
This proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Differential signed-jet inverse]
\label{prop:v21-tangent-signed-inverse}
Fix $M\ge3$ and the gap $g>0$.  Let
\[
 \vartheta_M=(\kappa_0,\kappa_1,q_{0,3},q_{1,3},\ldots,
 q_{0,M},q_{1,M})
\]
be the finite registered labelled graph-jet coordinate.  On every compact
positive set of such data the map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-action-jet-map}
 \vartheta_M\longmapsto
 (S_0''(0),S_1''(0),S_0^{(3)}(0),S_1^{(3)}(0),\ldots,
 S_0^{(M)}(0),S_1^{(M)}(0))
\end{equation}
has injective differential and a uniformly bounded left inverse.  Equivalently,
there is $C_M$ such that for every tangent vector $h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-tangent-stability}
 \|h\|
 \le C_M\sum_{b=0}^1\|D_\vartheta S_b[ h]\|_{C^M([-u_*,u_*])}.
\end{equation}
In particular, if $D_\vartheta S_b(u)[h]\equiv0$ for both labels near zero,
then $h=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $a_b=S_b''(0)$.  At fixed $g$, the leading recovery formulas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-leading-recovery}
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}\!\bigl(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}\bigr),
 \qquad c=\cosh\gamma,
 \qquad c_0=c\sqrt{a_0/a_1},\quad c_1=c\sqrt{a_1/a_0},
 \qquad \kappa_b=\frac{c_b-1}{g}
\end{equation}
define a smooth inverse from $(a_0,a_1)$ to $(\kappa_0,\kappa_1)$ on every
compact positive set.  Thus vanishing of the first two components of the
differential in \eqref{eq:v21-action-jet-map} forces
$D\kappa_0[h]=D\kappa_1[h]=0$, quantitatively.

Assume inductively that the tangent components through order $n-1$ vanish.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:v21-first-appearance}, differentiation of the degree
$n$ relation gives
\[
 D s_n[h]=\mathcal M_nDq_n[h],
\]
because the derivative of the lower-jet remainder is zero by the induction
hypothesis.  Since $\det\mathcal M_n=1$, $D s_n[h]=0$ implies
$Dq_n[h]=0$.  Moreover $\mathcal M_n^{-1}$ is uniformly bounded on compact
positive sets for every fixed $n$.  Induction through $M$ proves injectivity
and, by composing the finitely many bounded inverse blocks, the estimate
\eqref{eq:v21-tangent-stability}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Recovery from signed support}

\begin{theorem}[Signed-endpoint action and contact rigidity]
\label{thm:v19-signed-rigidity}
Let the two labelled facing contacts be $C^\infty$ and strictly convex.  No
evenness, reflection symmetry, equality of the two contacts, or supplied
contact curvature is assumed.  Suppose the selected physical channel and its
registered oriented signed endpoint coordinates are retained.

There is $d_*>0$ such that the two families
\[
 \{\Pi^{\mathrm{end},\infty}_{00,d}:0<d<d_*\},\qquad
 \{\Pi^{\mathrm{end},\infty}_{11,d}:0<d<d_*\}
\]
determine the two half-line actions $S_0,S_1$ on a fixed contact
neighborhood.  If the physical time origin is retained, the onset times also
determine the gap $g$.  Consequently the gap, both contact curvatures, and
every finite jet of both labelled contact graphs are determined.

More precisely, for every $n\ge3$ the next labelled graph-jet pair is recovered
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-general-recursion}
 q_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&-\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 -\mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix}(s_n-R_n),
\end{equation}
where $R_n$ depends only on the already recovered leading geometry and lower
jets.  The finite-order inverse is locally Lipschitz on compact positive
geometric and finite-jet sets.

For real-analytic contacts, equality of the two signed endpoint-law germs as
$d\downarrow0$ and of the physical onset determines both labelled analytic
contact germs.  If the participating obstacle boundaries are connected and
real analytic, their boundary images are determined in the fixed registered
contact frames.  For a smooth compact family and every fixed $M$, the same
reconstruction from actual finite even-flight endpoint-support families over
$0<d<d_*$ recovers both graph jets through order $M$ with error
$O_M(\tau^j)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $B_b$ is strictly positive on the common endpoint box, the interior of
support in \eqref{eq:v19-signed-endpoint-density} is exactly
\[
 \{(u,v):S_b(u)+S_b(v)<d\}.
\]
Define
\[
 T_b(u,v)=\inf\{d>0:(u,v)\in
       \operatorname{int}\operatorname{supp}
       \Pi^{\mathrm{end},\infty}_{bb,d}\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v19-support-threshold}
 T_b(u,v)=S_b(u)+S_b(v),\qquad S_b(u)=T_b(u,0),
\end{equation}
because $S_b(0)=0$.  Thus the signed support recovers the full action, not only
an even pushforward.

The gap is the physical onset per flight: for the selected itinerary, the
infimum of physical times at which its near-onset event has nonzero probability
is $jg$.  Hence $g$ is recovered from the time axis.  The half-line Hessian
identities give
\[
 a_b=\frac{c\sinh\gamma}{g c_{1-b}},
 \qquad c=\sqrt{c_0c_1}=\cosh\gamma.
\]
Therefore $a_0a_1=\sinh^2\gamma/g^2$ and
$a_0/a_1=c_0/c_1$, which proves \eqref{eq:v19-leading-recovery} and recovers
both curvatures without supplying either one.

Proposition~\ref{prop:v21-first-appearance} now gives the determinant-one
block at every degree.  Since its inverse is displayed in
\eqref{eq:v19-general-recursion}, induction recovers every finite labelled
jet.  Smooth dependence of all coefficients and the finite product of the
bounded inverse blocks give the local Lipschitz estimate on compact sets.

For analytic contacts, equality of all recovered signed jets gives equality of
the convergent graph germs.  Analytic continuation along a connected analytic
boundary and uniqueness for the planar Frenet equations then determine the
participating boundary image in the registered frame.

For an actual finite even-flight law the positive physical flux implies that
its endpoint support threshold is the exact stationary excess action
$E_{j,b}(u,v)$.  The relative estimate \eqref{eq:v8-relative} gives
\[
 \|E_{j,b}-(S_b\oplus S_b)\|_{C^M}\le C_M\tau^j.
\]
Taking $v=0$ and applying the finite-order locally Lipschitz inverse proves the
$O_M(\tau^j)$ reconstruction claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite registered channel network]
\label{cor:v21-channel-network}
Let a finite collection of registered signed channels be given in one common
Euclidean laboratory frame.  The signed endpoint-law germs and physical onset
for each channel determine every contact germ incident to the collection,
with the contact label retained.  If the participating obstacle boundaries are
connected and real analytic, and the channel collection meets every obstacle
to be reconstructed, the corresponding labelled boundary images are
determined in that common frame.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-signed-rigidity} to every channel.  Whenever two
channels meet the same labelled analytic boundary, each recovered local germ
continues uniquely along that connected boundary.  The common registration
identifies the continued images in one Euclidean frame.  No conclusion is
asserted for obstacles not incident to the chosen channel collection.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the scalar profile remains useful]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-signed-rigidity} and the scalar profile inverse concern
different coarsenings of the same physical record.  Integrating the signed
endpoints produces a symmetrized profile and necessarily loses the odd branch
displacement.  Retaining the endpoint signs recovers the unsymmetrized action
support and hence the full labelled local jets.  The difference is an
observation-theoretic loss, not a hidden reflection symmetry of the table.
\end{remark}
