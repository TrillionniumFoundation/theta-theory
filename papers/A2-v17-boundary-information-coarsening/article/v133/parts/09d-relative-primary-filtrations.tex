% Finite exact diagrams and geometric primary signatures.
\subsubsection{Finite diagrams and primary models}

We give an algebraic construction which distinguishes persistence of an
associated point from exhaustion of the associated set.  Every restriction
to a base stratum in this construction is followed by formation of the
indicated kernels on that stratum.  We do not identify these kernels with
the pullback of a kernel from an earlier, unflattened stratum.

\begin{lemma}[Spreading a finite exact diagram]\label{lem:finite-exact-diagram}
Let \(D\) be a noetherian domain with fraction field \(K\), let \(A\)
be a finite type \(D\)-algebra, and fix a finite diagram of finite
\(A_K\)-modules and maps, including finitely many short exact sequences.
After replacing \(D\) by \(D_g\), the diagram has coherent models for
which all the specified short exact sequences remain exact after every
base change over \(D_g\).  The same assertion holds for a diagram over
a finite extension of \(K\), after a finite faithfully flat cover of a
nonempty open of \(\Spec D\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose presentations, maps, and the finitely many identities expressing
commutativity.  Clear their denominators.  Kernels and homology modules
are finite over \(A\).  A finite module which vanishes after tensoring
with \(K\) is killed by a nonzero element of \(D\), so a further
localization makes the specified sequences exact.  Apply generic
freeness to their finitely many terms and cokernels
\cite[Tag 051S]{StacksGenericFree}.  In a short exact sequence with
flat cokernel, tensoring with any base algebra preserves the injection;
right exactness preserves the remaining terms.  This proves the claim
without any four-term kernel inference.

For a finite field extension \(L/K\), choose finitely many generators
of \(L\), clear denominators in their monic equations, and let \(D'\)
be the finite \(D_g\)-algebra which they generate.  After another
localization, \(D'\) is finite free of positive rank over \(D_g\).
It is therefore faithfully flat.  Apply the preceding construction over
\(D'\).  A further shrinking upstairs descends to a shrinking downstairs:
the image of its closed complement is closed under the finite morphism
and misses the generic point.  No separability is needed for this step.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Primary quotients on a splitting cover]
\label{prop:relative-primary-models}
Let \(A\) be a finite type algebra over a noetherian domain \(D\), and
let \(M\) be a finite \(A\)-module.  There is a nonempty open of
\(\Spec D\) and a finite faithfully flat cover of that open on which
there exist ideals \(\mathfrak p_i\), modules \(Q_i\), and maps
\[
 M\longrightarrow Q_i,\qquad
 M\longrightarrow\bigoplus_i Q_i,\qquad
 A/\mathfrak p_i\longrightarrow M
\]
with the following properties.  On every geometric fibre the first
maps are surjective, the last two displayed maps are injective, the
rings \(A/\mathfrak p_i\) are nonzero integral domains, and each
\(Q_i\) is a nonzero \(\mathfrak p_i\)-primary module.  The primes
may be taken distinct on every geometric fibre.  Consequently
\[
 \Ass(M_{\bar s})=\{\mathfrak p_{i,\bar s}\}_i,
 \qquad
 0=\bigcap_i\ker(M_{\bar s}\longrightarrow Q_{i,\bar s})
\]
is an irredundant primary decomposition.  All exact diagrams used to
construct these maps are preserved by arbitrary base change on the
chosen cover.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the geometric generic fibre choose an irredundant primary
 decomposition \(0=\bigcap_i N_i\), and put \(Q_i=M/N_i\), with
\(\Ass Q_i=\{\mathfrak p_i\}\).  Choose also an associated element
for each \(\mathfrak p_i\); it gives an injection
\(A/\mathfrak p_i\hookrightarrow M\).  The diagonal map
\(M\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_i Q_i\) is injective.

We must spread the assertion that a quotient is primary, not merely its
support.  Choose \(n_i\) with \(\mathfrak p_i^{n_i}Q_i=0\), and set
\[
 F_{i,j}=(0:_{Q_i}\mathfrak p_i^j),\quad 0\le j\le n_i,
 \qquad E_{i,j}=F_{i,j}/F_{i,j-1}.
\]
Each \(E_{i,j}\) is killed by \(\mathfrak p_i\) and is torsion-free
over the domain \(A/\mathfrak p_i\).  Indeed, if
\(h\notin\mathfrak p_i\) and \(hv\in F_{i,j-1}\), then
\(h\mathfrak p_i^{j-1}v=0\); multiplication by \(h\) is injective
on the primary module \(Q_i\), so \(v\in F_{i,j-1}\).  Clearing
fraction-field denominators now gives injections
\[
 E_{i,j}\hookrightarrow (A/\mathfrak p_i)^{r_{i,j}}.
\]
These injections, the filtration sequences, the diagonal map, the
quotient maps, and the associated-element injections form a finite
diagram.  All its coefficients descend to a finite extension of the
generic field.  The prime quotients can be descended at the same time
with geometrically integral generic fibres.  After shrinking, their
geometric fibres are integral and nonempty: apply generic flatness,
spreading of geometric reducedness and irreducibility, and the fact
that the image of a finite-presentation morphism containing the generic
point contains a nonempty open
\cite[Sections 37.26--37.27]{StacksGeometricFibres}.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-exact-diagram} to the whole finite diagram.

In a geometric fibre, every \(E_{i,j}\) embeds in a free module over
an integral prime quotient.  It therefore has no associated prime
other than \(\mathfrak p_i\).  The filtration gives
\(\Ass Q_i\subset\{\mathfrak p_i\}\); the surviving quotient map
and associated-element data imply nonvanishing whenever that index
occurs.  More explicitly, the diagonal injection gives the inclusion
\[
 \Ass M_{\bar s}\subset\bigcup_i\Ass Q_{i,\bar s}
 \subset\{\mathfrak p_{i,\bar s}\}_i.
\]
The associated-element injections give the reverse inclusion.  This
is the exhaustion argument: an additional embedded prime anywhere in
the fibre would contradict the diagonal injection into these filtered
primary modules.  It is not an open-neighbourhood containment argument.

For every generic noncontainment \(\mathfrak p_i\not\subset
\mathfrak p_j\), choose \(h_{ij}\in\mathfrak p_i\setminus\mathfrak p_j\).
Include multiplication by \(h_{ij}\) on \(A/\mathfrak p_j\) and its
cokernel in the finite diagram.  Fibrewise injectivity preserves that
noncontainment.  Generic containments spread by finite generation.
Thus distinctness and the containment poset persist.  The kernels of
the quotient maps have zero intersection by the diagonal injection.
No component can be omitted: otherwise the associated-prime inclusion
for the remaining diagonal injection would exclude its distinct
associated prime.  This proves irredundance as well as primaryness.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{A relative module for the local torsion length}

\begin{proposition}[No new packet torsion after refinement]
\label{prop:no-new-packet-torsion}
In the situation of Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-primary-models}, fix
a tracked prime \(\mathfrak p\).  After shrinking the base there are
an integer \(n\), a coherent submodule \(T\subset M\), and an element
\(h\in\mathfrak p\) such that, after every base change on this open,
\[
 T=(0:_M\mathfrak p^n)=H^0_{\mathfrak p}(M),
 \qquad h:M/T\longrightarrow M/T\ \text{is injective}.
\]
At each generic point \(p\) of a geometric packet fibre,
\[
 T_p=H^0_{p\mathcal O_p}(M_p).
\]
There are nonnegative integers \(r_0,\ldots,r_{n-1}\), independent
of that geometric fibre, for which
\[
 \length_{\mathcal O_p}H^0_{p\mathcal O_p}(M_p)
 =\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}r_j.
\]
Here \(H^0_I\) denotes elements killed by some power of the ideal
\(I\); the notation does not denote a sheaf cohomology group.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the split generic fibre the annihilator chain stabilizes.  Choose
\(n\) with \(T=(0:_M\mathfrak p^n)=H^0_{\mathfrak p}(M)\), and put
\(Q=M/T\).  This quotient has no \(\mathfrak p\)-power torsion.
Equivalently, no associated prime of \(Q\) contains
\(\mathfrak p\).  Prime avoidance therefore supplies
\(h\in\mathfrak p\) outside all the associated primes of \(Q\).
Multiplication by \(h\) is injective on \(Q\).  When \(Q=0\) this
assertion is vacuous and no choice is needed.

Spread the sequence \(0\to T\to M\to Q\to0\), the relation
\(\mathfrak p^nT=0\), and the injection \(h:Q\to Q\).  Flatten
\(Q\) and the cokernel of this injection, together with the other
finite diagram terms.  They remain exact after arbitrary base change.
An element of the base-changed \(M\) killed by \(\mathfrak p^a\)
has image in \(Q\) killed by \(h^a\).  The image is zero, so the
element belongs to \(T\).  The converse follows from
\(\mathfrak p^nT=0\).  This proves the asserted equality for the
entire relative torsion module, not only for a submodule chosen at the
generic point.  Localization gives the displayed local equality.

Consider the finite filtration
\[
 T\supset\mathfrak pT\supset\cdots\supset\mathfrak p^nT=0,
 \qquad G_j=\mathfrak p^jT/\mathfrak p^{j+1}T.
\]
Include its exact sequences and the quotients \(T/\mathfrak p^jT\)
in the finite diagram.  Their flatness makes this the same power
filtration on every fibre.  On the generic integral packet each
\(G_j\) becomes free of rank \(r_j\) after inverting an element
\(g_j\notin\mathfrak p\).  Spread such a free presentation and its
inverse.  Include multiplication by \(g_j\) on \(A/\mathfrak p\)
and its cokernel in the diagram.  It remains injective, so \(g_j\)
is nonzero at the generic point of every geometric packet fibre.
Thus \((G_j)_p\) is an \(r_j\)-dimensional vector space over the
residue field at \(p\).  Additivity of length gives the sum.  All
these operations were performed over the splitting cover, hence also
after geometric field extension.  In particular, inseparable residue
field extension is not silently treated as an ordinary rational point.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite geometric-assassin stratification]
\label{lem:finite-geometric-assassin-stratification}
Let \(X\to S\) be of finite presentation with \(S\) noetherian,
and let \(\mathcal F\) be a finitely presented coherent module flat
over \(S\).  There is a finite locally closed stratification with
finitely many closed packets \(Z_\nu\) on each stratum such that
\[
 \Ass(\mathcal F_{\bar s})
 =\bigcup_\nu\{\text{generic points of the irreducible components
 of }(Z_\nu)_{\bar s}\}
\]
for every geometric fibre.  The packets may split geometrically.
After further finite refinement they are flat, and their dimensions,
incidences, minimal-versus-embedded status, and local torsion lengths
at associated generic points are constant.  On finite splitting covers
of affine charts the packets are realized by the actual primary
quotients of Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-primary-models}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first construct the required data on a dense open of an integral
base.  Choose a finite affine cover of \(X\).  Apply
Propositions~\ref{prop:relative-primary-models} and
\ref{prop:no-new-packet-torsion} on these charts, using one common
finite extension of the generic field and one common shrinking.
Associated points are local for an affine open cover.  Consequently
every fibrewise associated point is one of the primes in those finite
primary models.  This proves exhaustion on the whole fibre.

Take the schematic closures on the original base of the contractions
of the tracked geometric generic points.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:power-certified-packets} to each conjugacy orbit, with
one common splitting cover and refinement.  It constructs the ideal
of each packet downstairs and proves exact equality of its geometric
component set using the power sandwich and a base-change-exact
diagonal sequence.  In characteristic zero, the stronger reduced
orbit-ideal construction of Lemma~\ref{lem:galois-packet-descent}
gives the explicit fpqc datum and its cocycle.  On a geometric lift
over the same algebraically closed field, the already proved
associated-point equality upstairs is an equality for the original
fibre.  Thus the generic points of these descended packets are
exactly the associated points, in both directions.  The overlap
construction in Lemma~\ref{lem:power-certified-packets} proves the
same statement across the affine charts.

The noncontainment witnesses in
Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-primary-models} fix the containment
poset, hence minimal and embedded status, on the splitting cover.
Conjugate generic points have equal dimensions and torsion lengths;
these equalities persist by Proposition~\ref{prop:no-new-packet-torsion}
and give the downstairs lengths by
Proposition~\ref{prop:descended-packet-torsion}.  That proposition
also constructs the coherent relative torsion module on the original
base, not just on its cover.  Generic flatness for the finitely many
packet closures fixes their fibre dimensions.  For intersections,
apply the same finite-presentation dimension and image stratifications
to the finitely many intersections of packet closures.  This fixes
nonemptiness and dimensions of their incidences as well.  Finally
flatten their structure sheaves.

There are finitely many irreducible components of the reduced base.
Carry out the preceding construction on dense opens of those
components, remove these opens, and repeat on the proper closed
complement, with the modules and kernels re-formed there.  Noetherian
induction gives a finite stratification.  Nilpotents in the base do
not alter its geometric fibres; reduced locally closed strata suffice.
The exact-diagram constructions hold after every base change within
a final stratum.  They make no assertion that a kernel or a torsion
module commutes with a specialization crossing two different strata.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why a torsion construction is necessary]
Over \(D=\C[t]\) the module
\[
 M=D[x,y]/(x^2,x(y^2-t))
 =D[y]\oplus xD[y]/(y^2-t)
\]
is flat.  The second summand is its finite torsion contribution along
\((x,y^2-t)\).  For \(t\ne0\) it splits into two geometric embedded
points of length one; at \(t=0\) they form one embedded point of
length two.  The quotient \(D[y]\) is protected from new torsion by
multiplication by \(y^2-t\).  Flatness of the total module alone
would neither identify this torsion summand nor keep the length of an
individual geometric component constant across the collision.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Grading and the finite family of multiplication maps}

\begin{lemma}[Graded consequences of a finite flat diagram]
\label{lem:graded-flat-diagram}
Let \(A=D[x_1,\ldots,x_m]\) have its standard grading, and let
\(L\) be a finite graded \(A\)-module flat over a noetherian ring
\(D\).  Every \(L_n\) is a finite locally free \(D\)-module.  On
a connected base its Hilbert function, and therefore its Hilbert
polynomial, is constant.  A prescribed finite family of homogeneous
\(A\)-linear maps whose sources, targets, images, and cokernels are
flat has base-change-compatible, constant rank in every degree on
such a base.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every graded component is a direct summand of the underlying flat
\(D\)-module and is finite over \(D\).  It is thus finite flat,
hence locally free.  Its rank is locally constant and is constant on
a connected base.  This applies simultaneously to all degrees because
flatness of the whole graded module has already been obtained; it does
not require an infinite sequence of independent refinements.  Apply
the same observation to the image and cokernel of each map, and
base-change the two short exact sequences defining its image.
\end{proof}
