\begin{abstract}
A full normal second jet of a squared-distance function determines a compression of the inverse information matrix. For a native Hankel family, the exact ambiguity is the annihilator of products of normal polynomials. We determine the global codimension of failure for $L$ independently designed $c$-dimensional normal spaces in a $k$-dimensional polynomial space: when $4\le c<k$ and $L\binom{c+1}{2}\ge2k-1$, it is
\[
 \min\{L\tbinom{c+1}{2}-2k+2,\ Lc-3\}.
\]
A common-secant incidence attains the second bound and is a maximal-dimensional component when that bound is smaller. The proof stratifies annihilating Hankel forms by rank and counts their isotropic Grassmannians. It also gives the exact generic number of independently designed contacts. A fixed-space realization criterion separates positivity from a finite coordinate-decomposition obstruction to global fibre separation. These results yield the sharp single-contact threshold $k=d+\lceil(3+\sqrt{16d+1})/2\rceil$ for synchronized quadratic images. We identify the physical cone experiment at coalesced roots, and construct contact-score statistics directly from observed counts, with an exact formula for their local information loss. Quantitative inversion, finite-sample estimation, and complementary contact and design results are included.
\end{abstract}
