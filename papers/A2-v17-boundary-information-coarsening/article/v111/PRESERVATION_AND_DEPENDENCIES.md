## External inputs and internally proved steps

The global codimension theorem uses the classical projective dimensions of the entire Hankel rank loci (Conca–Mostafazadehfard–Singh–Varbaro, Sections 1–2, arXiv:1709.05685) and the classical maximal-minor height bound (recalled by Eisenbud–Huneke–Ulrich, Introduction, arXiv:math/0209184). It proves the isotropic incidence count, optimization over all feasible ranks, matching common-secant construction and generic finiteness of its projection. It does not depend on rational-curve maximal rank for the saturated conclusion. The classical injective-range comparison is retained with exact hypotheses in Appendix A, using Larson, arXiv:1711.04906, Theorem 1.2.

The fixed-space realization proof adds the exact coordinate-decomposition obstruction to positivity. The contact-score experiment comparison proves a jittered Poisson total-variation approximation, explicit sufficient-statistic kernels and a Gaussian information projector identity. Standard asymptotic and constrained-estimation theory is attributed; the theorem is restricted to the specified known-mark-law least-favourable local target submodel.

## Boundaries that must survive further revision

Complex codimension is not automatically the real codimension of every stratum. The real native common-secant family is proved separately. Independently designed contacts are not arbitrary points on one fixed loading. No complete classification of all failure components, global scheme reducedness, random condition-number tail or dimension-uniform stability constant is asserted. The new statistical theorem is not a global equivalence with jointly unknown marks and exposures, nor an independent physical distance-measurement model. Auxiliary jitter values are not retained. Computed contact features remain a re-encoding theorem. The boundary Gaussian vector is not an unconstrained physical estimator. No sign-identifiability claim is made for root amplitudes.
