\begin{abstract}
We study the spectrum of a polynomial family observed through stochastic
channels and an unknown scalar normalization. At every spectrally
identifiable datum of the closed model, finitely many cluster-coefficient
envelopes determine the exact local observation exponent. A joint
rescaled coefficient fibre determines its leading root-multiset diameter.
This construction is intrinsic to the full admissible observation germ;
it permits singular channels and does not replace nonlinear constraints
by a tangent space. On immersed analytic charts it reduces to a Newton
formula for the determinant composed with all model jets. A rank-one
channel example with simultaneous double and triple component roots has
a singleton exact spectral fibre and a square-root intrinsic modulus.
The same exponent determines the squared minimax risk on shrinking
observation neighbourhoods. The local theory is complemented by global
additive normalization bounds, regular quotient geometry, and
boundary-uniform matrix-polynomial estimates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An observation can be singular without its spectral target being
unidentified. The distinction becomes essential when stochastic
factorizations, scalar normalization, and repeated roots interact.
The relevant local object is not a chosen coefficient tangent space.
It is the joint image of the actual observation and spectral maps,
with all admissibility constraints retained.

The main result, Theorem~\ref{thm:germ-newton-v94}, associates an
exact Newton law to this image germ. After factoring the spectral
polynomial into its separated base clusters, one forms the envelopes
of its finitely many cluster coefficients on a Hellinger ball. Their
orders, divided by the corresponding root weights, give the spectral
exponent. Their joint weighted limit gives the exact leading diameter.
This formulation distinguishes the information carried by separate
coefficient orders from the dependence needed for a sharp constant.
It also remains meaningful at a rank-loss point, where taking the
determinant of the observed matrix would give the zero polynomial.

On an immersed analytic chart, the envelopes reduce to homogeneous
jets. The determinant must first be composed with the full coefficient
map. Theorem~\ref{thm:composed-jets-v94} gives the resulting formula
and a finite cutoff once a nonzero edge is known. Curved positive
rational examples in Proposition~\ref{prop:curved-cancellation-v94}
have the same affine tangent but different exact spectral orders.
They isolate the reason that a first-order admissible slice cannot
classify a nonlinear singularity.

The stochastic application is not a perturbation declared admissible
only in an enlarged rational experiment. In
Theorem~\ref{thm:stochastic-intersection-v94}, one channel has rank
one, two component roots coincide, and three others coincide. An
isolated intersection of an affine cubic pencil with the real-rooted
interval constraint makes the exact spectral fibre a singleton. A
whole-model estimate and actual stochastic alternatives prove a
square-root modulus. The leading constant is then supplied by the
same intrinsic Newton law. The example also has a noninjective
ambient coefficient Jacobian, so a regular Fisher inverse cannot be
substituted for the admissible-germ calculation.

The classical ingredients include semialgebraic elimination and
Puiseux preparation \cite{BCR98,BPR06}, rational interpolation and
realization \cite{VBB,AA90,BL00,MA07}, and matrix-polynomial root
perturbation \cite{GLR82,MBO97,TH,KPM09}. We do not claim these
mechanisms as new. The results specify their composition in the
probability-normalized experiment, including its leading metric
object, its closed stochastic constraints, and its statistical scale.
The passage from inverse moduli to inference belongs to optimal
recovery and honest confidence theory \cite{Donoho,CaiLow,Dufour}.
Latent factor identification and singular rates have established
precedents \cite{AMR,BJR,BCV,LLB,HN}; our target is the aggregate
unordered spectrum, not a globally stable component labelling.

A separate issue is uniformity as identification deteriorates. The
additive normalization theorem below controls a moving class with
specified uniform constants. The germ theorem gives an exact law at
a fixed datum. These are different quantifiers: the pointwise
constant is not declared uniform as a cell floor, $\tau$, or a rank
margin tends to zero. The full global, quotient, inference, and
common-flag arguments are retained in the appendices. The main text
is organized around the single observation-germ principle and its
stochastic application.

\section{The polynomial experiment and its additive inverse bound}
\label{sec:model-v94}
Fix $d\ge1$, $2\le k\le\min(m_1,m_2)$, an interval $[L,D]$,
distinct clocks $D<T_1<\cdots<T_\ell$ with $\ell\ge d+1$,
and $0<\alpha_*<1/k$. The columns of
$U\in\R^{m_1\times k}$ and $V\in\R^{m_2\times k}$ are probability
vectors. Let $\alpha_b\ge\alpha_*$ and $\sum_b\alpha_b=1$. Put
\begin{align}
 f_b(z)&=\prod_{a=1}^d(z-\lambda_{ba}),\quad \lambda_{ba}\in[L,D],
 &q(z)&=\sum_b\alpha_b f_b(z),\nonumber\\
 K(z)&=U\diag(\alpha_b f_b(z))V^{\mathsf T},
 &P(T)&=K(T)/q(T).\label{eq:model}
\end{align}
The closed parameter set $\cK_d$ is compact; singular channels,
boundary roots, and repeated roots are included. Every $q(T_j)$
and every component probability $\alpha_bf_b(T_j)/q(T_j)$ has
uniform positive bounds. A common positive scalar detector disappears
on normalization; a component-dependent detector defines a different
experiment. Write $F(\theta)=(\operatorname{vec}P_\theta(T_j))_j$.
The target is the unordered multiset $\Lambda$ of all $kd$ roots,
with no component partition in the output. Set
\[
 d_\infty(\Lambda,\Lambda')=\min_\pi\max_a
          |\lambda_a-\lambda'_{\pi(a)}|,
 \qquad \delta=\max_j\|P(T_j)-P'(T_j)\|_F.
\]
For real multisets this is the maximum discrepancy of increasing
orders. Unless otherwise stated, uniform constants depend only on
the displayed experiment constants. Internal separation is charged
only when specified. Proposition~\ref{prop:explicit} gives explicit
degree and clock dependence without claiming stable monomial
interpolation in growing degree.

Let $W=(T_j^r)_{j=1,\ldots,\ell;r=0,\ldots,d}$ and let $\Omega$
project orthogonally onto $\operatorname{col}(W)^\perp$.
Write $q(z)=z^d+\sum_{r<d}a_rz^r$. For $c\in\R^d$, put
$r_c(z)=\sum_{r<d}c_rz^r$ and define
\begin{align}
 \cN_Pc&=(\Omega\otimes I)
              (r_c(T_j)\operatorname{vec}P(T_j))_j,
 &\tau(P)&=\sigma_{\min}(\cN_P),\nonumber\\
 b_P&=-(\Omega\otimes I)
              (T_j^d\operatorname{vec}P(T_j))_j,
 &\kappa&=\sigma_k(U)\sigma_k(V).\label{eq:normalization}
\end{align}
Here $I$ has size $m_1m_2$, and the norms are Euclidean coefficient
and product Frobenius norms. The equation is $\cN_Pa=b_P$.
Thus $\tau$ is observable; $\kappa$ is a representation quantity.

\begin{theorem}[Normalization, clock design, and spectral recovery]
\label{thm:main}
Suppose the true channels have full column rank. If $\ell\ge2d+1$
and $g=\gcd(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$ is monic, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 \ker\cN_P=\{(q/g)s:\deg s<\deg g\},\qquad
 \dim\ker\cN_P=\deg g.
\end{equation}
For any design with $\ell\ge d+1$ and $\tau(P)>0$, every competitor
in $\cK_d$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 \|a-a'\|_2&\le C\min\{1,\delta/\tau(P)\},\label{eq:q-bound}\\
 d_\infty(\Lambda,\Lambda')&\le
 C\min\{1,[\delta(\kappa^{-1}+\tau(P)^{-1})]^{1/d}\}.
 \label{eq:poly-bound}
\end{align}
For simple roots within each true component, separated by $\nu>0$,
the spectral exponent is one, with the constant also depending on
$\nu$. Cross-component collisions are allowed. No rank or separation
hypothesis is imposed on a competitor.

At an interior parameter with internally simple roots, $\tau(P)=0$
gives a nonconstant local exact fibre with fixed channels and changing
aggregate roots. For every fixed admissible $k$, relatively prime
interior examples fail identification at $2d$ clocks. This worst-case
statement is realized by an affine-line matrix curve. If the component
polynomials span all polynomials of degree at most $d$, then $d+2$
clocks suffice and $d+1$ do not.
\end{theorem}
The proof, including the clock-complexity alternatives, is retained
in the appendices. To state its observable strengthening, reduce
$K$ in orthonormal frames of its rank-$k$ row and column spaces,
write $M(z)=\sum_{j=0}^dM_jz^j$, and put $A_0=M_d$.
For each distinct complete component polynomial $f$, let $E_f$ be
the joint spectral projector of the commuting $M_jA_0^{-1}$, and set
\[
 B_d(P)=\sum_f\|A_0^{-1}E_f\|_\op,
 \qquad \eta_d(P)=(B_d(P)+\tau(P)^{-1})^{-1}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:joint} proves observability, frame independence, and
$d_\infty(\Lambda,\Lambda')\le
C\min\{1,[\delta/\eta_d(P)]^{1/d}\}$.
Separated clusters of at most $m$ roots per component have exponent
$1/m$. The two losses add; they are not multiplied.

In degree one, $f_b=z-\xi_b$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine}
 h=\sum_b\alpha_b\xi_b,\quad
 A=U\diag(\alpha)V^{\mathsf T},\quad
 C=U\diag(\alpha_b\xi_b)V^{\mathsf T},\quad B=hA-C.
\end{equation}
Then $P(T)=A+B/(T-h)$, $K(z)=zA-C$, and at three clocks
$\tau(P)\asymp\gamma(P)=\|B\|_F$.
The affine condition is $\sig=\kappa\gamma/(\kappa+\gamma)$,
zero on either boundary, with exact-fibre envelope
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 \sig_*(P)=\max\{\sig(\omega):\mathcal P(\omega)=P\}.
\end{equation}
The residue condition is developed in Appendix~\ref{sec:residue};
$B_1$ is its residue sum and $\eta_1\asymp\eta$.
Appendix~\ref{sec:algorithm} gives the least-squares and polynomial-root
estimator without a supplied signal floor. This is exact-arithmetic
stability, not a bit-complexity or confidence-optimization assertion.
