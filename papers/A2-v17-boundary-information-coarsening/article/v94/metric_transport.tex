\section{Coordinate transport of the observation metric}
\label{sec:metric-transport-v94}
Algebraic covariance and statistical isometry are different statements.
Throughout the rational-coordinate results in Appendix~\ref{sec:constrained-newton},
including Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-newton}, a change of numerator
coordinates transports the observation metric as specified here.
It never declares the transformed matrix entries to be new probability
cells. This convention is part of the hypotheses of the invariance
assertion, including its leading diameter constant.

\begin{proposition}[Left--right coordinates and the metric]
\label{prop:metric-transport-v94}
Let $T(X)=AXB$ for fixed invertible real matrices $A,B$, and let
$\mathfrak l$ be the original scalar normalization functional. Set
$\widetilde{\mathfrak l}=\mathfrak l\circ T^{-1}$ and
$\widetilde M=T(M)$. Then the determinant root multiset and its
algebraic Newton orders are preserved. The exact observation moduli,
the Fisher ellipsoids, and their leading diameter constants are
preserved when the transformed observations are equipped with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transported-metric-v94}
 \widetilde h(Q,Q')=h_w(T^{-1}Q,T^{-1}Q').
\end{equation}
At one clock the vectorized map is $\mathsf T_0=B^{\mathsf T}\otimes A$;
on the stacked clock arrays it is $\mathsf T=I_\ell\otimes\mathsf T_0$.
If $H$ is the Fisher form on those arrays, the transported form is
\[
 \widetilde H=\mathsf T^{-\mathsf T}H\mathsf T^{-1}.
\]
Without this transport, a general invertible left--right map is not
asserted to preserve an entrywise Hellinger metric or even the
probability simplex.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The denominator is unchanged because
$\widetilde{\mathfrak l}(\widetilde M)=\mathfrak l(M)$. Thus transformed rational
observations are $T(P)$, and applying $T^{-1}$ recovers the original
statistical experiment exactly. Equation~\eqref{eq:transported-metric-v94}
therefore preserves every observation ball, not only its tangent
approximation. The determinant is multiplied by the constant
$\det A\det B\ne0$, which does not change any roots or any
nonzero Newton order. On differentiating, $\widetilde J=\mathsf TJ$
and
$\widetilde J^{\mathsf T}\widetilde H\widetilde J=J^{\mathsf T}HJ$.
This proves the assertion about ellipsoids and constants as well.
For rectangular arrays the same proof applies to the observation
coordinates wherever the invertible left--right map is defined.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scalar interpolation notation.}
The operator denoted by $\mathcal H_eM$ in the retained affine proof
is more explicitly
$\mathcal T_P(e)=I_d((e(T_j)P_j)_j)$; it is an interpolation
operator, not multiplication by a polynomial $\mathcal H_e$.
The scalar shear is $E=\mathcal T_P(e)+H$, with
$\one^{\mathsf T}H\one=0$. It transports the complete numerator
map and the same observation metric. Applied to a nonlinear germ,
it must be applied to all of that germ's jets. Theorem~\ref{thm:germ-newton-v94}
then gives germ invariance; mere basis invariance of an affine
slice is its strictly narrower special case.

\paragraph{Explicit local convention for the affine appendix.}
In the definition of $\omega_P^{\mathcal S}$ in
Appendix~\ref{sec:constrained-newton}, fix $\rho>0$ such that the
rational observation map is an embedding on a neighbourhood of
$\{x:\|x\|\le\rho\}$, and use exactly
\[
 \{x:\|x\|\le\rho,\quad h_w(F_{\mathcal S}(x),P)\le t\}.
\]
All determinant-root multisets there are in $\C$ with the metric
stated in Section~\ref{sec:germ-newton-v94}. The radius is fixed
before $t$ tends to zero. The algebraic rigidity alternative follows
from the following elementary fact: a degree-$n$ polynomial with
nonzero leading coefficient which retains every base root with its
full multiplicity is a nonzero scalar multiple of the base polynomial.
These conventions also specify the empty-edge case of the affine theorem.
