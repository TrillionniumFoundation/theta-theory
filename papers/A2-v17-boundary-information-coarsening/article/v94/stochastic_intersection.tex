\section{An identifiable rank-loss intersection in the stochastic model}
\label{sec:stochastic-intersection-v94}
The full-collapse binary fibre is not an identifiable singularity.
We give instead a closed-model datum at which one channel loses rank,
one component has a double root, another has a triple root, and the
spectral image of the exact fibre is a singleton. All probability
cells, channel entries, and weight inequalities are strict. The root
constraint has one active endpoint. The upper bound below is over the
whole closed model, not over the displayed family alone.

Take $k=m_1=m_2=2$, $d=3$, $[L,D]=[0,1]$, and at least seven
distinct clocks greater than one. Fix $0<r<1$, put $s=2r/3$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic-pair-v94}
 f(z)=z(z-r)^2,\qquad g(z)=(z-s)^3.
\end{equation}
Let $u$ be a strictly positive probability vector, let
$U=(u,u)$, and let $V=(v_1,v_2)$ be a strictly positive invertible
column-stochastic matrix. Choose
$\alpha\in(\alpha_*,1-\alpha_*)$ and define
\[
 K(z)=u\{\alpha f(z)v_1^{\mathsf T}
                    +(1-\alpha)g(z)v_2^{\mathsf T}\},
 \qquad q(z)=\alpha f(z)+(1-\alpha)g(z).
\]
Denote the resulting observation by $P_*$.

\begin{lemma}[An isolated real-rooted pencil]\label{lem:cubic-pencil-v94}
For the monic affine pencil $h_c=(1-c)f+cg$, the only polynomials
whose three roots all belong to $[0,1]$ are $h_0=f$ and $h_1=g$.
Furthermore, on every bounded $c$-range there are constants $C$ and
$\epsilon_0>0$ such that, if a monic polynomial $p$ has all roots
in $[0,1]$ and $\|p-h_c\|_{\rm coef}\le\epsilon<\epsilon_0$, then
\[
 \min\{|c|,|1-c|\}\le C\epsilon.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All polynomials $h_c$ have root sum $2r$. Their exact identities are
\begin{align}
 h_c(0)&=-\frac{8r^3}{27}c,\nonumber\\
 h_c(s+y)&=y^3-\frac{r^2}{3}(1-c)y
                         +\frac{2r^3}{27}(1-c),\label{eq:cubic-identities-v94}\\
 \operatorname{disc}(h_c)&=-\frac{4r^6}{27}c(1-c)^2.\nonumber
\end{align}
Nonnegative real roots imply $h_c(0)\le0$, hence $c\ge0$.
Their centered sum of squares is $2r^2(1-c)/3$, hence $c\le1$.
A real-rooted cubic has nonnegative discriminant, which on $[0,1]$
forces $c=0$ or $c=1$.

For the quantitative assertion, compactness first places $c$ in
arbitrarily small neighbourhoods of zero and one as $\epsilon$
tends to zero. Near zero, $p(0)\le0$ and the first identity give
$c\ge-C\epsilon$. The discriminant is locally Lipschitz in bounded
monic coefficients; its nonnegativity and the third identity give
$c\le C\epsilon$ there.

Near one put $t=1-c$ and depress the actual polynomial $p$ by its
own root mean. Since that mean is $s+O(\epsilon)$, its depressed
coefficients $a,b$ satisfy
\[
 a=-\frac{r^2}{3}t+O(\epsilon),\qquad
 b=\frac{2r^3}{27}t+O(\epsilon).
\]
Real-rootedness gives $a\le0$ and
$|b|\le 2(-a)^{3/2}/(3\sqrt3)$. Consequently
\[
 |t|\le C\epsilon+C(|t|+\epsilon)^{3/2}.
\]
In a sufficiently small fixed neighbourhood the last term involving
$|t|$ is absorbed in the left side, proving $|t|\le C\epsilon$.
This argument is important: a Lipschitz bound for the discriminant
alone would only give $|1-c|=O(\sqrt\epsilon)$ at its double zero.
The real-rooted cubic inequality supplies the sharper estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rank loss, multiple roots, and a vanishing intrinsic modulus]
\label{thm:stochastic-intersection-v94}
At $P_*$, all observed cells are positive, $\tau(P_*)>0$,
$\operatorname{rank}U=1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic-fibre-v94}
 \Lambda(F^{-1}(P_*))=\{\{0,r,r,s,s,s\}\}.
\end{equation}
For the entire closed stochastic model with these fixed experiment
constants, not merely a pointed subfamily, there is $C_*>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic-modulus-v94}
 \omega_{P_*}(t)=C_*\sqrt t+o(\sqrt t).
\end{equation}
The constant is the joint coefficient-fibre diameter in
Theorem~\ref{thm:germ-newton-v94}. Its observation-neighbourhood
minimax squared risk has order $N^{-1/2}$ and satisfies the constant
bounds of Corollary~\ref{cor:germ-risk-v94}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Positivity at the clocks is immediate. We first prove $\tau>0$
without applying any full-rank-channel theorem. If a polynomial $e$
of degree below three lies in the normalization kernel, interpolation
gives a matrix polynomial $E$ of degree at most three with
$E(T_j)=e(T_j)P_*(T_j)$. The polynomial $qE-eK$ has degree at most
six and vanishes at seven clocks, so it is identically zero.
Take the second marginal and use invertibility of $V$ and positivity
of the weights. It follows that $q$ divides both $ef$ and $eg$.
Since $f$ and $g$ are coprime, $q$ divides $e$, and $e=0$.

For any exact competitor, its monic normalizer solves the same
injective normalization equation. Thus $q'=q$, and interpolation
gives $K'=K$. Write the second marginal as a column of polynomials
\[
 B(z)=K(z)^{\mathsf T}\one
       =V\diag(\alpha,1-\alpha)\binom{f(z)}{g(z)}.
\]
Its two by four coefficient matrix has rank two. In any competitor
representation $B=V'\diag(\alpha')\binom{f'_1}{f'_2}$, the matrix
$V'$ must therefore be invertible. Each $f'_b$ is a linear combination
of $f,g$, and monicity makes it $h_{c_b}$ for a real $c_b$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic-pencil-v94} forces $f'_b\in\{f,g\}$.
The rank of $B$ prevents both from being the same. This proves
\eqref{eq:cubic-fibre-v94}, for all closed-model competitors.
In particular it is not a statement about the fibre of a selected
rank-one parametrization.

We prove a quantitative upper bound before extracting its leading
constant. Put $\delta=\max_j\|P'_j-P_{*,j}\|_F$ for an arbitrary
closed-model competitor. The normalization equation at $P_*$ gives
\[
 \|q'-q\|_{\rm coef}\le C\delta,
 \qquad \|K'-K\|_{\rm coef}\le C\delta.
\]
For completeness, substitute the bounded coefficients of $q'$ into
the difference of the two normalization equations, apply the left
inverse of $\mathcal N_{P_*}$, and then interpolate $q'P'-qP_*$.
This uses $\tau(P_*)>0$, but not the rank of $U$ or of a competitor.
It also gives $\|B'-B\|_{\rm coef}\le C\delta$.

The coefficient matrix of $B'$ has its second singular value bounded
below. The component polynomial coefficients and weights range over
a fixed compact set. In
$B'=V'\diag(\alpha')\binom{f'_1}{f'_2}$ this forces the least
singular value of $V'$ to be bounded below as well: otherwise a unit
left singular vector of $V'$ would contradict the lower singular
value of $B'$. Its inverse and the inverse weights are consequently
uniformly bounded for small $\delta$. Multiplying the coefficient
error $B'-B$ by these inverses shows
\[
 f'_b=(1-c_b)f+c_bg+O_{\rm coef}(\delta),\qquad |c_b|\le C.
\]
Here monicity makes the two combination coefficients sum to
$1+O(\delta)$; its $O(\delta)$ correction is included in the
remainder. The quantitative part of Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic-pencil-v94}
places each $f'_b$ within $C\delta$ of $f$ or $g$. They cannot both
approach the same polynomial, since $B'$ retains rank two with a
fixed singular-value lower bound. After a component permutation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic-coefficient-bound-v94}
 \|f'_1-f\|_{\rm coef}+\|f'_2-g\|_{\rm coef}\le C\delta.
\end{equation}
The anchored real-rooted estimate
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchored-real-roots}, whose proof uses the first two
cluster symmetric functions and allows endpoint roots, gives
$d_\infty(\Lambda',\Lambda_*)\le C\sqrt\delta$.
For the stated Hellinger convention,
$\delta\le2t/\sqrt{\min_jw_j}$ on $B_{P_*}(t)$.
The triangle inequality proves $\omega_{P_*}(t)\le C\sqrt t$.

For a matching lower order, keep the channels, weights, and $f$
fixed and replace $g$ by
\[
 g_u(z)=(z-s)((z-s)^2-u^2),\qquad |u|\text{ small}.
\]
These are actual stochastic alternatives: all three roots
$s-u,s,s+u$ remain in $(0,1)$, and every other constraint is
unchanged. Their aggregate root displacement is $|u|$, whereas
the polynomial and observation changes are $O(u^2)$. The derivative
of the observation with respect to $u^2$ is nonzero. Indeed a zero
derivative would give $E(T_j)=e(T_j)P_{*,j}$ for the nonzero
numerator derivative $E$ and its entry sum $e$. The already proved
normalization-kernel argument would force $e=0$ and then $E=0$,
a contradiction. Hence
$h_w(P_u,P_*)=\gamma u^2+o(u^2)$ with $\gamma>0$.
This proves the lower bound $\omega_{P_*}(t)\ge c\sqrt t$.

The entire exact spectral fibre is the singleton already established.
Theorem~\ref{thm:germ-newton-v94} applies to $A_\theta=f'_1f'_2$
and supplies a positive exact leading constant. The matching orders
force $\alpha_{P_*}=1/2$, proving \eqref{eq:cubic-modulus-v94}.
The statistical assertion follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:germ-risk-v94}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why regular coefficient charts do not prove this theorem.}
There is a genuine kernel in the ambient normalized coefficient
experiment at this datum. Put $\beta_1=\alpha v_1$ and
$\beta_2=(1-\alpha)v_2$. For small $c$, the analytic changes
\[
 f\longmapsto h_c,
 \qquad \beta_1\longmapsto\frac{\beta_1}{1-c},
 \qquad \beta_2\longmapsto\beta_2-\frac{c\beta_1}{1-c}
\]
leave $B$, $K$, and $P_*$ exactly unchanged. The coefficient vectors
remain positive, and their sums give strict normalized weights and
stochastic columns. The curve therefore witnesses the failure of an
injective ambient coefficient Jacobian. Its nonzero points, however,
are excluded by the actual real-root and endpoint constraints, exactly
as Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic-pencil-v94} proves. Replacing the admissible
germ by that ambient tangent would lose identification altogether.

The anchored coefficient bound \eqref{eq:cubic-coefficient-bound-v94}
is a consequence of this particular inequality geometry. It is not
an assertion of an analytic coefficient inverse on an open
neighbourhood of the rank-loss datum. Nor is the example a new exponent
different from $1/2$: its contribution is a whole-model, spectrally
identifiable intersection for which the regular full-rank recovery
hypotheses fail. The exact constant comes from the full admissible
coefficient fibre, not from the ambient singular Fisher matrix.
