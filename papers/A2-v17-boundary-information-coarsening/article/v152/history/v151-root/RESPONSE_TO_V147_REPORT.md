## 2. A structural image criterion: report §7 and §15.2

The new theorem `thm:recognition-dichotomy-v148` starts from an unmarked subspace K in degree D, not from a supplied list of coefficients. Its hypotheses can be read intrinsically:

* the radical of the ideal generated by K in the recovered full symmetric algebra is a determinant hypersurface;
* after recovering the unordered matrix rulings and cancelling the determinant line, the residual relation is invariant under the full group on one factor.

The second condition is **necessary and sufficient** for the residual relation to be a sum of one-sided full-factor Cauchy modules. Complete reducibility and Schur's lemma recover the coefficient spaces as the multiplicity spaces, and exclude hidden graphs across different Schur types. Thus the result identifies the image of the construction among determinant-radical relations; it does not simply repeat an inverse theorem on a supplied coefficient presentation.

Within this recognized class, the theorem gives an exact dichotomy. One admissible orientation exists precisely in the proper-support case. Both orientations are admissible precisely when every support is zero or full. This is stronger than a convenient sufficient certificate, but its scope is explicit: it does not classify every homogeneous relation space or every degeneracy scheme.

## 3. Complete ambiguity and stabilizers: report §7 and §15.2

Theorem `thm:exact-coefficient-stabilizer-v148` calculates the stabilizer in the **unrestricted** general linear group of the cotangent space.

For a system with a nonzero proper support, it is `(G_L × GL(U))/G_m`, where the same left transformation stabilizes every coefficient space. There is no transpose component. For a nonzero zero/full system, it is the full determinant-preserver group `((GL_n × GL_n)/G_m) ⋊ C_2`. The zero relation system is treated separately: its stabilizer is `GL_(n²)` and there is no recovered determinant geometry.

These formulas are affine group-scheme statements. Composing with the retained first-relation automorphism theorem gives the entire nonlinear substitution kernel, rather than identifying all algebra automorphisms with the linear quotient.

Proposition `prop:full-global-ambiguity-v148` further handles the previously omitted zero/full **moving** systems. Their actual source-bundle classes are still recovered. A ruling exchange exists exactly when the source bundle is projectively trivial; both actual source bundles must then be the same line twist of a trivial rank-n bundle. The proof uses the actual normal map to identify the line twists. Thus an exchange is not confused with an undetected bundle twist.

Corollary `cor:intrinsic-orbits-v148` presents the recognized proper-support locus as a geometric orbit-set correspondence between a product of coefficient Grassmannians modulo PGL(V), the unmarked relation stratum modulo GL_(n²), and the associated ungraded Artin algebras. The computed stabilizers record the ineffective right factor and nonlinear automorphisms. No moduli-stack equivalence over nonreduced bases is asserted.

## 4. A consequence native to quadratic pencils: report §8 and §15.3

Theorem `thm:covering-pencil-moduli-v148` replaces reliance on the q=1 auxiliary example by a global statement **inside the original quadratic-pencil construction**.

For each morphism f=[a:b] of degree m from P¹ to P¹, choose H=span(x,y) in V and the pencil bundle R_f=(ax+by)H. Every fibre is in the single singular-pencil orbit of span(x²,xy). Its order-d failure thickening is Zariski locally isotrivial. Nevertheless,

`Y_f ≅ Y_f'` if and only if `f' = alpha ∘ f ∘ sigma`, with independent projective transformations of source and target.

This realizes the whole covering-map equivalence problem for every degree m, not only one pair. The proof cannot be obtained by classifying the fibres: they are all the same. The unmarked moving inverse first recovers one constant left transformation; equivariant recovery of the common linear factor of the pencil and of H then recovers the covering map.

The proof explicitly calculates all the additive bundle data. Writing e=n² and M=binom(N,2), the top graded bundle is

`O^(binom(e+d-1,d)-3M) ⊕ O(m)^(2M)`.

All lower graded bundles are constant symmetric powers. The pencil, quotient and first-relation bundles are respectively `O(-m)^2`, `O(2m) ⊕ O^(N-3)`, and `O(-2m)^M`. The key quotient is proved by the exact sequence with column `(a²,ab,b²)` and rows `(-b,a,0)` and `(0,-b,a)`. Therefore these data are the same for every cover of fixed degree, not merely numerically equal in K-theory.

Corollary `cor:pencil-native-pair-v148` uses z³ and z³+z. Their ramification indices differ, so their total failure schemes are nonisomorphic. For n=3 the schemes have finite rank 167945 and top graded bundle `O^75537 ⊕ O(3)^30`. These are genuine degree-11 quadratic-pencil failure thickenings, not the q=1 rank-712 auxiliary objects. The latter are also retained.

The construction uses singular pencils because they give a natural common-factor orbit. It does not restrict the all-pencil inverse to that orbit, discard regular pencils, or substitute a generic assertion for the existing theorem.
