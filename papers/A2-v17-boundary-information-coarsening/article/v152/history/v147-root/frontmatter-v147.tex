\title[Finite failure schemes and quadratic pencils]{Finite failure schemes and the reconstruction of quadratic pencils}
\author{Qian Qi}
\date{September 24, 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{14M12, 14B05, 14D20, 13C40}
\keywords{Quadratic pencil, nonreduced degeneracy locus, intrinsic reconstruction, infinitesimal neighbourhood}
\hypersetup{pdftitle={Finite failure schemes and the reconstruction of quadratic pencils, revision 147},pdfauthor={Qian Qi}}
\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct a quadratic pencil varying over a smooth connected
projective complex base from an abstract unmarked finite thickening.
The scheme recovers the reduced base, the actual source bundle, and
the whole pencil subbundle up to one constant left transformation.
The theorem holds for every pencil, including singular pencils, and
for arbitrary source bundles.  It follows from a coefficient-descent
principle for moving subspaces in multiplicity-one Schur modules:
the first relation kernel recovers a determinant cone, asymmetric
coefficient supports orient its tensor factors, and the actual
normal map removes the line-twist ambiguity.  We distinguish this
orbit rigidity from the general first-relation truncation lemma,
whose entire nonlinear freedom is a unipotent automorphism kernel.
For embedding dimension \(n\ge3\), a single unmarked Artin local
algebra already determines a constant pencil at the smallest uniform
order \(d=n^2+2n-4\); all lower orders forget it.  The same order
works on the socle Grassmannian or on a Schubert line.  We give the
local automorphism group as an affine group scheme and retain the
filtered geometric automorphism sequence for projective reductions.
Two isotrivial families with identical graded vector bundles are
separated by their unmarked algebra structures.  A nonisotrivial
pencil family passes through spectral collisions without loss of reconstruction.  The marked universal construction commutes
with arbitrary complex base change, and regular pencils retain their
full spectral and higher Fitting data.
\end{abstract}
