## New comparison: conics and birational moduli models

Kiryong Chung and Han-Bom Moon, *Mori's program for the moduli space of conics in Grassmannian*, Taiwanese Journal of Mathematics 21 (2017), 621–652; arXiv:1608.00181.

Primary full text inspected: `https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.00181`.

Theorem 1.1 and Section 3.1 were read, including the rendered pages containing the principal theorem and Definition 3.3. The work identifies birational models of degree-two stable-map spaces to Grassmannians, including Hilbert and Chow constructions. Its linear-envelope description provides a concrete example of how conic moduli and their birational models are related. These are different targets and moduli objects from the present coefficient graph. Revision 164 therefore calls its construction a collision specialization and horizontal closure, not a stability wall crossing or a completion of Mori's program. No claim that the cited paper proves our scheme fibre, quotient model, torsion sheaf, or nilradical twist is made. No claim of nonanticipation is inferred merely from this difference in formulation.

## Flatness input and fixed points of the involution

The Stacks Project, Tag 0539, Lemma 15.22.10:
`https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0539`.

The statement and its proof were checked. It gives the torsion-free criterion for flatness over a valuation ring. Applied to the localizations of C[delta] at its primes, it gives flatness of the integral horizontal collision model. It does not imply flatness of the full pullback: that space has the explicit nonzero delta-torsion computed in the manuscript.

The involution on the ordered blow-up fixes its exceptional divisor. A theorem requiring a free finite action would not justify smoothness of this quotient. The submitted proof instead writes its invariant rings directly: `C[lambda,e,C]` with `delta=-e^2 C`, and `C[lambda,u,delta]`. Their overlap identifies the quotient and its smoothness, including at the fixed divisor. Normalization after the quadratic base change is proved by adjoining the integral element d with `d^2=-C` in the same fraction field. These are the actual calculations used, not an appeal to a free-action quotient criterion.

## Exact new claim and its dependence

Theorem `thm:collision-family-v164` is a calculation of the full pullback along `(x^2-delta,0,0)` and its horizontal closure. It uses the established complete first-contact charts, then identifies the entire horizontal projective scheme as a finite quotient of an ordered blow-up. The vertical conic component and its torsion are proved by exact scheme ideals. Theorem `thm:nilradical-sheaf-v164` obtains the global line-bundle twist of the nilpotent module from the collision divisor and computes coherent cohomology. These assertions are not inferred from pointwise dimensions, radicals, or numerical experiments.

The explicit genuine-pencil realization supplies an application in the fixed complete-quadric target; its comparison uses the established embedded quotient theorem. The effective failure-family statement is a consequence through reconstruction. It is not an internal boundary operation on a closed Artin algebra before the source and pencil have been recovered. The independent-collision product describes the stated disjoint-support family; it does not classify every degeneration crossing a higher-corank stratum.
