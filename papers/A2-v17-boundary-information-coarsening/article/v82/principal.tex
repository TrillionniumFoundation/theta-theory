\section{Action rigidity under selective detection}
\label{sec:v82-main}

The information in an endpoint experiment depends on the detector as
well as on the dynamics. A detector common to all trajectories acts by
a scalar on a joint readout matrix. A detector which can vary freely
with both the trajectory and the deadline can instead compensate a
change of action. Between these two regimes lies a natural question:
can the action be separated from an unknown, branch-selective detector
whose dependence on the deadline belongs to a fixed finite-dimensional
family? We answer this question for log-affine detectors, and then for
polynomial log-detectors. Neither the detector coefficients nor the
readout channels are calibrated.

Fix five distinct deadlines $T_1<\cdots<T_5$, and a compact connected
rectangle $Q\Subset\R^d$. At endpoint $z$, two finite readings are
conditionally independent given the latent component. Their probability
matrices $U(z)\in\R^{u\times B}$ and $V(z)\in\R^{v\times B}$ have
full column rank. Their columns sum to one, and their entries are
nonnegative. Both channels may depend on $z$, but not on the deadline.
The positive integer $B\le\min(u,v)$ is not supplied to the observer.
Before arbitrary branch-blind rescaling, the joint matrices are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-observation}
 M_j=U\diag\{a_b e^{\kappa_bT_j}(T_j-W_b)\}_{b=1}^B V^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad a_b>0,\quad W_b<T_1.
\end{equation}
Every coefficient in this formula is an unknown function of $z$.
The observations are positive projective classes $[M_j]$, represented
without loss by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-normalized}
 P_j=\frac{M_j}{\one^{\mathsf T}M_j\one}
     =U\diag(\pi_{j,b})V^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \pi_{j,b}=\frac{a_be^{\kappa_bT_j}(T_j-W_b)}
                   {\sum_c a_ce^{\kappa_cT_j}(T_j-W_c)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, failure counts, success masses, endpoint exposures and
any common positive detector factor at each deadline may all be absent.
Normalization is of the entire joint matrix, not of each row or column.

A physical instance has retention
$e_b(T,z)=\eta_b(z)\exp(\kappa_b(z)(T-T_*))$, restricted to the
finite clock interval so that it is a probability. The factor
$\eta_b e^{-\kappa_bT_*}$ is absorbed in $a_b$. An exponential survival
probability with an unknown nonnegative, phase-dependent rate is an
example. The mathematical model does not require an observer to know
that rate or to measure the phase point.

\begin{theorem}[Five-deadline selective-detector rigidity]
\label{thm:v82-main}
Assume \eqref{eq:v82-observation} on $Q$ with $C^m$ coefficients,
where $m\ge0$ is an integer. At every endpoint assume that the pairs
$(W_b,\kappa_b)$ are pairwise distinct and that the actions are not
all equal. Then five projective observations determine the visible
component count $B$, all absolute actions $W_b$, both channels, the
relative weights $a_b/a_c$, and all differences $\kappa_b-\kappa_c$.
They do so up to one simultaneous permutation on the connected gate.
The complete continuous ambiguity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-gauge}
 a_b\longmapsto h a_b,\qquad
 \kappa_b\longmapsto\kappa_b+\rho,
 \qquad h(z)>0,
\end{equation}
common to every component. It does not change an action.

The reconstruction is locally Lipschitz in $C^m$, without loss of
spatial derivatives, on the quantitatively bounded classes specified
in Proposition~\ref{prop:v82-stable}. No component audit, semantic
branch catalogue, calibrated channel, or measured exposure is used.
Actions of different components may coincide at an endpoint: distinct
detector rates separate such components, provided another pair of components has unequal actions at that same
endpoint.
\end{theorem}

The unequal-action condition is pointwise, not merely somewhere on $Q$.
A uniformly separated anchor pair can be chosen locally and is selected
from the observations. It is not an extra label supplied by the apparatus.

The theorem concerns the number of full-rank visible components. It
does not count invisible duplicates in a rank-deficient representation.
Nor is conditional independence obtained by copying one noisy reading.
The two readings must arise from independent randomizations conditional
on the trajectory and endpoint. These assumptions are part of the
observation operator.

\subsection{The principal mechanism and its context}

The proof separates two issues. Simultaneous diagonalization first
recovers latent probability trajectories from the matrices. A scalar
interpolation theorem then separates the absolute action from an unknown
exponential detector rate and from all projective normalizations.
The second step, rather than a new claim about diagonalization, is the
information-rigidity assertion of this revision.

Algebraic identification of latent classes is classical; see
Allman--Matias--Rhodes~\cite{V81AllmanMatiasRhodes}. Observable-operator
methods and moment methods are developed in
\cite{V81AnandkumarHsuKakade}; joint diagonalization in a nonorthogonal
basis is treated directly in Bonhomme--Jochmans--Robin
\cite{V82BonhommeJoint}. Those results motivate the spectral part below.
Here the third experimental coordinate is a prescribed deadline, not
another unknown component response: its affine zero is the absolute
geometric action, even after unknown selective attenuation.
Our scalar proof is elementary and is included in full. No priority
claim for the general matrix-pencil or latent-class mechanisms is made.

The conversion of recovered generating functions into canonical graphs
uses classical variational calculus~\cite{MarsdenWest2001,LallWest2006}.
Return-time primitives and contact suspensions have their usual meaning
\cite{Hutchings2016,V81Geiges2008}. The new conclusion precedes those
constructions: the absolute functions, their count, and their local
pairing are recovered despite a selective detector and without semantic
branch labels. The subsequent geometric application still states its
phase-coverage hypothesis explicitly.

\section{Observable components without action separation}
\label{sec:v82-components}

Fix an endpoint for the moment. Since the weights in
\eqref{eq:v82-normalized} are positive and both channels have full
column rank,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-rank}
                         B=\operatorname{rank}P_1.
\end{equation}
Choose row and column index sets $I,J$ of size $B$ for which
$(P_1)_{IJ}$ is invertible, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-operators}
 K_j=(P_j)_{IJ}(P_1)_{IJ}^{-1}
     =U_I\diag(q_{j,b})U_I^{-1},\qquad
 q_{j,b}=\pi_{j,b}/\pi_{1,b}.
\end{equation}
Neither $K_2$ nor $K_3$ needs to have simple spectrum.

\begin{lemma}[Joint spectral separation]\label{lem:v82-separation}
If the pairs $(W_b,\kappa_b)$ are distinct, the vectors
$(q_{2,b},q_{3,b})$ are pairwise distinct.
Consequently the matrices $K_2,K_3$ determine their common rank-one
spectral projectors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose two such vectors were equal. The ratio of their unnormalized
component weights would take the same value at $T_1,T_2,T_3$.
Its logarithm, up to a constant, is
\[
 f(T)=(\kappa_b-\kappa_c)T+
                      \log(T-W_b)-\log(T-W_c).
\]
If $W_b\ne W_c$, then
$f''(T)=-(T-W_b)^{-2}+(T-W_c)^{-2}$ has a strict constant sign
on $T>\max(W_b,W_c)$. A strictly convex or strictly concave function
cannot take one value at three ordered points. If $W_b=W_c$, $f$ is
a nonconstant affine function because the parameter pairs are distinct,
and cannot take one value even at two points. This is a contradiction.
A linear combination of $K_2,K_3$ avoiding the finitely many pairwise
eigenvalue coincidences has simple spectrum and the desired common
projectors.
\end{proof}

There is a finite deterministic choice in the last sentence. Set
$L=B(B-1)/2$. Among $K_2+tK_3$, $t=0,\ldots,L$, at least one has
simple spectrum: a pair can exclude at most one value of $t$.
Let $R_b$ be its spectral projectors, labelled locally. For every clock,
form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-component-matrices}
 E_{j,b}=(P_1)_{:J}(P_1)_{IJ}^{-1}R_b(P_j)_{I:}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Recovery of channels and probability trajectories]
\label{lem:v82-components}
The preceding construction gives
\[
 E_{j,b}=\pi_{j,b}U_{:b}V_{:b}^{\mathsf T},\quad
 \pi_{j,b}=\one^{\mathsf T}E_{j,b}\one,\quad
 U_{:b}=E_{1,b}\one/\pi_{1,b},\quad
 V_{:b}=E_{1,b}^{\mathsf T}\one/\pi_{1,b}.
\]
The result is independent of the invertible minor and of the separating
linear combination, up to the same permutation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In the common diagonalizing basis,
$R_b=U_I e_be_b^{\mathsf T}U_I^{-1}$, and
$(P_1)_{:J}(P_1)_{IJ}^{-1}=UU_I^{-1}$.
Also $(P_j)_{I:}=U_I\diag(\pi_{j,c})V^{\mathsf T}$.
Substitution proves the first identity. Summation proves the remaining
identities since each channel column has total mass one. The resulting
expressions are intrinsic component matrices and hence do not depend
on the construction choices.
\end{proof}

These lemmas explain why a coincidence of actions need not destroy
component recovery. Distinct selective rates can separate the observed
clock trajectories even when the two actions are equal. They do not
resolve two components with identical $(W,\kappa)$ by merely declaring
their channel columns different.

\section{Five values separate actions from detector rates}
\label{sec:v82-interpolation}

For two recovered components define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-logratio}
 \ell_j^{bc}=\log\frac{\pi_{j,b}}{\pi_{j,c}}
 =A_{bc}+K_{bc}T_j+\log(T_j-W_b)-\log(T_j-W_c),
\end{equation}
where $A_{bc}=\log(a_b/a_c)$ and
$K_{bc}=\kappa_b-\kappa_c$. All common exposures have disappeared.

\begin{lemma}[Five-value rigidity]\label{lem:v82-five}
Let $x,y,\widetilde x,\widetilde y<T_1$, and let $T_1<\cdots<T_5$.
If
\[
 A+KT_j+\log(T_j-x)-\log(T_j-y)
 =\widetilde A+\widetilde K T_j+
                  \log(T_j-\widetilde x)-\log(T_j-\widetilde y)
\]
for $j=1,\ldots,5$, and $x\ne y$, then
$(A,K,x,y)=(\widetilde A,\widetilde K,\widetilde x,\widetilde y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $H(T)$ be the difference of the two expressions. It has five
zeros. Its derivative is
\[
 H'(T)=k+\frac1{T-x}-\frac1{T-y}
             -\frac1{T-\widetilde x}+\frac1{T-\widetilde y},
 \qquad k=K-\widetilde K.
\]
Multiply by
$D(T)=(T-x)(T-y)(T-\widetilde x)(T-\widetilde y)$.
The rational part has numerator of degree at most two, because its
four residues sum to zero. Thus $D H'=kD+R$, $\deg R\le2$.
Rolle's theorem supplies four distinct zeros of $H'$ in $(T_1,T_5)$.
If $k\ne0$, these are all four roots of the quartic $kD+R$.
Their sum, by its cubic coefficient, is
$x+y+\widetilde x+\widetilde y<4T_1$.
Each root exceeds $T_1$, a contradiction. If $k=0$, four zeros force
$R$ to vanish identically. Therefore $H'$ is identically zero.
Its limit at infinity also gives $k=0$.

Equality of the rational functions now gives equality of the signed
pole multisets
$\{x,\widetilde y\}=\{y,\widetilde x\}$, with multiplicities.
Since $x\ne y$, it follows that $x=\widetilde x$ and
$y=\widetilde y$. Finally, a value at one deadline gives
$A=\widetilde A$. Repeated poles cause no difficulty in the polynomial
argument or in this cancellation argument.
\end{proof}

No optimization algorithm or genericity argument is used in this global
uniqueness statement. Five is a sufficient count; the theorem does not
claim that it is the smallest global count.

\begin{lemma}[An observable anchor and the remaining components]
\label{lem:v82-anchor}
The second divided difference of the three numbers
$\ell_1^{bc},\ell_2^{bc},\ell_3^{bc}$ is nonzero exactly when
$W_b\ne W_c$. Once such a pair has been recovered by
Lemma~\ref{lem:v82-five}, every other $W_b,A_{bc},K_{bc}$ is uniquely
determined from three deadlines and the known anchor action $W_c$,
even when $W_b=W_c$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The affine part of \eqref{eq:v82-logratio} has zero second divided
difference. For unequal actions the logarithmic part has a second
derivative of strict constant sign, as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v82-separation}; for equal actions it vanishes.
This proves the first assertion and makes selection of an unequal pair
an operation on the data.

For the second assertion add $\log(T_j-W_c)$ to the observed values.
Two alternative parameter triples would make
$A+KT+\log(T-w)-\log(T-\widetilde w)$ vanish at three clocks,
with the constants understood as differences. If $w\ne\widetilde w$
this function has second derivative of strict constant sign, which is
impossible. Thus the actions agree, and two clocks determine the affine
coefficients.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the exact part of Theorem~\ref{thm:v82-main}]
Rank gives $B$, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:v82-separation} and
\ref{lem:v82-components} recover the channels and all $\pi_{j,b}$.
There is an observable unequal-action anchor pair by hypothesis.
Lemmas~\ref{lem:v82-five} and \ref{lem:v82-anchor} recover every action
and every relative coefficient. Relative coefficients determine the
weights and rates precisely modulo \eqref{eq:v82-gauge}; conversely
that gauge plainly leaves every projective observation unchanged.
This proves both identification and completeness of the continuous
ambiguity. Any alternative full-rank representation has the same $B$
and must pass through the same reconstruction. Local permutations
agree on overlaps after matching the recovered component matrices.
Since distinct parameter pairs cannot exchange continuously, two
continuous global representations differ by one constant permutation
on the connected gate. No sorting by action is required at a crossing.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite-order stability and the number of clocks}
\label{sec:v82-stability}

A local inverse is not obtained by applying a global nonlinear solver
and assuming its success. We exhibit its nonsingular local coordinates.
For an unequal-action anchor write
$\Phi_j(A,K,x,y)=A+KT_j+\log(T_j-x)-\log(T_j-y)$.
On any four distinct deadlines its Jacobian has rows
\[
                    (1,T_j,-(T_j-x)^{-1},(T_j-y)^{-1}).
\]
A direct Vandermonde calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-jacobian}
 \det D\Phi=
 \frac{(y-x)\prod_{i<j}(T_j-T_i)}
      {\prod_j(T_j-x)(T_j-y)},
\end{equation}
where both products use these four deadlines. Indeed, multiplication
of row $j$ by $(T_j-x)(T_j-y)$ reduces the columns to four polynomials
of degrees at most three; their coefficient determinant is $y-x$.
The determinant is nonzero. For a remaining component with known anchor,
the three local coordinates $(A,K,w)$ have independent derivative
columns $1,T,-1/(T-w)$: multiplication by $T-w$ reduces the question
to a polynomial of degree at most two at three distinct points.

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative local inverse]\label{prop:v82-stable}
Fix $m,u,v,B$, the deadlines, a finite gate cover with fixed smooth
gluing functions, and bounds for the $C^m$ norms of $P_j,U,V,W_b$
and the relative logarithmic weights and rates. Fix positive lower
bounds for the residuals $T_1-W_b$, all $\pi_{j,b}$, the least
singular value of each chosen $(P_1)_{IJ}$, and
\[
 \min_{b\ne c}\max\{|q_{2,b}-q_{2,c}|,|q_{3,b}-q_{3,c}|\}.
\]
On each member of the cover fix an anchor with
$|W_r-W_s|\ge\gamma>0$. Also fix upper bounds for $|W_b|$ and the
residuals. Then the inverse, after a local permutation and gauge choice,
extends to a neighborhood of the exact normalized matrix data and
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-stability}
 \|\widehat W-W\|_{C^m}+
 \|\widehat U-U\|_{C^m}+\|\widehat V-V\|_{C^m}
 +\|\widehat A-A\|_{C^m}+\|\widehat K-K\|_{C^m}
 \le C\max_j\|\widehat P_j-P_j\|_{C^m}.
\end{equation}
Here $A,K$ are relative to one fixed component on each local chart.
The constant depends only on the displayed bounds, margins, cover and
clock gaps, and not on unknown derivatives above order $m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Matrix inversion on the fixed minor charts is smooth with controlled
derivatives. If the displayed joint gap is at least $\delta$, one
can choose $t\in\{0,\ldots,L\}$ with every gap of $K_2+tK_3$
at least $\delta/(4L+4)$, where $L=B(B-1)/2$.
To see this, a given pair can have a gap smaller than that quantity at
at most one integer $t$: small gaps at two distinct integers would force
both its $q_3$ difference and its $q_2$ difference below $\delta$.
There are at most $L$ pairs. A finite refinement makes the same choice
valid on each local chart.

For a simple real eigenvalue, the characteristic-polynomial derivative
is the product of its eigenvalue gaps. The implicit function theorem
therefore gives smooth real eigenvalues on a neighborhood with uniformly
bounded derivatives of every fixed order. Realness follows also by
conjugation and uniqueness in a small disk. Spectral projectors are
polynomials in the chosen matrix divided by gap products. Thus
\eqref{eq:v82-component-matrices}, summation, positive division and
logarithms all have controlled smooth local extensions.

Equation~\eqref{eq:v82-jacobian} gives a smooth local inverse for the
anchor parameters using four clocks. The fifth clock supplies global
uniqueness on exact data by Lemma~\ref{lem:v82-five}; it need not be
inverted a second time locally. The three-coordinate inverse above
recovers the other components, including equal-action ones. Repeated
chain and product rules through order $m$ establish
\eqref{eq:v82-stability}. Fixed smooth gluing functions combine the
local extensions. Exact outputs agree on overlaps after their recovered
permutation and relative-gauge transitions. Perturbed outputs need not
satisfy all latent-model identities; they obey the error bound.
This proves a local inverse on an open neighborhood, not an assertion
that arbitrary data lie in the model.
\end{proof}

On any compact parameter class with full-rank channels, positive weights,
uniformly distinct $(W,\kappa)$ pairs and positive action range, the
needed finite covers and margins follow by compactness. They deteriorate
as a component becomes invisible or all actions coalesce. Thresholding
singular values recovers the visible count if the observation error is
less than a fixed fraction of the least nonzero singular value. Without
such a margin, counting an arbitrarily weak component is not stable.
The norm in \eqref{eq:v82-stability} is a norm of normalized observations;
converting an absolute error for unnormalized matrices to it requires a
lower bound on their total observed mass.

\begin{proposition}[Three clocks cannot identify this class]
\label{prop:v82-three}
There are two-component, full-rank models with unequal actions and
strict probability margins for which three projective deadlines have
an action-changing one-parameter ambiguity. Four clocks give a local
inverse for the anchor scalar parameters. Five clocks give the global
inverse proved above; no global optimality claim for five is made.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix strictly positive full-rank channels and unequal $x,y<T_1$.
At three clocks the map $(A,K,x,y)\mapsto(\Phi_1,\Phi_2,\Phi_3)$
has rank three. The columns $1,T,1/(T-x)$ are already independent,
by multiplying a putative relation by $T-x$. The implicit function
theorem gives a local one-dimensional level set. Its tangent cannot
have both action coordinates zero, since $1,T$ are independent.
Along a sufficiently small nonconstant part of the level set the actions
therefore vary. For two components each probability vector is determined
by its log ratio, so the entire three normalized matrices are unchanged.
Positivity and all strict finite margins persist. Baseline weights or
retention probabilities can be decreased beforehand to keep all raw
success probabilities strictly below one. The four-clock assertion is
\eqref{eq:v82-jacobian}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The equal-action obstruction]\label{prop:v82-equal}
If all actions equal $w$, arbitrary projective deadline observations
cannot determine $w$, even when the rates are distinct and both channels
have full column rank.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The common factor $T_j-w$ cancels from \eqref{eq:v82-normalized}.
Any other common action below the first deadline gives the same data.
Distinct rates can still identify different components; they do not
supply the missing common action. This is an exact nonlinear obstruction,
not a failure of an estimator.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Polynomial detector families}

The preceding dichotomy extends beyond a single unknown rate. Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-polynomial}
 M_j=U\diag\{a_b\exp(P_b(T_j))(T_j-W_b)\}V^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 P_b(T)=\sum_{\nu=1}^q\theta_{b,\nu}T^\nu,
\end{equation}
with a fixed known degree bound $q\ge1$. Constants in $P_b$ are absorbed
in $a_b$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-degree detector rigidity]\label{thm:v82-polynomial}
Assume positive weights, full-rank clock-invariant channels, pairwise
distinct tuples $(W_b,\theta_{b,1},\ldots,\theta_{b,q})$, and at least
two unequal actions at each endpoint. Any $q+5$ distinct projective
deadlines determine all actions, channels, relative weights and relative
polynomial coefficients. The remaining gauge is a common positive
weight multiplier and addition of one common degree-$q$ polynomial.
The inverse is locally Lipschitz in $C^m$ under the corresponding compact
margins. For an anchor pair, $q+3$ clocks give a local inverse, whereas
$q+2$ clocks admit an action-changing ambiguity in a two-component class.
For $q=1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:v82-main} improves the sufficient global
count from six to five.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a component pair, its log weight ratio is a degree-$q$ polynomial
plus $\log(T-x)-\log(T-y)$. If $x\ne y$, the derivative of order
$q+1$ of the logarithmic part has strict constant sign on
$T>\max(x,y)$, since it is a nonzero constant times
$(T-x)^{-q-1}-(T-y)^{-q-1}$. Such a function cannot take one constant
value at $q+2$ distinct clocks, by repeated Rolle's theorem. If $x=y$,
a nonconstant polynomial of degree at most $q$ cannot do so either.
Thus the joint eigenvalue vectors from the first $q+2$ clocks are
pairwise distinct. A separating real linear combination exists: the
excluded combinations form finitely many proper hyperplanes. Local
finite choices follow by compactness. The same component-matrix formula
as \eqref{eq:v82-component-matrices} recovers the channels and weights.
An unequal-action pair is detectable by a divided difference of order
$q+1$.

Two alternatives for its log ratio differ by a polynomial $Q$ of degree
at most $q$ and four signed logarithms. Multiplication of the derivative
by the four-pole denominator gives $Q'D+R$, $\deg R\le2$, of degree
at most $q+3$. If the difference vanishes at $q+5$ clocks, its derivative
has at least $q+4$ distinct zeros and this polynomial vanishes identically.
The rational identity has no nonzero polynomial part at infinity, so
$Q'=0$; pole cancellation then identifies the two unequal actions.
One value gives the constant. With an anchor known, the same argument,
or repeated Rolle applied to two logarithms, identifies every other
component and its relative polynomial.

For the local anchor inverse the derivative columns are
$1,T,\ldots,T^q,-1/(T-x),1/(T-y)$. Multiplication by $(T-x)(T-y)$
turns a relation into a polynomial of degree at most $q+2$.
Vanishing at $q+3$ clocks forces it to vanish identically, and its poles
and polynomial part force all coefficients to vanish. The remaining
components use $q+2$ local coordinates with one action denominator.
Smooth finite-order stability follows exactly as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:v82-stable}. At $q+2$ clocks, the anchor Jacobian
has rank $q+2$ already on columns $1,T,\ldots,T^q,1/(T-x)$.
Its one-dimensional level set cannot vary only the polynomial
coefficients, since their values at these clocks have full column rank.
Thus an action changes while the two-component observations stay fixed.
\end{proof}

A degree bound is substantive. With $J$ fixed clocks and arbitrary
branchwise log-detectors, any change of admissible actions can be
compensated at those clocks by polynomial interpolation of degree at
most $J-1$. The finite-degree theorem therefore does not imply recovery
for an unrestricted detector by increasing the degree after seeing the
data. It specifies an identifiable observation class and its exact
gauge before acquisition.

\section{Return dynamics and a nonconvex crossed-action example}
\label{sec:v82-geometry}

We spell out the passage to geometry without treating full iterates as
observed group elements. On a fixed canonical phase domain let
$\lambda=p\,dx$. An exact lift is a pair $g=(F,A)$ with
$dA=F^*\lambda-\lambda$. Its product and inverse are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-lift}
 (F,A)(G,B)=(F\circ G,B+A\circ G),\qquad
 (F,A)^{-1}=(F^{-1},-A\circ F^{-1}).
\end{equation}
An absolute regular type-I sheet $S(x,y)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v82-graph}
                 p=-S_x(x,y),\quad q=S_y(x,y),\quad A(x,p)=S(x,y),
\end{equation}
with invertible $S_{xy}$.

\begin{corollary}[Unpaired return sheets with selective detection]
\label{cor:v82-return}
Let $g=(F,A)$ be an exact lift and let $r,s$ be coprime positive integers.
For each iterate assume a finite family of regular endpoint gates in
fixed canonical coordinates. On each gate the actual absolute generating
sheets form the latent components of Theorem~\ref{thm:v82-main}.
Their count, channel columns and cross-gate pairing are not given.
Assume that the resulting phase graphs cover all domains required by
a fixed Bezout word, including its inverse factors, with fixed collars.
Then five projective deadlines per gate determine $g$ on the covered
target. With fixed $C^{k+2}$ action bounds, inverse mixed-Jacobian and
phase-map inverse bounds, the inverse from $C^{k+2}$ normalized matrix
data to the $C^k$ lift is locally Lipschitz.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:v82-main} recovers the unordered absolute functions on
each gate, including at action coincidences. Formula~\eqref{eq:v82-graph}
recovers their canonical graphs and absolute primitives. At an initial
phase point the local equation for $y$ is regular. Any overlapping
representations have the same image and primitive, because the
observation class consists of sheets of the same deterministic lift.
This gives $g^r,g^s$ on the required domains without a supplied matching
of sheet indices. Choose integers $u,v$ with $ur+vs=1$. Since these are
powers of one lift,
\[
                           g=(g^r)^u(g^s)^v.
\]
The domain hypothesis allows every factor to be evaluated, and
\eqref{eq:v82-lift} preserves the additive primitive constants.
This proves uniqueness.

For stability, the first step has no derivative loss. Solving
$p=-S_x$ by the quantitative implicit function theorem gives $C^k$
map estimates from $C^{k+2}$ actions. The same bounds control the
additional $C^{k+1}$ norms needed for composition and inverse estimates.
A finite product on fixed nested collars gives the asserted $C^k$ bound.
Fixed local extensions handle perturbed data without asserting their
exact symplectic realizability. The elementary Bezout identity is not
claimed as a new group-theoretic theorem; its inputs have just been
reconstructed from weaker observations.
\end{proof}

For a pre-existing Reeb flow with a smooth transverse global section,
the first-return map and time satisfy
$F^*\lambda-\lambda=d\tau$. Indeed the section flow-box map pulls
back the contact form to $dt+\lambda$, and differentiation of
$\phi_{\tau(x)}(x)=F(x)$ gives that identity. Hence the corollary,
when its actual return sheets are covered, recovers $(F,\tau)$ and its
section-preserving strict contact suspension. It does not infer the
existence of the section or of the phase cover from endpoint matrices.

\begin{proposition}[Common deadlines and a fixed source]
\label{prop:v82-physical}
Suppose actual return actions on the gates satisfy $0\le W_b\le H$.
For $h>0$ take a common external release delay, uniform on
$[0,D]$ with $D=H+6h$, and deadlines $T_j=H+jh$, $1\le j\le5$.
Use the same normalized phase source $\rho$ at every setting. Then a
trajectory is accepted by the time gate with probability $(T_j-W_b)/D$,
with both residual and unused-delay margins at least $h$.
A subsequent log-affine selective retention produces
\eqref{eq:v82-observation}. No true-component audit is needed to set
these clocks.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For an independent delay $\alpha$, success means $\alpha+W_b\le T_j$.
The stated inequalities keep this event strictly inside the uniform-delay
interval. On a regular sheet, change initial momentum to terminal
coordinate. Its successful density before the extra readouts is
\[
 \frac{\rho(x,-S_{b,x})|\det S_{b,xy}|}{D}
       \eta_b e^{\kappa_b(T_j-T_*)}(T_j-W_b).
\]
The factors outside $e^{\kappa_bT_j}(T_j-W_b)$ are independent of the
setting and are absorbed in $a_b$. Independent readings give the product
columns; an additional branch-blind detector acts by projective scaling.
This proves the assertion using a literally fixed source density.
For $J$ deadlines replace $6h$ by $(J+1)h$, so the polynomial-detector
protocol also has common windows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Three regular sheets through an action crossing]
\label{prop:v82-shear}
The preceding observation and return hypotheses have a nonconvex
three-sheet realization which includes an action coincidence, not merely
separated abstract mixture components.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On $\R^2$ put
\[
 \phi(p)=p^3-p,\quad F(x,p)=(x+\phi(p),p),\quad
 A(p)=1+\tfrac34p^4-\tfrac12p^2.
\]
Then $F^*\lambda-\lambda=p\phi'(p)\,dp=dA$ and
$A\ge11/12$. Its positive-roof quotient preserves $dt+p\,dx$ and
has $F$ as return map with return time $A$.
For a fixed return count $n$, write $t=(y-x)/n$ and let
$p_-(t),p_0(t),p_+(t)$ be the three solutions of $\phi(p)=t$
near $-1,0,1$. They exist on an interval containing zero. With
$G(p)=p^4/4-p^2/2$, their generating sheets are
\[
 S_{n,b}(x,y)=n+p_b(t)(y-x)-nG(p_b(t))=nA(p_b(t)).
\]
They satisfy $-S_{n,b,x}=S_{n,b,y}=p_b(t)$ and
$S_{n,b,xy}=-1/(n\phi'(p_b(t)))\ne0$. At $x=y$, the two outer
actions equal $5n/4$, while the central action is $n$.
Thus there is always an unequal-action anchor on a sufficiently small
compact rectangle through the diagonal, although action separation of
all three sheets fails. The central and outer mixed derivatives have
opposite signs, so this is not a globally convex minimization model.

Use a selective detector with survival rate $r(p)=r_0+r_1p>0$ on these
compact momentum patches, where $r_1\ne0$. Its retention is
$\eta\exp[-r(p)(T-T_1)]$, with $0<\eta<1$. The unknown rates
$\kappa_b=-r(p_b)$ separate the two outer components at their action
coincidence. For the first readout take the probability vector
$(1,e^{\alpha p},e^{2\alpha p})/(1+e^{\alpha p}+e^{2\alpha p})$,
and for the second use the analogous vector with an unknown
$\beta\ne0$, where also $\alpha\ne0$. Draw the readings independently
given the phase point. The three columns form normalized Vandermonde
matrices at distinct momenta, hence have full rank and positive entries.
The inverse is not supplied $\alpha,\beta,r_0,r_1$ or the response
curves. This is a physical measurement mechanism, not an observed
momentum coordinate disguised as a branch label.

A fixed smooth normalized source positive on all three compact phase
patches gives positive weights. Compactness gives all required margins,
and Proposition~\ref{prop:v82-physical} gives common clocks.
For return counts two and three, choose finite compact endpoint
rectangles containing the input and output patches needed for
$g=g^3(g^2)^{-1}$; the momentum is unchanged and the horizontal shifts
are bounded. This supplies the finite covered target in
Corollary~\ref{cor:v82-return}. The example includes the diagonal
crossing but not the fold points $\phi'(p)=0$. No caustic-crossing
claim is made.
\end{proof}

\section{Observation errors and the boundary of the conclusion}
\label{sec:v82-scope}

The inverse acts on conditional joint readout matrices at each endpoint.
For a finite-sample implementation let $H_j(z)$ be the observed joint
density of endpoint and the two readings, and write
$f_j(z)=\one^{\mathsf T}H_j(z)\one$. Raw and retained-record budgets
are different; either can estimate its corresponding $H_j$.

\begin{corollary}[Error transfer without a calibration sample]
\label{cor:v82-error}
On a gate with $f_j\ge f_*>0$ and bounded $C^m$ densities, suppose
$\max_j\|\widehat H_j-H_j\|_{C^m}\le\varepsilon$.
If the true model has the margins of Proposition~\ref{prop:v82-stable}
and $\varepsilon$ is sufficiently small, normalization of
$\widehat H_j$ followed by the selective-detector inverse gives
$C\varepsilon$ error in actions, channels and relative detector
coefficients. The same conclusion holds with an additional
$C^m$ model perturbation $\eta$, replacing $\varepsilon$ by
$\varepsilon+\eta$. There is no calibration-sample term.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The sum of the estimated cell densities differs from $f_j$ by at most
$uv\varepsilon$ in $C^m$ and has floor $f_*/2$ for small error.
The $C^m$ product and reciprocal estimates bound
$\widehat H_j/(\one^{\mathsf T}\widehat H_j\one)-H_j/f_j$
by $C\varepsilon$. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:v82-stable}.
A perturbation adds by the triangle inequality. Threshold failures can
be assigned a fixed reference output to define an estimator everywhere;
this statement concerns the event on which the displayed errors and
margins hold. It makes no minimax assertion about the density-estimation
rate or about geometric derivative loss.
\end{proof}

The fixed-source compensation construction in the retained physical
analysis allows a different retention function for every component and
deadline. It remains valid in that larger apparatus class. It does not
contradict the present result: the exact compensator need not have an
affine, or a fixed-degree polynomial, logarithm in the deadline.
Conversely, restricting the detector does not make all geometry
unconditionally observable. A single common action is still hidden,
independent readouts still need rank, and the return theorem still needs
a covered phase domain. These are explicit mathematical hypotheses,
not statements that an unavailable experiment has been performed.

The full revision retains the earlier two-raw and three-projective
clock inverses, conditional-law information theory, calibrated-label
analysis, fixed-source physical lower bound, convex regularity proofs,
and marked billiard reconstruction. The marked billiard theorem still
uses its word/lift roles, scalar-coordinate registrations and reference
atlas. The latent components recovered here are not silently renamed
as those semantic marks. The strengthened result is a single
observation-rigidity theorem with a specified selective detector family,
its exact gauges and obstructions, and its consequences for unpaired
regular generating sheets. The companion regimes retain their own
assumptions and proofs.
