\section{Honest inference and sharp observable signal classes}
\label{sec:statistics}
We first record the compact-quotient principle behind residual
confidence sets. It applies to the polynomial model as well as its
affine specialization.

\begin{lemma}[Residual inversion]\label{lem:modulus}
Let a compact parameter space have a continuous observation map $F$
into a finite product of probability simplices and a continuous target
$g$ in a compact metric space. For a true parameter $\omega$, put
\[
 \mathfrak m_\omega(t)=\sup\{d(g(\omega),g(\omega')):
                             \|F(\omega')-F(\omega)\|\le t\}.
\]
Then $\mathfrak m_\omega(0)=0$ exactly when the target is constant on
the exact fibre. In that case $\mathfrak m_\omega(t)\to0$ as
$t\downarrow0$. If $e=\|\widehat F-F(\omega)\|$, a minimum-residual
estimate has target error at most $\mathfrak m_\omega(2e)$.
The target image of a residual ball of radius $\rho$ about
$\widehat F$, when nonempty, has diameter at most
$2\mathfrak m_\omega(e+\rho)$ and contains the truth on $e\le\rho$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The zero-radius assertion is the definition. If continuity at zero
failed, compactness would give a limiting competitor in the exact
fibre with different target. A residual minimizer has residual at most
$e$, because the truth is feasible. The triangle inequality gives
observation error at most $2e$. Every point of the residual ball is
at observation distance at most $e+\rho$ from the truth, proving the
remaining assertions.
\end{proof}

For the affine experiment, take $n$ independent categorical draws at
each of the three clocks and put $N=3n$, $r=m_1m_2$. Let
$\widehat P_j$ be the empirical matrices. For $0<\zeta<1$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:confidence}
 \rho_{N,\zeta}=\sqrt{\frac{r}{2n}\log\frac{6r}{\zeta}},
 \qquad
 \mathcal C=\{\omega\in\cK_1:
          \max_j\|P_\omega(T_j)-\widehat P_j\|_F\le\rho_{N,\zeta}\}.
\end{equation}
Its target image is $\mathcal C_\xi$. If it is empty, report model
inconsistency and the empty set. No action is assigned by the confidence
procedure in that case. Coordinate extrema of the ordered spectra give
simultaneous bands whenever the set is nonempty.

\begin{theorem}[Risk, honesty, and adaptation]\label{thm:upper}
The set \eqref{eq:confidence} has coverage at least $1-\zeta$
uniformly over the entire closed model, including singular boundaries.
The empty set is reported explicitly, and its diameter is defined as
zero solely for the expected-diameter loss. For either
$\psi=\eta$ or $\psi=\sig_*$, and every true datum with $\psi(P)>0$,
the direct estimator of Theorem~\ref{thm:algorithm} satisfies
\begin{align}
 \E_P d_\infty(\widehat\xi,\xi)^2
      &\le C\min\{1,(N\psi(P)^2)^{-1}\},\nonumber\\
 \E_P\diam_\infty\mathcal C_\xi
      &\le C_\zeta\min\{1,(\sqrt N\psi(P))^{-1}\}.
                                                        \label{eq:stat-upper}
\end{align}
On the coverage event the diameter is at most
$C\min\{1,\rho_{N,\zeta}/\psi(P)\}$.
Neither signal nor either channel factor is supplied to the procedures.
At $\psi=0$ the statements are coverage and bounded-range loss, not
shrinking diameter.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over the $3r$ cell frequencies
give
\[
 \Pr\{e>\rho_{N,\zeta}\}
       \le6r\exp(-2n\rho_{N,\zeta}^2/r)=\zeta.
\]
An entrywise error bound $t$ gives a Frobenius bound $\sqrt r\,t$;
for binary readouts the factor is $2$, not $1$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:residue} and Lemma~\ref{lem:modulus} give the
eventwise assertions, and on every outcome with nonempty set give
$\diam\mathcal C_\xi\le C\min\{1,(e+\rho_{N,\zeta})/\psi(P)\}$.
Further,
\[
 \E e^2\le\sum_{j=1}^3\E\|\widehat P_j-P_j\|_F^2
       =\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^3
              \left(1-\sum_{a,b}(P_j)_{ab}^2\right)\le3/n.
\]
No exceptional outcome is discarded. The empty-set convention
preserves the diameter bound. Taking expectations proves its asserted
order. Squaring \eqref{eq:algorithm-affine} and using this second
moment proves the risk assertion; $\eta\ge c\sig_*$ supplies the
other signal. Compactness gives extrema of the residual region. For
fixed $n$ the categorical sample space is finite, so choosing
minimizers, singular bases and tie conventions causes no measurable
selection issue.
\end{proof}

Exact computation of $\mathcal C_\xi$ is an information-theoretic
construction; it is not implied to be efficient by the preceding
point estimator. Finite forward nets with mesh $r_0$ provide an
exhaustive approximation, with residual padding $Cr_0$ and action
padding $r_0$, by uniform forward Lipschitz continuity. This may be
exponential in dimension and resolution. For degree $d>1$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:modulus} and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} give the analogous
polynomial confidence modulus. The following sharp statements concern
the affine experiment.

\subsection{Two positive least-favourable families}
We give all constants in one lemma, so that the parameter class and the
testing displacement use the same admissible neighbourhood.

Let $u_0=m_1^{-1}\one$, $v_0=m_2^{-1}\one$. Choose unit zero-sum
vectors $e,f$ and, when $k>2$, orthonormal zero-sum vectors
$e_3,\ldots,e_k$ perpendicular to $e$ and
$g_3,\ldots,g_k$ perpendicular to $f$. The dimension assumption
$m_i\ge k$ gives these vectors. Fix a small constant $c_0>0$ and
interior distinct actions whose first pair is $a_0-b_0,a_0+b_0$,
with $b_0>0$, all other actions being above this pair. Set
$\alpha_b=1/k$. For $0\le t\le t_0<b_0/2$, let
$\lambda=1-t/b_0$ and put
\begin{align}
 \xi_{1,t}&=a_0-b_0+t,& \xi_{2,t}&=a_0+b_0-t,
       &\xi_{b,t}&=\xi_b\quad(b\ge3),\nonumber\\
 U_{t,:1}&=u_0+\frac{\epsilon_u e}{2\lambda},&
 U_{t,:2}&=u_0-\frac{\epsilon_u e}{2\lambda},&
 U_{t,:b}&=u_0+c_0e_b\quad(b\ge3),\label{eq:product-family}\\
 V_{t,:1}&=v_0+\frac{\epsilon_v f}{2\lambda},&
 V_{t,:2}&=v_0-\frac{\epsilon_v f}{2\lambda},&
 V_{t,:b}&=v_0+c_0g_b\quad(b\ge3).\nonumber
\end{align}
The pole $h$ is fixed along this family.

The second family fixes positive full-rank channels and positive
weights. Choose distinct real $z_b$ with $\sum_b\alpha_bz_b=0$ and
put, for an interior $a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse-family}
 \xi_b(t)=a+t+s z_b,\qquad 0\le t\le t_0,
 \qquad B=-sU\diag(\alpha_bz_b)V^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
Here $h_t=a+t$, while $A,B$ do not depend on $t$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform family constants]\label{lem:families}
There exist fixed $t_0>0$, $\epsilon_*>0$, $s_0>0$, a cell floor
$p_*>0$, and positive constants $c_1,C_1,C_2$, depending only on the
fixed experiment and the fixed choices just made, with the following
properties. Both families are admissible throughout these ranges,
all joint cell probabilities are at least $p_*$ for every
$T\in[T_1,T_3]$, and $d_\infty(\xi(t),\xi(0))=t$.
For either signal $\psi\in\{\eta,\sig_*\}$ and positive
$w=\epsilon_u\epsilon_v$ in \eqref{eq:product-family}, or $w=s$
in \eqref{eq:collapse-family}, uniformly on $[0,t_0]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-constants}
 c_1w\le\psi(P_t)\le C_1w,\qquad
 \sup_{T\in[T_1,T_3]}\|P_t(T)-P_0(T)\|_F\le C_2wt.
\end{equation}
In the product family both observed marginals are exactly fixed, and
\begin{align}
 P_t(T)-P_0(T)
  &=\frac{T-a_0}{2k(T-h)}\epsilon_u\epsilon_v
                              (\lambda^{-2}-1)ef^{\mathsf T},
                                                    \label{eq:product-identity}\\
 \kappa(\omega_t)&=\frac{\epsilon_u\epsilon_v}{2\lambda^2},
       &\sigma_k(A_t)&=\kappa(\omega_t)/k.       \label{eq:product-singular}
\end{align}
In the collapse family,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse-identity}
 P_t(T)-P_0(T)=\frac{tB}{(T-a-t)(T-a)}.
\end{equation}
If $w=0$, the respective family has exactly the same laws for all $t$
and all clocks in the interval, despite its changing action multiset.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $c_0,\epsilon_*$ small enough that every channel entry in the
product family is at least half the corresponding uniform entry.
Shrink $t_0$ and $s_0$ to keep every action in the declared interval
and to preserve order. The collapse family also has a fixed positive
cell floor, since its channels are strictly positive. These choices
precede all later choices of $N,w$ and the testing displacement.

In the first two terms of the product family the unnormalized sum is
\begin{align*}
 &(T-a_0+\lambda b_0)
       (u_0+\epsilon_ue/(2\lambda))(v_0+\epsilon_vf/(2\lambda))^{\mathsf T}\\
 &\quad +(T-a_0-\lambda b_0)
       (u_0-\epsilon_ue/(2\lambda))(v_0-\epsilon_vf/(2\lambda))^{\mathsf T}.
\end{align*}
Its constant part is $2(T-a_0)u_0v_0^{\mathsf T}$, its linear parts
are $b_0\epsilon_u ev_0^{\mathsf T}+b_0\epsilon_vu_0f^{\mathsf T}$,
and its interaction part is
$(T-a_0)\epsilon_u\epsilon_v ef^{\mathsf T}/(2\lambda^2)$.
Division by $k(T-h)$ proves \eqref{eq:product-identity}. Zero sums
of $e,f$ prove exact marginal cancellation.

The latent vector $(1,-1,0,\ldots,0)/\sqrt2$ is a right singular
vector of $U_t$ with singular value $\epsilon_u/(\sqrt2\lambda)$.
Its orthogonal complement maps, independently of $\epsilon_u$, into
the independent directions $u_0,e_3,\ldots,e_k$, all perpendicular to
$e$. Its least singular value is a fixed positive number. Choosing
$\epsilon_*$ smaller if necessary gives the stated smallest singular
value, and the same proof applies to $V_t$. In $A_t=U_tV_t^{\mathsf T}/k$
the pair direction maps $f$ to $(\kappa/k)e$; the complementary
rank-$(k-1)$ map is fixed and has its nonzero singular values bounded
below. This proves \eqref{eq:product-singular}.

Taking the marginals of $B$ gives components of magnitude
$b_0\epsilon_u/k$ and $b_0\epsilon_v/k$ in the $e$ and $f$
directions. Thus $\gamma\ge c\max(\epsilon_u,\epsilon_v)$ and
$\sig\asymp\epsilon_u\epsilon_v$. The actions are simple and both
channels have full rank when the two contrasts are positive.
Proposition~\ref{prop:factors}, including its rank argument for every
alternative exact representation, gives $\sig_* =\sig$.
Further, \eqref{eq:beta-bounds} and \eqref{eq:product-singular} imply
$\beta\asymp\kappa^{-1}$; hence $\eta\asymp\epsilon_u\epsilon_v$.
Since $\lambda$ stays in a fixed compact subinterval of $(0,1]$,
$\lambda^{-2}-1\asymp t$, uniformly on the chosen interval.

For the collapse family, subtraction of $A+B/(T-a-t)$ gives
\eqref{eq:collapse-identity}. The fixed channel ranks imply
$\gamma\asymp s$, while $\kappa$ stays bounded below. Simple-spectrum
uniqueness gives $\sig_* =\sig\asymp s$ for $s>0$.
The inequalities $c\sig_*\le\eta\le\gamma$ give $\eta\asymp s$.
The denominators in both exact identities have fixed positive bounds,
which gives \eqref{eq:family-constants} with one common set of
constants after taking minima and maxima. Both exact identities also
prove the assertions at $w=0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Adaptive experiments and the lower bounds}
An adaptive experiment consists of parameter-independent probability
kernels $\pi_i(dT\mid\mathcal H_{i-1})$ on $[T_1,T_3]$. Here
$\mathcal H_{i-1}$ contains all preceding clocks and categorical
observations, and any external randomization independent of the
parameter. At step $i$ sample the clock with this kernel and then one
categorical observation with law $P_\omega(T_i)$. Deterministic
predictable clocks and fixed designs are included. The complete
transcript, including clocks, is available to the estimator.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp classes and honest-diameter lower bounds]
\label{thm:statistics}
For either $\psi=\eta$ or $\psi=\sig_*$, there exists $s_*>0$
such that for $0<s\le s_*$ and $N=3n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimax}
 \inf_{\widehat\xi}\sup_{\omega:\psi(\mathcal P(\omega))\ge s}
       \E_\omega d_\infty(\widehat\xi,\xi)^2
                   \asymp\min\{1,(Ns^2)^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}
The direct estimator at the balanced three-clock design gives the upper
bound. The lower bound remains valid when the infimum includes every
adaptive experiment just defined.

If a random spectrum set $J$ is honest at level $1-\zeta$ over
$\cK_1$, with $0<\zeta<1/4$, then at the base point of either
positive family,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diameter-lower}
 \E_0\diam_\infty J\ge c_\zeta
               \min\{1,(\sqrt N\psi(P_0))^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}
This is true under any such clock strategy. The exact singular limits
of these families have nonvanishing risk and honest-diameter lower
bounds. Equal channel contrasts $\epsilon$ give the transition
$N\epsilon^4\asymp1$; full-spectrum spread $s$ with fixed channels
gives $Ns^2\asymp1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper bound follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:families} and its cell floor give, uniformly in the
clock,
\[
 \KL(P_t(T),P_0(T))
 \le\sum_{a,b}\frac{((P_t)_{ab}-(P_0)_{ab})^2}{(P_0)_{ab}}
 \le Cw^2t^2.
\]
The conditional clock kernel is the same under both parameters.
The relative-entropy chain rule therefore gives
\[
 \KL(\mathbb P_t^{(N)},\mathbb P_0^{(N)})
   =\sum_{i=1}^N\E_t\KL(P_t(T_i),P_0(T_i))\le CNw^2t^2.
\]
This equality includes the external randomization and the entire
transcript, so no fixed-design assumption is hidden in the bound.
Take $t=c\min\{t_0,(\sqrt Nw)^{-1}\}$ with a sufficiently small
fixed $c$. Pinsker's inequality makes the total variation at most
$1/2$. The spectra are distance $t$ apart. Turning an estimate into
the nearer of the two spectra gives maximal squared risk at least
$t^2/16$.

Here is the class bookkeeping. Fix once and for all the constants in
Lemma~\ref{lem:families}, choose $M\ge1/c_1$, and set
$w=Ms$, $\epsilon_u=\epsilon_v=\sqrt{Ms}$ in the product family.
Take $s_*\le\epsilon_*^2/M$. Then every $t\in[0,t_0]$, including
the testing displacement just chosen, has positive cell floor $p_*$,
admissible actions and weights, and $\psi(P_t)\ge c_1Ms\ge s$.
All constants in the entropy estimate remain unchanged. This proves
the lower bound on the exact class in \eqref{eq:minimax}; the collapse
family gives the other attained singular order.

For an honest set, transfer coverage at the alternative to the base
law. The union bound gives
\[
 \Pr_0\{\xi(0),\xi(t)\in J\}
       \ge1-2\zeta-\TV(\mathbb P_t^{(N)},\mathbb P_0^{(N)}).
\]
Decreasing $c$ depending on $\zeta$ leaves a positive fixed lower
bound. On this event the diameter is at least $t$.
The comparison $\psi(P_0)\asymp w$ proves
\eqref{eq:diameter-lower}. At $w=0$ use a fixed nonzero $t$;
all conditional laws agree at every clock, so the transcript laws are
identical for every policy. The same testing and coverage arguments
give the stated nonvanishing bounds.
\end{proof}

The two paths identify different losses of the observation map. The
first removes both first-order marginal changes and leaves a tensor
product of weak channel directions. The second keeps the channels
fixed and removes the whole projective contrast. Neither construction
charges a small gap between two otherwise visible actions. The results
are class-sharp and pathwise sharp as stated, rather than a complete
classification of all directional moduli at intersections of singular
strata.
