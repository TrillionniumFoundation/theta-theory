\section{The exact local metric condition}
\label{sec:local-condition}
The certificate $\eta_d$ controls a whole closed-model inverse problem.
It need not equal the sharp infinitesimal condition of an individual
datum. We now give that condition on a natural regular chart, including
semisimple collisions between roots of different component polynomials.
The two notions are thus related without being identified.

Let $P\in\mathcal R_d$ have strictly positive channel entries, strict
weight inequalities, pairwise distinct component polynomials, and simple
roots of each component in $(L,D)$. Roots from different components may
coincide. Use independent stochastic entries, $k-1$ weights and the
$kd$ nonleading polynomial coefficients as local coordinates $\theta$.
Write $F(\theta)=(\operatorname{vec}P(T_j))_j$ and
$J=D_\theta F$. Let $g(\theta)$ list the roots with continuous component
labels and increasing order within each component, and put $G=D_\theta g$.
On the observation space use the product Euclidean norm $\|\cdot\|_2$.

\begin{theorem}[Two-sided local conditioning]\label{thm:exact-condition}
The matrix $J$ has full column rank. Its Gram matrix and $G$ are
computable from $P$, up to coordinate changes and component permutation.
The exact local inverse condition is
\begin{align}
 \mathfrak c_2(P)
 &:=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\sup_{\substack{P'\ \mathrm{in\ the\ model}\\
                      0<\|P'-P\|_2\le\epsilon}}
       \frac{d_\infty(\Lambda(P'),\Lambda(P))}{\|P'-P\|_2}\nonumber\\
 &=\max_{a\le kd}\sqrt{\big[G(J^{\mathsf T}J)^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}\big]_{aa}}.
                                                        \label{eq:exact-condition}
\end{align}
The equality is invariant under all local coordinate changes. In
particular the supremal first-order upper bound is attained along a
smooth admissible path. If the within-component gaps are at least
$\nu$, then $\mathfrak c_2(P)\le C_\nu/\eta_d(P)$.

The same equality holds in any specified positive definite observation
metric $H$, with $J^{\mathsf T}J$ replaced by $J^{\mathsf T}HJ$.
For balanced categorical sampling the per-draw Fisher metric is
\[
 H_P=\frac1\ell\diag\left(\frac1{(P_j)_{ab}}\right)_{j,a,b}.
\]
Thus the Fisher version of \eqref{eq:exact-condition} is also a finite,
observable expression, rather than a supremum over exact latent fibres.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a linear combination of the coefficient matrices $C_j$ with
simple eigenvalues separating the $k$ distinct polynomials. Its
spectral projectors and their coefficient tuples vary smoothly nearby.
For a singleton group, lifting $E_fA_0$ into the original frames gives
\[
 Q_1E_fA_0Q_2^{\mathsf T}=\alpha_bU_{:b}V_{:b}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The sum of its entries recovers $\alpha_b$, and its normalized row
and column sums recover $U_{:b},V_{:b}$. This constructs a smooth local
inverse for all coefficient coordinates. The simple scalar roots also
vary smoothly by the implicit function theorem. Differentiating the
inverse identity proves that $J$ has full column rank.

This chart accounts for all sufficiently nearby model data, not just
one selected parameterization. If this failed, compactness would give
a different limiting representative of $P$. The normalization equation,
rank-$k$ leading matrix and joint projectors force that representative
to have the same polynomials and the same singleton rank-one matrices.
It therefore differs only by permutation. Relabeling and taking a
subsequence puts it in the displayed chart, a contradiction.

Let $v=\theta'-\theta$. Uniform differentiability gives
$F(\theta')-F(\theta)=Jv+o(\|v\|)$ and
$g(\theta')-g(\theta)=Gv+o(\|v\|)$. Since $J$ is injective,
$\|Jv\|$ bounds $\|v\|$ below. Within a group of equal roots of the
true aggregate multiset, every matched true value is the same number;
for nearby spectra its maximum displacement is exactly the maximum
of the absolute displacements of the continuously labeled roots in
that group. Distinct true root groups are separated. Consequently
\[
 d_\infty(\Lambda(P'),\Lambda(P))=\|Gv\|_\infty+o(\|v\|),
\]
even at cross-component collisions. The desired limit is therefore
$\sup_{v\ne0}\|Gv\|_\infty/\|Jv\|_2$. Each row is a linear
functional on the parameter space with Gram matrix $J^{\mathsf T}J$;
Cauchy--Schwarz gives precisely \eqref{eq:exact-condition}. A maximizing
row and vector give equality. Strict inequalities on the parameters
make the corresponding line a two-sided admissible smooth path.
The upper comparison follows by differentiating Theorem~\ref{thm:joint}.
The weighted assertion has the identical proof. For the last formula,
the categorical score in direction $v$ at clock $j$ is
$(Jv)_{j,ab}/(P_j)_{ab}$. Averaging its squared expectation over the
$\ell$ equally frequent clocks gives $v^{\mathsf T}J^{\mathsf T}H_PJv$.
\end{proof}

For clarity, an alternative derivative formula avoids differentiating
an eigenvector normalization. For an admissible observation tangent
$Z=(Z_j)$, put
\begin{align}
 r_Z&=\cN_P^{\dagger}\!\big[-(\Omega\otimes I)
                         (q(T_j)\operatorname{vec}Z_j)_j\big],
                                                        \label{eq:normal-tangent}\\
 \dot M_Z&=Q_1^{\mathsf T}I_d(qZ+r_ZP)Q_2.\nonumber
\end{align}
The first expression denotes the polynomial with that coefficient
vector. If the aggregate root $x$ is simple, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-tangent}
 \dot x=-\operatorname{tr}\big((\Res_{z=x}M(z)^{-1})\dot M_Z(x)\big).
\end{equation}
Indeed differentiating $\cN_Pa=b_P$ gives
\eqref{eq:normal-tangent}; differentiating a simple zero of
$\det M$ gives \eqref{eq:root-tangent}. At a cross-component collision
one instead differentiates the distinct joint coefficient tuples and
their simple scalar roots, as in the proof above. The trace of a
combined residue gives a sum of velocities, not the individual
velocities, and is not substituted for them.

The exact condition in \eqref{eq:exact-condition} is local and metric
specified. The certificate $\eta_d$ is global, one-sided and uniform
against singular competitors. Neither theorem equates $\eta_d$ to a
distance from a discriminant, and no pointwise statistical lower bound
for every estimator at a single fixed parameter is inferred from a
neighbourhood minimax assertion.
