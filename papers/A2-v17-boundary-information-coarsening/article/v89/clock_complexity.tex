\subsection{Functional complexity and a second sharp clock count}
The worst-case obstruction in Proposition~\ref{prop:clocks} is a
low-functional-rank obstruction, even when the latent capacity is
large. The following result determines a different sharp count for
curves whose numerator uses the whole scalar polynomial space.

\begin{proposition}[Full polynomial span]\label{prop:full-span}
Let the channels have full rank, and let
$s=\dim\operatorname{span}\{f_1,\ldots,f_k\}$. At any design,
\[
 \rank\cN_P\le\min\{d,(s-1)(\ell-d-1)\}.
\]
If $s=d+1$, every $d+2$ distinct admissible clocks give $\tau(P)>0$.
This count is necessary and sufficient on that full-span class.
For every $k\ge d+1$ the class contains interior real-rooted examples
with strictly positive full-rank stochastic channels.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The curve belongs to an affine matrix space of dimension at most
$s-1$, since its scalar functions are $f_b/q$ and their weighted sum
is one. Write it as $P_0+\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}w_i(T)D_i$. The residual
annihilates $r(T)P_0$, and each remaining scalar residual belongs to
an $(\ell-d-1)$-dimensional space. This proves the rank bound.

Now let $\ell=d+2$ and let $v$ span the one-dimensional orthogonal
complement of the degree-$d$ Vandermonde space. Each $v_j$ is nonzero:
otherwise its other $d+1$ coordinates would annihilate an invertible
square Vandermonde matrix. If $r\in\ker\cN_P$, applying the fixed
channel inverses shows that $\sum_jv_jr(T_j)f(T_j)/q(T_j)=0$ for every
$f\in\operatorname{span}\{f_b\}$. When that span is the whole
$\R[z]_{\le d}$, the vector $(v_jr(T_j)/q(T_j))_j$ therefore lies in
the same one-dimensional complement and equals $cv$. Thus
$r(T_j)=cq(T_j)$ at $d+2$ nodes. Polynomial identity and monicity give
$c=0$ and $r=0$. At $d+1$ clocks the residual projector is zero, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-fibre} gives actual nonidentification at interior
simple-root parameters. Finally choose a monic $F$ with simple roots
in $(L,D)$ and the $d+1$ polynomials
$F,F+\epsilon,F+\epsilon z,\ldots,F+\epsilon z^{d-1}$.
For sufficiently small nonzero $\epsilon$ they retain simple interior
roots and span the whole space. Extra components may repeat $F$.
Choose any positive full-rank stochastic channels and strict weights.
\end{proof}
