\subsection{One law and an unknown positive offset}

For an oblique marked polygon the calibration can be reduced further.
At phase $b$ let $d_b>0$ be fixed during the experiment but unreported;
retain just the one conditional law $f_{b,d_b}$.  The first derivatives
of its physical actions are known from the polygon:
$A_b'(0)=-p_b$, $C_b'(0)=p_b$.  They are not unknown curvature data.
The anchored quotient of this single law satisfies
\[
 \mathcal R_b(u,v)=\frac{f_{b,d_b}(u,v)f_{b,d_b}(0,0)}
 {f_{b,d_b}(u,0)f_{b,d_b}(0,v)}
 =1-\frac{A_b(u)}{d_b-A_b(u)}\frac{C_b(v)}{d_b-C_b(v)}.
\]
In particular $\partial_{uv}\mathcal R_b(0,0)=p_b^2/d_b^2$.
When $p_b\ne0$, the law determines its own offset; the marked slopes
then fix the remaining scalar ambiguity of the two factors.

\begin{theorem}[One-law determination without supplied offsets]
\label{thm:v72-main}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:v70-main}, suppose the marked
polygon is oblique at every phase.  One limiting endpoint law per
phase, at a fixed but unreported positive offset $d_b$, determines the
offsets and the complete smooth reflecting contact germs.  No second
offset, acceptance probability, flux amplitude or curvature is supplied.
On uniformly oblique bounded positive classes, the joint inverse obeys
\eqref{eq:v72-profile-stability} in the same finite $C^m$ law topology.
Independent finite preparations yield \eqref{eq:v72-rate}, with
$J=r$ groups, estimating both profiles and offsets with failures charged.
Every clear periodic polygon with at least three distinct physical
contacts is oblique, so the assertion applies to every such polygon.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:v72-clock} identifies
$d_b=|p_b|/\sqrt{\partial_{uv}\mathcal R_b(0,0)}$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v72-separation} separates both actual actions, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v72-rigidity} applies the smooth envelope inverse.
Its fixed-slice reconstruction is $C^m$-stable without an additional
derivative loss.  Theorem~\ref{thm:v72-sampling} gives the joint
finite-preparation statement, including its explicit budget and error
conditions.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v72-obliquity} proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

Here ``unreported'' means a fixed nuisance offset in a known positive
interval, possibly different at different phases.  The acceptance event
uses its actual value, and the success tag, itinerary label and endpoint
gate are still exact.  The theorem does not cover random clock drift or
uncertain gating.  The marked polygon and frames are still supplied.
At normal incidence the displayed mixed derivative vanishes; the
general two-offset theorem and the retained known-offset symmetric
inverse continue to apply under their respective hypotheses.

This consequence uses the linear physical momenta which an action gauge
must not erase.  It removes a second observation and numerical offset
calibration from the nonsymmetric problem while retaining the same
relative physical limit and actual functional inverse.  The unknown
normalization and endpoint amplitudes are eliminated algebraically;
that elimination alone would not prove either of the analytic interfaces.
