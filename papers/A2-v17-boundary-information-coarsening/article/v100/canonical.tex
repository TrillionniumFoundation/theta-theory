\section{The constrained weighted deformation}\label{sec:canonical}
We identify the object of Section~\ref{sec:joint} before discussing its
metric consequences. The datum, the weight filtration and the observation
norm are part of the object. A change of parametrization is not a change
of any of these three data.

Fix an identified nonrigid datum. Let $S$ be the germ at the origin of the
\emph{image} of the actual observation--coefficient relation
\[
 x\longmapsto (R(x),c(x)).
\]
All real and imaginary parts are included. The defining inequalities and
selected real branches are retained in this image. Set
$w=(q,\ldots,q,(p k_{r,a})_{r,a})$ when $\alpha=p/q$, and write
$D_\varepsilon y=(\varepsilon^{w_i}y_i)_i$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-deformation}
 \mathfrak D_w^+(S)=
 \overline{\{(\varepsilon,D_\varepsilon^{-1}y):
           \varepsilon>0,\ y\in S\}},\qquad
 T_w^+S=\{v:(0,v)\in\mathfrak D_w^+(S)\}.
\end{equation}
Closure is ordinary real closure, locally in the displayed coordinates.
Replacing a representative of the germ by a smaller representative does
not change its zero fibre. The superscript emphasizes positive real
specialization, not a new algebraic deformation operation.

\begin{theorem}[Comparison and real liftability]\label{thm:canonical}
The fixed-centre joint initial fibre satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-identification}
 \cI_0=T_w^+S\cap\{(U,V):\|U\|\le1\},\qquad
 \cC_0=\pr_V\cI_0.
\end{equation}
Thus it is the observation-unit section of the positive-real weighted
tangent relation. It depends on the relation germ, not on a resolution,
a square-slack presentation or a proper surjective presentation of that
relation.

Suppose an algebraic presentation of the relation has ideal $I$ over
$\R$, with its actual real branch conditions specified separately.
Form the algebraic weighted deformation by substitution
$y_i=\varepsilon^{w_i}v_i$ and saturation by $\varepsilon$.
Its zero-fibre ideal is the weight-initial ideal of $I$, with the
minimum-weight convention. The set $T_w^+S$ consists exactly of those
real points of this algebraic zero fibre which have a positive-real
semialgebraic lift satisfying the original branch conditions. Merely
taking all real points of the algebraic initial fibre can give a strictly
larger set, even when $I$ is real prime.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The forward inclusion in \eqref{eq:canonical-identification} follows
from the defining substitutions \eqref{eq:weighted}. For the reverse
inclusion take a sequence
$(\varepsilon_n,U_n,V_n)$ in the positive deformation with limit
$(0,U,V)$ and $\|U\|\le1$. If $\|U\|<1$, it eventually satisfies the
unit constraint. At a boundary point put
\[
 a_n=\max(1,\|U_n\|^{1/q}),\qquad
 \varepsilon'_n=a_n\varepsilon_n.
\]
Then $a_n\to1$, and the same unscaled relation point has coordinates
$U'_n=a_n^{-q}U_n$, $V'_{r,a,n}=a_n^{-p k_{r,a}}V_{r,a,n}$ at
$\varepsilon'_n$. These satisfy the unit constraint and have the same
limit. This proves the boundary inclusion, which need not follow by
interchanging an arbitrary intersection and closure. The coefficient
bounds in Corollary~\ref{cor:orders} make this section compact.
Image germs are unchanged by the stated surjective presentations, proving
the presentation assertion.

Algebraically, contract the ideal generated by
$f(D_\varepsilon v)$, $f\in I$, from
$\R[\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{-1},v]$ to $\R[\varepsilon,v]$.
This is precisely saturation by $\varepsilon$. For each $f$, divide
$f(D_\varepsilon v)$ by its lowest power of $\varepsilon$; its value
at zero is the minimum-weight part of $f$. Taking all $f\in I$, or a
weight-adapted Groebner basis, shows that the special-fibre ideal is
$\operatorname{in}_w I$. This is the usual associated-graded
construction, not an additional resolution theorem.

Every real limiting point satisfies the saturated equations. Conversely,
a real point is in the constrained zero fibre precisely when it is a
limit from the specified positive real part. Semialgebraic curve
selection supplies an arc in that part; its positive first coordinate
can be used as parameter after a finite Puiseux reparametrization.
This proves the liftability characterization. For strictness, take
$S=\{(x,y):y^2=x^3\}$ at the origin with weights $(1,1)$.
The saturated equation is $v_y^2=\varepsilon v_x^3$.
Its algebraic zero fibre has real points $v_y=0$, with either sign of
$v_x$. Positive real liftability instead requires $v_x\ge0$, and
every such point is realized. The ideal $(y^2-x^3)$ is real prime:
its coordinate ring embeds in $\R[t]$ by $(x,y)=(t^2,t^3)$.
Thus even real primality does not recover the missing accessibility
condition from the initial ideal alone.
\end{proof}

For weight one the algebraic construction is the tangent-cone instance
of the normal-cone construction; see \cite{Fulton98,StacksCone}.
Weighted initial ideals are treated in \cite{MS15}. These algebraic
objects do not, by themselves, specify the positive real liftable part
or the observation-unit section. Inner-metric tangent limits, as in
\cite{BL07}, retain different data: here one rescales a constrained
\emph{outer} relation and subsequently applies the specified root map.
No identification with an inner-metric tangent cone is asserted.

\begin{theorem}[Filtered naturality]\label{thm:filtered-naturality}
Let $\phi$ be a semialgebraic homeomorphism between two relation germs,
fixing the origin. Suppose, locally uniformly on bounded sets,
\[
 \phi_\varepsilon=D_\varepsilon^{-1}\phi D_\varepsilon
       \longrightarrow\phi_0,\qquad
 (\phi^{-1})_\varepsilon\longrightarrow\phi_0^{-1},
\]
where $\phi_0$ is a homeomorphism. Then
$T_w^+\phi(S)=\phi_0(T_w^+S)$. If the observation block of $\phi_0$
preserves its norm, the corresponding unit sections are also carried
onto each other. The same statement holds with the norm transported by
$\phi_0$. With the target decoding transported at the same time, the
leading spectral image and its diameter are unchanged.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a bounded convergent sequence of rescaled relation points $v_n$,
uniform convergence on a compact neighbourhood gives
$\phi_{\varepsilon_n}(v_n)\to\phi_0(\lim v_n)$. This proves one
inclusion of tangent relations. Apply the inverse hypothesis for the
other. Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical} then identifies their unit sections;
the last assertion is equality of the decoded images. For example,
weighted analytic coordinate changes whose invertible leading graded
map is $\phi_0$ and whose remaining terms have strictly larger weight
satisfy the stated convergence, as do their filtered inverses.
\end{proof}

The transport of the metric is essential. Equivalent observation norms
preserve optimal orders, but need not preserve a leading diameter.
Section~\ref{sec:valuative} gives this distinction inside the full binary
experiment, without adding a discrete experimental label.

\subsection{Exactly which scalar probes recover the joint set}
Write $C_t$ for the coefficient section scaled as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:joint}. For $a\in\R^N$ let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-probe}
 m_t(a)=\min_{v\in C_t}\|v-a\|^2.
\end{equation}
The dimension $N$ includes both parts of complex coefficients.

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic reconstruction and finite witnesses]\label{thm:reconstruction}
The limits $m_0(a)=\lim_{t\downarrow0}m_t(a)$ exist locally uniformly
in $a$ and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probe-reconstruction}
 m_0(a)=\dist(a,\cC_0)^2,\qquad
 \cC_0=\bigcap_{a\in\Q^N}
           \{v:\|v-a\|^2\ge m_0(a)\}.
\end{equation}
In particular these scalar probes, unlike their vanishing orders alone,
determine the joint coefficient set and hence its decoded spectral set.
For compact nonempty sets $C,D$ in a fixed compact ball $B$, and a finite
$\eta$-net $A\subset B$ (with an arbitrarily slightly enlarged ball if
rational points are required),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-probes}
 \max_{a\in A}|\dist(a,C)-\dist(a,D)|
 \le d_H(C,D)
 \le \max_{a\in A}|\dist(a,C)-\dist(a,D)|+2\eta.
\end{equation}
For algebraic input one quantifier-free formula with $a$ free describes
$m_0(a)$. Distinct initial fibres therefore admit an effectively
searchable rational probe with unequal limiting values.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For compact sets the distance functions differ uniformly by at most their
Hausdorff distance. Squaring is uniformly Lipschitz on bounded distance
intervals. Theorem~\ref{thm:joint} therefore proves the first assertion.
A point of $\cC_0$ satisfies every inequality in
\eqref{eq:probe-reconstruction}. If $v\notin\cC_0$, put
$d=\dist(v,\cC_0)>0$ and choose rational $a$ with $\|a-v\|<d/3$.
Then $\dist(a,\cC_0)>2d/3>\|a-v\|$, excluding $v$.

The left inequality in \eqref{eq:finite-probes} is the distance-function
bound. The Hausdorff distance is attained as $\dist(x,D)$ for some
$x\in C$, or with $C,D$ exchanged. At such an $x$ it equals
$|\dist(x,C)-\dist(x,D)|$. Each distance function is one-Lipschitz;
replacing $x$ by a net point proves the other inequality.

Finally, membership in $C_t$, minimization in
\eqref{eq:quadratic-probe}, and the limit are first-order formulas over
a real closed field, with $a$ free. Theorem~\ref{thm:effective} and
quantifier elimination yield one finite formula for their limit graph.
If $C\ne D$, the strict separating inequality above persists on an open
set of probe centres. Enumeration of rational points, with exact
comparison of the resulting algebraic values, must find a witness.
The enumeration concerns a witness only, not an approximation to the
whole set by scalar coordinate envelopes.
\end{proof}

All affine support values determine only a closed convex hull. Quadratic
distance probes determine the actual compact set, including its
nonconvex real faces. The reconstruction theorem thus gives a precise
sufficiency statement to accompany, rather than overstate, the
insufficiency statements of Sections~\ref{sec:separation} and
\ref{sec:valuative}.
