\begin{abstract}
We study unordered polynomial spectra observed through stochastic channels
and an unknown scalar normalization. The joint leading coefficient fibre
is identified with the observation-unit section of a positive-real weighted
tangent relation. We prove its presentation invariance, filtered
naturality and reconstruction from quadratic scalar probes; algebraic
initial ideals alone can fail to retain its real liftable part. Within the
full binary observation model, two experiments have identical observation
orders on every real analytic arc and identical orders for every polynomial
scalar function of the target coefficients, but different leading spectral
diameters. A relative real atlas supplies finite rational weights, Nash
leading diameters and compact-uniform power remainders. For finite exact
fibres, remote entrance orders also form finite rational lists in
fixed-format semialgebraic families, without a regular-corner hypothesis;
every rational order at least one is realizable as format varies.
Algebraic tests recover the regular linear regime and its second-order
coefficient. We retain the complete multiplicity law for the fixed-degree,
full-rank, coprime binary model, the exact rank-deficient cubic fibre, and
the distinct weight-wall and endpoint-wall mechanisms. These computations
separate local root instability from the entrance of a remote exact target.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A singular inverse problem involves more than the orders at which its
coordinates vanish. The real relations among their leading coefficients,
the norm imposed by observation and the possibility of remote exact
representatives all affect the feasible target set. We study these data
for a normalized polynomial experiment. Its target is an unordered
spectrum, so the object to be determined is a set of root multisets and
its bottleneck diameter, rather than independent coefficient bounds.

There are two distinct degenerations. Near an identified datum, multiple
roots require different weights on different polynomial coefficients.
Near an identification wall, a distant parameter representative can enter
the observation ball while the local inverse remains coercive. The
article develops a constrained weighted relation for the first phenomenon
and a finite-fibre entrance theory for the second. Their connection is
semialgebraic: both must retain real feasibility before taking their
metric output. Neither is determined by an unconstrained complex
algebraic specialization.

\subsection*{The joint relation and its scalar information}
At a datum with a unique exact target, write $c_{r,a}$ for the coefficient
of degree $a$ in the factor of the target belonging to a root of
multiplicity $m_r$. Put $k_{r,a}=m_r-a$ and
\[
 R_{je}=\sqrt{w_j}(\sqrt{F(x)_{je}}-\sqrt{P_{je}}),\quad
 G=\|R\|^2,\quad H_{r,a}=|c_{r,a}|^2.
\]
Real monomial orders give
$\alpha=\min_{r,a,i}b_{r,a,i}/(a_i k_{r,a})=p/q$, where only
accessible real divisors contribute. Hold the datum fixed and make the
simultaneous substitutions
\[
 R=\varepsilon^q U,\qquad
 c_{r,a}=\varepsilon^{p k_{r,a}}V_{r,a}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical} identifies the resulting initial fibre as
\[
 \cI_0=T_w^+S\cap\{\|U\|\le1\},\qquad
 \cC_0=\pr_V\cI_0,
\]
where $S$ is the actual real relation germ and $T_w^+S$ its positive-real
weighted tangent relation. The algebraic weighted deformation is the
usual saturated initial construction. What must additionally be retained
is its positive real liftable part and its specified observation-unit
section. The cusp $y^2=x^3$ already shows why taking all real points of an
algebraic initial fibre is insufficient.

Theorem~\ref{thm:filtered-naturality} proves invariance under filtered
changes of presentation, with the observation norm and target decoding
transported. Theorem~\ref{thm:reconstruction} supplies an exact converse
to indiscriminate scalar-insufficiency claims: the limiting minima of
$\|V-a\|^2$, for rational $a$, reconstruct the compact joint fibre.
Finite nets give quantitative witnesses for its Hausdorff distance from
another candidate. Thus specified scalar values can suffice even though
orders, or separate coordinate envelopes, do not.

The latter distinction holds inside the principal experiment, not only
in a disjoint-label example. Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-separation} gives
two interior full-rank binary experiments with all parameters unknown.
Their observation orders coincide along \emph{every} real analytic arc
in common parameter coordinates. The orders of every polynomial scalar
function of the target coefficients also coincide, as do their accessible
real divisorial order data. Their squared leading spectral diameters are
nevertheless
\[
 \frac{129609775953}{25280}\quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \frac{10058351786055}{1396384}.
\]
The proof gives the two exact profiled Fisher ellipsoids. It isolates
precisely the information lost on discarding positive leading units.
The sharp-marginal separation theorem is retained separately; neither
assertion denies reconstruction from the quadratic probes above.

\subsection*{Finite atlases and singular entrances}
Theorem~\ref{lem:relative} supplies a finite relative real monomial
presentation of the actual constrained graph. Its proof retains the
Rees base-change, finite-diagram spreading, real-cover descent and
accessibility arguments in Appendix~\ref{app:foundations}. The fixed-centre
Hausdorff theorem then gives
\[
 \omega_\theta(t)=C(\theta)t^\alpha+O_H(t^{\alpha+\nu}),
 \qquad C(\theta)=\diam\{(Z_r(V))_r:V\in\cC_0(\theta)\},
\]
uniformly on compact subsets $H$ of finite Nash strata. The exponent is
constant on each stratum and the positive leading diameter is Nash.
No leading set is assumed to be quadratic. Hardt triviality supplies
topological information; the finite-support and threshold arguments,
not triviality, supply the metric remainder.

For a remote representative $x_j$ of a finite exact fibre, define
$d_j(\theta,\delta)$ as its least observation distance from a specified
moving centre. Theorem~\ref{thm:singular-entrances} proves, with no
corner or derivative-rank hypothesis, the alternative of eventual exact
membership or
\[
 d_j(\theta,\delta)=a_j(\theta)\delta^{\rho_j}
                 +O_H(\delta^{\rho_j+\nu_j}),
 \qquad \rho_j\in\Q,\quad\rho_j\ge1,
\]
for a $C^1$ centre path. The exponents form a finite list for fixed
format. It also decides all semialgebraic critical thresholds by the
exact sign of $t-d_j$, including equality, and determines the limiting
union of feasible exact target spectra. This applies the general atlas
machinery beyond the direct regular-corner calculation.
Theorem~\ref{thm:entrance-realization} realizes every rational
$\rho\ge1$ in polynomial probability models as their format varies.
Proposition~\ref{prop:corner-criterion} derives the regular regime from
algebraic rank and cone-separation tests.

\subsection*{The complete binary and cubic laws}
The experiment has two degree-$d$ component polynomials with roots in
$[0,D]$, positive mixture weights and binary stochastic channels. With
coprime components, strictly positive invertible channels and at least
$2d+1$ exterior clocks, Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-atlas} determines the
whole-model leading set for every multiplicity and boundary pattern.
A repeated interior cluster has exponent $1/2$ and leading constant
\[
 C=\max_j2\sqrt{(m_j-1)/m_j}\,\kappa_j^{-1/4},
\]
where $\kappa_j$ is a projected Fisher-cone minimum. Otherwise the
exponent is one. These are fixed-degree, full-rank, coprime binary
assertions, with competitors in the entire closed model.

For the rank-deficient cubic family
$f=z(z-r)^2$, $g=(z-s)^3$, the exact fibre is governed by
\[
 B=(3D-r)s-2rD,\qquad
 E=(1-\alpha)(r+3s)(2r-3s)^2-4\pi r^3.
\]
The identifying locus is
$\{\det U\ne0\}\cup\{B<0\}\cup\{E<0\}$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-fibre} classifies the entire fibre. The inverse
through rank loss uses second-marginal recovery and robust pencil
isolation rather than division by $\det U$, $B$ or $E$.
Its local Fisher law remains coercive at both walls.

Theorems~\ref{thm:wall} and \ref{thm:geometric-wall} treat respectively
the weight wall and the endpoint wall. Their exact feasible charts are
different, but both have linear remote entrance and retain a finite
positive local square-root constant. The regular value expansion is
\[
 d_j(\delta)=\mu_j\delta+k_j\delta^2+o(\delta^2),\qquad
 k_j=\frac1{\mu_j}\min_{v\in M_j}
       \langle J_jv-b,Q_j(v,v)-c\rangle.
\]
The residual is common on the set $M_j$ of first-order minimizers;
uniqueness of its preimage is unnecessary. On the nonidentified side,
the weight-wall pencil interval opens linearly and may produce a
square-root spectral opening, whereas the endpoint-wall interval opens
quadratically and its exact spectral diameter remains $D-r$.
The algebraic critical examples and both signs of the general second
correction remain part of the article.

\subsection*{Relation to existing theories}
Resolution, real algebraic geometry and scalar valuative exponent
calculations are established inputs
\cite{BM88,BM97,BM08,BE09,BPR06,BCR98,ENV03,Ha26,Hardt80}.
We do not claim a new resolution algorithm. Normal cones and weighted
initial degenerations supply the algebraic comparison
\cite{Fulton98,StacksCone,MS15}; real liftability and the observation
section are identified explicitly in Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical}.
Subanalytic inner-metric tangent limits \cite{BL07} address a different
metric object from this weighted outer relation with a prescribed target
map. Variational geometry and optimization sensitivity
\cite{RW98,BS98,BS00} provide the setting for the regular-corner
calculation. Our singular entrance theorem removes its corner hypothesis
rather than claiming that metric projection is itself new.
Nonstandard statistical rates in finite mixtures \cite{Chen95} concern
estimation of a mixing distribution; the present deterministic
whole-model modulus also records a remote exact target at positive
spectral distance. Such an entrance must be established from its exact
fibre and is not implied by a local statistical rate.

The effective claims used for spectral and entrance outputs are the
direct first-order algorithms of Theorem~\ref{thm:effective} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:singular-entrances}. The supplementary geometric
certificate search is retained in Appendix~\ref{app:effective}; it is
not needed to execute these output algorithms and no complexity or
practical resolution implementation is claimed. Appendix
\ref{app:output-separation} makes this distinction explicit. Exact
arithmetic checks certify the displayed finite examples, not the
universal quantifiers of the theorems.
