\section{Return actions and reconstruction without convexity}
\label{sec:v80-intrinsic}

The deterministic part of the inverse can be stated without clocks.
We first identify which action is intrinsic to a return system, and then
separate an algebraic reconstruction of the dynamics from the problem
of observing its actions.  The symplectic composition law used below is
classical; see~\cite{MarsdenWest2001,LallWest2006}.  Its role here is to
specify exactly what is recovered, including the otherwise invisible
additive constants.

\begin{proposition}[The action of a Reeb return is its elapsed time]
\label{prop:v80-reeb}
Let a complete Reeb flow $\phi_t$ on a contact manifold $(M,\alpha)$
have a smooth transverse global section $i:\Sigma\to M$.  Suppose every
orbit meets the section in both time directions, the first-return map
$F$ is a diffeomorphism, and its first-return time $\tau\ge\tau_->0$ is smooth.
Put $\lambda=i^*\alpha$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-reeb-action}
                       F^*\lambda-\lambda=d\tau.
\end{equation}
The flow on $M$ is the suspension of $(F,\tau)$, with contact form
$dt+\lambda$ in flow coordinates and identification
$(x,t)\sim(Fx,t-\tau(x))$.  In particular the roof is fixed by the
contact form and section; it is not an additional freely chosen
observable.  Its $r$th return action is
\[
                 A_r(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\tau(F^i x).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Reeb equations imply $\mathcal L_R\alpha=0$ and $\alpha(R)=1$.
For $\Psi(x,t)=\phi_t(i(x))$ one consequently has
$\Psi^*\alpha=dt+\lambda$.  Differentiating the variable-time identity
$i(Fx)=\phi_{\tau(x)}(i(x))$ and evaluating $\alpha$ gives
$F^*\lambda(v)=\lambda(v)+d\tau(v)$ for every $v\in T_x\Sigma$.
This proves~\eqref{eq:v80-reeb-action}.
Every point has a unique representation with $0\le t<\tau(x)$,
apart from the endpoint identification.  Transversality and the smooth
return map give compatible local flow charts at those endpoints.  These
charts identify the suspension smoothly with $M$ and preserve the displayed
contact form.  Summing successive first-return times proves the last
formula.  The hypotheses that this is a global section, not merely a
local transversal, are used in this identification.
\end{proof}

The proposition is the standard geometric relation between contact
return time and exact symplectic action; compare~\cite{Hutchings2016}.
It applies to an existing Reeb system with the stated section.  In the
convex-twist construction of Section~\ref{sec:v79-suspensions}, the
unknown system is the particular suspension associated with $L$ and a
fixed offset $c$.  Its manifold and contact form vary with $L$.  These
are two uses of the same relation, not an assertion that the ordinary
time of an unsuspended discrete map is an action.

\begin{definition}[An exact map with its absolute action]
On a connected exact symplectic manifold $(\Sigma,d\lambda)$, an
\emph{action lift} is a pair $g=(F,A)$ with $F$ a diffeomorphism and
$dA=F^*\lambda-\lambda$.  The function $A$, not just its differential,
is part of the pair.  Composition and inversion are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-lift-group}
 (F,A)(G,B)=(F\circ G,B+A\circ G),\qquad
 (F,A)^{-1}=(F^{-1},-A\circ F^{-1}).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The chain rule verifies that these are again action lifts.  Associativity
follows either by substitution or from composition of the maps
$(x,t)\mapsto(Fx,t-A(x))$.  Thus the lifts form a group.  Constant
actions above the identity are retained in this group; discarding them
would discard absolute elapsed time.

\begin{theorem}[Coprime return actions determine the one-step lift]
\label{thm:v80-coprime}
Let $g=(F,A)$ be an action lift.  If $m,n$ are relatively prime positive
integers, the two lifts $g^m$ and $g^n$ determine $g$ uniquely.  For any
integers $u,v$ satisfying $um+vn=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-bezout}
                         g=(g^m)^u(g^n)^v.
\end{equation}
In particular, absolute type-I generating functions for the two
iterates, on regular charts covering their phase graphs, determine $F$
and $A$.  No convexity, minimizing property, or uniqueness of an
endpoint stationary path is required.

The same conclusion holds on a compact target if the supplied charts
cover every intermediate state needed by the finite word on the
right of~\eqref{eq:v80-bezout}, including its inverse factors.
On nested chart domains with fixed collars, uniform inverse-Jacobian
bounds and bounded $C^{k+2}$ generating functions, this reconstruction
is locally Lipschitz from $C^{k+2}$ action data to $C^k$ lifts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Powers of a single group element commute, and the group law gives
$(g^m)^u(g^n)^v=g^{um+vn}=g$.  Hence any other lift with the same two
powers must give the same right side.  This elementary identity also
specifies a reconstruction, rather than only uniqueness.

On a type-I chart with initial and terminal coordinates $(x,y)$, a
known absolute generating function $S_r(x,y)$ gives
\[
       p=-\partial_x S_r(x,y),\qquad
       q=\partial_y S_r(x,y),\qquad
       A_r(x,p)=S_r(x,y(x,p)).
\]
The nonzero determinant of $\partial_{xy}^2S_r$ gives the local phase
map and the action by the implicit function theorem.  Several stationary
sheets can occupy the same endpoint gate: they are distinguished by
their phase covectors.  The assumption that the charts cover the graph
of the actual deterministic iterate makes these phase maps agree on
overlaps.  It does not select arbitrary roots of unrelated stationary
functions.  They therefore specify $g^r$.  Apply the group formula.

For the compact statement only finitely many chart restrictions along
the prescribed word are used.  Differentiating the implicit equations
through order $k+1$ bounds these maps and their inverses by the stated
$C^{k+2}$ bounds and inverse-Jacobian margins.  Subtracting two implicit
equations gives their $C^k$ local Lipschitz estimates by induction on
$k$.  Products, inverses and compositions on the fixed nested domains
are locally Lipschitz in $C^k$ when their $C^{k+1}$ norms are bounded.
Applying these estimates to the finite word proves the claim.  The
collars ensure that nearby data are evaluated inside the same charts.
\end{proof}

For $m=n+1$ this reduces to $F=F^{n+1}(F^n)^{-1}$ together with
subtraction of the corresponding primitive.  This identity is not a
new group-theoretic rigidity phenomenon.  Its observational content in
this paper is that the \emph{absolute} lifts, rather than only their
canonical graphs, can be obtained from raw endpoint records with
unknown weights and incorrect positive labels.

\begin{proposition}[The arithmetic restriction cannot be dropped]
\label{prop:v80-coprime-sharp}
If $d=\gcd(m,n)>1$, there exist distinct positive-roof exact symplectic
return systems whose $m$th and $n$th action lifts agree.  The example
can be taken on a disk, with regular local type-I charts for the two
observed iterates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On a disk in the $(x,p)$ plane put
$\lambda_0=(p\,dx-x\,dp)/2$ and $h=xp/2$, so
$\lambda=p\,dx=\lambda_0+dh$.  Rotations $F_\theta$ preserve
$\lambda_0$.  Therefore
\[
 g_\theta=(F_\theta,c+h\circ F_\theta-h)
\]
is an action lift, and
$g_\theta^r=(F_{r\theta},rc+h\circ F_{r\theta}-h)$.
The lifts for $\theta$ and $\theta+2\pi/d$ have identical $m$th and
$n$th powers but different first maps.  Choose $c$ larger than the
oscillation of $h$ on the disk; both roofs are strictly positive and
have the contact suspensions of Proposition~\ref{prop:v79-contact},
whose proof applies equally on this invariant disk.  Choosing $\theta$
outside the finite forbidden angle sets makes the mixed-coordinate
Jacobians of both observed iterates nonzero.  They then admit the
claimed type-I charts.  No claim about a particular billiard subclass
is needed for this example.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Four raw laws for a covered Reeb return system]
\label{cor:v80-four-intrinsic}
In Proposition~\ref{prop:v80-reeb}, suppose the section has specified
canonical charts with $\lambda=p\,dx$, and the two relatively prime
return counts have a regular phase-graph cover as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-coprime}.  At each chart use two deadline trials
with a common normalized source and a common retention rule, with an
independent uniform external release delay.  Suppose the delay and
deadlines have strict residual margins on the chart.  If branch labels
are reported through the calibrated full-rank channels of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-channel}, the four raw endpoint laws per pair of
charts determine the return map and its absolute return time on the
covered target.  They therefore determine the section-preserving strict
contact suspension there.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write the $r$th return action in endpoint coordinates as $S_r(x,y)$.
Changing the initial momentum to the terminal coordinate contributes
$|\det\partial_{xy}^2S_r|$.  Integrating a uniform external delay of
length $D$ contributes $(T_j-S_r)/D$ on an admissible gate.  Thus the
raw subdensity is
\[
 D^{-1}e_r(x,y)\rho_r(x,-\partial_xS_r(x,y))
           |\det\partial_{xy}^2S_r(x,y)|(T_j-S_r(x,y)).
\]
No action is evaluated by the apparatus; this is the density of an
elapsed-time event in the given Reeb flow.  Deconvolving the tags and
using two raw deadlines recovers $S_r$ absolutely on every chart.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-coprime} and Proposition~\ref{prop:v80-reeb}
complete the reconstruction.
\end{proof}

The number four refers to two observations at each of two prescribed
return counts, not to a universal lower bound on every dynamical inverse
problem.  For example, when the first return itself is observed on a
complete regular cover, its two raw laws already determine its action
lift.  The corollary does not manufacture a regular cover across an
unobserved caustic, nor does it replace a local transversal by a global
section.  It does allow several nonminimizing stationary sheets whenever
the stated phase-graph coverage is available.
