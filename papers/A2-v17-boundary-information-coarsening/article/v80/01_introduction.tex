\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:v78-introduction}\label{sec:v80-introduction}

An endpoint experiment does not directly measure an action.  Its
successful records are selected by the source, the clock and the
detector; its branch labels may themselves be wrong.  A geometric
inverse must distinguish these losses of information before using a
generating function.  We study this problem for reflected trajectories
and exact symplectic return systems.  The central observation is a
raw endpoint measure whose density depends affinely on a known deadline,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-intro-pencil}
                       r_T(z)=a(z)(T-W(z)).
\end{equation}
Here $W$ is an absolute action, $a$ is an unknown positive weight, and
all unsuccessful preparations are retained as failure symbols.
The affine dependence is obtained by integrating a physical free-time
coordinate or an independent release delay.  It is not an extra
numerical value supplied with each endpoint.

There are two distinct inverse problems.  An archive containing only
the conditional densities $f_T=r_T/\int r_T$ has discarded the success
masses.  Three deadlines recover a nonconstant action in this archive;
two do not, because an unknown normalizing factor leaves a nontrivial
action gauge.  By contrast, a raw archive contains the masses as well.
Writing $\Delta=T_2-T_1$, its two successful subdensities give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-intro-inverse}
 a=\frac{r_{T_2}-r_{T_1}}{\Delta},\qquad
 W=\frac{T_1r_{T_2}-T_2r_{T_1}}{r_{T_2}-r_{T_1}}.
\end{equation}
This formula also recovers constant actions, has no anchor condition,
and loses no spatial derivatives.  The distinction matters whenever
a preparation budget is stated in raw launches: the failures being
charged contain information and need not be discarded by the estimator.

We combine this distinction with a finite calibrated channel for
incorrect positive branch labels.  On a common endpoint gate let
$r_{j,b}$ be the true raw subdensity of branch $b$, and let $g_{j,a}$
be the observed joint subdensity of reported label $a$.  A
class-dependent classifier has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-intro-channel}
                  g_j=C_j r_j.
\end{equation}
A calibrated left inverse recovers the component subdensities
\emph{before} any normalization.  The clock inverse then recovers the
absolute branch actions.  This allows false word or lift labels on
scientific records; it does not assume that every positive label is
correct.  It does require an exhaustive finite catalogue and a
calibrated full-rank channel.  These are measurable experimental
conditions, not conclusions inferred from a small fitting residual.

\begin{theorem}[Calibrated action rigidity]\label{thm:v80-main}
Consider a finite catalogue of regular branches on specified scalar
endpoint gates.  At two admissible deadlines on each acquisition block,
suppose the true raw endpoint measures have the form
$r_{j,b}=a_b(T_j-W_b)$ with unknown common positive weights, and the
reported labels have known full-column-rank channels $C_j$ as
in~\eqref{eq:v80-intro-channel}.  The full raw laws determine every
absolute branch action and raw weight, with a locally Lipschitz inverse
in every fixed $C^m$ norm on bounded classes with positive weight,
clock-gap and channel singular-value margins.

When the catalogue supplies a finite identifying atlas of regular
prefixes and one-flight extensions for a periodic dispersing billiard,
these laws determine the entire marked table and its lattice Gram
matrix up to a common Euclidean isometry.  When instead they supply a
phase-graph cover for two relatively prime iterates of an exact return
map, they determine its one-step map and absolute action on every
compact region covered by the associated finite inverse word.
The latter assertion needs neither convexity nor minimizing stationary
paths.

For the uniformly convex negative-twist class
\eqref{eq:v79-convex-class}, four fixed raw deadlines, two at each of
any prescribed consecutive return counts $n,n+1$, determine the unknown
two-variable generating function on a prescribed compact square.
The gates, source phase box, release interval and deadlines depend
only on the class bounds.  For $C^k$ reconstruction, fixed $C^{k+4}$
bounds on the generating function and $C^{k+2}$ bounds on source and
efficiency suffice for the uniform $C^{k+2}$ to $C^k$ estimate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The raw inverse and channel estimate are
Theorems~\ref{thm:v80-two-raw} and~\ref{thm:v80-channel}.
The absolute actions are the inputs of the prefix, distance and
registration proof in Sections~\ref{sec:v77-prefix}--\ref{sec:v77-coverage};
no conditional normalizing constants enter that deterministic proof.
The exact-map assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-coprime}.
The class-wide construction and its explicit derivative budget are
proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-four-convex}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Statistical and physical stability}

The geometric consequence persists when the channel must be estimated
and the clock weights are only approximately shared.  On a bounded
reference-atlas neighborhood, let $N$ be the scientific raw-launch
budget at each of $J$ settings.  Let $N_c$ be the raw calibration budget,
with true-component audit probability at least $p_c$.  For target
$C^k$ geometry put $m=k+2$, and assume $s\ge1$ additional density
derivatives for smoothing.  The estimator in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-finite-geometry} has, with high probability,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-intro-rate}
 d_k(\widehat{\mathcal T},\mathcal T)
 \le C\left\{
       \left(\frac{\ell}{N}\right)^{s/[2(m+s)+2]}
       +\delta^{s/(m+s+3)}
       +\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{N_cp_c}}+\epsilon\right\},
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is logarithmic in the budgets and inverse confidence,
$\delta$ bounds coordinate errors, and $\epsilon$ bounds
clock-dependent log-efficiency errors in $C^{k+2}$.
The number of raw preparations is $J(N+N_c)$, including unsuccessful
calibration attempts.  The constants explicitly depend on branch
weight and channel conditioning.  The estimator never obtains an
exact true branch label on its scientific trials.

The term $\epsilon$ has a physical, not only an abstract, interpretation.
A strictly interior endpoint-retention probability admits every small
smooth log perturbation as an actual Bernoulli retention rule
(Proposition~\ref{prop:v80-retention-tangent}).  Along a genuine smooth
deformation of a billiard, a ratio of its source--Jacobian factors and
its clock residuals produces valid retention probabilities for which
\emph{all raw record laws coincide}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-physical-compensation}).  An outward deformation
of one obstacle gives noncongruent tables separated by order
$\epsilon$.  Thus Corollary~\ref{cor:v80-physical-floor} proves that
the linear physical efficiency term in~\eqref{eq:v80-intro-rate} is
unavoidable for this apparatus class.  This is not a claim that the
sampling exponent for geometric loss is minimax optimal.

The conditional-law archive has a complementary, fully retained theory.
Common positive reweighting gives an ambient Banach quotient, and a
nonconstant two-anchor chart splits it smoothly into action and
functional residual.  The normal space is infinite-dimensional;
the assertion is a complemented split, not finite codimension.
The residual tests transverse misspecification but not perturbations
exactly confounded with another action.  On the specified unrestricted
H\"older weighted-action class, the conditional-record minimax $C^m$
action risk is
\[
          \asymp (\log n/n)^{(\beta-m)/(2\beta+d)}+\epsilon,
                              \qquad \beta>m.
\]
The theorem and both lower-bound arguments remain in
Section~\ref{sec:v79-statistics}.  It has a different observation
operator and parameter class from the physical geometric lower bound.

\subsection{Deterministic content and comparison with prior work}

The exact part of the reconstruction has two levels.  At the phase
level, an exact map together with its absolute primitive forms an action
lift.  Relatively prime iterates determine this lift by B\'ezout's
identity in the lift group.  This observation separates the deterministic
inverse from the clock experiment and allows several nonminimizing
endpoint sheets when their phase graphs are covered.  At the billiard
level, determinism identifies a prefix from equality of initial
covectors.  Its positive stationary Hessian gives a unique downward
matching zero, and subtraction yields a last-flight distance kernel.
A finite rank certificate reconstructs boundary arcs and registers
them through labelled overlaps.  The entire smooth, nonanalytic
whole-table proof is included, not replaced by the phase-level identity.

Generating-function composition, discrete Euler--Lagrange equations,
and symplecticity are classical ingredients
\cite{MarsdenWest2001,LallWest2006}.  Likewise positive exact actions
have classical contact realizations~\cite{Hutchings2016}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:v80-reeb} explains the converse interpretation
needed here: for an existing Reeb flow with the stated global section,
its return time is its exact symplectic action, and the flow is recovered
as that suspension.  The class-wide convex example is a separate
application in which the unknown suspension varies with $L$.
Neither the elementary composition identity nor construction of a
contact suspension is claimed as a new existence theorem.

In inverse billiards, the periodic and exterior observations are
materially different from the scalar gates used here.
B\'alint--De Simoi--Kaloshin--Leguil
\cite{BalintDeSimoiKaloshinLeguil2020} extract dynamical and geometric
information from marked lengths of open dispersing billiards.
De Simoi--Kaloshin--Leguil \cite{DeSimoiKaloshinLeguil2023} establish
analytic marked-length determination under their symmetry hypotheses.
Noakes--Stoyanov \cite{NoakesStoyanov2020} treat planar scattering and
travelling-time data.  Finamore--Leguil \cite{FinamoreLeguil} prove
finite-horizon Sinai rigidity from an enriched marked length spectrum.
Our exact comparison with canonical data is
Proposition~\ref{prop:v78-lens}: a scalar canonical graph and one
absolute action value determine a conditional clock-law orbit, and
conversely.  No global ordering of the different marks is inferred.

Calibrated matrix correction for class-dependent errors is also not a
new principle~\cite{Patrini2017}.  Mutual-contamination models with
unknown components and mixing proportions require additional
identifying hypotheses; see~\cite{KatzSamuelsBlanchardScott2019}.
Here the matrix acts on \emph{raw joint subdensities}, so correction
preserves precisely the masses needed for the two-deadline inverse.
The theorem couples that correction to absolute action and geometric
reconstruction, with explicit calibration cost and a physical
retention floor.  It does not assume that more deadlines separate an
arbitrary unknown mixture.  The rank counterexample in
Proposition~\ref{prop:v80-rank} records the exact distinction.

The rank-two and rank-five distance certificate used below is an
explicit sufficient reconstruction rule.  General generic rigidity of
complete bipartite frameworks is treated in

some conic arcs nor the presence of richer scalar labels proves a
necessity statement about geometric rigidity.

\subsection{Scope and arrangement}

The structural scalar atlas, word catalogue, coordinate transports and
lift conventions remain part of the data design.  For billiards the
uniform statement is on bounded neighborhoods of a reference atlas,
not on all unknown tables with a universal acquisition rule.  The
class-wide fixed protocol is proved for the explicitly bounded convex
twist class.  The Reeb statement requires a genuine global section and
the declared phase-graph coverage.  These hypotheses distinguish three
geometric consequences of the same raw action inverse.

Sections~\ref{sec:v80-raw} and~\ref{sec:v80-intrinsic} establish the
raw observation and deterministic action principles.
Sections~\ref{sec:v78-information}--\ref{sec:v79-statistics} retain
the conditional quotient and sharp action-risk theory.
Sections~\ref{sec:v77-prefix}--\ref{sec:v77-coverage} give the full
billiard proof.  The convex suspension, its regularity budget, and the
mass--potential subfamily follow.  The last sections combine raw costs,
physical nuisance and calibrated finite records.  The exact-label
conditional results are special cases with a different archive, not
assumptions silently imposed on the calibrated scientific records.

The periodic boundary-law inverse remains a separate companion.  Its
relative determinant normalization, positive quadratic inverse, signed
jets, smooth envelope, finite-preparation consequences and flat smooth
perturbations are retained without changes.  They are not invoked as
an unproved bridge between periodic boundary data and the endpoint
experiment studied here.
