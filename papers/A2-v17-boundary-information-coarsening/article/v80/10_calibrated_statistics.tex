\section{Finite raw experiments with audited calibration}
\label{sec:v80-finite}

We now estimate the quantities used by the raw inverse.  This section
counts unsuccessful preparations and audited calibration trials.  It
does not grant an exact classifier on the scientific sample.
Throughout, $Q_0\Subset Q_+\subset\R^d$ are fixed rectangles;
all reconstruction gates lie inside $Q_0$, and smoothing uses records
on $Q_+$.  There are finitely many acquisition blocks and two deadlines
per block; write $J$ for the total number of settings.

\begin{definition}[Audited raw calibration]\label{def:v80-calibration}
At each setting collect $N$ independent scientific raw trials and,
independently, $N_c$ calibration raw trials from the same acquisition
mechanism.  A calibration audit reveals the true component $b$ of a
record \emph{after} the physical gate and retention stages, as well as
its reported classifier label $a\in\{0,1,\ldots,A\}$.  The label $0$
is classifier rejection.  Trials not passing the physical stages are
also counted and return no component.  Every component has probability
at least $p_c>0$ of being available to this audit.  The only additional
audit output used is this true component, not a flight time, position in
Euclidean space, or other geometric measurement.
\end{definition}

The audit is an explicit additional measurement capability.  It can be
replaced by a known channel matrix.  Without an audit or another
identifying calibration condition, a channel matrix is not treated as
known merely because it appears in a model.  Since the channel is
independent of the endpoint given the true component, its column can
be estimated from these audited trials without knowing the component's
endpoint distribution.

\begin{lemma}[Calibration and subdensity estimation]
\label{lem:v80-raw-estimation}
Suppose the joint subdensities $g_{j,a}$ are bounded in
$C^{m+s}(Q_+)$, where $m\ge0$ and $s\ge1$ are integers.
Estimate $C_j$ by the reported-label frequencies within each audited
true component, including rejection in the denominator.  Estimate the
joint subdensities by
\[
 \widehat g_{j,a}(z)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N
       \one\{\text{reported label }a\}\,K_h(z-Z_i),
\]
where a failed trial contributes zero.  Here $K$ is a smooth compactly
supported kernel of order at least $s$, and recorded endpoints have
errors at most $\delta$.  Put
$\ell=\log(C_0J(N+N_c+2)/\zeta)$, with $C_0$ depending on the
fixed dimensions.  If $N_cp_c\ge C\ell$, then, with probability
at least $1-\zeta$, simultaneously at all settings,
\begin{align}
 \max_j\|\widehat C_j-C_j\|_{\rm op}
       &\le C\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{N_cp_c}},
                     \label{eq:v80-calibration-rate}\\
 \max_{j,a}\|\widehat g_{j,a}-g_{j,a}\|_{C^m(Q_0)}
       &\le C\left\{h^s+
          \sqrt{\frac{\ell}{Nh^{2m+d}}}
          +\frac{\ell}{Nh^{m+d}}
          +\delta h^{-(m+d+1)}\right\}.
                     \label{eq:v80-raw-density-rate}
\end{align}
The bandwidth is sufficiently small for the fixed collar and may be
chosen polynomially in $N$ as below.  An arbitrarily small deterministic
grid remainder is absorbed in the displayed bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The number of calibration trials having any fixed available true
component is binomial with mean at least $N_cp_c$.  Chernoff's
inequality and a union bound show that all these denominators exceed
$N_cp_c/2$, except on an event of probability at most $\zeta/4$.
Conditional on the denominators and available components, reported
labels have the corresponding channel-column law.  Hoeffding's
inequality and a union bound over the finitely many entries prove
\eqref{eq:v80-calibration-rate}, after conversion from entrywise to
operator norm.  The endpoint-independence assumption is used here.

For subdensity estimation no random success denominator is introduced.
For $|\alpha|\le m$, a derivative-kernel summand has envelope
$Ch^{-d-|\alpha|}$, variance at most $Ch^{-d-2|\alpha|}$, and
spatial Lipschitz bound $Ch^{-d-|\alpha|-1}$.  Failures contribute
zero and satisfy the same bounds.  Taylor expansion of $D^\alpha g$
and the kernel moments give bias at most $Ch^s$.  On a sufficiently
fine polynomial grid in $Q_0$, Bernstein's inequality and a union bound
control all derivatives, settings and labels by the second and third
terms of~\eqref{eq:v80-raw-density-rate}.  Interpolation between grid
points uses the Lipschitz bound; the grid can be chosen to make this
remainder smaller than $N^{-2}$, then absorbed.  These steps are the
usual uniform-kernel argument; compare~\cite{GineGuillou2002}.
Finally the mean value theorem for the derivative of $K_h$ bounds the
change caused by each coordinate error by
$C\delta h^{-d-m-1}$.  Averaging cannot increase this deterministic
bound.  Combining the exceptional events proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Calibrated finite-record geometric rigidity]
\label{thm:v80-finite-geometry}
Fix a bounded neighborhood of a finite identifying billiard atlas, with
the scalar labels, overlaps and all geometric margins specified in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v78-records}.  Partition the possible reported records
into finitely many acquisition blocks of the kind in
Section~\ref{sec:v80-raw}.  Across these blocks the catalogues include every prefix and extension
branch required by the atlas.  Within each block the catalogue is
exhaustive, and its two fixed deadlines are admissible on the larger
gates for every component.  A prefix and its extension need not belong
to the same block or share deadlines.  The normalized raw source and baseline endpoint retention
are shared by these two deadlines.  Allow false positive word or lift
labels through class-dependent matrices with
$\sigma_{\min}(C_j)\ge\sigma>0$.  Endpoint labels are otherwise
observed to coordinate accuracy $\delta$.

Set $m=k+2$, with $k\ge2$, and let $s\ge1$.  Suppose all component
weights have a positive raw floor $a_-$, the relevant actions, weights
and actual joint subdensities have the bounded regularity required
above, in particular $C^{m+s}$ for smoothing, and
\[
             r_{j,b}=a_b(T_j-W_b)e^{\eta_{j,b}},\qquad
             \max_{j,b}\|\eta_{j,b}\|_{C^m}\le\epsilon.
\]
Use the scientific and calibration budgets of
Definition~\ref{def:v80-calibration}.  For sufficiently small total
error there is an explicit estimator satisfying, with probability
at least $1-\zeta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-geometric-rate}
 d_k(\widehat{\mathcal T},\mathcal T)
 \le C\left\{
       \left(\frac{\ell}{N}\right)^{s/[2(m+s)+d]}
       +\delta^{s/(m+s+d+1)}
       +\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{N_cp_c}}+\epsilon\right\},\qquad d=2.
\end{equation}
Its output on this event is a smooth strictly convex disjoint periodic
table.  The number of raw preparations is exactly $J(N+N_c)$; every
failed launch and every failed calibration attempt is included.  The
constant depends on the fixed atlas, smoothness bounds, raw weight
floor, clock gaps and $\sigma$.  The calibration term and its cost are
omitted when the channel is known.

This is recovery with imperfect labels in a calibrated reference-atlas
experiment.  It is not a table-independent unmarked protocol, an
unsupervised estimate of the catalogue, or a minimax sampling-rate
claim.  The dependence on physical efficiency uncertainty is sharp
by Corollary~\ref{cor:v80-physical-floor}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose
\[
 h=\max\left\{(\ell/N)^{1/[2(m+s)+d]},
                   \delta^{1/(m+s+d+1)}\right\}.
\]
For sufficiently small total error this bandwidth fits inside the gate
collar.  At this choice the square-root and linear Bernstein terms in
\eqref{eq:v80-raw-density-rate} are bounded by a constant times the
first two terms on the right of~\eqref{eq:v80-geometric-rate}.
Indeed they decrease as $h$ increases, while $h^s$ is bounded by the
sum of those two terms; and the coordinate term is at most $h^s$.
The lemma supplies simultaneous density and channel errors.  Smallness
of the calibration term ensures $\|\widehat C_j-C_j\|\le\sigma/2$.

Apply~\eqref{eq:v80-channel-inverse} to the estimated \emph{joint}
subdensities, and then the two-raw formula to each recovered component.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-channel} gives $C^{k+2}$ action errors bounded
by the braces in~\eqref{eq:v80-geometric-rate}.  No exact positive
label was used on a scientific trial.  The error estimate compares the
reconstruction with the true physical actions, not with a possibly
misleading zero-residual fit.

Use the root and distance reconstruction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v77-cancellation}, the finite distance certificate,
trilateration and overlap registration, and the fixed patch blending
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v78-records}.  These maps are defined and locally
Lipschitz on an open neighborhood of the true finite-atlas data; their
intermediate inputs need not satisfy exact stationary identities.
Their fixed margins persist under the small $C^{k+2}$ action error.
They give~\eqref{eq:v80-geometric-rate}.  The same open $C^2$
conditions as in that theorem preserve strict convexity, disjointness
and the lattice in the blended output.  Defining a fixed reference
output outside the admissible reconstruction neighborhood gives a
measurable estimator everywhere.  Finally the sample count follows
from the raw-trial definitions, before any selection or rejection.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite records for the convex suspension]
\label{cor:v80-finite-convex}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-four-convex}, impose bounded
$C^{k+4+s}$ generating functions and bounded $C^{k+2+s}$ source and
efficiency functions on the fixed larger domains, with positive floors.
The same raw estimator, using $m=k+2,d=2$, has the bound
\eqref{eq:v80-geometric-rate} with
$d_k(\widehat{\mathcal T},\mathcal T)$ replaced by
$\|\widehat L-L\|_{C^k([-R,R]^2)}$.  Without label corruption it
uses four settings and $4N$ raw trials.  Calibration adds only the
explicitly declared settings and raw trials.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:v80-stationary-bounds}, with $m$ replaced by
$m+s$, supplies the actual subdensity regularity and positive weight
floors.  Apply the raw density lemma, the two-deadline inverse, and the
uniform prefix bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-four-convex}.  When a
calibrated channel is present, insert its left inverse as in the
preceding theorem.  No geometric sampling lower bound is invoked.
\end{proof}
