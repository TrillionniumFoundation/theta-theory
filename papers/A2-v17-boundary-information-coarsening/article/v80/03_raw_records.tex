\section{Raw endpoint measures and calibrated branch labels}
\label{sec:v80-raw}

A conditional law and a raw record are different data.  A raw trial has
an outcome in $Q\cup\{\dagger\}$, where $\dagger$ includes every
unsuccessful launch.  Its successful part is a subprobability measure,
not a probability measure.  If $p_j$ is the success probability and $f_j$
the conditional density, the observable subdensity is $r_j=p_jf_j$.
The normalization of the source is fixed before any gate, time, or
branch selection.  Changing the deadline does not renormalize this source.

\begin{theorem}[Two raw deadlines]\label{thm:v80-two-raw}
Let $T_1<T_2$, $\Delta=T_2-T_1$, and let $a,W\in C^m(Q)$ satisfy
$a>0$ and $W<T_1$.  Suppose the successful subdensities of two raw
experiments are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-raw-pencil}
                 r_j=a(T_j-W),\qquad j=1,2.
\end{equation}
Then the full raw laws determine both $a$ and the absolute action $W$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-two-inverse}
 a=\frac{r_2-r_1}{\Delta},\qquad
 W=\frac{T_1r_2-T_2r_1}{r_2-r_1}.
\end{equation}
This includes constant actions and requires no clock anchor.  On bounded
$C^m$ classes with $a\ge a_->0$ and $\Delta\ge\Delta_->0$, the inverse
is uniformly locally Lipschitz in $C^m$.  One raw deadline does not
identify $W$ when the positive weight is unknown, even locally in a
strictly admissible class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Subtracting the two identities in~\eqref{eq:v80-raw-pencil} gives
$r_2-r_1=\Delta a$; eliminating $aW$ gives the second formula.
For $C^m$ functions on a compact rectangle, multiplication is continuous
and the reciprocal map is smooth on the positive cone.  The lower bound
$r_2-r_1\ge\Delta_-a_-$ proves the stated stability, including its
extension to a neighborhood of the exact data.  For one deadline and
any sufficiently small nonzero $h\in C^m(Q)$, put
$V=W+h$ and $\widetilde a=r_1/(T_1-V)$.  These remain admissible and
have exactly the same subdensity and hence the same failure mass.
\end{proof}

Known exposure factors may be divided out before applying the theorem.
Unknown deadline-dependent exposure factors may not.  In particular,
applying~\eqref{eq:v80-two-inverse} to conditional densities $f_j$ in
place of $r_j$ is incorrect.  Proposition~\ref{prop:v78-two-clock}
exhibits precisely the ambiguity left by that loss of masses.  Thus the
two-raw and three-conditional inverse statements concern genuinely
different observation operators.

For three or more raw deadlines an exact model has a testable affine
residual.  With $\theta_j=(T_j-T_1)/\Delta$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-affine-residual}
             E_j=r_j-(1-\theta_j)r_1-\theta_jr_2.
\end{equation}
On the open set where~\eqref{eq:v80-two-inverse} gives $a>0$ and
$W<\min_jT_j$, all these residuals vanish if and only if all subdensities
have the same weight and action.  Indeed the first two subdensities
already give the unique affine pencil.  Additional clocks validate
this pencil; they are not necessary to fix its absolute scale.

\subsection{A finite channel for false positive labels}

We now allow incorrect positive branch labels.  In one acquisition block
there are $B$ possible true branches, with the same recorded endpoint
coordinate $z\in Q$, and $A\ge B$ reported labels.  These branches are an
\emph{exhaustive} list of the components which can produce a reported
record in the block.  A branch may represent a reflection word, a lift,
or a regular sheet of a return map.  Known coordinate transports can
first put its endpoint labels in this common gate.  The channel below
corrupts the branch label, not the endpoint coordinate.

For deadline $j$, let $C_j$ be an $A$ by $B$ nonnegative matrix with
column sums at most one.  Conditional on the true branch $b$ of a record
which has passed the physical gate and retention stage, its reported
label is $a$ with probability $(C_j)_{ab}$, independently of $z$.
The missing column mass is reported as failure.  This is a
class-dependent error model; it does not assume that a reported label
is usually correct.  Calibration specifies which column corresponds
to which structural branch.  It is not an unlabelled mixture model.

If the true raw subdensities are $r_{j,b}$, the observed joint
subdensities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-channel}
                    g_j(z)=C_j r_j(z).
\end{equation}
Both the frequency of each reported label and its conditional endpoint
law are used in $g_j$.  Rowwise normalization before inversion would
change~\eqref{eq:v80-channel} and discard necessary information.

\begin{theorem}[Calibrated recovery with false positive labels]
\label{thm:v80-channel}
Suppose $C_1,C_2$ have full column rank and the true subdensities satisfy
\[
          r_{j,b}=a_b(T_j-W_b),\qquad a_b>0,\quad W_b<T_1.
\]
Two raw deadlines determine every $a_b,W_b$, including their absolute
scales.  An explicit inverse is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-channel-inverse}
 r_j=C_j^\dagger g_j,\qquad
 C_j^\dagger=(C_j^TC_j)^{-1}C_j^T,
\end{equation}
followed by~\eqref{eq:v80-two-inverse} in each component.

For a quantitative statement fix $m$, bounded $C^m$ weights and actions,
$a_b\ge a_->0$, positive residual and clock-separation bounds, and
$\sigma_{\min}(C_j)\ge\sigma>0$.  Allow actual subdensities
$r_{j,b}=a_b(T_j-W_b)e^{\eta_{j,b}}$ with
$\max_{j,b}\|\eta_{j,b}\|_{C^m}\le\epsilon$.
If $\|\widehat C_j-C_j\|_{\rm op}\le\delta_C\le\sigma/2$ and
$\max_j\|\widehat g_j-g_j\|_{C^m}\le e$, the same inverse, using hats,
has, for sufficiently small $e+\delta_C+\epsilon$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-channel-stability}
 \max_b\bigl(\|\widehat W_b-W_b\|_{C^m}
             +\|\widehat a_b-a_b\|_{C^m}\bigr)
        \le C(e+\delta_C+\epsilon).
\end{equation}
The constant depends only on the displayed bounds, dimensions and
clock values; in particular it is allowed to deteriorate as
$\sigma$ or $a_-$ tends to zero.  No spatial derivatives are lost.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A left inverse in~\eqref{eq:v80-channel-inverse} recovers the true raw
components, and Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-two-raw} proves exact recovery.
Singular-value perturbation gives
$\|\widehat C_j^\dagger\|_{\rm op}\le2/\sigma$.
Writing $\widehat r_j=\widehat C_j^\dagger\widehat g_j$ and using
$\widehat C_j^\dagger\widehat C_j=I_B$ gives the useful exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v80-calibration-error}
 \widehat r_j-r_j
 =\widehat C_j^\dagger(\widehat g_j-g_j)
  +\widehat C_j^\dagger(C_j-\widehat C_j)r_j.
\end{equation}
Since these matrices do not depend on $z$, this identity holds after
every spatial derivative of order at most $m$.  Its norm is at most
$C\sigma^{-1}(e+\delta_C\max_j\|r_j\|_{C^m})$.
The $C^m$ algebra estimate gives
\[
 \|a_b(T_j-W_b)e^{\eta_{j,b}}-a_b(T_j-W_b)\|_{C^m}
                         \le C\epsilon.
\]
The two-raw inverse is defined on an open neighborhood of the exact
pencils; its denominator is bounded below there by
$\Delta a_-/2$.  Applying its local Lipschitz estimate proves the result.
Intermediate estimated subdensities need not be normalized or projected
onto a statistical model.
\end{proof}

For example the two-class channel with correct-label probability $1-q$
and incorrect-label probability $q$ has singular values $1$ and
$|1-2q|$.  Both $q<1/2$ and $q>1/2$ are identifiable when the channel
is calibrated.  The critical issue is separation of its columns, not
whether the names attached to the columns are more often right than wrong.

\begin{proposition}[Rank is an exact obstruction for raw action pencils]
\label{prop:v80-rank}
Suppose the same channel $C$ is used at all deadlines and has nontrivial
kernel.  In the unrestricted positive action-pencil class, arbitrarily
many raw deadlines need not determine the branch actions.  This remains
true with smooth strictly positive subdensities and strict residual
margins.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $0\ne v\in\ker C$, positive smooth $a_b$, and strictly admissible
$W_b$.  For a sufficiently small real $t$ define
\[
                 W_b^{(t)}=W_b+t\,v_b/a_b.
\]
Then $r_{j,b}^{(t)}=r_{j,b}-t v_b$ for every deadline.  Thus
$C r_j^{(t)}=C r_j$, including equality of the total reported masses
and of the failure probabilities.  Positivity and smoothness persist
for small $|t|$.  At least one action changes.  Choosing the original
subdensities to have total mass strictly below one also preserves the
raw probability constraint before classification.
\end{proof}

The proposition is a sharp boundary for this channel model, not a claim
that every rank-deficient channel destroys every special geometric
subclass.  It explains why adding clocks cannot replace branch
separation.  Conversely, Theorem~\ref{thm:v80-channel} removes exact
per-record word and lift classification while retaining a calibrated
finite catalogue.  It does not supply that catalogue, identify an
unknown number of branches, or absorb an omitted background component.
Those distinctions will remain explicit in the geometric theorem.
