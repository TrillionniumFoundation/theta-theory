\subsection{From relative limits to physical observation protocols}
The relative theorem compares complete physical experiments, not only
normalized densities.  For a fixed list of preparations,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-transfer} works on common unscaled endpoint and
residual-time coordinates, retains the failure atom and allows predetermined
physical windows $t_i$; the parameter-dependent excess
$d_i(\xi)=t_i-j_i g_e(\xi)$ is used only inside the proof.  The acquisition
rule therefore need not observe the unknown gap.

For adaptive designs and stopped procedures,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer} compares the entire transcript:
\[
 \|\mathsf P_\xi^\pi-\mathsf Q_\xi^\pi\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_\xi^\pi}
                 \sum_{i=1}^{T}Y_i\tau^{j(a_i)}.
\]
Every failure, successful record, chosen design and stopping time may be
retained, and the raw preparation count remains the physical cost.  A
deterministically capped policy stopping no later than its $k$th success has
comparison error at most $Ck\tau^J$ when all used flight numbers are at
least $J$.

Section~\ref{sec:v20-raw-physical} specializes these common-coordinate
results to the unknown-geometry local experiment.  Its main design uses the
parameter-independent physical times
\[
 t_{j,\ell}=jg_0+d_\ell
\]
and the multirate local coordinate
$g=g_0+\delta a/j$, $\vartheta=\delta h$.  Thus the unknown gap is part of
the likelihood rather than supplied to the policy.  Deterministic preparation
caps make the stopped policy satisfy the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer} literally; their exceptional
probability is exponentially small in the target success count.

A centered-window formulation is also available, but only after calibration.
Corollary~\ref{cor:v20-pilot-centered} uses the charged onset pilot of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration} and proves the precise Hellinger/Le Cam
smallness condition under which replacing $g$ by $\widehat g$ is negligible.
The pilot transcript and its preparation cost are not removed from the
physical protocol.
