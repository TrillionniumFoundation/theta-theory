\section{A nonconvex return system with three unresolved sheets}
\label{sec:v81-example}

The blind hypotheses can occur on actual generating sheets, not only on
abstract positive matrices.  The following example has no globally
convex generating function and uses no true-component audit.

\begin{proposition}[A three-sheet shear]\label{prop:v81-shear}
On $\R^2$ with $\lambda=p\,dx$, put
\[
 \phi(p)=p^3-p,\qquad F(x,p)=(x+\phi(p),p),\qquad
 A(p)=1+\tfrac34p^4-\tfrac12p^2.
\]
Then $(F,A)$ is an exact action lift with $A\ge11/12$.  There exist
compact endpoint rectangles on which its canonical graph has three
regular type-I sheets with positive, pairwise separated absolute
actions.  Two uncalibrated, conditionally independent three-valued
readouts can have strictly positive full-rank channels on all three
sheets.  The resulting clock experiment satisfies
Theorem~\ref{thm:v81-main}, including its projective version.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The inverse shear is $(x,p)\mapsto(x-\phi(p),p)$, and
$F^*\lambda-\lambda=p\phi'(p)\,dp=dA$.  Minimizing the quadratic
polynomial in $p^2$ gives $A\ge11/12$.  Write
$G(p)=p^4/4-p^2/2$ and $s=y-x$.  For sufficiently small $|s|$, the
equation $\phi(p)=s$ has three smooth solutions $p_-(s),p_0(s),p_+(s)$
continuing $-1,0,1$.  Define
\[
 S_b(x,y)=1+p_b(s)s-G(p_b(s)).
\]
Differentiation gives $\partial_sS_b=p_b(s)$, so
$-\partial_xS_b=\partial_yS_b=p_b(s)$ and
$\partial_{xy}^2S_b=-1/\phi'(p_b(s))\ne0$.  Substitution of
$s=\phi(p_b)$ gives $S_b=A(p_b)$, the actual absolute primitive.
At $s=0$, $S_0=1$ and $S_-=S_+=5/4$.  The derivative of
$S_+-S_-$ there is $2$.  Therefore on a sufficiently small compact
interval $0<s_0\le s\le s_1$, all three actions are separated.
Choose an endpoint product rectangle with $y-x$ in this interval.
The middle sheet has mixed derivative of opposite sign to the two
outer sheets.  These are regular parts of a folded endpoint projection,
not three minimizers of a globally convex action.

For any unknown nonzero constants $\kappa,\nu$, let the first reading
conditional on momentum $p$ have probabilities
\[
 \frac{(1,e^{\kappa p},e^{2\kappa p})^{\mathsf T}}
                   {1+e^{\kappa p}+e^{2\kappa p}},
\]
and give the second reading the analogous probabilities with $\nu$.
Use independent randomizations conditional on the phase point.  Given
an endpoint and its sheet, the momentum is fixed, so the two readings
are conditionally independent.  The channel columns at the three
momenta form a normalized Vandermonde matrix in $e^{\kappa p_b}$,
respectively $e^{\nu p_b}$.  Their determinants are nonzero; compactness
gives positive singular-value margins.  Every label has positive
probability on every sheet.  No reported label identifies a true sheet,
and the inverse need not know either constant or either response curve.
This is a genuine extra measurement mechanism, not a relabelling of
one common noisy output.

Choose one smooth normalized initial phase density positive on the
three compact phase patches, and a fixed positive retention.  The
positive-roof suspension of $(F,A)$ has actual first-return time $A$.
The delayed experiment of Proposition~\ref{prop:v81-common-clocks}
then supplies common clock windows and the matrix model on the chosen
rectangle.  All its action, weight and channel margins are positive.
\end{proof}

This example proves nonvacuity in a nonconvex multi-sheet system.  It
does not assert recovery through the fold points where $\phi'(p)=0$,
nor does it remove the independent-readout hypothesis.  In particular
it does not serve as a table-independent billiard acquisition theorem.

\paragraph{Geometric conventions for the billiard lower bound.}
For this lower bound fix an integer $k\ge2$.  With fixed scalar
parametrizations of the labelled quotient boundaries $R_a$ and a marked
lattice matrix $L$, use the distance
\[
 d_k(\mathcal T,\widetilde{\mathcal T})=
 \inf_{O\in\mathrm O(2),\,v\in\R^2}
 \max\left\{\max_a\|\widetilde R_a-(OR_a+v)\|_{C^k},
                     \|\widetilde L-OL\|\right\}.
\]
The fixed raw sources are smooth and positive on the compact initial
states feeding the observed gates.  Regularity bounds and positive
floors refer to these gates and larger fixed launch charts.  A change
of the physical embedding of the same scalar phase chart is a change
of geometry, not a change of its fixed source density.
In a lower-bound statement, a fixed post-retention readout means a fixed
Markov kernel of the retained component and scalar endpoint, including
fixed calibrated label channels.  It does not mean an additional sensor
which measures momentum, flight time or other geometric information
not contained in that retained statistic.  Such an additional observation
need not preserve the lower-bound equivalence.
