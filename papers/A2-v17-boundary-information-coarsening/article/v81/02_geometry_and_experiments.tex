\section{Unpaired generating sheets and exact return systems}
\label{sec:v81-geometry}

The blind inverse recovers functions without structural names.  For an
exact symplectic map, this is enough to recover its graph: the initial
covector identifies a phase point and hence pairs local sheets on an
overlap.  We give the precise domain assumption, since a missing phase
cover cannot be replaced by an algebraic identity.

Work in a fixed canonical phase space $X\subset\R^{2h}$ with
$\lambda=p\,dx$.  An action lift is a pair $g=(F,A)$ with
$dA=F^*\lambda-\lambda$.  Its product is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v81-lift}
 (F,A)(G,B)=(F\circ G,B+A\circ G),\qquad
 (F,A)^{-1}=(F^{-1},-A\circ F^{-1}).
\end{equation}
The chain rule proves closure; associativity follows by substitution.
An absolute type-I generating sheet $S(x,y)$ represents a part of a
lift by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v81-sheet}
 p=-S_x(x,y),\quad q=S_y(x,y),\quad A(x,p)=S(x,y).
\end{equation}
The mixed derivative $S_{xy}$ is invertible on a regular sheet.  Several
such sheets may occupy the same endpoint gate.  They need not minimize
an action, and they need not be convex.

\begin{definition}[Unpaired regular observation cover]
\label{def:v81-cover}
For each of two prescribed return counts $r=m,n$, finitely many endpoint
gates are specified in the fixed canonical coordinates.  On each gate
the actual iterate has a finite collection of regular generating sheets,
whose number and pairing with other gates are not supplied.  Their
phase graphs cover every point at which the iterates or their inverses
are used in a specified finite reconstruction word.  The union is the
graph of the actual deterministic iterate, with its actual absolute
primitive.  All endpoint gates and intermediate phase domains have
fixed collars.  This is a coverage and realizability hypothesis, not
observed sheet labels or observed phase covectors.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Reconstruction from unpaired projective records]
\label{thm:v81-unpaired}
Let $g=(F,A)$ be an exact lift and let $m,n$ be relatively prime positive
integers.  Suppose the two iterates have an unpaired regular observation
cover.  On each gate acquire the matrices of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v81-main}, with the latent actions equal to the absolute
generating sheets of that iterate.

Two raw clocks on every gate determine $g$ on the covered target.  Three
projective clocks suffice instead on gates having at least two separated
actions.  Single-sheet gates may use the two-raw protocol.  Neither
calibrated channels, true-component audits, a supplied sheet count, nor
cross-gate sheet pairing is required.  The conclusion includes the
absolute primitive, not only the canonical transformation.

For a $C^k$ target estimate, fixed $C^{k+2}$ bounds on the generating
sheets, inverse mixed-Jacobian margins, fixed nested phase domains and
the spectral margins of Theorem~\ref{thm:v81-main} give a locally
Lipschitz inverse from $C^{k+2}$ matrix data to the $C^k$ lift.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Recover the unordered functions $S_{r,b}$ on each endpoint gate by the
blind inverse.  Their derivatives in~\eqref{eq:v81-sheet} give the
union of local canonical graphs and their absolute primitives.
For any initial phase point in the covered domain, regularity gives
local solutions of $p=-S_x(x,y)$.  All solutions representing that phase
point have the same output and primitive: the data arise from the graph
of one actual action lift.  Thus overlapping recovered sheets agree as
lifts.  This reconstructs $g^m,g^n$ on the needed domains without an
external matching between sheet indices.  It does not choose a root
among unrelated stationary functions which are not known to represent
the same deterministic graph.

Choose integers $s,t$ with $sm+tn=1$.  Powers of one lift commute, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v81-bezout}
                       g=(g^m)^s(g^n)^t.
\end{equation}
The cover assumption includes all domains used by this finite word and
its inverse factors.  Equation~\eqref{eq:v81-lift} evaluates the right
side and recovers the primitive including its constant.  Any alternative
system with the same observations yields the same local actions, the
same two lifted iterates and hence the same right side.

For stability, the first stage yields $C^{k+2}$ action errors.  Implicit
differentiation of $p=-S_x$ gives $C^k$ estimates for the local maps and
primitives; bounded $C^{k+2}$ actions also supply the additional bounded
$C^{k+1}$ local maps needed for composition estimates.  Inverting the
maps uses their inverse-Jacobian bounds on the fixed collars.  A finite
cover and fixed smooth local extension and gluing operators give
nearby outputs even for data not lying exactly on the model.  The
finite products and inverses in~\eqref{eq:v81-bezout} are locally
Lipschitz in $C^k$ under these bounds.  A finite number of such estimates
proves the assertion.  We do not assert that arbitrary perturbed outputs
satisfy every exact symplectic identity.
\end{proof}

The group identity~\eqref{eq:v81-bezout} is elementary.  The content of
the theorem is that its inputs are recovered from unpaired projective
endpoint matrices, not handed to the observer as full group elements.
The phase cover remains assumed.  The theorem neither crosses an
unobserved caustic nor discovers a global section.

\begin{corollary}[Return time in an existing contact flow]
\label{cor:v81-reeb}
Let a complete Reeb flow on $(M,\alpha)$ have a smooth transverse global
section with smooth first-return map $F$ and positive return time $\tau$.
On canonical subdomains of the section, suppose the two return counts
satisfy Theorem~\ref{thm:v81-unpaired}.  Clocked endpoint observations
of those actual returns determine $(F,\tau)$ on the covered target and
therefore its section-preserving strict contact suspension there.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the section inclusion $i$ put $\lambda=i^*\alpha$.  The Reeb
identities $\mathcal L_R\alpha=0$ and $\alpha(R)=1$ give
$\Psi^*\alpha=dt+\lambda$ for $\Psi(x,t)=\phi_t(i(x))$.
Differentiating $i(Fx)=\phi_{\tau(x)}(i(x))$ gives
$F^*\lambda-\lambda=d\tau$.  Hence $g=(F,\tau)$ is an action lift,
and the elapsed time of the $r$th return is its $r$th absolute action.
The global section identifies the flow with
$(x,t)\sim(Fx,t-\tau(x))$, preserving $dt+\lambda$.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v81-unpaired}.  The clock realization used here
is proved in the next proposition.
\end{proof}

\section{Common deadlines and the boundary of detector invariance}
\label{sec:v81-physical}

There are two acquisition mechanisms in the article.  The original
billiard flow-box experiment requires a deadline inside both adjacent
free flights.  Its branchwise admissibility does not imply simultaneous
admissibility for a mixture.  An externally delayed, fixed-return-count
experiment has a different window and supplies common deadlines
without knowing the hidden word or sheet count.

\begin{proposition}[A common-clock construction without branch auditing]
\label{prop:v81-common-clocks}
Fix a return or collision count $r$ and a class-wide upper bound $H_r$
for its elapsed action on the observed regular gates.  Assume these
actions are nonnegative.  Choose $h>0$, a uniform external release delay
on $[0,D]$ with $D=H_r+4h$, and deadlines
\[
                     T_j=H_r+jh,\qquad j=1,2,3.
\]
For every branch in every such gate, independently of its name or
number, $h\le T_j-W_b\le D-h$.  With one normalized phase source
shared by the settings, the resulting endpoint subdensities before
readout have the form $a_b(T_j-W_b)$, with $a_b$ independent of $j$.
The same settings work throughout the bounded class.

For unit-speed finite-horizon billiards with free-flight bound $H$,
one may take $H_r=rH$ for a segment containing at most $r$ flights.
No grouping by the unknown reflection word is used in this clock
construction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Prepare an initial phase point from the same normalized source at each
setting, wait an independent uniform delay $\alpha\in[0,D]$, and record
the endpoint at the prescribed return count only when
$\alpha+W_b\le T_j$.  Conditional on that trajectory, the success
probability is $(T_j-W_b)/D$, because it lies strictly between zero and
one.  No numerical action is stored in the record; the event is an
ordinary elapsed-time threshold.  On a regular type-I sheet, change
initial momentum to terminal coordinate.  If the initial phase density
is $\rho$, the pre-readout density is
\[
 D^{-1}e_b(z)\rho(x,-\partial_xW_b(z))
                  |\det\partial_{xy}^2W_b(z)|(T_j-W_b(z)).
\]
Here $e_b$ is any baseline retention common to the clocks.  A further
branch-blind detector contributes the factor $c_j(z)$ of the projective
model.  The asserted inequalities follow immediately from
$0\le W_b\le H_r$.  Summing at most $r$ flights proves the billiard
bound.  The argument requires neither a branch classifier nor a count
of the regular endpoint sheets.
\end{proof}

This proposition proves simultaneous windows for the delayed protocol;
it does not silently change the physical meaning of the older
adjacent-flight experiment.  It does not prove that all return paths
are regular or have separated actions.  The spectral inverse still
requires a regular gate, full-rank independent readouts and the stated
separation.  Thus a common-window obstruction has been removed without
claiming a universal observation cover.

For the original flow-box experiment, a useful precise sufficient
condition is the following.  On a common compact gate, suppose
$\ell_b(z)=\min(\tau_b^-(z),\tau_b^+(z))$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v81-old-window}
 \max_b\sup_Q W_b+4h
             <\min_b\inf_Q(W_b+\ell_b).
\end{equation}
Then $T_j=\max_b\sup_QW_b+jh$, $j=1,2,3$, are simultaneously
admissible with positive margins.  Compactness makes this condition
persistent on a reference neighborhood.  The proof is substitution.
Absent this or another simultaneous-window condition, the old
calibrated-block theorem has an additional acquisition hypothesis;
branchwise admissibility alone is insufficient.

\begin{theorem}[A fixed-source branch-selective efficiency floor]
\label{thm:v81-fixed-source}
Fix a smooth periodic dispersing table with a finite regular identifying
atlas and strict margins.  Fix, once, normalized raw source densities
in common scalar phase coordinates, supported in compact launch charts
which remain physical under small deformations.  These source functions,
the lattice, endpoint labels and deadlines are held fixed.

If independently branch- and deadline-dependent retention is permitted,
then for all sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$ there exist noncongruent
tables at geometric distance at least $c\epsilon$ with identical full
raw laws and retention log changes of $C^{k+2}$ size at most $\epsilon$.
This holds for any fixed readout mechanism applied after retention.
Consequently any estimator, at any sample size, has worst-case expected
geometric error at least $c'\epsilon$ in this branch-selective apparatus
class.  The source law need not vary between the two parameter points.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a nonzero nonnegative smooth normal displacement $\varphi$ on
a small boundary arc of one obstacle.  Let the displacement be
$t\varphi$, $t\ge0$, keeping other obstacles and the lattice fixed.
For small $t$, strict convexity and disjointness persist; the obstacle
expands, so it cannot open a new free corridor.  Every margin of the
finite atlas persists by smooth dependence.  The area derivative is
$\int\varphi\,ds>0$.  Area is isometry invariant and locally Lipschitz
in the bounded $C^1$ boundary embeddings.  Hence the geometric distance
modulo Euclidean isometry is at least $c_0t$.

Here is a source choice which is literally independent of $t$.
For each launch chart choose a larger compact set of scalar phase
coordinates $(s,p)$ contained in the common physical domain for all
small $t$.  This is possible by nongrazing and compactness.  Choose one
smooth normalized density on that fixed coordinate domain, positive on
the required initial states, and one fixed delay or free-time density.
Push forward this same probability distribution to each physical table
by its scalar phase parametrization.  Its coordinate density and total
normalization do not change.  The physical map from a scalar phase
coordinate to position and velocity does change; this is part of the
geometric parameter, not a change in the experimental source law.

Let $W_b^{(t)}$ be the resulting action and $J_b^{(t)}$ the endpoint
Jacobian times this fixed source evaluated at the corresponding initial
momentum (and the fixed delay factor).  In the scalar billiard case,
\[
 J_b^{(t)}(s,y)=D^{-1}\rho(s,-\partial_sW_b^{(t)}(s,y))
                                  |\partial_{sy}^2W_b^{(t)}(s,y)|
\]
for the delayed protocol; omit $D^{-1}$ for the original flow box.
Finite regular stationary equations give smooth $C^{k+2}$ dependence
of these positive functions on $t$, after imposing the corresponding
finite derivative budget on the physical family.

Choose strictly interior baseline probabilities $e_b$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v81-fixed-compensation}
 e_{j,b}^{(t)}=e_b\frac{J_b^{(0)}}{J_b^{(t)}}
                    \frac{T_j-W_b^{(0)}}{T_j-W_b^{(t)}}.
\end{equation}
Positive denominator margins and smooth dependence imply that these
remain in $(0,1)$ and that
$\max_{j,b}\|\log(e_{j,b}^{(t)}/e_b)\|_{C^{k+2}}\le C_0t$.
Multiplying~\eqref{eq:v81-fixed-compensation} by
$J_b^{(t)}(T_j-W_b^{(t)})$ proves equality of every successful joint
measure.  The complementary failure mass is also equal.  A fixed
post-retention readout preserves equality of laws.  Taking
$t=\epsilon/C_0$ proves the geometric separation.  Under the two
parameter values any estimator has the same distribution; the triangle
inequality bounds the sum of its two expected errors below by the
parameter distance.  This gives the claimed lower bound.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:v81-fixed-source} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:v81-detector} describe different detector classes.
In the latter, one function $e_j(z)$ multiplies every latent component;
in the former, $e_{j,b}(z)$ may vary with the hidden branch.  The
compensator~\eqref{eq:v81-fixed-compensation} is generally of the second
kind.  It cannot be inserted into the first class to deduce an
impossibility theorem there.  This is a nonlinear identifiable versus
nonidentifiable distinction with the non-efficiency source fixed,
not a universal claim about all physical detectors.

\section{Finite observations without a calibration sample}
\label{sec:v81-statistics}

We state a sampling theorem for the new observation operator.  Its
purpose is to propagate actual readout and endpoint error through the
blind inverse; a sharp geometric minimax exponent is not asserted.
Let $Q_0\Subset Q_+$ be nested rectangles in $\R^d$.  There are $J$
settings and at most $uv$ joint readout cells.  At each setting obtain
$N$ independent raw trials, a failure contributing zero, or $N$
independent retained records with their joint conditional density.
These are distinct sampling budgets.  In the retained-record version
we do not count $N$ as the number of raw preparations.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-record blind action reconstruction]
\label{thm:v81-sampling}
Assume the uniform hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:v81-main} on $Q_+$,
including the observed scale, rank and separation margins.  Suppose
the observed joint densities have bounded $C^{m+s}$ norms, with
integers $m\ge0$, $s\ge1$.  Recorded endpoint errors have norm at most
$\delta$.  Let $\eta$ bound, in $C^m$, an additional joint-matrix
perturbation away from an exact admissible model.  It is not an arbitrary
total-variation contamination budget.

There is a locally defined estimator of the visible count and unordered
actions such that, with probability at least $1-\zeta$, the count is
correct above the fixed signal threshold and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v81-rate}
 \max_b\|\widehat W_b-W_b\|_{C^m(Q_0)}
 \le C\left\{\left(\frac{\ell}{N}\right)^{s/[2(m+s)+d]}
       +\delta^{s/(m+s+d+1)}+\eta\right\},
 \quad \ell=\log\frac{C_1Juv(N+2)}{\zeta},
\end{equation}
when the right side is sufficiently small.  Neither a calibration
sample size nor a true-component audit probability appears.
A common detector, however different at the clocks, belongs to the
exact model and contributes no $\eta$ term.

For Theorem~\ref{thm:v81-unpaired}, use $m=k+2$ in this bound, its
action regularity hypotheses, and $C^{k+2+s}$ bounds on the observed
densities.  If those density bounds are derived from physical generating
actions rather than assumed, $C^{k+4+s}$ action bounds and
$C^{k+2+s}$ source, retention, channel and exposure bounds are sufficient
on the fixed larger domains with the stated nondegeneracy margins.  The resulting bound
controls the $C^k$ recovered lift with the same braces.  The constant
also includes its finite phase-cover and inverse-Jacobian margins.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Estimate each observed matrix entry by a compactly supported smooth
kernel of order at least $s$:
\[
 \widehat N_{j,ab}(z)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N
             \one\{\text{readout pair }(a,b)\}K_h(z-Z_i).
\]
Failures give zero in the raw version.  The retained version estimates
the joint conditional density, not a separately normalized density for
each readout pair.  For $|\alpha|\le m$, the derivative summand has
envelope $Ch^{-d-|\alpha|}$, variance at most
$Ch^{-d-2|\alpha|}$ and spatial Lipschitz bound
$Ch^{-d-|\alpha|-1}$.  Taylor expansion gives $C^m$ bias at most $Ch^s$.
Bernstein's inequality on a polynomially fine grid, followed by the
Lipschitz bound between grid points, gives simultaneously
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v81-kernel-bound}
 \|\widehat N-N\|_{C^m(Q_0)}\le
 C\left(h^s+\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{Nh^{2m+d}}}
             +\frac{\ell}{Nh^{m+d}}+
                  \delta h^{-(m+d+1)}\right).
\end{equation}
The last term follows directly from the mean value theorem for the
derivative of the kernel at the perturbed endpoint.  A sufficiently
fine fixed polynomial grid makes its interpolation remainder smaller
than $N^{-2}$.  The classical kernel ingredients may be compared with
\cite{GineGuillou2002}.

Take
$h=\max\{(\ell/N)^{1/[2(m+s)+d]},
\delta^{1/(m+s+d+1)}\}$, small enough for the fixed collar.
The decreasing stochastic and coordinate terms are bounded by a constant
times the first two terms of~\eqref{eq:v81-rate}; the bias is bounded
by their sum.  Add $\eta$ by the triangle inequality.  Singular-value
thresholding first fixes the visible rank.  Equations
\eqref{eq:v81-alpha-beta} and~\eqref{eq:v81-rescale}, when needed,
then correct the exposures, and the blind inverse gives the displayed
bound by Proposition~\ref{prop:v81-stability}.

Use fixed chart choices on a small model neighborhood and define a fixed
reference output if the positivity, rank or separation tests fail.
This supplies a measurable estimator everywhere and the stated locally
smooth reconstruction on the high-probability event.  For the lift,
apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v81-unpaired}.  This proof does not infer a
minimax sampling rate from a deterministic Lipschitz estimate.
\end{proof}

The constants in~\eqref{eq:v81-rate} depend on $d,m,s,J,u,v$, the kernel,
the gate collar, density bounds, clock values and gaps, the minor and
two-plane singular-value margins, action separation, weight floors,
residual floors, observed rescaling floors and the fixed gluing cover.
The geometric specialization additionally depends on return counts,
phase collars and inverse mixed derivatives.  Deterioration near an
invisible branch or an action collision is not suppressed in a
``uniform'' constant.

A small extra component is handled as a matrix perturbation only when
its contribution is small in the norm just specified.  New components
above the rank margin can instead be discovered by the exact unknown-
count theorem, provided the enlarged channel pair remains full rank.
An arbitrary omitted component, or an arbitrary singular contamination,
is not covered by the smooth perturbation claim.

\section{The retained geometric and conditional-law developments}
\label{sec:v81-retained}

The rest of the full manuscript retains the calibrated inverse,
conditional-law quotient and action-risk theory, prefix cancellation,
distance certificates, finite coverage, convex-suspension inverse,
regularity budgets and finite-record billiard reconstruction.  These
results serve different observation regimes.  The new projective
multi-sheet theorem does not replace the single-sheet conditional
inverse, nor does it delete a marked geometric theorem merely because
its mark structure is stronger.

\subsection{Hypotheses for the calibrated companion}

Every calibrated theorem below uses a \emph{known finite exhaustive
catalogue}, with structural branch identities attached to the columns.
Its channels $C_j$ are independent of endpoint conditional on the true
component, and may differ between deadlines.  The new blind theorem
instead allows endpoint-dependent channels but requires their invariance
across clocks and requires two independent readouts.  Neither set of
hypotheses is silently transferred to the other.

For every audited-calibration statement below impose the following
explicit sampling design.  Calibration raw trials are independent of
the scientific trials and of one another.  Conditional on a trial having
passed the physical stages with true component $b$, its reported label
has column law $C_j(:,b)$ including rejection.  The audit-availability
indicator is conditionally independent of this reported label given
$b$.  A sufficient design is independent Bernoulli selection for audit
after retention, using a coin independent of endpoint and reported
label; its probability may depend on $b$.  Every component is available
with raw probability at least $p_c$.  Then, conditional on the audited
component counts, reported labels are independent samples from the
correct columns.  Chernoff and Hoeffding bounds used in the companion
are consequently unbiased.  Selecting audits by the reported label
without inverse-probability correction does not satisfy this design.

The original calibrated billiard blocks also require common transported
endpoint coordinates and simultaneous windows.  Condition
\eqref{eq:v81-old-window} is sufficient for the original protocol;
Proposition~\ref{prop:v81-common-clocks} supplies a different explicit
protocol at a fixed return count.  Neither an audit nor branchwise
admissibility proves those conditions by itself.

In particular the new latent indices are not automatically reflection
words.  In the retained billiard theorem those semantic roles are still
part of the marked experiment.  A universal table-independent stopping
rule, arbitrary caustic resolution, arbitrary missing components and
geometric sampling optimality are not consequences of the present
proofs.  The positive new conclusion is the blind projective recovery
of the action sheets and the exact common-detector quotient; the
retained assumptions on downstream geometry remain visible.
