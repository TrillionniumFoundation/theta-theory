\begin{lemma}[Harmonics for the full orthogonal group]
\label{lem:full-orthogonal-harmonics}
Let \(n\ge2\), \(\rho=x_1^2+\cdots+x_n^2\), and
\(\mathcal H_a=\ker(\Delta:\Sym^a\C^n\to\Sym^{a-2}\C^n)\).
For every \(k\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-orthogonal-fischer}
 \Sym^k\C^n=\bigoplus_{t=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
                  \rho^t\mathcal H_{k-2t}.
\end{equation}
Each \(\mathcal H_a\) is irreducible for the full complex group
\(O_n\), and distinct nonnegative \(a\) give inequivalent modules.
These statements remain true after tensoring every summand by the
same determinant character.  In particular they include \(n=2\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a harmonic homogeneous polynomial \(h\) of degree \(a\),
\[
 \Delta(\rho^t h)=2t(2a+2t+n-2)\rho^{t-1}h.
\]
Induction on \(k\) proves \eqref{eq:full-orthogonal-fischer}:
the formula makes \(\Delta\) an isomorphism from
\(\rho\Sym^{k-2}\C^n\) to \(\Sym^{k-2}\C^n\) after decomposing
the latter by the induction hypothesis.  Its complementary kernel
is \(\mathcal H_k\).

Here is a proof of the group assertion that also treats the disconnected
case.  Restriction to the real unit sphere gives \(\mathcal H_a\) a
positive definite \(O(n,\mathbb R)\)-invariant Hermitian inner product.
It is positive definite because a homogeneous polynomial vanishing
on that sphere vanishes on \(\mathbb R^n\), hence identically.
The stabilizer of \(e_1\) is the full \(O(n-1,\mathbb R)\).
A homogeneous polynomial of degree \(a\) fixed by this stabilizer
has the form
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}
 c_jx_1^{a-2j}(x_2^2+\cdots+x_n^2)^j.
\]
Indeed, expand in powers of \(x_1\); invariance makes each
homogeneous coefficient constant on the real spheres in the other
variables, and invariance under their simultaneous negation kills
its odd degrees.  The harmonic equation is the recurrence
\[
 2(j+1)(2j+n-1)c_{j+1}
       =-(a-2j)(a-2j-1)c_j.
\]
All denominators are nonzero, so the fixed subspace in
\(\mathcal H_a\) is one-dimensional.  This argument includes
\(n=2\), when the stabilizer is \(O(1)=\{1,-1\}\).

Let \(K_v\) represent evaluation at a point \(v\) of the sphere
under this inner product.  The vector \(K_{e_1}\) is nonzero,
as witnessed by the harmonic polynomial
\(\tfrac12((x_1+\sqrt{-1}x_2)^a+(x_1-\sqrt{-1}x_2)^a)\).
The \(K_v\) span \(\mathcal H_a\) and form one orthogonal-group
orbit.  If \(M\subset\mathcal H_a\) is invariant, its orthogonal
projection commutes with the group and sends \(K_{e_1}\) into its
one-dimensional stabilizer-fixed space.  Idempotence makes that
scalar zero or one.  The orbit spans, so the projection is zero or
the identity.  This proves irreducibility for the real compact group,
and therefore for its containing complex orthogonal group.

Finally, writing \(E=\sum_i x_i\partial_i\), the invariant operator
\[
 \Omega=\sum_{i<j}(x_i\partial_j-x_j\partial_i)^2
             =\rho\Delta-E(E+n-2)
\]
acts on \(\mathcal H_a\) by \(-a(a+n-2)\).  These eigenvalues
are distinct for \(a\ge0\) and \(n\ge2\), which proves
inequivalence.  Tensoring by a common one-dimensional character is
an invertible operation on representations.  In dimension two the
two nonzero \(SO_2\)-weight lines are exchanged by a reflection;
we have not asserted their irreducibility for \(SO_2\).
\end{proof}

The Fischer decomposition and harmonic irreducibility are classical;
compare \cite[\S1, equations (1)--(3)]{DeBieEelbodeRoels}.
The proof above supplies the full-group and low-dimensional conventions
needed here, rather than importing them from a stable-range statement.
