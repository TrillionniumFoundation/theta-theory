\section{Nine-dimensional variation in the K3 case}
\label{sec:moduli}
We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main-moduli}. Work on the standard chart of
$\Gr(4,\Sym^2\C^4)$ where the four square coefficients are the identity.
Write $x=(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)$ and order the six cross terms as
\[
 m=(x_0x_1,x_0x_2,x_0x_3,x_1x_2,x_1x_3,x_2x_3).
\]
For $C\in\operatorname{Mat}_{6\times4}$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chart-quadrics}
 q_i=x_i^2+\sum_{k=1}^6 C_{ki}m_k,\qquad
 J_C=(\partial q_i/\partial x_j),\qquad F_C=\det J_C.
\end{equation}
This parametrizes the relation space, not an additional choice of its
basis: the square coefficients fix that basis on this chart.

\begin{lemma}[An integral differential witness]\label{lem:differential}
At the relation system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-star}
 C_*=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 2&-1&2&0\\-2&0&-1&1\\2&-1&-2&0\\
 1&-1&2&1\\1&-1&0&0\\1&-2&1&-2
 \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
the differential of $C\mapsto[F_C]\in\Pj(\Sym^4\C^4)$ has
rank $24$. This relation system is basepoint-free and $V(F_{C_*})$
is smooth over $\C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\operatorname{cof}_{ij}(J)$ denote the signed cofactor. The
$24$ parameter derivatives are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobian-derivatives}
 G_{ik}=\left.\frac{\partial F_C}{\partial C_{ki}}\right|_{C_*}
       =\sum_{j=0}^3\operatorname{cof}_{ij}(J_{C_*})
                                    \frac{\partial m_k}{\partial x_j}.
\end{equation}
Order degree-four monomials by descending lexicographic order of their
exponent tuples, and form the $35\times25$ integral coefficient matrix
$D_*$ with columns $F_{C_*}$, followed by $G_{ik}$ with $i=0,\ldots,3$
outermost and $k=1,\ldots,6$ innermost. The determinant of its first
$25$ rows and all columns is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-differential-det}
 4279473148893659522379284480\ne0.
\end{equation}
Its residue modulo $101$ is $41$. Thus $F_{C_*}$ and the $24$
derivatives are linearly independent. Quotienting the target tangent
space by the line spanned by $F_{C_*}$ proves the differential assertion.

For completeness, Appendix~\ref{sec:certificates} specifies the full
coefficient recipe and two additional finite rank certificates. The
Macaulay matrix of the $q_i$ in degree five has rank $56$, the dimension
of all degree-five polynomials. The analogous matrix of the four
partials of $F_{C_*}$ in degree nine has rank $220$, again the entire
homogeneous piece. Explicit square minors are nonzero modulo $101$,
with residues $44$ and $79$. The integer matrices therefore have full
row rank over $\Q$. Every monomial of those degrees belongs to the
respective homogeneous ideal over $\Q$, and hence over $\C$.
A common projective zero is impossible. This proves basepoint-freeness
and smoothness without a numerical tolerance or a probabilistic test.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nine-image}
The Jacobian map on $G_4^\circ$ has a $24$-dimensional image in the
space of embedded smooth quartics. Its image in polarized quartic K3
moduli has dimension nine. Its general fibres after projective
coordinate equivalence are finite.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Changing a basis of $R$ multiplies the determinant by a nonzero scalar,
so the projective Jacobian map is well-defined. A change of coordinates
in $V$ acts equivariantly. Lemma~\ref{lem:differential} and the
$24$-dimensional domain show that its image has dimension $24$ and
that the map is generically finite onto that embedded image. The locus
where the differential is injective is nonempty and open; it is invariant
because the map is equivariant. The smooth admissible locus is
irreducible by Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-open}.

The projective stabilizer of a smooth quartic K3 is finite. Indeed,
contraction with its nonzero two-form identifies its tangent bundle
with its cotangent bundle, and $H^0(\Omega^1)=0$ by $H^1(\OO)=0$
and Hodge symmetry over $\C$. The stabilizer, a closed finite-type
subgroup of $\PGL_4$, therefore has zero-dimensional Lie algebra and
is finite. Its orbits have dimension $15$. Two smooth quartic
embeddings define the same polarized K3 precisely when they are related
by $\PGL_4$, because their embeddings are given by the complete
polarizing linear systems. The geometric quotient of the smooth
quartic locus gives the quartic K3 moduli space; equivalently one may
compute on a generic geometric quotient. The invariant image consequently
has dimension $24-15=9$ after quotienting. Standard background on
polarized K3 moduli is given in \cite[Chapter 5]{Huy}.

To check the finiteness assertion directly, take a general embedded
quartic in the $24$-dimensional image where the fibre is finite.
Every relation system mapping to a projectively equivalent quartic
can be moved by the corresponding projective transformation into that
finite fibre. Thus there are only finitely many projective equivalence
classes above the general polarized point. This argument also makes
clear that the assertion is generic, rather than a statement about
all exceptional fibres.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-moduli}]
Combine Proposition~\ref{prop:nine-image} with the recovery of the
polarized quartic in Theorem~\ref{thm:main-reconstruction} and the
algebra-orbit equivalence of Lemma~\ref{lem:algebra-orbits}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nonisotrivial}
There is a flat family of failure schemes on the corank-one open whose
canonical polarized invariant has a nine-dimensional image. In
particular the associated K3 surfaces are not isotrivial, and the
failure schemes are not all abstractly isomorphic. Their reduced
divisors become the same scheme after identifying the vector spaces
$S_R$ of rank six.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Flatness is Corollary~\ref{cor:relative-flat}. A fixed abstract K3
cannot give a positive-dimensional algebraic family of polarization
classes on that surface: its Picard scheme is discrete, since
$H^1(\OO)=0$. Thus the positive-dimensional polarized image also
implies non-isotriviality of the surfaces. Distinct values of the
canonical polarized invariant give nonisomorphic failure schemes by
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-reconstruction}. The graph description of
$\Delta$ in \eqref{eq:graph-delta} is independent of $R$.
\end{proof}
