\section{Quadratic relations and a relative ramification family}
\label{sec:relations}
Put $N=e(e+1)/2$ and $P=\Pj(V^*)$. Let $\mathcal H\subset
V\otimes\OO_P$ be the tautological hyperplane bundle. Contraction is
normalized by $c_\alpha(vw)=\alpha(v)w+\alpha(w)v$.

\begin{lemma}[Restriction and contraction]\label{lem:contraction}
There is a locally split exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\Sym^2\mathcal H\longrightarrow\Sym^2V\otimes\OO_P
 \xrightarrow{c}V\otimes\OO_P(1)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The cokernels of $\Sym^2\mathcal H\to S_R\otimes\OO_P$ and
$R\otimes\OO_P\to V\otimes\OO_P(1)$ are naturally isomorphic.
Their determinant ideals define the same degree-$e$ hypersurface
when nonzero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Locally choose $\ell$ with $\alpha(\ell)=1$. The decomposition
$\Sym^2V=\Sym^2H\oplus H\ell\oplus\C\ell^2$ identifies contraction
with zero, the identity, and multiplication by two on the three
summands, respectively. The twist accounts for rescaling $\alpha$.
Both cokernels are the quotient by $R+\Sym^2\mathcal H$; hence their
zeroth Fitting ideals agree. Finally, $\det c_R$ is a section of
$\det V\otimes(\det R)^*\otimes\OO_P(e)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:finite-map}
If $R$ is basepoint-free, its quadratic morphism
$\phi_R:P\to\Pj(R^*)$ is finite and surjective of degree
$2^{e-1}$. Its ramification Fitting scheme is $Y_R$ in
\eqref{eq:Y-intro}. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstorder}
 R\text{ is basepoint-free}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \gamma_R(HV)=S_R\ \text{for every hyperplane }H\subset V.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The pullback of $\OO(1)$ is $\OO_P(2)$. A positive-dimensional
projective fibre contains a curve on which this ample line bundle
would be trivial. Thus the proper morphism is quasi-finite, and hence
finite. Its image has dimension $e-1$ and is the whole target.
Intersecting $e-1$ pulled-back hyperplanes computes its degree.

For $J=(\partial q_i/\partial x_j)$, Euler's identity reads
$J(x)x=2(q_1(x),\ldots,q_e(x))^t$. On charts where a source coordinate
and a target coordinate are nonzero, choose the radial vectors first.
The radial differential is a unit; its quotient is the projective
differential. Removing that invertible block identifies the two
ramification Fitting ideals. The contraction matrix is $J^t$, up to
bases, so Lemma~\ref{lem:contraction} applies.

A functional on $S_R$ vanishing on $\gamma_R(HV)$ pulls back to one
vanishing on $R+HV$. If $H=\ker\alpha$, then
$(HV)^\perp=\C\alpha^2$. Thus such a nonzero functional exists exactly
when all $q\in R$ vanish at $[\alpha]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The universal smooth open]\label{prop:relative-open}
The set $G_e^\circ$ is nonempty, open, and $\PGL(V)$-invariant.
The determinant construction over it is a relative effective Cartier
divisor $\mathcal Y\subset G_e^\circ\times P$, proper and smooth
over $G_e^\circ$. It is obtained by descent from the frame bundle of
the universal quotient, and its formation commutes with arbitrary base
change. Each fibre is integral, with trivial canonical bundle; for
$e=4$ it also has $H^1(\OO)=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a $p$-dimensional space $S$ and set
$T=\Hom(\Sym^2V,S)$. Restriction over $T\times P$ to
$\Sym^2\mathcal H$ is a surjective evaluation map to the rank-$p^2$
bundle of square matrices. The incidence of corank at least two has
codimension four on each fibre over $P$, hence dimension
$\dim T+e-5<\dim T$. Its projection to $T$ is proper and has proper
closed image. Similarly, $HV$ has dimension $p+e-1$; nonsurjectivity
of its restriction has codimension $e$ and universal dimension
$\dim T-1$. Remove these two images and nonsurjective tensors.
Also require invertibility of the square restriction at one fixed
hyperplane. Each is a nonempty open condition on the irreducible
space $T$, so their intersection $T_1$ is nonempty.

On $T_1\times P$ the universal determinant section is nonzero on
every fibre. Since a nonzero homogeneous polynomial is a non-zero-divisor
on projective space over a field, its zero scheme $Z$ is a relative
Cartier divisor and is flat over $T_1$. More explicitly, apply the
fibrewise flatness criterion to
$0\to\OO(-e)\to\OO\to\OO_Z\to0$: the first two terms are base-flat
and the first map stays injective on every fibre. This also proves
that the same sequence stays exact after any base change.

The total space $Z$ is smooth over $\C$. Indeed, evaluation is a
submersion in the tensor directions, and the determinant hypersurface
of square matrices is smooth at matrices of corank one; the corank-two
incidence was removed. Every fibre is a nonempty degree-$e$
hypersurface. The generic fibre over $T_1$ is regular, since its local
rings are localizations of the regular local rings of $Z$. In
characteristic zero it is geometrically regular, hence smooth. The
nonsmooth locus of $Z\to T_1$ is closed, and its image is closed by
properness and misses the generic point. Deleting that image gives a
nonempty open with smooth fibres.

Surjections $\Sym^2V\twoheadrightarrow S$ form the frame bundle of
the rank-$p$ universal quotient on $\Gr(e,\Sym^2V)$. The conditions
of basepoint-freeness and smooth determinant fibre depend only on the
kernel. Their bad loci have closed proper projections from the
corresponding projective incidences. They therefore define the asserted
open downstairs. The determinant ideal, relative Cartier property,
and flatness descend along the faithfully flat frame bundle; flatness
with smooth geometric fibres gives smoothness. The cokernel and
Fitting constructions themselves commute with arbitrary base change.
The extra fixed-hyperplane condition above is used only to produce a
nonempty open in $T$; it is not imposed in the definition downstairs.

A smooth positive-degree hypersurface in $\Pj^{e-1}$ is integral here:
distinct positive-degree components would meet and give a singular
point, and repeated components are not smooth. Adjunction gives
$\omega_{Y_R}=\OO_{Y_R}$. The hypersurface sequence gives genus one
when $e=3$ and $H^1(Y_R,\OO)=0$ when $e=4$. Thus the latter is a K3
surface. Smoothness of the determinant also excludes corank at least
two in the square restriction, since all cofactors then vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:algebra-orbits}
The algebras $B_R$ and $B_{R'}$ are isomorphic exactly when $R$ and
$R'$ lie in the same $\PGL(V)$-orbit.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An isomorphism preserves the maximal ideal and its square. On their
associated graded spaces it induces an isomorphism of $V$ carrying
the kernel of quadratic multiplication to the other kernel. Conversely
such a linear isomorphism gives an isomorphism of the graded algebras.
Scalar matrices act trivially on relation subspaces.
\end{proof}
