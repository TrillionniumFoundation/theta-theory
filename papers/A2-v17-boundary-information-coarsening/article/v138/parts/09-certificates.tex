\section{An explicit system and exact certificate specifications}
\label{sec:certificates}
The rational system used throughout is the following. It is the same
relation system as in the preceding manuscript's exact smoothness
example, now used for a differential and a higher-corank calculation.
\begin{align*}
q_{0}&=x_{0}^{2} + 2 x_{0} x_{1} - 2 x_{0} x_{2} + 2 x_{0} x_{3} + x_{1} x_{2} + x_{1} x_{3} + x_{2} x_{3}\\
q_{1}&=- x_{0} x_{1} - x_{0} x_{3} + x_{1}^{2} - x_{1} x_{2} - x_{1} x_{3} - 2 x_{2} x_{3}\\
q_{2}&=2 x_{0} x_{1} - x_{0} x_{2} - 2 x_{0} x_{3} + 2 x_{1} x_{2} + x_{2}^{2} + x_{2} x_{3}\\
q_{3}&=x_{0} x_{2} + x_{1} x_{2} - 2 x_{2} x_{3} + x_{3}^{2}
\end{align*}
For a compact complete specification of its quartic, list the $35$
degree-four exponent tuples in descending lexicographic order, starting
with $(4,0,0,0)$ and ending with $(0,0,0,4)$. The coefficients of
$F=\det(\partial q_i/\partial x_j)$ in that order are the rows of
\[
\begin{pmatrix}-8 & -4 & -20 & 16 & 20 & 0 & -28\\-2 & -36 & 40 & 20 & -50 & -20 & -26\\72 & -6 & 0 & -66 & -6 & 30 & 4\\-14 & -4 & -6 & -12 & 28 & 18 & -56\\28 & 6 & -18 & 8 & -2 & 16 & 2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus this display, together with the fixed monomial order, specifies
every coefficient, including zeros. Alternatively, the determinant
of the displayed four quadrics is its coordinate-free recipe.

For any list of homogeneous forms $f_1,\ldots,f_s$ define the degree-$d$
Macaulay coefficient matrix $\mathcal M_d(f)$ as follows. Rows are
all degree-$d$ monomials in the same descending lexicographic order.
Columns are the forms $x^\beta f_i$, with $i$ outermost and all
$\beta$ of degree $d-\deg f_i$ in descending lexicographic order
innermost. The entry in row $\alpha$ is the coefficient of $x^\alpha$.
All entries for this example are integers.

The differential minor consists of the first $25$ rows and all columns;
its full integer value is \eqref{eq:exact-differential-det}.
The following deterministic rule specifies the other two minors without
printing large matrices. Reduce the displayed coefficient matrix modulo
$101$. Scan columns from left to right. At the next pivot step choose
the first remaining row with nonzero entry, exchange it with the next
pivot row, normalize that row, and eliminate below. Skip a column if
there is no nonzero remaining entry. Record the original row indices
in the order selected and the pivot columns. The square submatrix of
the \emph{original}, unreduced matrix with exactly these row and column
orders has the residue in the final column of the table. Full recorded
index lists, along with the coefficient-generating program, are included
in \texttt{evidence/K3\_CERTIFICATES.json} and
\texttt{checks/exact\_k3.py}.

Every step is finite-field arithmetic on integer data. A nonzero
residue of a specified square minor proves that integer minor is nonzero,
so it proves a rank statement over $\Q$, not merely a rank in one
numerical specialization. The Macaulay rank statements imply the
homogeneous ideal containments used in Lemma~\ref{lem:differential}.
The exact integer determinant is also recomputed independently with
rational-domain elimination. No floating-point rank, tolerance, or
unverified smoothness inference is used.

For the corank-two quotient in \eqref{eq:BC-witness}, one may reproduce
its quotient map by multiplying the six-dimensional quadratic quotient
by
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&-1/2&-1&1&0&0\\
 0&-1/2&-1&0&1&0\\
 0&-3/2&-2&0&0&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
This matrix annihilates the three columns of $\Sym^2H$ and has rank
three. It yields the displayed $B_H,C_H$ and the quartic
\eqref{eq:h-witness} by ordinary multiplication. The factor $2$ in
the coefficient of $uv$ is essential: it records the square of
$u x_2+v x_3$, not a divided-power convention.

An additional exact regression test uses the normalized matrix
\eqref{eq:residual-matrix} with
\[
 C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&0\\0&1/2&-3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then $h(u,v)=uv(u-v)(u-2v)$, so the four ideals are
$(a,b)$, $(c,d)$, $(a-c,b-d)$, and $(a-2c,b-2d)$.
Elimination computes both sides of \eqref{eq:main-coranktwo} over
$\Q$, and mutual Gr\"obner reduction verifies their equality. It also
checks that $\delta^2$ is nonzero, that every element of
$\mathfrak m\delta^2$ vanishes, and that $\delta^3=0$.
This test checks a concrete instance; the general coefficient-ring
proof is Lemma~\ref{lem:cone-ideal} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:coranktwo-proof}, not an extrapolation from that instance.
