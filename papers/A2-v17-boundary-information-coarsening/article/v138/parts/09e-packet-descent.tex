\subsubsection{Descent of the closed packets}

A packet is a closed subscheme on the original base whose geometric
irreducible components are a full conjugacy orbit of tracked associated
points.  Its ideal, rather than its finite image as a set, is the object
that must be constructed.  We give an effective descent construction in
characteristic zero and a power-certified construction in arbitrary
characteristic.  The latter retains the noetherian-base generality of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded-principal-colon-stratification}; it does not
require a packet to be geometrically reduced.

\begin{lemma}[Effective Galois descent of an orbit ideal]
\label{lem:galois-packet-descent}
Let \(D\) be a noetherian domain with fraction field \(K\) of
characteristic zero.  Let \(A\) be a finite type \(D\)-algebra.
After shrinking \(\Spec D\), a finite Galois extension \(L/K\)
with group \(G\) can be modelled by a finite etale \(G\)-torsor
\(D'/D\).  Put \(B=A\otimes_DD'\).  Suppose that a finite
\(G\)-stable set of generic primes has closures \(P_i\subset B\).
For a full orbit \(O\), set \(I'_O=\bigcap_{i\in O}P_i\).
There is a unique ideal \(I_O\subset A\) with its natural inclusion
in \(A\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit-ideal-descent}
 I_O B=I'_O,\qquad
 I_O=\{a\in A:a\otimes1\in I'_O\}.
\end{equation}
If the \(B/P_i\) have nonempty geometrically integral fibres, a
further shrinking makes every geometric fibre of \(V(I_O)\)
exactly the reduced union of the intended components.  The orbit-ideal
identity and the fibrewise intersection identity persist after every
base change on this open.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a finite \(D\)-subalgebra of \(L\), include the conjugates
of its generators, and invert one nonzero element of \(D\).
The resulting algebra \(D'\) is finite free of positive rank.
The generic Galois isomorphism
\[
 D'\otimes_DD'\longrightarrow\prod_{g\in G}D',
 \qquad b\otimes c\longmapsto(b\,g(c))_g
\]
is an isomorphism after another shrinking: its kernel and cokernel
are finite \(D\)-modules with zero generic fibre.  The torsor
identities, or equivalently this isomorphism together with faithful
flatness, give a finite etale \(G\)-torsor.  No normalization or
excellence hypothesis on \(D\) is used.

Schematic closure commutes with each automorphism \(g\), so
\(g(P_i)=P_{g i}\) and \(g(I'_O)=I'_O\).  On the double overlap
\(\Spec B\times_{\Spec A}\Spec B\), identify the two pullbacks
of \(I'_O\) on the component indexed by \(g\) by the restriction
of this semilinear action.  On a triple overlap the two successive
identifications are multiplication of the group elements.  They
therefore equal the direct identification.  This is the cocycle
condition, as an identity of maps of ideal sheaves, not just of their
supports.  The inclusion \(I'_O\hookrightarrow B\) is equivariant.
Effective fpqc descent for quasi-coherent modules, together with full
faithfulness, descends this inclusion to a submodule of \(A\)
\cite[Tag 023T and Section 35.6]{StacksDescent}.  Multiplication
preserves the submodule after the faithfully flat extension, hence
before it; the descended submodule is an ideal.  Faithful flatness
also proves both identities in \eqref{eq:orbit-ideal-descent}.

To identify the fibres, include the actual exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intersection-diagonal-descent}
 0\longrightarrow B/I'_O\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{i\in O}B/P_i\longrightarrow C_O\longrightarrow0
\end{equation}
in the finite diagram and flatten \(C_O\) and its terms over
\(D'\).  After arbitrary base change the diagonal map stays
injective.  Consequently the extended ideal \(I'_O\) is the
intersection of the extended \(P_i\); this conclusion would not
follow from flatness of the individual prime quotients alone.  On a
geometric fibre each \(P_i\) is prime, so this intersection is
radical.  Distinctness is protected by the noncontainment witnesses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-primary-models}.  The torsor over an
algebraically closed field is a disjoint union of copies of that field.
Pulling the descended packet to any one of these copies identifies
its fibre with precisely this intersection.  Thus no additional
component is introduced downstairs.  All subsequent pullbacks of
\eqref{eq:orbit-ideal-descent} remain equalities; the flattened
sequence gives the asserted intersection compatibility.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Packets without a separability hypothesis]
\label{lem:power-certified-packets}
Let \(D\), \(K\), and \(A\) be as above, without a restriction
on the characteristic.  A full orbit of geometric generic associated
points has a closed packet on \(\Spec A\), after shrinking the
base, with exactly those geometric fibre components supplied by the
split primary models.  These packets and their intersections can be
refined and flattened on the original base.  Their component
identification remains valid after every base change in the final
stratum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathfrak q\subset A_K\) be the contraction of a member of
the orbit.  Geometric components over \(\mathfrak q\) form one
conjugacy orbit: over the maximal separable subextension this is the
usual transitivity of primes in a Galois extension, and purely
inseparable extension is a universal homeomorphism.  The associated
point formula for extension of the ground field identifies these
components with the tracked geometric associated points
\cite[Section 31.7]{StacksRelativeAssassin}.

Construct the ideal on the original ring first:
\[
 Q=\ker(A\longrightarrow A_K/\mathfrak q).
\]
This is the schematic-closure ideal and is finite because \(A\)
is noetherian.  Take a common finite splitting extension \(L/K\)
and a finite faithfully flat model \(D'/D\) as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-exact-diagram}.  On \(B=A\otimes_DD'\),
let \(P_i\) be the closures of the generic geometric primes in
this orbit and put \(I'=\bigcap_iP_i\).  On the generic fibre
\[
 \sqrt{\mathfrak q A_L}=\bigcap_i(P_i)_L.
\]
Finite generation therefore gives an integer \(n\ge1\) with
\((I'_L)^n\subset\mathfrak q A_L\subset I'_L\).
Clear the finitely many denominators in these two inclusions.  After
shrinking on the original base, they become actual ideal inclusions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packet-power-sandwich}
 (I')^n\subset QB\subset I'.
\end{equation}
As before, a shrinking needed upstairs is made downstairs by deleting
the finite image of its bad closed complement.  Finite images are used
only to shrink a base on which an ideal has already been constructed;
they are not used as a substitute for descent.

Flatten the diagonal sequence
\(0\to B/I'\to\bigoplus_iB/P_i\to C\to0\), and arrange the
nonempty geometrically integral fibres and distinctness of the
\(P_i\) by the split primary models.  For every geometric fibre
\(\Omega\), the intersection identity and
\eqref{eq:packet-power-sandwich} now give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packet-exact-geometric-components}
 \sqrt{(QB)_\Omega}=\bigcap_i(P_i)_\Omega.
\end{equation}
Indeed, products of finitely generated ideals commute with extension
of scalars, while the flattened diagonal sequence supplies exactly the
intersection identity that is needed here.  The right side is an
intersection of distinct primes.  A geometric point of \(\Spec D\)
has a lift to \(\Spec D'\) over the same algebraically closed
field: the nonzero finite fibre algebra has a residue field equal to
that field.  At this lift, the fibre of \(A\) and that of \(B\)
are the same algebra.  Hence \eqref{eq:packet-exact-geometric-components}
identifies the components of the packet \(V(Q)\) on the original
base itself.  This argument does not assert that a nonreduced
inseparable fibre is reduced or that a rational local length is
unchanged by inseparable extension.

Schematic closures glue on affine overlaps.  In a finite affine
cover, use also a finite affine refinement of the overlaps and one
common shrinking.  A closure whose generic restriction to a chart is
empty has empty restriction after shrinking, by constructibility of
the image of that finite-presentation restriction.  Thus the chartwise
component statements glue without an untracked boundary component.
For any finite collection of packets, their scheme-theoretic
intersections are defined downstairs by sums of their ideals.
Flatten these finitely many quotient algebras and the packet algebras
on the original base.  Sums commute with base change, and all previous
exact sequences and inclusions survive this further restriction.
Stratifying their images and fibre dimensions fixes nonemptiness and
dimensions of their incidences.  This proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The torsion module on the original base]
\label{prop:descended-packet-torsion}
Let \(I\subset A\) be any one of the packet ideals just constructed
and let \(M\) be a finite \(A\)-module.  On a nonempty open of
the integral base there are a finite submodule \(T\subset M\)
and an integer \(n\) such that, after every base change on this
open,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:downstairs-torsion}
 T=(0:_M I^n)=H^0_I(M).
\end{equation}
At the generic point \(p\) of each geometric component of the
packet, \(T_p=H^0_{p\mathcal O_p}(M_p)\).  After common
refinement with the split primary models, these geometric local
lengths are constant and equal on the components of each packet.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the generic fibre choose \(n\) for which
\(T_K=(0:_{M_K}I_K^n)=H^0_{I_K}(M_K)\) and set
\(Q_K=M_K/T_K\).  There is no associated prime of \(Q_K\)
containing \(I_K\); otherwise an associated element would give
nonzero \(I_K\)-torsion.  Prime avoidance gives an element
\(h_K\in I_K\) regular on \(Q_K\).  If \(Q_K=0\), omit
this map.  Spread the submodule, the relation \(I^nT=0\), and
\[
 0\to T\to M\to Q\to0,\qquad h:Q\longrightarrow Q.
\]
Flatten the quotient \(Q\) and the cokernel of the second map.
After every base change the first injection and regularity of \(h\)
remain true.  An element killed by a power of \(I\) has image in
\(Q\) killed by a power of \(h\), hence has zero image.  This
proves \eqref{eq:downstairs-torsion} downstairs, with no descent
assertion about a chosen primary decomposition.

At a minimal prime \(p\) of a geometric packet, the radical of
\(I\mathcal O_p\) is the maximal ideal \(p\mathcal O_p\).
Zeroth local torsion depends only on this radical, which proves the
local equality.  On the splitting cover apply the finite power
filtrations and free generic presentations of
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-new-packet-torsion} to every component.
They give a constant integer for each component.  Conjugate generic
points have equal lengths, so the integers agree within an orbit.
At a geometric lift over the same algebraically closed field, the
local ring and module are literally the same as downstairs.
Consequently their lengths agree.  This supplies both the relative
module and the length statement on the original base.
\end{proof}
