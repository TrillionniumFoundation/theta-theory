\section{Vector boundary information for a non-dominated local experiment}
\label{sec:v20-vector-information}

The scalar theorem of Section~\ref{sec:v17-boundary-information} already
contains the essential warning for a moving support: for a fixed nonzero local
parameter the alternative need not be absolutely continuous with respect to
the reference law.  We therefore formulate the vector theorem without a
finite-$n$ Radon--Nikodym derivative for the original experiment.  The main
point of this section is that, at the logarithmic critical scale, a
parameter-independent collapse of a vanishing reference collar produces a
dominated experiment at vanishing Le Cam distance from the original one.  The
collapsed experiment is uniformly LAN, and the Gaussian shift, testing,
contiguity and minimax conclusions transfer back to the original
non-dominated family.

Let $\Theta\subset\mathbb R^r$ be a neighborhood of the origin.  On a fixed
bounded open set $U\subset\mathbb R^m$ suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-vector-density}
 f_\theta(x)=a_\theta(x)(w_\theta(x))_+,
 \qquad \int_U f_\theta(x)\,dx=1.
\end{equation}
Assume, uniformly for $\theta$ near zero, that $a_\theta,w_\theta$ are $C^3$
in $(\theta,x)$ on a common compact neighborhood of the relevant supports,
that $a_\theta$ is bounded above and bounded away from zero there, and that
\[
 \Sigma:=\{w_0=0\}\cap\operatorname{supp}a_0
\]
is a compact regular hypersurface with $|\nabla w_0|\ge c_*>0$.  We also
assume that $w_\theta$ is uniformly negative near $\partial U$, so the moving
supports remain compactly contained in $U$.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-boundary-velocity-matrix}
 V(x)=D_\theta w_\theta(x)|_{\theta=0}\in\mathbb R^r,
 \qquad
 \mathcal J_\Sigma=
 \int_\Sigma\frac{a_0(x)V(x)V(x)^t}{|\nabla w_0(x)|}\,d\sigma(x).
\end{equation}
Multiplying $w_\theta$ by a positive smooth factor and dividing $a_\theta$
by the same factor leaves $\mathcal J_\Sigma$ unchanged.

For $x\in D:=\{w_0>0\}$ write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-vector-score}
 \mathsf S(x)=D_\theta\log a_\theta(x)|_0+\frac{V(x)}{w_0(x)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform vector collar estimates]
\label{lem:v20-vector-collar}
There are $q_0,C>0$ such that, for $0<q<q_0$ and unit vectors
$u,v\in\mathbb R^r$,
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E_0[(u^t\mathsf S)(v^t\mathsf S)
       \mathbf 1_{\{w_0\ge q\}}]
 &=u^t\mathcal J_\Sigma v\log(1/q)+O(1),
                                      \label{eq:v20-vector-second}\\
 \mathbb E_0[|u^t\mathsf S|^3\mathbf 1_{\{w_0\ge q\}}]
 &\le Cq^{-1},                       \label{eq:v20-vector-third}\\
 \mathbb E_0[|u^t\mathsf S|^4\mathbf 1_{\{w_0\ge q\}}]
 &\le Cq^{-2},                       \label{eq:v20-vector-fourth}\\
 \left\|\mathbb E_0[\mathsf S\mathbf 1_{\{w_0\ge q\}}]\right\|
 &\le Cq,                            \label{eq:v20-vector-centering}\\
 \mathbb P_0(0<w_0<q)&\le Cq^2.     \label{eq:v21-collar-mass}
\end{align}
The constants are uniform for the indicated unit directions and on compact
$C^3$ families satisfying the hypotheses above.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the flow of $\nabla w_0/|\nabla w_0|^2$ to write a fixed tubular
neighborhood as $(y,s)$ with $s=w_0$ and Jacobian
$|\nabla w_0|^{-1}+O(s)$.  Since $f_0=a_0s$ in the interior collar, the
singular part of
$(u^t\mathsf S)(v^t\mathsf S)f_0$ is
\[
 \frac{a_0(u^tV)(v^tV)}s.
\]
Integration of $s^{-1}$ from $q$ to a fixed collar width gives
\eqref{eq:v20-vector-second}; all terms containing at most one inverse power
of $s$ are integrable.  The same coordinates give $s^{1-k}$ for the $k$th
singular score moment and prove
\eqref{eq:v20-vector-third}--\eqref{eq:v20-vector-fourth}.  They also give
quadratic collar mass.  Differentiating $\int f_\theta=1$ at zero yields
$\mathbb E_0\mathsf S=0$ in the improper integrable sense.  The omitted
collar has first score moment $O(q)$, which proves
\eqref{eq:v20-vector-centering}.
\end{proof}

The next estimate will also be used for the billiard-to-boundary transfer.
It isolates the nonregular cost of a support error.

\begin{lemma}[Hellinger stability under a support remainder]
\label{lem:v21-support-stability}
Let
\[
 f=a(w)_+,
 \qquad \widetilde f=\widetilde a(\widetilde w)_+
\]
be probability densities on the same fixed $U$, belonging to a compact class
with the regularity, positivity and regular-boundary bounds above.  Put
\[
 \varepsilon=\|w-\widetilde w\|_{C^1(U)},
 \qquad \eta=\|a-\widetilde a\|_{C^0(U)}.
\]
For $\varepsilon+\eta$ sufficiently small,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-support-stability}
 H^2(f\,dx,\widetilde f\,dx)
 \le C\left\{\varepsilon^2\log\frac e\varepsilon+\eta^2\right\},
\end{equation}
with the first term interpreted as zero when $\varepsilon=0$.  The constant is
uniform on the compact class.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A common tubular chart exists after shrinking the class once.  On the collar
$|w|\le C_0\varepsilon$ both densities are $O(\varepsilon)$ and the collar
volume is $O(\varepsilon)$, giving $O(\varepsilon^2)$.  On the common interior
$w\ge C_0\varepsilon$, positivity and the elementary inequalities for square
roots give
\[
 |\sqrt{a w}-\sqrt{\widetilde a\widetilde w}|^2
 \le C\eta^2 w+C\frac{\varepsilon^2}{w}.
\]
The first term is integrable with total $O(\eta^2)$; coarea integration of the
second gives $C\varepsilon^2\int_{C_0\varepsilon}^{c}ds/s$.  Outside the
fixed collar all quantities are regular and contribute
$O(\varepsilon^2+\eta^2)$.  The same estimates include the support-exclusive
pieces, and prove \eqref{eq:v21-support-stability}.
\end{proof}

Let $\delta_n\downarrow0$, $0<p_n\le1$, and assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-vector-rate}
 n p_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1.
\end{equation}
For $h\in\mathbb R^r$ define the one-observation law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-original-local-law}
 P_{n,h}=(1-p_n)\delta_\dagger+p_nf_{\delta_nh}(x)\,dx.
\end{equation}
The family in \eqref{eq:v21-original-local-law} is generally not dominated by
$P_{n,0}$: an outward motion of the support creates positive alternative mass
where the reference law has none.  We now replace only a vanishing reference
collar by one atom.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-collapse-scale}
 L_n=(\log(1/\delta_n))^{1/4},
 \qquad q_n=\delta_nL_n,
 \qquad D_n=\{x:w_0(x)\ge q_n\}.
\end{equation}
Let $K_n$ be the parameter-independent Markov map which leaves $\dagger$
fixed, leaves a successful point $x\in D_n$ unchanged, and sends every other
successful point to a new symbol $\partial$.  Put
\[
 \widetilde P_{n,h}=K_nP_{n,h}.
\]
For a compact $K\subset\mathbb R^r$, write
$\mathcal E_n^K=\{P_{n,h}^{\otimes n}:h\in K\}$ and
$\widetilde{\mathcal E}_n^K=\{\widetilde P_{n,h}^{\otimes n}:h\in K\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Parameter-independent collar collapse]
\label{lem:v21-collar-collapse}
For every compact $K\subset\mathbb R^r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-lecam-collapse}
 \Delta(\mathcal E_n^K,\widetilde{\mathcal E}_n^K)
 \le C_Knp_nq_n^2=o(1),
\end{equation}
where $\Delta$ denotes Le Cam distance.  For all sufficiently large $n$ the
collapsed family $\{\widetilde P_{n,h}:h\in K\}$ is dominated by
$\widetilde P_{n,0}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Uniform $C^1$ parameter control gives
$|w_{\delta_nh}-w_0|\le C_K\delta_n$.  Since
$q_n/\delta_n=L_n\to\infty$, $D_n$ lies in the interior of every support with
$h\in K$ for large $n$.  Hence the retained continuous part is common, while
the atom $\partial$ has positive reference mass and absorbs all remaining
successful observations.  This proves domination.

The successful mass outside $D_n$ is at most
$C_K(q_n+\delta_n)^2\le C_Kq_n^2$, uniformly in $h\in K$, by the same tubular
coordinate estimate as in Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-vector-collar}.  The product
probability of observing at least one collapsed success is therefore at most
$C_Knp_nq_n^2$.  The map $K_n^{\otimes n}$ gives one deficiency exactly.
For the reverse deficiency, whenever a $\partial$ symbol occurs, reconstruct
it from any fixed reference probability supported in
$\{0<w_0<q_n\}$; when no $\partial$ occurs, use the identity.  The two product
laws can then differ only on the event that a collapsed symbol occurs, giving
the same bound.  Finally
\[
 np_nq_n^2
 =(np_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n))
 \frac{L_n^2}{\log(1/\delta_n)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform LAN of the collapsed experiment]
\label{lem:v20-vector-likelihood}
For every compact $K\subset\mathbb R^r$ the dominated family
$\widetilde{\mathcal E}_n^K$ satisfies, under
$\widetilde P_{n,0}^{\otimes n}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-vector-uniform-expansion}
 \log\frac{d\widetilde P_{n,h}^{\otimes n}}
          {d\widetilde P_{n,0}^{\otimes n}}
 =h^t\Delta_n-\frac12h^tQ_nh+o_P(1)
\end{equation}
uniformly for $h\in K$, where
\begin{align}
 \Delta_n&=\delta_n\sum_{i=1}^nY_i
 \mathsf S(X_i)\mathbf 1_{\{w_0(X_i)\ge q_n\}},
                                                  \label{eq:v20-delta}\\
 Q_n&=\delta_n^2\sum_{i=1}^nY_i
 \mathsf S(X_i)\mathsf S(X_i)^t
 \mathbf 1_{\{w_0(X_i)\ge q_n\}}. \label{eq:v20-Q}
\end{align}
Here $Y_i$ is the success indicator and $X_i\sim f_0$ on a reference
success.  Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-central-limits}
 \Delta_n\Rightarrow N_r(0,\mathcal J_\Sigma),
 \qquad Q_n\longrightarrow\mathcal J_\Sigma
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\end{equation}
The $o_P(1)$ and the likelihood expansion are uniform on compact local sets.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-collar-collapse}, under the reference product law the
probability of seeing the atom $\partial$ is $o(1)$.  On its complement every
successful observation lies in $D_n$, where the supports of all bounded local
alternatives coincide.  Taylor expansion before division by $w_0$ gives,
uniformly for $h\in K$,
\[
 \frac{f_{\delta_nh}}{f_0}
 =1+\delta_n h^t\mathsf S+\delta_n^2R_{n,h},
 \qquad |R_{n,h}|\le C_K(1+w_0^{-1}).
\]
Since $|\delta_n h^t\mathsf S|\le C_K/L_n$ on $D_n$, a Taylor expansion of the
logarithm gives
\[
 \log\frac{f_{\delta_nh}}{f_0}
 =\delta_n h^t\mathsf S
 -\frac{\delta_n^2}{2}(h^t\mathsf S)^2
 +\delta_n^2\widetilde R_{n,h}
 +O_K(\delta_n^3|\mathsf S|^3).
\]
The regular remainder has total expectation $O(np_n\delta_n^2)=o(1)$,
because the $w_0^{-1}$ envelope is integrable against $f_0$.  By
\eqref{eq:v20-vector-third}, the cubic contribution is
\[
 O\!\left(np_n\delta_n^3q_n^{-1}\right)
 =O\!\left(\frac{np_n\delta_n^2}{L_n}\right)=o(1).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:v20-vector-uniform-expansion} on the event with no
$\partial$ symbol, hence in probability globally.  The likelihood ratio of
the $\partial$ atom need not be expanded because that atom is observed with
probability $o(1)$ uniformly under the reference law.

The centering error of $\Delta_n$ is at most
$Cnp_n\delta_nq_n=O(L_n/\log(1/\delta_n))=o(1)$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:v20-vector-second} gives covariance
$\mathcal J_\Sigma+o(1)$.  Every scalar summand of the normalized central
sequence is $O_K(L_n^{-1})$, so Lindeberg holds.  Cram\'er--Wold gives the
first limit in \eqref{eq:v20-central-limits}.  Finally,
\eqref{eq:v20-vector-fourth} gives
\[
 np_n\delta_n^4q_n^{-2}
 =O\!\left(\frac1{L_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)}\right)=o(1),
\]
which implies entrywise concentration of $Q_n$ about its mean.  The mean
converges to $\mathcal J_\Sigma$ by
\eqref{eq:v20-vector-second}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Non-dominated vector boundary LAN and Gaussian limit]
\label{thm:v20-vector-boundary-lan}
Assume \eqref{eq:v20-vector-rate}.  For every compact
$K\subset\mathbb R^r$ the original non-dominated local experiment
$\mathcal E_n^K$ is at Le Cam distance $o(1)$ from the dominated experiment
$\widetilde{\mathcal E}_n^K$, and the latter is uniformly LAN:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-lan}
 \log\frac{d\widetilde P_{n,h}^{\otimes n}}
          {d\widetilde P_{n,0}^{\otimes n}}
 =h^t\Delta_n-\frac12h^t\mathcal J_\Sigma h+o_P(1)
\end{equation}
uniformly for $h\in K$.  Consequently every fixed finite local
subexperiment of the \emph{original} family converges to the Gaussian shift
with information matrix $\mathcal J_\Sigma$.

For every bounded sequence $h_n$, the sequences
$P_{n,h_n}^{\otimes n}$ and $P_{n,0}^{\otimes n}$ are mutually contiguous.
For fixed $h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-testing-profile}
 \|P_{n,h}^{\otimes n}-P_{n,0}^{\otimes n}\|_{\rm TV}
 \longrightarrow
 2\Phi\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{h^t\mathcal J_\Sigma h}}2\right)-1.
\end{equation}

If $\mathcal J_\Sigma$ is positive definite, define on the original sample
space
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-local-estimator}
 \widehat h_n=\mathcal J_\Sigma^{-1}\Delta_n,
\end{equation}
where observations outside $D_n$ are simply omitted from $\Delta_n$.  Then,
uniformly for $h$ in compact local sets,
\[
 \widehat h_n\Rightarrow N_r(h,\mathcal J_\Sigma^{-1})
 \qquad\text{under }P_{n,h}^{\otimes n}.
\]
For every positive semidefinite matrix $W$ and
$\ell_M(z)=\min\{z^tWz,M\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-minimax-truncated}
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \inf_{T_n}\sup_{\|h\|\le R}
 \mathbb E_{n,h}\ell_M(T_n-h)
 \ge
 \mathbb E\ell_M(Z),
 \qquad Z\sim N_r(0,\mathcal J_\Sigma^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Letting $M\to\infty$ gives the quadratic lower bound
$\operatorname{tr}(W\mathcal J_\Sigma^{-1})$.  On each fixed compact local
set the estimator \eqref{eq:v20-local-estimator} has uniformly integrable
quadratic loss and its quadratic risk converges to this Gaussian value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Le Cam comparison is Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-collar-collapse}; the ULAN
expansion is Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-vector-likelihood} with
$Q_n\to\mathcal J_\Sigma$.  The ordinary LAN theory therefore applies to the
collapsed experiment.  In particular, Le Cam's first lemma gives mutual
contiguity there for every bounded local sequence, and the third lemma gives
the translated Gaussian law of $\Delta_n$.  Vanishing Le Cam distance
transfers both assertions to the original family.  The same transfer and the
exact equal-covariance Gaussian calculation give
\eqref{eq:v20-testing-profile}.

The local asymptotic minimax theorem applied to the dominated collapsed
experiment gives \eqref{eq:v20-minimax-truncated}; asymptotic equivalence
transfers the lower bound to the original experiment.  Monotone convergence
in $M$ gives the quadratic lower bound.

It remains only to justify the asserted quadratic risk of the displayed
central-sequence estimator uniformly under bounded local alternatives.  On
$D_n$ and for $h$ in a fixed compact set, the density ratio is
$1+O_K(L_n^{-1})$.  Thus the second and fourth truncated score estimates of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-vector-collar} remain uniformly comparable under the local
alternatives.  The fourth moment of the normalized sum is bounded by the
square of its covariance plus
$Cnp_n\delta_n^4q_n^{-2}=o(1)$.  Hence
$\sup_{h\in K}\mathbb E_{n,h}\|\Delta_n\|^4<\infty$.  The probability of a
collapsed observation is $o(1)$ uniformly on $K$, so the same bound holds for
the original family after omission of the collar.  This proves uniform
integrability and the quadratic-risk convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finite-$n$ non-domination is not suppressed]
No expression of the form
$dP_{n,h}^{\otimes n}/dP_{n,0}^{\otimes n}$ is used above unless absolute
continuity actually holds.  The Radon--Nikodym derivative in
\eqref{eq:v20-lan} belongs to the explicitly constructed collapsed experiment.
The original moving-support family is recovered through
\eqref{eq:v21-lecam-collapse}.  Thus the singular support-exclusive mass is
not assigned an artificial likelihood value; it is shown quantitatively to be
asymptotically negligible at the critical logarithmic scale.
\end{remark}
