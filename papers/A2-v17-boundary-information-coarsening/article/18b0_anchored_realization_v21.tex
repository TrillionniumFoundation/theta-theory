\section{Physical realization of anchored finite contact-jet families}
\label{sec:v21-anchored-realization}

The statistical experiment of the next section is formulated in one fixed
laboratory chart.  We record here that the corresponding finite-dimensional
contact-jet models are realized by actual nearby billiard tables and are not
merely formal jet families.

\begin{proposition}[Anchored realization of finite labelled jets]
\label{prop:v21-anchored-realization}
Let a smooth periodic dispersing billiard contain a selected labelled closest
pair with positive gap $g_0$, positive contact curvatures, and a strict
clearance margin separating this pair from every competing collision segment
outside fixed small contact neighborhoods.  Fix $M\ge2$.  After choosing one
Euclidean laboratory chart in which the reference contacts have common
transverse coordinate $y=0$ and common tangent direction, there is a smooth
finite-dimensional family of actual periodic billiard tables through the
reference table with coordinates
\[
 (g,q_{0,2},\ldots,q_{0,M},q_{1,2},\ldots,q_{1,M})
\]
on an open neighborhood of the reference vector.  In the fixed laboratory
chart the two participating boundary patches are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-realized-anchored-graphs}
 (-\psi_{0}(u),u),\qquad (g+\psi_{1}(v),v),
\end{equation}
with
\[
 \psi_b(0)=\psi_b'(0)=0,
 \qquad
 \psi_b^{(n)}(0)=q_{b,n}\quad(2\le n\le M).
\]
The obstacle labels, the transverse coordinate and its orientation are the
same for the entire family.  Strict convexity, disjointness of obstacle
closures, and the selected-channel clearance margin hold uniformly after the
parameter neighborhood is made sufficiently small.

The same statement holds when the two contacts belong to different labelled
obstacles, to two lattice translates, or to two distinct contact neighborhoods
of one periodic obstacle, provided the perturbations are made equivariantly
with the lattice identifications.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose disjoint arclength neighborhoods of the two reference contacts so
small that the laboratory graph representation is valid there.  Let
$\chi_b$ be smooth cutoffs supported in these neighborhoods and identically
one on smaller neighborhoods of the contacts.  In the graph chart, prescribe
for small parameters
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-local-bump}
 \delta\psi_b(y)
 =\chi_b(y)\sum_{n=2}^M\frac{\delta q_{b,n}}{n!}y^n.
\end{equation}
Because $\chi_b\equiv1$ near zero, the displayed perturbation changes exactly
the indicated finite jet and neither the contact position in the transverse
coordinate nor the tangent direction.  A gap variation is realized by an
additional normal displacement of the second contact patch which is constant
on the smaller contact neighborhood and cut off before leaving the chosen
patch; its constant value is absorbed into the coordinate $g$ in
\eqref{eq:v21-realized-anchored-graphs}.  If the two contacts lie on the same
periodic obstacle, the two supports are disjoint and the two local
modifications are performed independently.  If a lattice identification is
present, repeat the same perturbation on all translates.

The resulting boundary is smooth.  Strict positive curvature is an open
$C^2$ condition, so it persists for all sufficiently small coefficients in
\eqref{eq:v21-local-bump} and for sufficiently small gap displacement.  The
reference table has positive clearance away from the selected contact
neighborhoods and, by hypothesis, a strict channel-isolation margin.  On a
fixed compact fundamental cell, both properties persist under sufficiently
small $C^1$ perturbations.  Distant lattice translates are controlled by the
unchanged lattice and a uniform diameter bound, leaving only finitely many
nearby translates to check.  Shrinking the parameter ball therefore preserves
disjoint closures and the selected physical channel.

The map from the bump coefficients to the displayed jets has identity
Jacobian because the cutoffs are constant near the contacts.  The inverse
function theorem gives the stated open finite-dimensional coordinate family.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Analytic versus smooth realization]
The proposition is used only for the finite-dimensional statistical local
experiment, whose boundary-information theorem requires finite smoothness on
compact parameter sets.  The analytic continuation conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-signed-rigidity} is a separate deterministic statement
for analytic contact germs; no compactly supported analytic bump is invoked
there.
\end{remark}
