\section{The complete endpoint--time acquisition experiment at its fastest local scale}
\label{sec:v23-full-endpoint-time}

The endpoint-output experiment of Section~\ref{sec:v22-raw-physical} is a
strict coarsening of the record for which the finite-to-boundary theorem is
proved.  We now restore the residual preparation time and the complete waiting
sequence.  The resulting local experiment is not Gaussian at its fastest shape
scale.  Its fastest geometric information is an extreme boundary process.
This gives a theorem for the entire stopped endpoint--time acquisition
transcript of item 2 in Section~\ref{subsec:v22-record-hierarchy}, rather than
only for its endpoint-output coarsening.

Fix a finite labelled contact-jet order \(M\) in the anchored realization of
Section~\ref{sec:v22-anchored-realization}.  Choose a finite positive design
\((b_\ell,d_\ell)\), \(1\le\ell\le L\), as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-finite-positive-design}.  Write
\[
 w_\ell(u,v)=d_\ell-S_{b_\ell}(u)-S_{b_\ell}(v),
 \qquad
 D_\ell=\{(u,v):w_\ell(u,v)>0\}.
\]
The boundary selected-record measure of Section~\ref{sec:v6-transfer}, after
conditioning on success and returning to unscaled residual time, has density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-base-density}
 q_{\ell,0}(u,v,r)
 =\rho_\ell(u,v)
   \mathbf 1_{\{0<r<w_\ell(u,v)\}},
 \qquad
 \int_{D_\ell}\rho_\ell w_\ell\,du\,dv=1,
\end{equation}
where \(\rho_\ell\) is positive and smooth on the common endpoint box.  Up to
the normalizing constant it is the half-line amplitude
\(B_{b_\ell}(u)B_{b_\ell}(v)\).  Thus the conditional density has a positive
trace at the moving ceiling \(r=w_\ell(u,v)\); integrating out \(r\)
multiplies the endpoint density by \(w_\ell\) and creates the linear vanishing
studied in Section~\ref{sec:v22-vector-information}.

Let \(k_{n,\ell}\to\infty\), put \(k_n=\sum_\ell k_{n,\ell}\), and assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-design-proportions}
 \frac{k_{n,\ell}}{k_n}\longrightarrow\alpha_\ell>0,
 \qquad \sum_{\ell=1}^L\alpha_\ell=1.
\end{equation}
Use an even flight number \(j_n\to\infty\) for every same-type design and the
fixed physical windows \(t_{n,\ell}=j_ng_0+d_\ell\).  At the endpoint--time
scale the local parameter is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-local-parameter}
 g_n(a)=g_0+\frac{a}{j_nk_n},
 \qquad
 \vartheta_n(h)=\frac{h}{k_n},
 \qquad z=(a,h)\in\mathbb R^{1+r_M}.
\end{equation}
For design \(\ell\), use the support velocity of
Section~\ref{sec:v22-raw-physical},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-support-velocity}
 U_\ell(u,v)z
 =a+\dot S_{b_\ell}(u)h+\dot S_{b_\ell}(v)h.
\end{equation}
Proposition~\ref{prop:v22-fixed-window-reduction}, with
\(\delta_n=k_n^{-1}\), gives uniformly on compact \(z\)-sets
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-ceiling-expansion}
 w^{\rm ET}_{n,\ell,z}(u,v)
 =w_\ell(u,v)-\frac1{k_n}U_\ell(u,v)z
   +o_K(k_n^{-1})
\end{equation}
in \(C^1\), while the conditional density trace at the ceiling is
\(\rho_\ell+O_K(k_n^{-1})\).  The residual gap-dependence of the half-line
action is \(O((j_nk_n)^{-1})\) and is absorbed by the remainder.

\subsection{The boundary point-process limit}

For a successful endpoint--time record \((u,v,r)\), define its null ceiling
slack and scaled slack by
\[
 s_\ell=w_\ell(u,v)-r,\qquad y=k_ns_\ell.
\]
Under the reference model \(y\ge0\).  Under a local alternative the same
reference coordinate may be negative when the ceiling expands.

For a compact local set \(K\), choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-RK}
 R_K>1+\max_{1\le\ell\le L}
 \sup_{(u,v)\in\overline D_\ell,\,z\in K}|U_\ell(u,v)z|.
\end{equation}
Let \(\mathcal N_{n,\ell}\) be the point measure formed from the
\(k_{n,\ell}\) successful records by retaining only points with
\(|y|\le R_K\) and recording \((u,v,y)\).

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint--time Poisson boundary experiment]
\label{thm:v23-et-poisson}
Assume \eqref{eq:v23-et-design-proportions} and let the local parameter be
\eqref{eq:v23-et-local-parameter}.  Then, uniformly for \(z\) in every fixed
compact set \(K\), the conditional successful endpoint--time experiment is
asymptotically equivalent to independent Poisson point processes
\(\mathcal N_1,\ldots,\mathcal N_L\) on
\(D_\ell\times[-R_K,R_K]\) with intensities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-poisson-intensity}
 d\Lambda_{\ell,z}(u,v,y)
 =\alpha_\ell\rho_\ell(u,v)
   \mathbf 1_{\{y>U_\ell(u,v)z\}}
   \mathbf 1_{\{|y|\le R_K\}}\,du\,dv\,dy.
\end{equation}
The part of the successful sample outside the displayed boundary layer is
asymptotically parameter-free after conditioning on the retained point
process.  Equivalently, the local experiment is the projective Poisson
boundary-shift experiment obtained by increasing \(R_K\).

The finite-strip family has a common dominating Poisson envelope, as
shown in Proposition~\ref{prop:v26-common-domination}.  It need not be
absolutely continuous with respect to the zero-shift member: if
\(U_\ell z<0\) on a set of positive \(\rho_\ell\,du\,dv\)-measure, the
alternative assigns positive probability to points in a region where the
reference intensity vanishes.  The resulting support-exclusive events
prevent contiguity in such expanding directions at this scale, and hence
prevent a finite-information Gaussian LAN representation there.  The
obstruction is reference absolute continuity, not common domination.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix one design and suppress \(\ell\).  The level set \(\{w=0\}\) is smooth
for \(d>0\).  Hence the coarea formula gives, uniformly for fixed \(R\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-corner-area}
 \operatorname{Leb}\{(u,v):0<w(u,v)\le 2R/k_n\}=O(k_n^{-1}).
\end{equation}
On this endpoint collar the residual interval has length at most
\(2R/k_n\), so its one-record probability is \(O(k_n^{-2})\).  Over
\(k_{n,\ell}=O(k_n)\) records the probability of seeing any point from this
corner is therefore \(O(k_n^{-1})=o(1)\).  This removes the codimension-two
intersection between the moving ceiling and the fixed face \(r=0\) from the
first-order experiment.

Away from that negligible collar, the condition \(r>0\) imposes no additional
restriction on a fixed \(y\)-strip.  By
\eqref{eq:v23-et-ceiling-expansion} and smoothness of the conditional trace,
the one-record law in reference coordinates is, uniformly on \(K\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-rare-density}
 \frac1{k_n}
 \bigl(\rho(u,v)+o_K(1)\bigr)
 \mathbf 1_{\{y>U(u,v)z+o_K(1)\}}
 \,du\,dv\,dy
\end{equation}
on \(|y|\le R_K\).  In particular the probability \(p_{n,R}\) of the whole
retained layer is \(O(k_n^{-1})\).  Conditional on entering the layer, the
location law converges in total variation to the normalized version of
\eqref{eq:v23-et-poisson-intensity}.  The standard binomial--Poisson coupling
therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-poisson-tv}
 d_{\rm TV}\bigl(
   \mathcal L(\mathcal N_{n,\ell}),
   \operatorname{PPP}(\Lambda_{\ell,z})
 \bigr)
 \le C k_{n,\ell}p_{n,R}^2+o_K(1)=o_K(1).
\end{equation}
The same estimate for a finite measurable partition of the layer, followed by
monotone refinement, gives the point-process statement; independent design
batches give the product process.

It remains to verify that no random first-order information is hidden in the
bulk.  Remove a reference ceiling layer of thickness \(R_K/k_n\).  The choice
\eqref{eq:v23-et-RK} makes the remaining support common to every \(z\in K\).
There the density ratio is a smooth perturbation of the form
\[
 1+k_n^{-1}a_z(u,v,r)+o_K(k_n^{-1})
\]
with uniformly bounded \(a_z\).  Its centered one-record log-likelihood has
variance \(O_K(k_n^{-2})\), hence the centered sum over
\(k_{n,\ell}=O(k_n)\) records has variance \(O_K(k_n^{-1})\) and converges to
zero uniformly on \(K\).  The deterministic first-order normalizing term is
forced, by total mass one, to equal the negative of the mass gained or lost in
the ceiling layer.  In the Poisson likelihood this is exactly the exponential
compensator
\(\exp\{-\Lambda_{\ell,z}+\Lambda_{\ell,0}\}\).
Thus, conditional on the boundary-layer point process, the bulk likelihood
ratio tends to one in probability uniformly on \(K\).

The extraction of \(\mathcal N_{n,\ell}\) is a parameter-independent Markov
kernel.  Conversely, generate the asymptotically parameter-free bulk from the
reference conditional law and attach it to the Poissonized boundary layer;
\eqref{eq:v23-et-poisson-tv} and the preceding bulk estimate make the reverse
deficiency tend to zero.  The explicit layer-count, bulk, and corner-safe reverse kernels, with
the verified relative-density hypothesis, are given in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-two-sided-deficiency}.  They prove the asserted
two-sided asymptotic equivalence, including exceptional configurations.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Identification at the \(k^{-1}\) geometric scale]
\label{cor:v23-et-identification}
Choose the positive finite design of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-finite-positive-design}.  Then the map
\[
 z\longmapsto
 \bigl(U_\ell(\cdot,\cdot)z\bigr)_{\ell=1}^L
\]
is injective.  Consequently the Poisson limit experiment of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-et-poisson} identifies the full finite coordinate
\((a,h)\): gap is visible at scale \((j_nk_n)^{-1}\), while the anchored
labelled contact geometry through order \(M\) is visible at scale
\(k_n^{-1}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If \(U_\ell z=0\) on \(D_\ell\) for every \(\ell\), it vanishes in
particular on the endpoint boundary
\(\{S_{b_\ell}(u)+S_{b_\ell}(v)=d_\ell\}\).  Hence
\(z^t\mathcal K_{\rm design}z=0\).  Positive definiteness from
Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-finite-positive-design} gives \(z=0\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Waiting counts and transfer to finite bridges}

For a fixed design, stop after \(k_{n,\ell}\) successful preparations and let
\(T_{n,\ell}\) be the total number of preparations.  Before the deterministic
cap is applied, the boundary law factorizes exactly into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-factorization}
 \mathcal L_z(T_{n,\ell})
 \otimes
 \bigl(\Pi^{\partial,\mathrm{ET}}_{n,\ell,z}\bigr)^{\otimes k_{n,\ell}},
\end{equation}
where \(T_{n,\ell}\) is negative binomial with success probability
\(p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)\).  Thus the successful marks are independent of the
waiting count.

\begin{theorem}[Complete stopped endpoint--time local experiment]
\label{thm:v23-complete-et}
In addition to \eqref{eq:v23-et-design-proportions}, assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-et-flight-conditions}
 j_n\in2\mathbb N,\qquad j_n\to\infty,\qquad
 j_n=o(\sqrt{k_n}),\qquad k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0.
\end{equation}
Use the reference-based deterministic caps of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-reference-cap}, with the target success counts
\(k_{n,\ell}\).  Then, uniformly on every fixed compact local parameter set:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the waiting-count component of the boundary stopped transcript is
asymptotically ancillary at the scale \eqref{eq:v23-et-local-parameter};
\item the entire capped boundary endpoint--time acquisition transcript is at
vanishing Le Cam distance from the Poisson boundary experiment of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-et-poisson}, up to a parameter-free factor;
\item the actual finite-bridge stopped endpoint--time transcript is at
vanishing total-variation distance from that boundary transcript, and hence
has the same Poisson local experiment.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the declared endpoint--time acquisition process has a complete local
limit at its fastest geometric scale; this statement retains failures,
waiting counts, designs, successful residual times and stopping times.  It
does not identify the still richer full growing collision array.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The selected-channel term in Theorem~\ref{thm:g-stability},
\eqref{eq:g-competition}, and its smooth fixed-order parameter dependence give
\[
 \log\frac{p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)}
          {p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)}
 =O_K(k_n^{-1})+O_K(j_nk_n^{-1})
 =O_K(j_n/k_n).
\]
For a negative-binomial experiment stopped after \(k_{n,\ell}\) successes,
the Fisher information for \(\log p\) is
\(k_{n,\ell}/(1-p)\).  Hence the squared Hellinger distance between the
waiting-count laws is
\(O_K(k_n(j_n/k_n)^2)=O_K(j_n^2/k_n)=o(1)\).
This proves asymptotic ancillarity.  The same success-ratio estimate is
\(1+o(1)\), so the single reference-based cap remains adequate and its failure
probability is exponentially small.

The exact factorization \eqref{eq:v23-et-factorization}, cap negligibility and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-et-poisson} prove the boundary statement.  Finally
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-stopped-local-transfer} applies to the complete stopped
endpoint--time transcript and gives total-variation error
\(O_K(k_n\tau^{j_n})\)\(=o(1)\).  Le Cam distance contracts under composition,
which proves the finite-bridge conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the endpoint Gaussian theorem is slower]
\label{rem:v23-et-versus-endpoint}
At the scale \(k_n^{-1}\), positive ceiling density makes the extreme
residual-time layer carry order-one information.  Integrating out residual
time replaces the positive ceiling density by the linearly vanishing endpoint
density \(\rho_\ell w_\ell\); the \(k_n^{-1}\) Poisson layer disappears and
the next surviving endpoint scale is
\(k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\asymp1\).  Thus the Gaussian endpoint experiment of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-fixed-window-gaussian} is the slower experiment
created by residual-time coarsening.
\end{remark}
