## 1. A complete higher-product embedded structure (R115 §§3, 8, 13, Route C)

Theorem 11.1 (`thm:quartic-primary`) determines the **actual maximal-minor ideal** for every symmetric degree `m>=2` on the full space of hyperplanes in the complete quartic binary series. Writing `T_m=m(m+1)/2`, it proves

`J_m = I_{S_4} I_{C_4}^{T_m-2} = I_{S_4} intersect I_{C_4}^{T_m}`.

This answers the primary-ideal question in this natural family, not only the associated-point question. The transverse completed local ring has equation `(AC-B^2)(A,B,C)^{T_m-2}` and a further smooth parameter. The nilpotency index is **exactly** `ceil(T_m/2)`, and the transverse length of the nilradical is `binom(T_m,3)`.

Corollary 11.5 (`cor:quartic-infinitesimal-layers`) gives the filtration by powers of the evaluation-curve ideal, every power of the nilradical, successive nilradical quotients, and the associated graded algebra/normal cone. The transverse structure is constant along the evaluation curve. These statements include `m=2`, where the embedded curve already occurs.

The proof has two multiplication-specific steps beyond the classical Schur normal form. First, the residual rows have successive orders `m,m-1,...,1`, so every maximal minor has order at least `T_m`. Second, the weighted-relation minor lemma proves, uniformly in `m`, that the initial minors span the complete degree-`T_m` part of `(AC-B^2)(A,B,C)^{T_m-2}`. The proof handles the four extremal weight monomials explicitly, including the exceptional step from `m=2` to `m=3`. An ideal containment and Nakayama's lemma then identify the **full ideal**, not just its tangent cone.

The row filtration and an explicit nonzero initial minor work for every `n>=4`. Lemma 11.2 and Corollary 11.3 therefore give exact order `T_m` and the quadratic lower bound `ceil(m(m+1)/4)` in all binary degrees. They also show the embedded evaluation curve occurs for every `m>=2`. We do not extend the quartic primary equality to `n>4` without a proof: the reduced secant ideal is then no longer principal, and that step is not supplied by the quartic argument.

## 2. A non-hyperplane global higher-product classification (R115 §§3, 7, 13, Routes A and D)

Section 12 gives a second global primary classification on Grassmannian families whose codimension is unbounded. Fix a degree-`d` effective divisor `Z`, possibly nonreduced, and take `n>=2d-1`. We vary **all base-point-free subseries containing** `H^0(O(n)(-Z))` with a prescribed residue dimension. This is a specified open Grassmannian, not a hand-picked one-parameter obstruction sector.

Theorem 12.1 (`thm:conductor-reduction`) proves a canonical isomorphism between the original multiplication cokernel and the cokernel of multiplication in the finite residue algebra of `Z`. It identifies **all Fitting ideals with the same Fitting index**, is compatible with arbitrary base change, and holds for flat families of divisors. The proof establishes the image identity `WU^{m-1}=gV_{mn-d}` as a sheaf identity; equality of fibre coranks alone is not used to infer an equality of schemes.

For residue dimension two, Theorem 12.2 (`thm:contact-pencil-primary`) determines the entire failure scheme for arbitrary contact multiplicities `Z=sum e_i p_i`. In a frame chart with normalized residue `z_i=lambda_i+c_{i1}t_i+...`, the maximal-minor ideal for `m>=d-1` is generated by

`Delta_Z = product c_{i1}^{binom(e_i,2)} product_{i<j}(lambda_j-lambda_i)^{e_i e_j}`.

The powers of its distinct ramification and collision divisor ideals give a complete, irredundant primary decomposition. There are no embedded associated primes. Every component has codimension one. For `2<=m<d-1`, the zeroth Fitting ideal is zero: the whole parameter space is the failure scheme. Above the wall, the ideal itself is independent of `m`, not only its reduced support.

The theorem also determines every multiplication corank, including simultaneous collisions and ramification of arbitrary order, through the degree of the minimal polynomial of `z` in the product of Artin contact algebras. Corollary 12.4 (`cor:contact-local-structure`) gives the complete weighted-arrangement germs and every nilradical quotient. The reduced-divisor and nonreduced-divisor cases are both verified by the same exact cokernel reduction. In particular `Z=3p+2q` yields three primary exponents `3,1,6` on a family of codimension-three subseries.

This addresses the request for a higher-product result with both non-hyperplane breadth and scheme-theoretic depth. Its scope is stated exactly: it is a divisor-conditioned Grassmannian classification, not a classification on the entire ambient Grassmannian of arbitrary subseries.

## 4. The four compressed proofs (R115 §9 and §14.2--3)

Section 10.1 (`parts/02j-proof-details.tex`) is now part of the active article.

**Plane derivative.** The split and tangent quotient bases, graph directions and polar pairings are written explicitly. On `a^m, w_1a^{m-1},...,w_{n-1}a^{m-1}`, the split matrix is `-lambda^m diag(m,1,...,1)` and the tangent matrix is `diag(m,1,...,1)`. Both have rank exactly `n`; their dual kernels are exactly the source monomials with at least two `W` factors.

**Residual image.** The split calculation starts with `W^2=g^2 V_{2n-4}` and gives each propagation step, using `2n-4>=n-1`. The tangent proof states the interval-semigroup induction from `[4,2n]` to `[4,mn]`. Neither identity is justified solely by an assertion of independent variation.

**Rank-two Hankel forms.** A separate lemma states the precise length-two radical, treats both reduced and double-point quotients, and displays the nondegenerate double-point pairing. The low-rank Hankel/secant input is located at the exact sections and displayed formula in Conca--Mostafazadehfard--Singh--Varbaro, rather than cited as an unspecified rank classification.

**Associated points.** A separate equivariant coherent-sheaf lemma states the finiteness, submodule and minimal-support steps. The references are Stacks Tags `00LC`, `02M3` and `02CE`. The argument is then applied to the nilradical supported on the transitive evaluation curve.
