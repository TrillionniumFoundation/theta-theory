\section{Exact conductor reduction in families}
\label{sec:conductor-reduction}
We work over $\C$. For a divisor $D\subset\Pj^1$ of length $d$
write $I_D=\mathcal O(-D)$. The notation $U^m$ means the image of
$\Sym^m U$ under multiplication, not an abstract symmetric power.
For a finite scheme $D$, a subspace of $H^0(\mathcal O_D(n))$ is
called generating if its evaluation map onto $\mathcal O_D(n)$ is
surjective. This condition is open in a family.

\begin{lemma}[Pencil multiplication]\label{lem:conductor-pencil}
If $U\subset V_n$ is base-point-free, then
$UV_t=V_{n+t}$ for $t\ge n-1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $0\ne f\in U$. For each of its finitely many zeros the
sections of $U$ vanishing there form a proper hyperplane. Choose
$g\in U$ outside their union. The binary forms $f,g$ are coprime.
The kernel of $(A,B)\mapsto fA+gB$ on $V_t\oplus V_t$ consists
of $(gh,-fh)$ with $h\in V_{t-n}$, where this space is zero for
$t<n$. For $t=n-1$ the map is between two $2n$-dimensional spaces
and is injective. For $t\ge n$ its rank is
$2(t+1)-(t-n+1)=n+t+1$. In either case its image is $V_{n+t}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Relative conductor reduction]\label{thm:conductor-reduction}
Suppose $n\ge2d-1$, $m\ge2$, and $1\le k\le d$. On
$\operatorname{Hilb}^d(\Pj^1)=\Pj^d$ let $\mathcal D$ be the
universal divisor and
$\mathcal E_j=\pi_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal D}(j)$, of rank $d$.
Let $X_{d,k}^\circ$ be the open relative Grassmannian of generating
$k$-planes $\mathcal A\subset\mathcal E_n$, and define
\[
 \mathcal W=\pi_*I_{\mathcal D}(n),\qquad
 \mathcal U=\ker\bigl(V_n\otimes\mathcal O
                      \longrightarrow\mathcal E_n/\mathcal A\bigr).
\]
Then $\mathcal U$ has rank $n+1-d+k$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conductor-saturated-product}
 \operatorname{im}\bigl(\mathcal W\otimes\Sym^{m-1}\mathcal U
                         \longrightarrow V_{mn}\otimes\mathcal O\bigr)
       =\pi_*I_{\mathcal D}(mn).
\end{equation}
Restriction induces an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conductor-relative-cokernel}
 \operatorname{coker}(\Sym^m\mathcal U\longrightarrow V_{mn}\otimes\mathcal O)
 \ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\
 \operatorname{coker}(\Sym^m\mathcal A\longrightarrow\mathcal E_{mn}).
\end{equation}
Both assertions remain true after arbitrary base change to
$X_{d,k}^\circ$. In particular the two cokernels have identical
Fitting ideals in every index, including on nonreduced bases.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first prove \eqref{eq:conductor-saturated-product} on a geometric
fibre. Write $D=(g)$, with $g$ a binary form of degree $d$, so
$W=gV_{n-d}$. The inverse-image subseries $U$ is base-point-free:
outside $D$ this follows from $W$, and on $D$ it follows from the
generating hypothesis on $A$.

Products of two elements of $W$ fill
$g^2V_{2n-2d}$. Since $2n-2d\ge n-1$, repeated applications of
Lemma~\ref{lem:conductor-pencil} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conductor-double-vanishing}
 W^2U^{m-2}=g^2V_{mn-2d}=H^0(I_D^2(mn)).
\end{equation}
For $m=2$ no application of the lemma is needed. For each further
factor the input degree is $2n-2d+jn\ge n-1$.

The restriction of $W$ to the first normal layer is surjective:
\[
 H^0(I_D(n))\longrightarrow H^0((I_D/I_D^2)(n)).
\]
Indeed the obstruction is $H^1(I_D^2(n))=H^1(\mathcal O(n-2d))$,
which is zero because $n-2d\ge-1$. A generating subspace $A$
contains a section nonzero at all support points of $D$: avoid the
finitely many evaluation hyperplanes. Such a section is a generator
of the invertible $\mathcal O_D$-module $\mathcal O_D(n)$. Lift it
to $u\in U$. Multiplication by $u^{m-1}$ identifies the first
normal layer in degree $n$ with that in degree $mn$. Consequently
$Wu^{m-1}$ surjects onto $H^0((I_D/I_D^2)(mn))$. This quotient
is also the quotient of $H^0(I_D(mn))$ by
$H^0(I_D^2(mn))$, since $mn-2d\ge-1$. Combining this fact with
\eqref{eq:conductor-double-vanishing} proves
\eqref{eq:conductor-saturated-product} on every geometric fibre.

All the targets just used are vector bundles on the parameter space:
the requisite first cohomology groups vanish uniformly. The map in
\eqref{eq:conductor-saturated-product} is therefore a surjection
of vector bundles by the fibrewise criterion and Nakayama's lemma.
It stays surjective under every base change. The exact sequence
$0\to\mathcal W\to\mathcal U\to\mathcal A\to0$ is locally split;
hence the kernel of $\Sym^m\mathcal U\to\Sym^m\mathcal A$ is
the image of $\mathcal W\otimes\Sym^{m-1}\mathcal U$. Restrict
the multiplication diagram to $\mathcal D$ and divide both source
and target by these kernels. The surjectivity just proved identifies
the two cokernels, giving \eqref{eq:conductor-relative-cokernel}.
This argument is compatible with base change. Presentation
independence and base change for Fitting ideals
\cite[Lemma 15.8.4, Tag 07ZA]{StacksFitting} finish the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the reduction preserves]\label{rem:conductor-scope}
The theorem identifies coherent modules, not only the ranks of their
fibres. Thus it preserves the equations of every corank scheme and
all primary structure of its zeroth Fitting scheme on the stated
base. It does not assert a primary decomposition for an arbitrary
finite-dimensional multiplication system. Such a decomposition is
proved below for $k=2$. The inequality $n\ge2d-1$ is a sufficient
uniform bound; no assertion that it is necessary is used.
\end{remark}
