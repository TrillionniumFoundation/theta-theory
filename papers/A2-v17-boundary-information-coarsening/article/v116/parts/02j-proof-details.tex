\subsection{Coordinate and associated-point details}
\label{sec:hyperplane-proof-details}
We record explicitly the three inputs to the preceding global proof
that are particularly sensitive to the distinction between a support
calculation and a scheme-theoretic assertion.

\begin{lemma}[Rank-two Hankel forms, including double support]
\label{lem:length-two-hankel-precise}
Let $0\ne\ell\in V_{2n}^*$, $n\ge2$, and suppose the pairing
$H_\ell(f,g)=\ell(fg)$ on $V_n$ has rank two. There is a length-two
effective divisor $Z$ on $\Pj^1$ such that $\ell$ factors through
$H^0(Z,\mathcal O_Z(2n))$ and
\[
 \operatorname{rad}H_\ell=H^0(\mathcal O(n)(-Z)).
\]
For $Z=p+q$ with $p\ne q$, the induced pairing is diagonal with
two nonzero evaluation coefficients. For $Z=2p$, after trivializing
the line bundle and writing $\mathcal O_Z=\C[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$,
its matrix is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\\beta&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
with $\beta\ne0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The classical Hankel rank-locus identity identifies the projective
rank-at-most-two locus with the secant variety of the degree-$2n$
rational normal curve. The precise input is the reduction among
Hankel matrix shapes in \cite[Section 1, p.~2]{CMSV}, followed by
the secant coordinate-ring description in
\cite[Section 2, p.~3, equation (2.0.1)]{CMSV}.
Consider the projective bundle of lines in the dual length-two
restriction spaces over $\operatorname{Hilb}^2(\Pj^1)$.
Its image in $\Pj(V_{2n}^*)$ is closed, contains every split secant,
and is their closure. This proves factorization through some $Z$,
including a nonreduced divisor.

Restriction $V_n\to H^0(Z,\mathcal O_Z(n))$ is surjective.
If $Z$ is split, the rank-two condition forces both evaluation
coefficients to be nonzero. If $Z$ is a double point, a functional
$\alpha\,\operatorname{ev}+\beta\,\operatorname{coeff}_{\epsilon}$
has the displayed multiplication pairing; rank two is equivalent
to $\beta\ne0$. In either case the quotient pairing is
nondegenerate, so its pullback radical is precisely the restriction
kernel. This proves the assertion without removing the tangent
secants from the parameter space.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The two plane-derivative matrices]
\label{prop:hyperplane-explicit-polar}
In the split and tangent normal forms of
Lemma~\ref{lem:hyperplane-residual-smoothness}, the plane derivative
on the quotient with basis $a^m,w_1a^{m-1},\ldots,w_{n-1}a^{m-1}$
has matrix, respectively,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyperplane-polar-diagonals}
 -\lambda^m\operatorname{diag}(m,1,\ldots,1),
 \qquad \operatorname{diag}(m,1,\ldots,1).
\end{equation}
Its dual kernel consists exactly of source monomials with at least
two $W$ factors. The residual image is $g^2V_{mn-4}$ in the split
case and the span of exponents $4,\ldots,mn$ in the tangent case.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the split case take $a=z_0+\lambda z_n$, $W=\Span(z_1,\ldots,z_{n-1})$,
complement $v=z_0$, and annihilator
$\ell_m=\operatorname{ev}_{\infty}-\lambda^m\operatorname{ev}_0$.
A graph direction is a linear functional $\alpha:U\to\C$,
with displacement $u\mapsto\alpha(u)v$. Differentiating and pairing
with $\ell_m$ gives $-m\lambda^m\alpha(a)$ on $a^m$ and
$-\lambda^m\alpha(w_i)$ on $w_ia^{m-1}$. Two $W$ factors make
both endpoint evaluations zero even after one factor is varied.
This proves the first diagonal matrix and identifies the kernel.
Since $W=gV_{n-2}$,
\[
 W^2U^{m-2}=g^2V_{2n-4}U^{m-2}.
\]
The pencil in the propagation lemma exists because $U$ is
base-point-free: a common zero would be a common zero of $W$, hence
one of the two endpoints, where $a$ does not vanish. Apply that lemma
successively at degrees $2n-4,3n-4,\ldots,(m-1)n-4$; the starting
degree satisfies $2n-4\ge n-1$. This proves the split image identity.

In the tangent case use $a=z_0$, $W=\Span(z_2,\ldots,z_n)$,
$v=z_1$, and $\ell_m=\operatorname{coeff}_{t^1}$.
The same derivative equals $m\alpha(a)$ and $\alpha(w_i)$ on
the displayed quotient basis. All monomials with two $W$ factors
have zero pairing after one variation, proving the second matrix.
Products in $W^2$ have exactly the exponents in $[4,2n]$.
For $D\ge2n$, the sum of $[4,D]$ and $\{0\}\cup[2,n]$
is $[4,D+n]$: the intervals $[4,D]$ and $[6,D+n]$ overlap.
Induction gives the asserted span $[4,mn]$. Both residual images
have dimension $mn-3$, and both diagonal matrices have rank $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Equivariant embedded curve]
\label{lem:equivariant-associated-curve}
Let $X$ be a scheme of finite type over $\C$, let $Y\subset X$ be
integral, and let $C\subset Y$ be an integral curve. Suppose a
group $G$ acts on these data, is transitive on the closed points of
$C$, and preserves an ideal $J\subset I_Y$. Assume that $J=I_Y$
off $C$ and that $I_Y/J$ is nonzero. Then
\[
 \operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal O_X/J)=\{\eta_Y,\eta_C\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The coherent submodule $I_Y/J$ is supported on $C$. Its support
is nonempty, closed and $G$-invariant, hence equals $C$.
The generic point $\eta_C$ is a minimal point of its support and
therefore is associated to it. Associated points of a submodule
are associated points of the containing module. For these facts,
see \cite[Tags 02CE and 02M3]{StacksAss}. The point $\eta_Y$ is
also associated, as the generic point of the reduced support.
Off $C$ the module is $\mathcal O_Y$, which has no other associated
point. On $C$, an additional point would have to be closed.
The associated set is finite, by \cite[Tag 00LC]{StacksAss}, and
is preserved by every automorphism of the module. Transitivity on
the infinitely many closed points of $C$ excludes that possibility.
\end{proof}
