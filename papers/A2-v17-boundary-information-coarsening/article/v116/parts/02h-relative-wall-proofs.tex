\subsection{Relative determinant and the normal derivative}
\label{sec:relative-wall-proofs}
In this subsection $B=\widetilde B_2^\circ$ is the connected base
labelling the two isotropic hyperplanes, and $\mathcal R$ is their
common radical bundle. We impose the wall hypotheses
\eqref{eq:wall-hypotheses}. These relative statements provide the
bundle identifications and the nonemptiness arguments for the node
normal form.

\begin{proposition}[The relative residual divisor]
\label{prop:relative-residual-divisor}
There is a subbundle $\mathcal T\subset V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal O_B$
with fibre $g^2V_{2n-4}$ and rank $h=p-4$. On
\[
 P=\prod_{\nu=1}^L\Gr_B(c-1,\mathcal R)
\]
let $\mathcal W_\nu$ denote the tautological bundles. The determinant
of residual multiplication
\[
 \tau:\bigoplus_\nu\Sym^2\mathcal W_\nu\longrightarrow\mathcal T
\]
is a section of the line bundle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-determinant-line}
 \det\mathcal T\otimes
 \left(\det\bigoplus_\nu\Sym^2\mathcal W_\nu\right)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Its zero scheme $Z_P$ is an integral reduced Cartier divisor in $P$.
For each sign pattern the signed open incidence $S_\varepsilon\to P$
is a smooth affine-space torsor. Its inverse image
$Z_\varepsilon=S_\varepsilon\times_P Z_P$ is likewise an integral
reduced divisor. These assertions hold on the entire distinct-support
labelled base, not only after fixing the two points.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The unordered pair of support points gives a line subbundle
$\mathcal G\subset V_2\otimes\mathcal O_B$. Multiplication gives
isomorphisms
\[
 \mathcal R=\mathcal G\otimes V_{n-2},\qquad
 \mathcal T=\mathcal G^{\otimes2}\otimes V_{2n-4}
       \ \subset V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal O_B.
\]
These formulas are homogeneous and so include a support point at
infinity. The source and target of $\tau$ have the same rank $h$
at the wall, proving \eqref{eq:relative-determinant-line}.
Trivializing $\mathcal G$ identifies $P$ with the product of the
base chart and $\Gr(c-1,V_{n-2})^L$, and identifies the determinant
zero scheme with the same product for the ordinary residual
multiplication divisor. By Theorem~\ref{thm:all-dimension} at
$(k',c')=(k-2,c-1)$, this residual divisor is integral and reduced:
its expected codimension is one and $L(c-1)-3>1$.

For completeness, these local product descriptions imply the global
assertion. Their projections are flat, with geometrically integral
fibres. Every irreducible component dominates $B$, whereas the
geometric generic fibre is irreducible, so there is just one component.
The product descriptions are reduced. The section is nonzero and is
locally a nonzerodivisor, hence defines a Cartier divisor. The fibres
of $S_\varepsilon\to P$ are the affine torsors
\[
 \prod_\nu\operatorname{Hom}
       (H_{\varepsilon_\nu}/\mathcal R,\mathcal R/\mathcal W_\nu).
\]
Local splittings trivialize them. Pulling back $Z_P$ is consequently
locally a product with affine space, proving the last assertion by
the same generic-fibre and reducedness arguments.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Intrinsic normal-derivative factorization]
\label{prop:normal-block-factorization}
Let $Y=\Pj(V_{2n}^*)\times X_L$, let $S=S_\varepsilon$ be the
signed open incidence over $B$, and let
$\mathcal E=\bigoplus_\nu\Sym^2\mathcal U_\nu$.
The incidence equations are a section $s$ of
$\mathcal E^*\otimes\mathcal L^*$, where
$\mathcal L$ is the annihilator line. Their normal derivative is a
map of rank-$e$ bundles
\[
 d_Ns:N_{S/Y}\longrightarrow\mathcal E^*\otimes\mathcal L^*.
\]
After passing to the labelled etale chart where necessary, the normal
bundle has an exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-bundle-sequence}
 0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_\nu\operatorname{Hom}
            (\mathcal U_\nu,V_n/H_{\varepsilon_\nu})
 \longrightarrow N_{S/Y}
 \longrightarrow\mathcal L^*\otimes\mathcal T^*
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Put $Q=\bigoplus_\nu\Sym^2\mathcal W_\nu$. The first term
maps isomorphically onto the kernel of the restriction
$\mathcal E^*\otimes\mathcal L^*\to Q^*\otimes\mathcal L^*$.
On the remaining associated quotients, $d_Ns$ is
$\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal L^*}\otimes\tau^*$.
Consequently its determinant and the residual determinant are
identified by an isomorphism of their determinant line bundles.
In any local frames this reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-unit-identity}
 \det(d_Ns)=u_0\det(\mu'_K),\qquad u_0\in\mathcal O_S^*.
\end{equation}
The unit ambiguity is exactly the change of frames; the equality of
zero divisors and their multiplicities is intrinsic.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First restrict $Y$ to the rank-two base. Relative to this base the
normal bundle of $\Gr(c,H_{\varepsilon_\nu})$ in $\Gr(c,V_n)$
is $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal U_\nu,V_n/H_{\varepsilon_\nu})$.
The normal sequence for $S\subset Y|_B\subset Y$ gives the first
term of \eqref{eq:normal-bundle-sequence} and the normal bundle of
$B$ in $\Pj(V_{2n}^*)$ as quotient.

The affine tangent space of the rank-two cone at $\ell$ is spanned
by the two evaluations and their first derivatives, with the latter
weighted by the nonzero coefficients. A polynomial annihilating this
four-dimensional space vanishes to order two at both support points.
Its space is precisely $g^2V_{2n-4}$. Therefore
\[
 N_{B/\Pj(V_{2n}^*)}
 =\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal L,\mathcal T^*)
 =\mathcal L^*\otimes\mathcal T^*.
\]
This identification is independent of coordinates on $\Pj^1$.

For one contact write $U=W\oplus\C a$ locally. The restriction
of a rank-two form has one nonzero quotient functional in the
$V_n/H_\varepsilon$ direction. Varying each vector of $W$ in this
direction prescribes the $wa$ entries, and varying $a$ prescribes
the $a^2$ entry, with its nonzero factor two. The $ww'$ entries
remain zero. Thus the plane-normal map is an isomorphism onto the
rank-$c$ space of equation forms vanishing on $\Sym^2W$.
Doing this for each contact proves the first block assertion.

Restrict the remaining equations to $Q$. Variations of the planes
contribute zero there. An annihilator-normal variation $\dot\ell$
contributes $q\mapsto\dot\ell(\mu(q))$, exactly $\tau^*$ under
the preceding normal-bundle identification. Splittings of the two
exact sequences therefore give an upper block triangular matrix with
an invertible plane block and the block
$\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal L^*}\otimes\tau^*$. Taking determinants
proves the assertion on determinant lines. A new splitting alters
only an off-diagonal block, and new frames change determinants by
units. Finally trivializing $\mathcal G$ cancels the common $g^2$
factor and identifies $\tau$ with $\mu'_K$, giving
\eqref{eq:normal-unit-identity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A simultaneous nonempty corank-one wall open]
\label{lem:wall-open-nonempty}
The integral divisor $Z_\varepsilon$ has a nonempty open subset where
the residual map has corank one and nonsingular Hankel annihilator
$\eta$, $K_1\to\C^2$ has full evaluation rank at the support points,
and the original multiplication has corank one. This open subset is
dense in $Z_\varepsilon$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first two residual conditions hold on a nonempty open subset of
the integral residual divisor by Theorem~\ref{thm:all-dimension}.
We verify that the evaluation-rank condition meets this subset rather
than presuming generality of a residual plane.

Write $c'=c-1$, $k'=k-2$; here $k'\ge2c'+1$ and $c'\ge9$.
For a nondegenerate form on a $k'$-space, the locus of isotropic
$c'$-planes contained in a fixed hyperplane has codimension at least
$c'$ in its orthogonal Grassmannian. For a nonisotropic normal vector
this follows by replacing $k'$ by $k'-1$ in the dimension formula.
For an isotropic normal vector $v$, planes not containing $v$ are
lifts of isotropic $c'$-planes in $v^\perp/\C v$, with $c'$
affine lift parameters, giving the same bound. Planes containing $v$
are parametrized by $\operatorname{OGr}(c'-1,k'-2)$ and have
codimension $k'-c'-1\ge c'$. This proves the bound in all cases.

Failure of $K_1$ to map onto the two evaluation coordinates means
containment in one member of their hyperplane pencil. The union has
codimension at least $c'-1>0$. Over the nondegenerate residual
annihilator incidence, its complement is thus nonempty and open.
That incidence is irreducible and dominates the residual determinant
divisor; its corank-one open is nonempty. Intersecting these two
nonempty opens in an irreducible space proves the simultaneous
residual assertion, for each fixed pair of support points.

Choose such a tuple and a nonzero
$k_0\in K_1\cap gV_{n-4}$; this intersection has dimension at least
$c-3>0$. Write $k_0=gk'_0$ and $U_1=gK_1+\C a_1$.
The already spanned space $g^2\operatorname{im}\mu'_K$ has one
missing direction in $g^2V_{2n-4}$. The product
$(gk_0)a_1=g^2k'_0a_1$ supplies it if $\eta(k'_0a_1)\ne0$.
Changing the lift by $a_1\mapsto a_1+gw$ changes this value by
$H_\eta(k_0,w)$. This is a nonzero functional because $\eta$ is
nondegenerate. It vanishes on $K_1$, since $K_1$ is isotropic, so it
is a well-defined nonconstant affine function on the actual lift
torsor, not merely on a space of redundant frames.

After this choice, products from $(gK_1)a_1$ supply both independent
first-derivative coordinates modulo $g^2V_{2n-4}$, using full
evaluation rank and the nonzero values of $a_1$ at both support
points. The square $a_1^2$ supplies a further, nonzero value coordinate.
The product span therefore has dimension at least $(p-4)+3=p-1$.
It is annihilated by $\ell$, so its dimension is exactly $p-1$.
All the conditions used are open, and we have exhibited choices
meeting them. Integrality of $Z_\varepsilon$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-residual-divisor} proves density.
\end{proof}
