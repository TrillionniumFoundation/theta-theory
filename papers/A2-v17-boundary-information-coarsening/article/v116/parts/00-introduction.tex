\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
The scheme structure of multiplication failure can behave in two
opposite ways as the symmetric degree grows. On one natural family
it acquires a quadratically growing embedded thickening; on another,
its full primary decomposition stabilizes at an explicit degree wall.
We determine both structures and relate them to residual multiplication.
Our two higher-product results are the following.

\begin{theorem}[Quartic hyperplanes]\label{thm:quartic-intro}
Let $C_4\subset\Pj(V_4^*)$ be the evaluation curve, $S_4$ its
secant cubic, and $\mathcal J_m$ the maximal-minor ideal of
$\Sym^m\mathcal U\to V_{4m}\otimes\mathcal O$ on the space of
hyperplanes. For every $m\ge2$, writing $T_m=m(m+1)/2$, one has
\[
 \mathcal J_m=\mathcal I_{S_4}\cap\mathcal I_{C_4}^{T_m}
            =\mathcal I_{S_4}\mathcal I_{C_4}^{T_m-2}.
\]
At an evaluation point its complete local equation is
$(AC-B^2)(A,B,C)^{T_m-2}=0$, with one further smooth parameter.
The nilpotency index is $\lceil T_m/2\rceil$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:quartic-primary} proves this assertion by a
weighted-relation spanning lemma for maximal minors, followed by
Nakayama's lemma. Corollary~\ref{cor:quartic-infinitesimal-layers}
determines every infinitesimal layer and the normal cone. The
argument also proves, for all $n\ge4$, exact order $T_m$ of the
hyperplane failure ideal along the evaluation curve and embedded
support in every degree $m\ge2$.

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary contact pencils]\label{thm:contact-intro}
Let $Z=\sum e_i p_i$ have degree $d\ge3$, let $n\ge2d-1$, and
let $U$ vary among base-point-free subseries containing
$H^0(\mathcal O(n)(-Z))$ with two-dimensional residue. This is an
open Grassmannian family of subseries of codimension $d-2$.
For $m\ge d-1$ its full maximal-minor ideal, on a residue frame
$\langle1,z\rangle$ with
$z_i=\lambda_i+c_{i1}t_i+\cdots\bmod t_i^{e_i}$, is generated by
\[
 \prod_{i:e_i\ge2}c_{i1}^{\binom{e_i}{2}}
 \prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)^{e_i e_j}.
\]
The powers of these distinct factors give its complete primary
decomposition. There are no embedded primes. Every corank and
complete local singularity is determined by the residue contact data.
\end{theorem}

Theorems~\ref{thm:conductor-reduction} and
\ref{thm:contact-pencil-primary} prove this result for reduced and
nonreduced divisors and in families with moving support. The key
identity is $WU^{m-1}=gV_{mn-d}$, where $W=gV_{n-d}$: it identifies
the actual cokernel, and hence all its Fitting ideals, with finite
residue multiplication. The ensuing power-basis determinant is
classical, but its use here is scheme-theoretic. In degrees
$2\le m<d-1$ the same parameter family is entirely a failure
scheme, giving a precise degree wall rather than a limiting
surjectivity assertion. These are classifications on the stated
natural parameter families, not assertions about every subseries
on the ambient Grassmannian.

We develop the residual geometry supporting these results together
with a quadratic binary component and singularity theory.

Let $V_n=H^0(\Pj^1,\mathcal O(n))$ and $k=n+1$. Multiplication
of sections defines, on the product of Grassmannians
$X_L=\Gr(c,V_n)^L$, the bundle map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-multiplication}
 \mu:\bigoplus_{\nu=1}^L\Sym^2\mathcal U_\nu
           \longrightarrow V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal O_{X_L}.
\end{equation}
There are no products between different factors. We study the scheme
$\mathscr D_L$ of maximal minors of this map, rather than only the
rank at a general tuple. Put
\[
 q=\binom{c+1}{2},\quad e=Lq,\quad p=2k-1,\quad
 M=Lc(k-c),\quad a=e-p+1,\quad b=Lc-3,
\]
and assume $e\ge p$. The competition between $a$ and $b$ reflects
two geometrically different kinds of annihilator. A nondegenerate
Hankel form leads to the expected determinantal codimension $a$;
a rank-two form leads to signed secant families of codimension $b$.
This competition is measured by multiplication on a smaller residual
space and governs the singularities of the failure scheme.

The reduced support $\Delta^{(L)}=|\mathscr D_L|$ is the set of
tuples for which $\sum_\nu U_\nu^2\ne V_{2n}$. The basic
codimension formula holds in the entire range $4\le c<k$.
\begin{theorem}[Reduced failure locus and independent contacts]\label{thm:global-intro}
Suppose $4\le c<k$, $L\ge1$, and $Lq\ge p$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:codimension}
 \codim_{X_L}\Delta^{(L)}=\min\{Lq-p+1,Lc-3\}.
\end{equation}
There is an irreducible common-secant subvariety $\Sigma_L\subset\Delta^{(L)}$ of codimension $Lc-3$. If $Lc-3\le Lq-p+1$, it is a maximal-dimensional irreducible component of $\Delta^{(L)}$. Consequently a general $L$-tuple is informative if and only if $Lq\ge p$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:all-dimension} refines this formula to a component
classification in all these dimensions. For $a\le b$ it determines
all components and proves reducedness and Cohen--Macaulayness; for
$b<a$ it determines all maximal-dimensional components. Exactly five
additional equality incidences occur outside the two principal rank
families. In each, the plane contains the full radical and is maximal
isotropic in the quotient. The orientation covers in
Section~\ref{sec:orientation-descent} determine their irreducibility.

The principal deformation result is independent of this binary
classification. Given any map $\beta:\Sym^m V\to F$, the plane
derivative at an annihilator defines a polar residual space $Q$ and
a map $\tau:Q\to\ker\ell$. Theorem~\ref{thm:polar-tangent}
expresses the tangent excess of the incidence, and of its corank-one
image, as $\dim\ker\tau$. In degree two,
\[
 Q=\bigoplus_\nu\Sym^2(U_\nu\cap\operatorname{rad}H_\ell),
\]
so the obstruction is a smaller multiplication problem. The formulation
also applies to products of sections on arbitrary projective varieties
and to higher symmetric powers.

This tangent calculation has a scheme-theoretic global form.
Theorem~\ref{thm:global-polar-singularities} determines the full
singular support in expected codimension: all points of original
corank at least two are singular, and a corank-one point is singular
exactly when $\tau$ is not injective. On every constant-rank polar
stratum, the Fitting ideals of the cotangent sheaf are the indicated
minors of $\tau$. These identities include the higher tangent-excess
strata and glue with exact Schur--Jacobian presentations at larger
corank. Corollary~\ref{cor:global-binary-singularities} consequently
handles all annihilator ranks of the quadratic binary problem,
including support collisions. Proposition~\ref{prop:excess-polar}
gives the corresponding local criterion in excess codimension.

The distinction between restricted ideals and neighbourhoods is addressed
by Theorem~\ref{thm:residual-matrix-germ}. At arbitrary original corank,
the joint polar relations are the obstruction to a transverse generic
matrix chart. Even when that obstruction is nonzero, the theorem gives
an exact matrix of residual convergent equations, retaining all higher
terms and all nested rank ideals. In corank one,
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-residual-equations} reduces these to
$\delta=\dim\ker\tau$ equations in $M-a+\delta$ parameters and
identifies their quadratic obstruction. In expected codimension they
form a regular sequence. Proposition~\ref{prop:every-degree-realization}
constructs transverse examples in every symmetric degree; the cubic
example in Section~\ref{sec:intrinsic-residual-germs} also exhibits a
nontransverse cuspidal failure germ. These are exact local statements,
not a claim that the binary higher-product component problem follows
from the two-point calculation.

At a rank-two annihilator with distinct support, its radical is
$gV_{n-2}$ for the vanishing quadratic $g$. Writing
$U_\nu\cap\operatorname{rad}H_\ell=gK_\nu$, the wall $a=b$
is exactly
\[
 L\binom c2=2k-5,
\]
the square threshold for the residual multiplication of the
$(c-1)$-planes $K_\nu$. Its entire determinantal rank stratification
is the pullback of the corresponding cotangent Fitting stratification
on the original corank-one locus
(Corollary~\ref{cor:whole-residual-wall}). On a nonempty dense
open subset of its integral divisor, the analytic equation is $uv=0$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-node}). The normal-bundle factorization,
relative determinant line and simultaneous nonemptiness needed for
this equation are proved separately in
Section~\ref{sec:relative-wall-proofs}. We also give all
same-annihilator signed intersections over the split rank-two base
and an explicit primary decomposition of the fixed-annihilator
isotropy fibre. These describe different objects: fibre embedded
structure is not being asserted for the global scheme.

For higher symmetric powers the general polar theorem remains
applicable. Section~\ref{sec:higher-products} additionally computes
the two-point annihilator sectors by algebraic dihedral descent.
This explicit family is not identified with all components of the
higher-product failure scheme. The arbitrary-$\beta$ theorem,
rather than such an identification, is the general extension of the
quadratic residual mechanism.

A complete higher-product family is provided by
Theorem~\ref{thm:higher-hyperplane-global}. For all hyperplanes
$U\subset V_n$, $n\ge4$, and every $m\ge2$, the entire reduced
failure locus of $\Sym^mU\to V_{mn}$ is the same secant threefold.
We determine every corank, including the jump to $m$ on the evaluation
curve. In degree at least three we also determine all associated
points of the projective failure scheme: the secant generic point
and the embedded evaluation-curve generic point. A lower bound
$\lceil m/2\rceil$ for the nilpotency index shows that the high-degree
scheme structure is not recovered from this fixed reduced support.
Corollary~\ref{cor:all-degree-embedded-support} strengthens the lower
bound to $\lceil m(m+1)/4\rceil$ and includes $m=2$ in the associated
point statement. The quartic primary theorem makes this stronger
bound exact and identifies the complete thickening.
This supplies a global excess-codimension consequence in every higher
degree, beyond merely inserting a multiplication map into the general
polar formalism.

The geometry arises naturally in recovery of calibrated information
metrics: second-order distance contacts give the moment functional
on the within-contact products $U_\nu^2$. A short application is
given in Section~\ref{sec:application-bridge}. The appendices give the exact-contact, native-realization,
boundary-experiment and finite-precision arguments, with their
distinct hypotheses. None
is used as an assumption in the algebraic proofs. Classical inputs
and the precise extent of the primary-source comparison are recorded
in Section~\ref{sec:prior-comparison}.
