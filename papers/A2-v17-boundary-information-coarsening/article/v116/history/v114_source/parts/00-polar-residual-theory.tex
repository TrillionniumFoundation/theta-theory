\section{Polar residual spaces and global singularity strata}
\label{sec:polar-residual}
The deformation mechanism does not require binary forms. Let $V,F$ be
finite-dimensional complex vector spaces, $\dim F=p$, and fix a linear map
\[
 \beta:\Sym^m V\longrightarrow F,\qquad m\ge2.
\]
Choose integers $0<c_\nu<\dim V$, put
$X=\prod_\nu\Gr(c_\nu,V)$, and write $M=\dim X$. The tautological
multiplication map, its source rank and its expected failure codimension are
\[
 \mu:\mathcal E=\bigoplus_\nu\Sym^m\mathcal U_\nu
       \longrightarrow F\otimes\mathcal O_X,
 \qquad e=\rank\mathcal E,\qquad a=e-p+1.
\]
Assume $e\ge p$, and let $D=V(I_p(\mu))$. No assumption on the
codimension of $D$ is made until explicitly stated. The incidence
$\mathcal I\subset\Pj(F^*)\times X$ is the zero scheme of
$\ell\mu=0$. Write $D^{(1)}$ for the locus where $\mu$ has corank one.

At a point $z=([\ell],U)$ of this incidence, choose a representative
$\ell$ and define the plane derivative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar-plane-derivative}
 \begin{split}
 B_z:T_UX&\longrightarrow\mathcal E_U^*,\\
 B_z(A)(u_1\cdots u_m)
   &=\sum_{i=1}^m\ell\beta(u_1\cdots A_\nu u_i\cdots u_m),
       \quad u_i\in U_\nu.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
This is well defined for $A_\nu:U_\nu\to V/U_\nu$: changing a lift
adds a product annihilated by $\ell$. Define the \emph{polar residual space}
and its multiplication map by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar-residual-space}
 Q_z=\ker(B_z^*:\mathcal E_U\longrightarrow T_U^*X),
 \qquad \tau_z=\mu_U|_{Q_z}:Q_z\longrightarrow\ker\ell\subset F.
\end{equation}
The kernel $Q_z$ is independent of the chosen nonzero representative
of $\ell$. On a constant-rank stratum of $B$, these spaces are vector
bundles and $\tau$ is a bundle map. Equivalently, before trivializing
the tautological line $\mathcal L=\mathcal O_{\Pj(F^*)}(-1)$, the
plane derivative has target $\mathcal E^*\otimes\mathcal L^*$.

\begin{theorem}[Polar residual tangent sequence]\label{thm:polar-tangent}
At every point $z=([\ell],U)\in\mathcal I$ there is an exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar-tangent-sequence}
 0\longrightarrow\ker B_z\longrightarrow T_z\mathcal I
 \longrightarrow(\mu_UQ_z)^\perp/\C\ell\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar-tangent-dimension}
 \dim T_z\mathcal I=M-a+\dim\ker\tau_z.
\end{equation}
The projection $\mathcal I\to D$ is a scheme isomorphism over
$D^{(1)}$, so the same formula computes $\dim T_UD$ there.
For $m=2$, set $H_\ell(v,w)=\ell\beta(vw)$ and
$W_\nu=U_\nu\cap\operatorname{rad}H_\ell$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-polar-identification}
 Q_z=\bigoplus_\nu\Sym^2W_\nu,
 \qquad \tau_z((q_\nu)_\nu)=\sum_\nu\beta(q_\nu).
\end{equation}
Thus the quadratic obstruction is an actual smaller multiplication
problem for every symmetric bilinear system, not only for binary forms.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating the incidence equations gives
$\dot\ell\mu_U+B_z(\dot U)=0$. A vector in $\mathcal E_U^*$
belongs to $\operatorname{im}B_z$ exactly when it annihilates $Q_z$.
The equation is therefore soluble in $\dot U$ exactly when
$\dot\ell$ annihilates $\mu_UQ_z$. Its set of solutions is a torsor
under $\ker B_z$. Taking $\dot\ell$ modulo $\C\ell$ proves
\eqref{eq:polar-tangent-sequence}. If $h=\dim Q_z$, then
$\rank B_z=e-h$, so its middle dimension is
\[
 M-(e-h)+p-1-\rank\tau_z=M-a+\dim\ker\tau_z.
\]
On a chart with an invertible $(p-1)$-minor, invertible row and column
operations put $\mu$ in the form $\operatorname{diag}(I_{p-1},S)$,
where $S$ is a single row. The incidence equations first solve the
normalized left kernel vector uniquely and then impose $S=0$.
This proves the scheme isomorphism, not just a bijection on points.

For the quadratic assertion, the map $V/U_\nu\to U_\nu^*$ induced
by $H_\ell$ has image $(U_\nu/W_\nu)^*$. Its transpose has kernel
$W_\nu$, and it descends through $U_\nu$ because $U_\nu$ is
isotropic. Choose $U_\nu=W_\nu\oplus Z_\nu$. Varying vectors of
$W_\nu$ in lifts dual to $Z_\nu$ prescribes every mixed symmetric
entry; varying the vectors of $Z_\nu$ prescribes their symmetric
block, using division by two. Consequently $\operatorname{im}B_z$
is exactly the space of forms vanishing on every $\Sym^2W_\nu$.
Taking annihilators gives \eqref{eq:quadratic-polar-identification}.
\end{proof}

We use $I_s(A)$ for the ideal of $s$-minors of a matrix, with
$I_s=(1)$ for $s\le0$ and $I_s=(0)$ if the requested minors are too
large. For a finite module with presentation $R^q\to R^N\to C\to0$,
$\operatorname{Fitt}_i C$ is generated by the $(N-i)$-minors of the
presentation matrix. This definition is independent of the presentation.
All statements about restrictions of these ideals below mean pullback
of the ideals of $D$; they do not mean the intrinsic singular scheme
of a locally closed stratum.

\begin{theorem}[Global singularities and residual Fitting strata]
\label{thm:global-polar-singularities}
Suppose $D$ is nonempty and of pure expected codimension $a>0$ in $X$,
and put $d=M-a$. Then its full singular support is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-singular-support}
 \operatorname{Sing}(D)_{
m red}
 =\{U:\operatorname{corank}\mu_U\ge2\}_{\rm red}
  \ \cup\ \{U\in D^{(1)}:\ker\tau_{([\ell_U],U)}\ne0\}_{\rm red}.
\end{equation}
The union denotes the indicated subsets of $D$, with their reduced
closed union; the second set is locally closed in $D$.
On any locally closed constant-rank stratum $T\subset D^{(1)}$ of
$B$, with $h=\rank Q$, one has, for every integer $j\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-fitting-recursion}
 \operatorname{Fitt}_{d+j-1}(\Omega_{D/\C})\mathcal O_T
       = I_{h-j+1}(\tau).
\end{equation}
In particular the restriction of the singular scheme
$V(\operatorname{Fitt}_d\Omega_{D/\C})$ is the residual
noninjectivity scheme $V(I_h(\tau))$, including its determinantal
scheme structure. Formula \eqref{eq:global-fitting-recursion} detects
all tangent-excess strata, including points outside a generic node
locus and points whose annihilator has colliding support.

At a point of original corank $\rho\ge1$, write $S=D_0-CA^{-1}B_0$
for the Schur complement of an invertible $(p-\rho)$-block of $\mu$.
Let $f_\alpha$ be all its $\rho$-minors and choose smooth local
coordinates $x_1,\ldots,x_M$ on $X$. On this chart the singular scheme
has the exact ideal
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schur-singular-scheme}
 I_\rho(S)+I_a\big((\partial f_\alpha/\partial x_i)_{\alpha,i}\big)
       \quad\hbox{in }\mathcal O_X.
\end{equation}
The Schur--Jacobian presentations hold on open charts for every
$\rho\ge1$, so they cover the whole failure scheme and are
compatible on overlaps. The residual-minor simplification is a
pullback to the stated strata, not an assertion of a product
neighbourhood transverse to those strata.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Over $D^{(1)}$ use the incidence isomorphism of
Theorem~\ref{thm:polar-tangent}. In local coordinates on
$\Pj(F^*)\times X$, the differential of its $e$ equations is the
map from a free module of rank $M+p-1$ to one of rank $e$ obtained
by adjoining $\dot\ell\mu$ to $B$. Its transpose presents the
cotangent module of the incidence. Restrict to $T$ and split off
an invertible $(e-h)$-minor of $B$. Constant rank means that its
remaining Schur block is identically zero on $T$, not just at a
chosen point. After elementary operations the remaining nonzero block
is the map from $F^*/\C\ell$ to $Q^*$ induced by $\dot\ell\mu$;
it is $\tau^*$, with the tautological-line twist if no representative
of $\ell$ was chosen. Fitting ideals commute with base change.
The minors defining $\operatorname{Fitt}_{d+j-1}$ have size
\[
 (M+p-1)-(d+j-1)=e-j+1.
\]
After removing the identity block their size is $h-j+1$.
This proves \eqref{eq:global-fitting-recursion}. Change of frames or
of a representative of $\ell$ multiplies the matrices by invertible
matrices and hence leaves these ideals unchanged.

Since the local dimension on $D$ is $d$, the tangent formula gives
regularity at a corank-one point exactly when $\tau$ is injective.
Over $\C$, regularity is smoothness. At a point of corank $\rho\ge2$,
Schur elimination identifies the defining ideal with $I_\rho(S)$,
where every entry of $S$ vanishes at the point. Thus this ideal is
contained in the square of the maximal ideal. All its first
differentials vanish, and $T_UD=T_UX$ has dimension $M>d$.
This proves \eqref{eq:global-singular-support}.
Finally the usual exact cotangent presentation for the quotient by
$(f_\alpha)_\alpha$ gives \eqref{eq:schur-singular-scheme} directly
from the definition of $\operatorname{Fitt}_d$. The presentation
independence of Fitting ideals proves compatibility with every other
Schur chart and with \eqref{eq:global-fitting-recursion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Every annihilator rank for quadratic binary subseries]
\label{cor:global-binary-singularities}
In the notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:global-intro}, suppose
$4\le c<k$, $e\ge p$, and $a\le b$. Theorem~\ref{thm:global-polar-singularities}
applies to $\mathscr D_L$. On its corank-one locus, put
$W_\nu=U_\nu\cap\operatorname{rad}H_{\ell_U}$ and
$h=\sum_\nu\binom{\dim W_\nu+1}{2}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-rank-singularity-test}
 U\text{ is smooth}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigoplus_\nu\Sym^2W_\nu\longrightarrow V_{2n}
 \text{ is injective}.
\end{equation}
On each fixed-radical-intersection-dimension stratum, the singular
ideal and all higher tangent-excess ideals are the minors in
\eqref{eq:global-fitting-recursion} of this smaller multiplication map.
No assumption on distinct support, maximal annihilator rank, or
membership in just one signed component is required.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:global-intro} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:expected-grade} give pure expected codimension.
Equation~\eqref{eq:quadratic-polar-identification} identifies the
residual map. Constant dimensions of the intersections give vector
bundles after stratification. The preceding theorem applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The entire residual wall stratification]
\label{cor:whole-residual-wall}
At $a=b$, on a signed rank-two stratum with original multiplication
of corank one and $U_\nu\not\subset\operatorname{rad}H_\ell$, the
pullback of the singular scheme is exactly the residual determinant
scheme of $\mu'_K$. More generally its tangent-excess-at-least-$j$
ideal is $I_{h-j+1}(\mu'_K)$, where
$h=L\binom c2=2k-5$. This includes every residual corank, not only
the open ordinary-double-crossing locus.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Here $Q=\bigoplus\Sym^2(gK_\nu)$, and multiplication factors as
$\mu'_K$ followed by the injective multiplication by $g^2$.
The source and residual target both have dimension $h$.
Apply \eqref{eq:global-fitting-recursion}; multiplication by $g^2$
is a subbundle inclusion on the split rank-two base.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Excess-codimension form of the residual criterion]
\label{prop:excess-polar}
Without the expected-codimension hypothesis, let $T\subset D^{(1)}$
be a constant-rank stratum of $B$. For any integer $t$, locally
\begin{equation}\label{eq:excess-fitting}
 \operatorname{Fitt}_{M-t}(\Omega_{D/\C})\mathcal O_T
       =I_{h-a+t}(\tau).
\end{equation}
At a point where $\dim_U D=M-t$, smoothness is equivalent to
$\dim\ker\tau=a-t$. For quadratic binary subseries in the excess
regime $b<a$, this gives $t=b$ at every corank-one point lying on a
maximal-dimensional component. It is a statement about the full local
failure scheme there, not only its selected component.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The same cotangent presentation has $M+p-1$ generators, so the
requested minors have size $p-1+t=e-a+t$. Remove the $(e-h)$-identity
block. This gives \eqref{eq:excess-fitting}. Compare
\eqref{eq:polar-tangent-dimension} with the stated local dimension.
In the last assertion the global dimension bound is $M-b$, and the
component through the point attains it.
\end{proof}

These results separate a full singular-locus calculation from a
classification of local analytic types or of associated primes.
Equation~\eqref{eq:schur-singular-scheme} retains nonlinear Schur
entries: replacing it by minors of their linear parts would lose
information. Neither the global criterion nor the excess formula
asserts that every germ is a normal crossing, or that every excess
scheme is unmixed.

The generality in $\beta$ has concrete instances. For a projective
variety $Y$ and a line bundle $A$, one may take
$V=H^0(Y,A)$, $F=H^0(Y,A^m)$ and $\beta$ to be multiplication of
sections; the same construction applies to multivariate polynomial
spaces and their linear quotients. Theorems~\ref{thm:polar-tangent}
and \ref{thm:global-polar-singularities} use precisely the hypotheses
stated above in each case. They do not require a secant
parameterization or a classification of catalecticant ranks. For
$m>2$ the polar residual $Q$ need not be a symmetric power of a
radical, which is why the general statement uses the kernel of the
plane derivative instead of imposing a quadratic description.
