\label{sec:prior-comparison}

classical. It is important here to distinguish the fixed complete
series, its varying proper subseries, the Hankel annihilator space,
and the scheme on the parameter space of subseries. The following
comparisons refer to specific results, not to an inference from the
titles of articles.

For the component theorem the additional assertions are the full
rank-incidence optimization, the five exceptional isotropic
incidences, and descent of the signed families. For the global
singularity theorem they are the identification of the polar residual
space and the equality of the restricted cotangent Fitting ideals
with its multiplication minors. The local node theorem further uses
a relative determinant divisor and its normal derivative, not merely
the dimensions of rank strata. The cotangent presentation, Schur
elimination and determinantal resolution themselves are standard
algebraic tools and are not claimed as new constructions.

The closely related paper \cite{Ballico93} requires a distinct
qualification. Its publisher's first page has been inspected: it
introduces higher-order embedding conditions and varying restriction
maps to finite schemes. The remaining pages, containing the theorem
statements needed for a complete comparison, were not available in
the primary text retrieved for this revision. Accordingly no assertion
that its theorems exclude or contain the results here is made. The
comparison with this particular paper remains unverified; the table
above is not a substitute for it. In particular a difference of
parameter-space descriptions on the first page would not by itself
establish a priority claim.

The local presentations in Section~\ref{sec:intrinsic-residual-germs}
use classical Schur elimination and the analytic implicit function
theorem. The generic determinantal singularity, the quadratic cone
$xz-y^2=0$, and the cubic cusp are not claimed as new singularities.
The assertion specific to multiplication is the formula for the joint
polar relations, its single-annihilator specialization, and the
explicit residual obstruction and realization maps. A theorem-level
comparison of these formulations with the failure-locus literature
must distinguish an exact nonlinear presentation from the restricted
Fitting identities; neither is a substitute for the unresolved
comparison with \cite{Ballico93}.

\subsection{The intrinsic derivative and classical determinantal geometry}
The intrinsic map to $\operatorname{Hom}(\ker\mu,\operatorname{coker}\mu)$
is the normal derivative to a matrix rank stratum. This interpretation,
and the tangent condition $\psi(\ker\varphi)\subset\operatorname{im}\varphi$,
are classical; see \cite[Remark 1.18]{FKZ21}. Invertible row and column
operations preserve minor ideals \cite[Lemma 1.3]{FKZ21}, and the generic
rank-stratum germ is a smooth factor times a smaller determinantal germ
\cite[Section 1.3.2, equation (13)]{FKZ21}. Essential nonsingularity is
transversality to that stratum \cite[Definition 2.11 and Proposition 2.12]{FKZ21}.
Fitting ideals are independent of the chosen presentation and commute
with base change \cite[Tags 07Z8 and 07ZA]{StacksFitting}.

Accordingly, neither the existence of an intrinsic normal derivative
nor the generic determinantal normal form is claimed as a new general
theorem. Section~\ref{sec:intrinsic-residual-germs} gives a
self-contained application of these tools, identifies their dual
obstruction through the actual multiplication polars, and separates
the resulting exact nonlinear equations from their first-order
Fitting data. The structural hypotheses are explicitly tested there;
the generic realization example is not used as a substitute for a
natural higher-product classification.

The latter role belongs to
Theorem~\ref{thm:higher-hyperplane-global}. It proves a global statement
on the natural parameter space $\Pj(V_n^*)$, for every $n\ge4$ and
$m\ge2$, rather than inferring a classification from a general
transversality criterion. The base-point-free pencil argument and
rank-two Hankel description are classical ingredients. The additional
scheme conclusions proved here are smoothness at every non-evaluation
secant, saturation off the evaluation curve, the complete associated-point
set for $m\ge3$, and a degree-dependent lower bound on nilpotency.
They follow from the polar rank computation, the order of the Schur
minors at an evaluation, and equivariance. They are not consequences
of the generic matrix normal form alone. This comparison identifies
the content requiring a priority assessment; it does not assert that
an exhaustive search has established its historical originality.

\section{An application to calibrated information recovery}
\label{sec:application-bridge}
Let $J$ be fixed and invertible, let $H(\theta)_{ij}=\theta_{i+j}$,
and set $R_\theta=JH(\theta)J^{\mathsf T}$ in its positive moment
cone. For a prescribed smooth contact, a normal frame $Z$ determines
$U=J^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{ran}Z$. The normal Hessian of the
squared $R_\theta^{-1}$-distance is the inverse of
$Z^{\mathsf T}R_\theta Z$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:normal}). Hence this
contact gives exactly the restriction of the moment functional to
$U^2$. Several independent contacts give the dual of
\eqref{eq:intro-multiplication}. The information-loss locus is
therefore the real positive realization of the failure geometry
studied above, subject to the exact contact hypotheses.

Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-realization} supplies the positive native
realization and global separation conditions. At a real crossing
with two real branches, Corollary~\ref{cor:node-tail} gives the local
small-singular-value law $\eps^a\log(1/\eps)$. These are applications
of the geometry, not assumptions in its proofs. The appendices give
the complete likelihood, boundary, score-compression, finite-offset
and estimation arguments. In particular they do not identify a
computed feature with an independent noisy distance observation.
