\subsection{A simple normal determinant gives an exact double crossing}
\label{sec:simple-normal-determinant}
The passage from a normal derivative to a local equation is an
independent analytic statement. We record the precise hypotheses;
first-order corank alone would not suffice.

\begin{lemma}[Simple normal determinant]\label{lem:simple-normal-determinant}
Let $Y$ be a smooth complex variety of dimension $d+e$, let
$S\subset Y$ be smooth of codimension $e$, and let $s$ be a section
of a rank-$e$ vector bundle $\mathcal F$ which vanishes identically
on $S$. At $z\in S$, assume that the normal derivative
\[
 d_Ns:N_{S/Y}\longrightarrow\mathcal F|_S
\]
has rank $e-1$. Suppose its determinant has a simple zero on $S$
at $z$, meaning that a local representative has nonzero differential
in $T_z^*S$. Then $d\ge1$ and the zero scheme $Z=V(s)$ has analytic
local equation $uv=0$ in a smooth $(d+1)$-dimensional space. More
precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simple-determinant-node-ring}
 \widehat{\mathcal O}_{Z,z}\simeq
 \C[[t_1,\ldots,t_{d-1},u,v]]/(uv),
\end{equation}
with $S$ corresponding to the branch $u=0$. No reducedness or
component-count assumption on $Z$ is needed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose coordinates $(t_1,\ldots,t_d,z_1,\ldots,z_e)$ in which
$S=\{z_1=\cdots=z_e=0\}$, and trivialize $\mathcal F$.
After invertible changes of the normal coordinates and of the
section's components, the first $e-1$ components have an invertible
Jacobian in $z_1,\ldots,z_{e-1}$ at the point. The implicit function
theorem solves these components as
$z_i=h_i(t,u)$, where $u=z_e$. Since the components vanish on $S$,
uniqueness implies $h_i(t,0)=0$. Their common zero scheme is a smooth
$(d+1)$-dimensional neighbourhood containing $S=\{u=0\}$.
For $e=1$ this step has no equations to eliminate.

Restrict the last component to this neighbourhood and call it
$f(t,u)$. It vanishes on $u=0$, so in the convergent power-series
ring it factors exactly as $f=uG(t,u)$. Along $S$, the chain rule
for the eliminated equations gives
\[
 G(t,0)=\partial_u f(t,0)
 =\frac{\det(d_Ns)(t)}{
    \det(\partial(s_1,\ldots,s_{e-1})/
                    \partial(z_1,\ldots,z_{e-1}))(t,0)}.
\]
For $e=1$ the denominator is $1$. This is the elementary Schur
complement identity for the normal Jacobian. The denominator is a
unit. Since the numerator vanishes simply on $S$, the differential
of $G(t,0)$ is nonzero. Consequently $du$ and $dG$ are linearly
independent at $z$. Complete $u,v=G(t,u)$ to analytic coordinates
$(u,v,t_1',\ldots,t_{d-1}')$ on the neighbourhood. In these
coordinates the final equation is exactly $uv=0$. Completion proves
\eqref{eq:simple-determinant-node-ring}. Changing any trivialization
multiplies the determinants by units, so the simple-zero condition
and the conclusion are intrinsic.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Normalization and conductor of the local crossing]
\label{cor:intrinsic-node-conductor}
In Lemma~\ref{lem:simple-normal-determinant}, the normalization of
the completed local ring is
\[
 \C[[t_1,\ldots,t_{d-1},u]]\oplus
 \C[[t_1,\ldots,t_{d-1},v]],
\]
and its conductor in that normalization is $(u)\oplus(v)$.
The conductor in the original ring is $(u,v)$, and the
scheme-theoretic intersection of the two branches is reduced and
smooth of dimension $d-1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The ring in \eqref{eq:simple-determinant-node-ring} embeds into the
displayed direct sum as the pairs whose restrictions at $u=v=0$
agree. The sum is finite over it: adjoining the idempotent $(1,0)$
suffices. Both summands are regular and integrally closed, so this
is the normalization. A pair multiplies every pair in the sum back
into the original ring precisely when both restrictions at zero
vanish. This is $(u)\oplus(v)$, whose inverse image is $(u,v)$.
The quotient by this ideal is $\C[[t_1,\ldots,t_{d-1}]]$.
\end{proof}

In the binary wall calculation below, $Y$ is the annihilator ambient
space, $S$ the chosen signed incidence, and $s$ its isotropy section.
The relative determinant proposition identifies $\det(d_Ns)$,
up to a unit, with the residual multiplication determinant. The
relative reduced divisor and the nonemptiness proposition supply
its simple-zero locus while the original multiplication still has
corank one. Lemma~\ref{lem:simple-normal-determinant} then proves
the exact local equation used in Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-node}.
Thus neither reducedness inferred from a tangent count nor an
unproved removal of higher-order terms enters the crossing argument.
