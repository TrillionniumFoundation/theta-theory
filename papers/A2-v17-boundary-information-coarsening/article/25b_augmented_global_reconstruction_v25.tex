\section{Augmented statistical separation and global physical reconstruction}
\label{sec:v25-uniform-physical-global}
\label{sec:v24-uniform-physical-global}

We construct the global estimator on the observation space of
Section~\ref{sec:v25-common-observables}.  The identifiable datum is the
\emph{pair} consisting of the onsets and the centered conditional laws.
The onsets are included in the estimation criterion, not merely used to
program subsequent observation times.  No Fisher-information model for the
entire analytic class is needed.

Fix \(M\ge2\) and let
\[
 \mathcal D_M(T)=\bigl(g_e,S_{e,0}^{(m)}(0),S_{e,1}^{(m)}(0):
                        e\in E,\ 2\le m\le M\bigr).
\]
Use the normalized finite-coordinate norm of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-global-modulus}, with a fixed positive scale \(g_*\)
for the gaps.  All constants may depend on \(M\) and the compact class.

\begin{lemma}[Finite mixed onset--law separators]
\label{lem:v25-augmented-separators}
\label{lem:v24-finite-separators}
For every \(\eta>0\) there are finitely many designs
\(a_r=(e_r,b_r,d_r)\), \(0<d_r<d_*\), bounded Lipschitz functions
\(\phi_r\) with \(|\phi_r|\le1\), and \(c>0\), such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-mixed-separation}
 \|\mathcal D_M(T)-\mathcal D_M(T')\|_M\ge\eta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|F(T)-F(T')\|_\infty\ge8c,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-augmented-moment-map}
 F(T)=\left((g_e(T)/g_*)_{e\in E},
       (Q_{T,a_r}^{\infty}\phi_r)_{1\le r\le R}\right).
\end{equation}
The map \(F\) is continuous.  The list of designs and tests and the
constant \(c\) depend only on \((\mathfrak K,M,\eta)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The set of pairs on the left of \eqref{eq:v25-mixed-separation} is compact.
For any such pair, either a gap coordinate differs, or every gap agrees.
In the latter case equality of all the two same-type centered laws at every
sufficiently small positive offset would imply equality of their endpoint
marginals and of the support thresholds
\[
                         S_{e,b}(u)+S_{e,b}(v).
\]
Putting \(v=0\) gives equality of the two action germs.  Together with the
already equal gaps this implies equality of \(\mathcal D_M\), a
contradiction.  Thus either a gap coordinate or one conditional law separates
the pair.  Bounded Lipschitz functions separate probability measures on the
fixed compact record box: continuous functions do so by uniqueness of finite
Borel measures, and Lipschitz functions are uniformly dense in the continuous
functions there.  Normalize a separating function to have absolute value at
most one.  Its Lipschitz constant need not be bounded independently of the
chosen pair.

Gap continuity and \eqref{eq:v25-centered-TV-continuity} make a strict
separation persist on a neighborhood of the pair.  A finite subcover gives
a finite list and a positive minimum separation \(8c\).  Include every gap
coordinate in \(F\), even when some neighborhoods were separated by laws.
If no conditional-law test is necessary, one harmless constant test may be
added.  Every selected offset is strictly inside the collar; the finite list
therefore has a positive lower offset and a positive upper collar margin.
This proves all assertions.
\end{proof}

The argument does not assert that a centered conditional law alone determines
a gap.  Already at quadratic order, fixing \(a_0=a_1=a\) permits
\[
       \kappa_0(g)=\kappa_1(g)
                    =\frac{\sqrt{1+g^2a^2}-1}{g}.
\]
This illustrates why the onset coordinate must not disappear from the
identifiability argument.  No assertion about equality of the complete
nonlinear laws for different members of this quadratic family is needed.

\subsection{A finite minimum-distance rule}

For the finite moment map above, choose a finite list of templates
\(T_1,\ldots,T_H\in\mathfrak K\) such that their images form a
\(c\)-net of \(F(\mathfrak K)\) in the maximum norm.  The list exists by
compactness and is fixed before observation.  Given a pilot and \(k\)
fresh successful records at each design, set
\[
 \widehat F=\left((\widehat g_e/g_*)_e,
 \left(\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^k
 \phi_r(\widehat C_{e_r,b_r}(X_{ri},Y_{ri},r_{ri}))\right)_{r=1}^R\right).
\]
Select
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-finite-estimator}
 \widehat T=T_{\widehat h},\qquad
 \widehat h=\min\operatorname*{argmin}_{1\le h\le H}
                       \|F(T_h)-\widehat F\|_\infty,
 \qquad \widehat{\mathcal D}_M=\mathcal D_M(\widehat T).
\end{equation}
On a flagged pilot or batch failure use \(T_1\).  This is a finite Borel
calculation with a fixed tie rule.  In particular, no selection theorem or
unbounded search over an unknown analytic parameter is hidden in the
estimator.  The existence of the library and separation constants is not an
effective rate for constructing them from collar constants alone.

If \(\|\widehat F-F(T)\|_\infty\le c\), a template within \(c\) of
\(F(T)\) has criterion at most \(2c\).  Consequently the selected template
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-deterministic-estimator-bound}
              \|F(\widehat T)-F(T)\|_\infty\le3c<8c,
 \qquad
              \|\mathcal D_M(\widehat T)-\mathcal D_M(T)\|_M<\eta.
\end{equation}
Both types of observed coordinates in \(\widehat F\) are essential to this
application of the separator lemma.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform finite-order physical estimation]
\label{thm:v25-fixed-order-physical}
\label{thm:v24-fixed-M-physical-estimability}
For every \(M\ge2\), \(\eta>0\), \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and prescribed
minimum flight number \(J_0\), there is a single deterministically capped
policy on \(\mathscr A,\mathscr X\) and the estimator
\eqref{eq:v25-finite-estimator} such that every preparation uses an even
flight number at least \(J_0\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-fixed-order-risk}
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}
 \Pr_T\{\|\widehat{\mathcal D}_M-\mathcal D_M(T)\|_M\ge\eta\}<\delta.
\end{equation}
No gap, contact frame, inter-channel registration or lattice metric is
supplied to the policy.  Each choice after the pilot is measurable in its
retained transcript, and every preparation is charged.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We give the order of design choices explicitly.  First choose the finite
separators, their constant \(c\), and the finite template library.  Decrease
\(c\) to at most one if necessary and take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-k-choice}
                k\ge 8c^{-2}\log(8R/\delta).
\end{equation}
Next choose the final even flight number \(J\ge\max(2,J_0)\) so large
that the finite-flight term in \eqref{eq:v25-test-bias} is at most \(c/4\)
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-full-transfer-budget}
                            C R k\tau^J<\delta/8.
\end{equation}
Only now choose \(h>0\), below the pilot and post-pilot collar margins,
so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-h-choice}
 C_{\mathscr F}(\sqrt h+h)\le c/4,\qquad h/(Jg_*)\le c.
\end{equation}
Run Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-observable-calibration} with \(j=J\) and
\(\beta=\delta/8\), using its grid and deterministic cap.  Thus the
required pilot precision is calibrated for the \emph{final} flight number;
that number is never increased afterwards.  The pilot and post-pilot
experiments are both even-flight experiments.

On a good pilot history program the \(r\)-th batch at
\((e_r,b_r,J,J\widehat g_{e_r}+d_r)\), taking fresh preparations and
stopping at its \(k\)-th success.  On this history every true offset is in
a fixed positive subcollar.  Uniform flux bounds therefore give a lower
success probability \(p_0>0\), depending on the already fixed design but
not the table or good pilot history.  Impose on each batch the cap
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-post-cap}
 B=\left\lceil\frac{2k+8\log(8R/\delta)}{p_0}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
A binomial lower-tail bound, using \(Bp_0\ge2k\), gives probability at
most \(\exp(-Bp_0/8)\le\delta/(8R)\) of failing to collect \(k\)
successes.  Thus all batch-cap failures cost at most \(\delta/8\).

Conditionally on the pilot, the marks of successive successes in an
\emph{uncapped} fixed-design batch are iid with the successful physical law.
The capped and uncapped constructions agree whenever the cap is not hit.
We apply concentration to the latter and then add the cap probability; we
do not condition on cap completion and incorrectly assert iid sampling under
that conditioning.  Proposition~\ref{prop:v25-test-implementation} and the
design choices give bias at most \(c/2\) for every test.  Hoeffding's
inequality on \([-1,1]\) and a union bound give
\[
 \Pr_T\left\{\max_r|\widehat\mu_r-
   \mathbb E_T(\widehat\mu_r\mid\text{pilot})|>c/2
                 \mid\text{good pilot}\right\}
             \le2R\exp(-kc^2/8)\le\delta/4.
\]
On the complement, the test coordinates of \(\widehat F\) are within
\(c\) of their ideal values; the gap coordinates are also within \(c\)
by \eqref{eq:v25-pilot-error} and \eqref{eq:v25-h-choice}.  Apply
\eqref{eq:v25-deterministic-estimator-bound}.  Pilot failure, empirical
error and cap failure have total probability at most \(\delta/2\), hence
strictly less than \(\delta\).  The full deterministic preparation cap is
\(B_{\rm pil}+RB\).

There is also a whole-transcript boundary comparison, not just the preceding
bound on a few statistics.  Keep the pilot exact.  Conditional on each good
pilot history the subsequent design is fixed and observable; the stopped
comparison of Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer} costs at most
\(CRk\tau^J\), since each batch stops no later than its \(k\)-th success.
Corollary~\ref{cor:v16-pilot-transfer} adds the pilot exceptional probability
when histories are integrated.  The finite-flight and boundary position
laws use the same physical embedding at each true table, and the estimated
normalization is a common transcript map.  This use of a parameterwise
embedding proves a distance bound; it does not pretend that an unknown
centering map has been observed.  Equation~\eqref{eq:v25-full-transfer-budget}
provides the stated budget for the complete post-pilot transcript, including
waiting counts and all failures.  The direct concentration proof above
already applies to the actual physical experiment.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A single growing-order sequence}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform global reconstruction from long same-type records]
\label{thm:v25-global-physical-reconstruction}
\label{thm:v24-physical-global-reconstruction}
On the compact marked analytic class of
Section~\ref{sec:v25-signature-stability}, there are finite capped policies
\(\pi_s\) and table estimators \(\widehat T_s\), using only the physical
records \eqref{eq:v25-design-record-spaces}, such that every used flight
number is even and at least \(s\), and, for every fixed \(q\ge0\) and
\(\epsilon>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-global-risk}
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}\Pr_T^{\pi_s}
                  \{d_q(\widehat T_s,T)>\epsilon\}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The recovered table includes its Euclidean period lattice.  Only one
simultaneous global element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\) is quotiented out.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each integer \(s\ge1\), use the inverse moduli in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-global-modulus} to choose \(M_s\ge s\), increasing,
and \(\eta_s>0\), decreasing, such that
\[
 \max_{0\le q\le s}\omega_q(C\eta_s+C2^{-M_s})<1/s.
\]
This is possible because every one of the finitely many moduli tends to
zero.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-fixed-order-physical} with
\((M_s,\eta_s,2^{-s},J_0=s)\), retaining its table-valued finite-template
estimator.  Except on an event of probability at most \(2^{-s}\), the
finite-data modulus implies \(d_q(\widehat T_s,T)<1/s\) for every
\(q\le s\).  This proves \eqref{eq:v25-global-risk} uniformly.  Every
stage has a predetermined finite preparation cap.  Replacing these caps by
their increasing cumulative envelope gives a single budget-indexed
procedure, using the largest completed stage and ignoring unused budget.

The exact-data injectivity used by the inverse modulus includes recovery of
the two marked deck holonomies and \(L=VM^{-1}\).  Thus the statistical
conclusion includes lattice scale, shape and orientation relative to the
root frame; it does not insert a prescribed lattice Gram form.
\end{proof}

The design constants, template library and inverse moduli are allowed to be
non-effective functions of the compact class.  The theorem asserts a finite
measurable acquisition procedure once those constants are fixed, not a
polynomial-time algorithm for generating them or a sharp analytic minimax
rate.  The preparation caps display the rare-event cost instead of replacing
it by an uncharged count of successful records.  The theorem is stronger
than an unrestricted finite-flight consistency assertion in one precise
respect: the smallest admissible flight number may tend to infinity, so its
proof cannot use a fixed short-flight support inverse.
