\begin{abstract}
We study polynomial spectra observed through an unknown scalar
normalization and stochastic channels. A common-factor module determines
the normalization defect, while an observable joint-projector gauge
yields a global additive inverse estimate. On the regular quotient,
a recovered covariance matrix determines both the local metric and
minimax geometry. Internal real-rooted multiplicities have a profiled
quartic leading law. For positive rational matrix observations we
identify the exact constrained spectral exponent from all homogeneous
parts of the determinant, and compute its leading Hellinger-ball
diameter by a finite root-multiset variational problem. First-order
Laurent exposure is not sufficient: strictly positive examples have
no visible first variation but a nonzero quadratic spectral response.
The determinant invariant is covariant under the scalar interpolation
gauge. With a free monic normalizer the exact local exponent is the
reciprocal true resolvent pole order, including cancellation-reduced
poles and coefficient algebras without a common flag. Uniform additive
flag bounds, exact-fibre geometry, honest confidence sets, and adaptive
design lower bounds distinguish the different scales of the inverse
problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A scalar normalization can conceal a polynomial factor and therefore
part of a spectrum. Recovering the normalization and recovering the
spectrum are nevertheless not independent perturbation problems. An
error in the normalizing polynomial has additional algebraic structure.
A general coefficient condition number can charge for this error a
second time, producing a product of losses which the observation map
does not possess.

There is a second distinction. Once a coefficient matrix polynomial
is recovered, its resolvent describes arbitrary perturbations, whereas
the observations may permit only a proper linear subspace of them.
The leading Laurent coefficient can annihilate that subspace without
making the spectral target constant. Higher homogeneous parts of the
determinant then govern the inverse. The central local result of this
paper retains these parts and transports their geometry through the
scalar normalization and the observation metric. The global additive
estimate and the exact local constrained modulus describe, respectively,
uniform control across weak identification and the geometry at a
specified identifiable datum.

\subsection{The polynomial experiment}
Fix $d\ge1$, $2\le k\le\min(m_1,m_2)$, an interval $[L,D]$, distinct
clocks $D<T_1<\cdots<T_\ell$, with $\ell\ge d+1$, and
$0<\alpha_*<1/k$. The columns of
$U\in\R^{m_1\times k}$ and $V\in\R^{m_2\times k}$ are probability
vectors. Let $\alpha_b\ge\alpha_*$ and $\sum_b\alpha_b=1$. Put
\begin{align}
 f_b(z)&=\prod_{a=1}^d(z-\lambda_{ba}),\qquad \lambda_{ba}\in[L,D],
       &q(z)&=\sum_b\alpha_b f_b(z),\nonumber\\
 K(z)&=U\diag(\alpha_b f_b(z))V^{\mathsf T},
       &P(T)&=\frac{K(T)}{q(T)}.                  \label{eq:model}
\end{align}
The parameter set $\cK_d$ is compact; singular channels and repeated
roots are included. All $f_b$ and $q$ are monic of degree $d$.
Every $q(T_j)$ and every component probability
$p_b(T_j)=\alpha_b f_b(T_j)/q(T_j)$ has uniform positive bounds.
An arbitrary positive scalar detector multiplying $K(T)$ disappears
on normalization. Component-dependent detectors define a different
experiment and are not silently incorporated into this model.

The target $\Lambda$ is the unordered multiset of all $kd$ roots,
counted with multiplicity. A partition into components is not part
of the output. For multisets of equal cardinality put
\[
 d_\infty(\Lambda,\Lambda')=\min_\pi\max_a|\lambda_a-\lambda'_{\pi(a)}|,
 \qquad \delta=\max_j\|P(T_j)-P'(T_j)\|_F.
\]
For real multisets this equals the maximum distance between their
increasing orders. Unless explicitly quantified, constants depend only
on $d,k,m_1,m_2,\alpha_*,L,D$ and the fixed clocks. An internal root
separation is charged only where stated. Proposition~\ref{prop:explicit}
gives explicit degree and clock dependence; it does not assert stable
monomial interpolation in growing degree.

Let $W=(T_j^r)_{j=1,\ldots,\ell;\,r=0,\ldots,d}$, and let $\Omega$
be the orthogonal projector onto $\operatorname{col}(W)^\perp$. Write
$q(z)=z^d+\sum_{r<d}a_rz^r$. For $c\in\R^d$, put
$r_c(z)=\sum_{r<d}c_rz^r$ and define
\begin{align}
 \cN_Pc&=(\Omega\otimes I)
       \big(r_c(T_j)\operatorname{vec}P(T_j)\big)_{j=1}^{\ell},
       &\tau(P)&=\sigma_{\min}(\cN_P),\nonumber\\
 b_P&=-(\Omega\otimes I)
       \big(T_j^d\operatorname{vec}P(T_j)\big)_{j=1}^{\ell},
       &\kappa&=\sigma_k(U)\sigma_k(V).           \label{eq:normalization}
\end{align}
Here $I$ has size $m_1m_2$. The singular value uses Euclidean
coefficient norm and product Frobenius norm. The normalization equation
is $\cN_Pa=b_P$. Thus $\tau$ is observable with specified norms;
$\kappa$ is a representation quantity used for comparison.
We write $F(\theta)=(\operatorname{vec}P_\theta(T_j))_j$ for the
finite observation map, also on the closed model.

\begin{theorem}[Normalization, clock design, and spectral recovery]
\label{thm:main}
Suppose the true channels have full column rank. If $\ell\ge2d+1$
and $g=\gcd(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$ is monic, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 \ker\cN_P=\{(q/g)s:\deg s<\deg g\},\qquad
 \dim\ker\cN_P=\deg g.
\end{equation}
For any design with $\ell\ge d+1$ and $\tau(P)>0$, every competitor
in $\cK_d$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 \|a-a'\|_2&\le C\min\{1,\delta/\tau(P)\},        \label{eq:q-bound}\\
 d_\infty(\Lambda,\Lambda')&\le
 C\min\{1,[\delta(\kappa^{-1}+\tau(P)^{-1})]^{1/d}\}.
                                                        \label{eq:poly-bound}
\end{align}
If the roots within each true $f_b$ are simple and separated by
$\nu>0$, the exponent in \eqref{eq:poly-bound} is $1$, with a constant
also depending on $\nu$. Cross-component collisions are allowed, and
no rank or root-separation assumption is made on the competitor.

At an interior parameter with simple roots within each component,
$\tau(P)=0$ gives a nonconstant local exact fibre with the same channels
and a changing root multiset. For every fixed admissible $k$, relatively
prime interior examples fail identification at $2d$ clocks. This is a
worst-case statement, realized by an affine-line matrix curve. If the
component polynomials span all polynomials of degree at most $d$, then
$d+2$ clocks suffice and $d+1$ do not.
\end{theorem}

To state the observable strengthening, recover $K$ and take orthonormal
frames for its rank-$k$ column and row spaces. Write its reduction as
$M(z)=\sum_{j=0}^dM_jz^j$, and put $A_0=M_d$.
The matrices $M_jA_0^{-1}$ commute. For each distinct complete
polynomial $f$, let $E_f$ be their joint spectral projector, and set
\[
 B_d(P)=\sum_f\|A_0^{-1}E_f\|_\op,
 \qquad \eta_d(P)=(B_d(P)+\tau(P)^{-1})^{-1}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:joint} makes these quantities observable and
frame-independent and proves
\[
 d_\infty(\Lambda,\Lambda')\le
 C\min\{1,[\delta/\eta_d(P)]^{1/d}\}.
\]
Near separated clusters with at most $m$ roots of each component the
exponent is $1/m$. Scalar normalization error and matrix error enter
as $\tau^{-1}+B_d$, not $\tau^{-1}B_d$.

In degree one write $f_b(z)=z-\xi_b$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine}
 h=\sum_b\alpha_b\xi_b,\quad
 A=U\diag(\alpha)V^{\mathsf T},\quad
 C=U\diag(\alpha_b\xi_b)V^{\mathsf T},\quad B=hA-C.
\end{equation}
Then $P(T)=A+B/(T-h)$ and $K(z)=zA-C$. At three clocks,
$\tau(P)\asymp\gamma(P):=\|B\|_F$. The affine condition is
$\sig=\kappa\gamma/(\kappa+\gamma)$, set to zero on either boundary,
and its observable exact-fibre envelope is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 \sig_*(P)=\max\{\sig(\omega):\mathcal P(\omega)=P\}.
\end{equation}
The residue condition $\eta$ and its proof are retained in
Section~\ref{sec:residue}; $B_1$ is its residue sum and
$\eta_1\asymp\eta$. The estimator of Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}
uses least squares, singular subspaces and polynomial roots without
a supplied signal floor. This is an exact-arithmetic stability result,
not a bit-complexity assertion or an algorithm for global confidence
optimization.

\subsection{The constrained local principle}
The positive rational extension observes a square numerator $M$ divided
by its monic entry sum $q$. This includes noncommuting and defective
coefficient algebras. Fix a real linear space $\mathcal S$ of allowed
numerator perturbations and a basis $E_x$. At a root $r$ of $\det M$
of multiplicity $m_r$, collect the determinant in homogeneous pieces:
\[
 \det(M(r+\zeta)+E_x(r+\zeta))
 =\det M(r+\zeta)+\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_aH_{r,j,a}(x)\zeta^a.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-newton} proves that its exact local
Hellinger modulus is
\[
 \omega_P^{\mathcal S}(t)=C_{\mathcal S}t^\alpha+o(t^\alpha),
 \qquad
 \alpha=\min_{r,j,a<m_r:\ H_{r,j,a}\not\equiv0}
                         \frac{j}{m_r-a}.
\]
The constant is a finite variational diameter: the observation Fisher
ellipsoid is mapped to the root multisets of the minimizing-edge
polynomials. The definition includes the finite permutation quotient
at collisions and retains every homogeneous order, not only the
linear exposure of a leading Laurent coefficient.

Proposition~\ref{prop:quadratic-exposure} gives positive probability
observations with no first-order visible pole at a root of multiplicity
$m$, yet with fixed-normalizer modulus of order $t^{2/m}$.
Allowing the monic normalizer to vary changes that order to $t^{1/m}$.
More generally Corollary~\ref{cor:free-normalizer-v93} identifies the
full rational exponent as $1/\nu$, where $\nu$ is the true resolvent
pole order, even when cancellations make it smaller than the path
count of any chosen flag estimate. Proposition~\ref{prop:newton-gauge}
proves covariance under the scalar interpolation gauge; the
bigraded version also records separate scalar and zero-sum error
scales. These local statements require $\tau(P)>0$ and strict
observed cells, but not a common flag.

The uniform additive common-flag theorem addresses a different
quantification. When the true normalized coefficients have a common
complete invariant flag, it proves
\[
 d_\infty(Z(M),Z(M'))\le C\{(\delta/\tau)^{1/d}
                            +(B_\triangle\delta)^{1/(kd)}\},
\]
with the sharper cluster and path counts where available. The
competitor need not preserve a flag. Its declared uniform constants
are not replaced by the datum-dependent constants in the exact local
law. Thus the pointwise cancellation theorem extends the available
local geometry without surrendering uniform additive control.

\subsection{Quotient geometry, real-rooted singularities, and inference}
Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient-chart} gives an analytic quotient theorem on
regular coefficient charts, including all nearby closed-model
representations up to the finite component action. If $J=DF$, $G$ is
the derivative of locally labelled roots, and $H$ is an observation
metric, then
\[
 V_H=G(J^{\mathsf T}HJ)^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Its largest diagonal square root is the exact radial inverse condition.
In the categorical Fisher metric the local asymptotic minimax constant
is $\E\|\mathcal N(0,V_H)\|_\infty^2$. The finite-permutation Gaussian
quotient handles cross-component root collisions without differentiating
sorting. The exact first-order Hellinger-ball diameter is
$2\diam_\Gamma(\mathcal E_{V_H})t+o(t)$; when aggregate roots are
distinct its coefficient is four times the radial Fisher condition.
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-eta-v93} gives equivalence with
$\eta_d^{-1}$ on fixed-margin compact regular classes, without claiming
an identity or constants uniform at vanishing cell floors.

Coefficient recovery extends analytically across internal
multiplicities, but scalar root extraction does not. The profiled
quartic theorem gives
\[
 \omega_P(t)=\diam_\Gamma(\mathcal X_S)\sqrt t+o(\sqrt t),
 \qquad v_g(x)=\tfrac12\sum_{i\in g}x_i^2,
 \qquad v(x)^{\mathsf T}Sv(x)\le4,
\]
with zero cluster sums and zero simple-root coordinates. For one
isolated double group the leading constant is $\sqrt2\,S^{-1/4}$;
when the double root splits by $2\Delta$ the uniform law is
$t+t/(\Delta+\sqrt t)$. This is a theorem on root extraction after
regular coefficient recovery, not a claim that coefficient recovery
remains regular at normalization-kernel or channel-rank boundaries.

The binary pointed family has observation ideal $(ab,ax,bx)$ and
transverse regular condition comparable to
$(ab)^{-1}+[x\sqrt{a^2+b^2}]^{-1}$. The selected latent intersection
has repeated equal component polynomials, whereas its observation
fibre contains other equality and multiplicity patterns and has
intrinsic diameter $D-L$. Section~\ref{sec:fibres-admissibility}
separates the observation germ, the pointed latent germ and the
stratified fibre, and proves the stochastic admissibility of the
whole-model binary tangent. We do not identify that pointed arc law
with a vanishing intrinsic modulus at $P_*$. The arbitrary-point
semialgebraic modulus and its minimax theorem apply also to other
closed-model singularities without postulating a classification by a
short list of defect integers.

The polynomial and affine inference theorems, their positive lower
families, and their adaptive-design proofs remain part of the paper.
They give honest confidence sets and sharp signal-class squared-risk
orders $\min\{1,(Ns^2)^{-1}\}$. At internal multiplicity $m$, the
fixed-neighbourhood squared-risk order is $N^{-1/m}$, while an
anchored observation ball has squared-risk order $N^{-1/2}$.
These are different experiments, not competing rates for the same
parameter neighbourhood.

\subsection{Relation to previous work}
Rational interpolation and realization provide the algebraic
background \cite{VBB,AA90,BL00,MA07}. The scalar-denominator residual
is a bounded-degree specialization, not a new general interpolation
algorithm. Matrix-polynomial resolvent exclusion, structured
pseudospectra and multiple-eigenvalue perturbation theory are classical
\cite{BF,Chu87,Chu03,GLR82,MBO97,TH,KPM09,HMT,SB}.
In particular the determinant identity and Newton rescaling used
below are not claimed as new perturbation mechanisms. Our local
result specifies the probability-normalized perturbation space at all
homogeneous orders, its exact Hellinger leading diameter and its scalar
gauge transport. The global result separates the normalization error
from the subsequent matrix loss in the observed experiment.

Latent diagonalization, factor identification, and nuisance quotients
have established precedents \cite{AMR,BJR,BCV,LLB}. The target here
is an unordered multiset, not stable component labels through every
collision. The passage from inverse moduli to inference belongs to
optimal recovery and honest confidence theory \cite{Donoho,CaiLow,Dufour};
singular mixture rates also have a substantial theory \cite{HN}.
The model-specific normal forms and positive adaptive alternatives
identify the losses for the present controlled categorical experiment.
