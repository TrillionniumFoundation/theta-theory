\section{Observation fibres and admissible coefficient perturbations}
\label{sec:fibres-admissibility}
A defect of a latent representative is not necessarily a defect of
an observation. This distinction is needed when a singular fibre
contains different component-equality or root-multiplicity patterns.
For the stochastic map $F$ in \eqref{eq:model}, write
\[
 \mathfrak F_P=\{\theta\in\mathcal K_d:F(\theta)=P\}.
\]
The intrinsic observation germ uses the entire inverse image of
shrinking observation balls, and hence includes $\mathfrak F_P$.
A pointed latent germ $(P,\theta_0)$ instead restricts the parameters
to neighbourhoods of a specified $\theta_0\in\mathfrak F_P$.
The stratified fibre records the subsets of $\mathfrak F_P$ having
each specified rank, equality, or multiplicity pattern, modulo the
finite component action. These are three different objects.

In the binary family of Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-collapse}, the
intersection $a=b=x=0$ always means the selected \emph{pointed latent}
intersection. Its representative has equal repeated component
polynomials. The observation $P_*$ itself has neither a well-defined
component-equality pattern nor a well-defined internal multiplicity:
both collapsed channels give the same $P_*$ for every admissible choice
of the component polynomials. Its intrinsic modulus is $D-L$.
The ideal $(ab,ax,bx)$ and the transverse arc orders describe that
pointed family; they do not define a vanishing exponent of
$\omega_{P_*}$. The quartic theorem concerns a different stratum,
where coefficient recovery is regular and scalar root extraction is
singular. Its matrix $S$ is reconstructed by first recovering the
monic normalizer and numerator coefficients, then their joint
projectors and component tuples, and finally the profiled Fisher form.
The only remaining ambiguity is a finite relabelling of components.
Whenever coefficient coordinates are changed in this paper, the actual
observed-cell map and its Fisher or Hellinger metric are transported
with them. Applying an entrywise Hellinger formula afresh to arbitrary
left-right transforms of probability matrices would define a different
experiment; invariance under that replacement is not asserted.

\subsection{Positive refactorization of pencils}
The following lemma makes explicit the admissibility of the binary
whole-model perturbation used below. Its neighbourhood can depend on
the base point; it is not a uniform lower bound for that neighbourhood
as the channel ranks or root separation degenerate.
\begin{lemma}[Analytic stochastic refactorization of a binary pencil]
\label{lem:binary-refactor-v93}
Let $K_\epsilon(z)=zA_\epsilon-C_\epsilon$ be a real analytic two by
two pencil with $A_0$ invertible and
\[
 \one^{\mathsf T}A_\epsilon\one=1,\qquad
 \one^{\mathsf T}C_\epsilon\one=h_\epsilon.
\]
Suppose $K_0=U\diag(\alpha_b(z-\xi_b))V^{\mathsf T}$, with
strictly positive stochastic columns, strict weights
$\alpha_b>\alpha_*$, full-rank channels, and distinct interior roots.
Then for all sufficiently small $|\epsilon|$ there is an analytic
factorization of the same type, with roots remaining interior and
normalizer exactly $z-h_\epsilon$. In particular a numerator
perturbation of zero total preserves a fixed normalizer exactly.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The relevant normalized coefficient is
$X_\epsilon=C_\epsilon A_\epsilon^{-1}$.
Its two eigenvalues $\xi_1(\epsilon),\xi_2(\epsilon)$ are distinct
real analytic functions for small real $\epsilon$, by the simple-root
implicit function theorem. They stay real because the real quadratic
has strictly positive discriminant at zero. They remain in the
permitted interval after shrinking the neighbourhood. Its spectral
projectors are explicitly
\[
 Q_b(\epsilon)=
 \frac{X_\epsilon-\xi_{3-b}(\epsilon)I}
      {\xi_b(\epsilon)-\xi_{3-b}(\epsilon)},\qquad
 W_b(\epsilon)=Q_b(\epsilon)A_\epsilon.
\]
They give
$K_\epsilon(z)=\sum_b(z-\xi_b(\epsilon))W_b(\epsilon)$.
Each $W_b$ has rank one, depends analytically on $\epsilon$, and at
zero equals $\alpha_bU_{:b}V_{:b}^{\mathsf T}$.
All its finitely many entries therefore remain strictly positive.
Set
\[
 \alpha_b(\epsilon)=\one^{\mathsf T}W_b(\epsilon)\one,
 \quad u_b(\epsilon)=\frac{W_b(\epsilon)\one}{\alpha_b(\epsilon)},
 \quad v_b(\epsilon)=\frac{W_b(\epsilon)^{\mathsf T}\one}
                           {\alpha_b(\epsilon)}.
\]
These are analytic; the weights remain above $\alpha_*$ and the
columns are strictly positive and stochastic. Rank one gives
$W_b=\alpha_bu_bv_b^{\mathsf T}$, since for a positive rank-one
matrix the product of its row and column sums equals that matrix
multiplied by its total sum. Also $\sum_bW_b=A_\epsilon$ and
$\sum_b\xi_bW_b=C_\epsilon$. Thus $\sum_b\alpha_b=1$ and
$\sum_b\alpha_b\xi_b=h_\epsilon$, proving the exact normalizer
identity. Full rank of the recovered channels persists by continuity.
\end{proof}

For the lower tangent in Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-collapse}, take
$A_\epsilon=A$ and $C_\epsilon=hA-B-\epsilon D$, where
$D=\tfrac14\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Then $\one^{\mathsf T}D\one=0$, its binary moments are $(0,0,1)$,
and the exact determinant is
\[
 \det((z-h)A+B+\epsilon D)
    =\tfrac14\{ab(z-h)^2+\epsilon(z-h)-abx^2\}.
\]
At $\epsilon=0$, both root derivatives are $-1/(2ab)$.
The lemma verifies every stochastic constraint for this perturbation;
an algebraic pencil perturbation is therefore an actual tangent of
the original model in this use.

\subsection{Comparison on fixed-margin regular classes}
\begin{proposition}[Compact regular equivalence]\label{prop:compact-eta-v93}
Fix positive lower margins for the observed cells, the channel least
singular values, $\tau(P)$, the weight inequalities, the distances of
roots from the endpoints, the internal simple-root gaps, and the
separation of distinct complete component coefficient tuples.
On every nonempty resulting compact regular class there are constants
$0<c\le C<\infty$, depending on these margins and the fixed design,
such that
\[
 c\eta_d(P)^{-1}\le
 \mathfrak c_{H_{P,w}}(P)\le C\eta_d(P)^{-1}.
\]
Cross-component scalar collisions are allowed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pass to ordered internal roots and the finite component quotient.
The specified set is compact. The analytic coefficient recovery
and quotient-chart theorems give local continuous matrices $J,G$;
the Fisher metric is continuous because the cells have a fixed
positive floor. The injective Jacobians remain injective throughout
this set, so $V_H=G(J^{\mathsf T}HJ)^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}$ is continuous
in each chart. Relabelling only permutes its root coordinates, and
$\mathfrak c_H=\max_a\sqrt{(V_H)_{aa}}$ is a continuous quotient
function, also at cross-component scalar collisions.
It is strictly positive: shifting every component root by the same
small amount is an admissible two-sided tangent with nonzero target
derivative; the injective observation derivative has nonzero finite
norm on that tangent.

The joint projectors, the leading coefficient inverse, and $\tau$
are continuous on the same quotient under the stated tuple and rank
margins. Hence $\eta_d^{-1}=B_d+\tau^{-1}$ is continuous, finite,
and strictly positive there. The ratio
$\mathfrak c_H/\eta_d^{-1}$ therefore attains a positive minimum
and a finite maximum on the compact set. This proves the assertion.
The argument supplies a fixed-margin equivalence, not a universal
sharp identity between the certificate and the Fisher condition.
It does not extend the constants across the vanishing-cell example
of Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate-modulus}.
\end{proof}
