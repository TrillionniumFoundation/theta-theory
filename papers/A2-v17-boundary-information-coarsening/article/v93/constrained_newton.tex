\section{Constrained determinant geometry and scalar gauges}
\label{sec:constrained-newton}
The pole of a resolvent describes unrestricted coefficient perturbations.
A probability normalization imposes a linear constraint, and the first
nonvanishing perturbation of the determinant need not be linear. We
therefore retain every homogeneous part of the determinant. This gives
an exact finite invariant, including its leading metric geometry, for
the positive rational experiment. No simultaneous triangularization is
needed in this section. The common-flag estimates remain the stronger
uniform statements when the base datum is allowed to approach a boundary.

Throughout this section, $M$ is a real $k$ by $k$ matrix polynomial of
degree $d$, $M_d$ is invertible, and
$q=\one^{\mathsf T}M\one$ is monic of degree $d$. At the fixed clocks
all cells of $P_j=M(T_j)/q(T_j)$ and all $q(T_j)$ are strictly positive.
Assume $\ell\ge d+1$ and $\tau(P)>0$. The normalizer coefficient
bounds, when imposed, contain the base in their interior. Competitors
in this section are positive rational observations; their determinant
roots may be complex. They are not asserted to admit a stochastic
factorization as in \eqref{eq:model}.

Let $\mathcal S$ be a real linear subspace of
\[
 \mathcal S_{\rm all}
 =\{E\in\R[z]^{k\times k}:\deg E\le d,
                         \one^{\mathsf T}E_d\one=0\}.
\]
The fixed-normalizer space is
\[
 \mathcal S_q=\{E:\deg E\le d,
                         \one^{\mathsf T}E(z)\one\equiv0\}.
\]
Fix a basis $E_1,\ldots,E_n$ of $\mathcal S$, write
$E_x=\sum_i x_iE_i$, and put $e_x=\one^{\mathsf T}E_x\one$.
Thus the actual experiment, including its denominator, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-observation}
 F_{\mathcal S}(x)_j=\frac{M(T_j)+E_x(T_j)}{q(T_j)+e_x(T_j)}.
\end{equation}
We take all sufficiently small $x$ in an open coefficient neighbourhood.
Let $\omega_P^{\mathcal S}(t)$ be the diameter of the determinant-root
multisets of the competitors in its Hellinger ball of radius $t$.
The Hellinger convention is
$h_w(P,P')^2=\sum_jw_j\sum_{a,b}(\sqrt{P_{j,ab}}-
\sqrt{P'_{j,ab}})^2$, with fixed $w_j>0$.

\subsection{The observation metric in numerator coordinates}
\begin{lemma}[Local rational coefficient chart]\label{lem:rational-chart-v93}
The map \eqref{eq:newton-observation} is an analytic embedding near
zero. Its derivative and the induced positive definite norm are
\begin{align}
 (\mathcal L E)_j&=\frac{E(T_j)-e(T_j)P_j}{q(T_j)},\nonumber\\
 \mathcal I_{\mathcal S}(x)
 &=\sum_jw_j\sum_{a,b}
                   \frac{(\mathcal L E_x)_{j,ab}^2}{P_{j,ab}}.
                                                        \label{eq:newton-metric}
\end{align}
Uniformly on bounded sets of $v$,
\[
 h_w(F_{\mathcal S}(tv),P)
       =\tfrac12 t\sqrt{\mathcal I_{\mathcal S}(v)}+O(t^2).
\]
In particular the rescaled parameter balls converge in Hausdorff
distance to $\mathcal K_{\mathcal S}=
\{v:\mathcal I_{\mathcal S}(v)\le4\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The derivative follows by differentiating the quotient. If
$\mathcal L E=0$, then $E(T_j)=e(T_j)P_j$ at every clock.
Since $\deg e<d$, applying the degree-$d$ residual to this equality
gives $\mathcal N_P e=0$. The assumption $\tau(P)>0$ implies $e=0$.
The values of each entry of $E$ consequently vanish at at least $d+1$
distinct clocks, so $E=0$. The derivative is injective, and an
invertible minor supplies a local analytic inverse on the image.
The square-root expansion at the finitely many positive cells gives
the formula, with a uniform remainder. Positive definiteness bounds
$x/t$ on a small observation ball. The uniform expansion gives the
outer limiting inclusion; replacing $v$ by $(1-\epsilon)v$ gives
the inner inclusion, and then $\epsilon$ tends to zero. This also
proves Hausdorff convergence, including the boundary of the ellipsoid.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite constrained Newton invariant}
Expand the determinant exactly, rather than truncating its first
variation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:det-homogeneous}
 \det(M(z)+E_x(z))=D(z)+\sum_{j=1}^k D_j(z,x),
 \qquad D=\det M,
\end{equation}
where $D_j$ is homogeneous of degree $j$ in $x$.
For a distinct root $r$ of $D$, let $m_r$ be its multiplicity and write
\[
 D(r+\zeta)=b_r\zeta^{m_r}+O(\zeta^{m_r+1}),\quad b_r\ne0,
 \qquad
 D_j(r+\zeta,x)=\sum_{a=0}^{kd}H_{r,j,a}(x)\zeta^a.
\]
Here $H_{r,j,a}$ is a homogeneous polynomial on the full allowed
space, not its value on a selected direction. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constrained-exponent}
 \alpha_r(\mathcal S)=
 \min_{\substack{1\le j\le k,\ 0\le a<m_r\\
                         H_{r,j,a}\not\equiv0}}
                    \frac{j}{m_r-a},\qquad
 \alpha(\mathcal S)=\min_r\alpha_r(\mathcal S).
\end{equation}
An empty minimum is $+\infty$. Only a finite determinant expansion
and Taylor coefficients of orders less than $m_r\le kd$ are required.
For algebraic input these are exact algebraic calculations. The
statement does not require a tolerance-based test of numerical zero.

Suppose $\alpha=\alpha(\mathcal S)<\infty$. For each $r$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-edge-polynomial}
 \Pi_{r,v}(\zeta)=b_r\zeta^{m_r}+
       \sum_{\substack{j+\alpha a=\alpha m_r\\1\le j\le k}}
                        H_{r,j,a}(v)\zeta^a.
\end{equation}
If $\alpha_r>\alpha$, this is just $b_r\zeta^{m_r}$.
Let $\mathcal Z_r(v)$ be its multiset of $m_r$ roots, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton-leading-constant}
 C_{\mathcal S}=
 \max_{v,w\in\mathcal K_{\mathcal S}}
               \max_r d_\infty(\mathcal Z_r(v),\mathcal Z_r(w)).
\end{equation}
Different $r$ label different base clusters, not ordered individual
roots. In particular this formula already takes the permutation
quotient inside each repeated-root block.

\begin{theorem}[Exact constrained observation modulus]
\label{thm:constrained-newton}
Under the hypotheses above, if $\alpha(\mathcal S)<\infty$, then
$0<C_{\mathcal S}<\infty$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-constrained-modulus}
 \omega_P^{\mathcal S}(t)
            =C_{\mathcal S}t^{\alpha(\mathcal S)}
                           +o(t^{\alpha(\mathcal S)}).
\end{equation}
The exponent, the limiting set of root multisets, and its diameter
are independent of the basis of $\mathcal S$. Constant invertible
left and right changes of numerator coordinates give the same result
when the normalization functional and allowed space are transported
with them. If $\alpha(\mathcal S)=\infty$, the spectral target is
constant on a coefficient neighbourhood and its local modulus is zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first give a uniform localization argument. If $\|x\|\le Ct$,
then $|H_{r,j,a}(x)|\le C_{j,a}t^j$. On
$|\zeta|=R t^\alpha$, divide \eqref{eq:det-homogeneous} by
$|\zeta|^{m_r}$. For $a<m_r$, the definition of $\alpha$ gives
$j-\alpha(m_r-a)\ge0$. Thus each such perturbation is bounded by
$C_{j,a}R^{-(m_r-a)}$. Terms with $a\ge m_r$ tend to zero uniformly
for fixed $R$, while $D(r+\zeta)/\zeta^{m_r}$ tends to $b_r$.
Choosing $R$ sufficiently large and then $t$ sufficiently small,
Rouch\'e's theorem puts exactly $m_r$ roots inside this circle,
uniformly over the parameter ball. Small fixed disjoint circles
about the distinct base roots and invertibility of the leading
coefficient exclude other roots and loss at infinity.

With $x=tv$ and $z=r+t^\alpha\zeta$, divide the determinant by
$t^{\alpha m_r}$. On bounded $\zeta$-sets, uniformly for bounded $v$,
the resulting polynomial converges to \eqref{eq:newton-edge-polynomial}.
Every omitted term has strictly higher order. The leading coefficient
of that degree-$m_r$ limiting polynomial is the fixed nonzero $b_r$.
The preceding localization and continuity of polynomial roots in the
multiset metric therefore give uniform convergence of all the
rescaled clusters to $\mathcal Z_r(v)$. One may also obtain uniformity
by contradiction: a failing sequence of $v$ has a convergent
subsequence, and Rouch\'e counting on small circles about the roots
of its limiting polynomial gives convergence on that subsequence.

Lemma~\ref{lem:rational-chart-v93} now shows that the sets of rescaled
root clusters converge in Hausdorff distance to
$\{(\mathcal Z_r(v))_r:v\in\mathcal K_{\mathcal S}\}$.
Distinct base clusters cannot be matched to each other once $t$ is
small, so their bottleneck distance is the maximum of the within-block
distances. Taking diameters proves \eqref{eq:exact-constrained-modulus}
and finiteness of its constant. To prove strict positivity, select
$r,j,a$ on a minimizing edge. At that fixed $a$ there is at most one
$j$ on the edge. Its nonzero homogeneous polynomial has a real
$v$ in the interior of $\mathcal K_{\mathcal S}$ on which it does not
vanish. Then $\Pi_{r,v}$ is not a multiple of $\zeta^{m_r}$ and
has a nonzero root; at $v=0$ all roots in this block are zero.

An invertible change of basis in $\mathcal S$ composes each
homogeneous polynomial with the same invertible linear map. It does
not change whether that polynomial vanishes identically, and it
transports the metric ellipsoid. A constant left-right change
multiplies every determinant coefficient by one common nonzero
factor. This proves the invariance assertions. If every minimum is
empty, each perturbed determinant is divisible by
$\prod_r(z-r)^{m_r}=D(z)/\det M_d$. Its degree is $kd$ and its
leading coefficient stays nonzero. It has exactly the same roots
with the same multiplicities throughout the neighbourhood.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Relation to Laurent exposure.}
For $z$ outside the base spectrum,
\[
 D_j(z,x)=D(z)\,
           \operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathop{\bigwedge}\nolimits^j
                                  (M(z)^{-1}E_x(z))\bigr).
\]
Consequently, if $\rho_{r,j}$ is the largest pole order at $r$ of
this exterior-power trace, as a homogeneous polynomial in $x$, then
\[
 \alpha_r(\mathcal S)^{-1}
       =\max_{1\le j\le k}\frac{\rho_{r,j}}j,
\]
where a trace with no pole contributes zero. These traces include
polynomial-in-$z$ perturbations automatically. The first-order
visible Laurent filtration is only the term $j=1$. It need not
determine the answer. Exterior powers here denote the usual induced
maps on alternating tensors; their traces are the coefficients of
$\det(I+sM^{-1}E_x)$. This identity also gives a direct finite way
to compute the constrained invariant without choosing a flag.

\subsection{Unknown normalization and gauge covariance}
Let $\nu_r$ be the pole order of the true inverse $M^{-1}$ at $r$,
and let $\nu=\max_r\nu_r$.
\begin{corollary}[Exact exponent with a free monic normalizer]
\label{cor:free-normalizer-v93}
On $\mathcal S_{\rm all}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:free-normalizer-v93}
 \alpha_r(\mathcal S_{\rm all})=1/\nu_r,
 \qquad \omega_P^{\rm rat}(t)
       =C_{\mathcal S_{\rm all}}t^{1/\nu}+o(t^{1/\nu}).
\end{equation}
This holds also when a flag-path count strictly exceeds $\nu$, and
also when the recovered coefficients have no common invariant flag.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:rational-chart-v93} bounds the full numerator coefficient
error by $Ct$. Near $r$ the true resolvent is bounded by
$C|z-r|^{-\nu_r}$. A Neumann estimate on circles of radius
$R t^{1/\nu_r}$, followed by determinant homotopy counting, gives
the corresponding upper displacement bound.
For the converse, let $L_r\ne0$ be the leading Laurent matrix.
There are real coordinate vectors $u,v$ such that
$c=v^{\mathsf T}L_ru\ne0$, even when $r$ is complex. Constant
$E=uv^{\mathsf T}$ belongs to $\mathcal S_{\rm all}$; its entry sum
is allowed to change the constant coefficient of the monic normalizer.
The determinant lemma gives
\[
 \det(M(z)+\epsilon uv^{\mathsf T})
 = (z-r)^{m_r-\nu_r}b(z)
       \{(z-r)^{\nu_r}+\epsilon(c+O(z-r))\},
 \qquad b(r)\ne0.
\]
For real $\epsilon$ this yields $\nu_r$ roots at displacement
$|c\epsilon|^{1/\nu_r}+o(|\epsilon|^{1/\nu_r})$.
All probability and denominator inequalities remain strict at the
finitely many clocks. The observation derivative of this nonzero
perturbation is nonzero by the lemma, so its Hellinger distance is
comparable to $|\epsilon|$. The upper and lower orders force the
claimed exponent; Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-newton} supplies the
exact leading diameter rather than only its order.
\end{proof}

For precision, this is a \emph{fixed-datum} theorem. Its coefficient
inverse constant depends on $P$, and can diverge as $\tau(P)$ tends
to zero. It neither replaces nor weakens the uniform additive
Theorem~\ref{thm:flag-gauge}. In particular, it does not insert
$\nu$ for $\nu_Q$ in that theorem while leaving its constants
unchanged. The pointwise exponent and the two-scale uniform certificate
answer different questions.

Choose any fixed left inverse $I_d$ of degree-$d$ evaluation and set
\[
 \mathcal H_e M=I_d\bigl(e(T_j)P_j\bigr)_j.
\]
For $E\in\mathcal S_{\rm all}$ put
$e=\one^{\mathsf T}E\one$ and $H=E-\mathcal H_eM$.
\begin{proposition}[Covariance of the determinant test under the scalar gauge]
\label{prop:newton-gauge}
The map
\[
 E\longleftrightarrow(e,H),\qquad
 E=\mathcal H_eM+H,
\]
is a linear isomorphism from $\mathcal S_{\rm all}$ onto
$\R[z]_{<d}\times\mathcal S_q$. The full homogeneous determinant
invariant and the metric limiting set are unchanged by this change
of coordinates. Thus cancellations must be computed in the complete
polynomial $\det(M+\mathcal H_eM+H)$, not separately in selected
products along a flag.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since every $P_j$ has total sum one and $I_d$ reproduces polynomials
of degree at most $d$,
$\one^{\mathsf T}\mathcal H_eM\one=I_d(e(T_j))=e$.
It follows that $H\in\mathcal S_q$. Conversely, every displayed
pair produces $E$ with total $e$ and hence zero degree-$d$ entry sum.
The two constructions are inverse and linear. Substitution in the
determinant gives a literal polynomial identity. Total homogeneous
degree and identically vanishing coefficient polynomials are preserved
under an invertible linear substitution. The observation derivative
and its ellipsoid transform by the same substitution. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-newton}.
\end{proof}

There is also a finite two-scale refinement. Choose coordinates $u$
for $e$ and $v$ for $H$, and collect the gauge determinant as
\[
 \det(M+\mathcal H_eM+H)
   =D+\sum_{p+q\ge1}D_{p,q}(z,u,v),
 \qquad p+q\le k,
\]
with bidegrees $(p,q)$. Denote the nonzero coefficient of
$(z-r)^a$ by $H_{r,p,q,a}(u,v)$ and retain only $a<m_r$.
For fixed $A,B>0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bigraded-gauge-exponent}
 \alpha_r(A,B)=
   \min_{H_{r,p,q,a}\not\equiv0,\ a<m_r}
                        \frac{Ap+Bq}{m_r-a}.
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Two-scale Newton law]\label{prop:bigraded-gauge}
In the coefficient boxes $\|e\|\le s$, $\|H\|\le t$, the maximal
root displacement from the base cluster at $r$ is bounded by
\[
 C\max_{H_{r,p,q,a}\not\equiv0,\ a<m_r}
                     (s^p t^q)^{1/(m_r-a)}.
\]
On $s=\epsilon^A$, $t=\epsilon^B$, the bound is attained up to
positive constants; the exponent is \eqref{eq:bigraded-gauge-exponent}.
The empty case has zero displacement. These are coefficient boxes,
not independent statistical observation balls for the two errors.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The coefficients are bounded by $Cs^pt^q$. On a circle whose radius
is a sufficiently large multiple of the displayed maximum, division
by $(z-r)^{m_r}$ makes the sum of the finitely many perturbations
smaller than the base unit. Rouch\'e gives the upper bound and cluster
count. For the lower bound take $e=\epsilon^A e_0$ and
$H=\epsilon^B H_0$. After rescaling by
$\epsilon^{\alpha_r(A,B)}$, the edge polynomial has at least one
nonzero nonleading coefficient for a suitable real pair $(e_0,H_0)$
in the interiors of the unit balls. Indeed, different bidegrees are
linearly independent as polynomials in the two independent groups
of variables. Their sum cannot vanish identically unless each term
does. A generic real pair therefore gives a nonzero root of the
edge polynomial, proving the matching order. The same compactness
argument as in Theorem~\ref{thm:constrained-newton} is an alternative
proof of attainment. If no coefficient is present below $m_r$, the
root is fixed with its full multiplicity.
\end{proof}
The error split \eqref{eq:flag-error-split} permits the substitution
$s=C\delta/\tau(P)$ and $t=C\delta$ in this finite bound. Unlike the
flag-path certificate, its coefficients retain every cancellation at
the specified datum. Its constants are datum-dependent. Changing
$I_d$ is a linear shear between the two coordinate groups; the total
degree invariant is unchanged, whereas the interpretation of separate
coefficient boxes must be transported with that shear.

\subsection{An invisible first variation with a nonzero modulus}
\begin{proposition}[A quadratic exposure family]\label{prop:quadratic-exposure}
For every $m\ge3$ there is a strictly positive rational probability
experiment with invertible leading coefficient and $\tau>0$ for which
all first-order constrained Laurent coefficients at one root vanish,
but
\[
 \omega_P^{[q]}(t)\asymp t^{2/m},\qquad
 \omega_P^{\rm rat}(t)\asymp t^{1/m}.
\]
The full leading constants in both formulas are given by
\eqref{eq:newton-leading-constant}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose distinct $0<a_1<\cdots<a_m$ and clocks $T_j>a_m$,
with $\ell\ge2m+1$. Set
\[
 f=z^m,\qquad g=\prod_{i=1}^m(z-a_i),\qquad
 M=\frac14\begin{pmatrix}f+g&f-g\\f-g&f+g\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad q=f.
\]
Its leading coefficient is $I/2$. At each clock $0<g(T_j)<f(T_j)$,
so every cell of $M(T_j)/f(T_j)$ is strictly positive. Also $f$ and
$g$ are coprime. To check $\tau>0$ directly, let a polynomial $e$ of
degree less than $m$ be in its normalization kernel. Interpolation
supplies a polynomial numerator of degree at most $m$. Multiplying
its off-diagonal entry by $f$ shows, at $2m+1$ clocks and hence
identically, that $f$ divides $e(f-g)$. Coprimality gives $f\mid e$,
so $e=0$.

Let $O=2^{-1/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Then $2O^{\mathsf T}MO=\diag(f,g)$. The condition
$\one^{\mathsf T}E\one\equiv0$ is exactly the condition that
$2O^{\mathsf T}EO$ have upper-left entry zero. Write this transformed
perturbation as
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&u(z)\\v(z)&h(z)\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
with all three polynomials arbitrary of degree at most $m$.
Apart from the harmless factor $1/4$, its determinant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-exposure-determinant}
                        f(g+h)-uv.
\end{equation}
At zero, the linear part is divisible by $z^m$, while the quadratic
coefficient $-u(0)v(0)$ need not vanish. Equivalently the first-order
resolvent trace is $h/g$, analytic at zero for every allowed polynomial
perturbation. Thus $\alpha_0(\mathcal S_q)=2/m$, despite the complete
absence of a first-order visible pole. The other roots are simple
and a constant $h$ exposes each of them, giving exponent one there.
Since $m\ge3$, the whole spectral exponent is $2/m$.
Taking $u=v=\epsilon$, $h=0$ yields roots with displacement
$|g(0)|^{-1/m}|\epsilon|^{2/m}+o(|\epsilon|^{2/m})$ at zero.
Positivity persists at the clocks, so these are admissible alternatives.

When the normalizer is free, the transformed upper-left entry may
also have an arbitrary constant perturbation $a$. The determinant
becomes $(f+a)(g+h)-uv$. The term $a g(0)$ exposes the full pole
of order $m$; the coefficient of $z^m$ in $q$ remains one.
Corollary~\ref{cor:free-normalizer-v93} gives exponent $1/m$.
This example belongs to the positive rational extension; no
real-rooted stochastic factorization of its alternatives is used.
\end{proof}

In the three by three cancellation example of
Section~\ref{sec:sharp-flag}, the true pole order is three although the
unweighted flag-path count is six. Its leading Laurent matrix is
exposed on $\mathcal S_q$. Hence the exact fixed-normalizer and
free-normalizer exponents are both $1/3$, not $1/6$.
The latter assertion no longer requires equality of the pole and path
counts. The proof uses the true numerator and the exact scalar gauge,
not a perturbed triangular path bound.

The determinant identity, Newton rescaling and root-counting tools are
classical perturbation mechanisms; see \cite{GLR82,MBO97,TH,KPM09}.
The assertions proved here specify the full probability-normalized
perturbation space, include higher-order exposure, pull its limiting
set back through the Hellinger metric, and transport it through the
unknown scalar normalizer. They classify this local rational
experiment, rather than all normalization-kernel or channel-rank
singularities of the original stochastic model.
