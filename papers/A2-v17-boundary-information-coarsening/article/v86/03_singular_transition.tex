\section{The rank-degenerate statistical transition}
\label{sec:v86-singular}
We now keep the reference and detector unknown at a single site and
allow the channels to approach rank deficiency. Three clocks suffice
for a constant relative detector, but the statistical price of that
identification diverges at a computable rate. The divergence is a
product of the two channel contrasts, not a fixed inverse constant.

Fix $T_1<T_2<T_3$ and bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-stat-bounds}
 0<D_0<\tau_-<\tau_+<T_1,\qquad 0<a_-<1<a_+<\infty.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal K$ consist of all
$\omega=(U,V,x,\tau,a)$ with $U,V$ two-by-two probability-column
matrices (including their rank-deficient boundary),
$x\in[0,D_0]$, $\tau\in[\tau_-,\tau_+]$ and
$a\in[a_-,a_+]$. The observation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-stat-model}
 p_j=\frac{a(T_j-x)}{a(T_j-x)+(T_j-\tau)},\qquad
 P_j=U\diag(p_j,1-p_j)V^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
Write $\|P-P'\|_* =\max_{1\le j\le3}\|P_j-P'_j\|_F$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-rank-product}
             \rho(\omega)=|\det U\det V|.
\end{equation}
All constants in this section depend only on the fixed bounds and
clocks, except when an additional dependence is stated.

\begin{lemma}[Observable rank and latent rank]\label{lem:v86-rank}
For any two-column probability channels, probability weights
$\pi_1,\pi_2\ge p_0$ with $\pi_1+\pi_2=1$, and $P=U\diag(\pi)V^{\mathsf T}$,
\[
 \sigma_2(P)\ge p_0\sigma_2(U)\sigma_2(V),\qquad
 \sigma_2(P)\le\sqrt2\min\{\sigma_2(U),\sigma_2(V)\}.
\]
Here a zero second singular value is allowed. In the binary model
\eqref{eq:v86-stat-model}, if $s(P)=\max_j|\det P_j|$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-observable-signal}
                 c\rho\le s(P)\le\rho/4.
\end{equation}
For any two binary probability matrices,
$|\det P-\det Q|\le\|P-Q\|_F$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The lower product inequality follows by restricting the three factors
to their rank-two column and row spaces. For the upper inequality,
replace $U$ by its best rank-one approximation and use
$\|V\|_{\rm op}\le\sqrt2$ and $\|\diag(\pi)\|_{\rm op}\le1$;
the analogous argument applies to $V$. In the binary case
$\det P_j=p_j(1-p_j)\det U\det V$. Compactness of the scalar
apparatus bounds gives $p_j,1-p_j\ge p_0>0$, proving the comparison.
Finally the Frobenius norm of the gradient of the determinant of a
two-by-two matrix equals the Frobenius norm of that matrix. Every
matrix on the segment joining two probability matrices has norm at
most one. Integration along the segment proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A uniform one-sided inverse modulus]
\label{thm:v86-product-modulus}
For every $\omega\in\mathcal K$ with $\rho(\omega)>0$ and every
$\omega'\in\mathcal K$, with no rank condition on the candidate,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-modulus-bound}
 |x-x'|+|\tau-\tau'|+|\log a-\log a'|
       \le C\min\left\{1,\frac{\|P-P'\|_*}{\rho(\omega)}\right\}.
\end{equation}
In particular its constant is uniform as either true channel loses
rank; all deterioration is displayed by $\rho^{-1}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $C_b=U_{:b}V_{:b}^{\mathsf T}$,
$r=a/(1+a)$, $h=rx+(1-r)\tau$ and $\gamma=\|C_1-C_2\|_F$.
The exact matrix curve has the representation
\begin{align}
 P(T)&=A+B/(T-h),\qquad A=rC_1+(1-r)C_2,\nonumber\\
 B&=r(1-r)(\tau-x)(C_1-C_2).\label{eq:v86-matrix-pole}
\end{align}
Both $r$ and $1-r$ are bounded below, $\tau-x$ is bounded below,
and $h$ stays in a fixed compact interval to the left of $T_1$.
Taking marginals of $C_1-C_2$ gives the differences of the channel
columns. For binary probability columns their determinant is the
difference of their first entries. Consequently
$\rho\le C\gamma$. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-pole-ratio}
 R:=\frac{\langle P_1-P_2,P_2-P_3\rangle_F}
                {\|P_2-P_3\|_F^2}
   =\frac{T_2-T_1}{T_3-T_2}\frac{T_3-h}{T_1-h},\qquad
       c\gamma\le\|P_2-P_3\|_F\le C\gamma.
\end{equation}

Set $\delta=\|P-P'\|_*$. It suffices to prove the assertion for
$\delta\le c_0\rho$, with a sufficiently small fixed $c_0>0$;
for the remaining pairs boundedness gives the result after enlarging
$C$. In this small-error case the candidate difference $P'_2-P'_3$
has norm comparable to $\gamma$. The ratio in
\eqref{eq:v86-pole-ratio} changes by at most $C\delta/\gamma$.
Its right-hand side is a smooth strictly increasing function of $h$
whose derivative is bounded below on the compact pole interval.
Applying the same identity to the candidate gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-pole-error}
                         |h-h'|\le C\delta/\gamma.
\end{equation}
This first step uses the visible matrix contrast, not its determinant.

Consider the affine matrix polynomial
\[
 K(T)=(T-h)P(T)=r(T-x)C_1+(1-r)(T-\tau)C_2.
\]
Its determinant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-determinant-polynomial}
 d(T)=\det K(T)=r(1-r)\det U\det V\,(T-x)(T-\tau).
\end{equation}
We claim that all three coefficients of $d-d'$ are bounded by
$C\delta$, uniformly in the channel ranks. A naive estimate of the
pole error before taking the determinant would not prove this claim.
Let $Q(T)$ be the affine matrix polynomial interpolating the true
$P_1,P_2$. Interpolating $(T_i-h')P_i$ at $i=1,2$ yields
$\widetilde K=K-(h'-h)Q$. Both $K$ and $Q$ have the form
$s(T)C_1+t(T)C_2$ with uniformly bounded affine scalar coefficients.
The identity
\[
        \det(sC_1+tC_2)=st\det U\det V
\]
therefore bounds the coefficient error from $\det\widetilde K-d$
by $C\rho(|h'-h|+|h'-h|^2)\le C\delta$, using
\eqref{eq:v86-pole-error}, the bounded pole interval and
$\rho\le C\gamma$. On the other hand $K'-\widetilde K$ is the
affine interpolant of $(T_i-h')(P'_i-P_i)$, so its coefficients have
norm at most $C\delta$. The determinant is Lipschitz on the bounded
set of such affine matrix coefficients. This proves the claim.

The leading coefficient of $d$ has absolute value at least $c\rho$.
Taking $c_0$ smaller ensures that the leading coefficient of $d'$ has
absolute value at least $c\rho/2$. Since $d(x)=0$, the claim gives
$|d'(x)|\le C\delta$. But the root ordering and apparatus gap imply
\[
 |d'(x)|=|r'(1-r')\det U'\det V'|\,
                |x-x'|\,|x-\tau'|\ge c\rho|x-x'|.
\]
Evaluating instead at $\tau$ gives
$|\tau-\tau'|\le C\delta/\rho$. Finally
$r=(\tau-h)/(\tau-x)$, so \eqref{eq:v86-pole-error} gives
$|r-r'|\le C\delta/\rho$. The log-odds map on the compact interval
of $r$ proves the estimate for $\log a$.
\end{proof}
The cancellation in the affine determinant polynomial is what retains
one power of the rank product. Replacing it by a generic spectral
condition number would lose the sharp degeneration order.

\subsection{Honest intervals and locally adaptive diameter}
At each clock take $n$ independent readouts from its four-entry
categorical law and denote the empirical matrix by $\widehat P_j$.
For coverage, independence is needed within each clock, not between
clocks. For $0<\zeta<1$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-stat-radius}
 \eta=\left(\frac{2}{n}\log\frac{24}{\zeta}\right)^{1/2},\quad
 \mathcal C=\{\omega\in\mathcal K:
              \max_j\|P_j(\omega)-\widehat P_j\|_F\le\eta\}.
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal C\ne\varnothing$, report the interval $I$ from its
smallest to its largest $x$ coordinate. Otherwise report model
inconsistency and the empty set. Write $w=\mathrm{diam}(I)$, with
$\mathrm{diam}(\varnothing)=0$ only for the loss calculations below.
No action estimate is assigned to an empty set.

\begin{theorem}[Rank-adaptive confidence and risk]
\label{thm:v86-adaptive}
Uniformly over the closed model $\mathcal K$, including singular
channels, $\Pr_\omega\{x\in I\}\ge1-\zeta$.
For each true $\rho>0$, on the same coverage event,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-adaptive-diameter}
 w\le C\min\{1,\eta/\rho\},\qquad
 w\le\min\left\{D_0,
            \frac{C\eta}{(\widehat s-\eta)_+}\right\},\quad
       \widehat s=\max_j|\det\widehat P_j|.
\end{equation}
A zero denominator in the second expression means the bound $D_0$.
For fixed $\zeta$, with $N=3n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-expected-diameter}
      \E_\omega w\le C_\zeta
                          \min\{1,(\sqrt N\rho)^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}
A minimum-residual estimator $\widehat x$ over the full compact model,
using no rank threshold, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-adaptive-risk}
      \E_\omega|\widehat x-x|^2
                         \le C\min\{1,(N\rho^2)^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}
At $\rho=0$ the guarantees are coverage and the bounded parameter-range
bounds, not a shrinking diameter.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Hoeffding's inequality applied to the twelve empirical entries and a
union bound give $e:=\max_j\|\widehat P_j-P_j\|_F\le\eta$ with
probability at least $1-\zeta$. The truth then belongs to $\mathcal C$.
Every feasible candidate is at distance at most $2\eta$ from the true
matrix tuple. Theorem~\ref{thm:v86-product-modulus}, applied separately
to two extreme candidates, gives the first diameter bound.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v86-rank} gives $s(P)\ge\widehat s-\eta$ and
$s(P)\le\rho/4$, which gives the observable version after a change
of constant.

For the expectation bound do not discard the exceptional event. Every
feasible candidate, on every sample outcome, has observation distance
at most $\eta+e$ from the truth. Thus the same one-sided inverse bound
gives $w\le C\min\{1,(\eta+e)/\rho\}$ whenever the set is nonempty;
the inequality also holds with the declared empty-set convention.
For each clock,
$\E\|\widehat P_j-P_j\|_F^2\le1/n$, whence
$\E e^2\le3/n$. It follows that
$\E w\le C\min\{1,(\eta+\sqrt{3/n})/\rho\}$, proving
\eqref{eq:v86-expected-diameter}. A residual minimizer exists by
compactness and differs from the truth in observation norm by at most
$2e$. The same argument, squared and integrated, proves
\eqref{eq:v86-adaptive-risk}. Fixed choices among minimizers are
measurable, since the sample space is finite.
\end{proof}
Rational grids of the simplex, action and throughput factors give a
finite exhaustive implementation. Padding the residual tolerance by a
forward Lipschitz constant times the grid radius and the action interval
by the grid radius preserves coverage. The same proof replaces $\eta$
by $\eta+O(\delta_{\rm grid})$ in the diameter bounds. No inverse
constant or unknown rank floor is needed by this procedure.

\subsection{A least-favourable family with exactly preserved marginals}
The product in the upper bound cannot be replaced by either individual
channel contrast. This can be proved inside strictly positive channels,
without invoking a generic singular model.

Choose interior $x_0,\tau_0$ and let $h=(x_0+\tau_0)/2$,
$d=h-x_0>0$. For $0\le t\le t_0$, with a fixed sufficiently small
$t_0>0$, put
\begin{align}
 x_t&=x_0+t,\quad \tau_t=\tau_0-t,\quad a_t=1,\quad
 \lambda_t=1-t/d,\nonumber\\
 U_{t,:1}&=m+\epsilon_u e_0/(2\lambda_t),\quad
 U_{t,:2}=m-\epsilon_u e_0/(2\lambda_t),\nonumber\\
 V_{t,:1}&=m+\epsilon_v e_0/(2\lambda_t),\quad
 V_{t,:2}=m-\epsilon_v e_0/(2\lambda_t),\qquad
 m=\tfrac12(1,1)^{\mathsf T},\quad e_0=(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}.
                                                \label{eq:v86-lf-family}
\end{align}
Take $0\le\epsilon_u,\epsilon_v\le\epsilon_*$, where $\epsilon_*>0$
is fixed and small. All channel entries and all joint cell
probabilities have a uniform positive lower bound. Positive contrasts
give full rank, and
$\rho(\omega_t)=\epsilon_u\epsilon_v/\lambda_t^2$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact cancellation of the marginal information]
\label{lem:v86-lf}
At every clock, the two observed marginals in
\eqref{eq:v86-lf-family} are independent of $t$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-exact-lf}
       P_j(\omega_t)-P_j(\omega_0)
       =\frac{\epsilon_u\epsilon_v}{4}
                        (\lambda_t^{-2}-1)e_0e_0^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
In particular the difference is the same at all clocks. Its Frobenius
norm is comparable to $\epsilon_u\epsilon_v t$ on $[0,t_0]$.
If either contrast is zero the entire family is observationally
identical, not merely tangent-indistinguishable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The mixing weights satisfy
$p_j(t)=\tfrac12+\lambda_t(p_j(0)-\tfrac12)$.
The channel mean is therefore
$m+\epsilon_u(p_j(0)-\tfrac12)e_0$, independent of $t$, and similarly
for $V$. For two conditionally independent readings, subtracting the
outer product of these marginals from the joint law gives
$p_j(t)(1-p_j(t))\epsilon_u\epsilon_v e_0e_0^{\mathsf T}/\lambda_t^2$.
Using $p_j(t)=1/2+\lambda_tv_j$ cancels the $v_j^2$ term and proves
\eqref{eq:v86-exact-lf}. Finally
$\|e_0e_0^{\mathsf T}\|_F=2$ and
$\lambda_t^{-2}-1$ is comparable to $t$ on the fixed small interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp singular transition and honest-diameter lower bound]
\label{thm:v86-singular-lower}
Assume the $N=3n$ records are independent across clocks as well.
There is $\rho_*>0$ such that for every $0\le\rho_0\le\rho_*$,
with $\mathcal K(\rho_0)=\{\omega\in\mathcal K:\rho(\omega)\ge\rho_0\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-minimax-transition}
 c\min\{1,(N\rho_0^2)^{-1}\}
 \le\inf_{\widetilde x}\sup_{\omega\in\mathcal K(\rho_0)}
                      \E_\omega|\widetilde x-x|^2
 \le C\min\{1,(N\rho_0^2)^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}
At $\rho_0=0$ the minimum on the right is interpreted as one.
For any random interval honest at level $1-\zeta$ over $\mathcal K$,
where $0<\zeta<1/4$, and any positive
$\epsilon_u,\epsilon_v\le\epsilon_*$, its expected length at the
base point of \eqref{eq:v86-lf-family} is at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-honest-lower}
 c_\zeta\min\{1,(\sqrt N\epsilon_u\epsilon_v)^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}
The same nonvanishing lower bound holds when one contrast is zero.
Thus the uniformly informative boundary is
$\sqrt N\rho\asymp1$; for equally weak contrasts $\epsilon$ it is
$N\epsilon^4\asymp1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:v86-adaptive}. For the lower bound
write $\rho_b=\epsilon_u\epsilon_v$. The positive cell-probability
lower bound and Lemma~\ref{lem:v86-lf} give
\[
 \mathrm{KL}(\mathbb P_t^{(N)},\mathbb P_0^{(N)})
       \le CN\rho_b^2t^2,
\]
using $\mathrm{KL}(P,Q)\le\sum(P-Q)^2/Q$ for each categorical law
and addition under independence. Choose
$t=c_1\min\{t_0,(\sqrt N\rho_b)^{-1}\}$ with a sufficiently small
fixed $c_1>0$. The total variation distance is at most $1/2$.
The test selecting the nearer of $x_0,x_t$ turns any estimator into
a two-point test; its larger squared risk is at least $t^2/16$.
For a specified $0<\rho_0\le\rho_*$ choose
$\epsilon_u=\epsilon_v=\sqrt{\rho_0}$. Both alternatives have rank
product at least $\rho_0$, since $\lambda_t\le1$, and are in the
fixed compact model when $\rho_*$ is small enough. This proves the
uniform lower bound. If $\rho_0=0$, choose a zero contrast and two
fixed distinct $t$; their laws agree exactly.

For an honest interval $J$, coverage at both alternatives implies
\[
 \Pr_0\{x_0,x_t\in J\}
 \ge1-2\zeta-\|\mathbb P_t^{(N)}-\mathbb P_0^{(N)}\|_{\rm TV}.
\]
Decrease $c_1$, now depending on $\zeta$, so that this is bounded
below by a positive constant. On this event the interval has length
at least $t$. This proves \eqref{eq:v86-honest-lower}. At zero
contrast the variation distance is zero and $t$ can be fixed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The observation modulus and its predecessors}
For a true parameter $\omega$ define the one-sided action modulus
\[
 \Omega_\omega(\delta)=\sup\{|x'-x|:\omega'\in\mathcal K,
                              \|P(\omega')-P(\omega)\|_*\le\delta\}.
\]
The determinant argument computes a uniform upper bound
$C\min\{1,\delta/\rho\}$; the family
\eqref{eq:v86-lf-family} attains its order, including its nonzero
value at zero noise when a contrast vanishes. A residual confidence
set has diameter bounded by twice this modulus at its observation
radius. Thus exact fibres, weak identification and confidence diameter
are three scales of the same observation quotient in this experiment.
This assertion does not identify the moduli of all detector spaces
or replace the geometric stability theorem used later.

The use of moduli to compare deterministic recovery and statistical
risk is classical; see Donoho \cite{V86Donoho}. Cai--Low
\cite{V86CaiLow} develop honest adaptation through between-class
moduli for linear functionals. Dufour \cite{V86Dufour} identifies
obstructions to uniformly informative inference near nonidentification.
Those general principles are not claimed as new here. The additional
calculation is the uniform product modulus for this nonlinear
unknown-channel experiment, the exact marginal-preserving family,
and the matching transition in both squared risk and honest expected
length. The compact action range gives nonshrinking bounded intervals
at the singularity, not the unbounded intervals of an unbounded
parameter experiment. The result is for the constant-relative-detector,
binary, three-clock model; higher-degree and higher-rank singular
strata are not silently assigned the same exponent.
