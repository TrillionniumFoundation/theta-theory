\section{Spatial self-calibration with two clocks}
\label{sec:v86-shared}
A clock count has meaning only relative to the indices over which the
nuisance may vary. We first treat a shared apparatus. The readout
channels remain unknown and may vary with the observation site.

Let $Q$ be a connected smooth domain without isolated points, and fix
$T_1<T_2$. At $z\in Q$ let $x(z)<T_1$ be continuous. We assume that
$x$ is not constant on any nonempty open subset of $Q$. Let $U(z)$ and
$V(z)$ be continuous probability-column matrices, with two columns
and full column rank. For two unknown constants $k_1,k_2>0$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-shared-model}
 r_j(z)=k_j(T_j-x(z)),\quad p_j(z)=\frac{r_j(z)}{1+r_j(z)},\qquad
 P_j(z)=U(z)\diag(p_j(z),1-p_j(z))V(z)^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
The matrices $P_j$ are the normalized projective observations. In the
two-arm apparatus this model follows from a constant unknown reference
$\tau$ and a shared relative detector law $c$:
\[
 k_j=\frac{\exp c(T_j)}{T_j-\tau},\qquad
       \sup_Qx<\tau<T_1.
\]
Only its values at the two clocks enter; no degree bound on $c$ is
needed. Sharing includes relative throughput, the constant term of $c$.
An independently varying throughput is a different nuisance class.

\begin{lemma}[Two mixtures determine their latent factors]
\label{lem:v86-two-mixtures}
At a site where $p_1\ne p_2$, the pair $(P_1,P_2)$ determines the two
channel columns and the two probability vectors, up to one simultaneous
interchange of the components. Hence it determines
$(r_1,r_2)$ up to simultaneous inversion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The column and row spaces of either rank-two matrix are those of $U$
and $V$. Choose orthonormal bases $A,B$ of these spaces and set
$S_j=A^{\mathsf T}P_jB$. With $H=A^{\mathsf T}U$ and
$G=B^{\mathsf T}V$, both invertible, we have
\[
 S_2S_1^{-1}=H\diag\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1},
                    \frac{1-p_2}{1-p_1}\right)H^{-1}.
\]
The eigenvalues are distinct. Its eigenlines recover the lines of the
columns of $U$; requiring column sum one recovers the actual columns.
Then $U^\dagger P_j=\diag(p_j,1-p_j)V^{\mathsf T}$.
The row sums give the weights and normalization gives $V$. The only
choice is the common permutation of the two eigenlines.
\end{proof}
This is the ordinary observable-pencil argument, not a new latent
factorization theorem; compare \cite{V83AMR,V83BJR}. The issue below is
whether its unobserved permutations can conceal another action field.

\begin{theorem}[Two-clock spatial rigidity]\label{thm:v86-shared}
Among the models \eqref{eq:v86-shared-model}, the two observed matrix
fields determine $x$ and $k_1,k_2$ uniquely. No channel labelling is
supplied. Points where the two mixture profiles coincide are allowed.
The conclusion also holds if a competing action is merely continuous;
it need not satisfy the nowhere-local-constancy condition separately.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The equality $r_1=r_2$ is the linear equation
$k_1(T_1-x)=k_2(T_2-x)$. It has at most one solution in $x$, since
$T_1\ne T_2$. Its preimage has empty interior. Thus the set $Q_*$
on which Lemma~\ref{lem:v86-two-mixtures} applies is open and dense.
Write a competing model with primes. At every point of $Q_*$ one of
two alternatives holds. If the components agree in order, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-direct-ambiguity}
 x'=a_jx+T_j(1-a_j),\qquad a_j=k_j/k'_j,\quad j=1,2.
\end{equation}
Unless $(k'_1,k'_2)=(k_1,k_2)$, consistency of these two expressions
is a nonzero linear equation in $x$, and therefore holds at at most
one action value. Indeed an identically zero equation would first
give $a_1=a_2=a$, then $(T_1-T_2)(1-a)=0$, hence $a=1$.
If the components are interchanged, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-swap-ambiguity}
 x'=T_j-\frac{b_j}{T_j-x},\qquad b_j=(k_jk'_j)^{-1}>0.
\end{equation}
Consistency requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-swap-polynomial}
 (T_1-T_2)(T_1-x)(T_2-x)-b_1(T_2-x)+b_2(T_1-x)=0.
\end{equation}
This polynomial is quadratic with nonzero leading coefficient, so it
has at most two roots. If the clock constants differed, $x(Q_*)$
would consequently be contained in a set of at most three points.
Continuity and density would give the same conclusion for $x(Q)$,
contrary to the hypothesis. Thus $k'_j=k_j$.

With these constants fixed, the direct alternative gives $x'=x$.
The swapped alternative remains possible only at the at most two
roots of \eqref{eq:v86-swap-polynomial}. The complement of their
preimages and of the single profile-collision preimage is dense:
each preimage is closed with empty interior, and there are finitely
many. On this dense set $x'=x$. Continuity proves equality on $Q$.
No continuation of eigenvectors through a multiple eigenvalue has
been assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite calibration and local stability]
\label{prop:v86-calibration}
Four sites with distinct true action values and noncoincident mixture
profiles determine $k_1,k_2$ from their two-clock matrices. A finite
procedure consists of at most $2^4$ labelling choices and linear
systems of size $4\times2$. On a fixed correctly labelled regular
neighbourhood, two sites with distinct actions already give a smooth
local inverse for their actions and the clock constants. The local
inverse is Lipschitz on compact subsets with positive channel,
eigenvalue, action-separation and clock-constant margins.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each simultaneous-inversion choice at the four sites, denote the
chosen odds by $s_{ij}$ and solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-calibration-system}
          s_{i1}h_1-s_{i2}h_2=T_1-T_2,\qquad i=1,\ldots,4.
\end{equation}
Keep only solutions $h_j>0$ whose resulting actions
$T_j-s_{ij}h_j$ agree and lie in the declared action range. The true
choice is retained. If another retained solution had different clock
constants, the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v86-shared} would allow its
four distinct action values to fall in a set of at most three values.
This is impossible. For the true choice, any two rows with different
actions are independent: the determinant of their un-signed odds
matrix is $k_1k_2(T_1-T_2)(x_i-x_l)$.
Thus the retained clock constants are unique, even when more than one
labelling choice gives them.

For two locally labelled sites put
$a=(r_{11}-r_{21})/(r_{12}-r_{22})$. Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-explicit-calibration}
 k_1=\frac{r_{11}-a r_{12}}{T_1-T_2},\qquad
 k_2=k_1/a,\qquad x_i=T_1-r_{i1}/k_1.
\end{equation}
All denominators are nonzero. The spectral normalization in the lemma
is smooth near a fixed simple spectrum, and these formulas are smooth.
Bounds on their derivatives give the stated compact-class estimate.
\end{proof}
The four-site assertion calibrates the two clock constants, not an
arbitrary finite set of action values at the exceptional swapped roots.
Continuity supplies those action values in Theorem~\ref{thm:v86-shared}.
The two-site assertion is local and does not supply global labels.

\begin{lemma}[Boundary distances have no open plateau]
\label{lem:v86-no-plateau}
For a smooth simple metric on a compact manifold of dimension at least
two, the boundary distance is not constant on any nonempty open subset
of $(\partial M\times\partial M)\setminus\mathrm{diag}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An open plateau would contain a product $A\times B$ of disjoint open
boundary sets. Fix $y\in A$. First variation with respect to the first
endpoint says that the initial velocity of the geodesic from $y$ to
every $z\in B$ is orthogonal to $T_y\partial M$. It must be the unique
inward unit normal. Uniqueness of the geodesic equation and simplicity
then give one exit endpoint, whereas $B$ contains more than one point.
This contradiction proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp shared-apparatus geometric clock threshold]
\label{cor:v86-shared-geometry}
Let $M$ be a compact surface with a smooth simple metric $g$. Observe
\eqref{eq:v86-shared-model} at every distinct boundary pair with
$x=d_g$, a constant unknown reference delay larger than the diameter,
and a shared relative detector law. Two clocks determine $g$ up to a
boundary-fixing diffeomorphism. One clock does not suffice, even with
a fixed constant reference, a fixed constant relative detector law,
and strictly positive full-rank channels. The indistinguishable
one-clock metrics can be chosen in a small scaling family near any
fixed simple metric with strict apparatus margins.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The lemma and Theorem~\ref{thm:v86-shared} determine the boundary
distance. The boundary-distance rigidity theorem of Pestov--Uhlmann
\cite{V84PU} gives the claimed isometry. This is the only geometric
rigidity input.

For necessity fix one clock $T$, a constant reference $\tau$, and a
constant relative throughput, all with strict margins. Put
$g_t=(1+t)^2g_0$. Then $x_t=(1+t)d_{g_0}$ changes, while the reference
and detector remain fixed. Let $w_t=(p_t,1-p_t)^{\mathsf T}$ be its
two mixture weights. Start with fixed strictly positive full-rank
probability matrices $U,V$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v86-one-clock-transform}
 S_t=I+(w_0-w_t)\one^{\mathsf T},\quad
 U_t=US_t,\quad V_t=V\diag(w_0)S_t^{-\mathsf T}\diag(w_t)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Since $\one^{\mathsf T}(w_0-w_t)=0$, $S_t$ is invertible with
$S_t^{-1}=I-(w_0-w_t)\one^{\mathsf T}$,
$\one^{\mathsf T}S_t=\one^{\mathsf T}$ and $S_tw_t=w_0$.
Consequently $U_t,V_t$ have column sums one, and
\[
        U_t\diag(w_t)V_t^{\mathsf T}
                         =U\diag(w_0)V^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Uniformly small $|t|$ preserves strict positivity and full rank,
because the weights have a uniform positive lower bound. The matrices
$S_t$ themselves need not be stochastic; the actual channel matrices
$U_t,V_t$ are positive stochastic matrices. All fields are smooth off
the boundary diagonal. Different $t$ give different boundary distances,
so the metrics are not boundary-fixing isometric.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What is shared, and what remains a gauge}
The pointwise polynomial threshold and the shared-apparatus threshold
concern different experiments. In the former the detector can be
rechosen at every boundary pair. In the latter,
\[
       \log r_j(z)=b_j+\log(T_j-x(z)),\qquad b_j=\log k_j,
\]
is taken modulo functions of the clock index alone. Spatial contrasts
remove this nuisance, but leave an unobserved inversion at every site.
The linear and quadratic obstruction calculation removes that last
ambiguity. It is not enough to impose a constant reference while
allowing the relative throughput to vary independently with $z$.

For an unrestricted shared detector, the two clocks identify $k_j$,
not its unsampled values or the reference separately: any admissible
$\tau'$ can be accompanied by
$c'(T_j)=\log k_j+\log(T_j-\tau')$. For a constant relative detector,
$k_j=a/(T_j-\tau)$, the recovered ratio $R=k_1/k_2>1$ instead gives
$\tau=(RT_1-T_2)/(R-1)$ and $a=k_1(T_1-\tau)$. The assertion about
geometry does not require recovering this otherwise unobservable
apparatus decomposition.

The closest predecessors are the latent-pencil method and blind
calibration, not a new boundary-distance theorem. The additional
statement is the sharp change of the geometric clock threshold under
shared nuisance, with unknown channels, unresolved labels and actual
one-clock metric alternatives. Classical boundary rigidity is invoked
only after the selective experiment has determined its input.
