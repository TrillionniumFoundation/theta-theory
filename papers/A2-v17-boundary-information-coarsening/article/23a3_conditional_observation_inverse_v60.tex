\subsection{Contracted evaluations and conditional stability from real observations}
\label{subsec:v60-real-observation}

The complete analytic inverse has two distinct ingredients: a stationary
representation of its derivative and a function-space inversion argument.
We first isolate the latter.  We then combine the inverse with a quantitative
restriction estimate to obtain a local conditional bound from real endpoint
laws.  The conclusion loses holomorphic radius and retains an analytic
prior.  It therefore does not identify real observation accuracy with the
same-disc norm of Theorem~\ref{thm:v59-analytic-contact-inverse}.

\begin{proposition}[An inversion criterion for contracted evaluations]
\label{prop:v60-contracted-criterion}
Let $q\ge1$, $d\ge1$, and let $X_q$ be the closed subspace of
$H^\infty(D_R;\mathbb C^d)$ consisting of functions vanishing to order
at least $q$, with the maximum component norm.  Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-abstract-operator}
 (Lf)_b(z)=(A(z)f(z))_b+
       \sum_{i\ge1}\sum_{c=1}^d w_{bci}(z)f_c(\phi_{bci}(z)),
\end{equation}
where all coefficients and maps are holomorphic on $D_R$,
$\sup_{D_R}\|A^{-1}\|\le a^{-1}$, $A$ is bounded, and
\[
 |\phi_{bci}(z)|\le\rho^i|z|,\qquad
 \sup_{b,z}\sum_c|w_{bci}(z)|\le W,\qquad 0<\rho<1.
\]
Write $L=A+K$.  Choose an integer $m>q$ with
$\nu_m=a^{-1}W\rho^m/(1-\rho^m)<1$.
Then $L:X_q\to X_q$ is boundedly invertible if and only if the
finitely many matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-graded-blocks}
 (B_n)_{bc}=A_{bc}(0)+
       \sum_{i\ge1}w_{bci}(0)\bigl(\phi_{bci}'(0)\bigr)^n,
                      \qquad q\le n<m,
\end{equation}
are invertible.  The operator $K$ is compact.  This criterion applies
to the derivative at a point of a holomorphic map between open subsets
of $X_q$ and then yields a local holomorphic inverse of that map.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Higher-order Schwarz estimates give
\[
 \|K|_{X_n}\|\le \frac{W\rho^n}{1-\rho^n},\qquad n\ge q.
\]
Here $X_n$ denotes the corresponding order-$n$ vanishing subspace.
The series in \eqref{eq:v60-abstract-operator} converges in operator
norm.  Both summands, and multiplication by $A^{-1}$, preserve every
$X_n$.  Thus $L|_{X_m}$ is invertible by the Neumann series, with
inverse norm at most $a^{-1}/(1-\nu_m)$.

Let $P_m$ retain Taylor degrees $q,\ldots,m-1$.  Cauchy's coefficient
bound makes $P_m$ a bounded projection on $X_q$.
Modulo $X_m$, the matrix of $L$ is block lower triangular with diagonal
\eqref{eq:v60-graded-blocks}: only the constant weight and the linear
part of an argument contribute to the first nonzero degree of a
variation.  If those blocks are invertible, solve this finite quotient
first and then solve the residual in $X_m$, exactly as in
\eqref{eq:v59-full-linear-inverse}.  In particular, if
$C_0=\|(P_mL|_{P_mX_q})^{-1}P_m\|$, then
\[
 \|L^{-1}\|\le C_0+\frac{a^{-1}}{1-\nu_m}(1+\|L\|C_0).
\]
Conversely, if the finite quotient were singular, a nonzero polynomial
class $p$ would satisfy $Lp\in X_m$.  Solving
$Lt=Lp$ inside $X_m$ would give a nonzero kernel vector $p-t$.
Consequently invertibility of $L$ implies the stated finite condition.

To prove compactness, replace each evaluated $f_c$ by its Taylor
polynomial of degrees $q,\ldots,n-1$.  The resulting operator has rank
at most $d(n-q)$.  Cauchy's coefficient bound gives the operator-norm
remainder estimate
\[
       \frac{W\rho^n}{(1-\rho)(1-\rho^n)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The truncation is evaluated only at contracted arguments; convergence
of Taylor partial sums on the whole open disc is not asserted.
The final assertion follows from the holomorphic inverse function
theorem, or from the contraction proof already given above.
\end{proof}

For the contact action, $q=3$, $d=2$, the initial multiplier is diagonal,
and the only nonzero weight at visit $i$ is $v_{b,i}$ in the component
of type $b+i$.  Its bound is $2W$ in the notation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v59-envelope-operator}.  The matrices $B_n$ are the
actual $M_n$.  The protected-domain construction and the vanishing
terminal term prove that this is indeed the derivative of the physical
action; they are not assumptions supplied by functional analysis.
The qualitative Fredholm observation suggested in~\cite{A2ReviewV59}
is now also transparent: $A^{-1}L$ is identity plus compact, and
nonresonant jets kill its analytic kernel.  The finite-low-order and
tail construction above additionally records an inverse bound.
Neither argument makes the inverse improve holomorphic radius.

\begin{lemma}[A bounded analytic function from its real restriction]
\label{lem:v60-real-to-disc}
Fix $0<s<r<\varrho<R$ with $r+2s<\varrho$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-propagation-constants}
 A_0=\frac{2(r+s)}s,
 \qquad b_0=\frac{r+s}{\varrho-s}<1,
 \qquad \vartheta=\frac{\log(1/b_0)}{\log(A_0/b_0)}\in(0,1).
\end{equation}
If $F\in H^\infty(D_R)$, $\|F\|_R\le M$, and
$\sup_{[-s,s]}|F|\le\epsilon\le M$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-propagation}
 \|F\|_r\le
 \left(1+\frac{\varrho}{\varrho-r}\right)
                  M^{1-\vartheta}\epsilon^{\vartheta}.
\end{equation}
The assertion includes complex-valued functions and the case
$\epsilon=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $n\ge1$ take the $n$ roots $x_k$ of $T_n(x/s)$, where $T_n$ is
the Chebyshev polynomial.  Their monic nodal polynomial is
$P_n(z)=2^{1-n}s^nT_n(z/s)$, and
\[
 |P_n'(x_k)|\ge n(s/2)^{n-1}.
\]
The Lagrange interpolation polynomial $I_nF$ therefore satisfies
$\|I_nF\|_r\le\epsilon A_0^{n-1}$: the numerator of each basis
polynomial is a product of $n-1$ factors of modulus at most $r+s$.
The residue formula on $|\zeta|=\varrho$ gives
\[
 F(z)-I_nF(z)=\frac1{2\pi i}
   \int_{|\zeta|=\varrho}
       \frac{F(\zeta)P_n(z)}{P_n(\zeta)(\zeta-z)}\,d\zeta.
\]
This holds also at the interpolation nodes by continuity.  The bounds
$|P_n(z)|\le(r+s)^n$ and
$|P_n(\zeta)|\ge(\varrho-s)^n$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-interpolation-balance}
 \|F\|_r\le \epsilon A_0^{n-1}
           +\frac{\varrho}{\varrho-r}M b_0^n.
\end{equation}
For $0<\epsilon<M$, choose
$n=\lceil\log(M/\epsilon)/\log(A_0/b_0)\rceil$.
The two terms are bounded by their respective constants times
$M^{1-\vartheta}\epsilon^\vartheta$.  For $\epsilon=M$, take
$n=1$; for $\epsilon=0$, let $n$ tend to infinity.
The case $M=0$ is immediate.  All contours are strictly inside the
given holomorphic domain.
\end{proof}

This is a conditional continuation estimate, not continuity of
unrestricted analytic continuation.  Its elementary interpolation
proof is included to display the constants; the general ill-conditioning
of continuation under analytic priors is discussed in~\cite{Trefethen2020}.
We use only the local estimate just proved, not continuation around an
entire obstacle.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional contact stability from real endpoint laws]
\label{thm:v60-real-observation-stability}
Fix a positive-curvature real analytic anchored pair and a positive gap.
There exist radii $0<s<s_+<r<\varrho<R$, with
$r+2s<\varrho$, and a bounded neighborhood $\mathcal U$ of that pair
and gap in the real part of
\[
 \mathbb C\times\{\psi\in H^\infty(D_R;\mathbb C^2):
                              \psi_b(0)=\psi_b'(0)=0\},
\]
for which the following assertions hold.  The curvatures and gap may
vary within $\mathcal U$.  Choose a fixed sufficiently small positive
offset $d$ and the radii so that
$Q_+=[-s_+,s_+]^2$ is strictly inside every relevant positive-density
region.  Put $Q=[-s,s]^2$ and fix $a\in(-s,s)\setminus\{0\}$.

For each of two admissible members $(g,\psi)$ and
$(\widetilde g,\widetilde\psi)$ retain a same-type limiting endpoint
density for each type, possibly after separate positive recording
factors.  On $Q_+$ these densities have the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-recorded-density}
 f_b(u,v)=c_b U_b(u)V_b(v)
           \{d-S_b(u)-S_b(v)\},\qquad c_b,U_b,V_b>0,
\end{equation}
and the analogous form for $\widetilde f_b$.
Assume common bounds $m_f\le f_b,\widetilde f_b\le M_f$ and
$q_{f_b},q_{\widetilde f_b}\ge q_*>0$, where $q_f$ is the scalar
anchor \eqref{eq:v26-ratio-anchor}.  The two sets of recording factors
need not coincide, and need not have holomorphic extensions.
The origins, signs, physical units and $d$ are fixed observation
conventions.

Let $e_g=|g-\widetilde g|$ and
$e_\infty=\max_b\|f_b-\widetilde f_b\|_{C^0(Q)}$.
With $\vartheta$ as in \eqref{eq:v60-propagation-constants}, there is
a finite constant $C$ such that, for sufficiently small errors,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-real-density-bound}
       \|\psi-\widetilde\psi\|_r
                    \le C(e_g+e_\infty^\vartheta).
\end{equation}
For every $0<t<r$ this simultaneously controls all contact coefficients:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-all-real-coefficients}
 \sum_{n\ge2}\frac{t^n}{n!}
       \max_b|\psi_b^{(n)}(0)-\widetilde\psi_b^{(n)}(0)|
 \le C\frac{(t/r)^2}{1-t/r}
                         (e_g+e_\infty^\vartheta).
\end{equation}

If, in addition, all four densities have a common Lipschitz bound $H$
on $Q_+$, then, with
$e_1=\max_b\|f_b-\widetilde f_b\|_{L^1(Q_+)}$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-weak-density-bound}
       \|\psi-\widetilde\psi\|_r
                          \le C_H(e_g+e_1^{\vartheta/3}).
\end{equation}
In particular $e_1$ may be replaced by twice the maximum total variation
of the recorded laws.  For a rectangular grid on $Q_+$ of side at most
$\delta$, with the outside as an additional category, it may instead
be replaced by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-histogram-error}
 \eta=e_{\rm cell}+2\sqrt2 H|Q_+|\delta,
 \qquad
 e_{\rm cell}=\max_b
       \|\mathbf p_\delta(P_b)-\mathbf p_\delta(\widetilde P_b)\|_1.
\end{equation}
The constants depend on the fixed local analytic neighborhood, the radii
and the stated density/anchor margins, but not on a jet truncation order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We specify the neighborhood before using small observational error.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v59-holomorphic-halfline}, including its finite
leading-variable extension, gives an action map on a disc $D_R$ near
the chosen base.  Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:v59-analytic-contact-inverse} to the same base on a
smaller disc to obtain an inverse ball on $D_r$, with $r<R/4$.
Write $Y_r$ for the space of pairs vanishing to order two.  The bounded
splitting into the Hessians and the order-three tail turns the last
clause of that theorem into a local inverse of
\[
       (g,\psi)\longmapsto(g,\mathscr S_g(\psi))
                       \quad\hbox{in }\mathbb C\times Y_r.
\]
Indeed $|S_b''(0)|\le2r^{-2}\|S_b\|_r$, and subtracting the
quadratic polynomial is a bounded operation.  Thus no additional
measurement of the curvatures or action Hessians is required here.
On a smaller convex input ball, its two-point bound has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-joint-inner-inverse}
 \|\psi-\widetilde\psi\|_r
       \le C_r\{e_g+\|\mathscr S_g(\psi)
                   -\mathscr S_{\widetilde g}(\widetilde\psi)\|_r\}.
\end{equation}

Intersect the $D_R$ construction neighborhood with the inverse image
of this $D_r$ ball under restriction.  This is an open neighborhood of
the base, independent of the observed errors.  Shrink it to a bounded
ball on which the action norms on $D_R$ have a common bound $B$.
Continuity of the holomorphic forward map provides such a $B$; no
inverse norm is inferred from the observations.  The two constructions
of the action commute with restriction: on the smaller real collar
both are the unique small physical half-line action, and equality
extends holomorphically to $D_r$.  Choose
$\varrho=R/2$ and then $s_+<r/2$ small enough for the common physical
collar and fixed offset, followed by $s<s_+$.  These choices imply
$r+2s<\varrho$.  Positive contact curvature permits a fixed nonzero
anchor with a uniform action-anchor margin.  The recorded density
bounds remain separate assumptions on the recording experiment.

For \eqref{eq:v60-recorded-density}, the four-density identity cancels
$c_b,U_b,V_b$ exactly, just as in
\eqref{eq:v26-rank-one-defect}.  On the stipulated density range its
ratios are Lipschitz in the uniform real norm.  Moreover
\[
 |q_f-q_{\widetilde f}|=
 \frac{|R_f(a,a)-R_{\widetilde f}(a,a)|}{q_f+q_{\widetilde f}},
\]
so the anchor division is uniformly controlled.  The final map
$t\mapsto dt/(1+t)$ is Lipschitz for the nonnegative real $t$ here.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v60-real-action-bound}
 \max_b\sup_{[-s,s]}|S_b-\widetilde S_b|\le C_1 e_\infty.
\end{equation}
This argument compares two realizable laws directly; it does not
assume that the straight line joining their densities factorizes.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:v60-real-to-disc} componentwise to
$S-\widetilde S$, whose $D_R$ norm is at most $2B$.
Equations \eqref{eq:v60-real-action-bound} and
\eqref{eq:v60-joint-inner-inverse} give
\eqref{eq:v60-real-density-bound}.  Cauchy's coefficient bound on
$D_r$ and summation of a geometric series give
\eqref{eq:v60-all-real-coefficients}, including odd orders.

For the weaker norm, put $w=f_b-\widetilde f_b$ and choose a fixed
nonnegative smooth kernel $k$ supported in the unit ball of
$\mathbb R^2$, with integral one.  For $x\in Q$ and
$0<h\le h_0<\operatorname{dist}(Q,\partial Q_+)$,
\[
 |w(x)-(k_h*w)(x)|\le C Hh,
 \qquad |(k_h*w)(x)|\le\|k\|_\infty h^{-2}e_1.
\]
Take $h=h_0 e_1^{1/3}$ when $0<e_1\le1$; for $e_1=0$ let
$h$ decrease to zero.  Thus $e_\infty\le C_H e_1^{1/3}$, proving
\eqref{eq:v60-weak-density-bound}.  Only the stated first-order real
bound is used at this step.  The restriction of an $L^1$ difference
to $Q_+$ is at most twice the total variation of the full laws.
Finally, the $L^1$ difference between one density and its cell averages
is at most $\sqrt2 H|Q_+|\delta$.  The difference of the two cell
averages integrates to the sum of the absolute cell-mass differences.
Adding the two approximation errors proves $e_1\le\eta$ in
\eqref{eq:v60-histogram-error}.
\end{proof}

The neighborhood and outer holomorphic bound in this theorem are a
geometric regularity prior, not a consequence of small real data error.
The radius loss is essential to the argument.  For example,
$F_N(z)=\epsilon(z/R)^N$ has norm $\epsilon$ on $D_R$ while all its
fixed-order real norms on $[-s,s]$, $s<R$, tend to zero; its norm on
$D_r$, $r<R$, also tends to zero.  Thus the estimate does not conflict
with the same-disc topology distinction.  The exponent is explicit
but not asserted optimal.  The theorem compares locally admissible
candidates and controls their complete germs on an inner disc.  It
neither constructs a map on arbitrary corrupted densities nor identifies
a global matching branch or an unknown contact frame.  The histogram
bound is deterministic; no uniform preparation cost over arbitrarily
small recording efficiencies follows from it.  Quantified continuation
to entire obstacles and the physical acquisition theorem retain their
separate hypotheses and proofs.
