\section{An amplitude-free inverse from one endpoint law}
\label{sec:v26-single-offset}

An endpoint position in the plane supplies a point of the unknown boundary.
A signed transverse endpoint supplies only one coordinate of that point.
The result below concerns the latter datum.  It recovers the two half-line
actions from one positive-offset endpoint law of each type, without retaining
longitudinal positions, residual times, waiting counts, or the unknown flux
amplitudes.  The gap remains a separate onset datum.  This strengthens the
support-family inverse of Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity}: a continuum
of offsets is unnecessary when the density, rather than only its support,
is retained.

\subsection{A four-density identity}

Fix a known \(d>0\).  Let \(I=[-r,r]\), and suppose on a neighborhood of
\(I\) that \(S(0)=S'(0)=0\), \(S''>0\), and \(B>0\).  Shrink \(r\)
until \(\max_I S<d/4\).  The endpoint law has on \(I^2\) the positive
smooth density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-density}
 f(u,v)=Z^{-1}B(u)B(v)\{d-S(u)-S(v)\},\qquad Z>0.
\end{equation}
Its continuation outside this interior square may vanish at the moving
boundary.  A law in this class determines the displayed continuous density
uniquely: two continuous versions equal almost everywhere on an open set
are equal everywhere there.  Thus values on the slices below are determined
by the probability law, although a finite sample does not supply them
without estimation.

Choose a fixed \(a\in I\setminus\{0\}\).  For a positive density \(f\)
define
\begin{align}
 R_f(u,v)&=\frac{f(u,v)f(0,0)}{f(u,0)f(0,v)},
                                      \label{eq:v26-density-ratio}\\
 q_f&=\sqrt{1-R_f(a,a)},\qquad
 T_f(u)=\frac{1-R_f(u,a)}{q_f},
                                      \label{eq:v26-ratio-anchor}\\
 \mathcal S_d(f)(u)&=\frac{dT_f(u)}{1+T_f(u)},\qquad
 \mathcal B(f)(u)=\frac{f(u,0)}{f(0,0)}(1+T_f(u)).
                                      \label{eq:v26-explicit-inverse}
\end{align}
The square root in \eqref{eq:v26-ratio-anchor} is the positive scalar root
at the fixed nonzero anchor, not a pointwise square root differentiated at
the degenerate minimum \(u=0\).

\begin{theorem}[Single-offset law inversion]\label{thm:v26-density-inverse}
For \eqref{eq:v26-density}, all quantities in
\eqref{eq:v26-ratio-anchor}--\eqref{eq:v26-explicit-inverse} are defined and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-recovery}
              \mathcal S_d(f)=S,\qquad
              \mathcal B(f)=B/B(0)\quad\hbox{on }I.
\end{equation}
Consequently one same-type signed endpoint law at one known positive offset
determines the complete action germ and the normalized amplitude germ.
Neither an amplitude model nor its normalizing constant is supplied.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set \(t(u)=S(u)/(d-S(u))\).  Direct cancellation in
\eqref{eq:v26-density-ratio} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-rank-one-defect}
 R_f(u,v)=\frac{d\{d-S(u)-S(v)\}}{(d-S(u))(d-S(v))}
             =1-t(u)t(v).
\end{equation}
Strict convexity and the normalization of \(S\) imply \(S(a)>0\), hence
\(q_f=t(a)>0\).  Therefore \(T_f(u)=t(u)\), and solving this relation
for \(S(u)\) gives the first identity in \eqref{eq:v26-recovery}.  Also
\[
 \frac{f(u,0)}{f(0,0)}
       =\frac{B(u)}{B(0)}\frac{d-S(u)}d
       =\frac{B(u)/B(0)}{1+t(u)},
\]
which proves the second.  No symmetry of \(S\) or \(B\) was used.
In particular values at negative and positive \(u\) remain separate and
all odd derivatives of \(S\) are retained.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fixed-order stability and finite flights}

\begin{proposition}[Interior stability of the law inverse]
\label{prop:v26-density-stability}
Fix an integer \(M\ge0\), the interval \(I\), the anchor \(a\), and \(d>0\).
On a compact family of densities \eqref{eq:v26-density} with uniformly
positive \(f\), uniformly bounded \(C^M\) norms, and \(q_f\ge q_*>0\),
there are \(\varepsilon_*,C_M>0\) such that
\[
 \|\widetilde f-f\|_{C^M(I^2)}<\varepsilon_*
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|\mathcal S_d(\widetilde f)-S\|_{C^M(I)}
 +\|\mathcal B(\widetilde f)-B/B(0)\|_{C^M(I)}
 \le C_M\|\widetilde f-f\|_{C^M(I^2)}.
\]
The perturbed function need not factorize or have total mass one.  It must
be defined on a neighborhood of the closed square.  The constants are
fixed-order constants; they are not uniform as \(M\to\infty\), \(a\to0\),
or \(d\downarrow0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Evaluation on each fixed slice is a bounded linear map on \(C^M\).
All density denominators in \eqref{eq:v26-density-ratio} have a common
positive lower bound.  Products and reciprocal functions are locally
Lipschitz in \(C^M\) on sets with such bounds, as follows by differentiating
\(hh^{-1}=1\) inductively.  Thus the ratios are locally Lipschitz in
\(C^M\).  The scalar \(1-R_f(a,a)\) is at least \(q_*^2\), so its
positive square root has a bounded derivative on a common neighborhood.
The denominator \(q_{\widetilde f}\) is then at least \(q_*/2\).
For the exact density, \(1+T_f\ge1\); after a further uniform reduction of
\(\varepsilon_*\), \(1+T_{\widetilde f}\ge1/2\).  Applying the same
product and reciprocal bounds to \eqref{eq:v26-explicit-inverse} proves the
claim.  This proof avoids differentiation of \(\sqrt{t(u)^2}\) at zero.
\end{proof}

The hypotheses can be arranged uniformly on any compact smooth contact
class with positive curvature and gap bounds.  Choose one \(d\) inside its
common positive-offset collar, then a common \(I\) on which
\(S_b(u)<d/4\).  A fixed nonzero \(a\in I\) has
\(S_b(a)\ge c a^2\), hence a common positive anchor bound.  The amplitude
and normalization bounds give a positive lower bound for \(f\) on \(I^2\).

\begin{corollary}[Finite-flight law reconstruction]
\label{cor:v26-finite-flight-inverse}
Fix \(M\ge2\) on such a compact class.  Let \(f_{j,b,d}\) be the successful
signed endpoint density for an even \(j\)-flight bridge, in the exact
contact transverse chart, at the fixed offset \(d\).  On the selected
interior square, for all sufficiently large even \(j\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-finite-density-error}
 \|\mathcal S_d(f_{j,b,d})-S_b\|_{C^M(I)}\le C_M\tau^j.
\end{equation}
More generally an estimated density \(\widehat f_{j,b,d}\) with interior
\(C^M\) error at most \(\epsilon\), together with a gap estimate of error
at most \(\epsilon_g\), recovers both labelled graph jets through order
\(M\) with error at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-jet-error}
                   C_M(\tau^j+\epsilon+\epsilon_g),
\end{equation}
provided the errors are sufficiently small.  This is a stability statement
in a density norm, not an assertion that total variation alone controls
arbitrarily high derivatives.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the transverse chart the finite endpoint density is a positive
normalizing constant times \(b_j(u,v)(d-E_j(u,v))_+\).  The differentiated
relative theorem gives
\(b_j=B_b\otimes B_b+O_{C^M}(\tau^j)\) and
\(E_j=S_b\oplus S_b+O_{C^M}(\tau^j)\).
On the chosen square the cutoff argument is bounded away from zero.
Over the whole common endpoint box the inequality
\(|x_+-y_+|\le|x-y|\), the uniform amplitude bounds, and its finite volume
give an \(O(\tau^j)\) error for the normalizing integral.  That integral
has a positive uniform lower bound at fixed \(d\).  Division therefore
proves \(\|f_{j,b,d}-f_{b,d}\|_{C^M(I^2)}\le C_M\tau^j\).
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:v26-density-stability}.

Before applying the contact-jet recursion, subtract the zeroth and first
Taylor terms of the estimated action.  This bounded projection fixes the
true anchored action, preserves the error bound, and does not change any
coefficient of order at least two.  Thus a small finite-flight or density
error is not interpreted as an unknown change of contact origin or tangent.
For completeness, if \(a_b=S_b''(0)\), the leading inversion is
\[
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}),\quad
 c_b=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_b/a_{1-b}},\quad
 \kappa_b=(c_b-1)/g.
\]
At each \(3\le n\le M\), use
\(q_n=M_n^{-1}(s_n-R_n)\), where \(R_n\) is already determined by
lower-order graph jets and \(M_n\) is the determinant-one block of
\eqref{eq:v22-general-jet-block}.  Each of these finitely many operations is
smooth on a neighborhood of the compact positive geometric set.  Induction
in \(n\) gives \eqref{eq:v26-jet-error}.  No infinite-order condition bound
is inferred from the determinant identity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The intrinsic data map}

\begin{theorem}[Periodic determination from one endpoint law of each type]
\label{thm:v26-single-offset-global}
For every measured channel \(e\), retain its gap \(g_e\), its signed
transverse coordinate conventions, and the two conditional endpoint laws
\(\Pi^{\mathrm{end},\infty}_{e,00,d_e}\) and
\(\Pi^{\mathrm{end},\infty}_{e,11,d_e}\) at one known sufficiently small
positive offset \(d_e\).  No longitudinal coordinate, residual time, waiting
count, or amplitude is part of these conditional-law data.  Then:

\begin{enumerate}
\item For smooth strictly convex contacts the data determine every finite
labelled contact jet, without reflection symmetry or supplied curvatures.
\item For connected real-analytic incident boundaries they determine the
complete boundary images in their separate channel frames.
\item Suppose the data are realized by a labelled periodic dispersing
billiard with connected real-analytic strictly convex obstacle boundaries
and the marked lifted-channel graph is connected.  Assume that this graph
contains a rank-two anchoring pair of signature-rigid cycles based at one
channel frame and a rooted signature-rigid spanning tree reaching every
obstacle orbit, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-uncalibrated-periodic-rigidity}.  Then the data determine
the complete marked periodic table and the Euclidean realization of its
rank-two lattice, including its Gram form and scale, up to one simultaneous
element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).
\end{enumerate}
On a compact class all \(d_e\) can be the same fixed \(d_0>0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-density-inverse} recovers each action on a neighborhood
of zero.  The weighted half-line construction, finite-truncation envelope
identity and homogeneous isolation in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity} then recover the labelled graph jets.
The density cancellation is not a replacement for that inverse: it supplies
its unsymmetrized action input.  Analytic continuation gives the complete
incident boundary images.  The intrinsic gluing theorem matches their
framed signatures.  For part (3), fix the common starting channel frame of
the anchoring pair.  Its two signature-rigid cycles determine translation
holonomies \(V=(v_1\ v_2)\).  If \(M\) has the two independent marked deck
displacements as columns, Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-lattice-gram-recovery} gives
\(L=VM^{-1}\) in this frame and the Gram matrix
\(M^{-T}V^TVM^{-1}\).  This step determines the lattice, not the placements
of obstacle orbits outside the anchoring cycles.  The rooted
signature-rigid spanning tree supplies those placements: at each successive
incidence the already placed analytic boundary and the uniquely matched
framed signature determine the adjacent channel placement.  Induction from
the root reaches every obstacle orbit.  The assumed realization supplies
existence and admissibility, while this propagation gives uniqueness, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-uncalibrated-periodic-rigidity}.  Changing the initial
frame applies one simultaneous element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\), rather than
independent motions to the recovered obstacles.  These steps use neither
ambient position samples nor an externally supplied lattice metric.
Uniform collar bounds give the last assertion.
\end{proof}

This theorem concerns an intrinsic, law-valued observation map with specified
signed transverse origins and axes, as does the preceding support-family
inverse.  It does not treat an unknown centering transformation as a common
statistical kernel.  Section~\ref{sec:v25-common-observables} obtains such
origins and axes from a charged physical pilot, and
Section~\ref{sec:v25-uniform-physical-global} implements the law inverse by
bounded tests after that pilot.  Exact longitudinal positions are available
in that richer acquisition protocol and permit the independent direct
sampling route of Section~\ref{sec:v26-position-benchmark}.  Neither route
is claimed to be statistically equivalent to the other.
