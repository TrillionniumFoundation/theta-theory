\section{A fixed protocol for an unknown discrete mechanical system}
\label{sec:v78-mechanics}

We give an application in which the acquisition can be fixed on an
entire infinite-dimensional class, rather than on an unknown object's
local certificate.  Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v78-lagrangian}
 L_{\mu,P}(x,y)=\frac{\mu}{2}(y-x)^2+P(x),\qquad \mu>0,
\end{equation}
where both the mass $\mu$ and the smooth potential $P$ are unknown.
Assume, for fixed constants,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v78-mechanical-class}
 0<\mu_-\le\mu\le\mu_+,
 \qquad 0<\kappa\le P''\le\Lambda,
 \qquad |P(0)|\le A_0,\quad |P'(0)|\le A_1.
\end{equation}
No analyticity is required.  For $n\ge1$ define the stationary action
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v78-stationary-action}
 S_n(s,t)=\min_{x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}}
       \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}L_{\mu,P}(x_i,x_{i+1}),
       \qquad x_0=s,\quad x_n=t.
\end{equation}
For $n=1$ this is $L_{\mu,P}$ itself.  The endpoint coordinates here
are the known scalar configuration coordinates, not unknown planar
boundary embeddings.

\begin{lemma}[Global twist and finite conditioning]
\label{lem:v78-mechanical-twist}
The minimum in~\eqref{eq:v78-stationary-action} exists uniquely and is
smooth in $(s,t)$.  Its mixed derivative never vanishes.  Put
$D=2\mu_++\Lambda$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v78-mechanical-twist-bound}
 |(S_n)_{st}|\ge
 u_n:=\mu_-\left(\frac{\mu_-}{D}\right)^{n-1}.
\end{equation}
At a prefix match between $S_n$ and $S_{n+1}$, the positive Schur
complement is at most $D$, and consequently the matching derivative
has magnitude at least $u_n^2/D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The interior Hessian is tridiagonal, with diagonal
$2\mu+P''(x_i)$ and neighboring entries $-\mu$.  Its quadratic form
is the sum of the nonnegative kinetic second variation and
$\sum P''(x_i)h_i^2$, and is positive definite.  Strong convexity and
coercivity give a unique minimum.  The implicit function theorem gives
smooth dependence.  The corner-cofactor formula yields
\[
             |(S_n)_{st}|=\frac{\mu^n}{\det H_{n-1}},
\]
with empty determinant one.  Hadamard's inequality gives
$\det H_{n-1}\le D^{n-1}$, proving the bound.  Eliminating earlier
interior coordinates in $S_{n+1}$ gives a positive Schur complement
at $x_n$ which is no larger than its original diagonal entry
$2\mu+P''(x_n)\le D$.  Formula~\eqref{eq:v78-root-sign} completes
the argument.
\end{proof}

The associated scalar canonical map is deterministic: from an initial
covector $p=-L_x(x,y)$ one obtains
$y=x+(p+P'(x))/\mu$.  The stationary equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v78-backwards}
 x_{i-1}=2x_i-x_{i+1}+P'(x_i)/\mu.
\end{equation}
Thus equality of initial covectors has exactly the common-trajectory
meaning used in Theorem~\ref{thm:v78-generating}.

\subsection{The threshold experiment}
Take a source with positive density $\rho(s,p)$ on a relevant initial
phase domain, independent of an auxiliary variable $\alpha$ on a fixed
interval $(0,A)$.  Retain a trajectory of $n$ steps when
$\alpha+S_n(s,t)<T$, with an additional positive efficiency $e(s,t)$.
Record its endpoints.  On a gate where $0<T-S_n<A$, the change
$p=-(S_n)_s$ and integration over $\alpha$ give
\[
 f_{n,T}(s,t)=Z_{n,T}^{-1}a_n(s,t)(T-S_n(s,t)),\qquad
 a_n=e(s,t)\rho(s,-(S_n)_s)|(S_n)_{st}|.
\]
The source and efficiency are held fixed across the three thresholds of
one branch, but need not agree between $n$ and $n+1$.  A cutoff outside
a larger phase domain makes the preparation a probability measure;
the resulting scalar factor disappears on conditioning.  This is an
action-threshold measurement, not an assertion that $S_n$ is elapsed
physical time.  The threshold device supplies a binary retention event,
not an individual action value.

\begin{theorem}[Six laws recover mass and a smooth potential]
\label{thm:v78-mechanical-main}
Fix $R>0$, an integer $n\ge1$, and the constants
in~\eqref{eq:v78-mechanical-class}.  There are fixed endpoint gates,
three fixed thresholds and one fixed auxiliary interval $(0,A)$,
independent of $(\mu,P)$ in this class, such that the six normalized
endpoint laws for $S_n$ and $S_{n+1}$ determine $\mu$ and the entire
function $P|_{[-R,R]}$.  They do so with unknown positive joint weights
for each branch, and without a supplied root bracket or absolute action
anchor.  The result applies for every prescribed finite $n$.

On bounded finite-smooth subclasses with positive source and retention
margins, the inverse is uniformly locally Lipschitz from $C^{k+2}$ laws
to $\mu$ and $C^k([-R,R])$ potential for each finite $k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first choose gates uniformly.  Put $r_0=r_1=R+1$ and recursively
\[
 r_{j+1}=(2+\Lambda/\mu_-)r_j+r_{j-1}+A_1/\mu_-.
\]
For a prescribed final pair $|t|,|v|\le R+1$, the backward recurrence
\eqref{eq:v78-backwards} gives $|x_{n-j}|\le r_{j+1}$, because
$|P'(x)|\le A_1+\Lambda|x|$.  In particular its initial coordinate
lies in $[-r_{n+1},r_{n+1}]$.  Take
\[
 I=[-r_{n+1}-1,r_{n+1}+1],\qquad J=[-R-1,R+1],
\]
and observe $S_n,S_{n+1}$ on the fixed gates $I\times J$.
There is a matching root in the interior of $I$ for every $(t,v)\in
J^2$: backward recurrence constructs its stationary path and strict
convexity identifies it with the minima defining these two actions.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v78-generating} will therefore recover $L(t,v)$
on this entire target rectangle.

The preparation phase domain can also be fixed.  Write $S=r_{n+1}+1$
and $K=\max(S,A_1/\kappa)$.  Every minimizing interior coordinate on
these gates lies in $[-K,K]$.  Indeed, at a maximum larger than $K$,
the stationarity equation
$\mu(2x_i-x_{i-1}-x_{i+1})+P'(x_i)=0$ has a nonnegative first term
and a strictly positive second term; a minimum below $-K$ is excluded
in the same way.  Consequently the initial covector has magnitude at
most $2\mu_+K+A_1+\Lambda K$.  A fixed larger phase rectangle therefore
contains all required preparations, independently of the unknown
system.  This maximum principle also supplies a uniform compact domain
for every derivative bound used below.

We also choose thresholds uniformly.  Set
$C_-=A_0+A_1^2/(2\kappa)$.  Taylor's lower bound implies
$P(x)\ge-C_-$ for every $x$.  A linear interpolating trial path with
endpoints in $I\times J$ stays in $[-S,S]$.  Hence both actions, of
length $m\in\{n,n+1\}$, lie between the fixed bounds
\[
 w_-=-(n+1)C_-,\qquad
 w_+=2\mu_+S^2+(n+1)(A_0+A_1S+\Lambda S^2/2).
\]
Indeed the total trial kinetic term is
$\mu(t-s)^2/(2m)\le2\mu_+S^2$.  Set
$T_j=w_++j$ for $j=1,2,3$ and $A=w_+-w_-+4$.
Then $1\le T_j-S_m\le A-1$ on both gates.  Thus all six laws have
the required common-weight representation with fixed thresholds and
fixed auxiliary interval.  The acquisition does not depend on the
unknown table, potential, mass, action range or root.

Recover $S_n,S_{n+1}$ by the three-clock inverse and recover $L$ by
root-selection-free subtraction.  Choose any fixed $t_*,v_0,h$ with
$h>0$ and $v_0-h,v_0,v_0+h\in J$.  The exact formulas
\begin{align}
 \mu&=\frac{L(t_*,v_0+h)-2L(t_*,v_0)+L(t_*,v_0-h)}{h^2},
       \label{eq:v78-mass-recovery}\\
 P(t)&=L(t,v_0)-\frac{\mu}{2}(v_0-t)^2,
             \qquad |t|\le R,\label{eq:v78-potential-recovery}
\end{align}
recover the unknowns, including the additive constant of the potential.
These formulas use full functions, not their jets or analyticity.

For stability, all gate minimizers stay in the fixed bounded region
just proved, and all matched paths also satisfy the backward bounds.  Positive interior Hessians and finite derivative
bounds control the required action derivatives there.  Lemma
\ref{lem:v78-mechanical-twist} supplies a uniform root derivative,
and the unit buffer in $I$ supplies a compact interior collar.  Positive
source bounds and the quantitative anchor argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v78-anchor-bound} give a uniform usable anchor
among finitely many corners.  Apply the finite-order generating-action
estimate and then the bounded evaluation and subtraction operations in
\eqref{eq:v78-mass-recovery}--\eqref{eq:v78-potential-recovery}.
\end{proof}

For example, $P(x)=c x^2/2+\varepsilon b(x)$, with $\kappa<c<\Lambda$
and a nonzero compactly supported smooth bump $b$ flat at the edge of
its support, belongs to this class for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.
Thus the theorem recovers nonanalytic smooth potentials and distinguishes
them from the quadratic potential on intervals meeting the bump.  The
explicit factor $u_n^2/D$ also records the conditioning cost of a long
prefix.  Neither the number of clocks nor the exact inversion removes
that cost.
