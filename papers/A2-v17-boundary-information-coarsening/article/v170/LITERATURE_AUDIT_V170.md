# A2 v170 — primary-source comparison and documentary limits

Date: 26 September 2026. The complete v169 literature record is inherited;
this addendum records the primary sources actually consulted for the new
ramified-boundary argument. It is not an exhaustive priority audit.

## Blow-ups and normalization

The Stacks Project, Section 31.33, Tag 01OF, *Blowing up*:
https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01OF

The flat-base-change lemma (31.33.3) and universal property (31.33.5) were
checked. They justify identifying the **full** coefficient pullback with
the Hilbert graph and the principalization comparison. Those facts are
classical; no new representability theorem is claimed.

The Stacks Project, Tag 035E, *Normalization*:
https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/035E

Finiteness in the finite-type/Nagata setting, localization, and the normal
universal property were checked. The paper separately proves the explicit
normalization charts and conductor orders. Normalization of a parameter
space is not normalization of an embedded universal-curve fibre.

## Semigroup normalization and monomial blow-ups

P. D. Gonzalez Perez and B. Teissier, *Toric geometry and the Semple–Nash
modification*, RACSAM, DOI 10.1007/s13398-012-0096-0.
Author-hosted primary full text:
https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~bernard.teissier/documents/Semple-Nash.pdf

Proposition 5 and Section 2.6 were consulted, including the semigroup
normalization formula and the monomial-blow-up construction. The proposition
was visually checked in the PDF. The paper uses these classical mechanisms:
its displayed normalized Rees algebra is the saturation of the explicitly
computed marked Newton polygon. It does not claim that the saturation
mechanism, toric chart gluing, or fan description is new in general.

The new manuscript gives separate proofs for the two-coordinate fibre
ideal, absence of parameter embedded points, exact nilpotence order, and
the conductor extending through all crossings. These scheme assertions are
not inferred merely from having written a fan or from different terminology.
No claim is made to have independently audited every theorem in the cited
50-page paper or to have proved historical nonanticipation from its title.

## Ramified toric covers

V. Alexeev and R. Pardini, *On the existence of ramified abelian covers*,
Rend. Sem. Mat. Univ. Politec. Torino 71 (2013), 307–315.
Official journal primary text:
https://seminariomatematico.polito.it/rendiconti/71-34/307.pdf

Definition 1 and Lemma 1 in Section 3 were consulted and the displayed
lattice rule was visually checked in the PDF. Finite-index lattice maps
and their primitive-ray ramification are classical. The new paper applies
that rule to the explicit diagonal cover and computes both e_i and the
residue power map of degree g_i; it does not claim to reprove a general
existence theorem for abelian covers.

The conductor and the parameter-fibre lengths are different from the
ramification indices. They require the binomial-curve calculation, the
depth lemma, and the divisorial-coordinate-ideal argument given in full.
Successive cover compatibility is stated for the marked boundary problem,
not for every unmarked contact/Kronecker stratum.

## Scope of the new independent application

The corollary on fibres of arbitrary normal toric surface modifications of
A2 does not use Paper I. Its proof is supplied directly by the lattice
lemma. The manuscript does not characterize this corollary as resolving a
recognized named open problem or as certifying a top-four editorial threshold.
The earlier Hilbert-versus-Quot and punctual genus-correction applications
remain in full, with their earlier primary-source comparison preserved.

## Permanent documentary limits

No complete Ballico 1993 text adequate for a full theorem/proof comparison
was obtained in this revision. Both papers retain the limitation and make
no historical priority claim requiring that missing comparison. No external
independent full proof audit of Paper I was obtained. The new proof checking,
finite algebra tests, build records, and preservation hashes do not substitute
for either external audit or historical evidence. No journal acceptance or
editorial endorsement is claimed.
