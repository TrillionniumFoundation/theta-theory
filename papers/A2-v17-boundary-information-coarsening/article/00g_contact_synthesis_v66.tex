A long billiard bridge is a rare event, but its conditional endpoint law
need not be a quadratic record of the orbit it shadows.  We prove that
on a fixed contact collar it retains nonlinear half-line actions, and
that these actions identify the complete smooth reflecting contact germs.
The forward statement is relative to an exponentially small endpoint
twist.  The inverse concerns the actual boundary, including both its
curvature and its flat part, not an arbitrary formal generating function.
No analytic hypothesis is needed for this local identification.  These
two statements form the principal mechanism of the paper.

For clarity we first give the conclusion for a supplied periodic polygon.
A \emph{marked polygon} consists of the lifted collision points $q_i$,
their signed tangent and outward-normal frames $t_i,n_i$, the phase labels
and closing translation, with the specular reflection condition imposed.
Write $L_i=|q_{i+1}-q_i|$, $e_i=(q_{i+1}-q_i)/L_i$ and
$\nu_i=e_i\cdot n_i>0$.  Unknown contacts are represented by
\[
 R_i(x)=q_i+\nu_i^{-1}\{t_i x-n_iF_i(x)\},\qquad
 F_i(0)=F_i'(0)=0,\quad F_i''(0)>0.
\]
The measured coordinate $x=\nu_i t_i\cdot(R_i-q_i)$ is a tangent
projection.  Neither the normal graph height nor the curvature is a mark.
Two fixed positive offsets are observed at every physical phase, on a
common square with positive residuals.  Their limiting densities have
the form
\[
 f_{i,d}(u,v)=Z_{i,d}^{-1}B_i^-(u)B_i^+(v)
              \{d-A_i(u)-C_i(v)\},\qquad d=d_1,d_2,
\]
with anchored actions and amplitudes.  The amplitudes are shared across
the two offsets, while the normalizers need not agree.  The physical
momenta are restored in $A_i=S_i^- -p_i x$ and
$C_i=S_i^+ +p_i x$, where $p_i=e_i\cdot t_i/\nu_i$.

Let $P$ be a returning oriented period, $N=nP$, and $\mathcal L$
the length of that period.  Denote by $W_{N,b}(u,v)$ the stationary
length along the selected itinerary and put
\[
 E_{N,b}=W_{N,b}-n\mathcal L+p_bu-p_bv,\qquad
 D_{N,b}=|-W_{N,b,uv}(0,0)|.
\]
For a returning signed frame the mixed derivative has the sign used in
the following formula.  The exact reference twist is
\[
 D_{N,b}=\frac{\sinh\chi}
 { (\mathcal M_b)_{12}\sinh(n\chi)},
\]
where $\mathcal M_b$ is the positive Jacobi transfer over one returning
period in the scaled signed coordinates.  It is not additional
observation data.  The normalized twist, rather than an absolute
approximation to $W_{N,b}$, is the quantity which survives conditioning.

\begin{theorem}[Identification from periodic boundary laws]
\label{thm:v66-main}
Fix a clear nongrazing marked polygon with finitely many distinct physical
contact points in a planar dispersing billiard.  On a contact collar
independent of $N$, for every separately fixed smooth order $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v67-principal-relative}
 \|E_{N,b}-S_b^-\oplus S_b^+\|_{C^k}
 +\left\|\frac{-W_{N,b,uv}}{D_{N,b}}
                       -B_b^-\otimes B_b^+\right\|_{C^k}
                                      \le C_k\tau^N,\qquad \tau<1.
\end{equation}
The physical endpoint measure has density
\[
 \frac{D_{N,b}}{2\pi\mathcal A}
       \frac{-W_{N,b,uv}}{D_{N,b}}
       \{d-E_{N,b}+p_bu-p_bv\}
\]
on a common positive-residual square, where $\mathcal A$ is the free
cell area.  Its conditional densities converge in the same fixed-order
topology to $f_{b,d}$ above.  The phase-resolved limiting laws at two separated positive offsets
identify the complete positive-curvature $C^\infty$ contact germs,
without supplied curvatures or amplitudes.  In particular they identify
every finite smooth contact jet.  These exact uniqueness conclusions
require no closeness between the candidates.
For actual connected closed analytic obstacle boundaries, the same laws
identify the entire labelled obstacle images when the lattice is fixed
and every obstacle is visited by the polygon.

On bounded positive $C^{m+3}$ contact classes, for a finite order $m$
depending on the marked contraction bounds, small $C^m$ real-law error
controls the $C^0$ error of the complete contact profiles on a common
real collar.  No analytic prior is used in this estimate.
At each analytic contact tuple the complete future-action map is also a
local biholomorphism in a bounded-holomorphic norm on a sufficiently small
complex disc.  A bounded local analytic prior gives conditional H\"older
stability from real-law error on a smaller disc.  At each separately fixed
smooth order, finite-flight density error gives the finite-jet error
$C_M(\tau^N+\varepsilon)$ on compact positive classes.  If the
curvature algorithm is stopped with certified error $E_m$, the bound
is $C_M(\tau^N+\varepsilon)+L_ME_m$, with the subsequent signed
recursion evaluated as in Proposition~\ref{prop:v66-finite-flight}.

Under the uniform finite-order geometric assumptions of
Section~\ref{sec:v69-sampled-contact}, the same marked experiment also
admits finite-sample recovery.  For fixed confidence, $B$ independent
preparations per phase--offset group, and endpoint recording error at most
$\delta$, a realizable estimator satisfies
\[
 \|\widehat F-F\|_{C^0}\le
 C\{(\log B/B)^\beta+\delta^\gamma\}
\]
with the stated high probability, for positive class-dependent exponents
$\beta,\gamma$ and a logarithmically chosen returning flight number.
The exact confidence dependence, priors, estimator, and exponents are
given in Theorem~\ref{thm:v69-charged-recovery}.  Failures count toward
$B$; no density-derivative observations are assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:v64-periodic-relative} supplies the relative limit;
Theorem~\ref{thm:v64-physical-law} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:v65-signed-jacobi} put it in the physical measured
coordinates.  Proposition~\ref{prop:v65-two-offset} separates the
unequal endpoint actions.  The global quadratic inverse of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v66-curvature}, followed by the actual signed recursion,
proves Theorem~\ref{thm:v66-global-rigidity}.  The nonlinear real
remainder estimate in Theorem~\ref{thm:v68-smooth-rigidity} then
identifies smooth germs, and Theorem~\ref{thm:v68-smooth-stability}
gives the finite-smoothness profile estimate.  The complete analytic
inverse and conditional real-law estimates are
Theorems~\ref{thm:v65-analytic-inverse} and
\ref{thm:v65-real-stability}.  Proposition~\ref{prop:v66-finite-flight}
proves the fixed-order finite-flight assertion.  The finite-preparation
conclusion is Theorem~\ref{thm:v69-charged-recovery}, using the
realizable selection and interior density estimate in the same section.
All these proofs are included below.
\end{proof}

The curvature step explains why exact identification is not confined to
one analytic inverse neighborhood.  If $k_i=L_i^{-1}$,
$c_i=F_i''(0)$ and $s_i=(S_i^-)''(0)$, the boundary Schur complement is
\[
 s_i=k_i+c_i-\frac{k_i^2}{k_i+c_{i+1}+s_{i+1}}.
\]
Rearranged as an equation for $c$, it is a strict contraction on the
positive orthant after taking positive parts.  Its constant is bounded
using $k_i$ and the observed $s_i$, not unknown curvature values.
The unique positive fixed point determines the signed orbit factors.
At degree $n$ the actual boundary-visit response is then
$(I+T_n)(I-T_n)^{-1}$, with
$(T_n z)_i=\sigma_i^n z_{i+1}$ and $|\sigma_i|<1$.
This identifies each successive contact jet.  A finite low-degree
inverse together with a norm-convergent high-order tail, rather than
coefficient uniqueness alone, gives the analytic norm theorem.

For smooth germs a further use of the complete action is essential.
Along the segment between two candidate graphs, the actual envelope has
the form
\[
 \mathcal S(G)-\mathcal S(F)=\overline\alpha(G-F)+K(G-F),
 \qquad \overline\alpha\ge\tfrac12.
\]
Every term of $K$ evaluates a graph difference at a later contact with
$|x_{b,j}(u)|\le a^j|u|$, $a<1$.  In the real norm
$\|h\|_{m,R}=\max_b\sup_{0<|u|\le R}|h_b(u)|/|u|^m$,
\[
 \|\overline\alpha^{-1}K\|\le\frac{6a^m}{1-a^m}<1
\]
for sufficiently large finite $m$.  Equal finite jets put the difference
in this space, and equality of the actual actions forces it to vanish.
Thus the relative law distinguishes even smooth flat contact changes.
A finite-jet alignment gives the stated real-profile stability.  The
weighted comparison is proved along actual graph segments, not assumed
for a formal map or presented as a data-only reconstruction algorithm.

\subsection{The mechanism and the observation models}

There are three logically different passages.  The cofactor identity and
two-ended trace-class comparison control the relative physical law before
conditioning.  The stationary boundary envelope, including its vanishing
terminal term, converts its nonlinear actions into contact geometry.
Registration and analytic continuation then assemble geometry in the
appropriate observation model.  The latter passage does not replace the
first two.  The obstruction in the forward problem is a scale mismatch:
$D_{N,b}$ is exponentially small, so division of an absolute action
remainder by $D_{N,b}$ gives no relative twist estimate.  In the proof,
the corner cofactor is divided by its reference value \emph{exactly};
the logarithm is then an edge sum minus
$\log\det(I+G_N\Delta H_N)$.  The perturbation has a trace norm
bounded by the two endpoint displacements, independently of the number
of flights.  Reflected Green estimates separate its two ends, and the
trace-series telescoping estimate passes to the half-line determinants.
This establishes \eqref{eq:v67-principal-relative} before probability
normalization.  It is not an application of weak convergence to a
sequence of already conditioned quadratic models.

The inverse has a different obstruction.  An abstract generating
function need not come from the stated reflecting curves.  The
stationary envelope is differentiated along actual graph variations:
its initial visit has weight one, every interior visit has weight two
at the orbit, and its terminal term is estimated before the half-line
limit is taken.  Interpolation of smooth representatives then shows
that the action jet depends on precisely the corresponding finite
contact jet.  The cyclic blocks are the resulting geometric derivative,
not a postulated formal normal form.  The full envelope also controls
the remainder beyond those jets.  Global curvature inversion, the real
contracted-visit estimate and, for complete analytic obstacles,
continuation complete this relative-law/contact mechanism; none replaces
the relative physical estimate.

There is a concrete information comparison within the physical class.
The area-preserving analytic family of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-jet-fiber} has the same gap, free area, contact
curvatures and leading endpoint and count data, but different fourth
contact jets.  The fixed-positive-offset laws distinguish its members
by the signed action inverse.  Thus the complete conditional law carries
strictly more geometric information than that leading record.  This
example compares those two observations; it makes no assertion about
the information of a marked length spectrum.

The smooth class exhibits a sharper distinction.  Proposition~\ref{prop:v68-flat-family}
constructs actual area-preserving obstacle perturbations with identical
\emph{complete} contact and action Taylor jets, but distinct boundary
laws on every sufficiently small positive contact square.  The first
nonzero real action response is flat, of size
$\varepsilon e^{-1/u^2}(1+o(1))$.  Consequently no formal all-order
contact record can replace the function-valued physical law in the smooth
rigidity theorem.  This compares specified observations in an actual
billiard class, not the full marked-length datum of a different inverse
problem.

The amplitudes also retain nonlinear dynamical coordinates.  With
$\zeta_b^\pm(x)=\int_0^x B_b^\pm(t)\,\dd t$, the actual stable
returns satisfy
\[
 T_b^\pm=(\zeta_b^\pm)^{-1}\circ
                (e^{-\chi}\zeta_b^\pm)
\]
on a smaller collar, by Corollary~\ref{cor:v64-transmission}.
The two-offset laws recover $B_b^\pm$, and the reconstructed quadratic
Jacobi data recover $\chi$.  Hence the same relative record determines
these nonlinear returns, not just their linear multiplier.  This is
a consequence of the proved transmission identity within the marked
observation model, not a claim of a reconstruction from lengths alone.

The alternating channel uses less supplied first-order geometry: its gap
and signed transverse conventions replace a full marked polygon, and one
same-type law at a fixed positive offset suffices at each end.  Its
normal two-site response is the specialization of the signed cyclic
response, but its law-to-action extraction is a different theorem.  Its
unregistered multichannel reconstruction can recover an unknown lattice.
In the marked-polygon conclusion above, in contrast, lattice and placement
are supplied, and full visitation turns exact contact identification into
whole-boundary identity.  No implication that removes these supplied
marks is used.  The charged finite-preparation results require their
calibration and sensor bounds in addition to the geometric inverse;
the earlier alternating-channel rates are not being transferred to a
different observation model.  Section~\ref{sec:v69-sampled-contact}
proves a general fixed-period estimate directly.  An interior kernel
estimate supplies the required $C^m$ density control from finitely many
recorded endpoints.  A measurable minimum-distance comparison stays
inside the realizable image, and a binomial lower-tail estimate accounts
for the exponentially small success probability.  This passage is a
consequence of the relative law and smooth contact inverse, not an
independent claim of a new general density-estimation method.  It also
gives a finite charged test between the actual equal-jet flat tables at
any fixed nonzero profile separation.

The nearby spectral questions concern different observations.
B\'alint, De Simoi, Kaloshin and Leguil~\cite{BDKL}, Theorem D and
Corollary E, extract general-period Lyapunov information from marked
length asymptotics; their Remark 2.3 explains the contact-separation
issue for those asymptotics.  Here phase-resolved functions and a supplied
polygon provide individual action responses.  We do not infer them from
a marked length spectrum.  The enriched spectrum of
Finamore--Leguil~\cite{FinamoreLeguil} and the analytic symmetric
open-billiard setting of De Simoi--Kaloshin--Leguil~\cite{DSKL} likewise
have their own data and hypotheses.  The exact global uniqueness proved
here is for the stated marked boundary-law observation.

The periodic relative and local coordinate statements are recorded next.
Their exact local uniqueness clauses are strengthened by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v66-global-rigidity}; their analytic norm estimates retain
their stated neighborhoods, priors and radius loss.  The subsequent
alternating formulations preserve the weaker-data results and their
complete proofs.
