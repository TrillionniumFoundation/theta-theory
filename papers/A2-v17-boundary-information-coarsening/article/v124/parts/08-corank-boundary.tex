\section{The entire rank-two residual presentation and the contact boundary}
\label{sec:ranktwo-boundary}

We now remove two artifacts of the simple-contact calculation:
the choice of four roots and the restriction to a chart on which those
roots remain distinct.  The primary decomposition itself necessarily
changes when roots collide, but the residual Fitting presentation and
the nilpotent-depth support do not.

For $0\le r\le3$, let $\Sigma_R^{(r)}\subset X_R$ be the
locally closed stratum on which the projection $\mathcal K\to V$ has
rank exactly $r$.  If $K\in\Sigma_R^{(r)}$ has image $H\subset V$,
choose a complement $V=H\oplus W$, where $c=\dim W=4-r$.  On a frame
chart around $K$, an invertible $r$-minor and Schur reduction put the
projection block in the form
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}I_H&0\\0&T\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad T\in\operatorname{Mat}_{c\times c}.
\]
Decompose the quotient multiplication tensor according to
\[
 \Sym^2V=\Sym^2H\oplus(H\otimes W)\oplus\Sym^2W
\]
and write the corresponding blocks as $A_H,B_H,C_H$.

\begin{theorem}[All-corank residual block presentation]
\label{thm:all-corank-presentation}
For every $R\in G_4^\circ$, every $0\le r\le3$, and every
$K\in\Sigma_R^{(r)}$, one has on the preceding frame chart, before
taking radicals,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-corank-presentation}
 \II_{\widehat D_R}
 =
 (\det T)\,
 I_6\!\left[
 A_H,\,
 B_H(1_H\otimes T),\,
 C_H\Sym^2T
 \right].
\end{equation}
The formula is independent of any injectivity, mixed-rank,
tensor-rank, or contact-transversality assumption.  Hence every
projection-corank stratum is governed by the same exact Fitting
mechanism; the separate local theorems below classify successive
pieces of this presentation rather than introducing unrelated models.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:full-fitting} gives on every frame chart
\[
 \II_{\widehat D_R}
 =(\det M)I_6(\gamma_R\Sym^2M).
\]
The Schur operations placing $M$ in the displayed block form are
invertible and therefore change neither the maximal-minor ideal nor
the local scheme.  On
$\Sym^2H\oplus(H\otimes W)\oplus\Sym^2W$ the induced map
$\Sym^2M$ is respectively
\[
 I_{\Sym^2H},\qquad 1_H\otimes T,\qquad\Sym^2T.
\]
Since $\det M$ is a unit times $\det T$, substitution proves
\eqref{eq:all-corank-presentation}.
\end{proof}

We now specialize this exact all-corank formula to $r=2$.  Write
$\Sigma_R=\Sigma_R^{(2)}$.  Then $c=2$, and after choosing bases
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \delta=\det T=ad-bc.
\]
Decompose the quotient multiplication tensor according to
\[
 \Sym^2V=\Sym^2H\oplus(H\otimes W)\oplus\Sym^2W
\]
and write
\[
 A_H:\Sym^2H\to S_R,\qquad
 B_H:H\otimes W\to S_R,\qquad
 C_H:\Sym^2W\to S_R.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact presentation on every rank-two chart]
\label{thm:ranktwo-full-presentation}
In the preceding coordinates one has, before taking radicals,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-ranktwo-presentation}
 \II_{\widehat D_R}
 =
 (\delta)\,
 I_6\!\left[
 A_H,\,
 B_H(1_H\otimes T),\,
 C_H\Sym^2T
 \right].
\end{equation}
The formula is valid at every projection-rank-two point; no
injectivity, mixed-rank, tensor-rank, or simple-contact hypothesis is
required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:full-fitting} gives
\[
 \II_{\widehat D_R}
 =(\det M)I_6(\gamma_R\Sym^2M).
\]
For $M=I_H\oplus T$, the induced map on the three summands of
$\Sym^2(H\oplus W)$ is respectively
\[
 I_{\Sym^2H},\qquad 1_H\otimes T,\qquad\Sym^2T.
\]
Substitution gives \eqref{eq:all-ranktwo-presentation}.  All row and
column operations used to reach the block form are invertible, so the
maximal-minor ideal is unchanged.
\end{proof}

Thus the exceptional rank-two locus is not omitted from the theorem:
what requires further hypotheses is only a closed-form primary
decomposition of the second factor in
\eqref{eq:all-ranktwo-presentation}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:mixed-regular}
A pair $(R,H)$ is \emph{mixed-regular} if
$A_H$ is injective and, after quotienting $S_R$ by its image, the
induced map
\[
 \overline B_H:H\otimes(V/H)\longrightarrow
 S_R/\im A_H
\]
is surjective with one-dimensional kernel generated by a rank-two
tensor.  No hypothesis is imposed on the multiplicities of the four
ramification contacts.
\end{definition}

These are precisely the first three conditions in
Definition~\ref{def:good-pair}; the discriminant condition has been
removed.  On this locus the same Gaussian elimination as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-matrix} is valid and yields
\[
 \II_{\widehat D_R}=\delta J,\qquad
 J=I_3[L(T),\,C\,T^{(2)}],
\]
with $L(T)$ and $T^{(2)}$ as in \eqref{eq:residual-matrix}.

\begin{lemma}[Root-free residual normal form]
\label{lem:rootfree}
Over the local auxiliary coefficient ring of a mixed-regular pair,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rootfree}
 J=\delta\mathfrak m+(g_0,\ldots,g_4),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak m=(a,b,c,d),
\end{equation}
where each $g_i$ is homogeneous of transverse degree four.
Modulo $(\delta)$, under the Segre parametrization $T=uv^t$, their
classes span
\[
 h_H(u)\cdot\Sym^4(\C^2_v).
\]
Here $h_H$ is the binary quartic obtained by restricting the
ramification equation to the pencil of hyperplanes containing $H$.
The identity is valid with repeated roots, and also when the
discriminant of $h_H$ vanishes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The four $3$-minors of the linear block $L(T)$ generate
$\delta\mathfrak m$, exactly as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cone-ideal}.  Modulo $\delta$, write $T=uv^t$.
The two pairs of linear columns are scalar multiples, by $v_1,v_2$,
of the two mixed columns determined by $u$, while the three quadratic
columns are scalar multiples, by
$v_1^2,v_1v_2,v_2^2$, of $C(u^2)$.  A minor containing one column
from each linear pair and one quadratic column is therefore
$h_H(u)$ times a degree-four monomial in $v$; the five choices span
$\Sym^4(\C^2_v)$.

Every minor containing at least two quadratic columns vanishes on the
rank-one cone, hence is divisible by the prime equation $\delta$.
Its transverse degree is at least five, so the quotient by $\delta$
lies in $\mathfrak m$.  The remaining minors are the already listed
linear and mixed ones.  This proves \eqref{eq:rootfree}.  No
factorization of $h_H$ was used.  When its roots are distinct,
factorizing $h_H$ recovers Lemma~\ref{lem:cone-ideal}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Depth two across contact collisions]
\label{thm:collision-depth}
At every mixed-regular projection-rank-two point,
the radical of $\II_{\widehat D_R}$ is $(\delta)$ and the nilradical
$\widehat\NN$ of the local failure ring satisfies
\[
 \widehat\NN^3=0,\qquad
 \widehat\NN^2\ne0,\qquad
 \Ann(\widehat\NN^2)=\mathfrak m/(\delta J).
\]
Consequently $\ZZ_2(\widehat D_R)$ is the rank-two stratum on the
mixed-regular locus, even when the contact quartic has multiple roots.
Moreover
\[
 \widehat\NN^2|_{\Sigma_R}
 \simeq
 \II_{\widehat\Delta_R}^{\,2}|_{\Sigma_R}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:rootfree},
$J\subset\mathfrak m^3$ and
$\delta\mathfrak m\subset J$.  Since $\delta\in\mathfrak m^2$,
we have $\delta^2\in J$, hence
$\delta^3\in\delta J$.  On the other hand
$\delta\notin J$ by transverse degree, so
$\delta^2\notin\delta J$.  Since
$\delta J\subset(\delta)$ and a power of $\delta$ belongs to
$\delta J$, the radical is exactly $(\delta)$.  Thus the nilradical
is generated by the class of $\delta$ and has exact index three.

For the annihilator, cancellation in the regular local coefficient
ring gives
\[
 (\delta J:\delta^2)=(J:\delta).
\]
The inclusion $\mathfrak m\subset(J:\delta)$ follows from
$\delta\mathfrak m\subset J$.  Conversely, use the transverse grading:
$J$ has no component of transverse degree two, whereas $\delta$ has
degree two.  If the transverse degree-zero part of $r$ were nonzero,
the degree-two part of $r\delta$ could not lie in $J$.
Hence $r\in\mathfrak m$, proving
$(J:\delta)=\mathfrak m$.

Finally $\delta$ is a local equation of the Schubert divisor
$\widehat\Delta_R$.  Therefore the generator $\delta^2$ of the
square of the nilradical transforms as the square of the conormal
line.  This gives the displayed line bundle after restriction to the
rank-two stratum.
\end{proof}

We next globalize the four contacts without labelling them.  Put
\[
 G=\Gr(2,V)
\]
and let $\mathcal Q$ be its universal rank-two quotient bundle.
The quartic equation $F_R$ of $Y_R\subset\Pj(V^*)$ restricts to every
line of hyperplanes containing $H$.  Equivalently it defines a global
binary quartic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-contact-quartic}
 h_R\in H^0(G,\Sym^4\mathcal Q).
\end{equation}
Define the contact incidence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contact-cover}
 \RR_R=
 \{(H,[\alpha])\in G\times Y_R:
                  H\subset\ker\alpha\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[The global contact cover]
\label{prop:contact-cover}
The projection $\RR_R\to Y_R$ is a $\Pj^2$-bundle; in particular
$\RR_R$ is smooth and irreducible.  Over the open set
$G^\mathrm{fin}_R\subset G$ on which the binary quartic
\eqref{eq:universal-contact-quartic} is not identically zero, the
map
\[
 \RR_R\longrightarrow G
\]
is finite flat of degree four.  Its branch divisor is the zero scheme
of the binary-quartic discriminant
\[
 \Disc(h_R)\in
 H^0\!\left(G,(\det\mathcal Q)^{12}\right).
\]
Whenever this section is not identically zero, the branch divisor has
Pluecker class $12H$.  On its complement the degree-four cover is
finite etale and its monodromy action on the four sheets is transitive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $[\alpha]\in Y_R$, the fibre of
$\RR_R\to Y_R$ consists of the two-planes in the three-dimensional
hyperplane $\ker\alpha$, hence is
$\Gr(2,\ker\alpha)\simeq\Pj^2$.  These fibres form the relative
Grassmannian of the rank-three hyperplane bundle on $Y_R$, proving
smoothness and irreducibility.

For fixed $H$, the other projection has fibre
$Y_R\cap\Pj(H^\perp)$, the zero scheme of the restricted quartic.
On the locus where the restriction is nonzero this is a relative
effective Cartier divisor of degree four in the $\Pj^1$-bundle
$\Pj(\mathcal Q^*)$, hence finite flat of degree four.

The discriminant of a binary quartic is homogeneous of degree six in
its coefficients and is a relative invariant of determinant weight
twelve.  Applied to the section of $\Sym^4\mathcal Q$ in
\eqref{eq:universal-contact-quartic}, it is therefore a section of
$(\det\mathcal Q)^{12}$.  Since
$\det\mathcal Q$ is the Pluecker line bundle up to the constant
factor $\det V$, its divisor class is $12H$.  Nonvanishing of the
discriminant is exactly the simple-root condition, hence gives the
finite etale locus.  The total cover is irreducible; therefore the
monodromy action of the etale restriction on a geometric fibre is
transitive.
\end{proof}

For $R$ in the nonempty open $G_4^\dagger$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:good-open}, the discriminant in
Proposition~\ref{prop:contact-cover} is not identically zero.  Its
complement is exactly the global locus on which the four local ideals
$P_i$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:coranktwo-proof} may be obtained by an
etale splitting.  Thus the $P_i$ are not four globally chosen
components: they are the four sheets of the canonical cover
\eqref{eq:contact-cover}, and their permutation is its monodromy.

The collision divisor has the five nonzero fibre types corresponding
to the partitions
\[
 1+1+1+1,\quad 2+1+1,\quad2+2,\quad3+1,\quad4
\]
of the degree of a binary quartic, together with the separate
containment locus $h_H\equiv0$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:collision-depth}
does not change across these collision types.  What changes is the
primary splitting of the contact part, so we do not force the
simple-root intersection formula onto the boundary.

Finally, the fifth-power component of
Theorem~\ref{thm:coranktwo-proof} is also global.  The rank-two
Schubert stratum has transverse ideal
$\mathfrak m=(a,b,c,d)$ on every normal slice, so the local component
$\mathfrak m^5$ is the restriction of the fifth ordinary power of
the ideal of the rank-two stratum.  This identifies its gluing without
choosing any of the four contact sheets.

\begin{corollary}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-depth}]
\label{cor:main-depth-proof}
The three assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-depth} hold.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Depth one is Proposition~\ref{prop:depth-one}.  The exact presentation
at every rank-two point is
Theorem~\ref{thm:ranktwo-full-presentation}.  On the mixed-regular
locus, Theorem~\ref{thm:collision-depth} identifies depth two and its
line bundle, while Proposition~\ref{prop:contact-cover} globalizes the
four simple-contact branches and their monodromy.
\end{proof}
