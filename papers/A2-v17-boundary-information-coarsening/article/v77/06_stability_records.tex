\section{Stability and charged finite records}
\label{sec:v77-stability}

Fix an identifying atlas, with finitely many nested endpoint rectangles
and fixed anchors.  A bounded admissible class has uniform bounds for
the required finitely many boundary and density derivatives, and positive
lower bounds for density values at all denominators, the three-clock
anchor determinant, residual and clock gaps, the matching derivative
and root bracket, and the distance and overlap singular values.  The
geometric forward assumptions in the preceding sections produce such
classes on small neighborhoods with bounded smooth sources and retention
functions.  The conditions are not bounds on an already computed
reconstruction error.

For two marked realizations $\mathcal T,\widetilde{\mathcal T}$ of this
atlas, let
\[
 d_k(\mathcal T,\widetilde{\mathcal T})
 =\inf_{(O,b)\in\mathrm E(2)}\left[
  \max_I\|R_I-(O\widetilde R_I+b)\|_{C^k(I)}
       +\|L-O\widetilde L\|\right].
\]
For a nonperiodic target omit the lattice term.  Coordinate overlaps
have their fixed meanings.  A smaller fixed target interval is used
where required by the root construction.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-order geometric stability]
\label{thm:v77-stability}
On the bounded class above, for every fixed $k\ge0$ there are
$C,\eta_0>0$ such that two realizable data tuples satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-geometric-stability}
 d_k(\mathcal T,\widetilde{\mathcal T})
 \le C\max_{\gamma,j}
       \|f_{\gamma,j}-\widetilde f_{\gamma,j}\|_{C^{k+2}(Q_\gamma)}
\end{equation}
whenever the maximum on the right is at most $\eta_0$.
The density restrictions retain their original gate normalizations.
For whole-table atlases this controls the complete boundary and lattice
in one common Euclidean gauge.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:v77-clock} first bounds the differences of the absolute
actions in $C^{k+2}$, with the same order.  Proposition
\ref{prop:v77-cancellation} bounds every final-flight distance kernel
in $C^k$.  Its matching root remains in the fixed bracket because of
the uniform endpoint signs.  Proposition~\ref{prop:v77-distance}
then compares each pair of canonical boundary patches in $C^k$.
The positive matrix square root fixes a common continuous choice of
root gauge in a neighborhood of the reference data.

At an overlap, two independent difference vectors from a fixed ordered
triple determine its Euclidean matching map.  Matrix inversion and,
for an extension off the realizable image, polar orthogonalization,
are smooth near this nondegenerate triple.  The corresponding
translation is determined by its first point.  Each matching map is
therefore locally Lipschitz in the reconstructed anchor positions.
Composing along the fixed finite spanning tree bounds every registered
patch in one root gauge.  Finally \eqref{eq:v77-lattice} compares the
lattice columns by differences of registered point values.  Taking the
infimum over the initial root isometry proves
\eqref{eq:v77-geometric-stability}.  This proof is a finite composition
of explicit inverses; no compact-inverse theorem is substituted for the
stated Lipschitz bound.
\end{proof}

There is no order threshold from a half-line contraction in this
finite-action estimate.  The corresponding price is different:
\eqref{eq:v77-matching-derivative} may be small for a long prefix.
The constants in \eqref{eq:v77-geometric-stability} retain its inverse,
the clock-anchor denominator, the distance ranks and the finite tree
condition numbers.  They are not uniform in the number of flights,
in a shrinking gate, or at grazing or conic degeneracies.  Exact finite
laws can carry weak endpoint interactions discarded by a long-return
limit; keeping them is not an assertion of inexpensive reconstruction.

\subsection{A finite-preparation consequence}

We include the sampling implication to specify what a law-level inverse
means for finite records.  It is a consequence of the geometric theorem,
not a separate rigidity mechanism.  Let $M$ be the total number of
branch--clock settings.  Let $m=k+2$ and fix an integer $s\ge1$.
Suppose the conditional densities have uniformly bounded
$C^{m+s}$ norms on the larger gates, and every attempt at every setting
has retained-success probability at least $p_*>0$.  These are a bounded
source-and-geometry class and a positivity condition on detection.
Without a retention floor no uniform deterministic preparation budget
is asserted.  The cross-clock common-weight hypothesis concerns the
latent laws before any bounded coordinate recording error.

\begin{corollary}[Charged reconstruction on a fixed finite atlas]
\label{cor:v77-sampling}
Use $B$ independent preparations at each setting, charging failures and
unretained successes as well.  Retained scalar coordinates may have
arbitrary recording errors of size at most $\delta$.  For sufficiently
large $Bp_*$ and sufficiently small $\delta$, there is a measurable
actual-table estimator with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-sampling}
 d_k(\widehat{\mathcal T},\mathcal T)
 \le C\left\{
 \left(\frac{L_B}{Bp_*}\right)^{s/[2(m+s)+2]}
       +\delta^{s/(m+s+3)}\right\},\qquad
 L_B=\log(C_0MB/\zeta),
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-2\zeta$.  The total charged number of
preparations is $MB$.  Constants are for the fixed atlas class; no
minimax assertion is made.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Take $n=\lfloor Bp_*/2\rfloor$ and use the first $n$ retained
successes at each setting.  A Chernoff bound gives probability at most
$M\exp(-Bp_*/8)$ that some setting has fewer than $n$ successes.
An infinite continuation of each independent preparation sequence
couples these first $n$ successes to independent draws from its
conditional density, whether or not the cap succeeds.  For
$Bp_*\ge C\log(M/\zeta)$ the cap failure is at most $\zeta$.

The interior derivative estimate of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v69-density-estimation}, whose proof uses only
independent endpoint samples and not the periodic geometry, gives
simultaneously in the $M$ settings the error
\[
 C\left(h^s+\sqrt{\frac{L_n}{nh^{2m+2}}}
    +\frac{L_n}{nh^{m+2}}+n^{-2}+\frac{\delta}{h^{m+3}}\right),
 \qquad L_n=\log(C_1Mn/\zeta).
\]
Choose $h$ as a sufficiently large constant times the maximum of
$(L_n/n)^{1/[2(m+s)+2]}$ and
$\delta^{1/(m+s+3)}$, below the fixed collar margin.
The square-root term is bounded by a constant times $h^s$, and the
linear term is no larger for these choices and large $n$; the recording
term is also at most a constant times $h^s$.  This proves the density
radius displayed in \eqref{eq:v77-sampling} with probability
$1-\zeta$ for the coupled success samples.

To ensure an actual-table output, use a countable subset dense in the
$C^m$ observation image of the bounded admissible class, selecting one
realization for each chosen point.  Such a set exists because the finite
product of these $C^m$ spaces is separable.  Choose the first tuple
within $n^{-1}$ of the infimum of its distance to the empirical tuple.
This is measurable, and its distance to the true tuple is at most twice
the empirical error plus $n^{-1}$.  For small enough radius apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:v77-stability}.  Put a fixed default table on a cap
failure.  Combining the two events proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

Unlike an approximation by limiting periodic laws, this fixed-branch
bound has no finite-flight asymptotic bias term: the recovered physical
actions are exact finite actions.  The small twist and acceptance
probability remain in the class constants and $p_*$.  The estimator
above is a measurable existence construction, not a claim of effective
enumeration of all smooth tables.

\subsection{The retained information and the determined object}

The geometry-free atlas theorem retains joint endpoint information,
exact branch and lift labels, scalar overlap identities and admissible
clock gates.  Three clocks do not separate an unlabelled mixture of
branches: on a common positive-residual domain,
\[
 \sum_\gamma a_\gamma(z)(T-W_\gamma(z))
 =\Bigl(\sum_\gamma a_\gamma(z)\Bigr)(T-\overline W(z)),
 \qquad
 \overline W=\frac{\sum_\gamma a_\gamma W_\gamma}
                   {\sum_\gamma a_\gamma}.
\]
This identity explains why branch selection is specified rather than
silently inferred.  It is a statement about the observation algebra,
not a realizability assertion for arbitrary averaged actions.

For a target consisting only of local patches the theorem determines
those patches and their relative placement.  Unvisited smooth boundary
pieces are not supplied by local action data.  The whole-table theorem
obtains them through the finite coverage proved above, not by applying
analytic continuation to smooth germs.  Likewise, an unknown source
normalization can confound a success-count area scalar in a local
experiment.  The Liouville-calibrated area theorem in the periodic part
remains unchanged on its own observation model.  When the entire table
has been geometrically reconstructed, its area is instead a consequence
of that reconstructed geometry.
