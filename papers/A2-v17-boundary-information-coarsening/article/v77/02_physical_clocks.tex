\section{Finite physical laws and absolute action recovery}
\label{sec:v77-clocks}

\begin{lemma}[Physical law with an unknown joint multiplier]
\label{lem:v77-physical}
On a regular finite branch and an admissible clock gate, the retained
conditional density is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-model}
 f_j(s,t)=Z_j^{-1}a(s,t)\{T_j-W(s,t)\},\qquad
 a(s,t)=e(s,t)\rho(s,-W_s(s,t))|W_{st}(s,t)|>0,
\end{equation}
where $Z_j$ is the integral of the numerator over the common gate.
The same multiplier $a$ occurs at all three clocks.  It is not required
to factor into a function of $s$ and a function of $t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $R'(s)=v(s)\mathbf t(s)$, $v(s)>0$, and let $\varphi$ be the
angle with the inward-to-free-space normal.  The tangential covector is
$p=v(s)\sin\varphi$.  In coordinates before the first collision, the
unit-speed phase-volume element is
\[
 v(s)\cos\varphi\,ds\,d\varphi\,d\alpha=ds\,dp\,d\alpha.
\]
Indeed the spatial Jacobian of a boundary point and distance along its
incoming ray is $v\cos\varphi$; specular reflection preserves the
tangential covector.  The first variation of stationary length gives
$p=-W_s$.  The change from $p$ to the last endpoint $t$ has Jacobian
$|W_{st}|$, which is nonzero by Lemma~\ref{lem:v77-regular-action}.

If the first collision occurs at time $\alpha$, the last occurs at
$\alpha+W$.  The no-previous-collision and no-additional-collision
conditions read
\[
 0<\alpha<\tau^-,\qquad 0<T-\alpha-W<\tau^+.
\]
Under \eqref{eq:v77-window}, the allowed interval is exactly
$0<\alpha<T-W$.  Integrating the source density, which is independent
of $\alpha$ in this box, gives $\rho(s,-W_s)|W_{st}|(T-W)$.
Multiplication by the retention probability and normalization give
\eqref{eq:v77-model}.  The parametrization is one-to-one on the selected
regular branch, so no unrecorded multiplicity has been inserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Clock design from coarse brackets]
\label{lem:v77-clock-design}
Suppose on a gate and a prescribed class one knows
$w_-\le W\le w_+$ and $\tau^\pm\ge\lambda>0$, with
$w_+-w_-<\lambda/4$.  Then
\[
 T_j=w_++j\lambda/4,\qquad j=1,2,3,
\]
is admissible, with margins on both sides.  Such brackets, with rational
endpoints and strict inequalities, exist after shrinking a gate about
any regular finite trajectory.  A finite family of these choices
persists under small finite-smooth perturbations of the table.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The lower residual is at least $\lambda/4$; the upper is at most
$3\lambda/4+w_+-w_-<\lambda$.  For a single regular branch, continuity
of its stationary length permits a gate on which its oscillation is
arbitrarily small.  The adjacent flights have a positive lower bound
on a smaller closed gate.  Choose rational enclosing bounds with room
in the inequalities.  The implicit construction in Lemma
\ref{lem:v77-regular-action} and continuity of the clear flights preserve
all strict inequalities on an open neighborhood.  No exact value of
$W$ is subsequently supplied to the inverse.
\end{proof}

A fixed design therefore needs coarse admissibility information, not
calibrated branch lengths.  The finite-atlas theorem is an existence and
persistence theorem for such designs.  It does not identify the finite
selection of suitable words and clocks with a universal finite adaptive
search over all unknown tables.  Scalar charts, words, rational subgates
and rational clock triples form a countable acquisition family containing
every finite design constructed in that theorem.

\subsection{Elimination of normalization and joint weights}

The following statement is independent of the dynamics.  Let $Q$ be a
connected rectangle and let the positive densities
\[
 f_j(z)=Z_j^{-1}a(z)(T_j-W(z)),\qquad j=1,2,3,
\]
be given on a neighborhood of $Q$.  Assume $T_1<T_2<T_3$, $a>0$,
$T_j-W>0$, and $W$ nonconstant.  Fix $z_0\in Q$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-normalized-slices}
 g_j(z)=\frac{f_j(z)}{f_j(z_0)},\qquad
 (c_1,c_2,c_3)=(T_2-T_3,T_3-T_1,T_1-T_2).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Three-clock absolute action inverse]
\label{thm:v77-clock}
There exists $z_*\in Q$ with $D_*:=\sum_jc_jg_j(z_*)\ne0$.
For any such point the data determine
\begin{align}
 W_0&=\frac{\sum_jc_jT_jg_j(z_*)}{\sum_jc_jg_j(z_*)},
       &d_j&=T_j-W_0,\label{eq:v77-absolute-anchor}\\
 Q_{12}(z)&=\frac{g_1(z)}{g_2(z)},
       &W(z)&=W_0+
       \frac{d_1d_2(1-Q_{12}(z))}{d_2-d_1Q_{12}(z)}.
       \label{eq:v77-absolute-action}
\end{align}
In these formulas $W_0=W(z_0)$.  Thus the three laws determine the
absolute action, without a supplied anchor value or any supplied
multiplier.  They also determine $a(z)/a(z_0)$.

For each integer $m\ge0$, the displayed inverse is locally Lipschitz
in $C^m(Q)$ on bounded sets with a fixed usable anchor $z_*$ and
positive floors for its scalar denominator and all density denominators.
It extends as a map on an open neighborhood of each such set, without
requiring perturbed functions to satisfy the model.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $w_0=W(z_0)$ and $b(z)=a(z)/a(z_0)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-affine}
 (T_j-w_0)g_j(z)=b(z)(T_j-W(z)).
\end{equation}
Since $\sum c_j=\sum c_jT_j=0$, taking the weighted sum gives
$\sum c_jT_jg_j=w_0\sum c_jg_j$.  Moreover
\[
 \sum_jc_jg_j(z)
 =b(z)(w_0-W(z))\sum_j\frac{c_j}{T_j-w_0}.
\]
The last sum equals
\[
 -\frac{(T_2-T_1)(T_3-T_1)(T_3-T_2)}
 {(T_1-w_0)(T_2-w_0)(T_3-w_0)},
\]
which is nonzero.  Nonconstancy supplies $z_*$, and
\eqref{eq:v77-absolute-anchor} follows.  With $H=W-w_0$,
\[
 Q_{12}=\frac{d_2(d_1-H)}{d_1(d_2-H)},\qquad
 d_2-d_1Q_{12}=\frac{d_2(d_2-d_1)}{d_2-H}>0.
\]
Solving gives \eqref{eq:v77-absolute-action}.  Finally
$b=g_1d_1/(T_1-W)$.

Point evaluation, multiplication and restriction are bounded in $C^m$;
reciprocal maps are locally Lipschitz on bounded sets bounded away from
zero.  Apply these facts to the successive formulas.  The last displayed
identity supplies the remaining denominator floor from clock separation
and bounded positive residuals.  All floors persist for a sufficiently
small nonmodel perturbation.  No differentiation of the observations is
used at this stage.
\end{proof}

For physical branches, $W_{st}\ne0$ ensures nonconstancy.  Usable
anchors can be specified by strict tests on the functions $g_j$; they
are scalar labels, not known spatial contact points.  In a finite atlas
one fixes such anchors and retains a positive margin after shrinking the
class.  An arbitrary additive constant in an action is not harmless:
the three absolute clock values are what determine it.

For comparison, when $W(z_0)$ is known, the second formula needs only
two clocks.  Conversely, in the unrestricted weighted-function model
two unanchored clocks leave a fractional-linear freedom.  This is an
algebraic explanation of the third clock, not an assertion that every
fractional-linear change of an action is a realizable billiard action.

The shared-weight hypothesis has a precise role.  A multiplier that
varies freely with the clock could absorb a change of $W$.  A source
varying with $\alpha$ would instead give
$\int_0^{T-W}\rho(s,p,\alpha)\,d\alpha$.  Neither alteration is
made in Theorem~\ref{thm:v77-main}.  The allowed multiplier is,
however, an arbitrary positive function of both retained endpoints.
