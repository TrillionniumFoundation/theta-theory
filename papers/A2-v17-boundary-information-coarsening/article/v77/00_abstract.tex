Three conditional endpoint laws at known absolute times determine the
absolute length action of a finite reflected branch with an unknown
positive joint source and retention weight.  Only scalar boundary labels
are observed; their Euclidean embeddings and metric calibration are not
supplied.  Comparing a branch with a one-flight extension eliminates the
common prefix and recovers a Euclidean distance kernel.  Explicit finite
rank certificates and overlapping boundary charts then determine smooth
boundary arcs and their relative placement.  We prove that finite-horizon
periodic dispersing tables with locally nonconic boundaries admit finite
identifying atlases for the entire table and lattice, with every measured
prefix longer than any prescribed bound.  Fixed atlases persist on open
classes containing nonanalytic smooth tables.  The inverse has finite-order
stability and a charged finite-preparation consequence.  The periodic
relative-law and smooth contact inverse are retained as a complementary
result for a different observation model.
