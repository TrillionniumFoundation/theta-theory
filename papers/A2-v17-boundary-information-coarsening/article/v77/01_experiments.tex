\section{Statistical actions without Euclidean contact marks}
\label{sec:v77-intro}

The observation of a reflecting boundary need not include the position of
any point of that boundary.  We consider experiments which retain only two
scalar endpoint labels and the identity of a finite reflection branch.
The labels are smooth coordinates on abstract boundary intervals; their
Euclidean embeddings, speeds, tangent vectors and origins in the plane
are unknown.  Three conditional laws, recorded at different absolute
clock times, determine the absolute action of the branch even when its
endpoint weight is an unknown function of both labels.  Two branches
with a common prefix then determine a one-flight distance kernel.  Finite
distance certificates register the unknown boundary intervals in one
Euclidean plane.  This yields a second, spatially extended rigidity
mechanism, distinct from the periodic-law inverse developed later.

The passage from statistics to absolute action is important here.
Neither a travel-time function nor the positions and directions of its
rays are observations.  The input consists of normalized densities;
each normalization, and a common positive multiplier depending jointly
on the two endpoints, is unknown.  Once this multiplier is removed, the
composition law for actual reflected trajectories supplies the missing
Euclidean geometry.  The inverse acts on smooth functions, not on their
Taylor series.

\subsection{Observation convention}

A periodic dispersing table is the complement in $\R^2$ of the family
\[
 \{K_a+L\lambda:1\le a\le q,\ \lambda\in\Z^2\},
 \qquad L\in\mathrm{GL}(2,\R),
\]
where the $K_a$ are compact strictly convex bodies with $C^\infty$
boundaries of positive curvature and the translated bodies are pairwise
disjoint.  There are finitely many obstacle orbits.  A boundary atlas
consists of abstract intervals with specified overlap maps and obstacle
and lift labels.  An unknown regular embedding $R_I:I\to\R^2$ realizes
each interval.  Labels on translates are transported by the abstract
deck action.  No Euclidean realization of $L$, arclength normalization,
contact point, tangent frame, or periodic polygon belongs to this atlas.
The unit of speed is fixed, so physical time has the unit of length.
Reflection words and gate membership remain exact parts of the experiment.

A branch $\gamma$ is a regular family of trajectories with the specified
successive boundary intervals, starting on $I$ and ending on $J$.  All
its flights are clear and its reflections nongrazing.  In small endpoint
rectangles it has a stationary length $W_\gamma(s,t)$.  On success at
absolute time $T$, the first collision is on $I$, the stipulated branch
is followed, the last collision is on $J$, and no additional collision
has occurred by $T$.  Only $(s,t)$ is retained; failure gives one symbol.
No position in $\R^2$, incidence angle, interior collision coordinate,
residual time, or trajectory length is recorded.  The branch identity
is not inferred by observing those discarded quantities.

In a flow box before the first collision write the phase coordinates as
$(s,p,\alpha)$, where $p$ is tangential covector and $\alpha$ is time
to the first collision.  Preparations are independent, with a smooth
positive density $\rho_\gamma(s,p)$ in the relevant part of this box,
independent of $\alpha$.  It is unknown and need not be Liouville-uniform.
A successful pair is retained with unknown probability
$e_\gamma(s,t)>0$.  The source and retention rule are unchanged across
the three clocks for this branch; they may differ between branches.
An extension and its prefix need not have the same source.  Smooth
positive local densities with this property extend to normalized
preparation measures by a cutoff and normalization outside a slightly
larger box.  Multiplication by the resulting scalar does not affect a
conditional law.

Let $\tau_\gamma^-,\tau_\gamma^+$ be the adjacent free-flight lengths
before the first and after the last collision.  A clock triple
$T_1<T_2<T_3$ is \emph{admissible} on a closed gate $Q$ when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v77-window}
 0<T_j-W_\gamma(z)
      <\min(\tau_\gamma^-(z),\tau_\gamma^+(z)),\qquad z\in Q,
 \quad j=1,2,3,
\end{equation}
with strict margins.  All densities in a triple are normalized on the
same gate.  Restrictions to an inner gate are not renormalized.  This
is a regularity condition on the experimental class, not supplied values
of $W_\gamma$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v77-clock-design} specifies clocks from
coarse brackets, and Theorem~\ref{thm:v77-finite-atlas} constructs fixed
admissible designs on nonempty open classes.

\begin{definition}[Finite identifying atlas]\label{def:v77-atlas}
A finite identifying atlas consists of finitely many pairs of branches
$(\gamma,\gamma e)$, where the latter extends the former by one flight,
together with their scalar endpoint gates, clock triples and lift labels.
The initial scalar coordinate is identical in a prefix and its extension.
For each pair, a common initial-coordinate interval and smaller terminal
rectangles specify the unique matching branch of
\eqref{eq:v77-matching}; its derivative and endpoint bracket have strict
margins as in Proposition~\ref{prop:v77-cancellation}.

The resulting final-flight distance patches have a connected registration
graph.  A node of this graph is a recovered pair of boundary patches.
An edge identifies overlapping intervals on the same lifted boundary,
using their specified scalar overlap map and three distinct parameters.
Each distance patch has the explicit rank-two and rank-five certificate
of Proposition~\ref{prop:v77-distance}.  The target is the union of the
patches.  In the periodic whole-table case the target contains every
boundary in a set of quotient representatives, and a common interval on
one representative and its two basis translates; the registration graph
connects these copies as well.
\end{definition}

All parts of this definition concern gates, labels, regularity, or finite
rank tests on reconstructed functions.  In particular it contains no
assumption that a space of possible geometric realizations is a singleton.
The same definition applies to a finite collection of nonperiodic
obstacles, with the statements about translates omitted.

\begin{theorem}[Smooth rigidity from weighted scalar records]
\label{thm:v77-main}
For a finite identifying atlas, the three conditional endpoint laws of
each branch determine the embeddings of all target patches, up to one
common element of $\mathrm E(2)$.  The clock times, scalar labels,
overlap maps, and branch and lift labels are supplied; the Euclidean
contact geometry, absolute branch lengths, source densities and joint
retention probabilities are not.  The source and retention functions
may be different for two candidate tables.

In the whole-table case the same data determine the complete marked
periodic table and the lattice Gram matrix.  There is no analyticity
assumption and no closeness assumption between two realizations of the
same admissible atlas.  On bounded classes with the indicated positive
margins, for each finite $k\ge0$ the inverse is locally Lipschitz from
$C^{k+2}$ density data to $C^k$ boundary embeddings modulo one Euclidean
motion, including the lattice vectors in the same gauge.
\end{theorem}

The proof is constructive at the level of exact functions.  Theorem
\ref{thm:v77-clock} recovers the absolute actions.  Proposition
\ref{prop:v77-cancellation} eliminates each common prefix, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:v77-distance} determines an actual boundary patch
from its distance kernel.  The finite registration argument is given in
Section~\ref{sec:v77-registration}; the stability assertion is proved
in Theorem~\ref{thm:v77-stability}.

The atlas condition can be obtained from geometry, rather than merely
assumed.  Call a boundary \emph{locally nonconic} if no nonempty open
subarc is contained in the zero set of a nonzero polynomial of degree at
most two.  Theorem~\ref{thm:v77-finite-atlas} proves that every
finite-horizon periodic table with locally nonconic boundaries admits a
finite identifying atlas.  Every measured prefix can be required to have
at least any prescribed number $J$ of flights.  For each such table and
$J$, one atlas and its absolute clocks persist on a $C^r$ open
neighborhood, for sufficiently large finite $r$.  The open neighborhood
contains nonanalytic $C^\infty$ tables.  Thus the smooth whole-boundary
conclusion is not an analytic-continuation corollary or an unverified
coverage assumption.

\subsection{Relationship to periodic laws and other inverse data}

The periodic theorem retained in this article answers a different
question.  Around a supplied periodic polygon, relatively normalized
long-return laws identify smooth contact germs, including flat
perturbations.  The present theorem instead uses exact finite-branch
laws and shared prefixes to recover Euclidean positions not supplied
by the observer.  It permits joint, nonseparable source and detection
weights, but adds a third clock and a spatially extended family of
branches.  These are explicit changes of the experiment; neither an
ordering of the two statistical experiments nor a uniform improvement
of their condition numbers is asserted.

Smooth global obstacle determination from rich exterior travelling-time
and scattering-length data is established by Noakes--Stoyanov
\cite{NoakesStoyanov2020}.  Finamore--Leguil
\cite{FinamoreLeguil} obtain rigidity of finite-horizon Sinai tables
from an enriched marked length spectrum.  Here no exterior lens map,
marked periodic-length family or individual travel-time function is an
input.  The new passage is from normalized, jointly weighted scalar
records to absolute generating actions and then to Euclidean distance
kernels.  Generating-function composition and Euclidean distance
geometry are classical; the latter has, in particular, the generic
complete-bipartite rigidity theory of Connelly--Gortler--Theran
\cite{V77ConnellyGortlerTheran}.  We use an explicit finite certificate
and give its proof, rather than import an unspecified genericity claim.
Standard flow-box and wave-front constructions for dispersing billiards
are described in \cite{V77ChernovMarkarian}; the
finite-branch arguments needed here are supplied below.

For a fixed domination inequality in the periodic inverse, the
nonnegative visit-matrix condition remains
$\rho(K_m)<1$.  In cyclic coordinates it becomes
$6\overline a^m/(1-\overline a^m)<1$, and return to a fixed unweighted
norm costs $\kappa(w)=\max w_b/\min w_b$.  Relabelling phases permutes
the domination matrix, and changing positive phase units conjugates it
by a diagonal matrix; these operations preserve its spectral radius,
not its unweighted norm-equivalence constant.  This refinement and its
conditioning cost are retained separately from the new finite-action
rigidity theorem.
