\section{Single-offset statistical separation and global physical reconstruction}
\label{sec:v26-uniform-physical-global}
\label{sec:v25-uniform-physical-global}
\label{sec:v24-uniform-physical-global}

We construct the estimator on the common observation space of
Section~\ref{sec:v25-common-observables}.  The identifiable datum consists
of the onsets and the centered conditional laws.  The onsets enter the
estimation criterion, not merely the programmed times.  The single-offset
inverse permits every post-pilot test to ignore residual time and both
longitudinal coordinates.  Only the two endpoint types at one fixed offset
per channel are needed.  No finite-dimensional model for the entire compact
analytic class is imposed.

Fix one \(d_0>0\) inside the common collar.  For \(M\ge2\), write
\[
 \mathcal D_M(T)=\bigl(g_e,S_{e,0}^{(m)}(0),S_{e,1}^{(m)}(0):
                         e\in E,\ 2\le m\le M\bigr).
\]
Use the normalized finite-coordinate norm of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-global-modulus}, with a fixed positive gap scale
\(g_*\).  The notation \(Q^\infty_{T,e,b,d_0}\) denotes the centered successful
endpoint--time law of
\eqref{eq:v25-centered-density}; all tests below are independent of its
residual-time coordinate.

\begin{lemma}[Single-offset mixed onset--law separators]
\label{lem:v26-single-offset-separators}
\label{lem:v25-augmented-separators}
\label{lem:v24-finite-separators}
For every \(M\ge2\) and \(\eta>0\), there are finitely many labelled
bounded Lipschitz tests \((e_r,b_r,\varphi_r)_{r=1}^R\), with
\(|\varphi_r|\le1\), and \(c>0\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-mixed-separation}
 \|\mathcal D_M(T)-\mathcal D_M(T')\|_M\ge\eta
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\|F(T)-F(T')\|_\infty\ge8c,
\end{equation}
where the continuous moment map is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-augmented-moment-map}
 F(T)=\left((g_e(T)/g_*)_e,
      \left(Q^\infty_{T,e_r,b_r,d_0}\varphi_r(u,v)\right)_{r=1}^R\right).
\end{equation}
All tests use the same fixed offset \(d_0\).  The list and the separation
constant depend only on the compact class, \(M\), and \(\eta\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The set of pairs on the left of \eqref{eq:v25-mixed-separation} is compact.
For any such pair, either a gap differs, or all gaps agree.  In the latter
case, equality of both endpoint marginals at \(d_0\) for every channel
would, by Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-density-inverse}, imply equality of every
half-line action germ.  It would therefore imply equality of
\(\mathcal D_M\), a contradiction.  Hence one endpoint law separates the
pair.  Bounded Lipschitz functions separate finite Borel probability
measures on the fixed compact endpoint box: continuous functions do so,
and Lipschitz functions are uniformly dense in the continuous functions.
Normalize a separating function to absolute value at most one; no uniform
bound on its Lipschitz constant is required before the finite list is chosen.

Gap continuity and \eqref{eq:v25-centered-TV-continuity}, followed by
marginalization, make each strict separation persist on a neighborhood of
the pair.  A finite subcover gives finitely many tests and a positive minimum
margin \(8c\).  Include every gap coordinate whether or not it was needed
in a particular neighborhood.  Add constant tests for any missing
\((e,b)\), so all \(D=2|E|\) design groups are represented and \(R\ge D\).
If the compact pair set is empty, any positive \(c\) and these constant
tests suffice.  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

A conditional law alone is not here asserted to recover the gap.  Already
at quadratic order, fixing \(a_0=a_1=a\) permits
\[
 \kappa_0(g)=\kappa_1(g)=\frac{\sqrt{1+g^2a^2}-1}{g}.
\]
This explains the separate onset coordinate without asserting equality of
the full nonlinear laws along this quadratic example.

\subsection{A finite minimum-distance rule}

Choose fixed templates \(T_1,\ldots,T_H\in\mathfrak K\) whose images form
a \(c\)-net of \(F(\mathfrak K)\).  For each \((e,b)\), collect \(k\)
fresh successful records after the pilot.  Apply the observable transverse
projection in \eqref{eq:v25-observable-normalization}, and evaluate all tests
belonging to that group on the same sample.  Put
\[
 \widehat F=\left((\widehat g_e/g_*)_e,
   \left(k^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^k
      \varphi_r(\widehat u_{e_r,b_r,i},\widehat v_{e_r,b_r,i})
                                      \right)_{r=1}^R\right).
\]
Select
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-finite-estimator}
 \widehat T=T_{\widehat h},\qquad
 \widehat h=\min\operatorname*{argmin}_{1\le h\le H}
                       \|F(T_h)-\widehat F\|_\infty,\qquad
 \widehat{\mathcal D}_M=\mathcal D_M(\widehat T).
\end{equation}
On a flagged pilot or batch failure use \(T_1\).  This finite rule is Borel
with a fixed tie convention.  It does not hide a selection theorem or an
unbounded optimization over unknown analytic functions.  Compactness gives
the library, not an effective rate for generating it from collar constants.

If \(\|\widehat F-F(T)\|_\infty\le c\), a template within \(c\) of
\(F(T)\) has criterion at most \(2c\).  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-deterministic-estimator-bound}
 \|F(\widehat T)-F(T)\|_\infty\le3c<8c,\qquad
 \|\mathcal D_M(\widehat T)-\mathcal D_M(T)\|_M<\eta.
\end{equation}
The first norm includes the gap coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform finite-order physical estimation at one offset]
\label{thm:v26-fixed-order-physical}
\label{thm:v25-fixed-order-physical}
\label{thm:v24-fixed-M-physical-estimability}
For every \(M\ge2\), \(\eta>0\), \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and prescribed
minimum flight number \(J_0\), there is one deterministically capped policy
on \(\mathscr A,\mathscr X\) such that every preparation uses the same
chosen even \(J\ge\max(2,J_0)\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-fixed-order-risk}
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}
 \Pr_T\{\|\widehat{\mathcal D}_M-\mathcal D_M(T)\|_M\ge\eta\}<\delta.
\end{equation}
No gap, contact frame, inter-channel registration or lattice metric is
supplied.  After the pilot there are exactly \(2|E|\) design groups,
all programmed at \(J\widehat g_e+d_0\); the estimator uses only their
transverse endpoint pairs and the pilot summaries.  Every preparation is
charged, and all design and stopping decisions are measurable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose the separators, \(c\le1\), and the template library first.  Take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-k-choice}
                         k\ge8c^{-2}\log(8R/\delta).
\end{equation}
Choose the final even \(J\ge\max(2,J_0)\) so that the finite-flight bias
in Proposition~\ref{prop:v25-test-implementation} is at most \(c/4\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-full-transfer-budget}
                               CDk\tau^J<\delta/8.
\end{equation}
Only then choose \(h>0\), below both pilot and offset-collar margins, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-h-choice}
 C_{\mathscr F}(\sqrt h+h)\le c/4,\qquad h/(Jg_*)\le c.
\end{equation}
Run the calibration theorem with \(j=J\), \(\beta=\delta/8\), its finite
grid and its deterministic cap \(B_{\rm pil}\).  Thus the pilot controls
\(J|\widehat g-g|\) for the final flight number, which is not changed later.

On a good pilot history program each group at
\((e,b,J,J\widehat g_e+d_0)\), take fresh preparations, and stop at its
\(k\)-th success.  The true offsets remain in a fixed positive subcollar.
Uniform flux gives a success lower bound \(p_0>0\), depending on the fixed
design but not on the table or the good history.  Impose the per-group cap
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-post-cap}
 B=\left\lceil\frac{2k+8\log(8D/\delta)}{p_0}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Since \(Bp_0\ge2k\), the binomial lower-tail bound gives probability at
most \(\exp(-Bp_0/8)\le\delta/(8D)\) of not obtaining \(k\) successes.
The union of batch-cap failures has probability at most \(\delta/8\).

Conditional on the pilot, successive marks in an uncapped fixed-design
success sequence are iid with its successful physical law.  The capped and
uncapped constructions agree whenever the cap is not hit.  Apply
concentration to the uncapped sequence and add the cap probability;
conditioning on completion of the cap is not used to assert iid sampling.
Proposition~\ref{prop:v25-test-implementation}, applied to
\(\phi_r(u,v,r')=\varphi_r(u,v)\), gives bias at most \(c/2\).
Hoeffding's inequality on \([-1,1]\) and a union bound give
\[
 \Pr_T\left\{\max_r|\widehat\mu_r-
     \mathbb E_T(\widehat\mu_r\mid\mathrm{pilot})|>c/2
                        \mid\mathrm{good\ pilot}\right\}
 \le2R\exp(-kc^2/8)\le\delta/4.
\]
Tests sharing a sample need not be independent for this union bound.
On the complementary event the test and gap coordinates are all within
\(c\) of \(F(T)\).  Apply \eqref{eq:v25-deterministic-estimator-bound}.
The sum of pilot, empirical and cap probabilities is at most \(\delta/2\),
hence strictly less than \(\delta\).  The full cap is \(B_{\rm pil}+DB\).

The complete-transcript comparison is retained as well.  Keep the pilot
exact.  Conditional on each good history the post-pilot designs are fixed
and observable.  The stopped comparison of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer} costs at most \(CDk\tau^J\), since
each group stops no later than its \(k\)-th success.
Corollary~\ref{cor:v16-pilot-transfer} adds the pilot exceptional probability
when histories are integrated.  At each fixed table the finite and boundary
position laws use the same physical embedding, and estimated projection is
a common transcript map.  This proves a distance bound without identifying
an unknown exact centering map with an observation.  The comparison includes
all failures, counts, residual times and stopping decisions, even though the
estimator discards some of them.  The direct concentration proof already
applies to the actual finite physical experiment.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Increasing order and a prescribed preparation budget}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform global reconstruction from long same-type records]
\label{thm:v26-global-physical-reconstruction}
\label{thm:v25-global-physical-reconstruction}
\label{thm:v24-physical-global-reconstruction}
On the compact marked analytic class of
Section~\ref{sec:v25-signature-stability}, there are finite capped policies
\(\pi_s\) and table estimators \(\widehat T_s\) such that every flight
number used by \(\pi_s\) is even and at least \(s\), and, for every fixed
\(q\ge0\) and \(\epsilon>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-global-risk}
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}\Pr_T^{\pi_s}
                    \{d_q(\widehat T_s,T)>\epsilon\}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The recovered table includes its Euclidean period lattice.  Only one
simultaneous global element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\) is quotiented out.
After calibration each policy uses the same fixed offset \(d_0\) and only
the two endpoint types in each channel.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-global-modulus} to choose increasing \(M_s\ge s\)
and decreasing \(\eta_s>0\) with
\[
            \max_{0\le q\le s}\omega_q(C\eta_s+C2^{-M_s})<1/s.
\]
Each of the finitely many moduli tends to zero.  Apply the preceding theorem
with \((M_s,\eta_s,2^{-s},J_0=s)\) and keep its table-valued template
estimator.  Except on an event of probability at most \(2^{-s}\), the
finite-data modulus gives \(d_q(\widehat T_s,T)<1/s\) for every \(q\le s\).
This proves the uniform assertion.  The exact inverse used by the modulus
includes the marked deck holonomies and \(L=VM^{-1}\), so no lattice Gram
form is inserted into the statistical conclusion.

For a prescribed total budget \(N\), let \(b_s\) be the deterministic
preparation cap of \(\pi_s\), and set
\[
 C_s=\max\{s,b_1,\ldots,b_s\},\qquad
 s(N)=\max\{s:C_s\le N\}.
\]
For \(N<C_1\) use a fixed default estimate with no preparations.  Otherwise
run \(\pi_{s(N)}\) afresh and spend no remaining budget.  This is a
budget-indexed family, not a cumulative execution of all preceding stages.
It uses at most \(N\) preparations, \(s(N)\to\infty\), and every flight
number in its entire acquired transcript is at least \(s(N)\).  This proves
the same consistency statement with the claimed flight restriction for the
prescribed-budget implementation.  A cumulative historical transcript would
still contain the earlier flight numbers; no diverging minimum is asserted
for that different implementation.
\end{proof}

The library, separation constants and inverse moduli may be non-effective
functions of the compact class.  The theorem gives finite measurable
acquisition once these constants are fixed, not polynomial-time design
generation or a sharp analytic minimax rate.  Its caps expose the rare-event
cost.  The direct-position benchmark also respects a diverging even flight
number.  The extra intrinsic conclusion is therefore not the exclusion of
that benchmark, but the law inverse and its implementation using scalar
post-pilot tests at one fixed offset.
