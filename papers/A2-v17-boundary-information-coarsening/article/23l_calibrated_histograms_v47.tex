\subsection{Offset and chart errors for hard endpoint categories}
\label{subsec:v47-calibration}

We now allow the contact charts and the programmed onset to be estimated.
This is an acquisition result for the same limiting data map, not a change
of the inverse in Theorem~\ref{thm:v46-quantized-inverse}.  In particular,
the exact-offset confidence statement above retains its hypotheses.
The distinction between a bounded Lipschitz test and a hard category is
relevant: small displacement of an endpoint can change a category, and the
probability of that event must be bounded before concentration is applied.

Fix a closed interval $I_d=[d_0-a,d_0+a]$, where $0<a<d_0$, inside the
common positive-offset collar.  Let $U$ be a fixed bounded endpoint box
containing the supports of the selected successful laws for offsets in
$I_d$.  The forward construction supplies uniform constants
$W_+,Z_->0$ such that, for each law,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-forward-density}
 f_d(x)=Z_d^{-1}w(x)(d-A(x))_+\mathbf1_U(x),\qquad
 0\le w\le W_+,\quad A\ge0,\quad Z_d\ge Z_-.
\end{equation}
Here $x=(u,v)$, $A(x)=S_b(u)+S_b(v)$ and
$w(x)=B_b(u)B_b(v)$.  The bounds are forward bounds on the same quantified
class, not measurements of $A,w$ or $Z_d$.  For example, on $Q_1$,
$f_{d_0}(0,0)\le H_1$ and $B_b(0)=1$ give a positive lower bound for
$Z_{d_0}$; a smaller positive-offset interval and the uniform forward
construction give $Z_-$.  Alternatively these constants can be read
directly from the positive-offset normalization estimates in
Section~\ref{sec:v25-common-observables}.  We use
\[
 C_{\rm off}=\frac{W_+|U|}{Z_-},\qquad
 H_* = \frac{W_+(d_0+a)}{Z_-}.
\]
Both constants can be enlarged uniformly over the finite design.  In
particular the upper bound $H_*$ holds for the density extended by zero,
including neighborhoods of the outer boundary of $Q_1$; an interior
$C^1$ bound alone is not used to control that exterior neighborhood.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform offset continuity]\label{lem:v47-offset}
For $d,d'\in I_d$, the limiting endpoint laws satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-offset-TV}
 \|P_d-P_{d'}\|_{\rm TV}\le C_{\rm off}|d-d'|,
 \qquad 0\le f_d\le H_*.
\end{equation}
The same conclusions hold simultaneously for both types of every selected
channel, with the uniform constants above.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $F_d=w(d-A)_+\mathbf1_U$.  The positive-part function is
$1$-Lipschitz, so
\[
 \|F_d-F_{d'}\|_1\le W_+|U||d-d'|,
 \qquad |Z_d-Z_{d'}|\le\|F_d-F_{d'}\|_1.
\]
Using $Z_d,Z_{d'}\ge Z_-$ gives
$\|F_d/Z_d-F_{d'}/Z_{d'}\|_1\le
2\|F_d-F_{d'}\|_1/Z_-$.  Total variation is one half of this
$L^1$ norm.  Finally $A\ge0$ and $d\le d_0+a$ give the density bound.
The moving zero set requires no differentiation.
\end{proof}

Let $c_\delta:\mathbb R^2\to\{1,\ldots,K_\delta\}$ be the grid map
from Section~\ref{sec:v46-quantized-stability}, with its complement category.
Let $\Gamma_\delta$ be the union of all cell edges, including the four
outer edges of $Q_1$.  Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-tube-volume}
 V_\delta(r)=\left|\{x:\operatorname{dist}_\infty
                   (x,\Gamma_\delta)\le r\}\right|,\qquad r\ge0.
\end{equation}
This is the area of a finite union of rectangles.  It can be computed
from the grid, and tends to zero with $r$ for every fixed grid.  For a
$q$-by-$q$ square grid of side $\delta=2r_1/q$, the union bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-grid-tube}
 V_\delta(r)\le 8r(r_1+r)(q+1).
\end{equation}
Indeed there are $2(q+1)$ full horizontal or vertical segments of length
$2r_1$, each with an $\ell^\infty$ tube of area $2r(2r_1+2r)$.
Their intersections only decrease the union area.  This formula includes
corners and crossings between $Q_1$ and its complement.

\begin{lemma}[Hard-category perturbations]\label{lem:v47-cells}
Let $P$ have a density bounded by $H_*$ on $\mathbb R^2$, and let
$\Phi$ be a Borel map satisfying $|\Phi(x)-x|_\infty\le r$ on its
support.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-cell-perturbation}
 \| (c_\delta\circ\Phi)_\#P-(c_\delta)_\#P\|_1
 \le 2\min\{1,H_*V_\delta(r)\}.
\end{equation}
Here and below $\ell^1$ denotes the norm of the finite probability vector.
No differentiability or invertibility of $\Phi$ is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Couple the two categories using the same $x$.  If they differ, the segment
joining $x$ to $\Phi(x)$ meets a cell edge; hence
$\operatorname{dist}_\infty(x,\Gamma_\delta)\le r$.
The mismatch probability is therefore at most $H_*V_\delta(r)$ and at
most one.  Under any coupling, the total variation of two finite laws is
at most their mismatch probability.  Multiplication by two proves the
claim.  Points exactly on an original edge have zero $P$-probability.
The image map may put mass on an edge; the half-open convention fixes its
category and the same segment argument applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A calibration-aware confidence radius}

Choose the final even flight number $j$ before taking a pilot.  Conditional
on its history $H$, the post-pilot designs and estimated contact projections
are fixed.  Suppose an event $G$, of probability at least $1-\alpha_{\rm cal}$,
gives correct signed design labels and the bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-pilot-premises}
 \max_e|\widehat g_e-g_e|\le v_g,\qquad
 \max_{e,b}\|\widehat x_{e,b}-x_{e,b}\|_\infty\le r.
\end{equation}
The second bound is uniform on the common physical endpoint collar.
The estimated projections are observable maps of physical positions; the
true projections occur only in the analysis.  For a fixed true table, a
boundary point is determined by its transverse coordinate on this collar,
so these maps induce maps $\Phi_{T,H}$ to which
Lemma~\ref{lem:v47-cells} applies.  No unknown chart is applied by the
statistician.

Program each group at $t=j\widehat g_e+d_0+\omega_{e,b}$, with
$|\omega_{e,b}|\le v_t$.  Its true offset is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-offset-amplification}
 d_{e,b}(H)=d_0+j(\widehat g_e-g_e)+\omega_{e,b},\qquad
 |d_{e,b}(H)-d_0|\le\Delta:=jv_g+v_t.
\end{equation}
Assume $\Delta\le a$.  On $I_d$ use the uniform finite-flight estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-uniform-flight}
 \sup_{e,b,d\in I_d}\|P_{j,e,b,d}-P_{e,b,d}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C_0\tau^j.
\end{equation}
This is the positive-offset, full-box normalization consequence of the
relative-law estimate, with the same argument as
Corollary~\ref{cor:v26-finite-flight-inverse}.  It is uniform in $d$ on
$I_d$; a bound at $d_0$ alone would not suffice for the random offsets.

\begin{theorem}[Histogram reconstruction with estimated calibration]
\label{thm:v47-calibrated-confidence}
Under the preceding hypotheses set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-calibration-bias}
 b^{\rm cal}_{j,\delta}
 =2C_0\tau^j+2C_{\rm off}(jv_g+v_t)+2H_*V_\delta(r).
\end{equation}
For every good pilot history, the probability vector of a fresh successful
observed category differs from the true limiting vector at $d_0$ by at most
$b^{\rm cal}_{j,\delta}$ in $\ell^1$.
Retain $n$ fresh successful categories in each of the $\mathcal L$ groups,
and use $u_n$ from Corollary~\ref{cor:v46-confidence}.  Replace $b_j$ there
by $b^{\rm cal}_{j,\delta}$.  With probability at least
$1-\alpha_{\rm cal}-\alpha$, the resulting confidence set contains the
true table, and every candidate satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-calibrated-radius}
 D(T,\widetilde T)\le
 \Omega\left(2v_g,\,
  2(u_n+b^{\rm cal}_{j,\delta})+C_{\rm bin}\delta\right),
\end{equation}
provided the second argument is at most one.  The same bound controls the
marked lattice Gram matrix with the enlargement already specified for
$C_{\rm reg}$.  On a compact metrizable candidate class with the stated
continuity, an approximate measurable selector adds its positive tolerance
to each argument.

If every good history has preparation success probability at least
$p_*(j)>0$ in each group, impose the cap
\eqref{eq:v46-cap}.  The failure bound becomes
$\alpha_{\rm cal}+\alpha+\alpha_c$ and the post-pilot preparation charge
is at most $\mathcal L T_j$.  Add the deterministic pilot cap to obtain
the total charge.  Independence between the pilot events is not needed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $T$ and a good history $H$.  In the true endpoint coordinates compare
successively the finite law at $d_{e,b}(H)$, the limiting law at the same
offset, and the limiting law at $d_0$.  Apply the same estimated projection
and grid map to each.  Data processing, \eqref{eq:v47-uniform-flight}, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:v47-offset} bound the first two vector differences by
$2C_0\tau^j$ and $2C_{\rm off}\Delta$.  Compare the last vector with
that formed in the true chart by Lemma~\ref{lem:v47-cells}.  Its cost is
at most $2H_*V_\delta(r)$.  This comparison is on a fixed true boundary;
it does not assert total-variation continuity of ambient position laws
on different curves.  Summation proves \eqref{eq:v47-calibration-bias}.

Conditional on the pilot history, the uncapped successful sequences are
iid with their actual category laws.  The categorical concentration proof
of Corollary~\ref{cor:v46-confidence} gives a simultaneous event of
conditional probability at least $1-\alpha$.  On that event the true
limiting vectors are within $u_n+b^{\rm cal}_{j,\delta}$ of the empirical
ones.  The gap bound and two triangle inequalities now give the arguments
of \eqref{eq:v47-calibrated-radius}.  Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:v46-quantized-inverse}.  The excess-residual selection
argument in the preceding corollary applies without alteration.
Integrating the uniform conditional bound over good histories adds at most
$\alpha_{\rm cal}$.

For the charged version, couple each capped experiment with its uncapped
successful stream.  The binomial lower-tail bound used for
\eqref{eq:v46-cap} holds conditional on every good history, uniformly with
$p_*(j)$.  Add its union failure probability $\alpha_c$ before integrating
over the pilot.  No concentration assertion is made after conditioning on
cap completion, and no failure is removed from the preparation count.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite joint choice of grid, pilot, and sample size}

The preceding theorem applies to the existing position pilot without
changing its observation space.  We make the joint choices explicit to
show that refinement of a hard grid is compatible with calibration.

\begin{corollary}[Charged acquisition of finite-resolution transverse data]
\label{cor:v47-physical-histograms}
Let the quantified class be a compact marked analytic class with the
persistent position-sensor design of Part~III.  For every $\varepsilon>0$,
$\alpha,\alpha_c,\beta\in(0,1)$ with $\alpha+\alpha_c+\beta<1$, and
minimum flight number $J_0$, there is a deterministically capped policy
whose pilot and subsequent preparations all use one even $J\ge J_0$.
Its table estimator satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-physical-risk}
 \sup_{T\in\mathcal K}\Pr_T\{D(T,\widehat T)>\varepsilon\}
 \le\beta+\alpha+\alpha_c.
\end{equation}
The reconstruction uses the pilot gap estimates and post-pilot transverse
grid categories.  Longitudinal positions are used to perform the physical
pilot and the observable projection, but are not inputs to the subsequent
law-to-table reconstruction.  The supplied marks do not contain Euclidean
deck vectors or inter-channel poses.  The estimate includes the unknown
marked Euclidean lattice and its Gram matrix.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
First choose finite tolerances $e_*>0$, $0<\eta_*\le1$ by
\eqref{eq:v46-accuracy-prescription}, so that
$\Omega(e_*,\eta_*)\le\varepsilon$.  Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-grid-choice}
 q=\max\left\{1,\left\lceil8C_{\rm bin}r_1/\eta_*\right\rceil\right\},
 \qquad \delta=2r_1/q,\qquad K_\delta=q^2+1.
\end{equation}
Then $C_{\rm bin}\delta\le\eta_*/4$.  Choose a finite even
$J\ge\max\{2,J_0\}$, above the uniform finite-flight threshold, such that
$2C_0\tau^J\le\eta_*/32$.  This is the final flight number and is
not increased after the pilot.

The calibration theorem gives a constant $C_\chi\ge1$ for which a pilot
of resolution $h$ at this same $J$ has, except with probability $\beta$,
\[
 J\max_e|\widehat g_e-g_e|\le h,\qquad
 \max_{e,b}\|\widehat x_{e,b}-x_{e,b}\|_\infty\le C_\chi\sqrt h.
\]
Choose its allowed upper resolution $h_0>0$ small enough to include all
localization and nonzero-denominator requirements of that theorem.  Any
positive $h$ at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-pilot-choice}
 \min\left\{\frac{h_0}{2},\frac a2,\frac{Je_*}{4},
 \frac{\eta_*}{64C_{\rm off}},\left(\frac{r_1}{C_\chi}\right)^2,
 \left(\frac{\eta_*}{1024H_*r_1(q+1)C_\chi}\right)^2\right\}
\end{equation}
is suitable.  Use the exact-clock convention of the physical experiment,
so $v_t=0$.  Set $v_g=h/J$ and $r=C_\chi\sqrt h$.
The offset stays in $I_d$, and \eqref{eq:v47-grid-tube} with $r\le r_1$
gives
\[
 2v_g\le e_*/2,\qquad
 2C_{\rm off}h\le\eta_*/32,\qquad
 2H_*V_\delta(r)\le32H_*r r_1(q+1)\le\eta_*/32.
\]
Thus $b^{\rm cal}_{J,\delta}\le3\eta_*/32$.
Choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v47-sample-choice}
 n\ge\left\lceil\frac{64}{\eta_*^2}
       \left(\sqrt{K_\delta}+\sqrt{2\log(\mathcal L/\alpha)}\right)^2
       \right\rceil,
 \qquad 0<\epsilon_{\rm sel}\le\min\{e_*/2,\eta_*/4\}.
\end{equation}
Then $u_n\le\eta_*/8$, and
\[
 2v_g+\epsilon_{\rm sel}\le e_*,\qquad
 2(u_n+b^{\rm cal}_{J,\delta})+C_{\rm bin}\delta+
      \epsilon_{\rm sel}\le15\eta_*/16<\eta_*.
\]
The selector in Theorem~\ref{thm:v47-calibrated-confidence} therefore has
error at most $\varepsilon$ on its coverage event.  Its monotone modulus
uses only nondecreasing finite-order expressions and their infimum.

For the pilot use \eqref{eq:v25-pilot-grid}--\eqref{eq:v25-pilot-cap}
with $(j,h,\beta)=(J,h,\beta)$.  For the subsequent groups use
\eqref{eq:v46-cap}; the uniform positive-offset success bound has the form
$p_*(J)=c_*e^{-\Gamma_*J}$.  The total deterministic cap is
$B_{\rm pil}+\mathcal L T_J$.  Each quantity is finite.  Every failure,
including every failed near-onset pilot preparation, is charged.  Apply
the preceding theorem with $\alpha_{\rm cal}=\beta$.
The specified choices also permit $J\ge s$ along any sequence of target
accuracies indexed by $s$, so that the minimum flight number in each
newly acquired transcript diverges.
\end{proof}

For a regular square grid the added chart cost is bounded by a constant
times $r/\delta$ when $r\le r_1$.  The pilot gives $r\le C_\chi\sqrt h$;
thus its precision is chosen after the grid, rather than assuming that
$h\to0$ alone suffices for a simultaneously refined grid.
The three distinct errors in \eqref{eq:v47-calibration-bias} are the
finite-flight error, the amplified onset or clock error, and the cell-edge
crossing error.  At exact calibration they reduce to the original $b_j$.
No assertion of a sharp information threshold or an optimal preparation
rate is needed for these finite-resolution bounds.
