\section{The first strata of visibility loss}\label{sec:walls}
The loss of an open active cell and the loss of cross-block rank are different
phenomena. In the former, all algebraic active-set Hessians remain distinct.
The following theorem shows that a missing cell need not destroy reconstruction.

\begin{theorem}[One missing cell in dimension at least three]\label{thm:one-missing}
Let $e\ge3$, let $B$ have column rank $e$, and suppose exactly $2^e-1$ active
sets have a strict feasible point in $X^\circ$. Then $G_Q$ has minimal endpoint
dimension $e$. Its entire fibre among $e$-endpoint positive representations
consists of positive diagonal endpoint congruences and permutations. The
normalized representation is reconstructed from the labelled open-cell
quadratic matrices even when the missing active set is empty, full, or a
singleton. Its continuous fibre dimension is $e$. No coordinate is deletable,
and no two algebraic active-set Hessians coalesce.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The algebraic rank and order statements in Lemma~\ref{lem:lattice} use only
column independence, not visibility. Consequently every $S_I$ is distinct,
and strict feasibility fails on an open cell only if that cell is absent.
The observed local polynomials are precisely the $2^e-1$ remaining matrices.
The span of the ranges of their differences has dimension $e$. If both extreme
vertices are present, use $A-S_{[e]}$. If one extreme is missing, differences
between the other extreme and the $e$ vertices next to the missing one have
ranges spanning $\ran B$. Thus every representation has at least $e$
endpoints. Any competing $e$-endpoint representation has independent cross
columns and exactly one absent active set, by the same polynomial count and
the strict-point argument in Lemma~\ref{lem:competitor}.

We give explicit reconstruction in each possible intrinsic configuration.
If both extremes and all $e$ rank-one covers of the largest matrix are
observed, the formula \eqref{eq:endpoint-reconstruct} applies without using
the missing cell. This covers every missing nonextreme nonsingleton vertex.
If the largest matrix $A$ is present and has only $e-1$ rank-one covers,
the absent set is a singleton, say $\{j\}$. The known columns are
$u_i=B_i/\sqrt{C_{ii}}$, $i\ne j$, oriented on the empty-active region.
Among the observed rank-two deficits $A-S_I$, those whose ranges contain
just one known column line are precisely the pairs $I=\{i,j\}$. The
intersection of two such ranges is $\ran B_j$, by independence. Choose a
unit vector $v$ on this line, oriented positively on the empty region.
For one such pair express its deficit in the independent basis $(u_i,v)$:
\[
 A-S_{\{i,j\}}=[u_i,v]K[u_i,v]^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For some $d>0$ and $|a|<1$ the matrix $K$ has diagonal entries
$1/(1-a^2)$ and $d^2/(1-a^2)$. Hence $d=\sqrt{K_{22}/K_{11}}$ and
$u_j=dv$. The full smallest matrix is observed, so
\eqref{eq:endpoint-reconstruct} again recovers the coupling.

If the full set is missing, the local order has $e$ minimal vertices and
one largest vertex $A$. All singleton and pair deficits are observed,
since $e\ge3$. Recover the oriented $u_i$ from the singleton deficits.
For each pair the identity
\[
 A-S_{\{i,j\}}=[u_i,u_j]\,
 \overline C_{\{i,j\},\{i,j\}}^{-1}[u_i,u_j]^{\mathsf T}
\]
recovers that $2\times2$ principal submatrix by a left inverse. These
submatrices determine every entry of $\overline C$.

If the empty set is missing, there are $e$ maximal vertices and one smallest
matrix $S=S_{[e]}$. Use the dual data
\[
 D=C^{-1},\qquad H=BC^{-1},\qquad
 S_I-S=H_JD_{JJ}^{-1}H_J^{\mathsf T},\quad J=I^c.
\]
All dual singleton and pair matrices are observed. Orient
$h_j=H_j/\sqrt{D_{jj}}$ negatively on the full-active region; its strict
minimizer satisfies $C^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}x<0$ there. The preceding pair
construction recovers $\overline D$, with diagonal one. Set
$C_*=\overline D^{-1}$, $H_*=(h_j)$,
$B_*=H_*C_*$, and $A_*=S+H_*C_*H_*^{\mathsf T}$.
This is the normalization with $\diag C_*^{-1}=\mathbf1$.

All the selections just made are intrinsic. Multiple maximal or minimal
vertices identify exactly the missing extreme. Otherwise the number of
rank-one covers of $A$ distinguishes a missing singleton from the first
case. Ranges identify the required pairs without active-set labels.
The same construction must therefore give the same normalized matrix for
every minimal competitor. Its remaining scales are exactly the $e$
positive endpoint scales; permutations are discrete. Minimality rules out
coordinate deletion. Independence already proved noncoalescence.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A codimension-one cell disappearance]\label{ex:seven-cells}
For $e=3$, $k=5$, put $v=(1,1,-1)$, $p=(1,-1,0)$, $q=(1,0,1)$ and take
the rows of $B_\epsilon$ to be
\[
 p,\quad -p-\epsilon v,\quad q,\quad -q,\quad v.
\]
Let $C=I_3$ and $A=B_\epsilon B_\epsilon^{\mathsf T}+I_5$. If
$\epsilon>0$, the image $B_\epsilon^{\mathsf T}X^\circ$ is all of
$\R^3$: the coefficients of $p,q$ can be arbitrary and the coefficient
of $v$ can have either sign while all five source coefficients are positive.
At $\epsilon=0$ that image is the half-space $v\cdot y>0$.
It meets every open sign orthant except $y_1,y_2<0<y_3$.
Thus exactly the active set $\{1,2\}$ disappears, while rank stays three.
For small negative $\epsilon$ the same seven cells persist by strict
witnesses, and the eighth remains excluded. Theorem~\ref{thm:one-missing}
reconstructs the quotient on the transition and on this neighbouring
nonvisible stratum. The wall is the single parameter equation $\epsilon=0$.
\end{example}

There is a genuine exception in endpoint dimension two: losing one cell
releases a scalar modulus. We give its entire minimal fibre. Write
$s_+=\max(s,0)$ and $s_-=\max(-s,0)$.

\begin{theorem}[Two-endpoint three-cell fibres]\label{thm:three-cells}
Suppose $B$ has rank two and exactly three active sets have open cells.
The minimal endpoint dimension is two. Up to exchanging the two endpoints,
the intrinsic function has one of the following forms. The linear forms
$u^{\mathsf T}x,v^{\mathsf T}x$ are independent, and every displayed cell
is nonempty in $X^\circ$.

\emph{Chain.} For an observed largest matrix $A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain-normal}
 G(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax-(u^{\mathsf T}x)_-^2-(v^{\mathsf T}x)_-^2,
 \qquad v^{\mathsf T}x<0\Longrightarrow u^{\mathsf T}x<0.
\end{equation}
All normalized two-endpoint representations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain-fibre}
 B(a)=[u,au+v],\qquad C(a)=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&a\\a&1+a^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad a_-\le a\le a_+,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 a_-=\sup_{x\in X,\ u^{\mathsf T}x>0}
       \frac{-v^{\mathsf T}x}{u^{\mathsf T}x},\qquad
 a_+=\inf_{x\in X,\ v^{\mathsf T}x<0}
       \frac{u^{\mathsf T}x}{v^{\mathsf T}x}.
\]
Here the missing set is $\{2\}$; $A-uu^{\mathsf T}-vv^{\mathsf T}>0$.

\emph{Missing empty cell.} For the observed smallest matrix $S$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fork-normal}
 G(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Sx+(u^{\mathsf T}x)_+^2+(v^{\mathsf T}x)_+^2,
 \qquad \text{not both }u^{\mathsf T}x,v^{\mathsf T}x>0.
\end{equation}
Put
\[
 a_0=\max\left\{
 \sup_{u^{\mathsf T}x>0}\frac{v^{\mathsf T}x}{u^{\mathsf T}x},\quad
 \sup_{v^{\mathsf T}x>0}\frac{u^{\mathsf T}x}{v^{\mathsf T}x}\right\},
 \quad x\in X.
\]
All normalized representations, for $a\in(-1,1)$ with $a\ge a_0$, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fork-fibre}
 D(a)=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\a&1\end{pmatrix},\quad C=D(a)^{-1},\quad
 B=[u,v]C,\quad A=S+[u,v]C[u,v]^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}

\emph{Missing full cell.} The dual form is
$G=x^{\mathsf T}Ax-(u^{\mathsf T}x)_-^2-(v^{\mathsf T}x)_-^2$,
with the two negative regions disjoint. All normalized representations have
$B=[u,v]$, $C=D(a)$, where $|a|<1$,
\[
 a\ge\max\left\{
 \sup_{u^{\mathsf T}x<0}\frac{v^{\mathsf T}x}{u^{\mathsf T}x},\quad
 \sup_{v^{\mathsf T}x<0}\frac{u^{\mathsf T}x}{v^{\mathsf T}x}\right\},
 \qquad A-[u,v]D(a)^{-1}[u,v]^{\mathsf T}>0.
\]
In each case one then applies arbitrary positive endpoint scales and
permutations. On the interior of each nontrivial parameter interval the
continuous fibre dimension is three: two scales and the displayed modulus.
No endpoint can be deleted. All ratio bounds are finite polyhedral tests
and include the boundary of $X$ by continuity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The three local matrices have either a chain order or a fork order. Their
rank-one differences span a two-dimensional range, so any representation
has at least two endpoints. A minimal competitor has independent cross
columns and three of the four active polynomials; it has the same order
type. We can therefore work with its two-dimensional KKT fan.

In the chain case normalize the first endpoint pivot and the second Schur
pivot to one. This gives exactly $C(a)$ and $B(a)$ in
\eqref{eq:chain-fibre}. Write $U=u^{\mathsf T}x$, $V=v^{\mathsf T}x$.
The change $w_1=z_1+az_2$, $w_2=z_2$ completes the quadratic terms to
$w_1^2+w_2^2+2Uw_1+2Vw_2$. The missing cell $\{2\}$ has the strict
inequalities $aU+V<0$ and $U-aV>0$. The image of $X^\circ$ in the
$(U,V)$-plane is convex. Since it meets the empty, first-only, and full
cells, absence of the fourth forces it to avoid $U>0,V<0$.
Indeed, if $a\ge0$, such a point is either in the fourth cell or in the
empty cell with $V<0$; joining the latter to a strict full-cell point
crosses the fourth. If $a<0$, it is either in the fourth cell or in the
full cell with $U>0$; joining the latter to a strict empty-cell point has
the same consequence. This proves the nesting in \eqref{eq:chain-normal}.
The three optimizers in $w$ are respectively $(0,0)$, $(-U,0)$ and
$(-U,-V)$. Their feasibility and inactive-coordinate inequalities are
exactly $aU+V\ge0$ when $U>0$, and $-U+aV\ge0$ when $V<0$.
These are precisely $a\ge a_-$ and $a\le a_+$. Hence they are both
necessary and sufficient, including by continuity all cell boundaries.
The two successive rank-one deficits determine $u,v$, with signs fixed
by their zero and active regions. The normalization thus gives every
minimal competitor. Its Schur complement is the observed smallest matrix,
independent of $a$, so positivity introduces no further restriction.

For the missing-empty case complete the square and use the dual problem:
\[
 G(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Sx+
 \max_{w\ge0}\{2(U,V)w-w^{\mathsf T}Dw\},\qquad
 D=C^{-1},\quad [u,v]=BC^{-1}
\]
after normalizing $\diag D=\mathbf1$. Only the zero, first-only and
second-only dual cells occur. Their convex planar image cannot contain
$U,V>0$: for $a\le0$ that quadrant is entirely in the both-active cell;
for $a>0$, a point outside its central wedge is in one singleton cell,
and joining it to a strict point of the other singleton cell crosses the
both-active wedge. Thus the positive regions are disjoint. The maximizers
are $(0,0)$, $(U,0)$, $(0,V)$. Their inactive inequalities are
$V-aU\le0$ on $U>0$ and $U-aV\le0$ on $V>0$, precisely the bound $a\ge a_0$.
The two rank-one increments above $S$, with signs fixed by their active
regions, recover $u,v$. The reconstruction \eqref{eq:fork-fibre} follows,
and its Schur complement is $S>0$.

For the missing-full case apply the same three-sector argument to the
primal minimization with $\diag C=\mathbf1$ and replace $(U,V)$ by
$(-U,-V)$. Its inactive inequalities give the stated lower bound. The
unobserved full Schur complement must additionally remain positive, which
is exactly the last displayed condition. These constructions exhaust the
minimal competitors because their rank-one labels and their planar order
type are intrinsic. The ratio inequalities are homogeneous linear
inequalities after multiplication by the indicated positive denominator
(or reversal for a negative denominator), and hence are tested on a
finite polyhedral cone. The free positive scalings and the nonconstant
normalized scalar give the fibre count. Minimality excludes deletion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rank-one cross-block stratum]\label{prop:rank-one-stratum}
Let $e=2$, $B=u d^{\mathsf T}$ with $u\ne0$, $d\ne0$, and $C>0$. Define
\[
 \kappa(d,C)=\max_{z\ge0}\{2d^{\mathsf T}z-z^{\mathsf T}Cz\}.
\]
It is the maximum of the feasible active-face values
\[
 0,\quad (d_1)_+^2/C_{11},\quad(d_2)_+^2/C_{22},\quad
 d^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}d\ \text{when }C^{-1}d\ge0.
\]
Writing $\kappa_- =\kappa(d,C)$ and $\kappa_+=\kappa(-d,C)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-one-profile}
 G_Q(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax-\kappa_-(u^{\mathsf T}x)_-^2
                         -\kappa_+(u^{\mathsf T}x)_+^2.
\end{equation}
If $u$ has both signs on $X^\circ$, the minimal endpoint dimension is one
when $\kappa_-\ne\kappa_+$ and zero when they agree. If $u$ has only one
sign on $X$, the function is quadratic and its minimal dimension is zero.

For a fixed nonquadratic function normalized as
$G=x^{\mathsf T}Sx-(u^{\mathsf T}x)_-^2$, its entire fibre \emph{within
the rank-one cross-block stratum} is
\[
 B=u d^{\mathsf T},\quad \kappa(d,C)-\kappa(-d,C)=1,\quad
 A=S+\kappa(-d,C)uu^{\mathsf T},\quad C>0,
\]
subject to $A-BC^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}>0$. On a fixed strict active-face
stratum this fibre has dimension four, including the two endpoint scales.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $u^{\mathsf T}x<0$ scale $z$ by $-(u^{\mathsf T}x)$; for positive
sign scale it by $u^{\mathsf T}x$. Completing the resulting maximum proves
\eqref{eq:rank-one-profile}. Maximizing on the two axes or in the interior
gives exactly the listed feasible-face values. If the two coefficients
are unequal, absorb the smaller multiple of $uu^{\mathsf T}$ into $A$
and orient and scale $u$ so that the remaining hinge has coefficient one.
This is a one-endpoint representation. Its positive definiteness follows
from $\kappa(\pm d,C)\le d^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}d$ and the original Schur
inequality. A nonquadratic function cannot have zero endpoints. In the
other cases a single positive quadratic form represents the function.

For the fibre statement the intrinsic rank-one Hessian difference fixes
the line and normalized magnitude of $u$. Comparing the two half-space
polynomials gives exactly the displayed equations. Conversely those
equations reproduce the function. On each strict face, $\kappa$ is smooth
and homogeneous of degree two in $d$. The difference has Euler derivative
two on its level one, so that level is regular. The two coordinates of
$d$ and three entries of $C$, minus one equation, give dimension four.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Coalescence without coordinate deletion]\label{ex:coalescence}
Take $C=I_2$, $d=(1,-1)$, a mixed-sign $u$, and $A>uu^{\mathsf T}$.
Then $\kappa_- =\kappa_+=1$ and $G_Q(x)=x^{\mathsf T}(A-uu^{\mathsf T})x$.
Its minimal endpoint dimension is zero, yet deleting either original
endpoint changes the value on one open half-space. Thus two Hessians can
coalesce while no individual coordinate is redundant. For $k=2$, the
rank-one condition on a nonzero $2\times2$ cross block is a codimension-one
rank-loss locus. It is different from the one-cell walls of
Theorems~\ref{thm:one-missing} and \ref{thm:three-cells}.
\end{example}
