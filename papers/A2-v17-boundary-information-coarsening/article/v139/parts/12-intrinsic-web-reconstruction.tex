\section{The deepest normal cone and reconstruction of the web}
\label{sec:intrinsic-web-reconstruction}

The polarized Jacobian retains only one of two irreducible components
of the Pluecker vector of a web.  The full failure scheme retains
both.  In this section we recover these two projective components
intrinsically and prove that they determine every web in the smooth
Jacobian open.  Exterior support excludes every exceptional pencil
in this geometric family.  Thus the deeper boundary separates the
finite ambiguity of the polarized-K3 invariant.  The normal cone
used here is along the deepest Schubert stratum, not along the
reduction of the failure scheme.

Throughout this section \(\dim V=4\), \(W=\Sym^2V\), and
\(E=\bigwedge^4W\).  There is a multiplicity-free decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-web-components}
 E=E_{175}\oplus E_{35},\qquad
 E_{175}=\mathbb S_{(4,3,1,0)}V,\qquad
 E_{35}=\mathbb S_{(5,1,1,1)}V
       \simeq\det V\otimes\Sym^4V.
\end{equation}
Write \(P\) and \(Q\) for its two equivariant projections, in
that order.  For a web \(R\), write \([w_R]\in\Pj(E)\) for
its Pluecker point.  On the locus where both projections are nonzero,
put
\[
 \mathscr L_R=\Pj\bigl(\C Pw_R\oplus\C Qw_R\bigr).
\]
The quadratic ideal of \(\Gr(4,W)\) is denoted by
\(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Pl},2}\).  Each of its quadrics satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plucker-linear-quotient}
 F(aPw_R+bQw_R)=(b-a)\ell_{F,R}(a,b),
 \qquad F\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Pl},2},
\end{equation}
for a linear form \(\ell_{F,R}\), since \(w_R\) is decomposable.
Put \(X=\Gr(4,W)\), and let \(X^\times\) be the open
where both projections are nonzero.  Set
\(H_w=\langle\ell_{F,w}:F\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Pl},2}\rangle\),
and let \(\pi:X^\times\to\Pj(E_{175})\times\Pj(E_{35})\)
send \([w]\) to its pair of component lines.
Define \(G_4^{\mathrm{rec}}\subset G_4^\circ\) by the conditions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reconstruction-open}
 Pw_R\ne0,\quad Qw_R\ne0,\quad
 \dim\langle\ell_{F,R}:F\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Pl},2}\rangle=2.
\end{equation}
These conditions are independent of a representative of \([w_R]\)
and invariant under \(\PGL(V)\).

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic reconstruction on the smooth Jacobian locus]
\label{thm:generic-intrinsic-web}\label{thm:smooth-intrinsic-web}
The reconstruction open is the whole smooth geometric locus:
\(G_4^{\mathrm{rec}}=G_4^\circ\).  Let \(R\in G_4^\circ\) and
\(R'\in G_4^\circ\).  Every isomorphism of complex schemes
\[
 \widehat D_R\simeq\widehat D_{R'}
\]
implies \(R'=gR\) for some \(g\in\PGL(V)\).  In particular,
the abstract nonreduced failure scheme determines the cube-zero
algebra \(B_R\), up to isomorphism, throughout \(G_4^\circ\).

More precisely, the normal cone of \(\widehat D_R\) along its
intrinsically determined deepest Schubert stratum recovers
\(([Pw_R],[Qw_R])\), up to the same projective change of coordinates
in \(V\).  The webs with this pair of projective components are
exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-plucker-pencil}
 \bigl(\mathscr L_R\cap\Gr(4,W)\bigr)
 \setminus\{[Pw_R],[Qw_R]\}.
\end{equation}
On \(G_4^{\mathrm{rec}}\), this intersection is the single
reduced point \([w_R]\), before taking projective-coordinate
orbits.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The intrinsic deepest stratum and its oriented tensor cone}

For clarity, set
\[
 \Sigma_r(R)=\{K\in\Gr(4,V\oplus S_R):
                    \rank(K\to V)\le r\},\quad 0\le r\le3,
\]
with reduced structure.  Thus \(\Sigma_3(R)=\widehat\Delta_R\)
and \(\Sigma_0(R)=\Gr(4,S_R)\).

\begin{lemma}[The deepest normal cone]
\label{lem:deepest-normal-cone}
Starting with the reduction of \(\widehat D_R\), three iterations
of taking the reduced singular locus give \(\Sigma_0(R)\).
Consequently \(\Sigma_0(R)\) and
\(\mathscr C_R=C_{\Sigma_0(R)/\widehat D_R}\) are intrinsic.
Let \(\mathcal K_0\) denote the tautological rank-four bundle on
\(\Sigma_0(R)\).  The ambient vector bundle determined by the degree-one part is
\(\Hom(\mathcal K_0,V)\).  At a point \(K_0\subset S_R\),
choose bases and write \(T:K_0\to V\).  The fibre cone is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deepest-cone-equation}
 \Spec\C[t_{ij}]/\bigl(d(T)J_R(T)\bigr),\qquad
 d(T)=\det T,\qquad J_R(T)=I_6(\gamma_R\Sym^2T).
\end{equation}
Its ideal is generated in degree sixteen and its reduction is the
determinant hypersurface.  The two rulings of its rank-one locus
are intrinsically distinguishable over \(\Sigma_0(R)\): the
\(V\)-ruling is the projectively trivial one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a point of projection rank \(r\), invert an \(r\)-minor
and take the Schur complement.  The remaining \((4-r)^2\)
entries are independent coordinates and the rank conditions become
the usual matrix rank conditions, times smooth auxiliary coordinates.
The Jacobian criterion for minors gives
\[
 (\operatorname{Sing}\Sigma_r(R))_{\mathrm{red}}
       =\Sigma_{r-1}(R),\qquad 1\le r\le3.
\]
This also follows directly by differentiating the minors: the
rank-\(r\) stratum is smooth, whereas at rank at most \(r-1\)
the tangent dimension exceeds the local dimension.  Since reduction
and reduced singular locus are preserved by abstract isomorphisms,
\(\Sigma_0(R)\) is intrinsic.

Choose a splitting \(S_R=K_0\oplus S'\).  A Grassmann chart
near \(K_0\) consists of graphs
\[
 (T,B):K_0\longrightarrow V\oplus S'.
\]
The deepest stratum is \(T=0\); \(B\) provides its eight
smooth coordinates.  The Fitting factorization gives precisely
\(d(T)J_R(T)\), independently of \(B\).  All generators of
\(J_R\) have degree twelve in \(T\), so passing to the
\((t_{ij})\)-adic associated graded gives the same homogeneous
ideal.  This proves \eqref{eq:deepest-cone-equation}.  There are
no degree-one relations.  The dual of the conormal bundle is therefore
\(\Hom(\mathcal K_0,V)\), as also follows by quotienting the
Grassmannian tangent bundle along \(\Gr(4,S_R)\).  If \(T\) is invertible, \(\gamma_R\Sym^2T\) is onto,
so its maximal minors do not vanish simultaneously.  Thus
\(V(dJ_R)=V(d)\); the determinant is irreducible, and the
Nullstellensatz gives \(\sqrt{dJ_R}=(d)\).  This argument
does not require any higher-colon or primary-boundary calculation.

The rank-one subcone is recovered from the reduced determinant cone
by iterated reduced singular loci in each fibre.  Its projectivization
is the relative Segre variety
\[
 \Pj(V)\times\Pj(\mathcal K_0^*)
          \longrightarrow\Sigma_0(R).
\]
The two components of the relative parameter space of its maximal
linear \(\Pj^3\)'s are \(\Pj(V)\times\Sigma_0(R)\)
and \(\Pj(\mathcal K_0^*)\).  Their identification as schemes is
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-one-fano-scheme} below.  On points, note that a vector space of rank-one tensors
cannot contain both \(v_1\otimes u_1\) and
\(v_2\otimes u_2\) with both pairs independent: their sum has
rank two.  Thus a maximal such space fixes one tensor factor.

The first parameter bundle is projectively trivial.  The second is
not.  Restrict to a Schubert line in \(\Gr(4,6)\), obtained by
varying \(K_0\) between a fixed three-plane and five-plane.  On
this line
\[
 \mathcal K_0^*|_{\Pj^1}\simeq
 \OO_{\Pj^1}^{\oplus3}\oplus\OO_{\Pj^1}(1).
\]
If its projectivization were a trivial \(\Pj^3\)-bundle, the
vector bundle would be a direct sum of four copies of one line
bundle.  This follows by comparing the relative \(\OO(1)\)
on a projective-bundle isomorphism and pushing forward.  Its
determinant degree would then be divisible by four, contrary to the
degree one displayed above.  Triviality is invariant under an
isomorphism of the base.  Since the base is connected, an isomorphism
of the cone cannot interchange these two parameter bundles on some
fibres but not others.  It must preserve the \(V\)-ruling.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the ruling argument is needed]
A single determinant cone of square matrices permits transposition.
The preceding relative argument removes that ambiguity using only
intrinsic data on \(\Sigma_0(R)\); it does not assume that an
isomorphism of failure schemes extends to the ambient Grassmannian.
A linear isomorphism of tensor spaces preserving the two rank-one
rulings has the form \(g\otimes h\).  Indeed, its restrictions
to the two Segre factors are projective linear, and after lifting
those maps the remaining linear map fixes every decomposable line.
Applying it to sums with one common factor shows that its scalar is
constant.  Thus the coordinate change in the recovered \(V\)
factor is an ordinary projective linear change, as required below.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Reading both Pluecker components from the residual ideal}

\begin{lemma}[The two-component readout]
\label{lem:plucker-component-readout}
At any fibre of the oriented cone in
Lemma~\ref{lem:deepest-normal-cone}, its degree-sixteen ideal
recovers the projective pair \(([Pw_R],[Qw_R])\) whenever both
components are nonzero.  It detects the vanishing of either component
as well.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:intrinsic-residual-ideal} recovers the residual
ideal as \(\mathcal J=(\mathcal I:\mathcal D)\), and its
canonical degree-twelve coefficient module as
\(\mathcal J_{12}\simeq L^*\otimes\mathcal I_{16}\).
In a fibre this is exactly \((J_R)_{12}\).  This uses the
nonreduced ideal, not just its support, and is independent of a
local generator of the determinant line.

Let \(U=K_0\) and choose a volume on \(S_R\).  Its pullback
\(\beta_R\in\bigwedge^6(\Sym^2V)^*\) pairs with
\(\mathcal F(T)\) to give exactly the maximal minors of
\(\gamma_R\Sym^2T\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:universal-schur-readout}
now gives, as an equality of subspaces in the Cauchy decomposition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minor-coefficient-readout}
 (J_R)_{12}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
       \C\beta_{R,\lambda}\otimes\mathbb S_\lambda U.
\end{equation}
This is the fixed-slice consequence of the universal block identity.
It does not treat the fixed slice as a \(GL(V)\)-representation.
Intersecting with a Cauchy summand and contracting its right factor
with all elements of \((\mathbb S_\lambda U)^*\) recovers exactly
\(\C\beta_{R,\lambda}\), including its possible vanishing.
Lemma~\ref{lem:readout-exterior-twist} identifies these two lines with
\([Pw_R]\) and \([Qw_R]\), with their labels and one common
determinant twist.  Right coordinate changes preserve the left lines;
left coordinate changes act on both by the same representation of the
coordinate transformation.  Naturality under an abstract isomorphism
is isolated in Lemma~\ref{lem:common-g-functoriality} below.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-intrinsic-web}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:global-schur-recombination} proves that the
rank-two condition holds on all of \(G_4^\circ\), rather than
merely on a nonempty subopen.  The coefficient-rank condition in
\eqref{eq:reconstruction-open} defines this open algebraically.  Nonemptiness of the geometric
locus is independently witnessed by the smooth integral web
\(C_*\) of Lemma~\ref{lem:differential}; Appendix~\ref{app:separating-pencils}
retains its exact separating quadrics and the square-web normalization.

Indeed the restricted quadrics are \((b-a)H_{w_R}\).
When \(H_{w_R}\) is the full space of linear forms, those
forms generate the unit ideal sheaf on the line, and the
intersection has ideal sheaf \((b-a)\).  It is the single
reduced point \([w_R]\).  Without that rank condition,
the points with the same two nonzero projective components are
precisely the decomposable points \([aPw_R+bQw_R]\) with
\(ab\ne0\), which proves \eqref{eq:exact-plucker-pencil}.
This is an exact characterization of the ambiguity of the
\emph{two-component invariant}; it need not describe every
additional invariant of the full failure scheme on an exceptional
locus.  If the line is not contained in the Grassmannian, its
scheme-theoretic intersection has length at most two, because the
Grassmannian ideal is generated by quadrics.

An abstract isomorphism \(\widehat D_R\simeq\widehat D_{R'}\)
induces the graded normal-cone map of
Lemma~\ref{lem:common-g-functoriality}.  Its commutative coefficient
square and Lemma~\ref{lem:universal-schur-readout} give one
\(g\in\PGL(V)\) for which the pairs
\(([Pw_{R'}],[Qw_{R'}])\) and
\(([P(gw_R)],[Q(gw_R)])\) agree.  The two components are
transported by the same \(g\); the source-factor change acts only
on the right Cauchy factors.  Replace \(R'\) by
\(g^{-1}R'\).  Its Pluecker point belongs to the pencil
\(\mathscr L_R\), and both components are nonzero.  The reduced
one-point intersection gives \([w_{R'}]=[w_R]\), hence
\(R'=R\).  The algebra-orbit equivalence of
Lemma~\ref{lem:algebra-orbits} gives the assertion for \(B_R\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Separation of the Torelli packet]
\label{cor:separate-torelli-packet}
On the finite etale locus of the web-to-K3 map in
Theorem~\ref{thm:torelli-monodromy}, every fibre is separated by
the isomorphism class of the full failure scheme.  No further
restriction to a reconstruction subopen is necessary.
Distinct projective web classes in one such fibre have nonisomorphic
failure schemes.  On the regular Reye locus, the full failure scheme
therefore also determines the web's Reye congruence and its Enriques
datum.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every web in the indicated finite etale cover belongs to
\(G_4^\circ=G_4^{\mathrm{rec}}\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-intrinsic-web}
therefore separates all its projective web classes directly.
The Reye and Enriques constructions on
the regular web locus are functorial under projective equivalence,
which proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The result concerns the actual degree-sixteen ideal of the deepest
transverse failure cone.  It uses information that is lost on passing
to the reduced incidence image or to the Jacobian quartic alone.
It neither identifies an incidence-resolution decomposition with a
primary decomposition of \(W_3\) or \(W_4\), nor asserts that
all higher embedded primary components have been classified.  Its
geometric conclusion is instead an intrinsic inverse theorem: for every
smooth Jacobian web, the deeper nonreduced boundary recovers the
original relation space.
