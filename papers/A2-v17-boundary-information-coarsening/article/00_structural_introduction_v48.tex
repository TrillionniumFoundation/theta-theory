\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:v48-introduction}

We study the geometry retained by conditional laws of long alternating
bridges in a periodic dispersing billiard.  The observation records the
signed transverse endpoints, but not their normal coordinates.  Although
the probability of a successful bridge decreases exponentially with its
length, the conditional law retains two nonlinear half-line actions.
We recover those actions without knowing the flux amplitudes, invert every
finite signed contact jet, and reconstruct the entire analytic table and
its marked period lattice.  Finite rotations of individual obstacles
produce only finitely many global matching alternatives.  They produce
no additional infinitesimal ambiguity when every obstacle is noncircular.

The principal mechanism is a relative nonlinear limit on a collar which
does not shrink with the flight number.  A quadratic approximation at the
periodic orbit would lose the higher contact terms.  Moreover, a formal
inverse of the action series would not justify the inverse for actual
smooth boundaries.  The proof therefore combines a weighted half-line
construction, trace-norm control of the normalized twist, and a finite
Taylor-remainder factorization before any analytic continuation is used.
The statistical results concern how this geometric datum can be acquired
and how its information changes when further observations are retained.

\subsection{The observation and the main theorem}

A table $T$ consists of $N$ labelled strictly convex analytic obstacles
$K_1,\ldots,K_N$ and their translates by $L\mathbb Z^2$, where
$L\in\mathrm{GL}^+(2,\mathbb R)$ is unknown.  Distinct lifted closures
are disjoint and all curvatures are positive.  The basis of the abstract
deck group $\mathbb Z^2$ is marked; its Euclidean realization is not.
A selected clear channel $e=(a,b,\ell)$ joins the closest pair of
$K_a$ and $K_b+L\ell$.  Each channel has its own oriented contact frame
and signed transverse coordinate, with no inter-channel registration.
The full geometric conventions are stated in
Section~\ref{sec:geometric-results} and
Section~\ref{sec:v24-rank-two-lattice}.

For each channel retain its gap $g_e$ and, for each starting contact type
$b\in\{0,1\}$, the limiting conditional joint law $P_{e,b}$ of the two
signed transverse endpoints of an even bridge at one known positive
excess time $d_e$.  The intrinsic datum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v48-datum}
       \mathcal D_E(T)=\bigl(g_e,P_{e,0},P_{e,1}\bigr)_{e\in E}.
\end{equation}
Obstacle, channel and deck marks and the signed conventions are design
information.  No longitudinal position, residual time, waiting count,
curvature, flux amplitude, Euclidean deck vector or inter-channel pose
is supplied.  A law is a function-valued datum, not one scalar sample.
The gap is retained separately from the conditional laws.

Let $\mathscr C_N$ be the class in which every obstacle is noncircular,
and let $\mathscr A_N\subset\mathscr C_N$ be the class in which every
obstacle has trivial proper Euclidean symmetry.  The latter is open and
dense in the relative $C^2$ analytic-support topology
(Proposition~\ref{prop:v45-generic-asymmetry}).  A
\emph{common-strip analytic-support family} is $C^1$ into the Banach space
of bounded holomorphic support functions on one fixed complex strip,
with $C^1$ lattice columns; see \eqref{eq:v48-support-family}.
In particular the parameter derivative is itself analytic on a smaller
strip.  The differential statements below do not merely assume that
each parameter slice is analytic.

\newtheorem{structuraltheorem}{Theorem}
\renewcommand{\thestructuraltheorem}{\Alph{structuraltheorem}}
\begin{structuraltheorem}[Single-offset periodic rigidity]
\label{thm:v48-main}
For each $T_0\in\mathscr C_N$ there is a persistent selected design
$E$ of $N+1$ channels, consisting of an obstacle spanning tree and two
independent marked cycle gains, and a common sufficiently small positive
offset, with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The datum $\mathcal D_E(T_0)$ determines a finite set of complete
labelled periodic tables, including their marked lattices, modulo one
common proper Euclidean motion.  This set is obtained by finite choices
of incidence congruences followed by explicit lattice reconstruction
and geometric compatibility tests.  If $T_0\in\mathscr A_N$, it
consists of one table.
\item For a common-strip analytic-support variation through $T_0$, if
all gap derivatives and all endpoint-density derivatives on
contact-centered positive interior squares vanish, the table variation
is one common infinitesimal proper Euclidean motion.  Conversely such
a motion has zero intrinsic derivative.  In a fixed Euclidean gauge
the data derivative is injective.
\item On every immersed $p$-dimensional $C^1$ common-strip
analytic-support model in that gauge, $p\ge1$, one can select $p$
scalar functions, each a gap or an expectation
$\int\phi\,dP_{e,b}$ with $\phi\in C_c^\infty$ supported in a positive
interior square, which form local $C^1$ coordinates at $T_0$.
Their inverse is locally Lipschitz, with local control of every fixed
real support norm and of the lattice matrix.
\end{enumerate}
\end{structuraltheorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:v45-clear-skeleton} supplies the persistent design.
The signed law inverse gives complete analytic channel-frame images.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v49-finite-fiber} enumerates the finite matching
alternatives and reconstructs their lattices; the asymmetric uniqueness
conclusion is also Theorem~\ref{thm:v45-generic-determination}.
Theorems~\ref{thm:v48-infinitesimal} and
\ref{thm:v48-finite-coordinates} prove the second and third assertions.
Their local registration uses Lemma~\ref{lem:v49-local-alignment}, not
a choice of one globally preferred symmetry branch.
\end{proof}

The finite bound in the first assertion is a product of orders of
obstacle rotation groups along a spanning tree of channel incidences
(\eqref{eq:v49-fiber-bound}); it is not a sharp degree assertion.
The $N+1$ count is minimal for the stipulated connected two-cycle
architecture, not an information lower bound over arbitrary experiments.
The selected design and its marks are not discovered by an unmarked
trajectory algorithm.  The scalar coordinates in the third assertion
may depend on the base table and model; finite dimensionality is not a
restriction on the first two assertions.

\subsection{Proof of the geometric inverse}

The proof follows one chain.  For a bridge with stationary length
$W_j$ put $E_j=W_j-jg$ and
$b_j=-W_{j,uv}/[-W_{j,uv}(0,0)]$.  On a flight-independent collar,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-factorization} proves
\[
 (E_j,b_j)\longrightarrow(S_0\oplus S_p,B_0\otimes B_p),
                  \qquad p=j\bmod2,
\]
in each fixed differentiated norm with an exponential relative error.
The twist is normalized before it is compared; no absolute error is
divided by an exponentially small quantity.  The common-domain sublevel
integral then gives the endpoint law of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-law}.

For a same-type law, on a positive interior square,
\[
 f(u,v)=Z^{-1}B(u)B(v)\{d-S(u)-S(v)\}.
\]
The four-density identity of Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-density-inverse} is
\[
 1-\frac{f(u,v)f(0,0)}{f(u,0)f(0,v)}=t(u)t(v),\qquad
 t(u)=\frac{S(u)}{d-S(u)}.
\]
One nonzero scalar anchor fixes the positive normalization of $t$;
$S=dt/(1+t)$ is recovered on the signed interval.  The inverse does
not take a pointwise square root at the minimum.  The gap and the two
quadratic action coefficients recover the contact curvatures.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity} then recovers each higher labelled
graph coefficient through an invertible two-by-two block after subtracting
lower-order terms.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization} proves
that this is an identity for actual smooth representatives, including
ones differing by flat terms.  Odd contact terms remain part of the
inverse.

Analytic continuation now gives complete channel-frame obstacle images.
For a noncircular obstacle its proper symmetry group is finite.  Matching
all channel frames therefore gives finitely many candidate orientations,
not a continuous family.  For each candidate, two marked cycle
translations $v_1,v_2$ determine the unknown lattice by
\[
                      L=(v_1\ v_2)M^{-1},
\]
with the actual fixed integer gain matrix $M$; unimodularity is not
assumed.  The compatibility tests in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v49-finite-fiber} retain precisely the realizable
candidates.  On $\mathscr A_N$ all congruence choices are unique.

For the differential assertion the same chain is differentiated at each
fixed finite order.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v48-forward-C1} includes the
moving-reference amplitude derivative and the moving-cap normalizer.
Zero law variation then gives zero signed contact-jet variation.
Analyticity of the variation makes each entire channel-frame image
stationary.  A single nonzero harmonic determines an angular velocity
along a local alignment branch, even when the body has finite symmetry.
The lattice cochain then eliminates an invisible change of scale or Gram
form.  Thus the differential assertion uses noncircularity, whereas the
global unique matching assertion uses trivial proper symmetry.  The
circular comparison in Proposition~\ref{prop:v49-circular-comparison}
exhibits the distinction for this selected-channel design.

\subsection{Acquisition, information and related inverse problems}

The deterministic inverse from finite transverse histograms to complete
tables is Theorem~\ref{thm:v46-quantized-inverse}.  Its quantified
analytic, geometric and asymmetry bounds are separate from the hypotheses
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v48-main}.  Finite contact order is chosen before
observational errors are reduced; qualitative analytic uniqueness is not
used as a bounded inverse for noisy continuation.  Conditional analytic
continuation is discussed in~\cite{Trefethen2020}.

The acquisition theorem uses a richer calibration sensor: noiseless
planar endpoint positions, an exact physical clock and preparation
outcomes.  A same-final-flight pilot is followed by the coarser
transverse-histogram inverse.  The proof retains separate finite-flight,
flight-amplified timing and internal/outer cell-crossing errors, with all
failed preparations charged.  It is not calibration from histograms
alone.  The information results in Part II distinguish endpoint,
endpoint--residual-time, count--endpoint and planar-position records;
none is a premise of the deterministic periodic inverse.

Finamore and Leguil~\cite{FinamoreLeguil} study enriched marked length
data for finite-horizon Sinai billiards.  De Simoi, Kaloshin and
Leguil~\cite{DSKL} study marked lengths for analytic open billiards
under non-eclipse, symmetry and genericity hypotheses.  Neither datum
is \eqref{eq:v48-datum}, and no reduction between these observation
maps is used here.  The fifth version of Florio and
Leguil~\cite{FlorioLeguil} retains dynamical conjugacy results after
removing an earlier geometric spectral-rigidity assertion affected by
an error.  We use neither that removed assertion nor an identification
of conjugacy with the present boundary-law inverse.

Part I gives the geometric setting and then proves the relative law,
the signed contact inverse, analytic image recovery, finite incidence
matching and the derivative-kernel theorem in that order.  Part II
specifies observation spaces and proves the local information results.
Part III gives quantitative histogram inversion, calibrated acquisition,
and the full-position benchmarks and global estimators.  Detailed
alternative statements and extended comparisons are collected in
Appendix~\ref{sec:v27-introduction}; they are not additional premises
in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v48-main}.  The remaining appendices
and the two-collision companion retain the complete auxiliary proofs.
