\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:v48-introduction}

A conditional trajectory law can retain the shape of a reflecting boundary
although neither the normal coordinate of a collision nor the frequency of
successful preparation is recorded.  We prove this for long alternating
bridges in a periodic dispersing billiard.  The resulting inverse determines
both boundaries of a channel, including their odd contact terms.  On an open
dense class of analytic tables, finitely many selected channels determine
all obstacle images and the unknown Euclidean realization of the marked
period lattice.  We also prove that the same law map has no infinitesimal
ambiguity other than a common Euclidean motion.  Its restriction to any
immersed finite-dimensional analytic-support model admits local coordinates
made of finitely many scalar observations.

The object behind these conclusions is a nonlinear relative law, rather
than the quadratic approximation at a periodic orbit.  The probability of
a long bridge is exponentially small.  After conditioning on success, a
relative limit on a fixed collar retains two nonlinear half-line actions.
A density identity eliminates the unknown amplitudes.  A smooth
finite-remainder argument then inverts every finite contact jet.  Analyticity
is used only in passing from these local jets to entire obstacle images;
incidence matching and two independent deck holonomies finish the periodic
reconstruction.

\subsection{The marked observation and the structural theorem}

A table consists of $N$ labelled strictly convex analytic obstacles
$K_1,\ldots,K_N$ and their translates by $L\mathbb Z^2$, where
$L\in\mathrm{GL}^+(2,\mathbb R)$ is unknown and the lifted closures are
disjoint.  Curvature is positive.  The basis of the abstract group
$\mathbb Z^2$ is marked; its Euclidean Gram matrix is not supplied.  A
selected clear channel $e=(a,b,\ell)$ joins the closest pair of
$K_a$ and $K_b+L\ell$.  Its oriented contact frame specifies a signed
transverse coordinate, but frames belonging to different channels have not
been registered.  These conventions are made precise in
Section~\ref{sec:v24-rank-two-lattice} and in the geometric setup below.

Let $g_e$ be the channel gap.  For each endpoint type $b\in\{0,1\}$,
retain the conditional joint law of the two signed transverse endpoints
of an even alternating bridge, at one known positive excess time $d_e$,
in the long-flight limit.  Write this law as $P_{e,b}$ and its density as
$f_{e,b}$.  The intrinsic datum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v48-datum}
       \mathcal D_E(T)=\bigl(g_e,P_{e,0},P_{e,1}\bigr)_{e\in E}.
\end{equation}
The channel labels and signed conventions are fixed design information.
The datum contains no longitudinal collision coordinate, waiting count,
residual time, supplied curvature, flux amplitude, Euclidean deck vector,
or inter-channel pose.  The gaps are retained separately from the
conditional laws.  In particular, an exact law is not a finite scalar
observation.

Let $\mathscr A_N$ denote the tables whose individual obstacles have no
nonidentity proper Euclidean symmetry.  This is an open dense subset of
the analytic table space in its relative $C^2$ support topology
(Proposition~\ref{prop:v45-generic-asymmetry}).  For the differentiable
assertions, a common-strip analytic-support family means a $C^1$ family
of support functions in the supremum norm on one fixed complex strip,
with $C^1$ lattice columns.  The exact definition, including the Euclidean
gauge, is given in Section~\ref{sec:v48-differential}.  It ensures that
the parameter derivative is itself analytic; analyticity of each slice
alone would not be an adequate substitute.

\newtheorem{structuraltheorem}{Theorem}
\renewcommand{\thestructuraltheorem}{\Alph{structuraltheorem}}
\begin{structuraltheorem}[Single-offset periodic rigidity]
\label{thm:v48-main}
For each $T_0\in\mathscr A_N$ there is a persistent selected design $E$
with $N+1$ channels, consisting of a spanning tree and two independent
marked cycle gains, and a common sufficiently small positive offset,
with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The datum $\mathcal D_E(T_0)$ determines the entire labelled periodic
table, including $L$ and $L^tL$, up to one common proper Euclidean motion,
among all analytic realizations of the same marked datum.
\item For every common-strip analytic-support variation through $T_0$,
if all gap derivatives vanish and the derivative of each endpoint density
vanishes on a positive interior square, then the table variation is the
infinitesimal action of one common proper Euclidean motion.  In a fixed
Euclidean gauge the variation is zero.  Conversely, a common Euclidean
motion has zero intrinsic data derivative.
\item On any $p$-dimensional $C^1$ common-strip analytic-support model,
$p\ge1$, immersed in the gauge-fixed table space at $T_0$, one can select
$p$ scalar functions, each either a channel gap or an expectation
$\int\phi\,dP_{e,b}$ with $\phi\in C_c^\infty$ supported inside a
positive-density square, which form $C^1$ local coordinates at $T_0$.
Their local inverse is Lipschitz after the parameter neighborhood is
shrunk.
\end{enumerate}
\end{structuraltheorem}

\begin{proof}
The clear skeleton and exact determination are
Theorems~\ref{thm:v45-clear-skeleton} and
\ref{thm:v45-generic-determination}; neither theorem supplies the unknown
lattice geometry.  The differential assertion is
Theorem~\ref{thm:v48-infinitesimal}.  The scalar-coordinate assertion is
Theorem~\ref{thm:v48-finite-coordinates}.  Their proofs below use the
explicit finite-order inverse, analytic uniqueness for the variation,
and differentiable incidence registration.  In particular, the second
assertion is not deduced from exact injectivity alone.
\end{proof}

The channel count is minimal for a connected graph with two independent
cycle gains, not an information-theoretic lower bound over all possible
experiments.  The scalar functions in the third assertion may depend on
the base table and the chosen finite-dimensional model.  They do not
identify an unrestricted infinite-dimensional analytic class from one
fixed finite vector.  Neither the skeleton nor its marks are discovered
by an unmarked measurement algorithm.

\subsection{The local inverse and its linearization}

On a common interior square, the limiting law for one contact type is
\[
 f(u,v)=Z^{-1}B(u)B(v)\{d-S(u)-S(v)\},\qquad
 S(0)=S'(0)=0,\quad S''>0,\quad B>0.
\]
The four-density ratio satisfies
\[
 R_f(u,v)=\frac{f(u,v)f(0,0)}{f(u,0)f(0,v)},\qquad
 1-R_f(u,v)=t(u)t(v),\qquad t(u)=\frac{S(u)}{d-S(u)}.
\]
One nonzero anchor fixes the positive scalar normalization of $t$.
The action $S=dt/(1+t)$ is recovered on the signed coordinate interval,
without choosing a pointwise square-root branch at its minimum.  This
identity gives a smooth inverse at every fixed differentiability order.
For the two actions together, the gap and their quadratic coefficients
recover the contact curvatures.  At degree $n\ge3$, the unknown two
labelled graph coefficients enter through an explicit invertible
$2\times2$ block, after lower-order terms have been subtracted.  The
finite-remainder factorization in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization} is essential: the calculation
is an inverse for actual smooth boundaries, not merely for formal series.

The differential argument follows the same chain at each finite order.
Zero interior density variation gives zero action variation, and the
triangular inverse gives zero contact-jet variation.  The derivative of
an analytic support family is analytic on every smaller strip, so the
identity theorem makes each entire channel-frame image stationary to
first order.  Finite nonzero support harmonics then register these
images differentiably.  The two cycle translations $v_1,v_2$ determine
\[
                     L=(v_1\ v_2)M^{-1},
\]
where $M$ is their known integer gain matrix.  Its determinant need not
be $1$.  This last step is what excludes an invisible infinitesimal
change of lattice shape or scale.  Only after these geometric steps do
finite-dimensional duality and the inverse function theorem produce the
scalar coordinates in Theorem~\ref{thm:v48-main}.

\subsection{Quantitative reconstruction and the physical experiment}

Theorem~\ref{thm:v46-quantized-inverse} complements exact rigidity with a
quantitative inverse from finite transverse histograms to full obstacle
images and the marked lattice.  It assumes a common holomorphic-support
bound, geometric and density margins, finite-order forward bounds, and
finite asymmetry witnesses.  The proof first fixes a sufficiently high
finite contact order, then reduces the observational errors.  It does
not assert continuity of all derivatives in total variation, or a
uniformly bounded inverse for analytic continuation.  Conditional analytic
continuation has a different role from the qualitative identity theorem
used in the infinitesimal argument; compare~\cite{Trefethen2020}.

The finite acquisition theorem uses a richer sensor in its calibration
stage: noiseless planar endpoint positions, an exact physical clock, and
preparation outcomes.  After a same-final-flight pilot, the inverse uses
estimated gaps and transverse histogram categories.  The proved error
budget separates finite-flight bias, the flight-number amplification of
onset error, and crossings of internal and outer cell edges.  All failed
preparations are charged.  The complete calibration theorem and its
strict finite tolerance prescription remain in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:v47-calibration}.  This is an acquisition of
the intrinsic datum, not calibration from histograms alone.

The local statistical results concern the retained record separately.
Endpoint--residual-time data, endpoint data, count--endpoint data and
planar position data have different local experiments and information.
Those results, including the compact-parameter Le Cam approximations and
the direct-position benchmark, are developed in Parts II and III.  They
are not premises of the exact or infinitesimal rigidity theorem.

\subsection{Relation to spectral rigidity and organization}

Finamore and Leguil~\cite{FinamoreLeguil} recover finite-horizon Sinai
billiards from an enriched marked length spectrum.  Their datum is not
\eqref{eq:v48-datum}; no reduction between the two observation maps is
used here.  Florio and Leguil~\cite{FlorioLeguil}, in the cited fifth
version, establish dynamical conjugacy results after removing an earlier
geometric spectral-rigidity assertion affected by an error.  We use
neither that removed assertion nor an identification of dynamical
conjugacy with the endpoint-law inverse.  The comparison concerns the
observation and the recovered object, not a claim that one theorem
simply removes hypotheses from the other.

Part I gives the relative law, its signed contact inverse, analytic
continuation, intrinsic registration, and the differential theorem.  Its
initial section records the detailed local statements and observation
conventions used throughout the article.  The finite-histogram and
calibration results follow the geometric theory.  Parts II and III retain
the complete local information and global physical reconstruction
arguments.  The auxiliary proofs and the two-collision companion are
independent reading paths through the same technical foundations.
