\subsection{Finite experiments and the complete local analytic target}
The relative law and the analytic inverse together give a finite-experiment
consequence without truncating the contact geometry.  On the local bounded
analytic class of Section~\ref{subsec:v62-finite-experiment}, two actual
finite-bridge endpoint densities at the fixed offset satisfy
\[
 \|\psi-\widetilde\psi\|_r
 \le C\{ |g-\widetilde g|+
       (\|f_j-\widetilde f_k\|_{C^0(Q)}
                         +e^{-\omega j}+e^{-\omega k})^\vartheta\}.
\]
The argument compares their realizable limiting laws; it does not regard a
finite density as exactly factorized.  In an exactly calibrated selected
channel experiment, $2N$ independent full-phase preparations, including all
failures, yield a measurable estimator of the entire contact pair with
confidence error
\[
 C\left(\frac{\log(C_*N/\alpha)}N\right)^{
                         \vartheta\omega/(4\omega+\Gamma)}.
\]
Here $e^{-\omega j}$ is a uniform relative error, $\Gamma$ bounds the
acceptance exponent, and the positive offset and analytic prior are fixed.
The flight number is even and proportional to $\log N$.  This is an upper
bound for complete germs on a smaller disc, not a claim of statistical
optimality or a finite-dimensional parametrization.  The proof estimates
cell densities, fits a realizable limiting law, and then invokes the
complete analytic inverse.  It charges the exponential rarity rather than
replacing raw preparations by free conditional samples.

Estimated calibration adds the separately visible terms $jv_g+v_t$ and
$r_c/h$, where $h$ is the transverse cell size, and its preliminary
preparation cap is added to the total charge
(Corollary~\ref{cor:v62-calibration}).  This application substantively uses
the existing position-sensor calibration theorem; the exactly calibrated
power bound is not asserted for its total preliminary cost.  All original
smooth, global matching, unknown-origin, differential and acquisition
results retain their hypotheses and proofs.

With the persistent position pilot and its compact marked-class hypotheses,
Corollary~\ref{cor:v62-total-budget} further gives error
$C\{[\log(CB/\alpha)]^2/B\}^{\vartheta\omega/(12\omega+\Gamma)}$
with confidence $1-\alpha$ and at most $B$ preparations in total.
Its slower power includes the entire onset scan and coordinate calibration.
This remains a local analytic-germ estimate, not a quantitative assertion
for unmarked discovery or global continuation.
