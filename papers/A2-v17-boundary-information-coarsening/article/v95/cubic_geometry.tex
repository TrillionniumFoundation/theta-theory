\section{Classification of a boundary cubic family}
\label{sec:cubic-classification-v95}
We now compute on a family for which the observation derivative need not
be injective. Throughout this section $k=m_1=m_2=2$, $d=3$, the root
interval is $[0,D]$, and at least seven distinct clocks exceed $D$.
The weights satisfy $\alpha_b\ge\alpha_*>0$. Fix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-v95}
 0<s<r<D,\qquad f=z(z-r)^2,\qquad g=(z-s)^3,
 \qquad \gamma=1-\alpha,\quad \alpha\in(\alpha_*,1-\alpha_*).
\end{equation}
Let $u$ be a positive probability column, $U=(u,u)$, and let
$V=(v_1,v_2)$ be positive, invertible, and column stochastic. Put
\[
 K=u\{\alpha f v_1^{\mathsf T}+\gamma g v_2^{\mathsf T}\},\qquad
 q=\alpha f+\gamma g,\qquad P_j=K(T_j)/q(T_j).
\]
All coefficient norms in this and the next section are Euclidean norms
of coefficients in increasing powers of $z$; matrix coefficient norms
are Frobenius norms of the stacked coefficients. Constants may depend
on the fixed datum and design, but never on a nearby competitor.

\begin{lemma}[The complete admissible pencil]\label{lem:pencil-classification-v95}
Set $h_c=(1-c)f+cg$. If $s\le2r/3$, the set of $c\in\R$ for which
all roots of $h_c$ lie in $[0,D]$ is $\{0,1\}$. If $s>2r/3$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pencil-thresholds-v95}
 z_*={rs\over3s-2r},\qquad
 c_*={4r^3\over(r+3s)(2r-3s)^2},\qquad
 R(z)={z(z-r)^2\over(z-s)^3}.
\end{equation}
For $z_*>D$ the same conclusion holds. For $z_*\le D$ the set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complete-pencil-v95}
 \{0,1\}\ \cup\ [c_*,c_D],\qquad c_D={R(D)\over R(D)-1}.
\end{equation}
The interval is a singleton when $z_*=D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Direct expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discriminant-v95}
 \operatorname{disc}(h_c)
 =c(c-1)^2(r-s)^3\{(r+3s)(2r-3s)^2c-4r^3\}.
\end{equation}
Also $h_c(0)=-cs^3$. Nonnegative roots exclude $c<0$.
Since $(r+3s)(2r-3s)^2=4r^3-27s^2(r-s)<4r^3$, the discriminant
excludes $0<c<1$. For $c>1$, $h_c$ is negative on $[0,s]$ and has a
root between $s$ and $r$. Any remaining nonnegative roots must exceed
$r$. On this last interval their equation is $R(z)=c/(c-1)>1$, and
\[
 {R'(z)\over R(z)}
 ={(2r-3s)z+rs\over z(z-r)(z-s)}.
\]
If $s\le2r/3$, $R$ increases from zero to one and there are no such
roots. If $s>2r/3$, it increases to its unique maximum at $z_*$ and
then decreases to one from above. At the maximum the double root
condition gives $c=c_*$; substitution into $h_c$ gives its third root
$4rs/(r+3s)\in(s,r)$. For $c>c_*$ the larger of the two roots above
$r$ increases strictly from $z_*$ to infinity, because $c/(c-1)$
decreases strictly to one. It reaches $D$ precisely when $c=c_D$.
These three roots exhaust the cubic. This proves all cases, including
the endpoint equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact-fibre classification]\label{thm:cubic-classification-v95}
For every datum \eqref{eq:family-v95}, $\tau(P)>0$. Its spectral fibre
in the entire closed stochastic model is a singleton unless all three inequalities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonidentification-criterion-v95}
 s>2r/3,\qquad z_*\le D,\qquad c_*\le {\gamma\over\alpha_*}
\end{equation}
hold. A second spectral value exists if and only if all three hold. In that event the complete additional component pairs, up to
permutation, are
\[
 (f,h_c),\qquad c_*\le c\le\min\{c_D,\gamma/\alpha_*\}.
\]
In particular, on the open parameter region
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stable-region-v95}
 0<s<{2rD\over3D-r},\qquad 0<r<D,
\end{equation}
the exact spectral fibre is $\{Z(fg)\}$. This region contains
$s=2r/3$ in its interior, not on its boundary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $e$ have degree below three and belong to the normalization kernel.
Interpolation yields a matrix polynomial $E$ of degree at most three
with $E(T_j)=e(T_j)P_j$. The polynomial $qE-eK$ has degree at most six
and vanishes at seven clocks. Hence $qE=eK$. For its second marginal,
\[
 q E^{\mathsf T}\one=e B,\qquad
 B=K^{\mathsf T}\one=V\diag(\alpha,\gamma)\binom f g.
\]
Multiplying by $\diag(\alpha,\gamma)^{-1}V^{-1}$ proves that $q$
divides both $ef$ and $eg$. Bezout's identity for the coprime polynomials
$f,g$ then implies $q\mid e$, so $e=0$. This proves $\tau(P)>0$
without a full-rank hypothesis on $U$.

Every exact competitor has the same monic normalizer, by the injective
normalization equation, and the same $K$, by interpolation. The
$2\times4$ coefficient matrix of $B$ has rank two. Thus in
$B=V'\diag(\alpha')F'_{\rm coef}$ both $V'$ and the row span of
$F'_{\rm coef}$ have rank two. Each monic component is $h_{c_b}$.
The two $c_b$ are distinct and, by summing the entries of $B$, satisfy
$\alpha'_1c_1+\alpha'_2c_2=\gamma\in(0,1)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:pencil-classification-v95} implies that one coefficient
is zero and the other is either one or belongs to $[c_*,c_D]$.
In the latter case the weight on $h_c$ must be $\gamma/c$. It is
admissible precisely when $c\le\gamma/\alpha_*$; the other weight is
$1-\gamma/c\ge\alpha>\alpha_*$. Conversely write
\[
 \beta_f'=\alpha v_1+\gamma(1-1/c)v_2,\qquad
 \beta_h'=\gamma v_2/c.
\]
Both columns are positive, their sums are the required weights, and
$\beta_f'f+\beta_h'h_c=\alpha v_1f+\gamma v_2g$. Normalizing the
columns and retaining $U=(u,u)$ realizes an exact stochastic competitor.
This proves necessity and sufficiency over the full model. Finally,
for $s>2r/3$, $z_*>D$ is equivalent to
$s<2rD/(3D-r)$. The latter upper endpoint strictly exceeds $2r/3$
when $r<D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Quantitative pencil isolation]\label{lem:robust-pencil-v95}
On \eqref{eq:stable-region-v95}, for each fixed bounded range of $c$
there are $C,\epsilon_0>0$ such that every monic $p$ with roots in
$[0,D]$ and $\|p-h_c\|_{\rm coef}\le\epsilon<\epsilon_0$ satisfies
$\min\{|c|,|1-c|\}\le C\epsilon$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Compactness and Lemma~\ref{lem:pencil-classification-v95} first confine
$c$ to small neighbourhoods of zero and one. Near zero, $p(0)\le0$
gives $c\ge-C\epsilon$. The discriminant is locally Lipschitz on
bounded coefficients and its derivative at zero in
\eqref{eq:discriminant-v95} is $-4r^3(r-s)^3<0$. Nonnegative
discriminant therefore gives $c\le C\epsilon$.
Near one set $v=1-c$ and depress $p$ using its own root mean. Its
depressed coefficients $a,b$ satisfy
\[
 a=f'(s)v+O(v^2+\epsilon),\qquad
 b=f(s)v+O(v^2+\epsilon),\qquad f(s)>0.
\]
For a real-rooted depressed cubic, $a\le0$ and
$|b|\le2(-a)^{3/2}/(3\sqrt3)$. Hence
$|v|\le C\epsilon+C v^2+C(|v|+\epsilon)^{3/2}$.
Shrinking the fixed neighbourhood absorbs the terms in $|v|$ on the
right. Thus $|v|\le C\epsilon$. This argument does not assume that
$f'(s)$ is nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole-model coefficient recovery and stability]
\label{thm:coefficient-stability-v95}
At every datum in \eqref{eq:stable-region-v95}, every sufficiently
nearby closed-model competitor can be permuted so that, with
$\delta=\max_j\|P'_j-P_j\|_F$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-inverse-v95}
 \|f'_1-f\|_{\rm coef}+\|f'_2-g\|_{\rm coef}
 +|\alpha'_1-\alpha|+\|U'-U\|_F+\|V'-V\|_F\le C\delta.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\omega_{P}(t)\asymp\sqrt t$ for the entire closed model.
The constants can be chosen uniformly on compact subsets of the strict
region, with positive channel, weight, clock-separation, and design-weight
margins. The coefficient bound and the square-root order persist when
$U$ is replaced by any sufficiently close positive column-stochastic
matrix, including matrices of rank two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Subtract the normalization equations, substitute the uniformly bounded
coefficients of $q'$, and apply the left inverse of $\mathcal N_P$.
Interpolation then gives
\[
 \|q'-q\|_{\rm coef}+\|K'-K\|_{\rm coef}
                   +\|B'-B\|_{\rm coef}\le C\delta.
\]
Here $B'=K'^{\mathsf T}\one$. Put $\beta=\sigma_2(B_{\rm coef})>0$
and choose $M$ bounding $\|\diag(\alpha')F'_{\rm coef}\|_{\rm op}$
on the compact closed model. For small $\delta$,
\[
 {\beta\over2}\le\sigma_2(B'_{\rm coef})
 \le\sigma_{\min}(V')\|\diag(\alpha')F'_{\rm coef}\|_{\rm op},
 \quad \|(V')^{-1}\|_{\rm op}\le {2M\over\beta}.
\]
The singular-value inequality follows by testing $B'^{\mathsf T}$ at a
unit left singular vector of $V'$ for its least singular value.
Set $L'=\diag(\alpha')^{-1}(V')^{-1}$. Then
$\|L'\|\le2M/(\alpha_*\beta)$ and
\[
 F'_{\rm coef}=L'B_{\rm coef}+L'(B'_{\rm coef}-B_{\rm coef}).
\]
The exact rows of $L'B$ belong to $\operatorname{span}\{f,g\}$,
with bounded coefficients. Since the rows of $F'$ are monic, these two
coefficients sum to $1+O(\delta)$. Absorbing their excess into the
remainder yields $f'_b=h_{c_b}+O_{\rm coef}(\delta)$ with bounded
$c_b$. Lemma~\ref{lem:robust-pencil-v95} places each row within
$C\delta$ of $f$ or $g$. If both rows approached the same polynomial,
$\sigma_2(F'_{\rm coef})\le C\delta$, and the uniform bound on
$\|V'\diag(\alpha')\|$ would contradict
$\sigma_2(B'_{\rm coef})\ge\beta/2$. This proves the polynomial part
of \eqref{eq:full-inverse-v95} after permutation.

For the remaining parameters put
$A'_b=\alpha'_bu'_b(v'_b)^{\mathsf T}$ and
$A_1=\alpha uv_1^{\mathsf T}, A_2=\gamma uv_2^{\mathsf T}$.
Choose fixed bounded coefficient functionals $\ell_1,\ell_2$ dual to
$f,g$. The matrix $(\ell_b(f'_c))_{bc}$ is $I+O(\delta)$.
Applying $\ell_b$ to $K'=\sum_c A'_cf'_c$ and inverting this matrix
gives $A'_b=A_b+O(\delta)$. Entry sums give the weights; normalized
row and column sums give $u'_b,v'_b$, since the weights are bounded
below. This proves the full bound, and in particular rules out an
uncontrolled nuisance drift in the calculation of a leading constant.

For a real-rooted cluster whose coefficient displacement is $O(\delta)$,
its first two symmetric functions give
$\sum_i y_i^2=(\sum_i y_i)^2-2\sum_{i<j}y_iy_j=O(\delta)$.
Coprime cluster factorization transfers the coefficient bound to each
cluster. Simple clusters move by $O(\delta)$, repeated clusters by
$O(\sqrt\delta)$. Since $\delta\le2t/\sqrt{\min_jw_j}$ in the
Hellinger ball, this proves the upper modulus bound. For a lower bound
replace $g$ by $(z-s)((z-s)^2-v^2)$ with actual roots $s-v,s,s+v$.
The observation derivative in $v^2$ is nonzero: a zero derivative would
produce a normalization-kernel polynomial, force the normalizer derivative
to vanish, and then force the nonzero numerator derivative to vanish.
Thus $h_w(P_v,P)\asymp v^2$ while the spectral displacement is $|v|$.

All inequalities used have strict margins on a compact subregion.
The second marginal $B$ is independent of the first channel; its rank,
the normalization argument, and the pencil isolation survive a change of
$U$. Recovery of $A_b$ by the same functionals still applies. Positivity
keeps the root-splitting alternatives admissible. This proves the last
two assertions without asserting that the rank-one locus itself is open.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The role of the endpoint.}
There is a genuine change when the simple root leaves the endpoint.
Take $f_a=(z-a)(z-r)^2$ with $0<a<s<r<D$. For negative sufficiently
small $c$, the two roots near $r$ of $(1-c)f_a+cg$ split into real
roots: the leading equation is
$(r-a)(z-r)^2+c(r-s)^3=0$. The simple root near $a$ stays in the
interior. All three roots therefore remain admissible. The changes
$\beta_1\mapsto\beta_1/(1-c)$ and
$\beta_2\mapsto\beta_2-c\beta_1/(1-c)$ preserve the marginal polynomial
and, with $U=(u,u)$, the entire observation. Strict channel entries and
weights remain strict. This explains precisely why the boundary face,
not the relation $s=2r/3$, supplies the isolation mechanism.
An affine change of the root coordinate gives the corresponding family
on any interval $[L,D]$; clocks and spectral distances transform with it.
