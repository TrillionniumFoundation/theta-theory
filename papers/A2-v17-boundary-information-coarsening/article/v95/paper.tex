\begin{abstract}
We study polynomial spectra observed through stochastic channels and
an unknown scalar normalization. For a spectrally identifiable datum,
a real monomialization of the observation--spectral graph gives a finite
valuative formula for the intrinsic root exponent, without first
optimizing coefficient envelopes. A real weighted specialization gives
the complete leading spectral set, including inequality constraints.
For a binary cubic family with one rank-one channel, a boundary root,
and simultaneous double and triple roots, we classify the exact spectral
fibre throughout its two-parameter root domain. On an open identifiable
region the whole-model modulus has a square-root asymptotic. We compute
its joint leading coefficient fibre and its exact constant by two finite
Fisher least-squares programs, and construct extremizing stochastic arcs.
The same modulus determines the squared local minimax order on specified
shrinking oracle neighbourhoods. Global additive normalization estimates,
quotient geometry, and boundary-uniform perturbation results are retained
as complementary parts of the theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A singular observation map can identify an unordered polynomial spectrum
even when it does not locally identify unrestricted coefficient
directions. Stochastic inequalities are decisive in this distinction.
A direction invisible in the ambient coefficient space may have no
nonconstant admissible representative. The local spectral law must
therefore be computed on the real observation--spectral graph, not on
an affine tangent experiment.

This paper develops that principle in two steps. The first is finite
algebraic computation at a singular observation germ. Suppose a compact
semialgebraic model has observation map $F$ and monic target polynomial
$A_x$, constant on the exact fibre of a datum $P$. Let $c_{r,a}$ be the
coefficient of degree $a$ in the monic cluster based at a root $r$ of
multiplicity $m_r$. On a real lift introduce the squared observation
distance $G$ and squared cluster coefficients $H_{r,a}$. A simultaneous
monomialization has integer orders $a_i,b_{r,a,i}$ on its accessible
real divisors. In the nonrigid case, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-newton-v95} gives
\[
 \alpha_P=\min_{r,a,i}{b_{r,a,i}\over a_i(m_r-a)},\qquad
 \omega_P(t)=C_Pt^{\alpha_P}+o(t^{\alpha_P}).
\]
Here $\omega_P$ is the diameter of all spectra compatible with a
radius-$t$ Hellinger ball. The formula includes singular charts and
positive-dimensional exact parameter fibres. Its input is the defining
real graph; coefficient-envelope maxima are not part of the input.
Proposition~\ref{prop:effective-fibre-v95} supplies a real weighted
specialization whose joint root image has diameter $C_P$. It also
specifies effective output for algebraic input, without a complexity
claim.

The second step is a classification and a complete leading calculation
in a stochastic singular family. Take
$f=z(z-r)^2$, $g=(z-s)^3$, $0<s<r<D$, a rank-one first channel,
and a positive invertible second channel. Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-classification-v95}
gives necessary and sufficient conditions for singleton spectral fibre
in the entire closed model. An open identifiable region is
\[
 0<s<\frac{2rD}{3D-r}.
\]
Its isolation persists under perturbations of the clocks, strict weights,
and positive channels; perturbing the first channel may remove its rank
defect without changing the square-root spectral order. Moving the simple
root off the boundary has a different effect, and we exhibit the exact
fibre that then appears. Thus the family separates the geometric role
of the inequality face from an accidental equality among root locations.

Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-fibre-v95} computes, rather than merely defines,
the leading coefficient set on this region. It consists of
\[
 z,\quad z^2-X,\quad z^3-Yz-Z,
 \qquad X,Y\ge0,\quad J(X,Y)\le4,\quad27Z^2\le4Y^3,
\]
where $J$ is an explicit piecewise quadratic Fisher cost after eliminating
linear nuisance directions and the one-sided endpoint motion.
Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-constant-v95} reduces its diameter to two
nonnegative least-squares numbers:
\[
 C_P=\max\{\sqrt2\,\kappa_x^{-1/4},\,2\sqrt{2/3}\,\kappa_y^{-1/4}\}.
\]
The necessity argument is uniform over arbitrary nearby closed-model
competitors. The sufficiency argument constructs stochastic root arcs
for every point of the limiting set. In particular, the symmetric
triple-root split is not the entire extremal mechanism.

\subsection*{Relation to classical theory}
Real uniformization and resolution are classical tools, not results
claimed here; we use the constructions of Bierstone and Milman
\cite{v95BM88,v95BM97}. Computing a \L{}ojasiewicz order from ratios of
orders on a principalization is likewise classical, and the effective
ideal-theoretic calculation in Bivi\`a-Ausina and Encinas
\cite{v95BE09} is a direct predecessor. The broader valuative framework
for filtrations in H\`a's preprint \cite{v95Ha26} further clarifies why
finite divisor data, rather than an unstructured search over arcs,
are the appropriate algebraic object.

These results do not by themselves specify the leading joint spectral
set of a probability-normalized real model. The additional construction
here keeps accessible real divisors, the weights $m_r-a$, and the real
closure of the jointly rescaled coefficients. An unrestricted complex
resolution or a product of coordinate envelopes would discard relevant
constraints. Definable metric regularity, as in Lee and Pham
\cite{v95LP20}, provides general H\"older regularity statements; it does
not compute the cubic feasible set or the Fisher constant above.
The family classification and its exact leading program are the
model-specific content beyond these classical mechanisms. We make no
claim of a new resolution algorithm or of a universal ordinary-jet cutoff.

\subsection*{Organization and quantifiers}
The main text passes from the model to the finite real-valuative formula,
then to the cubic classification and its leading constant. The global
normalization and clock bounds concern moving classes with stated
uniform margins; the intrinsic asymptotic concerns a fixed datum.
Neither type of constant is silently substituted for the other.
The statistical consequence is a local oracle-neighbourhood statement,
not a globally adaptive estimation or confidence theorem.
\ifdefined\AIIcoreonly\else
The appendices are grouped by dependence: additive recovery and clock
design; intrinsic moduli and composed jets; quotient, inference, and
algorithms; structured singular perturbations; and comparisons with
related models. All the earlier global and local arguments remain
available there. The special cubic proof gives an independent derivation
at $s=2r/3$, while the main text supplies its classification and leading
constant on an open family. In the immersed determinant calculation,
an invertible leading matrix fixes the determinant degree locally and
excludes roots escaping to infinity.
