\section{The complete leading fibre and its Fisher program}
\label{sec:cubic-leading-v95}
Continue at a rank-one datum in the strict region
\eqref{eq:stable-region-v95}. The conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:coefficient-stability-v95} is stronger than a root bound:
it controls every coefficient and every non-root parameter to first
order in the observation distance. It therefore identifies all nuisance
directions which can enter the leading spectral set.

For arrays $H=(H_j)$ define the positive definite Fisher inner product
\[
 \langle H,H'\rangle_I=\sum_jw_j\sum_e{H_{je}H'_{je}\over P_{je}},
 \qquad \|H\|_I^2=\langle H,H\rangle_I.
\]
This is the metric at the fixed datum and in the original probability
coordinates; no untransported change of matrix basis is made. Let
$\eta=(\dot\alpha,\dot u_1,\dot u_2,\dot v_1,\dot v_2)$, where each
column derivative has entry sum zero. These five scalar degrees of
freedom are unconstrained, since all the corresponding inequalities
are strict. For $a\ge0$, $X,Y\ge0$, and $b,c\in\R$, set
\begin{align}
 p_f(z)&=-a(z-r)^2-bz(z-r)-Xz,\nonumber\\
 p_g(z)&=-c(z-s)^2-Y(z-s),\label{eq:root-jets-v95}\\
 \dot K&=\alpha uv_1^{\mathsf T}p_f+\gamma uv_2^{\mathsf T}p_g\nonumber\\
 &\quad+f\{\dot\alpha uv_1^{\mathsf T}
       +\alpha\dot u_1v_1^{\mathsf T}+\alpha u\dot v_1^{\mathsf T}\}
   +g\{-\dot\alpha uv_2^{\mathsf T}
       +\gamma\dot u_2v_2^{\mathsf T}+\gamma u\dot v_2^{\mathsf T}\},
       \nonumber\\
 \dot q&=\alpha p_f+\gamma p_g+\dot\alpha(f-g),\nonumber\\
 S(a,b,c,\eta,X,Y)_j
   &={\dot K(T_j)-P_j\dot q(T_j)\over q(T_j)}.
       \label{eq:score-v95}
\end{align}
Thus $S$ is an explicitly specified linear map to a finite-dimensional
space of arrays. The variable $a$ is the one-sided motion of the
endpoint root. The variables $b,c$ describe motions of cluster sums;
$X,Y$ describe the leading splitting of the double and triple roots.

Let $\mathcal L$ be the span of $S(0,b,c,\eta,0,0)$, and let $\Pi$
be the orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal L^\perp$ for
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_I$. Put
\[
 p_0=\Pi S(1,0,0,0,0,0),\quad
 p_x=\Pi S(0,0,0,0,1,0),\quad
 p_y=\Pi S(0,0,0,0,0,1).
\]
Only finite linear algebra is needed for this projection; it may be
computed by a Gram matrix or a Moore--Penrose inverse. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J-v95}
 J(X,Y)=\min_{a\ge0}\|a p_0+Xp_x+Yp_y\|_I^2.
\end{equation}
In the present family $p_0\ne0$, as proved below. Consequently, with
$z=Xp_x+Yp_y$, this is the explicit piecewise quadratic function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J-explicit-v95}
 J(X,Y)=\|z\|_I^2-
 {\min\{0,\langle p_0,z\rangle_I\}^2\over\|p_0\|_I^2}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[The leading coefficient fibre]\label{thm:cubic-fibre-v95}
For the entire closed stochastic model, the joint leading cluster
polynomials at scale $\sqrt t$ are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-fibre-v95}
 z,\qquad z^2-X,\qquad z^3-Yz-Z,
 \quad X,Y\ge0,\quad J(X,Y)\le4,\quad 27Z^2\le4Y^3.
\end{equation}
The clusters are respectively based at $0,r,s$. The displayed set of
$(X,Y,Z)$ is compact. Every point is realized as a limit of actual
stochastic alternatives, not just as a solution of tangent equations.
In particular this is an explicit finite description of $\mathscr C_0$
for the binary rank-loss intersection.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first describe admissible arcs for arbitrary finite jet variables.
Choose real $y_1,y_2,y_3$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triple-shape-v95}
 \sum_i y_i=0,\qquad \tfrac12\sum_i y_i^2=Y,\qquad
 \prod_i y_i=Z.
\end{equation}
Such a triple exists exactly when $Y\ge0$ and $27Z^2\le4Y^3$, by
the discriminant of the real depressed cubic. For $\varepsilon>0$ use
roots
\begin{equation}\label{eq:realizing-arcs-v95}
 \varepsilon^2a,\quad
 r-\varepsilon\sqrt X+\varepsilon^2b/2,\quad
 r+\varepsilon\sqrt X+\varepsilon^2b/2,\quad
 s+\varepsilon y_i+\varepsilon^2c/3\ (1\le i\le3).
\end{equation}
Vary each non-root parameter by $\varepsilon^2$ times its component
of $\eta$. All constraints hold for small $\varepsilon$, including
when some of the $y_i$ coincide or $X=0$; the endpoint condition is
exactly $a\ge0$. The polynomial derivatives in $t=\varepsilon^2$ are
\eqref{eq:root-jets-v95}, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fisher-expansion-v95}
 P_t=P+tS+o(t),\qquad h_w(P_t,P)=\tfrac12t\|S\|_I+o(t).
\end{equation}
The terms containing $Z$ have order $t^{3/2}$ in the coefficients and
do not enter this score.

There is no nonzero feasible coefficient direction with zero score.
Indeed apply \eqref{eq:full-inverse-v95} to the arcs just constructed.
If $S=0$, their observation error is $o(t)$, so $p_f=p_g=0$.
Evaluation of $p_f$ at zero gives $a=0$, and then its quadratic and
linear coefficients give $b=X=0$. Likewise $p_g=0$ gives $c=Y=0$.
This also shows that $p_0\ne0$: otherwise a suitable nuisance direction
would cancel $S(1,0,0,0,0,0)$. The sum of a linear subspace and one
closed ray is closed, so all minima defining $J$ are attained.
Continuity of \eqref{eq:J-explicit-v95} and the zero-score assertion
on the compact segment $X,Y\ge0$, $X+Y=1$, yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercivity-v95}
 J(X,Y)\ge c_0(X+Y)^2\quad(X,Y\ge0),\qquad c_0>0.
\end{equation}
For clarity, if $J$ vanished at a point of this segment, its minimizing
$a$ and the nuisance least-squares solution would give the forbidden
zero-score arc. This proves coercivity without assuming invertibility
of the ambient coefficient Jacobian.

Now take any sequence in the observation ball of radius $t\downarrow0$.
The whole-model bound \eqref{eq:full-inverse-v95} gives $O(t)$ changes
of both component coefficients and all non-root parameters, after the
unique nearby component permutation. Coprime factorization of the
first component at $0$ and $r$ shows that its endpoint root is $t a_t$
with bounded $a_t\ge0$, and its pair sum is $2r+t b_t$ with bounded
$b_t$. Its pair splitting, divided by $\sqrt t$, has a subsequential
limit $\{-\sqrt X,\sqrt X\}$, $X\ge0$. The triple-root component
has sum $3s+t c_t$ with bounded $c_t$ and rescaled roots $y_i$ with
zero sum. The real-rooted coefficient bound gives bounded $y_i$.
Taking a common subsequence supplies bounded limiting $a,b,c,\eta,X,Y,Z$
with \eqref{eq:root-jets-v95}--\eqref{eq:triple-shape-v95}.
Taylor expansion of the smooth probability map in coefficients and
non-root parameters gives \eqref{eq:fisher-expansion-v95}, hence
$\|S\|_I\le2$. Minimizing over the nuisance variables and $a\ge0$
proves $J(X,Y)\le4$. The simple cluster has zero rescaled coefficient;
the pair and triple give precisely \eqref{eq:explicit-fibre-v95}.
This proves necessity over all closed-model competitors.

Conversely, take a point in \eqref{eq:explicit-fibre-v95}, choose the
least-squares nuisance variables and the minimizing $a$, and use
\eqref{eq:realizing-arcs-v95}. For $J(X,Y)<4$ these arcs lie in the
radius-$t$ ball for all small $t$. At the boundary replace
$(a,b,c,\eta,X,Y,Z)$ by
$(\rho a,\rho b,\rho c,\rho\eta,\rho X,\rho Y,\rho^{3/2}Z)$,
$0<\rho<1$, and then let $\rho\uparrow1$. This realizes the boundary
as a closure limit. If all jet variables vanish, the exact datum
itself suffices. Coercivity and the cubic discriminant bound prove
compactness. The joint Hausdorff convergence established in
Proposition~\ref{prop:effective-fibre-v95} now identifies this complete
set with $\mathscr C_0$.
\end{proof}

Define two nonnegative least-squares values, both on fixed finite spaces:
\begin{align}
 \kappa_x&=\min_{a,Y\ge0\,;\,b,c,\eta}
                  \|S(a,b,c,\eta,1,Y)\|_I^2
            =\min_{Y\ge0}J(1,Y),\nonumber\\
 \kappa_y&=\min_{a,X\ge0\,;\,b,c,\eta}
                  \|S(a,b,c,\eta,X,1)\|_I^2
            =\min_{X\ge0}J(X,1).
                  \label{eq:kappa-programs-v95}
\end{align}
They are strictly positive by \eqref{eq:coercivity-v95}. A Gram matrix
for the seven free directions $b,c,\eta$, followed by a quadratic
program in two nonnegative variables, computes each value.

\begin{theorem}[An explicit leading constant]\label{thm:cubic-constant-v95}
At every rank-one datum in \eqref{eq:stable-region-v95},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-formula-v95}
 \omega_P(t)=C_P\sqrt t+o(\sqrt t),\qquad
 C_P=\max\left\{\sqrt2\,\kappa_x^{-1/4},\,
                    2\sqrt{2/3}\,\kappa_y^{-1/4}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Thus the leading constant is determined by the displayed finite Fisher
programs in the stated root, channel, weight, and design parameters.
The programs do not contain
an inverse observation-ball optimization. Their minimizers give
admissible arcs attaining the limiting diameter. On the shrinking
oracle neighbourhood $h_w(F(\theta),P)\le cN^{-1/2}$, take independent
categorical samples with $n_j/N\to w_j>0$ and $0<c\le1/(8\sqrt2)$.
If $\mathcal R_N(P,c)$ denotes the squared local minimax risk, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:risk-constant-v95}
 {3cC_P^2\over32}\le\liminf_N\sqrt N\,\mathcal R_N(P,c)
 \le\limsup_N\sqrt N\,\mathcal R_N(P,c)\le cC_P^2.
\end{equation}
No unknown-centre adaptation is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The feasible set of $(X,Y)$ is star-shaped by homogeneity of $J$.
Its projections are intervals $[0,X_{\max}]$, $[0,Y_{\max}]$, where
\[
 X_{\max}=2/\sqrt{\kappa_x},\qquad
 Y_{\max}=2/\sqrt{\kappa_y}.
\]
For the double cluster the diameter of the sorted pairs
$\{-\sqrt X,\sqrt X\}$ is $\sqrt{X_{\max}}$.
For a sorted real triple $y_1\le y_2\le y_3$ with zero sum and
$\sum y_i^2=2Y\le2Y_{\max}$, elementary optimization gives
\[
 -2\sqrt{Y_{\max}/3}\le y_1\le0,\quad
 |y_2|\le\sqrt{Y_{\max}/3},\quad
 0\le y_3\le2\sqrt{Y_{\max}/3}.
\]
For the outer coordinates use $y_2+y_3=-y_1$ and
$y_2^2+y_3^2\ge y_1^2/2$; for the middle coordinate use the ordering
and zero sum, with the minimum norm at $(y_2,y_2,-2y_2)$ or its
reflection. These bounds show that the triple diameter is at most
$2\sqrt{Y_{\max}/3}$. Equality is attained by the two sorted triples
$(-2h,h,h)$ and $(-h,-h,2h)$ with $h=\sqrt{Y_{\max}/3}$.
Both have $Y=Y_{\max}$ and differ only in the sign of $Z=2h^3$;
they therefore have the same score and are simultaneously feasible.

For sufficiently small $t$, optimal spectral matchings cannot cross
the three separated base clusters. The diameter in the maximum of
these three cluster metrics equals the largest marginal diameter:
for finitely many coordinates, supremum and maximum commute. This
argument does not replace the joint feasible set by a product. The
simple cluster contributes zero. Substitution of $X_{\max},Y_{\max}$
proves \eqref{eq:constant-formula-v95} by
Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-fibre-v95} and weighted coefficient convergence.
The root arcs \eqref{eq:realizing-arcs-v95}, with minimizing nuisance
variables and radial approximation on the unit boundary, realize the
pair and triple extrema.

For completeness put $t_N=cN^{-1/2}$ and $D_N=\omega_P(t_N)$.
A constant decision chosen in the local target set has risk at most
$D_N^2$. A maximizing pair in that set has one-sample weighted
Hellinger distance at most $2t_N$. Eventually $n_j/N\le2w_j$, so
the product Hellinger distance squared is at most $8Nt_N^2$.
Its total variation is at most $\sqrt8c\le1/4$. A decision rule
induces a test by choosing the nearer of the two targets; a testing
error entails squared loss at least $D_N^2/4$. The two-point testing
bound therefore gives risk at least $3D_N^2/32$. Substituting
$D_N^2=C_P^2cN^{-1/2}+o(N^{-1/2})$ proves
\eqref{eq:risk-constant-v95}. The constant upper decision depends on
the specified centre, as the stated local experiment permits.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Which split is extremal?}
The symmetric triple split $(-v,0,v)$ has $Y=v^2$ and largest root
modulus $\sqrt Y$. At exactly the same first-order observation score,
$(-2h,h,h)$ with $h=\sqrt{Y/3}$ has largest root modulus
$2\sqrt{Y/3}>\sqrt Y$. The symmetric split establishes the exponent
but does not exhaust the leading triple fibre. In addition, optimizing
the nuisance directions and the other cluster can reduce its Fisher
cost. Formula \eqref{eq:kappa-programs-v95} includes both effects.
This distinction is why a one-path lower bound cannot evaluate the
leading diameter of the whole model.
