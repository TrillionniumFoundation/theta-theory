\subsection{Sharpness and the algebra-length problem}
\label{sec:length-sharpness}
For a unital subspace $W$ over a field, let $\ell(W)$ be the least
$j$ such that $W^j$ is the algebra generated by $W$. This is the
usual length of a generating subspace. Algebra-length bounds are
studied in the literature of Pappacena and Markova; see
\cite{Pappacena,Markova} and the more recent discussion in
\cite[Sections 1 and 6]{Khrystik}. No novelty is claimed here for
the following field-level dimension argument, which works even
without commutativity. If $W^j=W^{j+1}$, multiplication by $W$
stabilizes that space and the sequence is constant thereafter.
Until then its dimension strictly increases. Starting with dimension
$k$ in an algebra of dimension $d$ gives $\ell(W)\le d-k+1$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:codimension-stabilization}, by contrast, proves
an equality of submodules over a coefficient ring without assuming
the intervening images flat. A dimension count on residue fields
does not establish that equality. Its application here is stability
of all Fitting ideals after arbitrary base change.

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness in every codimension]
\label{prop:codimension-sharpness}
For every pair of integers $r\ge1$, $k\ge2$, there is a connected
commutative local algebra $B$ of dimension $r+k$ and a unital
$k$-plane $W\subset B$ such that
\[
 W^r\ne W^{r+1}=B.
\]
Thus $r+1$ is the optimal universal bound for codimension $r$,
even when the generating rank $k$ is prescribed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take
\[
 B=\C[z,\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_{k-2}]/
 (z^{r+2},z\epsilon_i,\epsilon_i\epsilon_j:1\le i,j\le k-2)
\]
and $W=\Span(1,z,\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_{k-2})$.
When $k=2$ the lists involving $\epsilon_i$ are empty.
A basis of $B$ is $1,z,\ldots,z^{r+1},\epsilon_1,\ldots,
\epsilon_{k-2}$. For $1\le j\le r+1$, a basis of $W^j$ is
$1,z,\ldots,z^j,\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_{k-2}$.
The assertions follow, with $z^{r+1}$ witnessing strictness.
\end{proof}

The result is an optimal bound depending on the initial codimension,
not a claim to improve every bound involving the minimal-polynomial
degrees or the internal structure of a particular algebra. The
relative proof uses commutativity of the full multiplication operators;
it does not assert that their compressed blocks commute.
