\subsection{The quadratic incidence fibre as a functor}
\label{sec:quadratic-fibre-functor}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:full-codimension-two}, the notation
$D\setminus|T|$ means the open complement of the support of the
possibly nonreduced closed scheme $T$. Nothing is subtracted from
its structure sheaf. We supply a full functorial description of the
rank-two action fibre in Proposition~\ref{prop:codim-two-incidence-fibres}.

Let $C\subset Q$ be the conductor flag on that exact-action-rank
stratum. Then $C$ is a rank-two algebra, $Q$ is a rank-two locally
free $C$-algebra, and $M=Q/C$ is an invertible $C$-module. The
incidence fibre parametrizes rank-three subalgebras $E'\subset Q$
containing $C$, with $Q/E'$ a line bundle over the base.

A morphism $U\to\operatorname{Spec}C$ is an algebra map
$\chi:C_U\to\mathcal O_U$. Write $I=\ker\chi$; it is a line
subbundle of $C_U$, since the unit splits $\chi$.
Tensoring $0\to I\to C_U\to\mathcal O_U\to0$ with the
invertible $C_U$-module $M_U$ gives
\[
 0\longrightarrow IM_U\longrightarrow M_U\longrightarrow
       M_U\otimes_{C_U,\chi}\mathcal O_U\longrightarrow0.
\]
The last term is a line bundle over $U$. The middle term has rank
two over $U$, so $IM_U$ is a line subbundle and this sequence is
locally split over $\mathcal O_U$. Let $E'_\chi$ be its inverse
image in $Q_U$. It is a rank-three subbundle containing $C_U$.
To check multiplicative closure, one may work faithfully flat locally
where $M_U$ has a $C_U$-generator lifted to $e\in Q_U$. Then
$Q_U=C_U\oplus C_Ue$, $e^2=\alpha+\beta e$, and
$E'_\chi=C_U+Ie$. Products stay in this submodule because $I$
is an ideal and $I^2\subset I$. The closure condition descends,
and the definition of $E'_\chi$ did not use the generator.

Conversely let $E'$ be such a subalgebra, and put $N=E'/C_U$.
The exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow M_U\longrightarrow Q_U/E'
          \longrightarrow0
\]
exhibits $N$ as a line subbundle. Since $C_U\subset E'$, the
submodule $N$ is $C_U$-stable. Its quotient line $L'=Q_U/E'$
therefore defines an algebra map
\[
 \chi:C_U\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_U}(L')
          =\mathcal O_U.
\]
The equality on the right is canonical for an invertible module.
Let $I=\ker\chi$. The quotient map factors through
$M_U\otimes_{C_U,\chi}\mathcal O_U\to L'$. This is a surjective
map of line bundles, hence an isomorphism. Thus $N=IM_U$ and
$E'=E'_\chi$. Conversely the action on the quotient constructed
from $\chi$ recovers $\chi$ itself. All the quotient sequences
are locally split as sequences of base modules, and tensoring an
invertible $C$-module is exact. The two constructions therefore
commute with every pullback and are inverse on every test scheme,
not just on geometric points. This proves the asserted fibre
isomorphism with $\operatorname{Spec}C$ by the functor of points.
