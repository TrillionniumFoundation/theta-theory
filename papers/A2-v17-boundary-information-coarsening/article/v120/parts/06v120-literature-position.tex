\subsection{Generators, algebra length, and the boundary classification}
\label{sec:v120-literature-position}
The scheme-theoretic study of generators is an established input.
Arpin--Bozlee--Herr--Smith \cite{ABHS} construct the index-form
non-monogenerator scheme (Definition 3.12) and describe the
polygenerator open by maximal minors (Proposition 3.14); the numbering
here refers to the identified preprint version. Thus neither
representability, index forms, nor the use of maximal minors is a
novelty claim of this paper. In their construction Cayley--Hamilton
bounds powers of each individual generator. The additional tasks here
are the codimension-sensitive equality of image modules, descent to
the generating Grassmannian, the conductor incidence, and the complete
primary decompositions of the full boundary in the stated classes.

The field-level length inequality is separated from the relative
assertion in Section~\ref{sec:length-sharpness}. We do not use a
special numerical estimate from the algebra-length literature as an
unproved premise. The elementary dimension argument and the sharpness
family are proved in the text. The coefficient-ring proof supplies
the Fitting-ideal consequence needed when the base is nonreduced.

Theorems~\ref{thm:loewy-factorization}--\ref{thm:loewy-conductor-boundary}
identify the present boundary contribution precisely: a full
multiplication ideal, all its associated strata for Hilbert function
$(1,2,2)$, their primary representatives, and their conductor position.
They do not assert a primary classification for every finite algebra.
The higher-contact and complete contact-hyperplane results remain
separate theorems with their original scope. The discussion of
\cite{Ballico93} in Section~\ref{sec:finite-prior-comparison} remains
subject to the same source-access limitation; no exhaustive priority
comparison with that article is asserted on the basis of its title
or bibliographic record.

\subsection{Logical dependencies}
The finite-algebra core is self-contained. It does not take a theorem
from a manuscript labelled A1 as an unstated premise. The evaluation
and conductor comparisons proved here are the interfaces through which
a global linear-series problem becomes a finite-algebra problem.
The outputs available to further work are canonical multiplication
cokernels and their Fitting ideals, exact-action conductor flags, and
the primary boundary classification. A downstream application must
verify the stated generating and regularity hypotheses and retain the
scheme structure; the determinant support alone loses the embedded
strata. The historical polar, residual and statistical sections are
not prerequisites for this core. This logical dependency statement
does not certify that any particular version of a later manuscript
already invokes these results.
