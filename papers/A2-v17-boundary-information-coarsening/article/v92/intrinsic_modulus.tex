\section{An intrinsic modulus at arbitrary model points}
\label{sec:intrinsic-modulus}
A local neighbourhood must be specified before one assigns a statistical
exponent to a singular point. A fixed coefficient neighbourhood, an
expanding tangent ball, and a shrinking observation ball are different
experiments. The preceding sections address the first two. We now give
a representation-free characterization for the third, allowing all
intersections of the singular sets of the closed stochastic model.

Fix positive design weights $w_j$, with $\sum_jw_j=1$. For probability
arrays define the Hellinger metric
\[
 h_w(P,Q)^2=\sum_jw_j\sum_e(\sqrt{P_{je}}-\sqrt{Q_{je}})^2.
\]
The cell index $e$ ranges over the $m_1m_2$ observed outcomes. This
normalization has no factor $1/2$. It remains meaningful at zero cells.
For a datum $P$ in the closed-model image put
\begin{align}
 \mathcal B_P(t)&=\{\theta\in\cK_d:h_w(F(\theta),P)\le t\},\nonumber\\
 \omega_P(t)&=\max_{\theta,\theta'\in\mathcal B_P(t)}
                   d_\infty(\Lambda(\theta),\Lambda(\theta')).
                                                  \label{eq:intrinsic-modulus}
\end{align}
Both the maximum and the set are intrinsic to $P$. In particular,
\(\omega_P(0)\) is the diameter of the spectral image of the exact
fibre, rather than the diameter of a chosen latent parametrization.
The function is nondecreasing, and compactness implies
$\omega_P(t)\downarrow\omega_P(0)$ as $t\downarrow0$.

\begin{theorem}[Observation-neighbourhood minimax equivalence]
\label{thm:modulus-minimax}
Take independent categorical samples at the fixed clocks, with
$n_j/N\to w_j>0$. Choose a fixed $0<c\le1/(8\sqrt2)$ and set
$t_N=c/\sqrt N$. For all sufficiently large $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modulus-risk-equivalence}
 \frac{3}{32}\omega_P(t_N)^2
 \le \inf_{\widehat\Lambda}\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal B_P(t_N)}
       \E_\theta d_\infty(\widehat\Lambda,\Lambda(\theta))^2
 \le \omega_P(t_N)^2.
\end{equation}
No rank, normalization, positivity-of-cells, or component-separation
hypothesis is imposed on $P$. The local decision rule may depend on
$P,t_N$, as usual for a minimax problem on a specified neighbourhood,
but not on its unknown member.

If $\omega_P(0)=0$, then either $\omega_P$ vanishes on a neighbourhood
of zero, or there are $a_P\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ and $b_P>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intrinsic-puiseux}
 \omega_P(t)=b_Pt^{a_P}+o(t^{a_P})\qquad(t\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
In the latter case the risk in \eqref{eq:modulus-risk-equivalence} has
order $N^{-a_P}$. If $\omega_P(0)>0$, the same equivalence retains that
exact-fibre diameter as a nonvanishing term.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The compactness assertion follows from the compact root and stochastic
parameter ranges and the positive clock denominators. For the upper
bound select any target in $\Lambda(\mathcal B_P(t_N))$ and use it as a
constant decision. Every target in this local experiment is within
$\omega_P(t_N)$ of that decision. This is a local information-theoretic
upper bound, not a proposed globally adaptive estimator.

For the lower bound choose a maximizing pair in
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-modulus}, and write $D=\omega_P(t_N)$. The triangle
inequality gives $h_w(F(\theta),F(\theta'))\le2t_N$. Eventually
$n_j/N\le2w_j$ for all $j$. The affinity of independent product laws is
the product of their affinities. Consequently their squared Hellinger
distance is at most the sum of the individual squared distances, and
\[
 h(\mathbb P_\theta^{(N)},\mathbb P_{\theta'}^{(N)})^2
 \le\sum_jn_jh(F(\theta)_j,F(\theta')_j)^2
 \le 8Nt_N^2.
\]
Total variation is at most the Hellinger distance in our normalization,
so it is at most $1/4$. Given any target estimate, choose the nearer
of the two targets as a test. A wrong choice has loss at least $D^2/4$.
The sum of the testing errors is at least $1-\TV$, whence the larger
of the two risks is at least $D^2(1-\TV)/8\ge3D^2/32$. This proof
also covers zero cells, since it uses no likelihood-ratio denominator.

We justify the exponent statement in the actual closed model. Work
first in labelled root coordinates, with the finite permutation
quotient imposed only by the target distance. The constraints on
$U,V,\alpha$ and the roots are polynomial inequalities and equalities.
The map $F$ is rational with denominators uniformly positive at the
clocks. Introduce nonnegative variables for the square roots of cell
probabilities. The Hellinger-ball constraints are then semialgebraic.
The bottleneck distance is the minimum, over finitely many
permutations, of a maximum of absolute coordinate differences, and
is semialgebraic too. Quantifier elimination shows that the graph of
the maximum in \eqref{eq:intrinsic-modulus} is semialgebraic, with the
entries of $P$ and the fixed experiment treated as real parameters.
A one-variable semialgebraic function which is positive near zero and
tends to zero has a positive leading Puiseux monomial. This standard
consequence of real algebraic geometry \cite{BCR98,BPR06} gives
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-puiseux}. A semialgebraic function cannot alternate
between zero and positive values infinitely often near zero, so these
cases exhaust the possibilities. Substituting $t_N=cN^{-1/2}$ in the
already proved two-sided bound proves the risk assertion.
\end{proof}

The exponent has an exact arc description. This is useful when several
singular mechanisms meet, because no singularity is suppressed before
forming the admissible alternatives. For a positive semialgebraic
function $u(s)$ tending to zero, let $\operatorname{ord}_0u$ denote
its leading Puiseux exponent.

\begin{corollary}[Admissible-arc characterization]\label{cor:arc-order}
In the nonzero case of \eqref{eq:intrinsic-puiseux},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arc-order}
 a_P=\min\frac{
 \operatorname{ord}_0d_\infty(\Lambda(\theta(s)),\Lambda(\theta'(s)))}{
 \operatorname{ord}_0\max\{h_w(F(\theta(s)),P),h_w(F(\theta'(s)),P)\}},
\end{equation}
where the minimum ranges over semialgebraic pairs of admissible arcs
whose observation distances tend to zero and whose displayed numerator
and denominator are not identically zero. The minimum is attained.
With real algebraic input data and experiment constants, its value is
computable in principle by quantifier elimination and one-variable
algebraic branch analysis. No polynomial-time bound is asserted.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For any such pair set the maximum observation distance equal to $t(s)$.
The definition gives $d_\infty\le\omega_P(t(s))$. Comparing positive
leading powers yields a ratio at least $a_P$. Semialgebraic choice
selects a maximizing pair for every sufficiently small $t>0$; the
selection can be restricted to an interval on which its coordinates
are continuous and have Puiseux expansions. Parameterize it by $s=t$.
If its actual maximal observation distance has order $r\ge1$, its
positive target distance has order $a_P$. The preceding inequality
forces $a_P/r\ge a_P$, hence $r=1$. This pair attains the minimum.
Compactness ensures that its limits belong to the exact observation
fibre. The final assertion follows by constructing the semialgebraic
graph in the proof of the theorem and extracting its leading algebraic
branch; it is an effective description, not a numerical algorithm for
large instances.
\end{proof}

This theorem specifies the interaction through the admissible
observation geometry. It is not a claim that kernel dimension, rank
and multiplicity integers alone determine $a_P$, nor a smooth
coordinate normal form at every singular point. The semialgebraic
and two-point ingredients are classical; their role here is to give
one exact modulus, in the same experiment, on all intersecting faces
of the model. The next result computes that modulus on a substantial
singular stratum rather than leaving it as a variational definition.

\subsection{Anchored real-rooted multiplicities}
\begin{lemma}[Anchored real-rooted root displacement]
\label{lem:anchored-real-roots}
Let $f_0$ be a monic real-rooted polynomial. On a sufficiently small
coefficient neighbourhood restricted to monic real-rooted polynomials,
\[
 d_\infty(Z(f),Z(f_0))\le C\|f-f_0\|_{\rm coef}^{1/2}.
\]
If all roots of $f_0$ are simple, the exponent is $1$. If $f_0$ has a
multiple root, the exponent $1/2$ cannot be improved for this anchored
comparison, irrespective of its multiplicity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Factor $f_0$ into its powers of distinct linear factors. These cluster
factors are pairwise coprime. The derivative of their multiplication
map, with monic leading terms fixed, is invertible: a vanishing linear
combination of the varied factors reduces modulo each cluster factor
to its own variation times an invertible coprime product. Each variation
must therefore vanish; domain and codomain dimensions agree. The
implicit function theorem gives analytic cluster factors for $f$
near $f_0$, with coefficient errors $O(\epsilon)$, where
$\epsilon=\|f-f_0\|_{\rm coef}$. Root continuity identifies the roots
of each factor with those near its corresponding base root.

Translate a cluster of multiplicity $r\ge2$ to zero and denote its
real perturbed roots by $y_1,\ldots,y_r$. Its first two elementary
symmetric coefficients satisfy $e_1=O(\epsilon)$ and
$e_2=O(\epsilon)$. Hence
\[
 0\le\sum_i y_i^2=e_1^2-2e_2\le C\epsilon,
\]
so every $|y_i|\le C\sqrt\epsilon$. A singleton cluster moves by
$O(\epsilon)$ instead. Matching within the separated base clusters
proves both upper bounds. For sharpness at a multiple root $x$, replace
two copies of $x$ by $x-s,x+s$, leaving all other roots fixed. The
polynomial changes by $-s^2 f_0(z)/(z-x)^2$, whereas its anchored
bottleneck displacement is exactly $s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The anchored multiplicity exponent]
\label{thm:anchored-multiplicity}
Suppose $P=F(\theta_0)$ has strictly positive full-rank channels, strict
weights, $\tau(P)>0$, pairwise distinct complete component polynomials,
and component roots in $(L,D)$. Repeated roots within a component and
cross-component collisions are allowed. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchored-exponents}
 \omega_P(t)\asymp
 \begin{cases}
 t,&\text{every component root is simple},\\
 t^{1/2},&\text{some component root is multiple}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Accordingly, the shrinking-observation-neighbourhood minimax squared
risk in Theorem~\ref{thm:modulus-minimax} has order $N^{-1}$ or
$N^{-1/2}$, respectively. The constants may depend on the base datum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-recovery} supplies an ambient analytic
coefficient inverse under exactly the hypotheses stated here, without
any simple-root assumption. It identifies all nearby closed-model
coefficient representatives up to component permutation, even though
the real-rooted coefficient set at the base need not be a manifold.
Its local derivative bound gives coefficient distance at most
$C\|F(\theta)-P\|_2$ from the recovered base representative. Positive cells imply local equivalence of this
norm and $h_w(F(\theta),P)$. The preceding scalar lemma, component by
component, therefore bounds the target distance from the base by
$Ct$ or $C\sqrt t$. The triangle inequality gives the asserted upper
bound for two targets in $\mathcal B_P(t)$.

At an internally simple base, move one component root by $s$ and keep
all other parameters fixed. The observation distance is at most $Cs$,
and the aggregate anchored displacement is $s$ for sufficiently small
$s$, including when the original root is shared by another component.
At a multiple root split two copies symmetrically as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchored-real-roots}. The component coefficient change
and thus the observation distance are $O(s^2)$, while the target
moves by $s$. All roots remain real and interior, and the strict
normalization, rank and tuple conditions persist. Choose $s$ a fixed
small multiple of $t$ or $\sqrt t$, respectively, and use the base
point as the other member of \eqref{eq:intrinsic-modulus}. This proves
the lower bounds. The risk conclusion is
\eqref{eq:modulus-risk-equivalence}.
\end{proof}

There is no conflict with Theorem~\ref{thm:multiplicity}. That theorem
takes a supremum over a \emph{fixed coefficient neighbourhood} and
compares two moving alternatives. Here both alternatives must remain
within $O(N^{-1/2})$ Hellinger distance of the \emph{fixed base datum}.
For example, choose the polynomial $H$ in
\eqref{eq:moving-alternatives} with distinct real roots of sum zero.
When $m>2$, both $H$ and $H+c$ have the same nonzero second elementary
symmetric coefficient, namely minus half the sum of squared roots.
Their dilations are each at coefficient distance of order $u^2$ from
the multiple-root base, but their mutual coefficient difference is
of order $u^m$. Analytic coefficient recovery and positive cells make
these also the corresponding local observation orders. With
$u=N^{-1/(2m)}$, their distance from the base is of order $N^{-1/m}$,
larger than $N^{-1/2}$. They belong to every sufficiently small fixed
neighbourhood eventually, but not to the shrinking ball used here.
Thus the two exponents describe different geometries of the same
real-rooted singularity; neither theorem replaces the other.
