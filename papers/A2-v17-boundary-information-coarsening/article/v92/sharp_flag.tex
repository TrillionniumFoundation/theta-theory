\section{Sharp flag exponents and observable cancellations}
\label{sec:sharp-flag}
The common-flag theorem allows noncommuting and defective coefficients,
but assumes simultaneous triangularizability. We retain that scope.
We now identify a finite cancellation invariant and a condition under
which its exponent is attained by positive probability observations.
The pole mechanism is classical spectral perturbation theory
\cite{MBO97,KPM09,TH}; the point here is to enforce the scalar
normalization constraint on the perturbations and to identify the
resulting observation modulus.

Work in the square probability-quotient model of
Section~\ref{sec:flag-gauge}, with $\one^{\mathsf T}M(z)\one=q(z)$,
$q(T_j)>0$, $\tau(P)>0$, and strictly positive observed cells. A fixed-normalizer
slice has numerator perturbations $E(z)$ satisfying
$\one^{\mathsf T}E(z)\one=0$. Its competitors need not share the true
flag. Let $\omega^{[q]}_P(t)$ denote the diameter of their spectral
targets in the Hellinger ball of radius $t$ with $q$ held fixed.
For small $t$, interpolation bounds their coefficient errors and
preserves the invertible leading coefficient; no extra root
identification is needed to define this local diameter. Let
$\omega^{\rm rat}_P(t)$ denote the analogous local modulus when the
bounded monic normalizer is also allowed to vary in the positive
square rational experiment. These two functions are distinguished
from the stochastic-model modulus $\omega_P$.

\subsection{A finite pole invariant}
At a root $r$ of $\det M$, write the triangular form as
$T=D_f+N$. For an increasing path
$\mathcal P=(i_0,\ldots,i_h)$, including paths of length zero, put
\[
 \Phi_{\mathcal P}(z)=(-1)^h
        \frac{\prod_{a<h}N_{i_ai_{a+1}}(z)}
             {\prod_{a\le h}f_{i_a}(z)}.
\]
For each nonzero edge write $N_{ij}(z)=(z-r)^{e_{ij}}n_{ij}(z)$ and
$f_i(z)=(z-r)^{m_i}g_i(z)$, with the residual factors nonzero at $r$.
Let
\[
 p(\mathcal P)=\sum_{a\le h}m_{i_a}-\sum_{a<h}e_{i_ai_{a+1}},
 \qquad
 \psi_{\mathcal P}=(-1)^h
       \frac{\prod_{a<h}n_{i_ai_{a+1}}}{\prod_{a\le h}g_{i_a}}.
\]
An empty numerator product is one. For $s\ge1$ define the matrix
$C_{r,s}$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-laurent-test}
 (C_{r,s})_{ij}=
 \sum_{\substack{\mathcal P:i\leadsto j\\p(\mathcal P)\ge s}}
       [(z-r)^{p(\mathcal P)-s}]\psi_{\mathcal P}(z).
\end{equation}
The bracket denotes a Taylor coefficient at $r$. Only finitely many
paths and Taylor coefficients, with orders bounded by $kd$, occur.

\begin{proposition}[Exact cancellation test]\label{prop:flag-pole-test}
The actual pole order of $M^{-1}$ at $r$ is
\[
 \nu_r=\max\{s:C_{r,s}\ne0\}.
\]
Its leading Laurent matrix is
$L_r=A_0^{-1}Q C_{r,\nu_r}Q^*$ in the notation of
\eqref{eq:flag-triangularization}. Both $\nu_r$ and $L_r$ are
independent of the chosen flag once expressed in the observation
coordinates. In particular $\nu_r$ is at most the flag-path count at
$r$; strict inequality is decided by the explicit vanishing conditions
\eqref{eq:path-laurent-test}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The finite upper-triangular inverse expansion gives
$(T^{-1})_{ij}=\sum_{\mathcal P:i\leadsto j}\Phi_{\mathcal P}$.
Expanding each summand at $r$ gives precisely
\eqref{eq:path-laurent-test}. Multiplication by the constant invertible
matrices in $M^{-1}=A_0^{-1}QT^{-1}Q^*$ cannot change the largest
nonzero pole order and gives the stated Laurent matrix. This also
proves invariance and the assertion about cancellations.
\end{proof}

For a concrete cancellation, take
\[
 T(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z^2&z&1\\0&z^2&z\\0&0&z^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The direct and two-edge terms in $(T^{-1})_{13}$ are
$-z^{-4}$ and $z^{-4}$ and cancel identically. The adjacent entries
are $-z^{-3}$, so $\nu_0=3$, while the uncorrected three-vertex
flag count is $6$. This is also realizable as a positive quotient:
with strictly positive invertible doubly stochastic $L,R$, put
$M=LTR^{\mathsf T}/3$ and
$q=z^2+2z/3+1/3$. Observations at positive clocks are strictly
positive. The constant upper-right entry makes the common divisor of
the numerator entries one, so $\tau>0$ at five clocks. Thus the
cancellation is not confined to a formal triangular matrix.

\begin{theorem}[Exposure, sharpness, and the observation modulus]
\label{thm:sharp-flag-modulus}
Let $\nu=\max_r\nu_r$. On the fixed-normalizer slice,
\[
                         \omega^{[q]}_P(t)\le C t^{1/\nu}.
\]
Suppose some real root $r$ with $\nu_r=\nu$ has an exposed leading
Laurent matrix, in the sense
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-exposure}
                        L_r\notin\C\,\one\one^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
Then there is a constant rank-one perturbation $E=uv^{\mathsf T}$
with $\one^{\mathsf T}E\one=0$ such that $c=v^{\mathsf T}L_ru\ne0$.
It preserves the normalizer and the positivity of the observed cells
for sufficiently small real perturbation size. Its root displacement
at $r$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-sharp-root}
           |c|^{1/\nu}|\varepsilon|^{1/\nu}
                                +o(|\varepsilon|^{1/\nu}).
\end{equation}
Consequently $\omega^{[q]}_P(t)\asymp t^{1/\nu}$.
If, in addition, $\nu$ equals the maximal path count $\nu_Q$ of the
cluster version of Theorem~\ref{thm:flag-gauge}, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-flag-modulus}
              \omega^{\rm rat}_P(t)\asymp t^{1/\nu_Q}.
\end{equation}
The latter assertion allows the normalizer and the competitor flag
to vary. It is a theorem for the positive rational extension, not an
assertion that its complex-root competitors belong to $\cK_d$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the fixed-normalizer slice, interpolation and positive fixed
clock denominators give coefficient error $O(t)$ from an observation
ball of radius $t$. Near each isolated root $r$ the true resolvent
has norm at most $C|z-r|^{-\nu_r}$. Neumann exclusion on circles of
radius $Ct^{1/\nu_r}$, and argument-principle counting along the
coefficient homotopy, match the same number of roots. Away from the
root circles the resolvent is uniformly bounded. The invertible
leading coefficient persists and prevents loss at infinity. The
triangle inequality gives the diameter bound.

The rank-one matrices with either $u\perp\one$ or $v\perp\one$
span the hyperplane of matrices with zero entry sum. Its annihilator
under $E\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(L_rE)$ is precisely
$\C\one\one^{\mathsf T}$. Because $r$ and the coefficients are real,
$L_r$ is real, and \eqref{eq:flag-exposure} yields real $u,v$ with
$c\ne0$. Write $\det M(z)=(z-r)^m b(z)$, where $b(r)\ne0$ and
$m\ge\nu$. The determinant lemma gives, near $r$,
\[
 \det(M+\varepsilon uv^{\mathsf T})
  =(z-r)^{m-\nu}b(z)
       \{(z-r)^\nu+\varepsilon(c+O(z-r))\}.
\]
Rescaling $z-r=|\varepsilon|^{1/\nu}\zeta$ and applying Rouch\'e's
theorem gives $\nu$ roots with modulus
$|c|^{1/\nu}|\varepsilon|^{1/\nu}+o(|\varepsilon|^{1/\nu})$;
the other $m-\nu$ roots remain at $r$. All base roots in this cluster
are equal, proving \eqref{eq:flag-sharp-root}. The fixed normalizer
is preserved because $E$ has zero entry sum, and strict positivity
at the finitely many clocks persists. Furthermore
\[
 h_w((M+\varepsilon E)/q,M/q)
   =\tfrac12|\varepsilon|\|(E(T_j)/q(T_j))_j\|_{H_{P,w}}
                                           +o(|\varepsilon|).
\]
The norm is positive, so this path proves the matching lower bound
inside the fixed-normalizer observation ball.

For the final assertion the fixed-normalizer slice supplies the same
lower bound in the larger experiment. Since $\tau(P)>0$ is fixed,
the cluster flag theorem gives upper bound
$C(t^{1/m_*}+t^{1/\nu_Q})$, where $m_*\le\nu_Q$ is the largest
scalar cluster count. This is $O(t^{1/\nu_Q})$. Apply it from the base
to each member of a pair to obtain \eqref{eq:full-flag-modulus}.
\end{proof}

When $\nu<\nu_Q$, the improved fixed-normalizer assertion remains
valid, but one must not simply substitute $\nu$ into the two-scale
unknown-normalizer bound. The interpolation gauge can break a
cancellation between products of entries. A uniform improvement in
that larger experiment requires controlling those gauge-dependent
cancellations as well. The exact test above characterizes the pole
at the specified datum; Theorem~\ref{thm:flag-gauge} retains its
uniform path certificate for the full gauge perturbation.

\subsection{Sharp positive families, including noncommuting coefficients}
Let $J_k$ be the nilpotent Jordan shift, and choose strictly positive
invertible doubly stochastic matrices $L,R$, for example
$(I+\one\one^{\mathsf T})/(k+1)$. First set
\[
 T(z)=(z-r)^dI+\beta J_k,\quad
 M(z)=LT(z)R^{\mathsf T}/k,\quad
 q(z)=(z-r)^d+\beta(k-1)/k,\qquad\beta>0.
\]
At clocks $T_j>r$, the observations are strictly positive. A constant
Jordan entry makes the greatest common divisor of the numerator
entries one, so $\tau>0$ at $2d+1$ clocks. At $r$ the pole order is
$kd$, and the leading Laurent matrix is a nonzero multiple of
$R^{-\mathsf T}e_1e_k^{\mathsf T}L^{-1}$. It is not proportional to
$\one\one^{\mathsf T}$. Thus the exponent $1/(kd)$ is attained by
normalizer-preserving positive perturbations, and agrees with the
upper bound in the whole positive rational experiment.

For a genuinely noncommuting and defective family, take $d=2$ and
\[
 T(z)=z^2I+\eta z\,\operatorname{diag}(c_1,\ldots,c_k)+\beta J_k,
 \qquad \eta,\beta>0,\qquad c_i>0\ \text{distinct},
\]
with the same $L,R$ and scaling by $1/k$. The scalar normalizer is
$z^2+\eta k^{-1}(\sum_i c_i)z+\beta(k-1)/k$. Positive clocks give
strictly positive probability matrices and $\tau>0$. The coefficient
of $z$ does not commute with the constant coefficient, since
$[\operatorname{diag}(c_i),J_k]\ne0$; the constant coefficient in the
normalized algebra is similar to the defective matrix $\beta J_k$.
Each diagonal polynomial has one root at zero. The longest path
there has count $k$, its top Laurent term cannot cancel, and its
Laurent matrix is again a nonzero multiple of
$R^{-\mathsf T}e_1e_k^{\mathsf T}L^{-1}$. The other roots
$-\eta c_i$ are distinct and simple. Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-flag-modulus}
therefore gives $\omega^{\rm rat}_P(t)\asymp t^{1/k}$, with a linear
scalar-normalization term and a matching $k$-vertex matrix term.
This is a sharp common-flag noncommuting law in the observation
metric, rather than a claim about arbitrary nontriangularizable
coefficient algebras.
