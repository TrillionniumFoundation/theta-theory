\subsection{Information lost by removing residual time}
The choice of reported coordinates has a sharp effect even when the
underlying table and physical preparation are unchanged. Put
$q_j=e^{-j\gamma}$ and $r_j=q_j^2\log(1/q_j)$. The quadratic
endpoint--residual law has equal densities on its common support with
the boundary law, and hence has an erasure transition at $kq_j$ of
order one. Integrating out residual time gives instead the density
\[
 \frac2\pi(1-e^{-\zeta_j}x_1^2-e^{\zeta_j}x_2^2)_+,
 \qquad \zeta_j=\log\frac{1+q_j}{1-q_j},
\]
after a common invertible whitening. Its linear vanishing at the moving
boundary changes the effective scale to $kr_j$. The distinction is not
visible in a weak limit of one record.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v17-boundary-information} identifies the responsible
boundary coefficient for a density $a_t(w_t)_+$:
\[
 \mathcal I_\Sigma=
 \int_{\{w_0=0\}}
       \frac{a_0(\partial_t w_0)^2}{|\nabla w_0|}\,\mathrm d\sigma.
\]
The Hellinger expansion is
$H^2=\mathcal I_\Sigma t^2\log(1/|t|)/4+O(t^2)$.
A truncated-score argument gives the corresponding normal likelihood
limit and retains independent rare-event thinning. The theorem is proved
directly, with the classical linear-vanishing-boundary phenomenon
attributed to Smith~\cite{Smith1985}; no finite-information regularity
is assumed at the moving boundary.

For the physical endpoint experiment the coefficient is exactly one.
Theorems~\ref{thm:v17-endpoint-tangent}
and~\ref{thm:v17-endpoint-physical} show that, if
$k_jr_j\to b$ and the positive offsets satisfy
$\omega(d_j)=o(r_j)$, then the total variation distance between the
finite and boundary endpoint laws tends to $2\Phi(\sqrt b)-1$.
Here $\omega(d)=\sqrt d$ for general smooth contacts, and $\omega(d)=d$
for individually even contacts. For raw preparations, $k_j$ is replaced
by $n_jp_{j,d_j}^0$, where $p_{j,d_j}^0=d_j^2/(2A\sinh(j\gamma))$.
The minimum equal-prior testing error is $\Phi(-\sqrt b)$.
At the same endpoint-critical scale, the complete endpoint--residual
records are already asymptotically separated, whereas success bits alone
are asymptotically indistinguishable
(Corollary~\ref{cor:v17-coarsening-hierarchy}). Thus the exact multiplier
sets different critical scales for different physical observations.
This is a sharp comparison of a fixed table's finite law with its boundary
model; it is distinct from both geometric reconstruction and minimax
acquisition of unknown smooth profiles.
