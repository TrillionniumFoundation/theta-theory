\section{A weighted determinantal primary decomposition}
\label{sec:weighted-flags}
For an ideal $J$, write $I_i(A)$ for the ideal of $i$-minors of $A$,
with $I_0(A)=(1)$. For a prime $P$ in a noetherian ring $R$, put
$P^{(s)}=P^sR_P\cap R$ for $s\ge1$, and $P^{(s)}=R$ for $s\le0$.

\begin{lemma}[Coefficientwise symbolic orders]
\label{lem:coefficient-symbolic}
Let $R_0=\C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, let $J\subset R_0$ be prime, and
put $P=(t,J)\subset R_0[t]$. For $m\ge1$ and
$f=\sum_{u\ge0}t^u f_u$, one has
\[
 f\in P^{(m)}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f_u\in J^{(m-u)}\quad\hbox{for every }0\le u<m.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Localize $R_0$ at $J$, and denote its maximal ideal by $\mathfrak m$.
In $R_{0,J}[t]$ the quotient by $(\mathfrak m,t)^m$ is an Artinian
local ring: it is a finite module over $R_{0,J}/\mathfrak m^m$
and $t$ is nilpotent. Hence $(\mathfrak m,t)^m$ is
$(\mathfrak m,t)$-primary, and contracting it from the localization
at $(\mathfrak m,t)$ does not enlarge it. This localization is
$R_0[t]_P$. Polynomial expansion gives
\[
 (\mathfrak m,t)^m
   =\bigoplus_{u\ge0}t^u\mathfrak m^{\max\{m-u,0\}}
\]
as an $R_{0,J}$-submodule. Contracting coefficient by coefficient to
$R_0$ proves the assertion. No assumption about ordinary and symbolic
powers of $J$ is involved.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Order of a minor at a rank stratum]
\label{lem:minor-symbolic-order}
Let $A$ be a generic $q$ by $p$ matrix, where $p\ge q$.
For $1\le j\le k\le q$,
\[
 I_k(A)\subset I_j(A)^{(k-j+1)}.
\]
The ideal $I_j(A)$ is prime and its quotient is normal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Primality and normality are the classical generic determinantal
results; for instance \cite[Theorem 3.5(c)]{BRW05}, with
$r=j-1$, gives normality and the domain assertion (the case $j=1$
is a polynomial quotient). To prove the order statement, invert a
$(j-1)$-minor not in $I_j(A)$; for $j=1$ no inversion is needed.
Invertible row and column operations replace $A$ by
$\diag(I_{j-1},A')$. At the prime $I_j(A)$, the entries of $A'$
generate its extension. A $k$-minor must use at least $k-j+1$
entries from $A'$. This proves the inclusion after localization
at $I_j(A)$, and contraction proves the symbolic inclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Weighted determinantal primary flag]
\label{thm:weighted-primary}
Let $A$ be a generic $q$ by $p$ matrix with $p\ge q\ge1$,
let $R=\C[A,t]$, and set
\[
 F=t\sum_{i=0}^q t^{q-i}I_i(A),\qquad P_j=(t,I_j(A)).
\]
Then an irredundant primary decomposition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-primary}
 F=(t)\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^q P_j^{(q-j+2)}.
\end{equation}
In particular
\[
 \operatorname{Ass}_R(R/F)=\{(t),P_1,\ldots,P_q\},\qquad
 \sqrt F=(t),\qquad
 \codim P_j=1+(q-j+1)(p-j+1).
\]
The reduced divisor has generic multiplicity one. At a point of
$t=0$ where $A$ has rank $a$, the nilradical of the local failure
ring has exact index $q-a+1$. In particular its maximal index is
$q+1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every displayed component is primary: $P_j$ is prime and a symbolic
power is the contraction of a power of the maximal ideal of $R_{P_j}$.
Membership in $(t)$ requires $f_0=0$. For $u\ge1$ put $k=q+1-u$.
If $1\le k\le q$, Lemma~\ref{lem:coefficient-symbolic} says that
the condition supplied by the $j$th component is
\[
 f_u\in I_j(A)^{(k-j+1)}.
\]
The condition with $j=k$ is exactly $f_u\in I_k(A)$.
It implies every condition with $j<k$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:minor-symbolic-order}; those with $j>k$ are void.
For $u\ge q+1$ all these conditions are void. Consequently the
intersection has coefficient ideal $I_{q+1-u}(A)$ in degrees
$1\le u\le q$, coefficient ideal $(1)$ in degrees $u\ge q+1$,
and zero constant coefficient. These are exactly the coefficient
ideals of $F$, proving \eqref{eq:weighted-primary}.

Here are explicit irredundancy witnesses. Choose any nonzero
$(j-1)$-minor $d_{j-1}$, with $d_0=1$. The polynomial
\[
 g_j=t^{q-j+1}d_{j-1}^2
\]
does not belong to $P_j^{(q-j+2)}$, since $d_{j-1}$ is a unit
at $I_j(A)$. It belongs to $(t)$. For $h>j$ its $t$-order alone
is at least $q-h+2$. For $h<j$, its order at $P_h$ is at least
\[
 q-j+1+2(j-h)\ \ge\ q-h+2
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:minor-symbolic-order}. It therefore belongs to
all the other primary components. Finally, the square of a nonzero
$q$-minor belongs to every $P_j^{(q-j+2)}$, since
$2(q-j+1)\ge q-j+2$, but not to $(t)$. Thus no component can be
removed, and the standard associated-prime characterization of an
irredundant primary decomposition gives the asserted set.
The codimension formula is the usual rank-matrix dimension count:
rank $j-1$ matrices have dimension $(j-1)(q+p-j+1)$.

At a rank-$a$ point invert an $a$-minor and perform block elimination.
The ideal generated by $A$ and $tI_q$ has a free rank-$a$ image;
a presentation of its cokernel is $[A',tI_{q-a}]$, with $A'$ the
$(q-a)$ by $(p-a)$ Schur complement. Since
\[
 I_q([A,tI_q])=\sum_{i=0}^q t^{q-i}I_i(A),
\]
the local failure ideal becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-local-corank}
 t\sum_{i=0}^{q-a}t^{q-a-i}I_i(A').
\end{equation}
All entries of $A'$ vanish at the point. Its coefficient ring is a
localized polynomial ring independent of $t$. Formula
\eqref{eq:weighted-local-corank} contains $t^{q-a+1}$ but not
$t^{q-a}$ when $q-a>0$: the latter membership would require
$1\in I_1(A')$. The same nonmembership persists in the local ring
at the point, since a denominator not vanishing there has invertible
constant term modulo $I_1(A')$. For $a=q$ the local ideal is $(t)$
and the nilradical is zero, whose index we take to be one.
This proves both the exact local indices and generic multiplicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is and is not specialized]
\label{rem:weighted-specialization}
The ideal $tI_q([A,tI_q])$ and its coefficient formula can be
substituted in any coefficient ring. The primary decomposition
\eqref{eq:weighted-primary} uses independent matrix entries and is
not asserted to remain a primary decomposition under an arbitrary
substitution. Polynomial extension and localization do preserve the
argument. The geometric application below is first made on the
universal tensor space, where independence holds; the elliptic
specialization is proved separately by local parameters.
\end{remark}

\section{Universal quadratic flags and their resolutions}
\label{sec:universal-flags}
Fix $e\ge2$, $b=e-1$, $p=\binom e2$, and $1\le q\le p$.
Let $V,S$ be fixed vector spaces of dimensions $e,q$, and let
\[
 \Gamma=\operatorname{Surj}(\Sym^2V,S),\qquad
 \mathscr X=\Gamma\times\Gr(e,V\oplus S).
\]
The algebra $\mathscr B=\mathcal O\oplus V\oplus S$ over $\Gamma$
has the universal quadratic multiplication $\gamma$ and
$S(V\oplus S)=0$. Write $\mathscr K$ for the tautological bundle
and $D$ for the failure scheme of $\mathcal O\oplus\mathscr K$
in any degree $m\ge2$.

Delete the locus $\rank(\mathscr K\to V)\le e-2$.
On its determinant divisor $\Delta$ there are bundles
\[
 H=\operatorname{im}(\mathscr K\to V),\qquad
 \Lambda=\mathscr K\cap S,
 \qquad \rank H=b,\quad\rank\Lambda=1.
\]
The subspace $HV\subset\Sym^2V$ is generated by products with one
factor in $H$. Delete also the closed subset of this divisor on which
$\gamma(HV)\ne S$. Denote the resulting open scheme by $\mathscr U$,
and continue to write $D,\Delta$ for their restrictions to it.
For $1\le j\le q$, define the closed subscheme
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-Zj}
 Z_j=\{(\gamma,K)\in\Delta:
             \rank(\gamma|_{\Sym^2H})<j\}
\end{equation}
using the $j$-minor ideal on $\Delta$. As a subscheme of $\mathscr U$
its ideal includes $\mathcal I_\Delta$.

\begin{theorem}[Universal first-order-spanning primary classification]
\label{thm:universal-primary-flag}
Put $j_0=\max\{1,q-b+1\}$. The schemes $Z_j$ with $j<j_0$
are empty; each $Z_j$ with $j_0\le j\le q$ is nonempty, integral
and normal. On the whole of $\mathscr U$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-primary-flag}
 \mathcal I_D=\mathcal I_\Delta\cap
       \bigcap_{j=j_0}^q\mathcal I_{Z_j}^{(q-j+2)}
\end{equation}
is an irredundant primary decomposition. Its associated points are
exactly the generic point of $\Delta$ and the generic points of
these $Z_j$. In particular there are no other associated supports
on $\mathscr U$.

The divisor $\Delta$ is smooth, and its generic multiplicity in $D$
is one. At a point of $\Delta$ where
$\rank(\gamma|_{\Sym^2H})=a$, the nilradical has exact index
$q-a+1$. Its maximal index on $\mathscr U$ is $\min\{q,b\}+1$.
The codimension of $Z_j$ in $\mathscr U$ is
$1+(q-j+1)(p-j+1)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work on a frame chart of $\mathscr K$. Write its inclusion as a
stacked matrix with upper block $M:\mathscr K\to V$.
The block-minor argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:loewy-factorization}
gives, over the universal coefficient ring,
\[
 \mathcal I_D=(\det M)I_q(\gamma\Sym^2M).
\]
Indeed all products of degree at least three in $K$ vanish, and
the image is $\mathcal O+K+K^2$. Every maximal minor of the full
presentation must use all $e$ linear columns. This supplies the
factor $\det M$ before any quadratic minor is taken.

Near a corank-one point, invert a $b$-minor of $M$. Block elimination
and invertible changes of the source and target bases give
$M=\diag(I_b,t)$, with $t$ a local equation for $\Delta$.
With respect to $V=H\oplus\langle v\rangle$, let $A,B,c$
be the blocks of $\gamma$ on $\Sym^2H,Hv,v^2$. They have sizes
$q\times p$, $q\times b$, and $q\times1$. Up to harmless nonzero
scalar factors in the symmetric basis,
\[
 \gamma\Sym^2M=[A,tB,t^2c].
\]
The condition on $\mathscr U$ says that $[A,B]$ is surjective
at $t=0$. Shrink the neighborhood so that it has a right inverse.
This gives an equality of column modules over the local coefficient
ring:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-module-reduction}
 \operatorname{im}[A,tB,t^2c]
        =\operatorname{im}[A,tI_q].
\end{equation}
For the nontrivial inclusion, write $I_q=AU+BV$ using the right
inverse and multiply by $t$. The reverse inclusion follows because
$tB$ and $t^2c$ are generated by $tI_q$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-weighted-normal}
 \mathcal I_D=t\sum_{i=0}^q t^{q-i}I_i(A).
\end{equation}

The entries of $A,B,c$ are independent coordinates of the universal
tensor space after the indicated invertible changes of basis; the
other frame coordinates add polynomial variables and localizations.
Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-primary} therefore applies. Its prime
$(t,I_j(A))$ is exactly the intrinsic ideal in
\eqref{eq:universal-Zj}. On the spanning open, a rank-$(j-1)$
choice of $A$ is possible exactly when $q-j+1\le b$.
It is obtained by completing its image with the $b$ columns of $B$.
Hence exactly the primes indexed by $j\ge j_0$ survive.
Their generic strata also meet the full-frame open: prescribe
$M=\diag(I_b,0)$ and give its last column a nonzero $S$ component.
The witnesses in Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-primary}, or its
local rank-eliminated form at each surviving generic point, show
that the surviving decomposition is irredundant.

For completeness, the global geometry of each support can be read
without a frame. The divisor of corank-one planes is the total space
of $\operatorname{Hom}(H,S/\Lambda)$ over
$\Gr(b,V)\times\Pj(S)$: a plane with projection $H$ and kernel
$\Lambda$ is exactly the inverse graph of a map $H\to S/\Lambda$.
Over this smooth base the restriction map from all tensors $\gamma$
to $\operatorname{Hom}(\Sym^2H,S)$ is a surjective vector-bundle map.
Thus $Z_j$ is, locally, a generic determinantal scheme times an
affine space, restricted to the nonempty spanning open. The
determinantal rank stratum is irreducible; the vector-bundle
description shows that these local models form an integral global
scheme. Lemma~\ref{lem:minor-symbolic-order} gives its normality.

Symbolic powers localize, so the local intersections glue.
Alternatively, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:frame-primary-descent} to the
intrinsic ideals just defined. It also identifies the associated
points on $\mathscr U$, rather than only on its frames.
Off $\Delta$, $M$ is invertible, so all ideals in the decomposition
are unit ideals and $D$ is empty. The local nilpotency calculation
is Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-primary}. Since
$a\ge\max\{0,q-b\}$ on the spanning locus and that lower rank
occurs, its maximal index is as stated. The same local models give
the codimension and generic multiplicity assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Resolutions of the embedded supports]
\label{thm:flag-resolution}
For $j_0\le j\le q$, let $\widetilde Z_j$ parametrize triples
$((\gamma,K),T)$ with $(\gamma,K)\in\Delta$ and
\[
 T\in\Gr(j-1,S),\qquad \gamma(\Sym^2H)\subset T.
\]
Then $\widetilde Z_j$ is smooth and integral, and the forgetful
map $\rho_j:\widetilde Z_j\to Z_j$ is a projective birational
resolution. At a point where $\gamma|_{\Sym^2H}$ has rank $r$,
its scheme-theoretic fibre is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-resolution-fibre}
 \Gr(j-1-r,S/\gamma(\Sym^2H))
            \simeq\Gr(j-1-r,q-r).
\end{equation}
These constructions are equivariant for changes of bases in $V,S$
and for frame changes of $K$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with
$\Gr(b,V)\times\Pj(S)\times\Gr(j-1,S)$, carrying the bundles
$H,\Lambda,T$. The condition
$\gamma(\Sym^2H)\subset T$ is the kernel of the surjective
bundle map
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(\Sym^2V,S)
       \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\Sym^2H,S/T).
\]
Consequently its solution space is a vector bundle. Adjoin the
independent bundle $\operatorname{Hom}(H,S/\Lambda)$ recording $K$,
and restrict to $\gamma(HV)=S$. This is precisely
$\widetilde Z_j$. It is a nonempty open of a vector bundle over an
integral smooth base, hence is smooth and integral. The projection
is projective, since it is a closed incidence in the relative
Grassmannian over $\Delta$ restricted to $Z_j$. It is surjective
and has one-point fibre when the image of $\Sym^2H$ has dimension
$j-1$; over that open the inverse sends a matrix to its image
subbundle and is a morphism. This proves birationality. Over a
point of rank $r$, choosing $T$ is exactly choosing a
$(j-1-r)$-subspace of the quotient by that image; the same statement
for test schemes proves the fibre formula with its scheme structure.
All defining maps are intrinsic, proving equivariance.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Conductor and restriction flags]
\label{prop:universal-conductor-flag}
On the corank-one locus of any fixed $B_\gamma$, put
$W=\C\oplus K$, $H=\operatorname{im}(K\to V)$ and
$\Lambda=K\cap S$. Then $W$ is a subalgebra exactly when
$\gamma(\Sym^2H)\subset\Lambda$. The largest ideal of $B_\gamma$
contained in $W$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-corank-one-conductor}
 \operatorname{cond}_{B_\gamma}(W)
   =\ker\left[K\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(V,S/\Lambda)\right],
 \qquad k\longmapsto\bigl(v\mapsto\gamma(\bar k,v)\bigr).
\end{equation}
On geometric fibres its dimension is
$1+\dim\{h\in H:\gamma(h,V)\subset\Lambda\}$.
The kernel commutes with base change on every exact-rank stratum
of the displayed map. Thus the restriction-rank flag defining the
primary supports and the conductor flag are computed by two specified
maps of the same quadratic tensor; neither is replaced by a numerical
conductor dimension.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The multiplication image is $\C+K+\gamma(\Sym^2H)$, proving
the closure assertion. A conductor element with nonzero constant
coefficient would be a unit of the local algebra and would force
$B_\gamma\subset W$, which is impossible. Over coefficient rings
the same exclusion follows when $q>1$ by multiplying with the
locally free quotient $S/\Lambda$; when $q=1$, multiply with $V/H$,
which is a free line and receives the scalar coefficient of
multiplication by a lift of $V/H$. For $k\in K$, multiplication
by $S$ is zero and multiplication by a lift of $v$ is
$\gamma(\bar k,v)$. Such a product is in $K$ exactly when it is
in $\Lambda$. This proves the kernel formula, including over
coefficient rings. Finally $K\to H$ has kernel $\Lambda$, and
on a scheme-theoretic exact-rank locus the indicated matrix has
locally split kernel and image. The dimension and base-change
assertions follow.
\end{proof}
