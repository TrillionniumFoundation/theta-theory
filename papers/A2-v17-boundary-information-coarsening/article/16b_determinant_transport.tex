\begin{corollary}[Physical determinant and scalar product]
\label{cor:v15-determinant-product}
For the physical return $R_b=\varphi_{1-b}\circ\varphi_b$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-intrinsic-density}, put
$h_b(u)=\log(R_b'(u)/\lambda)$. The physical edge product $U_b$,
Green operator $G^{(b)}$ and Hessian perturbation $\Delta H_b$ of
\eqref{eq:v4-half-product}--\eqref{eq:v4-half-amplitude} satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-physical-product}
 \frac{\exp U_b(u)}{\det(I+G^{(b)}\Delta H_b(u))}
       =B_b(u)
       =\exp\!\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}h_b(R_b^i(u))\right\}.
\end{equation}
All expressions have the same value $1$ at zero, and the series
converges in every fixed mixed norm on the common collar. More
precisely, the finite scalar product has the exact remainder
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v15-physical-product-remainder}
 \log B_b(u)-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}h_b(R_b^i(u))
                           =\log B_b(R_b^n(u)).
\end{equation}
For each fixed $k$ and each $\sigma\in(\lambda_+,1)$ the right
side has $C^k$ norm at most $C_{k,\sigma}\sigma^n$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first equality is the original relative Fredholm determinant
construction, with its convergent trace series. Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-intrinsic-density}
identifies its positive density by exact physical concatenation.
Composing its two first-flight cocycles gives
$B_b(R_b(u))R_b'(u)=\lambda B_b(u)$.
Let $\mathfrak B_b$ be the scalar density in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v15-scalar-product}. The positive quotient
$B_b/\mathfrak B_b$ is invariant under $R_b$ and equals one at
zero. Iteration to the contracting fixed point makes the quotient
identically one. This proves \eqref{eq:v15-physical-product}.
Telescoping the logarithmic cocycle proves
\eqref{eq:v15-physical-product-remainder} for every finite $n$.
For the mixed norm bound use
$R_b^n=\zeta_b^{-1}(\lambda^n\zeta_b)$ and write
$\log B_b(\zeta_b^{-1}(x))=xC_b(x)$ with $C_b$ jointly smooth.
The remainder is then $\lambda^n\zeta_b(u)C_b(\lambda^n\zeta_b(u))$.
The same differentiated estimate as in
\eqref{eq:v15-scalar-iterates} proves the stated bound.
\end{proof}

In particular, the infinite scalar product is not introduced as a new
linearization theorem. Equation~\eqref{eq:v15-physical-product} is
an identification of two normalized constructions for the same physical
branch. The finite-bridge trace-norm comparison, the physical reference
twist, the first-hit selection and the residual-time integration still
have their separate proofs. The scalar product does not replace them.
