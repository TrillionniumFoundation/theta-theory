\subsection{Equivariant reconstruction of perturbed densities}
\label{subsec:v29-equivariant-density}

The inverse on the exact factorized family is independent of its anchor.
An extension to nearby densities is not.  We now specify an extension which
commutes with a change of the common transverse orientation, including away
from the factorized family.  The magnitude of the anchor is fixed once; its
sign is part of the coordinate convention, not an additional observation.

Write \(\varsigma(u)=-u\) and \(f^r(u,v)=f(-u,-v)\).  For each nonzero
\(\alpha\in I\), make the anchor in
\eqref{eq:v26-ratio-anchor}--\eqref{eq:v26-explicit-inverse} explicit:
\begin{align}
 q_\alpha(f)&=\sqrt{1-R_f(\alpha,\alpha)},&
 T_\alpha(f)(u)&=\frac{1-R_f(u,\alpha)}{q_\alpha(f)},
                                      \label{eq:v29-anchor-formulas}\\
 S_{d,\alpha}(f)&=\frac{dT_\alpha(f)}{1+T_\alpha(f)},&
 B_\alpha(f)(u)&=\frac{f(u,0)}{f(0,0)}\{1+T_\alpha(f)(u)\}.
                                      \label{eq:v29-action-amplitude}
\end{align}
For \(M\ge0\), let \(\mathcal U^M_\alpha\) be the set of real \(C^M\)
functions on a neighborhood of \(I^2\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v29-formula-domain}
 \inf_{I^2}f>0,\qquad 1-R_f(\alpha,\alpha)>0,\qquad
                  \inf_I\{1+T_\alpha(f)\}>0.
\end{equation}
All these conditions are open in the restricted \(C^M\) norm.  Neither
factorization nor a mass normalization is required.  Fix \(0<a<r\).  In
orientation sector \(\epsilon\in\{+1,-1\}\) use \(\alpha=\epsilon a\)
and set
\[
 \mathcal F_{d,a}(\epsilon,f)
       =\bigl(\epsilon,S_{d,\epsilon a}(f),B_{\epsilon a}(f)\bigr).
\]
The common reversal acts by \((\epsilon,f)\mapsto(-\epsilon,f^r)\), and on
the output by changing the tag and precomposing both functions with
\(\varsigma\).  For a channel collection the same tag is used for every
contact and edge; fixed positive anchor magnitudes may differ between edges.

\begin{proposition}[Transported-anchor equivariance and stability]
\label{prop:v29-transported-anchor}
Reflection is an isometric bijection
\(\mathcal U^M_\alpha\to\mathcal U^M_{-\alpha}\), and on this entire domain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v29-exact-off-model-equivariance}
 \begin{split}
 T_{-\alpha}(f^r)&=T_\alpha(f)\circ\varsigma,\\
 S_{d,-\alpha}(f^r)&=S_{d,\alpha}(f)\circ\varsigma,\qquad
 B_{-\alpha}(f^r)=B_\alpha(f)\circ\varsigma.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
In particular \(\mathcal F_{d,a}\) is equivariant, also for nonfactorized
perturbations.  On sets on which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v29-uniform-domain}
 \inf f\ge m>0,\quad \|f\|_{C^M}\le K,\quad q_{\epsilon a}(f)\ge q_*>0,
 \quad \inf(1+T_{\epsilon a}(f))\ge\nu>0,
\end{equation}
it is Lipschitz in each sector with one constant
\(C_M=C(M,I,a,d,m,K,q_*,\nu)\), the same in the two sectors.  The estimate
holds for every pair of functions satisfying these bounds, not only for a
perturbation compared with an exact model member.

Every compact exact-model family satisfying the hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v26-density-stability}, together with its reflected
copy, has a neighborhood of this form when the anchor is transported.
On the exact family the output is \((\epsilon,S,B/B(0))\), independently
of the admissible anchor magnitude.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitution in the four-density ratio gives
\[
 R_{f^r}(u,v)=R_f(-u,-v),\qquad q_{-\alpha}(f^r)=q_\alpha(f).
\]
It follows that \(T_{-\alpha}(f^r)(u)=T_\alpha(f)(-u)\).  Substituting in
\eqref{eq:v29-action-amplitude}, including the density ratio on the axis,
proves all of \eqref{eq:v29-exact-off-model-equivariance}.  Each strict
inequality in \eqref{eq:v29-formula-domain} is preserved.  Reflection is an
involution and preserves the supremum of every partial derivative of order
at most \(M\), proving the domain and isometry assertions.

For completeness, all uniform estimates can be proved between the two
endpoints, without requiring a segment to remain in the formula domain.
Restriction to the axis or to a fixed slice has operator norm at most one
for the usual maximum-derivative norm.  Leibniz's rule bounds products in
\(C^M\).  If \(h\ge m\), differentiating \(h h^{-1}=1\) bounds the
\(C^M\) norm of \(h^{-1}\) in terms of \(m^{-1}\) and \(\|h\|_{C^M}\).
For two such functions, the identity
\(h^{-1}-k^{-1}=(k-h)h^{-1}k^{-1}\) gives the corresponding difference
bound.  Applying these estimates to \(R_f\) gives
\[
 \|R_f-R_g\|_{C^M(I^2)}\le C\|f-g\|_{C^M(I^2)}.
\]
At the chosen anchor,
\[
 |q_\alpha(f)-q_\alpha(g)|
 =\frac{|R_f(\alpha,\alpha)-R_g(\alpha,\alpha)|}
             {q_\alpha(f)+q_\alpha(g)}
 \le \frac{C}{2q_*}\|f-g\|_{C^M(I^2)}.
\]
The lower bound on \(q_\alpha\) now gives a difference bound for \(T_\alpha\).
Its \(C^M\) norm is uniformly bounded by the same estimates.  The reciprocal
bound using \(\nu\), followed by the product bound, proves the claims for
\(S_{d,\alpha}\) and \(B_\alpha\).  Reflection preserves every bound used,
so the constants agree in the two sectors.

For the last assertion, on an exact model \(1+T_\alpha\ge1\) and
\(q_\alpha=S(\alpha)/(d-S(\alpha))>0\).  The compact lower bounds give a
common neighborhood with positive density denominators, anchor radical and
\(1+T_\alpha\).  Its reflected copy has exactly the same bounds.  Exact
recovery and anchor independence follow from
\eqref{eq:v26-rank-one-defect}.  No differentiated square root at the action
minimum is taken.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Orbit stability and finite-order contact reconstruction]
\label{cor:v29-density-orbit-stability}
The transported-anchor action and amplitude reconstruction descends to the
common-orientation quotient with the same fixed-order Lipschitz constant.
On a compact positive contact class, the finite-jet reconstruction of
Corollary~\ref{cor:v26-finite-flight-inverse}, with this anchor convention,
is also equivariant.  Its quotient error bound through any fixed order
\(M\ge2\) remains \(C_M(\tau^j+\varepsilon+\varepsilon_g)\).
Here \(\varepsilon\) is the interior \(C^M\) density error and
\(\varepsilon_g\) is the gap error, as in that corollary.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For tagged representatives \((\epsilon,f)\), \((\delta,g)\), let
\(\eta\in\{0,1\}\) be the unique value with
\(\epsilon=(-1)^\eta\delta\).  Define their orbit distance by
\[
 \bar d_M([\epsilon,f],[\delta,g])
                  =\|f-g^{r^\eta}\|_{C^M(I^2)}.
\]
It is independent of the chosen representatives because reflection is an
isometry.  Define the output distance by the sum of the two corresponding
function norms, using the same tag alignment.  Equivariance and the
same-sector estimate in Proposition~\ref{prop:v29-transported-anchor} prove
the quotient estimate with the same constant.  For finitely many contact
laws use the corresponding sum or maximum product norm throughout.

The anchored projection
\(Ph(u)=h(u)-h(0)-u h'(0)\) is bounded on \(C^M(I)\) and satisfies
\(P(h\circ\varsigma)=(Ph)\circ\varsigma\).  Reflection fixes the gap and
both quadratic action coefficients.  It therefore fixes the leading
curvatures and \(\gamma\) in \eqref{eq:v22-leading-recovery}.  At degree
\(n\), it multiplies both graph and action coefficients by \((-1)^n\).
The finite smooth-jet factorization and reflection of the half-line action
show that the lower-jet remainder obeys
\[
 R_n\bigl(((-1)^m q_m)_{2\le m<n}\bigr)
                       =(-1)^n R_n\bigl((q_m)_{2\le m<n}\bigr).
\]
One may verify this identity on smooth polynomial representatives; finite
smooth-jet factorization makes it independent of their cutoffs and
remainders.  The block \(M_n\) depends only on the reflection-invariant
quadratic geometry.  Thus the recursion
\(q_n=M_n^{-1}(s_n-R_n)\) commutes with reflection, by induction in \(n\).
Its finitely many operations are smooth on the compact positive set and on
a sufficiently small neighborhood of it.  Combining their Lipschitz bounds
with the density estimate and the finite-flight estimate proves the stated
error bound.  The alignment argument above then gives the same bound on
orientation orbits.
\end{proof}

The extension just constructed is an action, amplitude and finite-jet
reconstruction on its specified neighborhood.  An arbitrary perturbed
density is not asserted to be the exact law of a periodic billiard, and its
output is not asserted to admit analytic continuation or global incidence
gluing.  The exact global classification still uses the exact law image.
All constants remain fixed-order constants.

\begin{remark}[Why a fixed signed anchor cannot be used in both sectors]
\label{rem:v29-fixed-anchor-witness}
For a fixed signed anchor the identity is instead
\(S_{d,a}(f^r)=S_{d,-a}(f)\circ\varsigma\).  This cannot in general be
replaced by \(S_{d,a}(f)\circ\varsigma\).  For example, take \(d=1\) and
\[
 f_\zeta(u,v)=\frac2\pi(1-u^2-v^2)_+
                       \{1+\zeta uv(u+v)\},\qquad 0<\zeta\le1/100.
\]
This is a probability density on the unit disk: its perturbation integrates
to zero under simultaneous reversal, and its multiplier is positive.  On
\([-1/3,1/3]^2\) it is a positive smooth perturbation of the exact density
with \(S(u)=u^2\).  Set \(a=1/4\), \(D=1/225\), and
\(h=2\zeta a^3(224/225)\).  Direct substitution yields
\[
 T_a(f_\zeta^r)(a)^2=D+h,\qquad
 T_a(f_\zeta)(-a)^2=\frac{D^2}{D-h},\qquad
 (D+h)-\frac{D^2}{D-h}=-\frac{h^2}{D-h}\ne0.
\]
Both unsquared quantities are positive.  Hence the action outputs differ
as well.  At \(\zeta=1/100\) the last difference is
\(-49/2092500\).  This is a witness concerning an off-model formula, not
physical-table nonidentifiability.  Transporting the anchor restores the
identity exactly, without removing the perturbation neighborhood.
\end{remark}
