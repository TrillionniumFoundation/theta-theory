This record separates inspected statements, inherited comparisons and unavailable text. It is not an exhaustive priority certificate. The new exact fibre theorem is proved in the manuscript, not inferred from failure to find a matching title.

## Formal patching: the precise theorem used

B. Bhatt, *Algebraization and Tannaka duality*, arXiv:1404.7483.
Primary PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.7483

Theorem 1.4, Proposition 5.6, Example 5.8 and Lemma 5.12(2) were inspected, including rendered PDF pages 2, 21 and 22. Theorem 1.4 gives derived formal gluing under a derived-isomorphism condition on the closed support. The manuscript uses the more specific Proposition 5.6(4) and Example 5.8: a flat Noetherian completion gives gluing of quasi-coherent sheaves, including torsion. The proof retains quotient structure maps and punctured identifications. It does not apply the unrestricted module assertion for a nonflat completion, which the source explicitly warns against. Existence of patching is not attributed to faithfulness of completion alone.

## Connectedness and the completed local criterion

The Stacks Project, Lemma 37.53.6, Section 37.53 (Stein factorization):
https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03GX

This result was checked with its hypotheses: a proper morphism to an integral normal base, reduced source, domination by generic points and the generic H^0 condition. A proper birational map of integral varieties in our setting satisfies them. It is the load-bearing connectedness input in the open-and-closed exhaustion of the full fibre. Connectedness is not inferred from a diagram of two selected families.

The Stacks Project, Lemma 41.11.3, Section 41.11:
https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0257

The completed local étaleness criterion was inspected. The manuscript specifies Noetherian local rings, finite presentation at the geometric application and residue field C, and applies it to an already constructed algebraic morphism. It does not assert algebraization of a formal family without such a morphism.

## Stable quotients

A. Marian, D. Oprea and R. Pandharipande, *The moduli space of stable quotients*, Geometry & Topology 15 (2011), 1651–1706, arXiv:0904.2992.
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/0904.2992v3

Definition and stability discussion in Section 2 and Theorem 1 were inspected. Theorem 1 concerns a proper separated finite-type Deligne–Mumford stack of stable quotients on nodal curves. Those objects retain quotient sheaves and stability conditions. Our Hilbert fibre retains an embedded subscheme and the coefficient-base point instead; its nonreduced tail and nilpotent parameter scheme are not deduced from that moduli theorem. No new general stable-quotient compactification is claimed.

## Parametrized quasimaps and graph spaces

I. Ciocan-Fontanine, B. Kim and D. Maulik, *Stable quasimaps to GIT quotients*, arXiv:1106.3724.
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1106.3724v2

The introduction, Definition 7.2.1 and Theorem 7.2.2 were inspected. The parametrized-component construction includes a morphism to a fixed curve, a principal bundle and a section, with stability. The theorem gives proper finite-type Deligne–Mumford moduli with an obstruction theory under its GIT hypotheses. It is a relevant comparison for graph compactifications, but the current paper does not identify its embedded Hilbert graph with that quasimap space or derive virtual-class results from the fibre calculation.

## Stable map-quotient comparison

C. Manolache, *Stable maps and stable quotients*, arXiv:1301.4393.
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1301.4393v1

The introduction and Section 1.1–1.2 were inspected. They describe an auxiliary moduli space dominating stable maps and stable quotients and its virtual-class comparison. The comparison is useful for distinguishing boundary objects; it is not an antecedent proof of the specific Hilbert–Burch coordinate equations `(eA,eB,e^2C)` used here. No exhaustive exclusion of equivalent formulations elsewhere is inferred.

## Determinantal low-degree Hilbert geometry

M. Lahoz, M. Lehn, E. Macrì and P. Stellari, *Generalized twisted cubics on a cubic fourfold as a moduli space of stable objects*, arXiv:1609.04573.
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1609.04573v3

Sections 1.1–1.2 were inspected, including the discussion of arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay and non-Cohen–Macaulay curves and the 3 by 2 minor presentation. This source recalls earlier twisted-cubic constructions and makes their attribution explicit. The manuscript therefore does not present Hilbert–Burch methods or determinantal Hilbert parameterizations as new. Its new assertion is the exact retained-coefficient graph chart, the complete fibre identification and its nilpotent structure. A fresh full reading of Piene–Schlessinger's original paper was not obtained, and is not claimed on the strength of this later exposition.
