\section{Fixed tensors across projection corank two}
\label{sec:coranktwo}
We now work with $e=4$ and $p=6$. All calculations below concern a
fixed quotient tensor $\gamma_R$, while its generating plane varies.
They are identities of ideals, before taking radicals.

\begin{definition}\label{def:good-pair}
A pair $(R,H)$, where $R\in G_4^\circ$ and $H\subset V$ is a
plane, is called admissible for the corank-two calculation if the
following conditions hold. The map
$A_H=\gamma_R|_{\Sym^2H}$ is injective. For a complement $W$ to $H$,
put $Q_H=S_R/\im A_H$ and let
\[
 B_H:H\otimes W\longrightarrow Q_H,\qquad
 C_H:\Sym^2W\longrightarrow Q_H
\]
be induced by multiplication. The map $B_H$ is surjective and its
one-dimensional kernel is generated by a rank-two tensor in
$H\otimes W$. Finally, the line of hyperplanes containing $H$
meets $Y_R$ in four distinct points.
\end{definition}
The first-order map $B_H$ is intrinsically a map from $H\otimes(V/H)$:
changing a lift of a vector in $V/H$ adds an element of $\Sym^2H$,
which is zero in $Q_H$. Thus these conditions are independent of the
chosen complement. The last condition is transverse intersection with
a projective line, not smoothness of a parameter-selected polynomial
without a geometric interpretation.

\begin{lemma}[Nonemptiness of the corank-two open]\label{lem:good-open}
The admissible pairs form a nonempty open subset of
$G_4^\circ\times\Gr(2,V)$. Its projection is a nonempty invariant
open $G_4^\dagger$. For each $R$ in that image, the admissible $H$
form a dense open subset of $\Gr(2,V)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Injectivity and surjectivity are open rank conditions. After taking the
line kernel, tensor rank two is the nonvanishing of its determinant.
On this locus the pencil restriction of the determinant is a binary
quartic; nonzero discriminant is open. These conditions are invariant.
Projection from a product over $\C$ is open, and $\Gr(2,V)$ is
irreducible, giving the density claim once nonemptiness is proved.

For $C_*$ in \eqref{eq:C-star}, take $H=\langle x_0,x_1\rangle$ and
$W=\langle x_2,x_3\rangle$. In the square-first, cross-second basis,
$\gamma=(-C_*\mid I_6)$. The three columns for $x_0^2,x_0x_1,x_1^2$
have rank three. After quotienting their image, the remaining maps are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BC-witness}
 B_H=\begin{pmatrix}-1/2&-1&1&0\\-1/2&-1&0&1\\-3/2&-2&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 C_H=\begin{pmatrix}-9/2&0&-1/2\\-5/2&0&1/2\\-13/2&1&7/2\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The four columns of $B_H$ correspond to
$x_0x_2,x_0x_3,x_1x_2,x_1x_3$; the three of $C_H$ correspond to
$x_2^2,x_2x_3,x_3^2$. The kernel vector is $(-4,3,1,1)$, whose
$2\times2$ determinant is $-7$. For $u x_2+v x_3$, the final square
column is $C_H(u^2,2uv,v^2)^t$. The determinant of that column and
the two mixed columns is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h-witness}
 h(u,v)=\frac{u^4-8u^3v-u^2v^2-4uv^3-9v^4}{2},\qquad
 \disc h(u,1)=-\frac{632301}{4}\ne0.
\end{equation}
Its leading coefficient is nonzero, so there is no root at infinity.
This determinant is the restriction determinant on
$\Sym^2(H+\C(ux_2+vx_3))$ after eliminating $A_H$, hence defines the
pencil intersection with $Y_R$. Lemma~\ref{lem:differential} places
this witness in $G_4^\circ$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The residual matrix]\label{lem:residual-matrix}
Near a plane $K$ whose projection has admissible rank-two image $H$,
there are smooth auxiliary parameters and four transverse coordinates
$a,b,c,d$ in which $\II_{\widehat D_R}=\delta J$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-matrix}
 \begin{split}
 T&=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\quad\delta=ad-bc,
 \quad\mathfrak m=(a,b,c,d),\\
 L(T)&=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&0&0\\c&d&a&b\\0&0&c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}a^2&ab&b^2\\2ac&ad+bc&2bd\\c^2&cd&d^2\end{pmatrix},\\
 J&=I_3\bigl[L(T),\ C\,T^{(2)}\bigr].
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $C$ is a $3\times3$ matrix depending only on the auxiliary
parameters, not on $a,b,c,d$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use Proposition~\ref{prop:full-fitting} and an invertible projection
two-minor to write $M=\diag(I_2,T)$. The basis of its first image
plane and a complement can be chosen independently of the Schur block
$T$: the Gaussian row operations depend on the first two columns, and
the remaining column operations remove their components from the last
two columns. This proves the asserted separation of parameters.

The quadratic columns divide into $\Sym^2H$, $H\otimes W$, and
$\Sym^2W$. Eliminate the injective rank-three $A_H$ block. The
residual matrix is $[B_H(I_H\otimes T),C_HT^{(2)}]$, and its maximal
minors generate the residual ideal: the eliminated identity block
removes a free direct summand of the cokernel. A rank-two generator
of $\ker B_H$ can be put in the form
$h_1\otimes w_2-h_2\otimes w_1$ by invertible changes of bases.
The quotient is then the symmetrization map from two two-dimensional
spaces, with the displayed matrix $L(T)$. These basis changes involve
only the auxiliary parameters. Finally $\det M$ is a unit times
$\delta$. This proves the ideal identity, not just its leading term.
\end{proof}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary-h}
 h(u_1,u_2)=\det\left[
 \begin{pmatrix}u_1\\u_2\\0\end{pmatrix},
 \begin{pmatrix}0\\u_1\\u_2\end{pmatrix},
 C\begin{pmatrix}u_1^2\\2u_1u_2\\u_2^2\end{pmatrix}\right].
\end{equation}
It is the binary quartic of the pencil intersection. After an \'etale
extension of the auxiliary coefficient ring, its four roots split as
distinct line subbundles $\ell_i\subset W$. Write $P_i$ for the
ideal of $\im T\subset\ell_i$; it is generated by the two entries
of the composite $\C^2\xrightarrow{T}W\to W/\ell_i$.

\begin{lemma}[A rank-one-cone computation]\label{lem:cone-ideal}
Under the simple-root hypothesis, in the local coefficient ring of
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-matrix} one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cone-ideal}
 I:=\bigcap_{i=1}^4P_i=(\delta,g_0,\ldots,g_4),\qquad
 J=I\cap\mathfrak m^3=\delta\mathfrak m+(g_0,\ldots,g_4).
\end{equation}
The degree-four polynomials $g_j$ can be chosen with restrictions
$h(u_1,u_2)v_1^{4-j}v_2^j$ on $T=u v^t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Give $a,b,c,d$ degree one and the auxiliary coefficients degree zero.
The coordinate ring of the rank-one cone is the diagonal subring
\begin{equation}\label{eq:segre-ring}
 A_0[a,b,c,d]/(\delta)
  \simeq\bigoplus_{r\ge0}
        \Sym^r(A_0^2)_u\otimes\Sym^r(A_0^2)_v,
 \qquad T=u v^t,
\end{equation}
where $A_0$ denotes the auxiliary ring. This follows directly from
$a=u_1v_1$, $b=u_1v_2$, $c=u_2v_1$, $d=u_2v_2$ and the single
relation $ad-bc$; the monomials on either side give the same basis.
In degree $r$, vanishing on the four distinct $u$-lines means divisibility
by $h$ in the $u$ variables. The resulting module is zero for $r<4$,
and for $r\ge4$ it is
\[
 h\,\Sym^{r-4}(A_0^2)_u\otimes\Sym^r(A_0^2)_v.
\]
The simple-root discriminant is a unit, so the elementary divisibility
and interpolation assertions used here hold over $A_0$ as well as
over its residue field. Multiplication of binary forms shows that this
ideal is generated in diagonal degree four by
$h v_1^{4-j}v_2^j$. Lifting those generators proves the first identity.

The four $3$-minors of the linear block $L(T)$ generate
$\delta\mathfrak m$, by direct expansion. Modulo $\delta$, its four
columns are
\[
 v_1B(h_1u),\quad v_2B(h_1u),\quad
 v_1B(h_2u),\quad v_2B(h_2u),
\]
and the three quadratic columns are
$v_1^2C(u^2)$, $v_1v_2C(u^2)$, and $v_2^2C(u^2)$.
Here $u^2$ has coefficient vector $(u_1^2,2u_1u_2,u_2^2)^t$.
A minor using one column from each of the two linear groups and one
quadratic column is $h$ times a degree-four monomial in $v$; these
span all five such monomials. Thus they furnish the lifts $g_j$.
A minor with at least two quadratic columns vanishes modulo $\delta$,
and its degree is at least five, so it already lies in
$\delta\mathfrak m$. All other minors have been accounted for.
This proves $J=\delta\mathfrak m+(g_j)$ exactly.

Finally $I$ is generated by its degree-two element $\delta$ and the
$g_j$ of degree four. Its intersection with $\mathfrak m^3$ deletes
only the scalar multiple of $\delta$ in degree two, proving the
second equality. Localizing in the auxiliary parameters or taking the
indicated \'etale extension preserves these explicit identities.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The corank-two primary structure]\label{thm:coranktwo-proof}
For an admissible pair the ideal $\delta J$ has the decomposition
\eqref{eq:main-coranktwo}. It is irredundant. In the local failure
ring its nilradical $\widehat\NN$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N-square}
 \widehat\NN^3=0,\qquad
 \widehat\NN^2\ne0,\qquad
 \Ann(\widehat\NN^2)=\mathfrak m/(\delta J).
\end{equation}
Consequently the rank-two stratum is intrinsic to this local scheme
as the annihilator support of $\widehat\NN^2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work on a connected regular \'etale auxiliary chart, so that its
coefficient ring is a domain. Each $P_i$ is a prime generated by two
independent linear coordinates. Its ordinary square is $P_i$-primary;
the same argument applies to $\mathfrak m^5$. The ideal $(\delta)$
is prime. At the generic point of $P_i$, the determinant has order
one, because the other row after choosing $\ell_i$ as a coordinate
line is not zero. Therefore
\[
 (P_i^2:\delta)=P_i,
 \qquad (\mathfrak m^5:\delta)=\mathfrak m^3.
\]
The second equality also follows immediately from the homogeneous
order two of $\delta$ and the polynomial associated graded ring of
$\mathfrak m$. Intersecting with $(\delta)$ and cancelling $\delta$
now gives
\[
 (\delta)\cap\bigcap_iP_i^2\cap\mathfrak m^5
 =\delta\left(\bigcap_iP_i\cap\mathfrak m^3\right)
 =\delta J
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:cone-ideal}.

At the generic point of the determinant divisor all the other ideals
are units, so $(\delta)$ is necessary. At the generic point of $P_i$
the other $P_j$ and $\mathfrak m$ become units, and the local ideal
is $\delta P_i=(\delta)\cap P_i^2$; its two components are necessary.
To see that $\mathfrak m^5$ is necessary, note that $\delta^2$ lies
in $(\delta)$ and in every $P_i^2$, but is not in $\delta J$:
$\delta\notin J$ because its degree is two. On the other hand
$\mathfrak m\delta^2\subset\delta J$ since
$\delta\mathfrak m\subset J$. This also proves that the annihilator
of the class of $\delta^2$ is exactly $\mathfrak m$; a nonzero
auxiliary coefficient cannot kill that degree-four class.

The radical of the failure ideal is $(\delta)$, so the nilradical is
generated by the class of $\delta$. Its square is the nonzero class
just considered and its cube vanishes, since
$\delta^2\in\delta\mathfrak m\subset J$. This proves
\eqref{eq:N-square}. Over the auxiliary ring the square is free of
rank one on the rank-two stratum. Intrinsically its transition functions
are those of $\II_{\widehat\Delta}^{\,2}$ restricted to that stratum;
thus it is $\OO_{\Sigma}(-2\widehat\Delta)$ on the admissible open
$\Sigma$. The generic transverse length is one.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-coranktwo}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:good-open} supplies the asserted nonempty open class.
Lemmas~\ref{lem:residual-matrix} and \ref{lem:cone-ideal} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:coranktwo-proof} supply the fixed-tensor calculation
on every indicated frame chart. The graph coordinates are smooth
auxiliary variables, so no transverse component has been discarded.
The individual $P_i$ are allowed to permute under monodromy; the
intersection and the intrinsic annihilator in \eqref{eq:N-square}
descend. Four local branches need not be four distinct global
irreducible components. This is why the primary decomposition was
stated \'etale locally rather than with globally labelled roots.
\end{proof}
