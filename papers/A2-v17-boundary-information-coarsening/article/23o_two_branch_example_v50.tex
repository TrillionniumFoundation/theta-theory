\subsection{A noncircular fiber with exactly two realizations}
\label{subsec:v50-two-branch}

Finite ambiguity in Theorem~\ref{thm:v49-finite-fiber} occurs inside
its noncircular class.  The following example was suggested in the
referee-style memorandum~\cite{A2ReviewV49}.  We give the all-lattice
clearance and completeness arguments, and then show that one additional
marked gap distinguishes the two branches.  This last observation is
specific to the example; it is not a uniform branch-selection assertion
for arbitrary noncircular tables.

\begin{proposition}[An exact two-table fiber]
\label{prop:v50-two-table}
Let $K$ have support function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v50-support}
 h(\theta)=\frac1{10}+\frac1{1000}\cos(4\theta),
 \qquad a=2^{-1/2},\qquad
 L_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&a\end{pmatrix},\quad
 L_-=\begin{pmatrix}1&-a\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The one-obstacle tables $T_\pm=(K,L_\pm)$ are admissible analytic
periodic dispersing tables.  For the two channels with fixed deck marks
$e_1,e_2$, they have the same gaps and the same two same-type signed
conditional endpoint laws at every common sufficiently small positive
offset.  The fiber of this marked datum, modulo one common proper
Euclidean motion, consists of exactly these two tables.  They have
different marked Gram forms, although the intrinsic data derivative
at either table is injective modulo common motion on the family class
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v48-infinitesimal}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The support is entire and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v50-convexity}
 h+h''=\frac1{10}-\frac{15}{1000}\cos(4\theta)
                       \ge\frac{17}{200}>0.
\end{equation}
It therefore defines a strictly convex analytic body with positive
curvature.  Its support center is zero, its proper rotation group is
exactly $C_4$, and
$K\subset\overline B(0,R)$ with $R=101/1000$.
Both lattice determinants equal $a>0$.  Their Gram eigenvalues are
$1-a,1+a$, so, for any nonzero integer mark $m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v50-separation}
 |L_\pm m|\ge\sqrt{1-a}\,|m|\ge\sqrt{1-a}>2R.
\end{equation}
This proves disjointness of every pair of distinct lifted closures,
not just of the selected pairs.

The two selected center displacements have length one.  In the first
channel their direction is $0$, while in the second it is $\pi/4$
for $T_+$ and $3\pi/4$ for $T_-$.  At all these normals $h'=0$.
The supporting points therefore lie on the corresponding center
segment, and the parallel supporting lines give the normal closest
pair.  Strict convexity makes this pair unique.  Since
$h(\theta+\pi)=h(\theta)$, the gaps are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v50-two-gaps}
 g_1=1-2\left(\frac1{10}+\frac1{1000}\right)=\frac{399}{500},
 \qquad
 g_2=1-2\left(\frac1{10}-\frac1{1000}\right)=\frac{401}{500}.
\end{equation}

We check clearance against the entire lattice.  Let $v_i=L_\pm e_i$.
For a lattice center not on the line $\mathbb Rv_i$, its distance
from that line is
$|\det(v_i,L_\pm m)|/|v_i|$, a nonzero integer multiple of $a$.
A lattice center on that line is an integer multiple of $v_i$;
unless it is one of the two incident centers, its distance from
$[0,v_i]$ is at least one.  Thus every third obstacle has distance at
least $a-R>3/5$ from the unit center segment.  The actual closest
segment is a subset of this segment.  Uniformly small contact gates
and a common positive-offset interval are consequently available in
both tables.

The first ordered obstacle pair agrees in the two tables.  Rotation
by $\pi/2$ preserves $K$ and takes $L_+e_2=(a,a)$ to
$L_-e_2=(-a,a)$.  Hence it identifies their second ordered pairs as
well.  These are proper motions, preserving the oriented signed
contact convention.  The local stationary actions and normalized
amplitudes, and therefore the two same-type conditional laws, agree
in each channel.  The overall phase-volume constant cancels under
conditioning; equality of the conditional laws does not assert equality
of the successful-preparation frequencies.

To prove that the fiber has no additional members, use the signed
smooth inverse and analytic continuation in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v49-finite-fiber}.  They fix the complete ordered
images in both channel frames for every realization of the datum.
Fix the first channel direction and the support center.  Its center
displacement is then $e_1$ and its centered body is $K$.  The four
possible relative $C_4$ rotations of the second pair give exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v50-four-candidates}
 v_2\in\{(a,a),(-a,a),(-a,-a),(a,-a)\}.
\end{equation}
Here the marked gain matrix is the identity, so the cochain uniquely
gives $L=(e_1\ v_2)$; no continuous placement variable is left.
The determinants of these four candidates are respectively
$a,a,-a,-a$.  Precisely the first two satisfy the positive-orientation
condition, and both have already passed separation and clearance.
This proves exact two-element completeness of the fiber.  The product
bound in \eqref{eq:v49-fiber-bound} is four in this example and is
strictly larger than the number of realizations.

Finally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v50-marked-grams}
 L_+^tL_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\a&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 L_-^tL_-=\begin{pmatrix}1&-a\\-a&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
A common Euclidean motion preserves a marked Gram form.  Changing
integer basis is not permitted when the deck marks are fixed, so the
two candidates are distinct in the announced quotient.  The body is
noncircular and the selected skeleton persists.  The differential
assertion follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:v48-infinitesimal} at each
candidate, with its actual local alignment branch.  It does not
identify the two distinct global branches.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A further marked channel separates the two branches}
\label{subsec:v50-separator}

\begin{proposition}[Separation by one additional gap]
\label{prop:v50-extra-gap}
For the two tables in Proposition~\ref{prop:v50-two-table}, the
channel with deck mark $e_1+e_2$ is clear in both tables.  Its closest
gaps satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v50-gap-separator}
             g_{+,e_1+e_2}>1>g_{-,e_1+e_2}.
\end{equation}
Thus the original two-channel datum together with this one additional
marked gap determines the table uniquely within its original exact
fiber.  Conditional endpoint laws on the additional channel are not
needed for this distinction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $w_\pm=L_\pm(e_1+e_2)$ and
\[
        \ell_+=|w_+|=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2},\qquad
        \ell_-=|w_-|=\sqrt{2-\sqrt2}.
\]
Since $K$ and $K+w_\pm$ lie in radius-$R$ disks about their
centers, their distance obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v50-gap-distance-bounds}
        \ell_\pm-2R\le g_{\pm,e_1+e_2}\le\ell_\pm+2R.
\end{equation}
The upper estimate needs no assumption that the closest points lie
on the center line.  Here
$\ell_+-101/500>1$ and $\ell_-+101/500<1$, which prove
\eqref{eq:v50-gap-separator}.

It remains to show that these closest segments are genuine clear
channels, rather than merely distances between two bodies.  For a
lattice center $L_\pm(m,n)$ off the line $\mathbb Rw_\pm$,
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(L_\pm(m,n),\mathbb Rw_\pm)
     =\frac{|\det(w_\pm,L_\pm(m,n))|}{\ell_\pm}
     =\frac{a|n-m|}{\ell_\pm}\ge\frac a{\ell_\pm}.
\]
A center on the line has $m=n$.  Excluding $0,w_\pm$, its distance
from $[0,w_\pm]$ is at least $\ell_\pm$.  Hence every third
center is separated from that center segment by at least
\[
 c_*:=\min\{\ell_-,\ell_+,a/\ell_-,a/\ell_+\}
                   =a/\ell_+>2R.
\]
For endpoints $x\in K$ and $y\in K+w_\pm$, each point of
$[x,y]$ is within $R$ of $[0,w_\pm]$: subtract the corresponding
convex combination of $0,w_\pm$ and use the radius bound at both
ends.  This applies in particular to the closest segment, which need
not be a subset of the center segment.  A third obstacle lies in the
radius-$R$ disk about its center.  Its distance from the closest
segment is therefore at least $c_*-2R>0$.  Positive curvature and
strict separation give the unique normal closest pair and allow
uniformly small gates.  The added channels are clear, completing
the proof.
\end{proof}

The two propositions separate three different statements.  The two
original function-valued channel observations have a genuinely
nontrivial finite exact fiber.  At each member the intrinsic derivative
has no non-Euclidean kernel.  A third, differently marked distance
observation resolves this particular discrete ambiguity.  Neither the
circular comparison nor these propositions assert a failure of the
noncircular finite-fiber theorem, a universal minimal channel count,
or a uniform statistical selector on the entire noncircular class.
