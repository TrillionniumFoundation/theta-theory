\section{The marked Hilbert graph}
\label{sec:chain-v171}
We give the chain construction in full, so the applications require no
unpublished predecessor theorem. Fix $a\geq2$, let
$S_a=\Spec k[b,c]$, and consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marked-map-v171}
 [s_0:t_0]\longmapsto[t_0^a:b s_0^a:cs_0t_0^{a-1}].
\end{equation}
Over $b\ne0$ this is a morphism, and its graph is an embedded curve in
$X_0=\Pj^1\times\Pj^2$. Its Hilbert polynomial with respect to
$\OO_{X_0}(1,1)$ is $P_a(l)=(a+1)l+1$. Define $T_a$ to be the
schematic closure of the resulting section in
$S_a\times\Hilb^{P_a}(X_0)$. This definition retains the coefficients;
it does not take a quotient by source or target automorphisms.

\begin{theorem}[The marked chain]
\label{thm:chain-v171}
Put $K_a=\prod_{j=1}^a(b,c^j)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain-blowup-v171}
 T_a=\operatorname{Bl}_{K_a}S_a.
\end{equation}
This is a smooth surface obtained by blowing up the origin and then
$a-1$ times the intersection of the newest exceptional curve with
the strict transform of $b=0$. Its exceptional divisors $E_i$ have
$v_{E_i}(b)=i$, $v_{E_i}(c)=1$ and form a chain of projective lines.
An open cover consists of the first and last charts
\[
 T^{(0)}=\Spec k[b,k_0],\quad c=bk_0;
 \qquad T^{(a)}=\Spec k[c,w_a],\quad b=c^aw_a,
\]
and the intermediate charts
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain-charts-v171}
 T^{(i)}=\Spec k[e_i,h_i],\qquad
 c=e_i h_i,\quad b=e_i^{i+1}h_i^i\quad(1\leq i<a).
\end{equation}
They are actual charts of the full blow-up, with adjacent transitions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain-overlaps-v171}
 \begin{gathered}
 k_0=e_1^{-1},\qquad b=e_1^2h_1,\\
 e_{i+1}=h_i^{-1},\qquad h_{i+1}=e_i h_i^2\quad(1\leq i<a-1),\\
 c=e_{a-1}h_{a-1},\qquad w_a=h_{a-1}^{-1}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All nonadjacent overlaps are obtained by composing these substitutions.
The universal embedded curve over $T_a$ is flat and retains the original
fixed target $X_0$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{A finite evaluation calculation}
Let $d=a+1$ and $m_a=a(a+1)/2+1$. Gotzmann's regularity theorem gives
a closed Grassmannian embedding of the projective Hilbert scheme in
degree $m\geq m_a$ \cite{Gotzmann171}. Indeed the Gotzmann
representation of $dl+1$ has $d$ linear binomial terms and
$(d-1)(d-2)/2$ constant terms, hence $m_a$ terms. We use the ordinary
Hilbert scheme after the Segre embedding. The fixed quadratic Segre
relations lie in every ideal; the relevant quotient therefore factors
through $H^0(X_0,\OO(m,m))$, without invoking multigraded regularity.

The restriction matrix $M$ has $q=(a+1)m+1$ rows and
$N=(m+1)\binom{m+2}{2}$ columns. The column indexed by
$0\leq l,h$, $l+h\leq m$, $0\leq t\leq m$ is the monomial
$b^hc^l$ in row
\[
 n=t+a(m-h-l)+(a-1)l,
\]
and is zero in every other row. It is the evaluation of a basis
monomial of bidegree $(m,m)$. Over $b\ne0$ it is surjective, since
restriction to the graph is $H^0(\Pj^1,\OO((a+1)m))$ in a regularity
degree. Its full minors are Pl\"ucker coordinates of the Hilbert point.

\begin{proposition}[Content and row factorization]
\label{prop:chain-factorization-v171}
For every $m\geq m_a$ put
\[
 C=\frac{(a-1)m(m+1)}2,\qquad
 E_j=m-j+1\ (j<a),\qquad
 E_a=\frac{(m-a+1)(m-a+2)}2.
\]
Then the ideal of maximal minors is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-product-v171}
 I_q(M)=b^C J_{a,m},\qquad
 J_{a,m}=\prod_{j=1}^a(b,c^j)^{E_j}.
\end{equation}
The divisorial content is $b^C$, whereas $J_{a,m}$ and all its powers
are integrally closed. Changing $m$ changes the weights $C,E_j$, but
not the graph \eqref{eq:chain-blowup-v171}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A nonzero full minor chooses one column from each row; columns from
different rows are distinct. Thus $I_q(M)$ is the product of the row
ideals $Q_n$. Put $d_0=am-n$. Rows with $n\geq am$ have $Q_n=(1)$.
For $d_0\geq0$ set
\[
 h_0=\max\left(0,\left\lceil\frac{d_0-m}{a-1}\right\rceil\right),
 \qquad r_0=d_0-ah_0.
\]
For a fixed $h$, the least feasible $l$ is $\max(0,d_0-ah)$, and
feasibility is equivalent to $h\geq h_0$ until this minimum is reached.
The minimal monomials of $Q_n$ are therefore
$b^{h_0+t}c^{\max(0,r_0-at)}$, with
$0\leq t\leq\max(0,\lceil r_0/a\rceil)$. Consequently $Q_n=(b^{h_0})$
if $r_0\leq0$, and if $r_0=av+j>0$, $0\leq j<a$, then
\[
 Q_n=b^{h_0}(b,c^a)^v(b,c^j),
\]
where the last factor is omitted when $j=0$.

The sum of $h_0$ over the rows is
$(a-1)(1+\cdots+m)=C$. A positive residual value $r_0\leq m$ occurs
$\min(a,m-r_0+1)$ times. One occurrence has $h_0=0$; the others are
specified by $m-a+2-h_0\leq r_0\leq m-h_0$. The total exponents of
the residual factors are
\[
 \sum_{\substack{1\leq r_0\leq m\\r_0\equiv j\pmod a}}
       \min(a,m-r_0+1)=m-j+1\quad(1\leq j<a),
\]
and
\[
 \sum_{r_0=1}^m\min(a,m-r_0+1)\lfloor r_0/a\rfloor
 =\sum_{n=a}^m(n-a+1)=E_a.
\]
For the first count, write $n=r_0+t$, $0\leq t<a$, and count each
$n=j,\ldots,m$ once. For the second, at fixed $n$ sum
$\lfloor r_0/a\rfloor$ for
$\max(1,n-a+1)\leq r_0\leq n$; the result is $\max(0,n-a+1)$.
This proves \eqref{eq:row-product-v171}.

For a fixed integer exponent of $b$, the smallest exponent of $c$
in $J_{a,m}$ is obtained by choosing $b$ first in factors with largest
$j$. Each integer abscissa of its lower Newton polygon is thus
attained by an actual generator. The successive integral slopes
are $-a,\ldots,-1$ with positive horizontal lengths $E_a,\ldots,E_1$.
All lattice points above the polygon belong to the ideal. Replacing
$E_j$ by $nE_j$ proves the same assertion for every power, hence
integral closure by the monomial Newton-polyhedron criterion
\cite[Proposition 1.4.6]{HunekeSwanson171}. There is no common
height-one factor of $J_{a,m}$, since it contains powers of both $b$
and $c$. Thus $b^C$ is precisely the divisorial content.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:chain-v171}]
For an integral affine base, the closure of a rational projective map
with coordinates $f_0,\ldots,f_N$ is the blow-up of their ideal.
On the open where its $i$th homogeneous coordinate is nonzero the
coordinate ring is $A[f_j/f_i]$ inside $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$;
these are exactly the affine charts of the Rees Proj. Applying this
to the full minors gives the closure of the Hilbert map, since both
the Grassmannian and the Hilbert scheme are closed in the chosen
projective space and contain its generic image.

Multiplication in degree $n$ by $b^{nC}$ gives a graded isomorphism
$\mathcal R(J_{a,m})\simeq\mathcal R(b^CJ_{a,m})$. Therefore the
principal content does not change Proj, though it does change the
raw degree-$m$ Fitting ideal. The all-power integral-closure result
makes the Rees algebra normal. Its fan has, in clockwise order, rays
\[
 (0,1),(1,1),(2,1),\ldots,(a,1),(1,0).
\]
The same is true for $K_a$, by the identical argument with all
$E_j=1$. Each adjacent determinant has absolute value one, so these
normal blow-ups are the same smooth surface. Inserting the rays in
order gives the stated sequence of point blow-ups. The dual bases
on consecutive cones give exactly \eqref{eq:chain-charts-v171}
and the end charts; their Laurent identities give
\eqref{eq:chain-overlaps-v171}. Thus the cover is exhaustive.
Finally pull back the universal Hilbert family along the graph
isomorphism. This proves flatness and keeps the embedding in $X_0$,
not merely the abstract isomorphism class of a curve.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The marked principalization category]
\label{prop:chain-category-v171}
Fix the dense coefficient torus $U=\{bc\ne0\}\subset S_a$.
Objects are integral locally Noetherian $S_a$-schemes $V$ for which
$U_V$ is dense and every ideal $(b,c^j)\OO_V$ is invertible as a
nonzero fractional ideal. Morphisms are arbitrary $S_a$-morphisms
between these objects, with the induced maps on $U_V$.
Then $T_a$ is terminal in this category. For normal objects after
any finite flat coefficient cover, the corresponding normalized
blow-up is terminal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A product of nonzero fractional ideals is invertible if and only if
all its factors are invertible. To check the nontrivial direction
locally, if $I_1\cdots I_a=(t)$, then
$I_i(t^{-1}\prod_{j\ne i}I_j)=\OO$; this gives a fractional inverse.
The blow-up universal property applied to $K_a$ gives a unique map
to $T_a$. After a finite flat cover the same property gives a map to
the pulled-back blow-up. On a normal test, pull back its finite
normalization and close the generic section over $U_V$. This is a
finite integral birational scheme over the test and hence is the
test itself. The lift is unique by density and separatedness.
No identification of an arbitrary test with $U$ is required; its
specified structural map on $U_V$ is retained.
\end{proof}
