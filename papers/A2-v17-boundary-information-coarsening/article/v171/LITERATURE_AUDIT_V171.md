# Primary-source comparison and documentary limits

Date: September 27, 2026. This record distinguishes classical inputs, new applications in the manuscript, and documentary limits. It is not an exhaustive originality certification.

## Trace duality, differents and the conductor

The Stacks Project, Section 49.8, Tag 0BW0, https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BW0, defines the complementary trace module and identifies it canonically with Hom over the base (Lemma 49.8.2). The manuscript uses that precise identification; it does not claim it as new.

Section 49.12, Tag 0BWB, https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BWB, attributes the complete-intersection different computation to Tate's appendix in Mazur–Roberts. Lemmas 49.12.3 and 49.12.4 identify the different and the Kähler different, including the Jacobian generator and trace element; Lemma 49.12.6 gives the smooth equal-dimensional formulation. These are the classical inputs to the coefficient-change theorem.

Section 49.15, Tag 0DWM, https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0DWM, compares the complementary-module construction with finite duality. The manuscript instead writes out the elementary adjunction for the multiplier identity `(E:B)=j B^dagger`; the identity is not presented as a new duality principle.

The new geometric use is the exact conductor ideal after arbitrary finite flat smooth coefficient change of an arbitrary normal proper birational model, including singular normal models and all codimensions. The proof explicitly tracks every height-one prime in the finite normalization and the tame inverse different. The tower formula is the chain rule and different transitivity applied to the actual conductors; ordinary and reflexive ideal products are not conflated. These formulations are offered with proofs, not with a claim that the general duality mechanism is historically new.

## Integral closure, complete ideals and Jacobian containments

C. Huneke and I. Swanson, *Integral Closure of Ideals, Rings, and Modules*, LMS Lecture Note Series 336, Cambridge University Press, 2006. Author-hosted source: https://www.math.purdue.edu/~iswanso/book/index.html and https://www.math.purdue.edu/~iswanso/book/SwansonHuneke.pdf.

Proposition 1.4.6 gives the Newton-polyhedron criterion for integral closure of monomial ideals. Chapter 5 treats normalized Rees algebras; Chapter 10 treats Rees valuations; Chapter 12 treats conductors; Chapter 14 treats complete ideals on regular surfaces. These supply the established context of the chain and diagonal constructions. Their use is explicitly cited in the new Paper II, rather than treating monomial normalization as a new representability mechanism.

The conductor chapter includes the Dedekind/Jacobian containment statements and Theorem 12.3.10 (Lipman–Sathaye). Containment of a Jacobian multiple of a normalization is not, on its own, the actual-ideal equality used here. The manuscript obtains that equality under its stronger finite-flat complete-intersection setup via complementary modules and computes the finite-map different. It does not claim to generalize the Lipman–Sathaye theorem in its full range of hypotheses.

The square-tangency calculation is explicitly outside diagonal coefficient monomialization by unit changes: the reduced coefficient divisors have contact order two, and D1 and D2 agree on the values of u,v but disagree on u+v². The normal rings, their finite free order sublattices, the two A1 points, the complete reduced fibre, and the all-order endpoint induction are written out. These are the specific calculations to compare, rather than a new name for an existing toric fan.

## Hilbert embedding

G. Gotzmann, *Eine Bedingung für die Flachheit und das Hilbertpolynom eines graduierten Ringes*, Math. Z. 158 (1978), 61–70, DOI 10.1007/BF01214566. The manuscript uses ordinary projective regularity after the Segre embedding, not an unstated multigraded theorem. It writes the Gotzmann representation for `(a+1)l+1`, the evaluation matrix and the row count; the graph/Rees identification is proved on the actual affine charts.

The earlier comparison with tropical Grassmannians, comprehensive Gröbner systems, fixed-source Quot spaces and stable quotients remains in the complete technical supplement and historical audits. It is not recast as a proof of novelty for the conductor identity.

## Paper I and Ballico

The report correctly identifies an unresolved external-audit requirement. No external independent full proof audit of Paper I was obtained in this revision. The new introduction separates its invariant recovery route and characteristic-zero inputs from downstream boundary applications, but that is an audit aid rather than an audit certificate.

The publisher's table of contents for Mathematische Nachrichten 163 (1993) confirms Ballico's *On the Failure Locus of Higher Order Properties of Embeddings in Projective Spaces*, pp. 5–13: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/toc/15222616/1993/163/1. The complete theorem/proof text of DOI 10.1002/mana.19931630102 was not obtained. The retained comparison with the available 1996 failure-cycle result does not replace it. The main reconstruction paper makes no anticipation or nonanticipation claim requiring that missing comparison.

## Evidence boundaries

Primary source statements are credited where used. New algebraic arguments, exact symbolic checks, archival source hashes, compiled PDFs and GitHub checks have different evidentiary roles. The exact checks test finite instances and the implementation of formulas. None of these records certifies a general proof, exhaustive historical originality or suitability for any particular journal.
