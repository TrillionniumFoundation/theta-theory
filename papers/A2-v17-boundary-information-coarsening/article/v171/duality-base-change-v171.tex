\section{Conductors under finite flat change of coefficients}
\label{sec:duality-base-change-v171}
Throughout this section $k$ is a field of characteristic zero. A variety is
an integral separated scheme of finite type over $k$. If $V$ is normal and
$D=\sum d_QQ$ is a Weil divisor, $\OO_V(D)$ denotes the subsheaf of its
function field specified by $v_Q(z)+d_Q\geq0$ at every prime divisor.
Products of such sheaves are ordinary products unless a double dual is
explicitly written. For a finite dominant morphism of normal varieties,
pullback of a Weil divisor means its valuation pullback, with the
ramification indices as coefficients; no Cartier assumption is implicit.

Let $h:X\to S$ be a finite flat dominant morphism of smooth varieties,
and let $f:T\to S$ be a proper birational morphism with $T$ normal.
There is no restriction on their common dimension. Set
\[
 Y=T\times_S X,\qquad Z=Y^\nu,
 \qquad \nu:Z\longrightarrow Y,\quad
 \pi:Z\longrightarrow X,\quad \psi:Z\longrightarrow T.
\]
The Jacobian ideal $\mathfrak j_h=\operatorname{Fitt}_0\Omega_{X/S}$ is
invertible and nonzero. Write $\mathfrak j_h=\OO_X(-R_h)$. For a prime
divisor $Q$ of $Z$ over a prime divisor $P$ of $T$, let $e_Q$ be its
ramification index, and put $R_\psi=\sum_Q(e_Q-1)Q$.
These definitions use the morphisms, not choices of local coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Conductor and coefficient change]
\label{thm:duality-conductor-v171}
In the preceding situation, $Y$ is integral and $S_2$, and its
normalization is finite. The conductor, as an actual ideal of $\OO_Z$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:duality-conductor-v171}
 \mathfrak c_{Z/Y}
 =\OO_Z(R_\psi-\pi^*R_h).
\end{equation}
In particular it is reflexive, and its coefficient at $Q$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobian-order-v171}
 c_Q=v_Q(\mathfrak j_h)-e_Q+1\geq0.
\end{equation}
There is no further condition supported in codimension at least two.
The scheme $Y$ is normal if and only if $c_Q=0$ for every $Q$.
The conductor is supported over the locus on which $f$ is not an
isomorphism. If $T$ is smooth, $Y$ is moreover a local complete
intersection and hence Cohen--Macaulay.
\end{theorem}

The identity is an application of classical complementary modules and
finite duality, not a new duality theorem. Its use here is that the same
formula computes a global conductor for nonmonomial coefficient changes,
for singular normal models, and for models in higher dimension. The
explicit tangency calculations in Section~\ref{sec:tangent-v171} will
supply its first nonmonomial Hilbert-boundary application.

\subsection{Trace lattices and the actual conductor ideal}
We give the algebraic argument, including the identification as an ideal
rather than just an isomorphism of abstract modules. For a normal
Noetherian domain $A$ with fraction field $K$, a finite separable
extension $L/K$, and a finite $A$-subalgebra $E\subset L$, define
\[
 E^\dagger=\{z\in L:\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(zE)\subset A\}.
\]
The trace pairing identifies this with $\operatorname{Hom}_A(E,A)$.
This is the Dedekind complementary module
\cite[Section 49.8]{Stacks171}. We shall also use the classical equality
of the different and the K\"ahler different for a finite flat local
complete intersection map \cite[Section 49.12]{Stacks171}.

\begin{lemma}[A trace-lattice identity]\label{lem:trace-lattice-v171}
Suppose $E$ is finite locally free over $A$, is a domain with fraction
field $L$, and $E^\dagger=j^{-1}E$ for a nonzero $j\in E$.
Let $B$ be the finite normalization of $E$ in $L$. Then, inside $L$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-conductor-v171}
 (E:B)=jB^\dagger.
\end{equation}
Here $(E:B)=\{z\in L:zB\subset E\}$ is contained in $B$ and is the
ordinary conductor. The assertion is local on $\operatorname{Spec}E$
when $E^\dagger$ is only locally generated in this way.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every $E$-module $M$ there is the elementary adjunction
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_E(M,\operatorname{Hom}_A(E,A))
 \simeq\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,A).
\]
The forward map evaluates at $1\in E$; the inverse sends a functional
$\lambda$ to $m\mapsto(e\mapsto\lambda(em))$. Apply it to $M=B$.
After extension to $K$, all functionals are given uniquely by the trace
pairing on the field $L$. Thus the adjunction identifies the two
submodules of $L$ by the same multiplier, not by an unspecified unit:
\[
 B^\dagger
 =\{z\in L:zB\subset j^{-1}E\}
 =j^{-1}(E:B).
\]
Since $1\in B$, a conductor multiplier lies in $E\subset B$. Conversely
$(E:B)$ is a $B$-ideal. This proves the claimed equality as ideals.
Localization and change of a local generator multiply both sides by
the same unit, so the local identities glue.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The complementary module on a normal model]
\label{lem:normal-complementary-v171}
Let $A\subset B$ be a finite inclusion of normal domains essentially of
finite type over a characteristic-zero field, with separable fraction
fields $K\subset L$. On $\operatorname{Spec}B$ one has
\[
 B^\dagger=\OO_B\left(\sum_Q(e_Q-1)Q\right),
\]
where $Q$ runs over prime divisors and $e_Q$ is computed over its
height-one contraction to $A$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A normal domain is the intersection of its height-one localizations in
its fraction field. Hence $z\in B^\dagger$ if and only if the trace of
$zb$ is in $A_P$ for every height-one $P\subset A$ and every $b\in B$.
The ring $B\otimes_A A_P$ is the semilocal normalization of the DVR
$A_P$ in $L$. Its maximal ideals are the primes $Q$ over $P$.
The local complementary module has valuation $-(e_Q-1)$ at $Q$:
residue characteristic zero gives tame ramification and a separable
residue extension. This is the inverse-different formula for DVRs.
One may see the exponent after an unramified extension by writing a
uniformizer of $A_P$ as a unit times an $e_Q$th power; the derivative
has order $e_Q-1$. Unramified trace duality contributes order zero.

Going down over the normal domain $A$, together with incomparability
for integral extensions, shows that every height-one prime of $B$
contracts to a height-one prime of $A$. Thus all height-one primes
of $B$ are included. Intersecting the preceding valuation conditions in $L$
is exactly the displayed divisorial sheaf. This also proves its
reflexivity without assuming that $B$ is Cohen--Macaulay.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:duality-conductor-v171}]
On an affine open of $T$, the coordinate algebra of $Y$ is finite
locally free over the coordinate domain of $T$. It therefore injects
into its generic algebra. Birationality of $f$ identifies that generic
algebra with the single field $k(X)$, not a product of fields.
Consequently every such algebra is a domain. This proves integrality,
including the exclusion of vertical or embedded structure. The finite
flat morphism $h$ is a local complete intersection: it is a morphism
between smooth varieties of equal dimension. Its base change is
syntomic of relative dimension zero. The flat depth formula, with
zero-dimensional complete-intersection fibres, transfers $S_2$ from
$T$ to $Y$. If $T$ is smooth the same description makes $Y$ a local
complete intersection. Finiteness of normalization follows from
excellence.

Let $C$ be the finite locally free algebra of $X$ over an affine part
of $S$, and $A$ the coordinate algebra of an affine part of $T$ above
it. Put $E=C\otimes A$. Finite locally free duality and its trace
element commute with arbitrary base change: this is checked by
dualizing a finite free module. The complete-intersection different
formula therefore identifies the image of the trace in the invertible
relative dualizing module with $\mathfrak j_hE$.

We may now localize $A$ at a point of $T$. The finite algebra $E$ is
semilocal, so this invertible module and the pulled-back Jacobian
ideal are free. Choose a generator $j$ of $\mathfrak j_hE$.
Under the trace identification the preceding assertion is exactly
$E^\dagger=j^{-1}E$. This argument does not require a single global
Jacobian equation on $X$, or apply a finite trace to a nonfinite
localization of $C$.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:trace-lattice-v171}, and then
Lemma~\ref{lem:normal-complementary-v171}, to the normalization $B$.
They give
\[
 \mathfrak c_{Z/Y}=j\,\OO_Z(R_\psi)
                 =\OO_Z(R_\psi-\pi^*R_h).
\]
The ideal on the left is contained in $\OO_Z$, so all its valuation
orders are nonnegative. This is an equality of sheaves, and therefore
already includes every point of codimension at least two.
It is the unit ideal precisely when $B=E$. On an open where $T=S$
the base change is $X$, which is normal, so no conductor occurs there.
All claims follow.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Defect cycles and composition}
Write $C(Z/Y)=\pi^*R_h-R_\psi$ for the effective conductor divisor.
Let $g:Y\to T$ be the finite projection. For a prime divisor
$P\subset T$, let
\[
 \delta_P=\operatorname{length}_{\OO_{T,P}}
   \left((\psi_*\OO_Z)/(g_*\OO_Y)\right)_P.
\]
This is a length on the base DVR; it is not the length at an individual
normalization branch.

\begin{corollary}[Normalization defect]\label{cor:defect-cycle-v171}
In the situation of Theorem~\ref{thm:duality-conductor-v171},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-cycle-v171}
 2\delta_P=\sum_{Q\mid P}[k(Q):k(P)]\,c_Q.
\end{equation}
Thus $2\sum_P\delta_PP=\psi_*C(Z/Y)$. The generic defect cycle is
supported on the exceptional locus of $T\to S$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Localize at $P$. Both the order $E$ and its normalization $B$ are free
lattices of the same rank over the DVR $A=\OO_{T,P}$. If a basis of
$E$ is expressed in a basis of $B$ by a matrix $M$, then
$v_A(\det M)=\operatorname{length}_A(B/E)=\delta_P$.
The matrices of the trace pairing satisfy $G_E=M^{\mathsf t}G_BM$.
Therefore
\[
 2\delta_P=v_A(\operatorname{disc}E)-v_A(\operatorname{disc}B).
\]
The first discriminant is the norm of the different generator $j_h$,
so its order is $\sum_{Q\mid P}[k(Q):k(P)]v_Q(j_h)$. The second has
order $\sum_{Q\mid P}[k(Q):k(P)](e_Q-1)$ by tame DVR duality.
Subtraction proves the formula. This argument also verifies that the
right side is even; no geometric splitting assumption was used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Transitivity of conductor divisors]
\label{thm:conductor-tower-v171}
Let $X_2\xrightarrow{h_2}X_1\xrightarrow{h_1}S$ be finite flat
dominant maps of smooth varieties. For a fixed normal proper
birational model $T\to S$, let
\[
 Z_i=(T\times_S X_i)^\nu,\qquad
 \chi:Z_2\longrightarrow Z_1,
 \qquad W=Z_1\times_{X_1}X_2.
\]
Then $W$ is integral, $Z_2=W^\nu$ canonically, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tower-conductor-v171}
 C(Z_2/T\times_S X_2)
 =\chi^*C(Z_1/T\times_S X_1)+C(Z_2/W).
\end{equation}
These identifications are associative for successive finite flat
covers. In terms of ideals, the equality is the reflexive product
of the two conductor ideals after reflexive pullback; it need not
be an equality of ordinary tensor products.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Finite flatness injects the coordinate rings of $W$ into their generic
field $k(X_2)$, so $W$ is integral. The finite normalization map to
$T\times_S X_1$ remains finite after base change. The two resulting
integral rings have the same fraction field and one is integral over
the other; their integral closures therefore coincide. This proves the
canonical identification and finiteness of $\chi$.

The chain rule gives
$R_{h_1h_2}=h_2^*R_{h_1}+R_{h_2}$.
For a prime of $Z_2$ above $Z_1$ and $T$, ramification indices multiply;
thus $e_1e_2-1=e_2(e_1-1)+(e_2-1)$ gives
$R_{\psi_1\chi}=\chi^*R_{\psi_1}+R_\chi$.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:duality-conductor-v171} to the original model
and also to the normal model $Z_1\to X_1$. Subtracting the two divisor
identities gives \eqref{eq:tower-conductor-v171}. The second summand
is an actual effective conductor divisor even when $Z_1$ is singular;
this is why normal, rather than only smooth, models were allowed.
Integral closure in one field and the chain rule are associative.
Equality in codimension one gives the stated reflexive-product
interpretation, and asserts no equality for unsaturated products.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Normal Hilbert models]\label{cor:general-hilbert-v171}
Let $S$ be smooth and let a rational map from $S$ to a separated
Hilbert scheme be defined on a dense open. Let $T$ be the normalization
of its retained-coefficient graph closure, assumed proper over $S$.
Every finite flat dominant map $X\to S$ from a smooth variety has the
conductor and defect formulas above for $T\times_S X$ and its
normalization, compatibly with towers of covers. The pulled-back
universal embedded family remains flat.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The normalized graph is a normal proper birational model of $S$,
so the theorem applies directly. Flatness of the universal family is
preserved by every base change, including normalization of parameters.
If the original graph was not normal, $T\times_S X$ is the pullback
of its normalization, not silently the original graph. The result
computes the new conductor defect; it does not classify all fibres of
that Hilbert scheme or normalize the embedded universal curves.
\end{proof}
