\section{The signed one-flight benchmark and the restriction to long bridges}
\label{sec:v25-one-flight-benchmark}

The signed support observation is richer than an integrated probability
germ.  It therefore has a short-flight inverse different from the two-flight
integrated benchmark in the preceding section.  Recording this distinction
specifies the contribution of the long-bridge results.

\begin{proposition}[One-flight signed support inverse]
\label{prop:v25-one-flight-inverse}
Suppose mixed-type one-flight endpoint support is observed as a function of
physical time in calibrated signed transverse coordinates, with its onset
retained.  Then the two labelled facing graph germs are determined without
symmetry, equal contacts or supplied curvatures.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On a sufficiently small contact box the one-flight length is
\[
 \ell(u,v)=\sqrt{(g+\psi_0(u)+\psi_1(v))^2+(v-u)^2}.
\]
The physical flux factor is positive there.  Hence the threshold in physical
time at which an endpoint pair enters the support is \(\ell(u,v)\): the
entrance residual time can be any sufficiently small positive number, and
no record is possible before the flight length.  Equivalently, in excess
time the support threshold is \(\ell-g\); the onset supplies \(g\).
Since \(\psi_0(0)=\psi_1(0)=0\), the coordinate slices give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-one-flight-inverse}
 \psi_0(u)=\sqrt{\ell(u,0)^2-u^2}-g,\qquad
 \psi_1(v)=\sqrt{\ell(0,v)^2-v^2}-g.
\end{equation}
The positive square-root branch is fixed by \(g+\psi_b>0\).
Thus the graph functions themselves, and not just finitely many of their
jets, are recovered.  For analytic boundaries the same continuation and
intrinsic gluing questions can subsequently be considered.
\end{proof}

This proposition does not identify its observations with the same-type
limiting laws.  It assumes access to a mixed-type one-flight support function
\(\ell(u,v)\).  The signed long-bridge theorem instead recovers two
half-line actions from the limiting \(00\) and \(11\) endpoint laws, and
then inverts their interaction with both contact graphs through the
order-by-order determinant-one blocks.  The two-flight benchmark concerns
integrated probability germs and is a third observation family.  No Markov
ordering between these three families is asserted without a common
acquisition model and an actual kernel.

An unrestricted finite-flight consistency theorem would not, by itself,
establish a need for long bridges: the identity
\eqref{eq:v25-one-flight-inverse} already gives a direct short-flight route
when its support data are available.  The physical global theorem here has
a more specific conclusion.  For any prescribed lower bound on the flight
number, its pilot and all inference batches use only even same-type records
above that bound.  Along the consistent reconstruction sequence that lower
bound tends to infinity.  Its pilot uses shrinking near-onset windows at
those same long even flight numbers, and not a hidden one-flight geometric
scan.  Thus its contact inverse is genuinely implemented in the restricted
long-bridge observation family.

The one-flight identity neither yields a relative law uniform on a fixed
offset collar as the flight number grows nor describes the information lost
by deleting residual time or waiting counts.  Those are separate assertions
of the relative boundary law, the explicit Poisson equivalence, and the
Gaussian coarsening theorems.  In particular, the necessity of the
long-bridge analysis in this article is tied to these observation and limit
restrictions, not to a claim that a billiard contact cannot be recovered from
any shorter experiment.
