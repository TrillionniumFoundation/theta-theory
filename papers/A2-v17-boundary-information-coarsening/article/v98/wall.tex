\section{A remote branch at the stochastic weight wall}\label{sec:wall}
Fix $r,s,D$ with $B>0$, positive $u,v_1,v_2$ with $v_1\ne v_2$,
and $U=(u,u)$, $V=(v_1,v_2)$. Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall-centre}
 \gamma_0=\pi c_*,\qquad \alpha_0=1-\pi c_*,\qquad
                   \pi<\alpha_0<1-\pi.
\end{equation}
The design is fixed. Let $\theta_\delta$ be the original $(f,g)$ centre
with weight $\alpha=\alpha_0+\delta$. Then $E=-A\delta$, so
$\delta>0$ is the identified side. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstar}
 a=\frac{4rs}{r+3s},\qquad b=\frac{rs}{3s-2r},\qquad
 h_*=(1-c_*)f+c_*g=(z-a)(z-b)^2.
\end{equation}
Substitution gives $s<a<r<b<D$. At $\delta=0$ the two exact component
pairs are $(f,g)$ and $(f,h_*)$. The remote pair has weight $\pi$ on
$h_*$ and second-channel columns
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remote-channel}
 v_f^*=\frac{\alpha_0v_1+(\gamma_0-\pi)v_2}{1-\pi},\qquad v_h^*=v_2.
\end{equation}
Let $\xi_*$ be this point; its observation is $P_0=P_{\theta_0}$.
Its second channel is invertible because
$v_f^*-v_2=\alpha_0(v_1-v_2)/(1-\pi)\ne0$.
The sorted base and remote spectra are respectively
\[
 (0,s,s,s,r,r),\qquad (0,a,r,r,b,b),
\]
whose distance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dstar}
 D_* =\max(r-s,b-r)>0.
\end{equation}
Thus a zero-length additional pencil interval already has a positive
exact-fibre spectral diameter.

\subsection{A complete real corner chart at the remote point}
Use the eleven coordinates
$v=(a_0,b_0,X,c_0,d_0,Y,k,\dot u_f,\dot u_h,\dot v_f,\dot v_h)$,
where a binary channel increment is represented by its one free
zero-sum coordinate. Set
\begin{align}
 p_f&=-a_0(z-r)^2-b_0z(z-r)-Xz,\nonumber\\
 p_h&=-c_0(z-b)^2-d_0(z-a)(z-b)-Y(z-a),\label{eq:remote-jets}\\
 \mathcal T_*&=\{v:a_0,X,Y,k\ge0\}.\nonumber
\end{align}
The remaining seven coordinates are free. Here $k$ is the increment
of the weight on $h_*$; the weight on $f$ has increment $-k$.
Let $J_*v$ be the full coefficient and stochastic vector with polynomial
increments $p_f,p_h$ and these stochastic increments.

\begin{proposition}[Remote tangent cone and converse realization]\label{prop:remote-cone}
In a neighbourhood of $\xi_*$, modulo the one component permutation,
the closed model has a Nash coordinate chart from a neighbourhood of
zero in $\mathcal T_*$. Its derivative is the invertible linear map
$J_*$. Consequently its coefficient--stochastic tangent cone is exactly
$J_*(\mathcal T_*)$. Every normalized secant has a subsequential limit
in this cone, and every vector of the cone is realized by a feasible
arc. For bounded $v\in\mathcal T_*$ the arcs can be chosen with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remote-second-order}
 \xi(\tau,v)=\xi_*+\tau J_*v+O(\tau^2),\qquad
 F_T(\xi(\tau,v))=P_0+\tau S_*v+O(\tau^2),
\end{equation}
uniformly on bounded coordinate sets. Here $S_*$ is the score
\eqref{eq:score} at the remote point. All assertions are uniform on
compact subsets of the wall family with its stated strict margins.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Factor each nearby polynomial into its separated simple and quadratic
factors. Coprime factorization is analytic in coefficients. Write the
simple root of the first component as $u$, the mean of its quadratic
roots as $r+v_f/2$, and their squared half-separation as $x$. Write the
corresponding coordinates of the second component as $a+w$, $b+v_h/2$
and $y$. Thus the exact polynomials are
\[
 (z-u)((z-r-v_f/2)^2-x),\qquad
 (z-a-w)((z-b-v_h/2)^2-y).
\]
These six coordinates are Nash functions of the coefficients near the
remote pair. Conversely the displayed formulas are polynomial in the
six coordinates. Their derivative has the three columns in
\eqref{eq:remote-jets} for each component. They are independent:
for the first polynomial, evaluation at $0$ and $r$ first determines
$a_0$ and $X$, and then the remaining coefficient determines $b_0$;
for the second, evaluation at $a$ and $b$ determines $c_0$ and $Y$,
and then $d_0$. The inverse function theorem gives the asserted local
coefficient chart.

The first simple root is feasible exactly when $u\ge0$, locally.
Its upper endpoint is inactive. All other cluster centres are strictly
inside $(0,D)$, so their interval constraints are inactive in this
neighbourhood. Real-rootedness of the two quadratic factors is exactly
$x,y\ge0$. The weight on $h_*$ is at its floor, giving $k\ge0$;
the other floor has strict margin $1-2\pi>0$. Channel entries are
strictly positive, so only their linear column-sum equations remain
active. This proves that the complete feasible chart is the indicated
orthant times seven free coordinates, not merely an outer cone.
Separated factors are unique. The alternative component labelling is a
second, identical chart under the permutation quotient and introduces
no extra directions.

For the secant assertion, let $\tau$ be the norm of a coefficient and
stochastic displacement. Analytic factorization bounds the simple and
mean shifts by $O(\tau)$. The quadratic coefficients bound $x,y$ by
$O(\tau)$ as well, since variance is mean squared minus the constant
coefficient. Dividing the exact chart coordinates by $\tau$ gives a
bounded vector in $\mathcal T_*$. Its subsequential limits give all
normalized tangent secants. In the norm-normalized case $J_*v$ has
norm one in the limit, so no nonzero secant disappears in the chart.

Conversely set $u=\tau a_0$, $v_f=\tau b_0$, $x=\tau X$,
$w=\tau c_0$, $v_h=\tau d_0$, $y=\tau Y$, choose weight
$\pi+\tau k$ on $h$, and take the specified order-$\tau$ channel
increments. The roots are
\[
 \tau a_0,\quad r+\tau b_0/2\pm\sqrt{\tau X},\quad
 a+\tau c_0,\quad b+\tau d_0/2\pm\sqrt{\tau Y}.
\]
They remain feasible for small $\tau$, uniformly for bounded coordinates.
They are analytic in $\sqrt\tau$; their polynomial coefficients are
analytic in $\tau$. In fact the exact identities are
\begin{align}
 f_\tau-f-\tau p_f
 &=\tau^2\{a_0b_0(z-r)+a_0X+b_0^2z/4\}
                         -\tau^3a_0b_0^2/4,\label{eq:remote-f-identity}\\
 h_\tau-h_*-\tau p_h
 &=\tau^2\{c_0d_0(z-b)+c_0Y+d_0^2(z-a)/4\}
                         -\tau^3c_0d_0^2/4.\label{eq:remote-h-identity}
\end{align}
There is no order-$\tau^{3/2}$ coefficient term at these simple/double
clusters. Taylor expansion of the rational observation map, whose
normalizers stay positive, proves \eqref{eq:remote-second-order}.
Compact strict root, channel, weight and clock margins make all inverse
chart and derivative bounds uniform as stated.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The constrained entrance program}
Put $B_0=\left.\frac{d}{d\delta}P_{\theta_\delta}\right|_{\delta=0}$
and use the Fisher norm at $P_0$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu}
 \mu_E=\frac12\min_{v\in\mathcal T_*}\|S_*v-B_0\|_{I,0}.
\end{equation}
This is an explicit finite-dimensional program on the cone just proved
to be the full feasible tangent cone.

\begin{lemma}[Positive entrance separation]\label{lem:mu-positive}
The score has no nonzero zero on $\mathcal T_*$, and $B_0$ is not in
its image. Thus $\mu_E>0$ and its minimum is attained. Eliminating seven
free variables gives sixteen independent-support KKT tests on four
nonnegative variables. For algebraic input $\mu_E$ is real algebraic.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $S_*v=B_0$. The differentiated normalizer equations,
using Lemma~\ref{lem:normalizer} at the coprime remote pair with invertible
second channel, imply $\dot q=f-g$ and
$\dot K=\left.\frac{d}{d\delta}K_{\theta_\delta}\right|_0$.
The derivative of the second marginal belongs to
$\operatorname{span}(f,g)$. Its stochastic derivative terms already
belong to that span; the remote second-channel weight matrix is
invertible. Hence $p_f,p_h\in\operatorname{span}(f,g)$. Since their
degrees are below three, write
$p_f=t_f(g-f)$ and $p_h=t_h(g-f)$.

Evaluation at $0$ gives $t_f=a_0r^2/s^3\ge0$, while evaluation at $r$
gives $t_f=-Xr/(r-s)^3\le0$. Therefore $t_f=0$ and $p_f=0$.
At $b$ the second polynomial gives
\[
 t_h=\frac{Y(b-a)}{f(b)-g(b)}\ge0,
\]
because $R(b)>1$. Differentiation of the monic normalizer now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall-obstruction}
 \pi t_h+c_*k=-1,
\end{equation}
an impossibility. For a zero score, the same argument has right-hand
side zero and implies $t_h=k=0$. All polynomial jets vanish.
Coefficient functionals dual to $f,h_*$ then imply that each coefficient
matrix has zero derivative; taking its row and column sums forces all
four channel derivatives to vanish. The independent chart columns in
Proposition~\ref{prop:remote-cone} force $v=0$.

The intersection of $\mathcal T_*$ with the unit sphere is compact,
so $\|S_*v\|_{I,0}\ge c\|v\|$ on the cone. Thus the objective in
\eqref{eq:mu} tends to infinity with $\|v\|$; its minimum is attained
and positive, since equality $S_*v=B_0$ was excluded.

Let $\mathsf L_*$ be the seven free columns, $\mathsf D_*$ the four
nonnegative columns, $W=\diag(w_j/P_{0,je})$, and $\mathcal P$ the
Fisher-orthogonal projection off $\operatorname{im}\mathsf L_*$. Put
\[
 Q_*=\mathsf D_*^{\mathsf T}W\mathcal P\mathsf D_*,\quad
 g_*=\mathsf D_*^{\mathsf T}W\mathcal P B_0,\quad
 b_*=B_0^{\mathsf T}W\mathcal P B_0.
\]
Coercivity implies full rank of the free columns, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entrance-QP}
 4\mu_E^2=\min_{z\ge0}\{z^{\mathsf T}Q_*z-2g_*^{\mathsf T}z+b_*\}.
\end{equation}
For each of the sixteen supports $A$ with $Q_{*,AA}$ positive definite,
set $z_A=Q_{*,AA}^{-1}g_{*,A}$, require $z_A\ge0$ and
$(Q_*z-g_*)_j\ge0$ off $A$, and take value
$b_*-g_{*,A}^{\mathsf T}Q_{*,AA}^{-1}g_{*,A}$. Include the empty
support. The minimal-positive-support argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic-Q} proves completeness, including singular
$Q_*$. All entries are rational in the wall coordinates; take the
positive square root after selecting the exact minimum.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local coefficient control at the remote point]\label{lem:remote-control}
In a coefficient and stochastic neighbourhood of $\xi_*$ in the closed
model,
\[
 d_{\rm par}(\xi,\xi_*)\le C\|F_T(\xi)-P_0\|.
\]
Every competitor at parameter distance $O(\delta)$ has bounded exact
chart coordinates at scale $\delta$ and observation expansion
$P_0+\delta S_*v+O(\delta^2)$. Conversely each bounded cone vector has
such a feasible realization.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If the inverse failed, normalize a sequence of displacements by their
norm. Proposition~\ref{prop:remote-cone} gives a nonzero limit $J_*v$
with $v\in\mathcal T_*$ and zero score, contradicting
Lemma~\ref{lem:mu-positive}. Equivalently in its chart, Taylor's theorem
and cone coercivity give
$\|F_T(\Phi(w))-P_0\|\ge c\|w\|-C\|w\|^2\ge c\|w\|/2$
for small feasible $w$. The chart is locally bi-Lipschitz in coefficients
and stochastic entries, which proves the inverse and the stated bounded
coordinate assertion. The expansions and converse are
\eqref{eq:remote-second-order}. This proof does not assume the inverse
in order to identify the tangent cone.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two-scale law with a quadratic entrance remainder}
Choose disjoint small closed coefficient and stochastic neighbourhoods
$N_0,N_*$ of the two exact representatives at the wall, modulo component
permutation, with $\xi_*$ in the relative interior of $N_*$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drem}
 d_{\rm rem}(\delta)=\min_{\xi\in N_*}h_w(F_T(\xi),P_{\theta_\delta}).
\end{equation}
Their boundaries have a positive observation separation from $P_0$.

\begin{theorem}[Weight-wall transition]\label{thm:wall}
Under \eqref{eq:wall-centre} and $B>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entrance-law}
 d_{\rm rem}(\delta)=\mu_E\delta+O(\delta^2)\quad(\delta\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
The minimum is independent of the sufficiently small neighbourhood
$N_*$ for all sufficiently small $\delta$. The base local constant
$C_{\loc}(\theta_\delta)$ has the finite positive limit
$C_{\loc}(\theta_0)$; neither $\kappa_x$ nor $\kappa_y$ vanishes at
this wall. For fixed $\lambda>0$ the whole-model modulus satisfies
\begin{align}
 0<\lambda<\mu_E:\quad
 \omega_{\theta_\delta}(\lambda\delta)
   &=C_{\loc}(\theta_0)\sqrt{\lambda\delta}+O_\lambda(\delta),
                                                        \label{eq:below-wall}\\
 \lambda>\mu_E:\quad
 \omega_{\theta_\delta}(\lambda\delta)
   &=D_*+O_\lambda(\sqrt\delta).                         \label{eq:above-wall}
\end{align}
The exact entrance curve for the closed ball is $t=d_{\rm rem}(\delta)$,
including equality. The layer not decided by the first-order threshold
alone has width $O(\delta^2)$ in $t$.

On the nonidentified side, for small $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonident-opening}
 \omega_{\theta_{-\varepsilon}}(0)
 =\max\left\{r-s,\ b-r+\sqrt{\frac{\chi\varepsilon}{\pi}}
                         +O(\varepsilon)\right\},\qquad
 \chi=\frac{f(b)-g(b)}{b-a}>0.
\end{equation}
If $b-r<r-s$, the exact diameter stays equal to $r-s$ initially;
if $b-r\ge r-s$, it opens above $D_*$ at the displayed square-root rate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-fibre} gives exactly the two representatives at
$\delta=0$. Compactness gives a positive observation gap outside
$N_0\cup N_*$. Since $\xi_*$ itself is at distance $O(\delta)$ from
$P_{\theta_\delta}$, a minimizer in \eqref{eq:drem} has observation
$O(\delta)$ from $P_0$ and lies away from the boundary of $N_*$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:remote-control} bounds its coefficient and stochastic
displacement by $O(\delta)$. Its exact chart coordinates are therefore
$\delta v$ for bounded $v\in\mathcal T_*$. Expansion at the common
positive probability array and the reverse triangle inequality give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entrance-expansion}
 h_w(F_T(\xi),P_{\theta_\delta})
       =\frac\delta2\|S_*v-B_0\|_{I,0}+O(\delta^2),
\end{equation}
uniformly on those bounded coordinates. The lower bound in
\eqref{eq:entrance-law} follows by minimizing the leading norm.
Conversely a minimizer of \eqref{eq:mu}, realized by the exact chart,
gives the upper bound. The error is quadratic because of
\eqref{eq:remote-f-identity}--\eqref{eq:remote-h-identity}, not merely
an appeal to a generic root-continuity bound. This also implies the
weaker $O(\delta^{3/2})$ entrance estimate. The same localization puts
all minimizers inside any fixed smaller remote neighbourhood for small
$\delta$, proving independence of its choice.

Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic-Q} and Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-local} apply
on a compact base path segment including $\theta_0$; this pair stays
locally coercive there. Thus $C_{\loc}$ is positive and locally Lipschitz
through the wall. If $\lambda<\mu_E$, the remote neighbourhood is
excluded from the radius-$\lambda\delta$ ball for small $\delta$.
The exterior gap excludes every other competitor, so the whole ball
is the base local ball. Formula \eqref{eq:cubic-C} gives
\eqref{eq:below-wall}. Replacing $C_{\loc}(\theta_\delta)$ by
$C_{\loc}(\theta_0)$ costs only $O(\delta^{3/2})$.

If $\lambda>\mu_E$, a remote minimizer and the base centre belong to
the ball. Every competitor belongs to one of the two neighbourhoods.
The two coefficient inverses put its roots within
$O_\lambda(\sqrt\delta)$ of the corresponding exact spectrum at the
wall. The bottleneck triangle inequality gives both bounds in
\eqref{eq:above-wall}. Compact attainment makes remote membership
exactly $t\ge d_{\rm rem}(\delta)$, including equality. The width of
the unresolved first-order layer follows from \eqref{eq:entrance-law};
it does not decide the sign of the difference on the critical ray.

For $\theta_{-\varepsilon}$ the extra exact pencil interval is
$[c_*,c_*+\varepsilon/\pi]$, since $c_D>c_*$ is fixed. At
$c=c_*+\sigma$, expansion at $z=b+u$ gives
\[
 h_c(b+u)=(b-a)u^2+u^3+\sigma(g(b)-f(b))+O(\sigma|u|).
\]
Since $R(b)>1$, $\chi>0$ and the two roots near $b$ are
$b\pm\sqrt{\chi\sigma}+O(\sigma)$. The third root remains in $(s,r)$.
The larger root increases with $c$ by the monotonic branch of $R$ above
its maximum. Comparing sorted lists, the distance to the base spectrum
is exactly $\max(r-s,b_+(c)-r)$. Two remote spectra are only
$O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$ apart, less than $D_*$ for small $\varepsilon$.
The exact-fibre diameter is therefore attained against the base at the
upper pencil endpoint. This proves \eqref{eq:nonident-opening} and
its two cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The critical ray]
At $t=\mu_E\delta$, remote membership is decided by the sign of
$d_{\rm rem}(\delta)-\mu_E\delta$. For algebraic input,
\eqref{eq:drem} has a compact first-order minimum formula. Elimination
and real algebraic branch isolation determine its Puiseux expansion.
The first nonzero higher coefficient decides the critical ray; if the
difference is identically zero, the closed ball includes the remote
branch. No strict regime is assigned to that ray by convention.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The geometric wall]
At $B=0$ with $E>0$, the remote double root is $b=D$, and the additional
pencil interval again consists of one point at positive spectral distance.
On the nonidentified side put $\ell_0=D-b$. Expansion at the strict
maximum of $R$ gives
\[
 c_D-c_*=
 \frac{-R''(b)}{2(R(b)-1)^2}\,\ell_0^2+O(\ell_0^3),\qquad
 \ell_0=\frac{B}{3s-2r}.
\]
The coefficient is positive and the weight floor is inactive. This
interval is born with quadratic length in geometric clearance, rather
than linear length in the weight parameter. The base local constants
remain finite on compact subsets of $\mathfrak F$. A two-scale entrance
law at that endpoint requires a different cone with one-sided root
coordinates. The complete entrance theorem here is for the weight wall.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[An exact wall datum]\label{ex:wall-exact}
Take $D=1$, $r=3/5$, $s=11/20$, $\pi=1/10$, the rank-one columns and
clocks in Section~\ref{sec:cubic}, and equal design weights. Then
\[
 \alpha_0=\frac{547}{675},\quad c_*=\frac{256}{135},\quad
 a=\frac{44}{75},\quad b=\frac{11}{15},\quad
 B=\frac3{25},\quad D_*=\frac2{15},\quad\chi=\frac3{64},
\]
and $v_f^*=(1457,3403)^{\mathsf T}/4860$. Exact rational evaluation gives
\begin{align*}
 5975.429249985072&<C_{\loc}(\theta_0)<5975.429249985073,\\
 2.131024914\cdot10^{-6}&<\mu_E<2.131024915\cdot10^{-6}.
\end{align*}
The empty positive support is admissible in each base program and in
the remote program. The ancillary exact records retain the rational
Gram matrices, squared entrance constant, fourth power of the local
constant and isolating intervals. The decimals follow from rational
power comparisons, not optimization tolerances. They illustrate two
different, poorly conditioned scales; they do not test the universal
quantifiers of the family theorems.
\end{example}

\subsection*{Three stability questions}
Multiplicity and one-sided boundary constraints determine the weights
in Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-atlas}. Exact identification in the cubic family
is instead determined by \eqref{eq:I}. Conditioning of a fixed local
law is measured by the positive Fisher programs. At the weight wall,
identification fails while those programs stay coercive and the local
square-root exponent persists. In Example~\ref{ex:crossover}, by
contrast, the exponent changes and the linear constant diverges.
The joint atlas retains these distinct limiting operations rather than
identifying their strata.
