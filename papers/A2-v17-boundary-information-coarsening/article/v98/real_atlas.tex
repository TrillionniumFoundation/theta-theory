\section{Real monomial data in parameter families}\label{sec:real}
We first work with a compact semialgebraic model $X$, a continuous
semialgebraic probability map $F$, and a continuous semialgebraic monic
polynomial $A_x$ of fixed degree $n$. Complex coefficients are represented
by their real and imaginary parts. Suppose $A_x=A_0$ on $F^{-1}(P)$,
and write $A_0(z)=\prod_r(z-r)^{m_r}$. Disjoint isolating discs and
compactness give, on a sufficiently small closed observation neighbourhood,
unique monic cluster factors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clusters}
 A_{r,x}(r+z)=z^{m_r}+\sum_{a<m_r}c_{r,a}(x)z^a,
 \qquad k_{r,a}=m_r-a.
\end{equation}
Multiplication of coprime monic factors has invertible differential.
Consequently these factors are analytic in the total coefficients; their
graphs, including the isolating conditions, are semialgebraic.

\begin{definition}[Input and families]\label{def:input}
An algebraic input is a finite first-order polynomial presentation of
$X,F,A,P$, the design weights and the isolating data. A real constant
is given by an integer polynomial and a rational isolating interval;
a complex constant is a pair of such real constants. All inequalities,
denominator exclusions and choices of algebraic branches are included.
A fixed-format family has a semialgebraic parameter space $\Theta$,
a semialgebraic $X\subset\Theta\times K$ with $K$ compact and compact
fibres $X_\theta$, continuous semialgebraic maps $F,A$ on $X$, and a
semialgebraic choice $P_\theta\in F_\theta(X_\theta)$. The degrees and
numbers of coordinates are fixed. Numerical algebraic output refers
to algebraic parameter values. No complexity bound is asserted.
\end{definition}

Adjoin nonnegative square-root coordinates and set
\[
 R_{je}=\sqrt{w_j}(\sqrt{F(x)_{je}}-\sqrt{P_{je}}),\quad
 G=\sum_{j,e}R_{je}^2,\quad
 H_{r,a}=(\Re c_{r,a})^2+(\Im c_{r,a})^2.
\]
They are polynomial functions on the resulting compact real graph
$\Gamma$. This presentation includes zero probability cells.

\begin{definition}[Real monomial presentation]\label{def:monomial}
Such a presentation is a finite collection of smooth real algebraic
sources and proper maps, collectively covering a neighbourhood of $G=0$
in $\Gamma$, with finite algebraic etale coordinate presentations.
Smaller real coordinate boxes are taken about the inverse image of $G=0$.
On each algebraic source component either $G\equiv0$, in which case
all $H\equiv0$, or
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monomial}
 G=u\prod_i|y_i|^{a_i},\qquad
 H_{r,a}\equiv0\quad\hbox{or}\quad
 H_{r,a}=v_{r,a}\prod_i|y_i|^{b_{r,a,i}}.
\end{equation}
The units are positive and bounded above and below on the smaller closed
boxes. A contributing divisor has $a_i>0$ and a real point away from
the other divisors, with an admissible real transversal on which $G>0$.
An identically zero $H$ has order $+\infty$; an exact component $G\equiv0$
is recorded separately and never enters an order ratio. Properness is
asserted of the source maps, not of an open coordinate box by itself.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The algebraic spreading interface}
We use characteristic-zero resolution and principalization in the forms
of \cite[Theorems 1.1 and 1.3]{BM08}. The first resolves a variety over
any characteristic-zero field by finitely many smooth-centre blow-ups,
isomorphically off its singular locus. The second principalizes a
nonzero ideal on a smooth variety, compatibly with a normal-crossing
boundary. Applied to a product of finitely many nonzero functions, it
makes every factor a monomial times a unit in common coordinates.
These are absolute theorems over a field. The following lemma specifies
the additional family argument; arbitrary specialization is not inferred
from functoriality for smooth maps.

\begin{lemma}[Spreading a finite resolution certificate]\label{lem:spread}
Let $S$ be an integral affine variety over a characteristic-zero field
$k$, let $K=k(S)$, and let $Y\to S$ be of finite type. Suppose that over
$K$ one has a finite descending chain of reduced closed subvarieties
\[
 Y_{0,K}=Y_{K,\mathrm{red}}\supset Y_{1,K}\supset\cdots
 \supset Y_{q,K}=\varnothing,
\]
projective maps $Z_{j,K}\to Y_{j,K}$ with smooth sources, isomorphisms
over $Y_{j,K}\setminus Y_{j+1,K}$, and, on each $Z_{j,K}$, a finite
sequence of smooth-centre principalizations of specified functions.
There is a nonempty algebraic open subset $S^\circ\subset S$ on which
these data extend with the following properties on every fibre:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the closed chain ends in the empty set and the indicated
regular-locus maps remain isomorphisms;
\item each $Z_j\to S^\circ$ and each centre in its subsequent
principalizations is smooth, and the principalization blow-ups commute
with formation of the fibre;
\item the final sources are smooth over $S^\circ$, all indicated divisor
intersections are smooth of their prescribed relative codimensions,
and the same monomial identities, integer orders, and invertible units
hold on a finite relative etale atlas;
\item functions identically zero on a generic source component remain
identically zero on that component; a function represented by a unit
times a relative normal-crossing monomial cannot become identically
zero on a component of a retained fibre.
\end{enumerate}
The exceptional base and all displayed data can be constructed from
finite algebraic presentations over an effective field $k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Replace $S$ by a dense smooth open subset. Every scheme, morphism,
closed immersion, inverse morphism and unit in the generic certificate
uses finitely many polynomial equations with coefficients in $K$.
Clear their denominators, take closures of the $Y_j$, and spread the
projective maps by their projective equations. Spread the inverse maps
on $Y_j\setminus Y_{j+1}$ as well. Their two composition identities,
and the equality $Y_q=\varnothing$, hold after one further localization:
on each affine chart the relevant equality is an equality of finitely
presented ideals or morphisms that holds after tensoring with $K$.
The finitely many denominators witnessing those equalities can be
inverted simultaneously. The reduced structure is immaterial to real
points; fibrewise reducedness may also be imposed on the generic
geometrically reduced pieces after shrinking.

For clarity about blow-up base change, use the actual ideal of each
spread centre. On its finitely many affine charts, generic freeness \cite[Tag 051S]{Stacks} is
applied to the ambient algebra and to the associated graded algebra
$\bigoplus_{m\ge0}I^m/I^{m+1}$, a finite-type algebra. After localization
both are flat over the base. The successive exact sequences of the
$I$-adic filtration show that every quotient by $I^m$ is flat. Thus
formation of every $I^m$ commutes with passage to a fibre. Formation of
the Rees algebra and its projective spectrum therefore commutes with
that passage. Repeat this argument for the finitely many blow-ups.
This explicitly rules out an unsupported base-change assertion for
blowing up a singular family.

The nonsmooth locus of each source or centre, and the loci where a
divisor intersection has the wrong rank or codimension, are determined
by finitely many Jacobian and nonvanishing conditions on affine charts.
For the final smooth sources, choose generic etale coordinates adapted
to the normal-crossing divisors. Spread their equations, their etale
Jacobians, the identities $f=u\prod y_i^{n_i}$, and the inverse units.
The complement of these finitely many charts is also included in the
bad locus. Every such bad locus has empty generic fibre. Its image in
$S$ is constructible by Chevalley's theorem \cite[Tag 054K]{Stacks}
and omits the generic point. A constructible subset
of an integral noetherian space which is dense contains an open subset,
and hence the generic point. The closure of each bad image is therefore
proper. Remove the union of these finitely many closures. This argument
uses constructibility, not properness of the original map $Y\to S$.

All the asserted ranks, identities and coverings now hold fibrewise.
A generically zero function is retained by its zero identity. A nonzero
monomial identity has an invertible unit and divisors of relative
codimension one; their union contains no fibre component. Such a
function cannot become identically zero on that component. Any parameter
where that happens belongs to the removed locus.

Ideal arithmetic, localization, the singular-locus tests, elimination
for constructible images, and generic flatness can be performed by finite
algebraic calculations. For example, generic Groebner bases with their
nonzero denominators give a valid open set; it need not be the largest
one. The lemma claims a constructible certificate and an open set, not
a canonical minimal exceptional set.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Relative real principalization]\label{lem:relative}
On the locus of a fixed-format family where the exact target polynomial
is unique, there is a finite semialgebraic stratification with fixed
cluster multiplicities, a fixed finite collection of algebraic source
chart presentations, and fixed integer orders and real-accessibility
flags as in Definition~\ref{def:monomial}. The source maps are fibrewise
proper real covers. Charts, units and the sizes of coordinate boxes may
vary with the parameter. On every compact subset of a stratum the cover
is usable over a common sufficiently small observation neighbourhood.
The construction is effective for the presentations of
Definition~\ref{def:input}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The real graph and its algebraic base.}
Apply semialgebraic triviality to the graph family and refine the base
into Nash strata. Since its fibres are compact, a product trivialization
makes the restricted graph map proper: the inverse image of a compact
set is homeomorphic to that set times a compact fibre. Partition by
cluster multiplicities and select disjoint isolating discs. Their radii
and a smaller observation radius can be chosen positive semialgebraically.
Refine until these choices and the labelled distinct roots are Nash.
The chosen cluster graph over a stratum is again proper after this
smaller closed observation restriction.

A Nash stratum is an open semialgebraic subset of the smooth real locus
of its Zariski closure after a finite refinement. Use the corresponding
integral algebraic component $S$ as base and retain the original real
sign conditions throughout. When a root label is algebraic over the
parameter functions, adjoin its coordinates and defining equations.
After removal of the discriminant locus this is a finite algebraic base
cover. Real branch selection on Nash cells selects precisely the intended
labelled roots; parameters on its exceptional locus are treated in the
induction below. No choice of a complex branch is counted as a real
parameter lift.

The graph restricted to this real base is relatively closed. Its
ordinary semialgebraic closure in the ambient algebraic space has the
same fibres over the stratum. Express that closed set as a finite union
of basic closed sets. On each piece replace every inequality $p\ge0$
by $p=s_p^2$. Projection of real points of this algebraic lift is exactly
the basic closed set, not a superset obtained by discarding signs.
Over a compact subset of the stratum the original graph is compact;
the slack coordinates are bounded by the square roots of maxima of
polynomials on that compact graph. The real lift is consequently proper
there. All subsequent maps factor through this lift. This is the
compatibility interface for the stochastic inequalities and square roots.

\emph{Construction over the generic fibre.}
For each lifted algebraic family over $S$, remove components not
dominating $S$ temporarily and pass to $K=k(S)$. Work with its reduced
generic fibre. Resolve that fibre, isomorphically over its regular locus.
Resolve its reduced singular locus separately, then its singular locus,
and continue. Dimension strictly decreases, so this gives a finite
closed chain ending in the empty set, and maps of precisely the type in
Lemma~\ref{lem:spread}. On each smooth source separate the functions that
are identically zero and principalize the product of all the remaining
$G,H$. A product with normal crossings gives simultaneous monomial
identities for every factor. The absolute constructions over $K$ are
finite, so Lemma~\ref{lem:spread} applies.

\emph{Coverage of every retained real fibre.}
Let $y$ be a real point of a lifted fibre over $S^\circ$. Choose the
unique first member of the closed chain for which
$y\in Y_j\setminus Y_{j+1}$. The spread isomorphism gives a real point
of the smooth fibre $Z_j$ above $y$. Each subsequent principalization
blows up a smooth real centre in a smooth real source. Above a real
centre point its fibre is a real projective space, which is nonempty;
elsewhere it is an isomorphism. These blow-ups do not lose real points.
Their composition, and the finite disjoint union over $j$, is proper
onto the real lifted fibre. A real singular point with no real generic
branch is covered by a later member of the chain. It is not silently
lost in a resolution of the top-dimensional component.

\emph{Special and vertical components.}
The closures of the images of nondominating original components are
proper algebraic subsets of $S$. Add them to the exceptional set of
Lemma~\ref{lem:spread}. On the retained open set the spread chain and
its isomorphisms cover every fibre point, including any component of a
fibre: this is stronger than merely resolving a generic component.
Parameters at which a function becomes identically zero, a chart ceases
to cover, or an additional vertical component occurs are either already
covered by those fibrewise certificates or belong to the exceptional
set. Recompute the lift and the entire generic construction on each
irreducible component of that exceptional set, with its reduced base.
Its dimension is smaller. There are finitely many components at each
stage and at most $\dim S+1$ stages along a branch. This proves finite
termination and a finite total collection of source charts and orders.
One does not reuse generic nonzero flags on a special fibre.

\emph{Descent, accessibility and neighbourhood sizes.}
Restrict the finite algebraic label covers to the selected real Nash
branches. On each such branch the fibre is the original labelled graph;
projecting back changes neither an integer order nor a real transversal.
If several labels are retained, take their finite union and the finite
permutation quotient. Real-empty source components are discarded. On a
smooth component with real points, the exact-fibre condition forces every
$H$ to vanish identically whenever $G$ does, since its real regular locus
is Zariski dense. The existence of a generic real point of a proposed
divisor, away from all other divisors and in the chosen coordinate chart,
is a first-order condition in the parameter and source coordinates.
Partition the base by these finitely many conditions. At such a point
an inverse etale chart gives a real transversal $y_i=t$ with other
coordinates fixed. All its points still project through the square-slack
lift, so it is admissible. A complex divisor with no such real point is
not recorded.

For a fixed parameter, compactness of the inverse image of $G=0$ gives
finitely many smaller coordinate boxes on which the units have positive
bounds. Properness then gives $G\le\varepsilon_0$ inside their images:
a contrary sequence with $G\to0$ would have a lifted accumulation point
above $G=0$ outside the chosen neighbourhood. Over a compact parameter
set the same argument applies to the proper family of source maps.
Equivalently, positive radii and unit bounds may be selected
semialgebraically and made continuous by refinement. Their minima and
maxima on that compact set provide the required uniform bounds.

Absolute constructive resolution, the finite spreading certificate,
induction on base dimension, and real quantifier elimination provide all
these representations. Coordinate-box selection can alternatively be
implemented by enumeration of algebraic neighbourhoods and verification
of covering and unit bounds by real elimination; existence and compactness
prove termination. This completes the construction rather than assuming
a simultaneous resolution on an unstratified base.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-centre real cover]\label{prop:real-cover}
Every graph $\Gamma$ above has a real monomial presentation. Exact pieces
and lower-dimensional real loci are included. Every recorded divisor
has a real admissible attaining arc.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{lem:relative} to a one-point base. The real-coverage
and transversal parts of its proof give the stated conclusions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Intrinsic scalar orders]\label{cor:orders}
For a nonrigid cluster coefficient put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exponent}
 \beta_{r,a}=\min_{\text{recorded real divisors}}\frac{b_{r,a,i}}{a_i},
 \qquad \alpha=\min_{r,a}\frac{\beta_{r,a}}{k_{r,a}}.
\end{equation}
Either the target is constant on an observation neighbourhood, or
$\alpha>0$ is rational and $\omega_P(t)\asymp t^\alpha$.
Every finite coefficient minimum is attained by an admissible arc.
The numbers are independent of the monomial presentation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On each nonexact box, $b_i\ge\beta a_i$ for $a_i>0$ and $b_i\ge0$
otherwise. Unit bounds give $H\le C G^\beta$, hence
$|c_{r,a}|\le C h_w^\beta$. A minimizing real transversal has
$G\asymp u^{a_i}$ and $H\asymp u^{b_i}$ and attains this order.
The inclusion $\{G=0\}\subseteq\{H=0\}$ forces positive relevant
orders. If no nonzero $H$ occurs near $G=0$, the target is locally rigid.
For a monic polynomial of degree $m$, its largest root modulus is
comparable to $\max_{a<m}|c_a|^{1/(m-a)}$: elementary symmetric
identities give one inequality and domination of the lower terms by
$z^m$ outside a fixed multiple of this maximum gives the other.
The centre lies in every ball, so diameter is between maximal centre
displacement and twice that displacement. Finally the optimal exponent
in the real coefficient inequality is intrinsic, proving independence.
The divisor-ratio calculation itself is classical; compare \cite{BE09}.
\end{proof}

\section{Joint specialization and uniform spectral asymptotics}\label{sec:joint}
\subsection{Closure with the parameter fixed}
On a nonrigid identified stratum write $\alpha=p/q$ in lowest terms.
Let $\Psi(\theta,\varepsilon,x,U,V)$ denote graph membership and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted}
 R_{je}(\theta,x)=\varepsilon^q U_{je},\qquad
 c_{r,a}(\theta,x)=\varepsilon^{p k_{r,a}}V_{r,a},\qquad
 \sum_{j,e}U_{je}^2\le1.
\end{equation}
Complex equalities stand for two real equalities. Define the family by
\begin{align}
 (\theta,U,V)\in\mathcal J\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 &\theta\in S\ \text{and}\ \forall\rho>0\ \exists\varepsilon,x,U',V':
 \nonumber\\
 &0<\varepsilon<\rho,\quad\Psi(\theta,\varepsilon,x,U',V'),\quad
 \|(U',V')-(U,V)\|^2<\rho^2.\label{eq:closure-formula}
\end{align}
Write $\cI_0(\theta)=\mathcal J_\theta$ and
$\cC_0(\theta)=\pr_V\cI_0(\theta)$. There is no quantified $\theta'$
in this formula. Taking closure of the union while allowing the centre
to drift would be a different operation.

\begin{theorem}[The definable joint initial family]\label{thm:joint}
The sets $\cI_0(\theta)$ and $\cC_0(\theta)$ form semialgebraic families
of nonempty compact sets. The coefficient images of the radius-$t$
observation ball, scaled by $c_{r,a}/t^{\alpha k_{r,a}}$, converge in
Hausdorff distance to $\cC_0(\theta)$ for every fixed $\theta$.
If
\[
 Z_r(V)=Z\left(z^{m_r}+\sum_{a<m_r}V_{r,a}z^a\right),\qquad
 \mathcal L_0(\theta)=\{(Z_r(V))_r:V\in\cC_0(\theta)\},
\]
then the correspondingly scaled cluster-root images converge to
$\mathcal L_0(\theta)$. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-diameter}
 \omega_\theta(t)=C(\theta)t^\alpha+o(t^\alpha),\qquad
 C(\theta)=\max_{V,V'\in\cC_0(\theta)}\max_r
                 d_\infty(Z_r(V),Z_r(V'))\in(0,\infty).
\end{equation}
This is a joint feasible image, not a Cartesian product of separate
coefficient envelopes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:orders} bounds all scaled coefficient coordinates
for sufficiently small $t$; $U$ is already bounded. The defining closure
is therefore compact and nonempty. First-order elimination gives its
semialgebraicity with $\theta$ as a free parameter. Bounded graph
coordinates can be eliminated after closure: every convergent sequence
of scaled coordinates has a subsequence of graph coordinates converging
in the compact fibre. Thus projection does not lose an initial point.

Fix $\theta$ and a point $V$ of the outer limit. The distance from $V$
to the positive-radius coefficient section is a bounded nonnegative
semialgebraic function of $t$. It has a subsequence tending to zero.
One-variable monotonicity gives limit zero. A finite net of the compact
outer limit gives the inner Hausdorff inclusion. If the outer Hausdorff
inclusion failed, bounded scaled coordinates would give an accumulation
point outside the defining outer limit, a contradiction. This proves
convergence for the entire ball, not just convergence along chosen arcs.
Continuity of monic root multisets on bounded coefficient sets gives
the asserted root-set limit.

Let $\Delta$ be the least distance between distinct base roots. For
small $t$ all roots in a cluster lie in its $\Delta/4$ disc. A matching
within clusters has error tending to zero; a matching across clusters
has an edge of length at least $\Delta/2$. An optimal matching therefore
stays within clusters. Translation and scaling within each cluster are
exact. Taking diameter in the maximum cluster metric proves
\eqref{eq:joint-diameter}. A minimizing coefficient transversal in
Corollary~\ref{cor:orders} prevents the limiting diameter from being zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An explicit uniform asymptotics lemma}
\begin{proposition}[Semialgebraic decay on strata]\label{prop:decay}
Let $e(\theta,t)\ge0$ be semialgebraic for
$\theta\in S$ and $0<t<\tau(\theta)$, where $\tau$ is positive and
semialgebraic. Suppose $e(\theta,t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$ for each
fixed $\theta$. There is a finite Nash stratification of $S$ such that,
on every stratum, some rational $\nu>0$ and positive continuous
semialgebraic $\tau_0(\theta)$ satisfy
\[
 0<t<\tau_0(\theta)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e(\theta,t)\le t^\nu.
\]
In particular the estimate is uniform on compact subsets of the stratum.
The exponent need not be the sharp decay exponent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Eliminate quantifiers in a formula for the graph of $e$. It uses a finite
list of polynomials in $(\theta,t,y)$. For fixed $\theta$, discard the
polynomials that specialize identically to zero. A one-dimensional
single-valued graph in the $(t,y)$ plane must, near each of its points,
lie on a zero of one of the remaining polynomials: otherwise all signs
are locally constant in two dimensions and the formula cannot define a
single-valued graph. Near $t=0$ finitely many semialgebraic pieces suffice.
The eventual-zero case is separated by the formula
$\exists a>0\ \forall t\,(0<t<a\Rightarrow e(\theta,t)=0)$.

In the other case, one-variable algebraic branch expansion gives
$e(\theta,t)=a_\theta t^{r_\theta}+o(t^{r_\theta})$ with
$a_\theta>0$ and $r_\theta>0$ rational. If a polynomial
$\sum p_{ij}(\theta)t^iy^j$ vanishes on that branch, the smallest
number $i+r_\theta j$ occurs at least twice. Hence $r_\theta$ is among
the positive ratios $(i-i')/(j'-j)$ formed from two distinct monomials
of the fixed finite polynomial list. Specialization may remove monomials
but cannot create a new support. There are consequently only finitely
many candidate positive orders, uniformly over all parameters. This
argument supplies the needed uniform lower bound on orders; pointwise
convergence alone would not supply it.

Choose rational $\nu$ smaller than the least candidate order; use
$\nu=1$ on an eventual-zero piece if there is no candidate. Then for
each parameter $e(\theta,t)\le t^\nu$ eventually. Since $\nu$ is rational,
this eventual inequality, with positive-power auxiliary variables, is a
first-order real formula. Semialgebraic choice supplies a positive
$\tau_0\le\min(\tau,1)$ for which it holds. A finite stratification
makes $\tau_0$ continuous and the strata Nash. Its minimum on a compact
subset is positive, which proves the uniform assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform spectral power law]\label{thm:uniform}
The identified nonrigid parameter locus has a finite Nash stratification
on which $\alpha$ is fixed, $\mathcal L_0$ has constant semialgebraic
topological type, and $C(\theta)$ is positive and Nash. On each compact
$H$ of a stratum, for some $\nu>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-law}
 d_H(\mathcal L_t(\theta),\mathcal L_0(\theta))=O_H(t^\nu),\qquad
 \omega_\theta(t)=C(\theta)t^\alpha+O_H(t^{\alpha+\nu}).
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal L_t$ is the exact clustered root image scaled by
$t^{-\alpha}$, and $d_H$ is Hausdorff distance for the maximum of the
cluster bottleneck metrics.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We specify the set-valued distance used in applying
Proposition~\ref{prop:decay}. Represent each cluster by all its labelled
root arrays through the elementary symmetric identities. For two arrays,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matching-formula}
 M_d(z,z')=\bigvee_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_m}
              \bigwedge_{i=1}^m |z_i-z'_{\sigma(i)}|^2\le d^2
\end{equation}
is a polynomial formula for bottleneck distance at most $d$. For complex
roots use real and imaginary parts. Taking the conjunction over clusters
represents the maximum metric. An upper bound $d$ on Hausdorff distance
is the conjunction of
\[
 (\forall z\in\mathcal L_t)(\exists z'\in\mathcal L_0)M_d(z,z'),
 \qquad
 (\forall z'\in\mathcal L_0)(\exists z\in\mathcal L_t)M_d(z,z').
\]
Together with minimality over $d\ge0$, this is a first-order formula for
a single nonnegative number. Compactness ensures the minimum exists.
Thus the Hausdorff error is semialgebraic in $(\theta,t)$. Fractional
powers of $t$ in $\mathcal L_t$ are encoded by their unique positive
algebraic roots. Theorem~\ref{thm:joint} says that this error tends to
zero pointwise. Proposition~\ref{prop:decay} gives its compact-uniform
power bound after refinement.

The family of leading coefficient sets and their root arrays is
semialgebraic, so Hardt triviality separately supplies constant topological
types. The diameter is specified by all pairs having distance at most
$C$ and some pair attaining $C$. Hence $C$ is semialgebraic and positive.
A semialgebraic function is Nash on a finite Nash stratification: stratify
its graph so that its projection has constant full rank, use the analytic
inverse function theorem, and repeat on the lower-dimensional exceptional
sets. Refine by this stratification and by the continuous positive
cluster-separation and observation-radius functions. On compact subsets
their positive minima exclude cross-cluster matchings uniformly.
Finally diameters of compact sets differ by at most twice their Hausdorff
distance. This gives the second assertion of \eqref{eq:uniform-law}.
Neither a rate nor Nash dependence has been inferred from topological
triviality alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A finite real spectral atlas]\label{thm:atlas}
A fixed-format family has a finite semialgebraic stratification retaining
these four distinct kinds of data:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the semialgebraic homeomorphism type and dimension of the exact
\emph{target coefficient fibre}, and of its root-multiset image;
\item on the identified locus, rigidity or the positive rational order
\eqref{eq:exponent}, the cluster multiplicities, and a finite real
monomial order list with fixed accessibility and minimizing indices;
\item on the nonrigid identified locus, the joint initial coefficient
and root-set types and the Nash leading diameter with the
compact-uniform remainder \eqref{eq:uniform-law};
\item finite algebraic descriptions of these objects. On a subfamily
where a finite Fisher-cone description is proved, its active supports
and leading-constant choices may be retained in the same refinement.
\end{enumerate}
On a nonidentified stratum, the modulus tends to the positive diameter
of the exact target root image. No general leading set is asserted to
be quadratic, and no local leading constant is asserted to exclude a
remote exact component.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exact target coefficient fibre is the compact image
$\{A_x:F_\theta(x)=P_\theta\}$, not the parameter fibre itself.
Apply Hardt triviality to this family and its root graph. Singleton
fibres are selected by a first-order formula. At fixed $\theta$,
decreasing compact observation balls converge in target image to the
exact image, by subsequence compactness; their diameters therefore
converge to its diameter. Apply Theorem~\ref{lem:relative} on the
singleton locus, Corollary~\ref{cor:orders} for the orders, and
Theorems~\ref{thm:joint} and \ref{thm:uniform} for the joint data and
uniform remainder. These are separate inputs to a common finite
refinement. On a proved Fisher subfamily there are only finitely many
support and comparison conditions, which can be added to that refinement.
\end{proof}

\section{Effective output and the role of computation}\label{sec:effective}
\begin{theorem}[Effective algebraic specialization]\label{thm:effective}
For an algebraic input, finite algorithms compute the rigidity decision,
the exponent $\alpha$ in the nonrigid case, quantifier-free presentations
of $\cI_0,\cC_0,\mathcal L_0$, and an integer polynomial with a rational
isolating interval for $C$. In parameter families the corresponding
strata and formulas are effectively describable. This assertion is
separate from the existence of the real cover and from any executable
finite regression in the accompanying material.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are two finite routes to the exponent. The geometric route uses
the algebraic interface of Lemma~\ref{lem:spread}, constructive
principalization, recursion on base dimension, and real accessibility
elimination, followed by the rational minima \eqref{eq:exponent}.
The following second route avoids treating the implementation of a
resolution algorithm as a prerequisite for numerical spectral output.

For each squared coefficient $H$ construct, by elimination, the graph
of the compact maximum
\[
 m_H(s)=\max\{H(x):x\in\Gamma,\ G(x)\le s\},\qquad s\ge0.
\]
Its graph is the explicit formula
\[
 s\ge0,\ y\ge0,\quad
 (\forall x\in\Gamma)(G(x)\le s\Rightarrow H(x)\le y),\quad
 (\exists x\in\Gamma)(G(x)\le s\ \wedge\ H(x)=y).
\]
The maximum is an output of quantifier elimination, not an oracle or an
input assumption. Detect eventual vanishing by a quantified formula.
Otherwise isolate its positive real algebraic branch at $s=0$ and use
Newton polygon and real branch selection to obtain its first Puiseux
order $\beta_H$. Since $G=h_w^2$ and $H=|c|^2$, this is exactly the
optimal coefficient exponent $\beta$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:orders}.
Take the finite minimum $\beta_H/k_H$. In a family, the finite candidate
orders come from the polynomial supports as in Proposition~\ref{prop:decay}.
For each rational candidate $b>0$, the formula
\[
 \exists c,C,\tau>0\ \forall s\in(0,\tau):
                   c s^b\le m_H(s)\le C s^b
\]
selects precisely the parameters with that order. Rational powers are
encoded by their positive roots. This gives an explicit elimination
test, including zero specializations, without quantifying over a real
exponent. All operations are over the encoded real algebraic field.

Substitute the resulting rational $p/q$ into
\eqref{eq:closure-formula}. Real quantifier elimination returns
$\cI_0$ and its coefficient projection. Introduce the labelled roots
by their symmetric identities and use \eqref{eq:matching-formula}.
The assertion that all pairs have distance at most $C$ and that a pair
attains $C$ defines a unique real number. Elimination and univariate
real-root isolation give its polynomial over the algebraic input field.
A primitive-element representation, field norm and renewed root isolation
give an integer polynomial and rational isolating interval selecting the
same number. These changes of representation use the arithmetic,
elimination and real-root algorithms of \cite{BPR06,BCR98}.

For completeness, the geometric route passes finite equations to
reduced decomposition, to characteristic-zero principalization, to the
finite spreading charts, and then to first-order real tests. Algebraic
factorization and ideal membership determine the identically zero and
vertical flags. Positive coordinate neighbourhoods, covering assertions,
and unit bounds are first-order conditions after algebraic chart
coordinates are adjoined. Finite branch selection and projection give
the base refinement. Thus this route, too, has specified input and output
representations. The semialgebraic-max route shows in addition that the
spectral-output algorithm does not depend on an undocumented software
implementation of this geometric route.
\end{proof}
