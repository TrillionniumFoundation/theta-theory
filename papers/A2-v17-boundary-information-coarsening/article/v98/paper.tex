\begin{abstract}
We study unordered polynomial spectra observed through stochastic channels
and an unknown scalar normalization. For compact semialgebraic models,
we construct a finite relative real monomial presentation and the joint
weighted initial coefficient fibre, with the datum held fixed in the
specialization. Its root-multiset image gives the exact leading bottleneck
diameter. In fixed-format families, a finite Nash stratification supports
constant rational exponents and compact-uniform power remainders.
Algebraic inputs admit finite exact descriptions of the leading sets and
constants. In the full-rank coprime binary model at fixed degree, these
objects have explicit formulas for every multiplicity and boundary pattern.
For a rank-deficient cubic family, we classify the entire exact fibre and
prove a coefficient inverse uniform through channel-rank changes and
identification walls. At a weight wall, a remote feasible corner enters
at distance $\mu_E\delta+O(\delta^2)$, while the base local Fisher constant
stays positive and finite. The resulting two-scale spectral transition
separates loss of identification from deterioration of local conditioning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A polynomial spectrum is an unordered collection, and its singular
perturbations are governed by relations among coefficients rather than by
independent coefficient bounds. When the observation is additionally
normalized and constrained by stochastic inequalities, an invisible
ambient direction need not correspond to any admissible alternative.
Two questions must therefore be kept separate: the geometry of all
nearby real observation fibres and the entrance of an exact component
which is remote in coefficient space. We give a joint real specialization
for the first question and an explicit stochastic transition for the second.

Let $X$ be a compact semialgebraic model, $F$ its continuous semialgebraic
probability map, and $A_x$ a continuous monic target polynomial. For a
datum $P$ with a unique exact target, let $c_{r,a}$ be the coefficient
of degree $a$ in the monic cluster at a root $r$ of multiplicity $m_r$.
On the real observation graph set $G=h_w(F(x),P)^2$ and
$H_{r,a}=|c_{r,a}|^2$. Real monomial orders give the intrinsic nonrigid
exponent
\[
 \alpha_P=\min_{r,a,i}\frac{b_{r,a,i}}{a_i(m_r-a)}.
\]
The scalar order calculation is classical. The leading spectral invariant
is not that finite list alone. It is the compact set obtained by the
\emph{simultaneous} substitutions
\[
 R=\varepsilon^q U,\qquad c_{r,a}=\varepsilon^{p(m_r-a)}V_{r,a},
 \qquad \|U\|\le1,\qquad \alpha_P=p/q,
\]
and real closure with $P$ fixed. Theorem~\ref{thm:joint} takes its
cluster-root image and identifies its exact bottleneck diameter. In
particular the construction retains inequality faces and relations among
coefficients that separate optimal scalar envelopes cannot recover.

For parameter families, three different ingredients are required.
Theorem~\ref{lem:relative} constructs relative real monomial data, including
special zero functions, vertical components, and lower-dimensional real
loci. Lemma~\ref{lem:spread} supplies the finite algebraic spreading
certificate on which that construction relies. Theorem~\ref{thm:joint}
defines the leading family by an explicit fibrewise quantified formula.
Finally Proposition~\ref{prop:decay} derives a uniform positive decay
power from finitely many polynomial supports and a continuous threshold
selection. Together they yield, on each compact subset $H$ of a finite
Nash stratum,
\[
 \omega_\theta(t)=C(\theta)t^\alpha+O_H(t^{\alpha+\nu}),\qquad
 \alpha,\nu>0,\quad \alpha\in\Q,\quad C\text{ positive and Nash}.
\]
Topological triviality is used for the topology of the fibres; it is not
used as a substitute for any of these metric or algebraic arguments.
Theorem~\ref{thm:effective} separately describes finite elimination
procedures for algebraic inputs.

The normalized experiment in Section~\ref{sec:model} has two monic
component polynomials with roots in $[0,D]$, positive mixture weights,
and binary column-stochastic channels. For fixed degree $d$, coprime
components and strictly positive invertible channels, at least $2d+1$
exterior clocks give an explicit whole-model atlas for every root
multiplicity and endpoint/interior pattern
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-atlas}). An interior repeated cluster gives exponent
$1/2$ and constant
\[
 C=\max_j 2\sqrt{(m_j-1)/m_j}\,\kappa_j^{-1/4},
\]
where $\kappa_j$ is a specified finite Fisher-cone minimum. With no
repeated interior cluster the exponent is one and the leading set is
polyhedral with a quadratic observation constraint. These assertions
concern all closed-model competitors, not only perturbations in the
chosen root-pattern stratum.

Rank deficiency requires a different inverse argument. For
$f=z(z-r)^2$, $g=(z-s)^3$, $0<s<r<D$, positive channels and invertible
second channel, define
\[
 B=(3D-r)s-2rD,\qquad
 E=(1-\alpha)(r+3s)(2r-3s)^2-4\pi r^3.
\]
The exact identifying locus is
$\{\det U\ne0\}\cup\{B<0\}\cup\{E<0\}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-fibre}). Proposition~\ref{prop:affine} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:pencil-isolation} give a quantitative coefficient inverse
through the rank locus, without dividing by $\det U$, $B$ or $E$.
The complete local square-root law is then computed by four support
tests for each of two constants. It extends through the identification
walls as a \emph{local} law.

At the weight wall $E=0$ with $B>0$, the additional exact pair is
$(f,(z-a)(z-b)^2)$ with $s<a<r<b<D$. Its spectrum is a positive
distance $D_*$ from the base spectrum. Proposition~\ref{prop:remote-cone}
constructs an exact real corner chart at this remote point. Theorem
\ref{thm:wall} gives its observation entrance distance
$d_{\rm rem}(\delta)=\mu_E\delta+O(\delta^2)$ with $\mu_E>0$.
For $t=\lambda\delta$ the whole-model diameter tends to zero at the
base square-root scale when $\lambda<\mu_E$ and tends to $D_*$ when
$\lambda>\mu_E$. On the other side the exact remote interval opens
with an explicit square-root spectral term. The local Fisher constant
remains finite throughout. This is distinct from a multiplicity crossover,
where even the local scaling exponent changes.

\subsection*{Classical inputs and the additional invariant}
Table~\ref{tab:comparison} separates scalar and topological inputs from
the joint metric output. Resolution, principalization and divisor-ratio
calculations are not claimed as new constructions. Related algorithmic
stratification in families is developed in \cite{ENV03}; our use requires
a proper cover of the actual real inequality graph and the finite
specialization certificate of Lemma~\ref{lem:spread}.

The comparison with H\`a uses version 2 of March 14, 2026. In particular,
Theorem 9.9 already supplies a non-toric finite-candidate chamber
principle; such a principle is not an absent antecedent. Even granting
these scalar results, they do not specify the simultaneous real coefficient
relations, their root-multiset image with its exact diameter, the
normalization and stochastic tangent constraints, or the distance at which
a remote exact component enters an observation ball. These are the outputs
retained in the present joint atlas and transition calculation.

\subsection*{Proof organization}
Section~\ref{sec:model} fixes the experiment and proves normalizer recovery.
Sections~\ref{sec:real} and \ref{sec:joint} prove the relative and joint
statements, followed by their effective algebraic interface in
Section~\ref{sec:effective}. Section~\ref{sec:multiplicity} gives the
explicit full-rank multiplicity atlas. Sections~\ref{sec:cubic} and
\ref{sec:wall} treat the rank-crossing inverse and the remote weight-wall
transition. The finite exact arithmetic for the displayed examples is
ancillary evidence for those examples, not a verification of the universal
family theorems.
