\section{An explicit multiplicity and boundary atlas}\label{sec:multiplicity}
Fix $d,D,\pi$ and $\ell\ge2d+1$. Let $\mathfrak M_d$ be the centres of
\eqref{eq:model} with strict weights, strictly positive invertible $U,V$,
and coprime component polynomials. No restriction is imposed on root
multiplicities or on contact with $0,D$. Competitors still range over the
entire closed model. Partition $\mathfrak M_d$ by the component assignment,
multiplicity and endpoint/interior status of each distinct root. At fixed
$d$ this is a finite semialgebraic partition. On a pattern stratum write
$r_j,m_j$ for its distinct roots and multiplicities, and $a(j)\in\{1,2\}$
for their component assignments.

For every cluster let $s_j$ denote its first-order sum displacement.
It is free at an interior root, nonnegative at $0$, and nonpositive at
$D$. For every repeated interior cluster let $X_j\ge0$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mult-jet}
 p_a(z)=-\sum_{j:a(j)=a}s_j\frac{f_a(z)}{z-r_j}
        -\sum_{\substack{j:a(j)=a\\r_j\in(0,D),\ m_j\ge2}}
                     X_j\frac{f_a(z)}{(z-r_j)^2}.
\end{equation}
Insert these polynomials into \eqref{eq:score}. Let $L$ contain the free
interior sum and stochastic columns. Let $D_0$ contain the nonnegative
endpoint sum columns (reverse the sign at $D$) and all $X_j$ columns.
With $W=\diag(w_j/P_{je})$, define the projected Gram matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mult-Q}
 Q=D_0^{\mathsf T}WD_0-D_0^{\mathsf T}WL
              (L^{\mathsf T}WL)^{-1}L^{\mathsf T}WD_0.
\end{equation}
For a repeated interior cluster $j$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mult-kappa}
 \kappa_j=\min\{v^{\mathsf T}Qv:v\ge0,\ v_{X_j}=1\}.
\end{equation}
All matrix entries are rational in the root, channel, weight and design
coordinates. Empty collections of free or cone columns have their usual
zero-dimensional meanings.

\begin{lemma}[Ordered cluster diameter]\label{lem:ordered}
For $m\ge2$, the set
\[
 \{y_1\le\cdots\le y_m:\ \sum_i y_i=0,\ \tfrac12\sum_i y_i^2\le X\}
\]
has diameter $\sqrt{2X(m-1)/m}$ in the maximum coordinate metric.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a coordinate $j$. Minimizing the squared norm under its value,
ordering and zero sum gives
\[
 \max y_j=\sqrt{\frac{2X(j-1)}{m(m-j+1)}},\qquad
 \min y_j=-\sqrt{\frac{2X(m-j)}{mj}}.
\]
For the maximum, the last $m-j+1$ coordinates are equal and the first
$j-1$ are equal; the minimum is analogous. This also covers the zero
endpoint extrema when $j=1$ or $j=m$. Put $a=j-1,b=m-j$.
For $a,b\ge1$, the assertion that the resulting coordinate width is
at most $\sqrt{2X(m-1)/m}$ is equivalent to
\[
 ab(a+b+2)^2\ge4(a+1)(b+1).
\]
Writing $S=a+b\ge2$ and $ab\ge1$, its difference is
$ab(S^2+4S)-4S-4\ge S^2-4\ge0$.
For $a=0$ or $b=0$ equality holds directly. An extreme vector with one
coordinate of absolute value $\sqrt{2X(m-1)/m}$ and the other $m-1$
coordinates equal attains the endpoint width against the zero vector.
The diameter of a subset in the maximum metric is its largest coordinate
width. This proves the formula, including possible simultaneous
constraints on other clusters when it is used below.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Multiplicity and boundary laws]\label{thm:mult-atlas}
Every centre in $\mathfrak M_d$ is exactly identifying. On each root
pattern stratum the following whole-model assertions hold uniformly on
compact subsets $H$.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(a)} If there is a repeated interior cluster, the exponent is
$1/2$. The complete leading root set consists of zero clusters at the
simple and boundary roots and ordered vectors $y_j\in\R^{m_j}$ at the
repeated interior roots satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mult-leading}
 \sum_i y_{j,i}=0,\quad X_j=\tfrac12\sum_i y_{j,i}^2,
 \qquad \min_{\text{endpoint sums }\ge0}v^{\mathsf T}Qv\le4.
\end{equation}
The $X_j$ in $v$ are the displayed variances. After grouping and scaling
roots by $\sqrt t$, the observation ball is within $O_H(\sqrt t)$ of
this set in Hausdorff distance. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mult-C}
 \omega_\theta(t)=C(\theta)\sqrt t+O_H(t),\qquad
 C(\theta)=\max_j 2\sqrt{\frac{m_j-1}{m_j}}\,\kappa_j^{-1/4}.
\end{equation}
Every leading point, including diameter-extremizing pairs, is realized
by stochastic alternatives.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(b)} If no interior root is repeated, the exponent is $1$.
For each simple interior cluster take a free real root increment, and
for each boundary cluster take an ordered vector of increments with
all entries nonnegative at $0$ or nonpositive at $D$. Let $s_j$ be
their sums and use \eqref{eq:mult-jet} without $X$ terms. The compact
set of these root increments with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-leading}
 \min_\eta\|S_\theta(p_1,p_2,\eta)\|_{I,\theta}^2\le4
\end{equation}
is the complete leading set $\cL_{\rm lin,\theta}$. Its diameter
$C_{\rm lin}(\theta)$ is positive, and
\[
 \omega_\theta(t)=C_{\rm lin}(\theta)t+O_H(t^2).
\]
The scaled-root Hausdorff error is $O_H(t)$.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(c)} The optimal coefficient orders of a cluster of multiplicity
$m$, with coefficient weight $k$, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-orders}
 \beta_k=\begin{cases}
 1,&r\in(0,D),\ k=1,\\
 k/2,&r\in(0,D),\ 2\le k\le m,\\
 k,&r\in\{0,D\},\ 1\le k\le m.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The real order list, boundary and multiplicity pattern, and Fisher
active supports admit a common finite semialgebraic refinement. On
its square-root strata $C^4$ is a selected rational function of the
root and experiment coordinates; the positive constants on the linear
strata are algebraic semialgebraic functions. Both are Nash after
refinement. Exact algebraic evaluation uses finite programs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:fullrank} gives exact identification and a uniform inverse
in coefficients and stochastic parameters. The partial fractions
$1/(z-r_j)$ and $1/(z-r_j)^2$ occurring in \eqref{eq:mult-jet} are
linearly independent at distinct roots, with their indicated pole
orders. The coefficient derivative, and hence its score, is injective
on these variables and the free stochastic coordinates. Thus $L$ has
full column rank and $Q$ is positive definite whenever it is nonempty.
The constrained minima exist, are positive, and have uniformly bounded
minimizers on $H$.

We first obtain coordinates for \emph{all} nearby competitors. Coefficient
recovery and analytic coprime cluster factorization give an $O_H(t)$
error in each cluster coefficient and in the stochastic parameters.
For an interior cluster, write its exact roots as
\[
 r_j+t s_j/m_j+\sqrt t\,y_{j,i},\qquad \sum_i y_{j,i}=0.
\]
The first coefficient bounds $s_j$, and the second bounds
$\sum_i y_{j,i}^2$, since for centred real roots their second elementary
symmetric function equals $-\tfrac12\sum_i y_{j,i}^2$. At an endpoint
all root displacements have the same sign. Their sum is $O_H(t)$,
so each is $O_H(t)$. These observations prove the upper bounds in
\eqref{eq:explicit-orders}. For the lower bounds, translate an interior
cluster by a nonzero order-$t$ amount for $k=1$; for $k\ge2$ split it
by $\sqrt t$ times a zero-sum real vector with nonzero $k$th elementary
symmetric function. The vector with $m-1$ equal nonzero coordinates
and the last their negative sum has this property for every $k\ge2$.
Its first nonzero coefficient is of order $t$ and the coefficient
inverse makes the observation distance comparable to $t$. At an
endpoint displace all roots by the same allowed order-$t$ amount.
This proves all lower orders, including the one-sided boundary case.
In root-arc parameter $\varepsilon$, the interior split has
$\operatorname{ord}G=4$, $\operatorname{ord}H_k=2k$, whereas translation
or an endpoint arc has orders $2,2k$. These are admissible witnesses
for the real order data, and \eqref{eq:explicit-orders} rules out any
smaller candidate.

For part (a), expansion of the exact root coordinates gives precisely
\eqref{eq:mult-jet} at order $t$. Terms involving three centred root
displacements are $O_H(t^{3/2})$; all higher ones and the bounded mean
and endpoint terms satisfy the same bound. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mult-expand}
 F_T(\xi)=P_\theta+tS_\theta+O_H(t^{3/2}),\qquad
 h_w(F_T(\xi),P_\theta)=\tfrac t2\|S_\theta\|_{I,\theta}
                          +O_H(t^{3/2}).
\end{equation}
This follows by Taylor expansion of the square-root probability vector
and the reverse triangle inequality; it does not require a nonzero score.
Membership in the ball implies $\|S_\theta\|\le2+M\sqrt t$.
Multiply all sum, variance and stochastic first-order variables by
$\rho=2/(2+M\sqrt t)$ and each centred root vector by $\sqrt\rho$.
This gives a point of \eqref{eq:mult-leading} within $O_H(\sqrt t)$ of
the scaled competitor. Endpoint and simple clusters contribute only
$O_H(\sqrt t)$ in these scaled coordinates.

Conversely take a point of \eqref{eq:mult-leading} and bounded minimizing
endpoint and free variables. Use the displayed interior root arcs;
at an endpoint realize its sum by equal one-sided displacements, and
use the prescribed order-$t$ stochastic increments. Scale first-order
variables by $\rho=1-M'\sqrt t$ and centred roots by $\sqrt\rho$.
Strict weight and channel margins and cluster separation on $H$ make
these genuine closed-model alternatives. For a sufficiently large
$M'$, \eqref{eq:mult-expand} puts them inside the observation ball.
This is the reverse Hausdorff inclusion, with the same uniform error.

Homogeneity gives $X_{j,\max}=2/\sqrt{\kappa_j}$. The marginal projection
of the leading set at cluster $j$ includes every ordered zero-sum
vector with squared norm at most $2X_{j,\max}$: scale a minimizing
feasible vector for $X_j=1$ and choose the desired shape in that cluster.
Lemma~\ref{lem:ordered} therefore gives its diameter
$2\sqrt{(m_j-1)/m_j}\,\kappa_j^{-1/4}$. The diameter in the maximum
cluster metric is the largest marginal diameter; no independence of
the clusters is used. Cluster separation excludes cross-cluster optimal
matchings. Diameters differ by at most twice Hausdorff distance, proving
\eqref{eq:mult-C}.

For part (b), every root displacement is $O_H(t)$ by the same sum
argument at the endpoints and the simple-root inverse in the interior.
Divide the exact displacements by $t$. They belong to the stated
polyhedral sign and ordering cone. Taylor expansion now has remainder
$O_H(t^2)$. The first-order score controls their sums and all stochastic
variables; on the one-sided endpoint cones the sums control the
individual displacements. Hence the root set in \eqref{eq:linear-leading}
is compact. The radial argument with $\rho=1-Mt$ proves both Hausdorff
inclusions with error $O_H(t)$. An allowed nonzero displacement gives
positive diameter. This proves (b).

For (c), enumerate the subsets of the cone coordinates other than a
fixed $X_j$. On a support $A$, put
$v_A=-Q_{AA}^{-1}Q_{Aj}$ and require
$v_A\ge0$ and $Q_{bj}+Q_{bA}v_A\ge0$ off the support. Its value is
$Q_{jj}-Q_{jA}Q_{AA}^{-1}Q_{Aj}$. Convexity proves necessity and
sufficiency. All tests and selected values are rational, and
\[
 C^4=\max_j\frac{16(m_j-1)^2}{m_j^2\kappa_j}.
\]
There are finitely many patterns and supports. For the linear law,
\eqref{eq:linear-leading} is an explicit quadratic inequality on a
polyhedral root cone after projecting the free stochastic variables.
Its coordinate maxima and minima, and hence its diameter, have finite
real algebraic descriptions by \eqref{eq:matching-formula} or by face
optimization. Quantifier elimination gives the algebraic value at
algebraic input. Stratify the resulting semialgebraic functions and
refine with the order lists of Theorem~\ref{thm:atlas}. Positivity and
compact-uniform coercivity ensure continuity within each fixed pattern;
Nash refinement gives the final assertion. No continuity of $C$ is
asserted across a change of scaling exponent.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A multiplicity crossover]\label{ex:crossover}
Let $x\in[0,b]$, $b<1/2$, take
$F(x)=(1/2+x,1/2-x)$, and target $A_x(z)=(z-r)^2-x$.
At a fixed centre $a\in(0,b)$, differentiation of the square-root
probability vector gives
\[
 h(F(a+u),F(a))=\frac{|u|}{\sqrt{1-4a^2}}+O_a(u^2).
\]
The admissible $x$ interval therefore has endpoints
$a\pm t\sqrt{1-4a^2}+O_a(t^2)$, and its spectral diameter is
\[
 \omega_a(t)=\frac{\sqrt{1-4a^2}}{\sqrt a}\,t+O_a(t^2).
\]
At $a=0$ it instead has leading law $\sqrt t$. More precisely, uniformly
for $\lambda$ in compact subsets of $[0,\infty)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mult-transition}
 \frac{\omega_{\lambda t}(t)}{\sqrt t}
 \longrightarrow \sqrt{\lambda+1}-\sqrt{\max(0,\lambda-1)}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the feasible interval endpoints are
$t\max(0,\lambda-1)+O(t^2)$ and $t(\lambda+1)+O(t^2)$, with truncation
at zero, and $x\mapsto Z(A_x)$ has ordered roots $r\pm\sqrt x$.
This example belongs to the general real-graph class of
Section~\ref{sec:real}; it is not a claim about an unobserved restriction
of the closed binary model. Here the linear constant really diverges
at a multiplicity wall. Section~\ref{sec:wall} gives a different mechanism.
\end{example}
