\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

This paper isolates a boundary mechanism in planar dispersing billiards and
uses it in three directions: long-bridge asymptotics, geometric rigidity and
nonregular statistical information.  A long alternating collision bridge has
an exponentially small endpoint twist.  After normalization by that twist,
the bridge converges on a fixed positive offset collar to a nonlinear boundary
law described by two half-line stationary actions and two determinant
amplitudes.  The signed support of the same law recovers the unsymmetrized
half-line actions.  Their jets in turn recover, at every order, the labelled
jets of the two participating contacts through explicit determinant-one
blocks.  In the real-analytic category this local mechanism globalizes: a
finite registered collection of channels meeting every obstacle determines
the whole labelled table.  Finally, when the boundary law is observed on
fixed physical windows, its moving support generates a non-dominated local
experiment with a logarithmic information scale.

The deterministic theorem chain is
\[
 \boxed{\text{relative long-bridge boundary law}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{all-order signed contact inverse}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{finite-channel analytic table rigidity}}.
\]
The statistical theorem uses the same boundary action but is logically
separate:
\[
 \boxed{\text{fixed laboratory endpoint law}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{common-collar LAN representative}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{Gaussian endpoint experiment}}.
\]
The second chain is formulated on a declared observation sigma-field.  We do
not write a finite-sample likelihood ratio for a moving-support family when
absolute continuity fails, and we do not identify the endpoint/residual-time
coarsening used by the finite-to-boundary transfer with the complete growing
collision history.

\subsection{The nonlinear two-boundary law}

Fix a labelled closest-pair channel between two strictly convex contacts in a
smooth periodic dispersing billiard.  In local transverse coordinates write
the facing boundaries as
\[
 (-\psi_0(u),u),\qquad (g+\psi_1(v),v),
\]
with gap $g>0$.  No symmetry or equality of the two contacts is assumed.  Let
$W_j(u,v)$ be the stationary length of the selected alternating $j$-flight
bridge and put
\[
 E_j(u,v)=W_j(u,v)-jg,
 \qquad
 t_j=-\partial_{uv}W_j(0,0)>0.
\]
The quadratic Jacobi recurrence has a hyperbolic exponent $\gamma>0$ and
$t_j\asymp e^{-j\gamma}$.  The basic asymptotic statement is relative rather
than absolute.  For each fixed derivative order, on an endpoint box and a
positive offset collar independent of $j$, there are strictly convex
half-line actions $S_0,S_1$ and positive half-line amplitudes $B_0,B_1$ such
that, with $p=j\bmod2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v22-relative}
 E_j(u,v)=S_0(u)+S_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),
 \qquad
 \frac{-W_{j,uv}(u,v)}{-W_{j,uv}(0,0)}
 =B_0(u)B_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j)
\end{equation}
for some $0<\tau<1$.  The proof localizes the action and determinant defects
at the two boundary layers.  In particular, no extensive absolute error is
divided by the exponentially small reference twist.

The amplitudes $B_b$ also coincide with the normalized scalar linearizing
densities of the physical stable returns.  Thus the determinant factor and
the stable transport density are two realizations of the same boundary
object, while the nonlinear stationary-action theorem remains independent of
that dynamical interpretation.

\subsection{Signed endpoint support and the determinant-one inverse}

Integrate out residual time but retain the two signed endpoint positions.  If
an even number of flights is used so that the initial and terminal contacts
have the same type $b$, the limiting endpoint density is positive precisely
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v22-support}
                         S_b(u)+S_b(v)<d.
\end{equation}
Hence the support threshold satisfies
\[
 T_b(u,v)=S_b(u)+S_b(v),\qquad S_b(u)=T_b(u,0),
\]
and the two unsymmetrized half-line actions are recovered from observable
support data.  The odd contact information lost by the scalar integrated
profile is therefore lost by coarsening, not by a symmetry of the table.

Let $a_b=S_b''(0)$.  Physical onset gives $g$, and the half-line Hessian
identities give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v22-leading}
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}),
 \quad
 c_0=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_0/a_1},
 \quad
 c_1=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_1/a_0},
 \quad
 \kappa_b=\frac{c_b-1}{g}.
\end{equation}
Thus neither contact curvature is supplied.  Write
\[
 \psi_b(y)=\frac{\kappa_b}{2}y^2+
 \sum_{n\ge3}\frac{q_{b,n}}{n!}y^n,
 \qquad
 s_n=(S_0^{(n)}(0),S_1^{(n)}(0))^t.
\]
After the lower graph jets have been fixed, the new pair
$q_n=(q_{0,n},q_{1,n})^t$ enters $s_n$ through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v22-jet-block}
 D_{q_n}s_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1=1.
\end{equation}
Its determinant is one for every $n\ge3$.

The all-order statement requires more than the formal evaluation of the two
geometric sums.  Section~\ref{sec:v22-signed-rigidity} gives a quantitative
proof.  On a weighted sequence space $X_\rho$, the quadratic half-line Jacobi
inverse is bounded with a strict exponential margin.  Along the nonlinear
stationary orbit the Hessian satisfies
\[
 \|H_b(u,q)-H_b(0,q_2)\|_{X_\rho\to X_\rho}\le C|u|,
\]
so a Neumann argument gives a uniform inverse.  We then differentiate finite
truncations of the infinite action.  Stationarity cancels every interior
orbit variation and the remaining tail is $O(\rho^{2N})$, giving the exact
envelope identity in the half-line limit.  A separate homogeneous-degree
lemma shows
\[
                    \partial_{q_{r,m}}S_b(u)=O(u^m).
\]
This resolves the derivative-order issue directly: derivatives of the
stationarity equations may contain a graph derivative one order higher, but a
graph jet of order $m$ cannot enter an action jet of order $n<m$.  At $m=n$
only the linear half-line orbit contributes to the degree-$n$ term, yielding
\eqref{eq:intro-v22-jet-block}.  The finite signed-jet map is block lower
triangular with invertible quadratic block and determinant-one higher blocks;
its least singular value is uniformly positive on compact finite-jet sets.

Consequently all smooth labelled contact jets, odd and even, are recursively
determined without reflection symmetry, equality of the contacts or supplied
curvature.  For real-analytic contacts the recovered jets determine the
analytic graph germs and, by analytic continuation and the Frenet equations,
the connected participating boundary images.

\subsection{A finite-channel table-level consequence}

The local inverse has a global consequence in the analytic category.  Suppose
a fixed periodic cell contains finitely many labelled connected real-analytic
strictly convex obstacles.  A finite registered channel cover is a finite
collection of labelled channels whose endpoints meet every obstacle label.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-multichannel-table-rigidity} states that the onset and
the two same-type signed endpoint-law germs for every channel in such a cover
determine the entire labelled table in the common laboratory registration.
Indeed, one measured channel determines the complete analytic boundaries of
its two participating obstacles; a spanning cover therefore reaches every
obstacle.  If absolute placement is not supplied, fixing one initial oriented
contact frame leaves only one common Euclidean motion.

This table-level theorem uses data different from marked or enriched length
spectra.  Existing global rigidity theorems organize periodic-orbit lengths
and obtain global conclusions under their corresponding analytic,
symmetry or genericity hypotheses
\cite{BDKL,DSKL,Osterman,FinamoreLeguil}.  The present data are globally
sparser in channel labels but locally richer: each selected channel carries a
continuum signed endpoint-support germ.  We make no claim that either data set
is a coarsening of the other.  The point is instead that the determinant-one
boundary mechanism now has an explicit global geometric consequence.

\subsection{A non-dominated moving-boundary experiment}

The density in \eqref{eq:intro-v22-support} vanishes linearly at a moving
regular hypersurface.  For a vector family
\[
 f_\theta(x)=a_\theta(x)(w_\theta(x))_+,
 \qquad
 V=D_\theta w_\theta|_0,
\]
the intrinsic boundary matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v22-information}
 \mathcal J_\Sigma=
 \int_\Sigma\frac{a_0VV^t}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma,
 \qquad \Sigma=\{w_0=0\}.
\end{equation}
The singular part of the score is $V/w_0$, while the null density contributes
one factor $w_0$; the second moment therefore diverges logarithmically.

At finite sample size the family may be non-dominated: an outward motion of
the support gives the alternative positive mass where the null has none.
Section~\ref{sec:v22-vector-information} first bounds both types of
support-exclusive mass by $O(p_n\delta_n^2)$, then applies the
parameter-independent censoring collar
\[
 q_n=\delta_n(\log(1/\delta_n))^{1/4}.
\]
The resulting family is genuinely dominated by its null law, including its
positive censoring atom.  At
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v22-critical}
 np_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1
\end{equation}
it is uniformly LAN on compact local sets, while the original and censored
product experiments are at Le Cam distance
$O((\log(1/\delta_n))^{-1/2})$.  The forward and reverse comparison kernels
are both parameter independent.

We keep the levels of asymptotic theory separate.  The LAN expansion belongs
to the dominated representative.  Asymptotic equivalence transfers
contiguity and finite-subexperiment limits to the original family.  If
$\mathcal J_\Sigma$ is singular, the Gaussian shift is formulated on the
identifiable space
$\operatorname{Ran}(\mathcal J_\Sigma)$ and the local minimax theorem is
stated on that quotient; no informal inverse of a singular information matrix
is used.  The central-sequence estimator is explicitly a reference-model
local statistic, not a globally adaptive reconstruction algorithm.

The one-dimensional moving-endpoint rate has classical antecedents
\cite{Akahira1975a,Akahira1975b,Smith1985,IbragimovHasminskii,HiranoPorter}.
Our statistical use of it is the intrinsic hypersurface matrix, its vector
non-dominated experiment formulation, and its coupling to the billiard
boundary inverse.

\subsection{Fixed laboratory windows and multirate unknown geometry}

A physical local experiment cannot center its observation rule at an unknown
gap or silently re-center endpoint coordinates at parameter-dependent
contacts.  We therefore fix one Euclidean laboratory chart and use the
anchored local family
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v22-anchored}
 \Gamma_{0,\vartheta}=(-\psi_{0,\vartheta}(u),u),
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{1,g,\vartheta}=(g+\psi_{1,\vartheta}(v),v),
 \qquad
 \psi_{b,\vartheta}(0)=\psi'_{b,\vartheta}(0)=0.
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{sec:v22-anchored-realization} realizes every fixed finite jet
model by actual nearby billiard tables.  The statistical endpoint patch lies
inside the region on which the realization cutoffs equal one, and global
embeddedness and channel isolation persist after shrinking the parameter
ball.

For same-type endpoint designs we choose an even sequence
$j_n\in2\mathbb N$, $j_n\to\infty$.  The programmed windows are
\[
                         t_{j_n,\ell}=j_ng_0+d_\ell.
\]
Under the local alternative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v22-multirate}
 g=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a,
 \qquad
 \vartheta=\delta_nh,
\end{equation}
the physical excess is exactly $d_\ell-\delta_na$.  Thus gap and shape enter
the same first-order boundary velocity
\[
 U_b(u,v)(a,h)=a+D_\vartheta S_b(u)[h]+D_\vartheta S_b(v)[h].
\]
The information matrix at one same-type window is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v22-physical-information}
 \mathcal K_{b,d}
 =\int_{\{S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d\}}
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)U_b(u,v)^tU_b(u,v)}
 {|\nabla(S_b\oplus S_b)(u,v)|}\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}

The exact fixed-window billiard density is quantitatively compared with this
ideal linear support motion.  The support error is
$O(\delta_n^2+\delta_n/j_n)$ in $C^2$ and the amplitude error is
$O(\delta_n)$.  The linearly vanishing support stability estimate then makes
the product comparison negligible at
$k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1$.  The tangent isomorphism of the signed
jet map implies that finitely many positive same-type windows give a
positive-definite summed information matrix on every fixed finite labelled
contact-jet model.  The finite design may depend on the jet order and the
reference geometry.

The preparation caps are fixed before any compact local parameter set is
chosen.  Specifically,
\[
 N_{n,\ell}=\left\lceil
 \frac{4k_{n,\ell}}{p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)}\right\rceil.
\]
When $j_n\delta_n\to0$, the local success probability is uniformly
asymptotic to its reference value on each fixed compact set.  Therefore the
same cap sequence has exponentially small cap-failure probability on every
fixed compact local set for all sufficiently large $n$.  This order of
quantifiers is the one used in the subsequent local asymptotic minimax
statement.

\subsection{Observation levels and finite-bridge transfer}

The physical transfer is deliberately stated at its exact observation level.
For one preparation, the historical theorem replaces the raw collision
history by a coarsened record consisting of the selected two endpoints and
residual time, or a failure atom.  An adaptive policy produces a stopped
transcript containing every design, every such success/failure record and the
stopping time.  Under
\[
                         k_n\tau^{j_n}\longrightarrow0,
\]
the entire stopped transcript at this declared record level is uniformly
close in total variation to its boundary analogue.  A further common Markov
coarsening gives the endpoint-output experiment to which the Gaussian
information matrix is attached.

These distinctions matter.  The theorem is not a total-variation comparison
of the full growing collision array.  Nor is the endpoint-output information
matrix an efficiency bound for the richer stopped endpoint--time transcript.
The success probability contains the long-bridge factor
$\sinh(j\gamma)^{-1}$, so waiting counts may contain an additional and
possibly faster information channel.  Every failed preparation is charged;
the preparation cost and the statistical sigma-field are separate parts of
the theorem.

A charged pilot can be used to center subsequent windows, but equivalence is
claimed only for the post-pilot endpoint-output sigma-field.  For generic
nearby tables, motion of the contact origin and tangent frame contributes
additional nuisance velocities in the fixed laboratory coordinate system.
Those directions can be included in the abstract boundary experiment; they
are not silently discarded from the anchored information matrix.

\subsection{Organization}

Part I proves the long-bridge relative law, the all-order signed inverse and
the finite-channel analytic table theorem.  Part II develops the
non-dominated vector boundary experiment, realizes finite contact-jet models
by actual anchored tables, proves the fixed-window multirate theorem and
transfers it to actual finite bridges at the declared observation level.  The
appendix collects auxiliary proofs and retained applications.  Historical
modules remain in the repository for provenance, but the active theorem chain
is identified by the present introduction and the main source file.
