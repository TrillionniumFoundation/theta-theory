\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:geometric-results}

A long alternating collision bridge in a dispersing billiard has two
features which pull in opposite directions.  Its stationary action has a
stable local geometric limit, but the physical probability of seeing the
bridge decays exponentially with the number of flights.  The first problem
of this paper is therefore relative rather than absolute: does the full
nonlinear physical law converge, after the natural hyperbolic
normalization, on a contact collar whose size does not shrink with the
flight number?  The second problem is what remains of that law after the
record is coarsened.  We prove that these two questions are linked.  The
same determinant normalization which produces the boundary law also
identifies three different statistical regimes according to whether one
retains residual time, only the collision endpoints, or only the success
indicator.

The central result is a chain rather than a collection of unrelated
estimates.  First, the stationary bridge admits a relative determinant
factorization on a fixed collar.  Second, residual-time integration turns
this factorization into complete boundary-energy profiles and, at the same
time, changes the local singularity of the finite-versus-boundary
experiment.  Third, the resulting moving-support density has an intrinsic
logarithmic boundary information.  In the billiard model an exact
Jacobi-Hessian whitening makes its coefficient equal to one.  Consequently
coarsening the same physical record changes the critical comparison scale
from $q_j=e^{-j\gamma}$ to
$q_j^2\log(1/q_j)$, while the success bit alone is asymptotically blind at
the tangent level.  This observation-dependent transition is not obtained
by applying data processing to the complete-record estimate; the latter is
strictly nonsharp after residual time is removed.

\subsection{The nonlinear boundary law}
Fix an oriented closest pair and write $j$ for the number of alternating
flights, $g$ for its gap, and $\gamma>0$ for the associated hyperbolic
exponent.  In transverse endpoint coordinates let $W_j(u,v)$ be the
stationary length, $E_j=W_j-jg$, and let $-W_{j,uv}$ be the mixed endpoint
derivative entering the physical flux.  The endpoint Hessian remains
nondegenerate as $j\to\infty$, whereas $-W_{j,uv}$ is of order
$e^{-j\gamma}$.  Thus an absolute Hessian estimate is insufficient: after
division by the physical flux its error can grow.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-main-relative} resolves this by normalizing the exact
cofactor identity before taking the long-bridge limit.  There are half-line
actions $S_0,S_1$ and determinant amplitudes $B_0,B_1$, defined on a
common fixed neighborhood, such that every fixed mixed geometric and
offset derivative of the finite bridge converges relatively at an
exponential rate.  In particular, for $p=j\bmod2$,
\[
 \frac{2A\sinh(j\gamma)}{d^2}\Pr(E_{j,e}(d))
       =\mathcal F_p(d)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_p(d)=\frac{q_p}{\pi d^2}
 \int(d-S_0(u)-S_p(v))_+B_0(u)B_p(v)\,du\,dv,
\]
with $0<\tau<1$ and a collar $0\le d\le d_*$ independent of $j$.
The proof uses the alternating Jacobi Hessian, trace-class summability of
the two separated nonlinear end perturbations, and a common Morse domain;
it does not acquire a factor proportional to the number of interior
collisions.

The determinant amplitudes have an intrinsic dynamical interpretation.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v14-intrinsic-density} identifies their transport under
the physical first-hit maps, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:v15-determinant-product} identifies $B_b$ with the
normalized scalar linearizing density of the corresponding smooth stable
return.  This scalar linearization is classical; what is specific here is
the equality with the physical half-line Fredholm amplitude, including its
normalization and its role in the residual-time measure.

\subsection{From boundary laws to information loss}
The long-bridge normalization has a statistical consequence which is
invisible at the level of the limiting probability alone.  For the complete
tangent record---two endpoints together with residual time---the finite and
boundary densities agree on their overlap.  Their distinction is an
exclusive-support event of size $q_j=e^{-j\gamma}$, giving the erasure
transition proved in Theorems~\ref{thm:v7-exact-tv} and
\ref{thm:v8-supercritical}.

Removing residual time changes the geometry of the density at its moving
boundary.  The endpoint law is no longer uniform on a cap; it is a
positive-part quadratic density and vanishes linearly on the support
boundary.  This leads to an infinite ordinary score variance and a
logarithmic information scale.  The general statement is
Theorem~\ref{thm:v17-boundary-information}.  If
$f_t=a_t(w_t)_+$ has a regular moving support hypersurface
$\Sigma=\{w_0=0\}$, then
\[
 H^2(f_t,f_0)=\frac{\mathcal I_\Sigma}{4}t^2\log\frac1{|t|}+O(t^2),
 \qquad
 \mathcal I_\Sigma=
 \int_\Sigma\frac{a_0(\partial_t w_t|_{t=0})^2}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma.
\]
The coefficient is unchanged by replacing the defining function $w_t$ by
a positive smooth multiple.  For independent thinned observations the
finite critical experiment has a Gaussian likelihood limit and an exact
total-variation profile.  The supercritical conclusion is obtained instead
from multiplicative Hellinger affinity.  In particular, no claim that
support-exclusive observations are negligible is made for arbitrary
supercritical sequences.

For the billiard endpoint law, determinant-one whitening gives
\[
 f_\zeta(x)=\frac2\pi
 (1-e^{-\zeta}x_1^2-e^{\zeta}x_2^2)_+,
 \qquad \mathcal I_\Sigma=1,
\]
independently of unequal contact curvatures and of flight parity.  Since
$\zeta_j=2q_j+O(q_j^3)$,
\[
 H^2(f_{\zeta_j},f_0)
       =q_j^2\log(1/q_j)+O(q_j^2).
\]
Thus the endpoint critical scale is
$r_j=q_j^2\log(1/q_j)$ and not $q_j$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:v17-endpoint-physical}
transfers the tangent profile to actual positive offsets whenever
$\omega(d_j)=o(r_j)$, using the uniform physical tangent estimate and a
fixed critical subsample in the supercritical regime.

Corollary~\ref{cor:v17-coarsening-hierarchy} is the conceptual synthesis.
For the same fixed table, the same finite/boundary pair and the same raw
preparations, complete records, endpoint-only records and success bits
have different asymptotic comparison scales.  At the endpoint critical
scale the complete-record distance tends to one, the endpoint distance
tends to $2\Phi(\sqrt b)-1$, and the success-bit distance tends to zero.
At the complete-record critical scale the endpoint distance tends to zero.
Therefore coarsening and the long-bridge limit do not commute at the level
of the local experiment.  The phenomenon is a property of the physical
billiard law, not a choice of a supplied convergence certificate.

This statement should be distinguished from classical nonregular
moving-support asymptotics.  One-dimensional location models with
parameter-dependent support have a long history; the classification in
Akahira~\cite{Akahira1975a,Akahira1975b} and the singularity theory of
Ibragimov--Has'minskii~\cite{IbragimovHasminskii} are part of that
background.  Smith~\cite{Smith1985} proves, for densities behaving like a
power at a moving endpoint, that the linearly vanishing case has a normal
limit at a nonstandard rate.  Hirano--Porter~\cite{HiranoPorter} study
parameter-dependent support through limits of experiments and local
asymptotic minimax efficiency.  We do not claim priority for the existence
of nonregular normal limits or for parameter-dependent support.  What is
proved here, in the form needed by the billiard problem, is the intrinsic
multidimensional hypersurface coefficient, its independent thinning law,
and the exact way in which the Jacobi determinant cancellation converts
that general boundary mechanism into a three-level physical observation
hierarchy.

\subsection{Geometric determination}
The boundary laws also carry inverse geometric information.  The two
even long-bridge laws determine normalized energy profiles
$\mathcal V_0,\mathcal V_1$ on a common closed collar.  Equivalently they
determine the corresponding sublevel widths.  The mixed-type law is then
fixed by a Volterra convolution identity.  The inverse is function-valued:
it determines the complete symmetrized energy profile for arbitrary smooth
contacts, not merely a formal Taylor series.  It does not determine the
midpoint of two inverse branches for a general asymmetric contact, and no
such unrestricted rigidity claim is made.

Under the additional hypothesis that the two labelled contacts are
even and that the gap and the two labelled leading curvatures are supplied,
Theorems~\ref{thm:v12-two-contact} and~\ref{thm:v13-two-flight} give two
independent block-triangular finite-jet inverses, respectively from the
long-bridge laws and directly from the two oriented two-flight germs.  The
separating factors remain nonzero even when the two curvatures coincide.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-realization} realizes the resulting independent jets
by analytic periodic tables while keeping the selected leading hierarchy
fixed; Theorem~\ref{thm:v13-two-flight-observation} supplies positive
physical windows on which the finite-dimensional inverse is observable.
Analytic continuation after recovery of the analytic germs is classical.
These finite-jet statements and the general smooth profile theorem have
different hypotheses and are not conflated.

\subsection{Observation of the full invariant}
The integrated physical flux also admits a stable function-valued inverse.
On differences of limiting fluxes, a second derivative followed by a
weighted Abel half derivative gives a two-sided Lipschitz coordinate for
the profiles (Theorem~\ref{thm:v11-abel-inverse}).  For profiles bounded in
$C^{m-1,1}$, $m\ge4$, positive-node regularization exploits the two extra
derivatives of the integrated flux and yields the sufficient preparation
bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:v12-acquisition}.  Every failed preparation is
charged.  The self-calibrated version separately estimates the gap, area
and multiplier without introducing a spurious division by a small offset.
These are constructive upper bounds for the stated procedures, not claims
of a sharp minimax theorem for an unknown smooth table.

The adaptive and stopped comparison results are retained because they
show that the relative law survives data-dependent sampling when the
physical error is charged against the common observed history.  They are
logically downstream of the relative boundary theorem.  Likewise, the
finite-dimensional, calibration and auxiliary acquisition experiments in
the appendix remain available with their original hypotheses, but they do
not define the central thesis of the paper.

\subsection{Relation to rigidity results}
The information set here differs from marked-length or spectral data.
De Simoi--Kaloshin--Leguil~\cite{DSKL} recover analytic open dispersing
billiards under their symmetry and nondegeneracy assumptions from marked
periodic-orbit lengths.  Finamore--Leguil~\cite{FinamoreLeguil} obtain a
global rigidity theorem for finite-horizon Sinai billiards from an
enriched marked length spectrum.  Zelditch~\cite{Zelditch} recovers
analytic plane domains in specified symmetric classes from the Laplace
spectrum.  Our observations are selected near-onset collision
probabilities.  The general target is a pair of smooth boundary-energy
profiles, while the independent-contact analytic rigidity statement uses
additional evenness and labelled leading geometry.  None of the global
information sets above is claimed to be contained in ours.

Classical Schur complements, tridiagonal Green functions and the relation
between action Hessians and monodromy enter the proof
\cite{Hill,Meurant,Nabben}.  The substantive forward step is the uniform
nonlinear relative estimate after normalization by an exponentially small
physical mixed derivative, including its transfer through residual-time
integration.  The substantive statistical step is that the same physical
normalization allows the coarsened limiting experiments to be computed
sharply rather than bounded by contraction.

\subsection{Organization}
Part I proves the geometric localization and stationary-action formulas,
the uniform nonlinear relative boundary law, the intrinsic determinant
transport, physical transfer, and the geometric/profile inverses.  The
finite two-contact and two-flight results are placed there as consequences
of the forward structure under their additional hypotheses.

Part II begins with the general boundary-information theorem and the exact
billiard endpoint experiment, because these results describe what the
boundary law becomes under observation coarsening.  It then develops Abel
stability, deautoconvolution and regularized observation of the complete
profile.  The appendix retains the full auxiliary proof chain: earlier
acquisition constructions, adaptive/stopped and supercritical experiments,
finite-dimensional and smooth-remainder analyses, contact-jet arguments,
and the original collision-record formulas.  These materials remain
active where cited but are subordinate to the two main chains: relative
boundary geometry and observation-dependent boundary information.
