\section{Classical maximal rank and monomial comparison}\label{app:monomial}
The proof of the global codimension theorem does not invoke curve postulation. For comparison, the complete classical statement used for a single general space includes the injective range as well.
\begin{proposition}[Classical rational maximal rank]\label{prop:classical-maxrank}
For $4\le c\le n+1$, a general $c$-plane $U\subset H^0(\Pj^1,\mathcal O(n))$ satisfies
\[
 \rank(\Sym^2U\longrightarrow H^0(\Pj^1,\mathcal O(2n)))
    =\min\{\tbinom{c+1}{2},2n+1\}.
\]
The full-rank locus is a nonempty Zariski-open set defined over $\R$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Precise classical reduction]
Apply the maximal-rank theorem in \cite[Theorem 1.2]{Larson} to a general Brill--Noether curve with genus $g=0$, degree $n$, ambient projective dimension $r=c-1\ge3$, and restriction degree two. Its Brill--Noether number is $(r+1)(n-r)\ge0$. A general $c$-dimensional subseries of $H^0(\Pj^1,\mathcal O(n))$ is base-point-free and embeds $\Pj^1$ in $\Pj^r$: its projection centre avoids the secant and tangent locus of the rational normal curve because $r\ge3$. The space of such subseries is irreducible and parametrizes the general rational Brill--Noether map, up to the choice of a basis and source coordinates. Restriction of quadrics has source $\Sym^2U$ and target $H^0(\Pj^1,\mathcal O(2n))$, exactly the multiplication map above. This specializes the general theorem to the classical nonspecial rational range of \cite{BE85,BE87}. The complete series is immediate from monomials. Nonvanishing maximal minors in real matrix charts gives the real assertion. This proof attributes, rather than reproves, the classical injective-range theorem.
\end{proof}

For a monomial space $U_S=\Span\{t^a:a\in S\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sumset}
 U_S^2=\PP_{2n}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S+S=\{0,\ldots,2n\}.
\end{equation}
This is the restricted additive $2$-basis problem, distinct from an unrestricted polynomial subspace \cite{Yu,Kohonen14,Kohonen17}.
\begin{proposition}[Strict monomial obstruction and constructive bases]\label{prop:monomial}
If $n\ge3$ and \eqref{eq:sumset} holds, then $\binom{|S|+1}{2}\ge2n+2$. Thus no monomial normal space attains the native optimum at $(d,k)=(3,8)$ or $(5,11)$.

For $a\ge2$, $n\ge2a$, write $n=ba+r$, $0\le r<a$. The set
\[
 S=[0,a-1]\cup\{a,2a,\ldots,ba\}\cup[n-a+1,n]
\]
has $S+S=[0,2n]$ and $|S|\le2a+\lfloor n/a\rfloor-1$. In particular such bases exist with $|S|\le2\sqrt{2n}+O(1)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Representing $0,1,2n-1,2n$ forces $0,1,n-1,n\in S$. The distinct unordered pairs $(0,n)$ and $(1,n-1)$ coincide in sum, so at least one of the $\binom{|S|+1}{2}$ products is redundant. At the displayed native optima the pair count is exactly $2n+1$, respectively $15$ and $21$, making monomial recovery impossible.

For the construction, adding the multiples $0,a,\ldots,ba$ to $[0,a-1]$ covers $[0,ba+a-1]\supset[0,n]$. Adding them to $[n-a+1,n]$ covers $[n-a+1,2n-r]$. Adding the upper interval to itself covers $[2n-2a+2,2n]$, which overlaps because $r\le a-1$. The last multiple belongs to the upper interval, giving the cardinality bound. Choose an integer nearest $\sqrt{n/2}$ for the asymptotic bound, handling the finitely many smaller values by the full interval.
\end{proof}
For $k=3d+2$ one has the explicit family $S=[0,d]\cup[2d+1,3d+1]$, with complementary exponents $E=[d+1,2d]$. The three sum intervals are $[0,2d]$, $[2d+1,4d+1]$ and $[4d+2,6d+2]$. Each column of the interpolation matrix $J$ has one strict coordinatewise sign, as follows by expanding $d_i\prod_{a\ne i}(t-r_a)$ with positive roots. In particular a signed $Je_{d+1}$ is positive. Taking $T=J\Span\{e_a:a\in E\}$ realizes the monomial complement locally by Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-realization}; generic small loading perturbations give globally separated full-product realizations. The unrestricted fibre dimension for this family is $5d(d+1)/2$. The smaller sets $\{0,1,3,5,6\}$ and $\{0,1,2,5,8,11,12,13\}$ also satisfy \eqref{eq:sumset}; no optimal restricted-basis constant is claimed.

For a nonorthonormal normal frame $W=ZO$, set $K=W^{\mathsf T}W$ and $G=\tfrac12W^{\mathsf T}D^2C_RW$. The exact dual readout is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonorthogonal}
 KG^{-1}K=W^{\mathsf T}RW.
\end{equation}
Indeed $K=O^{\mathsf T}O$ and $G=O^{\mathsf T}B^{-1}O$, and direct inversion proves the identity. If $W$ has columns $J^{-\mathsf T}e_a$ for $a\in S$, its readout entries are $\theta_{a+b}$. Dropping the Gram factors in \eqref{eq:nonorthogonal} would give an incorrect normalization.

\section{Finite calibrated samples and observed exposures}\label{app:raw}
Keep the roots, clocks and calibrated tables fixed and separated. Put $\delta=M_1-M_2\ne0$, assume $\pi_j\in[\eps,1-\eps]$ with $0<\eps<1/2$, and define
\[
 \kappa(u)=u^2(1-u)^2\sum_{a=1}^4\frac{\delta_a^2}{M_{2a}+u\delta_a},\qquad g(u)=1/\kappa(u).
\]
On this interval $g$ is Lipschitz with a finite constant $L_g$. We distinguish a chosen target-design weight $\lambda$ from the realized sample allocation $n_j/N$. The theorem below estimates the metric indexed by the chosen known $\lambda$. It estimates the realized normalized sample information only when those weights are chosen as $n_j/N$, and the limiting information when $n_j/N\to\lambda_j$.
\begin{theorem}[Fixed-design categorical recovery]\label{thm:raw}
Observe $n_j\ge1$ independent marks at clock $j$ with law $p_j$, independently across groups. For empirical cell frequencies $\bar p_j$, set
\[
 \widehat\pi_j=\operatorname{proj}_{[\eps,1-\eps]}
     \frac{\langle\bar p_j-M_2,\delta\rangle}{\|\delta\|_2^2},\qquad
 W_{\cdot j}=\frac{\rho_j}{\lambda_j}w_{2n}(T_j),\qquad
 \widehat\theta=W(g(\widehat\pi_j))_j.
\]
Then $\widehat\theta\in\Omega$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rawrisk}
 \mathbb E\|\widehat\theta-\theta\|_2^2
  \le\frac{\|W\|_{\rm op}^2L_g^2}{\|\delta\|_2^2}\sum_jn_j^{-1}.
\end{equation}
On compact interior design sets, the induced estimates of $R$ and the inverse normal blocks have squared risk $O(\sum_jn_j^{-1})$. If $\LL$ is injective, inversion of those induced jets returns $\widehat\theta$ exactly.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Before clipping, the estimate of $\pi_j$ is unbiased. For a one-hot cell vector $X$, $\operatorname{Var}\langle X,\delta\rangle\le\|\delta\|_2^2$. Projection cannot increase distance to the true point in the interval, so $\mathbb E|\widehat\pi_j-\pi_j|^2\le(n_j\|\delta\|_2^2)^{-1}$. Apply the Lipschitz bound for $g$ and the operator norm of $W$. All estimated moment weights are positive. On compact design sets they lie in a fixed positive box; the moment image is a compact convex subset of $\Omega$. The map to $R$ and positive block inversion are Lipschitz there. Finally the induced inverse jets satisfy $\widehat G_\nu^{-1}=\LL_\nu\widehat\theta$, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}
The bound allows the $\pi_j$ to be coupled by the polynomial model and does not assert a full-dimensional fixed-design likelihood. Its constants need not stay bounded when roots or clocks collide, or probabilities approach the boundary.

\begin{theorem}[An additional observed-exposure protocol]\label{thm:poisson}
Observe independent counts $N_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(N\lambda_j)$ and, conditionally, $N_j$ categorical marks of law $p_j$. Suppose $0<\lambda_-\le\lambda_j\le\lambda_+$. Clip $N_j/N$ to this interval, use the preceding clipped mark estimate, and use any value in $[\eps,1-\eps]$ when $N_j=0$. Substitution into \eqref{eq:native} gives uniform squared risk at most $K/N$ on fixed compact designs.

An open exposure box has an open image in the moment cone. If the mark laws are known and exposures are interior, the plug-in moment estimate has limiting covariance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-cov}
 \Sigma_\theta=\sum_j\frac{\rho_j^2}{\lambda_j^3\kappa_j^2}
          w_{2n}(T_j)w_{2n}(T_j)^{\mathsf T}>0
\end{equation}
after multiplication of its error by $\sqrt N$. This is the efficient functional covariance in that known-mark-law subexperiment; its squared-risk order $N^{-1}$ is locally unimprovable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Clipping gives $\mathbb E|\widehat\lambda_j-\lambda_j|^2\le\lambda_+/N$. For $X\sim\operatorname{Pois}(a)$,
\[
 \mathbb E[X^{-1};X>0]\le2\mathbb E[(X+1)^{-1}]
       =2(1-e^{-a})/a,\qquad\Pr(X=0)=e^{-a}\le1/a.
\]
Conditioning on the counts therefore gives mark-estimation risk $O(N^{-1})$. The weight functions $\rho_j/(\lambda\kappa(u))$ are uniformly Lipschitz on the fixed positive compact rectangle, proving the risk bound.

The exposure-to-moment Jacobian has columns $-\rho_jw_{2n}(T_j)/(\lambda_j^2\kappa_j)$ and full row rank. This gives the open-image assertion. The Poisson central limit theorem and the delta method give \eqref{eq:poisson-cov}. The exposure information per unit $N$ is $\diag(1/\lambda_j)$; its inverse pushed forward by this Jacobian is exactly the displayed covariance. The regular local information bound for smooth functionals, or the normal target experiment in Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-TV}, gives efficiency and the lower risk order \cite{vdV}.
\end{proof}
Counts are genuinely observed in this additional protocol. Unknown exposures cannot be inferred from fixed-count marks alone. Jointly unknown marks and exposures need not have the known-mark-law efficient covariance \eqref{eq:poisson-cov}.

\section{Computed finite-offset features and independent noise}\label{app:offset}
At a globally separated informative point $b_0$, choose the normal frame $Z$ and the $q$ vectors $v_a$ consisting of its columns and their pairwise sums. Quadratic evaluation is an isomorphism $\mathcal Q:\Sym_c\to\R^q$. Define
\[
 \mu_h(\theta)_a=\tfrac12\{C_{R_\theta}(b_0+hv_a)+C_{R_\theta}(b_0-hv_a)\}.
\]
Fix a compact convex $\Theta\subset\Omega$ containing the true parameter and the clipped plug-in estimates. For sufficiently small $h>0$ all targets are positive and lie in the common analytic projection tube. Symmetric Taylor expansion, also differentiated in $\theta$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:offset-expansion}
 h^{-2}\mu_h(\vartheta)=\mathcal Q((\LL\vartheta)^{-1})+h^2r_h(\vartheta),
 \qquad \sup_{0<h\le h_0}\|r_h\|_{C^1(\Theta)}<\infty.
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[Computed finite-offset encoding and inversion]\label{thm:offset}
For a raw estimator with squared risk at most $K/N$, compute $\widehat\mu_h=\mu_h(\widehat\theta)$ and set
\[
 \widetilde\theta_h=\LL^\dagger
       [\mathcal Q^{-1}(h^{-2}\widehat\mu_h)]^{-1}.
\]
For small enough $h$ all inverses exist and
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widetilde\theta_h-\theta\|^2\le2K/N+2C^2h^4/\gamma^2.
\]
For a fixed design, $h_N=o(N^{-1/4})$ makes this output equivalent to the raw estimator at scale $\sqrt N$. With uniform tube and metric bounds along varying designs, it suffices that $\sqrt Nh_N^2/\gamma_N\to0$. A deterministic contact-computation error at most $\tau_N$ adds parameter error at most $C'\tau_N/(\gamma_Nh_N^2)$, provided positive inversion remains valid.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Smooth positive inversion in \eqref{eq:offset-expansion} gives
\[
 F_h(\vartheta):=[\mathcal Q^{-1}(h^{-2}\mu_h(\vartheta))]^{-1}
             =\LL\vartheta+h^2E_h(\vartheta),\qquad\|E_h\|_{C^1}\le C.
\]
Thus $\widetilde\theta_h=\widehat\theta+h^2\LL^\dagger E_h(\widehat\theta)$ and its extra error is at most $Ch^2/\gamma$. Squaring proves the risk and scaling claims. An error $\tau_N$ perturbs the argument of inversion by at most $\|\mathcal Q^{-1}\|\tau_N/h_N^2$; use the inverse identity in Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}.
\end{proof}
This theorem concerns deterministic re-encoding of an already available estimator. It is not an independent observational route or a new minimax rate. Its offset condition controls approximation bias; the leading statistical risk is inherited from the raw estimate. The direct score reduction of Section~\ref{sec:score-contact} does not use this construction.

For comparison, consider the different experiment of independent noisy readings
\[
 Y_i=\mu_h(\theta)+\sigma\xi_i,\qquad\xi_i\sim N(0,I_q),\quad i=1,\ldots,N.
\]
For compact convex $\Theta$ and sufficiently small fixed $h$, the exact finite-offset mean satisfies
\[
 ah^2\|\theta-\theta'\|\le\|\mu_h(\theta)-\mu_h(\theta')\|
                         \le bh^2\|\theta-\theta'\|
\]
for $a,b>0$. Indeed $F_h=\LL\theta+h^2E_h$ and $h^2\|DE_h\|\le\gamma/2$ give a lower bound by the triangle inequality and the mean-value bound for $E_h$; positive inversion and $\mathcal Q$ transfer it to $\mu_h$. The upper bound follows directly from \eqref{eq:offset-expansion}. Fitting the exact mean by least squares consequently gives
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb E\|\widehat\theta_N-\theta\|^2
              \le\frac{4q\sigma^2}{Na^2h^4},
\]
since the fitted mean is within twice the sample-mean error of the true mean. At an interior parameter the information is $I_h=\sigma^{-2}D\mu_h^{\mathsf T}D\mu_h>0$, the asymptotic covariance is $I_h^{-1}$, and the local asymptotic minimax risk is $\tr(I_h^{-1})/N$ \cite{vdV}. The least eigenvalue of $I_h/h^4$ is comparable to $\gamma^2/\sigma^2$ under the fixed metric bounds, by differentiating \eqref{eq:offset-expansion}. Gaussian tails and the lower Lipschitz bound justify convergence of local risks. A quadratic-truncated fit at fixed $h$ would instead generally retain an $O(h^2)$ bias and is not used. The $h^{-4}$ variance in this independent-noise experiment must not be transferred to the correlated computed or raw-score statistics.

\section{Complete contact, design fibres, and endpoint representations}\label{app:complementary}
These statements concern different observation categories from a prescribed normal block. We record their mathematical interfaces and proofs so that the one-contact and multi-contact results do not erase the complementary conclusions.

\subsection{Complete conormal ambiguity}
Suppose $\mathcal A_A:\Sym_d\to\R^k$, $S\mapsto(a_i^{\mathsf T}Sa_i)_i$, is injective. The complete conormal union of the rank-one image is dense in the inverse image, under $\mathcal A_A^*$, of the singular symmetric matrices: a conormal $n$ at $q_A(\eta)$ satisfies $(\sum_i n_i a_ia_i^{\mathsf T})\eta=0$. Conversely a real singular matrix has a nonzero real kernel vector. For $d\ge3$, no nonzero quadratic form vanishes on all singular symmetric matrices. To see this, restrict a quadratic polynomial to matrices diagonal in any fixed orthogonal basis. Every monomial of degree at most two omits at least one of the $d$ diagonal variables, so vanishing on every coordinate hyperplane forces the restriction to vanish identically. Every real symmetric matrix is orthogonally diagonalizable, so the polynomial is zero. For $d=2$, direct substitution of rank-one matrices leaves exactly the determinant line. This is the elementary coordinate consequence of classical Veronese duality \cite{GKZ}.

Under the surjective map $\mathcal A_A^*$, pure kernel and mixed quadratic terms must also vanish, since singular symmetric matrices span $\Sym_d$. Thus the complete-conormal ambiguity is zero for $d\ge3$ and one-dimensional for $d=2$. In the latter case it is generated by $B_{ii}=0$, $B_{ij}=\det(a_i,a_j)^2$ and is intersected with the admissible native positive cone. In root coordinates the native linear span consists exactly of symmetric matrices satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-site}
 \frac{(r_j-r_i)X_{ij}}{d_id_j}
 -\frac{(r_l-r_i)X_{il}}{d_id_l}
 +\frac{(r_l-r_j)X_{jl}}{d_jd_l}=0\quad(i<j<l).
\end{equation}
The divided-difference identity for $(t-r_i)^{-1}(t-r_j)^{-1}$ proves these relations for $JH(\theta)J^{\mathsf T}$. Conversely they leave $k$ free diagonal entries and $k-1$ off-diagonal difference coordinates, hence dimension $2k-1$, equal to the Hankel span. Testing $B$ in \eqref{eq:three-site} gives the exact two-parameter transversality criterion. Equality of second jets here is not equality of all higher contact terms.

\subsection{Exact dual-jet query count}
In a deterministic exact-real oracle whose scalar answers are values of the dual normal quadratic forms, full product rank requires exactly $p=2k-1$ scalar queries. Squares span within-contact products by polarization, so select $p$ independent square functionals for sufficiency. For necessity, follow any adaptive transcript at an interior parameter. Fewer than $p$ scalar linear answers have a nonzero common kernel. A sufficiently small perturbation in that kernel remains admissible and reproduces the same answers, hence the same adaptive choices, by induction. This is a query statement about the specified dual oracle, not a statistical sample bound or a count of ordinary scalar distance readings.

\subsection{Reciprocal design fibres}
Put $a_j=\rho_j/\kappa_j>0$, let $V_m$ be the $p\times m$ moment matrix, and use reciprocal weights $\lambda_j=a_j/\omega_j$. Define on the open moment cone
\[
 \chi(\theta)=\min_{V_m\omega=\theta,\ \omega>0}\sum_j a_j/\omega_j.
\]
The zeroth moment bounds every fibre. The objective tends to infinity at its boundary and is strictly convex, so there is a unique minimizer. Its diagonal Hessian $D_\omega>0$ and differentiation of the constrained first-order equations give
\[
 D^2\chi=(V_mD_\omega^{-1}V_m^{\mathsf T})^{-1}>0.
\]
Indeed $D_\omega\,d\omega=V_m^{\mathsf T}d\nu$ and $V_m\,d\omega=d\theta$, while $\nu$ is the gradient of the value. The implicit function theorem also gives smoothness. Here $m=p+2$, so the affine fibre has dimension two. Every ray from its minimizer meets a higher level once, because the restriction is strictly convex and diverges at the positive-fibre boundary. Its higher levels are smooth circles. Consequently the unit-exposure-budget image is $\{\theta:\chi(\theta)\le1\}$, with singleton boundary fibres and circular interior fibres. Homogeneity $\chi(t\theta)=t^{-1}\chi(\theta)$ makes the boundary regular relative to the open cone. Injective contact coordinates transport this geometry without changing the fibres.

\subsection{Endpoint value-graph representations}
For a labelled semialgebraic value graph specified over algebraic coefficients, fix a hidden dimension and a specified normalization of a positive block matrix. Every representation
\[
 F(x)=\min_{z\ge0}(x,z)^{\mathsf T}
       \begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^{\mathsf T}&C\end{pmatrix}(x,z)
\]
is characterized exactly by positivity, that normalization, and the quantified relations
\[
 z\ge0,\quad Cz+B^{\mathsf T}x\ge0,\quad
 z_i(Cz+B^{\mathsf T}x)_i=0,
\]
together with the value equality for every labelled $x$. Strict convexity in $z$ makes these Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions necessary and sufficient. Real quantifier elimination therefore computes the representation fibre at the fixed hidden dimension \cite{BPR}. Enumeration over dimensions terminates under a finite-representability promise, yielding the first nonempty normalized fibre; no unconditional finite hidden-dimension bound is implied. This input is a scalar value graph, not an optimizer map as in inverse parametric programming \cite{HGL,NORHN}.

\subsection{Deterministic local root costs}
The native expansion $p(u,v)=p_0+L_pu+D_pv+O((\|u\|+\|v\|)^2)$ gives the information in Proposition~\ref{prop:native}. With squared Hellinger distance $h^2(p,q)=\sum_e(\sqrt{p_e}-\sqrt{q_e})^2$, profiling the free coordinates yields the quadratic efficient cost after multiplying by $4/t^2$. Comparing a physical target variance $tb$, $b\ge0$, with synchronized $v=tq_A(\eta)$ gives \eqref{eq:distance}. Full rank of $A$ gives coercivity and bounded scaled minimizing sequences, making the Taylor remainder uniform on the relevant local chart. Physical splitting has size $\sqrt t$. For $C^2$ nonlinear loadings $g_i$ with $g_i(0)=0$ and $Dg_i(0)=a_i^{\mathsf T}$,
\[
 g_i(\sqrt t\eta)^2=t(a_i^{\mathsf T}\eta)^2+O(t^{3/2})
\]
uniformly on bounded $\eta$. These local statements do not require injectivity of $\mathcal A_A$ on all of $\Sym_d$; global chart selection uses the separated-fibre hypotheses. At $t=N^{-1/2}$ the same cost is the physical local Gaussian profile of Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary}.
