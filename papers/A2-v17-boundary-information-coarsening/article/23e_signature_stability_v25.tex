\section{Finite analytic signatures and stability of intrinsic gluing}
\label{sec:v25-signature-stability}
\label{sec:v24-quantitative-gluing}

Exact analytic uniqueness, finite-signature matching, and a compact inverse
modulus are three different assertions.  We prove them in that order.  An
outside-arc gap alone does not prove uniqueness inside the correct arc.  The
local assertion will instead follow from a finite jet embedding, whose order
is selected uniformly from the complete analytic signatures.

Fix the marked measured graph, two signature-rigid anchoring cycles with
independent marked deck displacements, and a rooted signature-rigid spanning
tree.  Let \(\mathfrak K\) be a compact class of their realizations, modulo
one simultaneous global element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).  The designation
\emph{signature-rigid} has its meaning in
Section~\ref{sec:v23-intrinsic-multichannel-rigidity}: each transition used
by these cycles and the tree has a unique matching oriented framed point,
not merely a unique point after arbitrarily choosing a symmetry phase.
Obstacles not used for such transitions may have exact symmetries; the
classification theorem for general gluing spaces remains unchanged.

Assume uniform positive curvature, gap, separation, diameter, free-area and
persistent-channel collar bounds.  For some \(\rho,B>0\), the oriented
arclength curvature functions extend holomorphically to strips of width
\(\rho\), with absolute value at most \(B\).  The class is closed in a
fixed root-frame gauge and compact in every fixed labelled \(C^q\) topology.
A channel margin \(\chi>0\) excludes switching to a competing closest
pair.  The data-determined holonomy vectors satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-holonomy-margin}
       \sigma_{\min}(v_1(T)\ \ v_2(T))\ge\sigma_{\rm hol}>0.
\end{equation}
The compactness assumption includes the lattice and marked placements; it
is not inferred from a bound on the curvature of a single obstacle alone.
These are the uniform geometric assumptions used below.

A transition obstacle has trivial orientation-preserving symmetry group.
Indeed any such nonidentity symmetry carries each boundary point to a
different point with the same complete oriented signature, contradicting
the designated unique framed match.  A nonidentity rigid motion preserving
a compact strictly convex body is a rotation about an interior point and
has no boundary fixed point.  In particular, transition obstacles are not
circles.  This observation uses the exact signature-rigidity assumption
already required for unique global gluing; it is not a new reflection or
genericity assumption.

\subsection{Uniform finite embeddings}

Parametrize each transition obstacle by normalized oriented arclength
\(x\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), with its origin at a designated incidence.
The physical arclength derivative is denoted by \(\partial_s\).  Define
\[
 J_{T,i}^{[m]}(x)=
 (\kappa_{T,i}(x),\partial_s\kappa_{T,i}(x),\ldots,
                                      \partial_s^m\kappa_{T,i}(x)).
\]
All derivatives with respect to \(x\) below are of this vector-valued map.
Uniform upper and lower perimeter bounds make normalized and physical
arclength distances equivalent.  Fixed positive weights could be placed on
the coordinates without changing any assertion.

\begin{theorem}[Uniformly selected finite signature embedding]
\label{thm:v25-finite-signature-embedding}
There are an integer \(m\), numbers \(v,r,\sigma>0\), and \(U,B_2<\infty\),
uniform on \(\mathfrak K\) and its finite list of transition obstacles,
such that, writing \(J=J_{T,i}^{[m]}\),
\begin{align}
 |J'(x)|&\ge v,\qquad |J'(x)|\le U,\qquad |J''(x)|\le B_2,
                                            \label{eq:v25-signature-immersion}\\
 |J(x)-J(y)|&\ge(v/2)\operatorname{dist}(x,y),
 \quad \operatorname{dist}(x,y)\le r,
                                            \label{eq:v25-inarc-embedding}\\
 |J(x)-J(y)|&\ge\sigma,
 \quad \operatorname{dist}(x,y)\ge r.
                                            \label{eq:v25-outarc-separation}
\end{align}
Thus a sufficiently rich finite signature, not necessarily an initially
chosen scalar curvature signature, is a uniform embedding of every
transition boundary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If every derivative of positive order of an analytic curvature function
vanished at one point, it would be constant by the identity theorem.
Since transition obstacles are not circles, at each \((T,i,x)\) some
component of the derivative of a finite signature is nonzero.  The parameter
space of these triples is compact: use normalized arclength and the finite
obstacle list.  Each nonvanishing condition persists on a neighborhood.
A finite subcover gives an order \(m_1\) for which
\(|(J^{[m_1]})'|\) is bounded below by a positive constant \(v\).
Holomorphic strip bounds give a uniform second derivative bound for this
finite vector.  Taylor's integral formula then gives
\[
 |J^{[m_1]}(y)-J^{[m_1]}(x)|
 \ge v|y-x|-\tfrac12\|(J^{[m_1]})''\|_\infty|y-x|^2
 \ge(v/2)|y-x|
\]
on a uniform short arc.  Choose its radius \(r<1/4\).

For pairs at normalized arclength distance at least \(r\), equality of
all signature coordinates would, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-signature-symmetry}, give a nonidentity
orientation-preserving symmetry.  No such symmetry exists on a transition
obstacle.  Each pair is therefore separated by some finite coordinate.
The set of \((T,i,x,y)\) with distance at least \(r\) is compact.  A
second finite subcover supplies an order \(m\ge m_1\) and a positive
minimum separation \(\sigma\).  Adding coordinates preserves the local
lower bound already proved for the \(m_1\)-block.  The strip bounds supply
finite uniform \(U,B_2\) for the enlarged vector.  This proves
\eqref{eq:v25-signature-immersion}--\eqref{eq:v25-outarc-separation}.
\end{proof}

The argument also separates a prescribed target arc from its complement:
shrink the radius below \(r\), using \eqref{eq:v25-inarc-embedding} on the
intermediate annulus and \eqref{eq:v25-outarc-separation} outside.  Therefore
an existing outside-arc margin may be retained, but it is not used to infer
\eqref{eq:v25-inarc-embedding}.  The latter is an independently proved
consequence of analytic nonconstancy and compactness.

\begin{lemma}[Unique matching for noisy finite signatures]
\label{lem:v25-noisy-signature-match}
There are \(\epsilon_0,C>0\), uniform on the class, with the following
property.  Fix a true target \(J(x_0)\).  Suppose a recovered signature
curve \(\widetilde J\), expressed in the same normalized arclength
parameter, and a target vector \(y\) satisfy
\[
 \|\widetilde J-J\|_{C^2}\le\epsilon,\qquad
 |y-J(x_0)|\le\epsilon,\qquad 0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0.
\]
Then the minimum of \(|\widetilde J(x)-y|^2\) on the parameter circle is
attained at one point \(\widehat x\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-noisy-match-error}
                         \operatorname{dist}(\widehat x,x_0)\le C\epsilon.
\end{equation}
Locally this match is Lipschitz in the target vector and in the recovered
curve in \(C^1\).  A mere \(C^0\) perturbation of an arbitrary signature
curve is not asserted to have a unique nearest point.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a radius \(a<r/4\) so small that \(2B_2Ua<v^2/16\), decreasing
it if necessary to handle bounded perturbations of the derivatives.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-finite-signature-embedding} gives a positive uniform
separation between \(J(x_0)\) and every \(J(x)\) outside this arc, namely
at least \(\min(va/2,\sigma)\).  For sufficiently small \(\epsilon\),
no minimizer can lie outside the arc: the value of the residual norm at
\(x_0\) is at most \(2\epsilon\), while outside it is larger.

On the arc put \(f(x)=\tfrac12|\widetilde J(x)-y|^2\).  Its second
derivative is
\[
 f''(x)=|\widetilde J'(x)|^2+
                 (\widetilde J(x)-y)\cdot\widetilde J''(x).
\]
The derivative lower bound, the choice of \(a\), and then a sufficiently
small \(\epsilon_0\) give \(f''\ge v^2/8\) throughout the arc.
Hence the minimum is unique and interior.  At the minimum the residual norm
is at most \(2\epsilon\), so
\[
 |J(\widehat x)-J(x_0)|\le4\epsilon.
\]
The local embedding bound proves
\eqref{eq:v25-noisy-match-error}, for example with \(C=8/v\).
Finally compare the equations \(f'(\widehat x)=0\) for two nearby data
sets.  The derivative of \(f'\) is at least \(v^2/8\), and its change
under a perturbation of \(y\) and \(\widetilde J\) is bounded by a
constant times \(|\Delta y|+\|\Delta\widetilde J\|_{C^1}\).
The mean value theorem gives the stated local Lipschitz dependence.
\end{proof}

For example, a scalar curvature with a strict minimum can take each nearby
larger value twice inside the correct arc.  Adding a finite number of its
oriented derivatives as above resolves this failure; declaring the original
scalar match unique would not.  Exact finite symmetries can instead be
handled on a fixed symmetry quotient for a classification problem, but they
must not be assigned an arbitrary phase and called a unique Euclidean
gluing.  The unique-reconstruction theorem uses the signature-rigid
transitions specified at the start of the section.

\subsection{Propagation through the marked graph}

\begin{lemma}[Persistence of the prescribed gluing]
\label{lem:v24-gluing-persistence}
On \(\mathfrak K\), the prescribed incidence matches are unique and
continuous.  More precisely, if their recovered finite signature curves and
targets satisfy the hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v25-noisy-signature-match}, and the recovered point--tangent
frames are close in \(C^1\), then the incidence congruences and their finite
graph compositions depend locally Lipschitz-continuously on these quantities.
The lattice map obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-lattice-perturbation}
 L=VM^{-1},\qquad
 \|\widetilde L-L\|\le\|\widetilde V-V\|\,\|M^{-1}\|,
\end{equation}
where the fixed invertible matrix \(M\) has the two marked deck vectors as
columns and \(V=(v_1\ v_2)\).  Rank two persists for
\(\|\widetilde V-V\|<\sigma_{\rm hol}/2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The matching lemma supplies the unique in-arc point; its global separation
excludes other arcs.  Point and oriented tangent determine the required
orientation-preserving rigid motion between the two recovered curve frames.
The elementary formula for that motion is smooth in the point and unit
tangent data on a fixed orientation chart.  Products of a fixed finite
number of such motions are locally Lipschitz on bounded point data.
Consequently the tree placements and the two cycle holonomies inherit a
uniform local bound, with a constant depending on the finite graph.
The channel margin prevents a different closest pair from replacing the
selected one.  Formula~\eqref{eq:v25-lattice-perturbation} is direct matrix
multiplication by the fixed \(M^{-1}\), not inversion of a noisy matrix
\(V\).  The singular-value perturbation inequality proves rank persistence.
Also
\[
 \|\widetilde L^t\widetilde L-L^tL\|
 \le2\|L\|\,\|\widetilde L-L\|+\|\widetilde L-L\|^2,
\]
so the Gram form and subsequent deck corrections are stable as well.
\end{proof}

This lemma concerns already recovered signature curves in the stated
norms.  It does not turn noisy contact Taylor coefficients into a
well-conditioned unrestricted analytic continuation algorithm.  The next
argument supplies the distinct, non-effective continuity route needed for
global statistical consistency.

\subsection{The compact inverse modulus}

Normalize the action coordinates by
\[
 x_{e,b,m}(T)=\rho^mS_{e,b}^{(m)}(0;T)/m!,\qquad m\ge2,
\]
using any fixed positive scale \(\rho\), and the gaps by \(g_*\).
For a fixed enumeration \(|e|\) of the finite channel set define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-data-metric}
 \begin{split}
 d_{\mathcal D}(T,T')={}&\sum_{e,b}\sum_{m\ge2}2^{-(m+|e|+3)}
          \min\{1,|x_{e,b,m}(T)-x_{e,b,m}(T')|\}\\
 &+\sum_e2^{-(|e|+2)}\min\{1,|g_e(T)-g_e(T')|/g_*\}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The truncation by one and the summable weights make this metric compatible
with coordinatewise convergence of the intrinsic data.  This conclusion
requires no assertion that the action holomorphic radius equals the
curvature holomorphic radius.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform inverse modulus]
\label{thm:v24-global-modulus}
For every fixed \(q\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-global-modulus-def}
 \omega_q(t)=\sup\{d_q(T,T'):T,T'\in\mathfrak K,
                                   d_{\mathcal D}(T,T')\le t\}
\end{equation}
is finite, nondecreasing, and tends to zero as \(t\downarrow0\).
If \(\mathcal D_M\) consists of the gaps and action jets through order
\(M\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-finite-modulus}
 d_q(T,T')\le\omega_q\bigl(C\|\mathcal D_M(T)-\mathcal D_M(T')\|_M
                                             +C2^{-M}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The finite norm is the maximum norm of the normalized retained coordinates;
\(C\) depends only on the finite graph and the chosen weights.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equality of the complete action data and gaps gives the full analytic
contact germs by Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity}.  Analytic continuation,
exact signature-rigid gluing, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-lattice-gram-recovery} determine the marked periodic
table in the root-frame gauge.  This exact injectivity uses the complete
signatures; it does not depend on a possibly noisy finite matching rule.
The complete data map is continuous coordinate by coordinate, by the
half-line inverse estimates, and hence continuous in
\eqref{eq:v24-data-metric}.  A continuous injection from compact
\(\mathfrak K\) into this metric data space has a uniformly continuous
inverse on its image.  This proves the assertion about \(\omega_q\).
The terms with \(m>M\) in the data metric sum to at most \(C2^{-M}\);
the retained terms are bounded by the normalized finite maximum norm times
\(C\).  Substitution gives \eqref{eq:v24-finite-modulus}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two distinct stability statements]
\label{rem:v24-exact-versus-stable}
The local finite-signature estimates are proved by immersion, separation and
strict convexity of the matching criterion.  The global modulus is proved
by exact injectivity and compactness.  Neither proof substitutes for the
other.  The tail \(C2^{-M}\) is a product-metric truncation bound, not an
exponential analytic-continuation error rate.  No effective formula for
\(\omega_q\) or a uniform-in-order jet condition number is asserted.
\end{remark}
