\section{Smooth contact recovery from finite preparations}
\label{sec:v69-sampled-contact}

The preceding stability theorem requires a differentiated density norm.
A finite endpoint record does not supply this norm, and a discontinuous
histogram cannot be substituted for it.  We now construct the missing
statistical comparison, for the same marked periodic polygon and the
same two controlled offsets.  The target is the complete real contact
profile, not a truncated jet.  The proof regularizes the endpoint data,
compares realizable laws, and then applies the geometric inverse.  All
independent phase preparations, including failures, are counted.

\subsection{A fixed interior observation experiment}

Fix the marked polygon of Section~\ref{sec:v65-periodic-inverse}, with
$r$ physical phases, a returning oriented period $P$, and offsets
$0<d_1<d_2$.  Write $J=2r$ for the number of phase--offset groups.
Choose concentric squares
\[
 Q=[-R,R]^2\Subset Q_+=[-R_+,R_+]^2
\]
inside one common positive-residual collar.  A success means precisely
the selected itinerary event of Theorem~\ref{thm:v64-physical-law},
with its recorded endpoints in $Q_+$.  This gate is fixed in the calibrated
tangent coordinates; it uses no unknown normal graph height.  The
success/failure tag, including gate membership, is recorded exactly.
The bounded errors below affect endpoint readout after acceptance, not
selection tags or the two offsets.  Conditional
densities below are normalized on $Q_+$, and compared on $Q$.  Restricting
them to $Q$ does not renormalize them.  The unknown scalar normalizers
still cancel in Proposition~\ref{prop:v65-two-offset}.

Let $\mathscr C$ be a nonempty class of actual marked tables whose contact
tuples belong to the bounded positive class $\mathcal K_m$ of
\eqref{eq:v68-smooth-class}.  The integer $m\ge3$ is fixed so that
\eqref{eq:v68-weight-threshold} holds on the enlarged quadratic class.
Fix also an integer $s\ge1$.  Impose the uniform geometric hypotheses
of Theorems~\ref{thm:v64-periodic-relative} and
\ref{thm:v64-physical-law} through endpoint order $m+s$, including
the finitely many higher graph bounds used for that order, and a positive
upper bound for the free cell area.  Positivity, clearance, incidence,
flight-length bounds, and the separated offsets are fixed throughout.
These are forward smoothness and geometric priors, not bounds assumed
for an observed density error.  No analytic prior is imposed.  A compact
smooth family with the stated positive margins is one admissible choice;
the constants use only its finitely stipulated derivative bounds.

For $T\in\mathscr C$, denote its profiles by $F^T$, the limiting
density tuple by $f^T=(f^T_{b,d})$, the $N$-flight conditional density
by $f^T_{N,b,d}$, and its success probability by $p^T_{N,b,d}$.
Here and below $N$ is a multiple of $P$.  By shrinking both squares
within the stability collar, we obtain constants
$H,C_0,c_0,\omega,\Gamma>0$ and $N_0$ such that
\begin{align}
 \sup_{T,b,d,N\ge N_0}
 \bigl(\|f^T_{N,b,d}\|_{C^{m+s}(Q_+)}+
       \|f^T_{b,d}\|_{C^{m+s}(Q_+)}\bigr)&\le H,
       \label{eq:v69-forward-smoothness}\\
 \max_{b,d}\|f^T_{N,b,d}-f^T_{b,d}\|_{C^m(Q_+)}
                         &\le C_0e^{-\omega N},
       \label{eq:v69-forward-error}\\
 p^T_{N,b,d}&\ge p_*(N):=c_0e^{-\Gamma N}.
       \label{eq:v69-acceptance}
\end{align}
We take $c_0\le1$.  To see the uniformity explicitly, the numerator
of the conditional law is the normalized twist times its physical
residual.  The relative estimates bound its indicated derivatives;
a fixed positive square bounds its integral away from zero.  Division
therefore gives the first two estimates.  For the last one the exact
reference twist is
\[
 D_{N,b}=
 \frac{\sinh\chi}{(\mathcal M_b)_{12}\sinh(N\chi/P)}.
\]
The positive quadratic box bounds its prefactor below, and bounds
$\chi/P$ above by a finite $\Gamma$.  The residual integral has a
positive lower bound and the area has an upper bound.  The exact
phase-volume formula gives \eqref{eq:v69-acceptance}.  This argument
uses relative twist control before conditioning; division of an
absolute action error by $D_{N,b}$ would not give these estimates.

The law comparison in Theorem~\ref{thm:v68-smooth-stability} now reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v69-realizable-comparison}
 \|F^T-F^{\widetilde T}\|_{C^0([-R,R])}
 \le C\max_{b,d}\|f^T_{b,d}-f^{\widetilde T}_{b,d}\|_{C^m(Q)}
\end{equation}
when the right-hand density distance is at most a fixed $\eta_0$.
It applies to these restrictions because its proof uses the anchored
quotient and positive denominator floors, not a known normalizing
constant.  A smaller common $R$ can be chosen once for the class.

\subsection{Recovering the differentiated density norm}

Use the maximum of all derivatives of total order at most $m$ as the
$C^m(Q)$ norm.  Fix a smooth compactly supported kernel $K$ on
$(-1,1)^2$ with
\[
 \int K=1,\qquad \int z^\alpha K(z)\,dz=0
                   \quad(1\le|\alpha|<s).
\]
Such kernels need not be nonnegative.  For completeness, take a positive
smooth bump $w$ on $(-1,1)$, form the positive definite moment matrix
$M_{ij}=\int t^{i+j}w(t)\,dt$, $0\le i,j<s$, and solve
$Mc=(1,0,\ldots,0)^t$.  Then
$k(t)=w(t)\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}c_jt^j$ has the required one-dimensional
moments, and $K(z)=k(z_1)k(z_2)$ has the displayed moments.  Positive
definiteness follows by integrating the square of a nonzero polynomial
against $w$.  We write $K_h(z)=h^{-2}K(z/h)$.

\begin{lemma}[Interior derivative estimation]
\label{lem:v69-density-estimation}
Let each of $J$ groups contain $n\ge2$ independent latent endpoints
$Y_{g,l}$ with density $q_g$ on $Q_+$ and
$\|q_g\|_{C^{m+s}(Q_+)}\le H$.  Observed endpoints may have arbitrary
bounded recording errors
$|\widetilde Y_{g,l}-Y_{g,l}|_\infty\le\delta$.
Independence of the recording errors is not required.  Set
\[
 \widehat q_{g,h}(z)=\frac1n\sum_{l=1}^n
                               K_h(z-\widetilde Y_{g,l}).
\]
For $n^{-1}\le h\le h_0<\min(1,R_+-R)$ and $0<\zeta<1/2$, put
$L_n=\log(C_1Jn/\zeta)$, with a sufficiently large fixed $C_1$.
With probability at least $1-\zeta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v69-density-bound}
 \max_g\|\widehat q_{g,h}-q_g\|_{C^m(Q)}
 \le C\left\{h^s+
 \sqrt{\frac{L_n}{nh^{2m+2}}}+
 \frac{L_n}{nh^{m+2}}+n^{-2}+
 \frac{\delta}{h^{m+3}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The constants depend on the fixed squares, kernel, orders and $H$, not
on $n,h,\delta$ or the particular densities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First set $\delta=0$.  On $Q$ the kernel does not encounter the boundary
of $Q_+$.  Integration by parts gives
$D^\alpha\mathbb E\widehat q_h=K_h*D^\alpha q$ for $|\alpha|\le m$.
Taylor's integral remainder through order $s-1$, with the moment
cancellations, gives a uniform bias at most $CHh^s$.  Only derivatives
through $m+s$ on $Q_+$ are used; the zero extension of the density is
not asserted to be smooth at the boundary.

For a fixed $z$ and $|\alpha|\le m$, the summand
$D^\alpha K_h(z-Y)$ is bounded in absolute value by
$Ch^{-m-2}$ and has variance at most $CHh^{-2m-2}$.  These bounds
follow directly by scaling, since
\[
 \int |D^\alpha K_h(z-y)|^2q(y)\,dy
                  \le Hh^{-2|\alpha|-2}\|D^\alpha K\|_2^2.
\]
For independent centered summands bounded by $U$ with variance at most
$V$, Bernstein's bound for their average is
\[
 \mathbb P\!\left(\left|n^{-1}\sum_{l=1}^n Z_l\right|>
              \sqrt{2Vt/n}+2Ut/(3n)\right)\le2e^{-t}.
\]
One may derive it by expanding the exponential and using
$\mathbb E|Z_l|^j\le VU^{j-2}$ for $j\ge2$:
$\mathbb E e^{\lambda Z_l}\le
\exp\{\lambda^2V/[2(1-|\lambda|U/3)]\}$ for $|\lambda|U<3$.
Multiplication of the moment bounds and exponential Markov inequality
give the displayed tail estimate, with harmless enlargement of $U$
for centering.

Cover $Q$ by a grid of mesh at most $n^{-2}h^{m+3}$.  Its size is at
most $Cn^4h^{-2m-6}$.  A union bound over this grid, the fixed set of
multiindices and the $J$ groups, with $t=CL_n$, gives the square-root
and linear terms of \eqref{eq:v69-density-bound}; here $h\ge n^{-1}$
absorbs the grid logarithm into $CL_n$.  Both the empirical field and
its expectation have Lipschitz constant at most $Ch^{-m-3}$ after
any derivative of order at most $m$.  The grid error is consequently
at most $Cn^{-2}$.  This proves the asserted uniform stochastic bound.
This elementary fixed-bandwidth argument is sufficient here; sharper
classical uniform kernel results are discussed in~\cite{GineGuillou2002}.

Finally the mean value theorem, applied to each smooth kernel summand,
gives the deterministic comparison
\[
 \max_g\|\widehat q^{\rm observed}_{g,h}
             -\widehat q^{\rm latent}_{g,h}\|_{C^m(Q)}
                         \le C\delta h^{-m-3}.
\]
Adding it proves the result, even for data-dependent recording errors.
The estimates need not be densities or nonnegative functions.  No
nonlinear law identity has yet been applied to them.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Realizable estimation and complete profile recovery}

Let $\mathcal F=\{f^T|_Q:T\in\mathscr C\}$ in the product
$C^m(Q)^J$, with maximum norm.  Choose a countable observation-dense
subset $\{f^{T_j}:j\ge1\}$ of this image, retaining one actual table
for each element.  Such a subset exists because $C^m(Q)^J$ is separable
and metrizable; its subspaces are separable.  Given an estimated tuple
$\widehat f$ and a tolerance $\xi>0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v69-selection}
 \widehat T=T_{j_*},\qquad
 j_* =\min\left\{j:
 \|f^{T_j}-\widehat f\|_{C^m(Q)}
 \le \inf_{l\ge1}\|f^{T_l}-\widehat f\|_{C^m(Q)}+\xi\right\}.
\end{equation}
The set is nonempty by the definition of an infimum.  This is a
measurable estimator: each norm is a supremum of continuous derivative
evaluations over a fixed countable dense set, and the remaining
operations are countable.  This existence construction is not a claim
of efficient enumeration of all physical tables.  It supplies no
unknown curvature or flux amplitude as observation data.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample recovery of smooth contacts]
\label{thm:v69-successful-samples}
Under the stated class assumptions, collect $n$ successful endpoints at
$N$ flights in each phase--offset group, from independent preparations.
Allow recording error at most $\delta$, and use
\eqref{eq:v69-selection} with the kernel estimates and $\xi=n^{-1}$.
Let
\[
 \alpha=\frac{s}{2(m+s)+2},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{s}{m+s+3},\qquad
 h=\max\left\{(L_n/n)^{1/[2(m+s)+2]},
                    \delta^{1/(m+s+3)}\right\}.
\]
For $h\le h_0$ and sufficiently small combined error, with probability
at least $1-\zeta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v69-sample-profile}
 \|F^{\widehat T}-F^T\|_{C^0([-R,R])}
 \le C\left\{e^{-\omega N}+(L_n/n)^\alpha+\delta^\gamma\right\}.
\end{equation}
No candidate-closeness assumption, analytic prior, supplied density
jets, or inversion on arbitrary density tuples is used.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:v69-density-estimation}, denote its
right-hand bound by $e$.  The true limiting tuple is within
$e+C_0e^{-\omega N}$ of the estimates, by
\eqref{eq:v69-forward-error}.  Density of the countable image makes
this also an upper bound for the infimum in \eqref{eq:v69-selection}.
The triangle inequality gives
\[
 \|f^{\widehat T}-f^T\|_{C^m(Q)}
                \le 2e+2C_0e^{-\omega N}+n^{-1}.
\]
Both sides of this comparison are realizable limiting tuples.
Apply \eqref{eq:v69-realizable-comparison} when this bound is small.
In particular no factorization of the empirical or finite-flight
density is asserted.

To simplify the rate, write $h_n=(L_n/n)^{1/[2(m+s)+2]}$.
At $h=h_n$ the square-root term equals $h_n^s$; the linear term is
$h_n^{m+2s}\le h_n^s$.  Both stochastic terms decrease when $h$
increases.  Also $h^s\le h_n^s+\delta^\gamma$ and
$\delta h^{-m-3}\le\delta^\gamma$, with the zero-noise term
interpreted as zero.  For large enough $n$, $n^{-1}\le h_n^s$ and
$h\ge n^{-1}$.  These facts prove \eqref{eq:v69-sample-profile}.

For repeated independent preparations, each observation is a failure
or a successful mark.  Its law is the mixture of a failure atom and
$p^T_{N,b,d}f^T_{N,b,d}$.  It can therefore be generated by an
independent Bernoulli success sequence and an independent sequence of
marks with that conditional density.  The first $n$ successful marks
are independent with the required density.  No mixing assumption about
a single unreset billiard trajectory is being substituted for independent
preparations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A deterministic budget with failures charged}

\begin{theorem}[Charged smooth recovery]
\label{thm:v69-charged-recovery}
Perform exactly $B$ independent full-phase preparations in each of the
$J$ groups, all at $N\ge N_0$ flights.  Define
\[
 n_B=\lfloor Bp_*(N)/2\rfloor.
\]
Assume $n_B\ge2$ and $Bp_*(N)\ge8\log(J/\zeta)$.
Use the first $n_B$ successes in every group and the preceding estimator;
return any fixed member of $\mathscr C$ if a group has fewer successes.
Then \eqref{eq:v69-sample-profile} holds with $n=n_B$ and probability
at least $1-2\zeta$, subject to the same small-error and bandwidth
conditions.  Every one of the $JB$ preparations is charged.

In particular, put $L_B=\log(C_2JB/\zeta)$ and, for large enough $B$,
choose the returning flight number
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v69-balanced-length}
 N_B=P\left\lfloor
       \frac{\alpha\log(B/L_B)}{P(\omega+\alpha\Gamma)}
                              \right\rfloor\ge N_0.
\end{equation}
There is a constant $C$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v69-budget-rate}
 \mathbb P_T\!\left(
 \|F^{\widehat T}-F^T\|_{C^0([-R,R])}>
 C\{(L_B/B)^\beta+\delta^\gamma\}\right)\le2\zeta,
 \qquad
 \beta=\frac{\alpha\omega}{\omega+\alpha\Gamma}>0.
\end{equation}
The assertion is uniform over the stipulated marked class.  Constants,
orders and exponents need not be uniform in a varying period or a
vanishing hyperbolicity margin.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The number of successes in one group is binomial with mean
$\mu=Bp^T_{N,b,d}\ge Bp_*(N)$.  The elementary lower-tail bound
$\mathbb P(M<\mu/2)\le e^{-\mu/8}$ implies
\[
 \mathbb P(M<n_B)\le e^{-Bp_*(N)/8}.
\]
For example exponential Markov inequality at parameter $\log2$ gives
$\exp\{-\mu(1-\log2)/2\}$, which is no larger than $e^{-\mu/8}$.
The union bound gives probability at most $\zeta$ of any insufficient
group.  By the mixture representation at the end of the preceding proof,
the first $n_B$ marks have the requisite product law independently of
the success indicators.  Intersecting the sufficient-success event
with the density-estimation event loses at most another $\zeta$.
Arbitrary bounded recording errors are still handled pathwise.

Write $N_*=\alpha\log(B/L_B)/(\omega+\alpha\Gamma)$.
Then $N_B\in(N_*-P,N_*]$, so
\[
 e^{-\omega N_B}\le e^{\omega P}(L_B/B)^\beta,\qquad
 n_B\ge c B^{\omega/(\omega+\alpha\Gamma)}
                   L_B^{\alpha\Gamma/(\omega+\alpha\Gamma)}
\]
for sufficiently large $B$, with the floor absorbed into $c>0$.
The logarithm $L_{n_B}$ is bounded by $CL_B$ since $n_B\le B$.
Substitution gives
$(L_{n_B}/n_B)^\alpha\le C(L_B/B)^\beta$, proving
\eqref{eq:v69-budget-rate}.  The sufficient-success condition follows
for fixed confidence and large $B$; for varying confidence it remains
an explicit requirement.  The bandwidth and small-error restrictions
also remain explicit, especially when $\delta$ does not tend to zero.
\end{proof}

The factor $\Gamma$ in \eqref{eq:v69-budget-rate} records the cost of
rarity.  It would be absent if successful bridges were incorrectly treated
as free observations.  With a bounded period length, the total physical
observation time is at most $CJB(1+N_B)$, hence $O(JB\log B)$ at fixed
confidence.  The supplied polygon and frames and the two exact offsets
are still required.  This is a finite-sample result for bounded endpoint
recording errors, not arbitrary contamination, uncalibrated clocks,
a single correlated trajectory, or a claim of minimax optimality.

The estimate controls complete profiles and hence applies to the actual
flat family of Proposition~\ref{prop:v68-flat-family}, when restricted
to a small compact parameter interval.  To make this consequence precise,
fix $0<|u_*|<R$ in the region where its cutoff is one.  The two tables
at parameters $0$ and $\varepsilon>0$ have profile separation at least
$\Delta=\varepsilon e^{-1/u_*^2}$.  Whenever
$C\{(L_B/B)^\beta+\delta^\gamma\}<\Delta/3$,
\eqref{eq:v69-budget-rate} and comparison of the estimated profile's
distance to these two candidates give a test whose error at either table
is at most $2\zeta$.  The remote area correction
does not change this contact value.  Thus flat information is accessible
to a finite charged experiment at every fixed nonzero scale, although
its cost can be very large as $u_*$ approaches zero.  Equal Taylor jets
alone still supply no such test.  No assertion about equal full marked
length spectra, unvisited smooth arcs, or normalized density jets is
introduced by this observation.
