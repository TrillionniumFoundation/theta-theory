\begin{abstract}
We study polynomial spectra observed through a scalar normalization and
two unknown stochastic channels. The normalization defect is a common-factor
module, with sharp clock counts determined at the low-span and full-span
endpoints. An observable joint-projector gauge yields a global additive
inverse estimate against the entire closed model. On the regular quotient
we prove analytic identification of all nearby representations and identify
one observable covariance matrix governing both the exact metric condition
and the local asymptotic minimax constant, including cross-component root
collisions. The additive gauge also extends to noncommuting, defective
coefficient algebras with an invariant flag; scalar multiplicity and flag
paths enter at different scales. For arbitrary closed-model points, a
Hellinger-neighbourhood modulus gives matching local risk bounds and a
rational arc-order exponent whenever the exact spectral fibre is a
singleton. We compute an anchored square-root modulus at real-rooted
internal multiplicities and distinguish it from the retained fixed-neighbourhood
rates $N^{-1/m}$. Explicit spectral estimation, whole-model honest confidence
sets, and adaptive-design lower bounds complete the observation theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A scalar normalization can conceal a polynomial factor and therefore
part of a spectrum. Recovering the normalization and recovering the
spectrum are nevertheless not independent perturbation problems. An
error in the normalizing polynomial has additional algebraic structure.
A general coefficient condition number can charge for this error a
second time, producing a product of losses which the observation map
does not possess.

We study this distinction for polynomial mixtures viewed through two
unknown stochastic channels. A Vandermonde residual operator measures
the missing normalization. After it is recovered, the coefficient
matrices generate a commutative semisimple algebra. Its joint spectral
projectors separate \emph{complete component polynomials}, including
polynomials which share individual roots. These projectors give a
finite observable condition in every degree. The scalar normalization
perturbation remains scalar on each complete-polynomial subspacen.
Resolvent exclusion then yields an additive observation-to-spectrum
modulus, rather than a product of a normalization loss and a latent
channel condition number.

Three further distinctions are important. First, the sharp worst-case
clock count comes from low functional complexity. A full polynomial
span has a different, smaller sharp count. Second, an observable
one-sided certificate need not equal the sharp local inverse condition.
On regular coefficient charts we give the latter exactly, in a specified
observation metric. Third, multiplicity of a root within a scalar
component differs from a collision between components. The first changes
neighbourhood minimax exponents; the second may leave the spectral
inverse linear even though aggregate roots repeat. All three distinctions
are proved within the same finite-clock experiment.

\subsection{The polynomial experiment}
Fix $d\ge1$, $2\le k\le\min(m_1,m_2)$, an interval $[L,D]$, distinct
clocks $D<T_1<\cdots<T_\ell$, with $\ell\ge d+1$, and
$0<\alpha_*<1/k$. The columns of
$U\in\R^{m_1\times k}$ and $V\in\R^{m_2\times k}$ are probability
vectors. Let $\alpha_b\ge\alpha_*$ and $\sum_b\alpha_b=1$. Put
\begin{align}
 f_b(z)&=\prod_{a=1}^d(z-\lambda_{ba}),\qquad \lambda_{ba}\in[L,D],
       &q(z)&=\sum_b\alpha_b f_b(z),\nonumber\\
 K(z)&=U\diag(\alpha_b f_b(z))V^{\mathsf T},
       &P(T)&=\frac{K(T)}{q(T)}.                  \label{eq:model}
\end{align}
The parameter set $\cK_d$ is compact; singular channels and repeated
roots are included. All $f_b$ and $q$ are monic of degree $d$.
Every $q(T_j)$ and every component probability
$p_b(T_j)=\alpha_b f_b(T_j)/q(T_j)$ has uniform positive bounds.
An arbitrary positive scalar detector multiplying $K(T)$ disappears
on normalization. Component-dependent detectors define a different
experiment and are not silently incorporated into this model.

The target $\Lambda$ is the unordered multiset of all $kd$ roots,
counted with multiplicity. We do not require a partition of these roots
into components as an output. For multisets of equal cardinality put
\[
 d_\infty(\Lambda,\Lambda')=\min_\pi\max_a|\lambda_a-\lambda'_{\pi(a)}|,
 \qquad \delta=\max_j\|P(T_j)-P'(T_j)\|_F.
\]
For real multisets this equals the maximum distance between their
increasing orders. Unless explicitly quantified, constants depend only
on $d,k,m_1,m_2,\alpha_*,L,D$ and the fixed clocks. An internal root
separation is charged only where stated. Proposition~\ref{prop:explicit}
gives a bound with explicit degree and clock dependence. It does not
assert stable monomial interpolation in growing degree.

Let $W=(T_j^r)_{j=1,\ldots,\ell;\,r=0,\ldots,d}$, and let $\Omega$
be the orthogonal projector onto $\operatorname{col}(W)^\perp$. Write
$q(z)=z^d+\sum_{r<d}a_rz^r$. For $c\in\R^d$, put
$r_c(z)=\sum_{r<d}c_rz^r$ and define
\begin{align}
 \cN_Pc&=(\Omega\otimes I)
       \big(r_c(T_j)\operatorname{vec}P(T_j)\big)_{j=1}^{\ell},
       &\tau(P)&=\sigma_{\min}(\cN_P),\nonumber\\
 b_P&=-(\Omega\otimes I)
       \big(T_j^d\operatorname{vec}P(T_j)\big)_{j=1}^{\ell},
       &\kappa&=\sigma_k(U)\sigma_k(V).           \label{eq:normalization}
\end{align}
Here $I$ has size $m_1m_2$. The singular value uses Euclidean
coefficient norm and product Frobenius norm. The normalization equation
is $\cN_Pa=b_P$. Thus $\tau$ is an observable quantity with specified
norms; $\kappa$ is a representation quantity used for comparison.
We write $F(\theta)=(\operatorname{vec}P_\theta(T_j))_j$ for the
finite observation map, also at points of the closed model.

We first state the normalization theorem and its channel-coordinate
bound. The observable strengthening immediately following it is the
principal inverse estimate of the paper.
\begin{theorem}[Normalization, clock design, and spectral recovery]
\label{thm:main}
Suppose the true channels have full column rank. If $\ell\ge2d+1$
and $g=\gcd(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$ is monic, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 \ker\cN_P=\{(q/g)s:\deg s<\deg g\},\qquad
 \dim\ker\cN_P=\deg g.
\end{equation}
For any design with $\ell\ge d+1$ and $\tau(P)>0$, every competitor
in $\cK_d$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 \|a-a'\|_2&\le C\min\{1,\delta/\tau(P)\},        \label{eq:q-bound}\\
 d_\infty(\Lambda,\Lambda')&\le
 C\min\{1,[\delta(\kappa^{-1}+\tau(P)^{-1})]^{1/d}\}.
                                                        \label{eq:poly-bound}
\end{align}
If the roots within each true $f_b$ are simple and separated by
$\nu>0$, the exponent in \eqref{eq:poly-bound} is $1$, with a constant
also depending on $\nu$. Cross-component collisions are allowed, and
no rank or root-separation assumption is made on the competitor.

At an interior parameter with simple roots within each component,
$\tau(P)=0$ gives a nonconstant local exact fibre with the same channels
and a changing root multiset. For every fixed admissible $k$, relatively
prime interior examples fail identification at $2d$ clocks. This is a
worst-case statement, realized by an affine-line matrix curve. If the
component polynomials span all polynomials of degree at most $d$, then
$d+2$ clocks suffice and $d+1$ do not.
\end{theorem}

To state the observable condition, recover $K$ and take orthonormal
frames for its rank-$k$ column and row spaces. Write its reduction as
$M(z)=\sum_{j=0}^dM_jz^j$, and put $A_0=M_d$.
The matrices $M_jA_0^{-1}$ commute. For each distinct complete
polynomial $f$, let $E_f$ be their joint spectral projector, and set
\[
 B_d(P)=\sum_f\|A_0^{-1}E_f\|_\op,
 \qquad \eta_d(P)=(B_d(P)+\tau(P)^{-1})^{-1}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:joint} proves that these are observable and independent
of frames. In particular, it replaces \eqref{eq:poly-bound} by
\[
 d_\infty(\Lambda,\Lambda')
       \le C\min\{1,[\delta/\eta_d(P)]^{1/d}\},
\]
and by the corresponding $1/m$ bound near separated clusters containing
at most $m$ roots of each component. The scalar error and the matrix
error enter as $\tau^{-1}+B_d$, not $\tau^{-1}B_d$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-condition} gives the exact two-sided local metric
condition on regular coefficient charts. Theorem~\ref{thm:rational-module}
and Proposition~\ref{prop:general-bound} remove simultaneous
diagonalization from the module and a general resolvent estimate;
the joint algebra gives the sharper stochastic coefficient.
Theorem~\ref{thm:flag-gauge} further extends the additive mechanism itself
to noncommuting algebras with a common invariant flag.

In degree one write $f_b(z)=z-\xi_b$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine}
 h=\sum_b\alpha_b\xi_b,\quad
 A=U\diag(\alpha)V^{\mathsf T},\quad
 C=U\diag(\alpha_b\xi_b)V^{\mathsf T},\quad B=hA-C.
\end{equation}
Then $P(T)=A+B/(T-h)$ and $K(z)=zA-C$. At three clocks,
$\tau(P)\asymp\gamma(P):=\|B\|_F$. The original affine condition is
$\sig=\kappa\gamma/(\kappa+\gamma)$, set to zero on either boundary,
and its observable exact-fibre envelope is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 \sig_*(P)=\max\{\sig(\omega):\mathcal P(\omega)=P\}.
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{sec:residue} retains the sharper affine residue condition
$\eta$ and its complete proof; $B_1$ is exactly its residue sum and
$\eta_1\asymp\eta$. The direct estimator in Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}
uses least squares, singular subspaces and polynomial roots, with no
supplied signal floor. It is an exact-arithmetic stability result, not
a bit-complexity claim or an algorithm for global confidence optimization.

Theorems~\ref{thm:poly-inference} and \ref{thm:poly-sharp} give honest
inference and sharp $\min\{1,(Ns^2)^{-1}\}$ squared-risk orders on
observable signal classes in every degree. Theorem~\ref{thm:multiplicity}
gives neighbourhood minimax order $N^{-1/m}$ at internal multiplicity
$m$. Positive alternatives establish the lower bounds for adaptive
clock selection as well as fixed designs. Section~\ref{sec:statistics}
retains the affine channel-product and full-collapse families, including
their exact singular limits. These statements distinguish a uniform
certificate, an exact local metric condition, and class or neighbourhood
risk; none is replaced by an unproved equivalence among the three.

\subsection{Intrinsic geometry and the two perturbation scales}
Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient-chart} proves an analytic quotient theorem,
not merely a Jacobian formula on a selected latent chart. Every nearby
closed-model representation is recovered, up to the explicitly stated
permutation action. For an observation metric $H$, if $J=DF$ and $G$
is the derivative of the locally labelled component roots, define
\[
 V_H=G(J^{\mathsf T}HJ)^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-condition} identifies the exact radial inverse
condition as $\max_a\sqrt{(V_H)_{aa}}$. In the categorical Fisher metric,
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-minimax} identifies the local asymptotic minimax
constant as $\E\|\mathcal N(0,V_H)\|_\infty^2$. The proof handles
sorting at collisions by a finite-permutation Gaussian quotient
experiment; it does not differentiate the sorting map. Thus one
observable matrix controls the deterministic and statistical local
problems on the full regular quotient, rather than only along a
specially engineered lower-bound family.

Theorem~\ref{thm:flag-gauge} gives a second structural extension. For
normalized numerator coefficients with a common complete invariant
flag, the scalar interpolation gauge preserves the flag and its zero
edges. It yields
\[
 d_\infty(Z(M),Z(M'))\le C\{(\delta/\tau)^{1/d}
                         +(B_\triangle\delta)^{1/(kd)}\}.
\]
A refined version replaces these exponents by the reciprocal scalar
cluster size and the reciprocal maximal root count along a flag path.
The true algebra may be noncommutative and defective; the competitor
need not preserve the flag. Normalization error is not charged the
longest nilpotent path merely because general matrix noise is.

For the remaining singular intersections,
Theorem~\ref{thm:modulus-minimax} defines an intrinsic Hellinger-ball
spectral diameter and proves two-sided local risk bounds using that
same modulus. Corollary~\ref{cor:arc-order} gives its attainable
admissible-arc exponent without discarding rank or normalization
boundaries. Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored-multiplicity} computes the
exponent on the identifiable real-rooted multiplicity stratum: it is
$1/2$ for an anchored comparison at any internal multiple root, whereas
the fixed-neighbourhood minimax squared-risk exponent remains $1/m$.
The neighbourhood definitions, and not competing descriptions of the
same decision problem, explain this distinction. The general modulus
is an exact metric characterization, not a claim that a short list of
defect integers classifies all singularities.

\subsection{Relation to previous work}
Rational interpolation and realization are the immediate algebraic
predecessors. Van Barel--Bultheel \cite{VBB} study vector rational
interpolation; Anderson--Antoulas \cite{AA90} relate matrix interpolation
to state-variable realization. Beckermann--Labahn \cite{BL00} treat
matrix rational interpolation and matrix greatest common divisors, and
Mayo--Antoulas \cite{MA07} develop generalized realization through the
Loewner framework. Our residual equation is a scalar-denominator,
bounded-degree interpolation system. Theorem~\ref{thm:rational-module}
makes the precise specialization explicit. Linearizing interpolation,
computing common divisors and obtaining minimal realizations are not
claimed here as new algorithms.

Bauer--Fike \cite{BF}, Chu's pencil and matrix-polynomial perturbation
theorems \cite{Chu87,Chu03}, structured polynomial pseudospectra
\cite{TH}, conditioning of linearizations \cite{HMT}, and rational
linearizations \cite{SB} provide the classical spectral background.
Lemma~\ref{lem:roots} supplies the needed exclusion and multiplicity
argument in explicit form. The new observation-level assertion is the
joint-projector gauge in Theorem~\ref{thm:joint}: an unknown scalar
normalization is recovered from probability observations without
multiplying its error by a second channel loss. The comparison is made
at the level of these separate statements in
Section~\ref{sec:general-quotient}.

Latent diagonalization and factor identification are classical
\cite{AMR,BJR}; quantitative tensor uniqueness is studied in
\cite{BCV}, and nuisance transformations in bilinear identification
in \cite{LLB}. Our target is a multiset, not stable labels for every
factor through a collision. The conversion from inverse moduli to
inference belongs to the established theory of optimal recovery and
honest confidence sets \cite{Donoho,CaiLow,Dufour}; singular mixture
rates have a substantial theory \cite{HN}. The present positive
families and joint coefficient analysis identify the normalization,
channel-product and scalar-multiplicity losses for this controlled
categorical experiment, including its adaptive designs.
