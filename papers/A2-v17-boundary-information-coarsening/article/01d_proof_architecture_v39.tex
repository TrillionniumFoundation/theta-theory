\subsection{Proof architecture and the role of the observation map}
\label{sec:v39-proof-architecture}

The principal analytic assertion is the relative nonlinear boundary law;
the principal inverse assertion is recovery of the two labelled contact
jets from signed transverse laws.  Their composition precedes the global
and statistical applications.  We describe the dependencies to distinguish
the nonlinear arguments from the algebraic and compactness steps that
complete those applications.

\emph{Relative asymptotics.}
The finite Jacobi calculation identifies both the endpoint Hessian and
the normalization of the mixed derivative.  The perturbation of the
interior Hessian is summable in trace norm, uniformly in the number of
flights.  Lemma~\ref{lem:g-relative} therefore controls a relative
determinant, rather than an absolute error divided by an exponentially
small reference twist.  The half-line construction then separates the
two endpoint layers.  Gluing the two half-line solutions and estimating
the discarded middle block gives the factorization in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-factorization}.  The uniform sublevel integral and its
fixed-domain differentiation yield Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-main-relative}.
Every derivative order is finite and fixed; the constants may depend on
that order and on the corresponding boundary norms.  The offset collar
is independent of the flight number.  Geometric differentiation is
channel-centered, with physical time written as \(t=jg(\xi)+d\).

\emph{Signed inversion.}
The four-density identity in Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-density-inverse}
removes the unknown amplitudes at one positive offset.  This algebra
recovers actions, not graph jets directly.  To pass to the contact
geometry one must differentiate the half-line action through finite
truncations, cancel the interior variations by stationarity, and control
the terminal term before taking the limit.  The smooth-remainder
argument proves finite-jet factorization for actual smooth graphs,
including graphs differing by flat terms.  Homogeneous degree isolation
then gives the triangular recursion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity}, with the explicit two-by-two
blocks in \eqref{eq:v25-intro-jet-block}.  Invertibility is established at
each fixed order; no uniform inverse on an unrestricted infinite jet
space is inferred from the determinant identity.

\emph{Intrinsic global reconstruction.}
Only after the local inverse is proved does analyticity extend the
recovered germs to connected boundary images.  The gluing problem
retains the full oriented signatures and the marked deck labels.
A common-frame rank-two anchoring pair recovers the period metric;
a rooted signature-rigid tree reaching every obstacle orbit places the
remaining images.  Realizability and unique signature matching are
hypotheses of this global conclusion, not consequences of the matrix
identity for the lattice.  The finite-signature embedding and noisy
matching theorem supply the local separation and uniqueness needed
before the compact inverse-modulus argument.  Neither compactness nor
analytic continuation alone gives a numerical reconstruction rate.

\emph{Statistical and physical consequences.}
The transfer estimates are used only after the boundary law is available.
Finite likelihood-vector convergence and a separate uniform-modulus
argument supply the compact-local Gaussian comparisons.  The
likelihood-tilting and original-alternative moment estimates justify the
quadratic-loss statements without assuming that total variation controls
unbounded losses.  The moving-ceiling comparison uses two actual Markov
kernels, together with separate layer, bulk, and corner estimates.
Finally, the global physical protocol includes a charged position-based
pilot.  Its post-pilot transverse statistic is coarser than the total
position record, and the direct-position benchmark belongs to that same
richer acquisition experiment.  The smooth-nuisance envelope in
Section~\ref{sec:v9-envelope-minimax} is a distinct comparison model;
its lower bound does not assert realizability of every splice by a
billiard table.  These distinctions are part of the mathematical
statements and remain in force throughout the applications.

The complete auxiliary proofs are included in the appendices.  No
statistical equivalence between different observation maps is used in
the deterministic contact or periodic rigidity arguments.
