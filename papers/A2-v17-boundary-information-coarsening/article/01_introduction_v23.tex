\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Long alternating collision bridges in a dispersing billiard carry an awkward
combination of scales.  Their mixed endpoint twist is exponentially small in
the number of flights, but after normalization by that exact twist the bridge
retains a nonlinear boundary profile.  An extensive approximation followed by
division by the twist loses this information.  The first part of this paper
establishes instead a relative boundary law on an endpoint box and a positive
offset collar independent of the flight number.  The limit consists of two
half-line stationary actions and two determinant amplitudes and is stable under
every fixed mixed geometric and offset derivative.

We then use that boundary law in three directions.  First, signed endpoint
support recovers the two unsymmetrized half-line actions, whose complete jets
recover the two labelled contact jets through an all-order block triangular
inverse with determinant-one new block at every order.  Second, for analytic
periodic tables we formulate the global inverse on intrinsic lifted-channel
data rather than on channel frames already placed in one laboratory.  The
global ambiguity becomes an explicit admissible gluing problem involving both
obstacle symmetries and periodic deck holonomy.  A signature-rigid spanning
tree together with one nonzero lattice-anchoring cycle yields full periodic
table rigidity up to one global Euclidean motion.  Third, we identify the
different local experiments carried by the endpoint--time transcript, the
count--endpoint coarsening and the endpoint-only coarsening.  Their fastest
scales are respectively Poisson-boundary, two-speed Gaussian and
moving-endpoint Gaussian.  The complete stopped endpoint--time statement is
transferred to actual finite bridges at the exact record level for which the
historical coupling theorem is proved.

Finally, compactness of an analytic class converts the exact all-order inverse
into a growing-order statistical bridge.  Finitely many action jets resolve
any prescribed global \(C^q\) tolerance, and uniformly consistent fixed-order
estimators can be diagonalized along one sequence \(M_n\to\infty\) to produce
global table consistency without assuming a uniform-in-order condition number.

The deterministic chain is
\[
 \boxed{\text{relative boundary law}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{all-order signed inverse}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{intrinsic lifted-channel gluing}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{periodic table rigidity}},
\]
while the statistical chain is
\[
 \boxed{\text{endpoint--time transcript}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{Poisson boundary shift}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{count--endpoint multirate limit}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{endpoint Gaussian coarsening}}.
\]

\subsection{Relative boundary law and signed contact inversion}

Fix an oriented closest-pair channel and write its facing contacts in a
channel frame as
\[
 (-\psi_0(u),u),\qquad (g+\psi_1(v),v),
\]
with gap \(g>0\).  If \(W_j(u,v)\) is the stationary length of the selected
\(j\)-flight alternating bridge, set
\[
 E_j=W_j-jg,
 \qquad t_j=-W_{j,uv}(0,0)>0,
 \qquad p=j\bmod2.
\]
The Jacobi recurrence has hyperbolic exponent \(\gamma>0\), and
\(t_j\asymp e^{-j\gamma}\).  The principal relative theorem gives, for every
fixed derivative order,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-relative}
 E_j(u,v)=S_0(u)+S_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),
 \qquad
 \frac{-W_{j,uv}(u,v)}{-W_{j,uv}(0,0)}
 =B_0(u)B_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j)
\end{equation}
for some \(0<\tau<1\).  The positive amplitudes \(B_b\) agree with the
normalized scalar linearizing densities of the physical stable returns.

For even flights, integrating out residual time gives an endpoint density
proportional to
\[
                    (d-S_b(u)-S_b(v))_+B_b(u)B_b(v).
\]
Hence signed support recovers
\[
 T_b(u,v)=S_b(u)+S_b(v),\qquad S_b(u)=T_b(u,0).
\]
If \(a_b=S_b''(0)\), physical onset and the quadratic action jets give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-leading}
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}),
 \quad
 c_0=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_0/a_1},
 \quad
 c_1=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_1/a_0},
 \quad
 \kappa_b=\frac{c_b-1}{g}.
\end{equation}
Thus neither contact curvature is supplied.

Write
\[
 \psi_b(y)=\frac{\kappa_b}{2}y^2+
 \sum_{n\ge3}\frac{q_{b,n}}{n!}y^n,
 \qquad
 s_n=(S_0^{(n)}(0),S_1^{(n)}(0))^t.
\]
After all lower graph jets have been fixed, the new pair
\(q_n=(q_{0,n},q_{1,n})^t\) enters \(s_n\) through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-jet-block}
 D_{q_n}s_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1=1,
\end{equation}
so
\[
                         \det D_{q_n}s_n=1.
\]
The proof is all-order: a weighted inverse for the nonlinear half-line
stationarity operator controls repeated differentiation; a finite-truncation
envelope identity removes interior orbit variations; and the homogeneous
filtration
\[
                     \partial_{q_{r,m}}S_b(u)=O(u^m)
\]
prevents higher graph jets from entering lower action jets.  For every fixed
finite order \(M\), the resulting signed-jet map has an invertible differential
and a compact-uniform lower singular-value bound.  No bound uniform in
\(M\to\infty\) is asserted.

\subsection{Intrinsic periodic gluing}

The periodic geometric setting indexes a measured channel by
\[
                         e=(a,b,\ell),\qquad \ell\in\Lambda.
\]
The intrinsic datum in
Section~\ref{sec:v23-intrinsic-multichannel-rigidity} retains this lifted label,
the onset, the signed endpoint-law germs and the transverse sign convention,
but not the Euclidean placement of the channel frame relative to the lattice
or to any other channel.  The all-order inverse and analyticity recover the
complete oriented analytic boundary images of the two obstacle lifts in every
edge frame.

Let \(\Lambda_{\rm abs}\) be the marked abstract Euclidean lattice and let
\(\iota\) be an unknown oriented Euclidean realization.  For an incidence at
deck offset \(\nu(e,\sigma)\), an algebraic gluing assigns
\(A_e\in\mathrm{SE}(2)\) and requires
\[
 \tau_{-\iota(\nu(e,\sigma))}A_eC_{e,\sigma}
 =
 \tau_{-\iota(\nu(f,\sigma'))}A_fC_{f,\sigma'}
\]
whenever the two incidences carry the same quotient obstacle label.  We keep
only those gluings whose periodic translates form a genuine dispersing table
and preserve the declared measured channels.  Quotienting by one simultaneous
global \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\) action gives the admissible periodic gluing space
\(\mathfrak G_{\rm per}(\mathscr D)\).  Theorem
\ref{thm:v23-intrinsic-table-rigidity} identifies this space with all periodic
table realizations of the intrinsic data modulo global Euclidean motion.

The gluing theorem separates the actual obstructions.  Equality of complete
oriented curvature signatures at two points of a connected real-analytic
strictly convex curve occurs exactly along orientation-preserving Euclidean
symmetry orbits.  Moreover, a signature-rigid cycle has a data-determined
Euclidean holonomy \(H_c=\tau_{v_c}\).  If its accumulated deck displacement is
\[
                         \eta_c=\sum_i\epsilon_i\ell_{e_i},
\]
then every realization satisfies
\[
                         R_{e_0}v_c=\iota(\eta_c),
\]
where \(R_{e_0}\) is the rotation of the starting channel placement.  A cycle
with \(\eta_c\ne0\) therefore fixes the relative orientation of the marked
lattice and the channel network.  Starting from an obstacle met by this cycle,
a rooted signature-rigid spanning tree then propagates every remaining
obstacle placement.  This gives the fully intrinsic global theorem.

If the lattice realization and one root channel frame are supplied as ambient
calibration, the anchoring cycle is unnecessary and the rooted tree propagates
all remaining channel frames.  Supplying every channel frame recovers the
registered v22 theorem as a special case.

This data architecture is different from marked-length rigidity.  Marked and
enriched spectra encode globally organized periodic-orbit lengths and lead to
rigidity under their corresponding analytic, symmetry or genericity
hypotheses \cite{BDKL,DSKL,Osterman,FinamoreLeguil}.  Here finitely many
lifted channel families retain continuum signed support germs.  No dominance
relation between the two observation architectures is claimed.

\subsection{Three physical information levels}

The physical preparation generates nested but statistically different
experiments.  At the richest level treated here, a successful preparation
retains the two signed endpoints and the residual preparation-to-first-impact
time, while a failure atom is retained on failure.  The stopped transcript
also retains every design, the waiting sequence and the stopping time.  For a
successful boundary record at design \(\ell\), the reference conditional
density is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-et-density}
 q_{\ell,0}(u,v,r)=
 \rho_\ell(u,v)\mathbf1_{\{0<r<w_\ell(u,v)\}},
 \qquad
 w_\ell=d_\ell-S_{b_\ell}(u)-S_{b_\ell}(v).
\end{equation}
The density has a positive trace at the moving ceiling.  With \(k_n\)
successful records, shape perturbations of order \(k_n^{-1}\) and gap
perturbations of order \((j_nk_n)^{-1}\) move that ceiling at the extreme-value
scale.  The scaled ceiling slacks converge to Poisson point processes with
intensity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-poisson-intensity}
 \alpha_\ell\rho_\ell(u,v)
 \mathbf1_{\{y>U_\ell(u,v)z\}}\,du\,dv\,dy.
\end{equation}
The limit is generally non-dominated.  A finite positive design makes the
support-velocity map injective.  Under \(j_n=o(\sqrt{k_n})\) the waiting counts
are ancillary at this fastest scale, and the existing stopped coupling
transfers the complete endpoint--time experiment to finite bridges when
\(k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0\).

Integrating out residual time changes the support singularity: the endpoint
density now vanishes linearly at its moving boundary.  If waiting counts are
retained, the success probability
\(p_{n,\ell}\asymp e^{-j_n\gamma}\) supplies an additional channel.  Write
\(H_\gamma=\ker D_\vartheta\gamma\) and choose
\(v_\gamma\) with \(D_\vartheta\gamma(v_\gamma)=1\).  At
\[
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1,
\]
the coordinate
\[
 \vartheta=\frac{b}{j_n\sqrt{k_n}}v_\gamma+\delta_nh,
 \qquad h\in H_\gamma,
 \qquad
 g=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a
\]
has an independent product limit: a one-dimensional Gaussian waiting-count
shift in \(b\), and the moving-endpoint Gaussian shift in \((a,h)\).  Thus the
count channel sees a faster hyperbolic direction which disappears when waiting
counts are discarded.

Removing waiting counts as well gives the endpoint-output experiment.  The
parameter-independent common-collar construction produces a dominated LAN
representative with intrinsic boundary matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-boundary-info}
 \mathcal J_\Sigma=
 \int_\Sigma\frac{a_0VV^t}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}
The three limits are therefore limits of three different observation
sigma-fields, not three descriptions of one experiment.

\subsection{Growing-order analytic recovery}

For a compact analytic class with singleton admissible periodic gluing, let
\[
 \mathcal D_M(T)=
 \bigl(g_e,S_{e,0}^{(m)}(0),S_{e,1}^{(m)}(0):
           e\in E,\ 2\le m\le M\bigr).
\]
The lifted channel labels are fixed by the design.  Theorem
\ref{thm:v23-finite-coordinate-resolution} proves that for every integer
\(q\ge0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\) there exist finite \(M\) and \(\eta>0\) such
that
\[
 \|\mathcal D_M(T)-\mathcal D_M(T')\|<\eta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d_q(T,T')<\varepsilon.
\]
This follows by compactness and injectivity of the complete intrinsic data map;
it does not numerically continue a noisy Taylor series.

If every fixed \(\mathcal D_M\) is uniformly consistently estimable, one can
diagonalize simultaneously over jet order and all fixed \(C^q\) losses.  A
single deterministic sequence \(M_n\to\infty\) then yields global table
consistency.  This supplies the missing exact-to-statistical bridge without
assuming uniform conditioning as \(M\to\infty\).  Sharp analytic minimax rates
are not claimed.

The remainder of the paper proves these statements in the order above.  The
mathematical modules supporting the accepted earlier boundary and statistical
results are retained, while revision history and referee provenance are kept
outside the theorem narrative.
