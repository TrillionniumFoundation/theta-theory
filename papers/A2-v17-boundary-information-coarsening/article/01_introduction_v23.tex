\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A recurring difficulty in inverse hyperbolic dynamics is that the observable
quantity produced by a long orbit segment is simultaneously very small and
very structured.  For a long alternating bridge between two facing dispersing
contacts, the mixed endpoint twist is exponentially small in the flight
number.  Dividing an extensive approximation error by that twist destroys the
inverse problem.  The starting point of this paper is a relative boundary
limit which avoids that loss: after normalization by the exact endpoint twist,
the finite bridge converges on a fixed positive offset collar to two half-line
stationary actions and two determinant amplitudes.  Those boundary objects are
nonlinear and retain the signed contact geometry.

The paper develops this boundary law in three directions.  First, signed
endpoint support determines the two unsymmetrized half-line actions, and their
jets recover the complete labelled jets of the two contacts through an
all-order block triangular inverse whose new \(2\times2\) block has determinant
one at every order.  Second, in the analytic periodic category we remove the
common laboratory registration from the global theorem.  A connected graph of
intrinsic lifted-channel data determines framed analytic copies of the
participating obstacle lifts.  Their Euclidean matching, together with the
deck translations, produces a data-determined symmetry/lattice-holonomy space.
A signature-rigid spanning tree propagates the obstacle placements, while one
nonzero lattice-anchoring cycle fixes the relative orientation of the marked
periodic lattice.  Under these conditions the complete periodic table is
reconstructed up to one global Euclidean motion.  Third, we identify the local
statistical experiments carried by three successive physical observation
levels.  Retaining residual preparation time gives a non-dominated Poisson
boundary-shift experiment at the fastest geometric scale; integrating out
residual time but retaining waiting counts gives a two-speed Gaussian
experiment; discarding the waiting counts as well leaves the slower
moving-endpoint Gaussian experiment.  These statements are transferred to
actual finite bridges at the exact stopped-record level for which the physical
coupling is proved.

A final compactness theorem connects the finite-dimensional statistical
results to analytic global recovery.  On a compact analytic class on which the
intrinsic periodic gluing is unique, finitely many channel action jets resolve
any prescribed global \(C^q\) accuracy.  Consequently any collection of
uniformly consistent fixed-order estimators admits a diagonal choice of jet
order \(M_n\to\infty\) and a globally consistent table estimator.  This bridge
does not assume a uniform-in-order condition number and does not replace noisy
analytic continuation by a formal Taylor series.

The theorem architecture is
\[
 \boxed{\text{relative long-bridge law}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{all-order signed inverse}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{intrinsic lifted-channel gluing}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{periodic table rigidity}},
\]
and, on the statistical side,
\[
 \boxed{\text{endpoint--time transcript}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{Poisson boundary experiment}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{count--endpoint multirate experiment}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{endpoint Gaussian coarsening}}.
\]
The deterministic and statistical chains meet through the finite action-jet
coordinates and the growing-order compact analytic reconstruction theorem.

\subsection{The nonlinear boundary law}

Fix a labelled closest-pair channel between two strictly convex contacts in a
smooth periodic dispersing billiard.  In an oriented channel frame write
\[
 (-\psi_0(u),u),\qquad (g+\psi_1(v),v),
\]
with gap \(g>0\).  No reflection symmetry, equality of the contacts or
prescribed curvature is assumed.  Let \(W_j(u,v)\) be the stationary length of
the selected alternating \(j\)-flight bridge, put
\[
 E_j(u,v)=W_j(u,v)-jg,
 \qquad
 t_j=-\partial_{uv}W_j(0,0)>0,
\]
and let \(p=j\bmod2\).  The quadratic Jacobi recurrence has a hyperbolic
exponent \(\gamma>0\), with \(t_j\asymp e^{-j\gamma}\).  On an endpoint box
and an offset collar independent of \(j\), the relative theorem gives, for
each fixed derivative order,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-relative}
 E_j(u,v)=S_0(u)+S_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),
 \qquad
 \frac{-W_{j,uv}(u,v)}{-W_{j,uv}(0,0)}
 =B_0(u)B_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j)
\end{equation}
for some \(0<\tau<1\).  Here \(S_0,S_1\) are strictly convex half-line
actions and \(B_0,B_1\) are positive determinant amplitudes.  The error is
relative to the exponentially small twist, rather than an absolute error which
is subsequently divided by it.

The amplitudes also agree with the normalized scalar linearizing densities of
the physical stable returns.  Thus the determinant and dynamical
linearization constructions produce the same boundary amplitude, while the
stationary-action theorem itself is variational and does not require that
interpretation.

\subsection{Signed support and the determinant-one inverse}

Integrating out residual time but retaining signed endpoint positions gives,
for an even flight number and endpoint type \(b\), a limiting conditional
density proportional to
\[
                 (d-S_b(u)-S_b(v))_+B_b(u)B_b(v).
\]
Since \(B_b>0\), the observable support threshold is
\[
 T_b(u,v)=S_b(u)+S_b(v),\qquad S_b(u)=T_b(u,0).
\]
The two unsymmetrized actions are therefore recovered from signed endpoint
support.  Physical onset gives \(g\).  If \(a_b=S_b''(0)\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-leading}
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}),
 \quad
 c_0=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_0/a_1},
 \quad
 c_1=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_1/a_0},
 \quad
 \kappa_b=\frac{c_b-1}{g},
\end{equation}
so both labelled curvatures are recovered rather than supplied.

Write
\[
 \psi_b(y)=\frac{\kappa_b}{2}y^2+
 \sum_{n\ge3}\frac{q_{b,n}}{n!}y^n,
 \qquad
 s_n=(S_0^{(n)}(0),S_1^{(n)}(0))^t.
\]
After lower graph jets have been fixed, the new pair
\(q_n=(q_{0,n},q_{1,n})^t\) enters \(s_n\) through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-jet-block}
 D_{q_n}s_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1=1,
\end{equation}
and hence
\[
                         \det D_{q_n}s_n=1
                         \qquad(n\ge3).
\]
The proof is genuinely all-order.  A weighted inverse for the nonlinear
half-line stationarity operator gives uniform control of differentiated
stationary orbits.  A finite-truncation envelope identity removes all interior
orbit variations and sends the terminal term to zero.  A separate homogeneous
filtration proves
\[
                    \partial_{q_{r,m}}S_b(u)=O(u^m),
\]
so graph jets of order \(m>n\) cannot enter the \(n\)-th action jet.  At the
first allowed order only the linear half-line orbit contributes, producing
\eqref{eq:intro-v23-jet-block}.  For each fixed finite order \(M\), the signed
jet map has an everywhere invertible differential and a compact-uniform lower
singular-value bound.  No uniform-in-\(M\) conditioning is asserted.

\subsection{Intrinsic lifted-channel rigidity and periodic holonomy}

The local theorem is stated in a channel frame, while the periodic geometric
setting indexes a channel by
\[
                         e=(a,b,\ell),\qquad \ell\in\Lambda.
\]
The global data in Section~\ref{sec:v23-intrinsic-multichannel-rigidity}
therefore retain the lifted label \((a,b,\ell)\), onset, signed endpoint-law
germs and transverse orientation, but do not supply the Euclidean placement of
the channel frame relative to the marked lattice or to another channel.
The all-order inverse and analyticity recover complete oriented analytic
copies of the two lifted obstacle boundaries in every edge frame.

Let \(\Lambda_{\rm abs}\) be the marked abstract Euclidean lattice and
\(\iota\) an unknown oriented Euclidean realization of it.  If an incidence
\((e,\sigma)\) lies at deck offset \(\nu(e,\sigma)\), a periodic gluing assigns
an \(A_e\in\mathrm{SE}(2)\) to every edge and requires
\[
 \tau_{-\iota(\nu(e,\sigma))}A_eC_{e,\sigma}
 =
 \tau_{-\iota(\nu(f,\sigma'))}A_fC_{f,\sigma'}
\]
whenever the two incidences have the same quotient obstacle label.  Quotienting
these gluings by one simultaneous Euclidean motion of \(\iota\) and every
\(A_e\) gives the data-determined space
\(\mathfrak G_{\rm per}(\mathscr D)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-intrinsic-table-rigidity} identifies this space exactly
with the set of periodic table realizations modulo global
\(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).

The construction isolates two genuine obstructions.  First, for an analytic
strictly convex curve, equality of the complete oriented curvature signature
at two points occurs precisely when an orientation-preserving Euclidean
symmetry carries one framed point to the other.  Second, a spanning tree can
propagate obstacle placements but need not determine the orientation of the
periodic lattice relative to the channel network.  The latter is fixed by a
cycle whose accumulated deck displacement
\[
                         \eta_c=\sum_i\epsilon_i\ell_{e_i}
\]
is nonzero.  Signature-rigid matching around such a cycle produces a measured
translation \(v_c\) and the holonomy identity
\[
                         \iota(\eta_c)=v_c
\]
in the starting channel frame.  Because the lattice Gram form and orientation
are fixed, the image of one nonzero deck vector fixes \(\iota\) relative to
that frame.  A signature-rigid spanning tree together with one such
lattice-anchoring cycle therefore makes the periodic gluing unique.  If an
ambient oriented lattice frame is known independently, the anchoring cycle is
unnecessary and the spanning tree alone suffices.

This is different from marked-length rigidity.  Marked and enriched spectra
encode globally organized periodic-orbit lengths and lead to rigidity under
their corresponding analytic, symmetry or genericity hypotheses
\cite{BDKL,DSKL,Osterman,FinamoreLeguil}.  The present observations are local
to finitely many lifted channel families but retain continuum signed support
germs.  No dominance relation between the data architectures is claimed.  The
new point is that inter-channel placement and periodic deck holonomy are part
of the inverse problem rather than hidden input.

\subsection{Three physical information levels}

The same boundary law produces different local experiments under different
common coarsenings.  We keep these observation sigma-fields separate.

At the richest level considered here, one preparation retains the two signed
endpoints, the residual preparation-to-first-impact time and the failure
symbol.  A stopped policy retains all designs, every success or failure and
the stopping time.  Conditional on a successful record at design \(\ell\),
the boundary model has density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-et-density}
 q_{\ell,0}(u,v,r)=
 \rho_\ell(u,v)
 \mathbf 1_{\{0<r<w_\ell(u,v)\}},
 \qquad
 w_\ell=d_\ell-S_{b_\ell}(u)-S_{b_\ell}(v),
\end{equation}
with positive trace at the moving ceiling \(r=w_\ell(u,v)\).  With
\(k_n\) successful records, the local parameter
\[
 g=g_0+\frac{a}{j_nk_n},
 \qquad
 \vartheta=\frac{h}{k_n}
\]
moves that ceiling by \(k_n^{-1}U_\ell(a,h)\).  The extreme ceiling slacks
then converge to Poisson point processes with intensity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-poisson-intensity}
 \alpha_\ell\rho_\ell(u,v)
 \mathbf 1_{\{y>U_\ell(u,v)(a,h)\}}\,du\,dv\,dy.
\end{equation}
The limit is generally non-dominated.  A finite positive design makes the
collection of support velocities injective, so the full fixed finite jet is
visible at the \(k_n^{-1}\) shape scale and the gap at
\((j_nk_n)^{-1}\).  Under \(j_n=o(\sqrt{k_n})\), the waiting count is
asymptotically ancillary at this fastest scale.  The stopped finite-to-boundary
coupling transfers the complete endpoint--time result to actual finite
bridges when \(k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0\).

Integrating out residual time turns \eqref{eq:intro-v23-et-density} into a
linearly vanishing endpoint density.  If waiting counts are retained, a second
channel survives through the rare success probability
\(p_{n,\ell}\asymp e^{-j_n\gamma}\).  Decompose the shape tangent into the
hyperbolic direction \(v_\gamma\), on which
\(D\gamma(v_\gamma)=1\), and the iso-hyperbolic tangent
\(H_\gamma=\ker D\gamma\).  At
\[
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1
\]
the multirate local coordinate
\[
 \vartheta=\frac{b}{j_n\sqrt{k_n}}v_\gamma+\delta_nh,
 \qquad h\in H_\gamma,
 \qquad
 g=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a
\]
produces the independent product of a one-dimensional Gaussian count shift in
\(b\) and the endpoint Gaussian shift in \((a,h)\).  Thus the waiting counts
carry a faster hyperbolic direction which is absent after they are discarded.

Finally, if waiting counts are also removed, one obtains the endpoint-output
experiment developed from the general moving-boundary theorem.  A
parameter-independent common collar gives a dominated representative at
vanishing Le Cam distance from the original non-dominated family, and the
representative is uniformly LAN with intrinsic boundary matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-boundary-info}
 \mathcal J_\Sigma=
 \int_\Sigma\frac{a_0VV^t}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}
On the anchored billiard model, finitely many same-type positive windows give
a positive-definite summed matrix on every fixed finite labelled contact-jet
model.  Same-type designs use even flight numbers; physical times and endpoint
coordinates are fixed in one laboratory chart; deterministic caps are chosen
from the reference success probabilities before any compact local set is
quantified over.

The three limits are not competing descriptions of one experiment.  They are
the limits of three nested observation sigma-fields.  Retaining residual time
exposes the fastest extreme boundary layer.  Removing it leaves the count and
endpoint channels.  Removing the count channel as well leaves the slower
moving-endpoint Gaussian experiment.

\subsection{From finite-jet information to global analytic loss}

The exact global theorem uses the complete analytic germ, while each local
information theorem first fixes a finite contact-jet order.  Section
\ref{sec:v23-analytic-global-bridge} closes this logical gap on compact
analytic classes.  For \(M\ge2\), let
\[
 \mathcal D_M(T)=
 \bigl(g_e,S_{e,0}^{(m)}(0),S_{e,1}^{(m)}(0):
           e\in E,\ 2\le m\le M\bigr)
\]
be the finite intrinsic action data, with the lifted channel labels fixed by
the experimental design.  If the analytic class is compact and the periodic
intrinsic gluing is unique, then for every \(q\) and \(\varepsilon>0\) there
exist finite \(M\) and \(\eta>0\) such that
\[
 \|\mathcal D_M(T)-\mathcal D_M(T')\|<\eta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d_q(T,T')<\varepsilon.
\]
The proof is a compactness contradiction: otherwise one obtains two limiting
tables agreeing in every finite action jet and hence in the full intrinsic
lifted-channel data, contradicting global rigidity.

This finite-coordinate resolution gives a diagonal statistical consequence.
If, for every fixed \(M\), the physical experiment admits uniformly consistent
estimators of \(\mathcal D_M\), then one can choose a deterministic
\(M_n\to\infty\) arbitrarily slowly and obtain estimators of the complete table
which are uniformly consistent in every fixed global \(C^q\) loss.  This is a
genuine growing-order bridge, but it is not advertised as a sharp analytic
minimax theorem: an optimal relation among analytic radius, jet order,
preparation cost and global risk would require uniform quantitative
conditioning as \(M\to\infty\), which is not assumed here.

\subsection{Organization and scope}

The first part establishes the relative boundary law, its determinant
factorization and the all-order signed inverse, followed by intrinsic periodic
gluing.  The second part develops the non-dominated moving-boundary theorem,
the fixed laboratory endpoint experiment, the endpoint--time Poisson limit,
the count--endpoint multirate limit and the growing-order analytic bridge.
Historical auxiliary transforms and application lemmas needed by the theorem
chain are retained in the article and appendices, but the active statements
above do not depend on revision history.

We make three scope distinctions explicit.  First, the global analytic theorem
uses finitely many lifted channel families, not finitely many real numbers:
each channel supplies a continuum support germ containing all action jets.
Second, the complete stopped endpoint--time theorem concerns the exact
coarsened record for which the finite-to-boundary comparison is proved; it is
not a theorem for the full growing collision array.  Third, fixed-order
quantitative inverse bounds are not silently promoted to bounds uniform in jet
order.  These separations are part of the theorem statements rather than
after-the-fact qualifications.
