\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

This paper studies a single mechanism from three points of view.  A long
alternating collision bridge in a planar dispersing billiard has an
exponentially small endpoint twist.  After dividing by that small quantity,
the bridge has a nonlinear boundary law on an offset collar whose size does
not shrink with the number of flights.  The same boundary law encodes the
local geometry of the two participating contacts, and after a natural
coarsening it produces a nonregular statistical experiment with a logarithmic
information scale.  Our main result is the chain
\[
 \boxed{\text{relative boundary law}}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\text{signed local rigidity}}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\text{raw physical local experiment}}.
\]
The last arrow is formulated on fixed physical time windows and a common
registered endpoint coordinate system; it does not use a parameter-dependent
observation rule.

\subsection{The geometric setting and the relative law}

Fix a labelled closest pair of strictly convex contacts in a smooth periodic
dispersing billiard.  In the registered contact chart the facing boundaries
are written
\[
 (-\psi_0(u),u),\qquad (g+\psi_1(v),v),
\]
with gap $g>0$.  No reflection symmetry or equality of the two contacts is
assumed.  Let $W_j(u,v)$ be the stationary length of the selected alternating
$j$-flight bridge and write
\[
 E_j(u,v)=W_j(u,v)-jg,
 \qquad
 t_j=-\partial_{uv}W_j(0,0)>0.
\]
The quadratic Jacobi recurrence has exponent $\gamma>0$ and
$t_j\asymp e^{-j\gamma}$.  The principal forward theorem proves, on a fixed
endpoint box and a fixed positive offset collar, a relative factorization of
the exact mixed endpoint derivative and of the associated physical flux.  In
particular there are half-line actions $S_0,S_1$ and positive half-line
amplitudes $B_0,B_1$ such that, after the parity of the terminal contact is
fixed,
\[
 E_j(u,v)=S_0(u)+S_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),
 \qquad 0<\tau<1,
\]
for every fixed derivative order.  The corresponding normalized endpoint
flux factorizes as $B_0(u)B_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j)$.  The construction is
nonlinear: the exponentially small twist is normalized before the long-bridge
limit is taken, and the determinant comparison is localized at the two ends
through trace-class perturbations rather than estimated by summing a per-site
error.

The same half-line amplitudes are the normalized scalar linearizing densities
of the physical stable return maps.  This identification ties the relative
determinant law to the intrinsic one-dimensional transport along the stable
branches; it is not used as a substitute for the nonlinear bridge proof.

\subsection{Signed endpoint support determines arbitrary labelled contact jets}

If residual time is integrated out while the two signed endpoint positions
are retained, the limiting same-type endpoint density is positive precisely
on
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-support}
                   S_b(u)+S_b(v)<d.
\end{equation}
Hence the support threshold
\[
 T_b(u,v)=\inf\{d:(u,v)\text{ lies in the interior support}\}
\]
satisfies
\[
 T_b(u,v)=S_b(u)+S_b(v),\qquad S_b(u)=T_b(u,0).
\]
The endpoint record therefore recovers the \emph{unsymmetrized} half-line
actions.  The loss of odd information in the scalar onset/profile experiment
is not a geometric symmetry; it is caused by a coarsening of the record.

Let $a_b=S_b''(0)$.  Physical onset gives $g$, and the exact half-line Hessian
identities yield
\[
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}),
 \quad
 c_0=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_0/a_1},
 \quad
 c_1=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_1/a_0},
 \quad
 \kappa_b=\frac{c_b-1}{g}.
\]
Thus neither contact curvature is supplied.  If
\[
 \psi_b(y)=\frac{\kappa_b}{2}y^2+
 \sum_{n\ge3}\frac{q_{b,n}}{n!}y^n,
 \qquad
 s_n=(S_0^{(n)}(0),S_1^{(n)}(0))^t,
\]
then, after all lower jets are fixed, the new pair $q_n$ enters $s_n$ through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-jet-block}
 D_{q_n}s_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1=1.
\end{equation}
Its determinant is exactly one.  The two labelled contact jets are therefore
recovered recursively at every finite order, odd and even.  Real-analytic
signed endpoint-law germs determine the two participating analytic contact
germs; for connected analytic boundaries the germs continue to the
participating boundary images.  For finite long bridges the reconstruction
error is $O_M(\tau^j)$ through every fixed order $M$.

This theorem is local and labelled.  It does not claim that one selected
channel determines every obstacle in a billiard table.  Its information set is
also different from a marked length spectrum.  Marked-length rigidity for
open dispersing billiards uses periodic-orbit lengths over a symbolic family
and, in the strongest analytic results, obtains global geometric conclusions
under analyticity and additional symmetry or genericity hypotheses
\cite{BDKL,DSKL,Osterman}.  The recent enriched marked-length approach for
finite-horizon Sinai billiards is global and uses a substantially enlarged
periodic-data object \cite{FinamoreLeguil}.  Our endpoint law is narrower
globally---it concerns one registered closest-pair channel---but richer
locally: it contains a continuum of signed collision-coordinate support data
and determines arbitrary smooth labelled contact jets without reflection
symmetry or supplied curvature.  Neither data set is a formal coarsening of
the other in the present paper.  The point of comparison is therefore the
mechanism and the information content, not a claim that the endpoint law
subsumes marked-length rigidity.

\subsection{Boundary information}

The endpoint density in \eqref{eq:intro-support} vanishes linearly at a moving
support boundary.  For a general family
\[
 f_\theta(x)=a_\theta(x)(w_\theta(x))_+,
 \qquad \theta\in\mathbb R^r,
\]
with a regular base hypersurface $\Sigma=\{w_0=0\}$, put
\[
 V=D_\theta w_\theta|_0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal J_\Sigma
 =\int_\Sigma\frac{a_0VV^t}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma.
\]
The singular part of the score is $V/w_0$.  Since the base density contributes
a factor $w_0$, its truncated second moment diverges only logarithmically.
The paper proves, with explicit collar, centering, third-moment and
fourth-moment estimates, that at
\[
 np_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1
\]
the independently thinned experiment, with its failure atom retained, is
locally asymptotically normal with information $\mathcal J_\Sigma$.  The
scalar Hellinger expansion is
\[
 H^2(f_t,f_0)=\frac{\mathcal I_\Sigma}{4}
 t^2\log(1/|t|)+O(t^2).
\]
The vector theorem gives the corresponding Gaussian testing profile and the
finite-dimensional local asymptotic minimax bound.

The existence of nonregular asymptotic normality for linearly vanishing
one-dimensional endpoint models is classical; parameter-dependent support,
limit experiments and local asymptotic minimax methods are classical as well
\cite{Smith1985,IbragimovHasminskii,HiranoPorter}.  Recent work also studies
optimal testing in nonregular parameter-dependent-support models.  We do not
claim those mechanisms as new.  What is used here is the intrinsic
regular-hypersurface coefficient, its vector polarization, and its coupling
to the signed billiard inverse and the long-bridge transfer.

\subsection{The raw physical local experiment}

A statistical experiment for an unknown geometry cannot choose its observation
window by first reading the unknown gap.  We therefore formulate the billiard
LAN theorem on fixed physical windows.  Fix a reference registered geometry
$(g_0,0)$ and choose
\[
                  t_{j,\ell}=jg_0+d_\ell,
 \qquad 0<d_\ell<d_*.
\]
These are programmed physical times and do not change under the local
alternative.  Let $\vartheta\in\mathbb R^r$ denote the contact geometry in
the fixed registered frames.  The natural local scaling is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-multirate}
       g=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a,
 \qquad
       \vartheta=\delta_n h.
\end{equation}
Indeed the fixed physical window has excess
\[
 t_{j_n,\ell}-j_ng=d_\ell-\delta_na.
\]
Thus the gap direction moves the endpoint support by order $\delta_n$ even
though the gap itself changes only by $\delta_n/j_n$.

For a same-type design $(b,d)$ the limiting support velocity in the local
direction $z=(a,h)$ is
\[
 U_b(u,v)z
 =a+D_\vartheta S_b(u)[h]+D_\vartheta S_b(v)[h].
\]
Its boundary information matrix is
\[
 \mathcal K_{b,d}
 =\int_{\{S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d\}}
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)U_b(u,v)^tU_b(u,v)}
 {|\nabla(S_b\oplus S_b)(u,v)|}\,d\sigma.
\]
If a vector lies in the kernel of all these matrices, then taking the
$v=0$ slice and sending $u\to0$ first forces its gap component to vanish;
the remaining action derivative vanishes for both contacts, and the
signed-rigidity recursion forces all graph-jet components to vanish.  A
compactness argument therefore selects finitely many positive endpoint
windows whose weighted information matrix is positive definite on every
fixed finite labelled contact-jet model.

At those fixed physical windows, collect prescribed numbers of selected
endpoint records, stopping each batch when its target is reached.  If
$k_n$ is the total number of successful records and
\[
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1,
\]
the endpoint-output experiment is LAN for $(a,h)$ with the finite-design
matrix above.  The gap is therefore visible at rate $\delta_n/j_n$ and the
registered contact geometry at rate $\delta_n$ in the same
parameter-independent physical design.

The physical preparations are rare and are not treated as free.  The stopped
transfer theorem of Section~\ref{sec:v16-adaptive} compares the complete
finite-bridge transcript---including failures and stopping times---with the
boundary transcript.  In the present local experiment it yields the uniform
bound
\[
 \sup_{z\in K}
 \|\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}-\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le Ck_n\tau^{j_n}.
\]
Consequently
\[
                  k_n\tau^{j_n}\longrightarrow0
\]
transfers the local experiment, testing profile and bounded local risks to
actual long finite bridges.  The inference output may deliberately retain
only the successful endpoints, but the raw number of preparations needed to
produce them remains charged.  Waiting counts can contain additional
information through the factor $\sinh(j\gamma)^{-1}$; the paper does not call
them ancillary or assert that the richer transcript has no faster directions.

There is also a fully charged route back to centered windows.  The historical
self-calibration theorem estimates the gap from onset counts.  If the pilot
satisfies
\[
 j_n|\widehat g_n-g|=o(\delta_n)
\]
in the logarithmic Hellinger sense made precise in
Corollary~\ref{cor:v20-pilot-centered}, then the post-pilot windows
$j_n\widehat g_n+d_\ell$ are asymptotically equivalent to the oracle centered
windows.  The pilot preparation cost is printed explicitly; no calibration
is inserted as a free observation.

\subsection{Observation coarsening}

For a fixed table the same relative boundary structure produces three
different comparison scales.  Complete endpoint--residual records distinguish
the finite bridge from its boundary model at the $q_j=e^{-j\gamma}$ scale.
If residual time is integrated out but endpoints are retained, the linear
vanishing of the endpoint density moves the critical scale to
$q_j^2\log(1/q_j)$.  If only the tangent success indicator is retained, the
leading endpoint shape information disappears.  In the exactly whitened
endpoint tangent model the hypersurface coefficient is one.  This hierarchy
is a comparison of coarsenings of a fixed physical record and is separate
from the unknown-geometry LAN theorem above.

\subsection{Scope and organization}

The principal deterministic theorem concerns a selected labelled channel and
recovers the two participating local contact geometries.  The principal
statistical theorem concerns a local family around a specified registered
reference channel.  The absolute Euclidean pose, contact-label exchange and an
unknown construction of the endpoint frames are not silently included in the
geometric parameter.  If a physical apparatus does not provide that
registration, it is a nuisance parameter or a pilot problem; the paper states
the Hellinger scale at which a registration pilot is negligible rather than
assuming it for free.

Part I proves the nonlinear relative law, scalar transport and signed contact
rigidity.  Part II proves the scalar and vector boundary-information theorems,
the fixed-window raw physical local experiment, its finite-bridge transfer and
the fixed-table coarsening hierarchy.  Historical acquisition, calibration,
comparison and inverse results are retained in the auxiliary compendium for
traceability; they are not used to inflate the statement of the main theorem
package above.
