\subsection{A quantitative inverse for a coarsened observation}
\label{subsec:v46-intro-quantized}

Exact analytic determination and stable reconstruction from approximate laws
are different conclusions.  The following theorem passes between them under
quantitative analytic bounds.  Unlike a stability statement on already
recovered curve images, its error is measured directly in finitely many
probabilities of quantized transverse endpoint events.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-resolution law-to-table reconstruction]
\label{thm:v46-intro-quantized}
Fix a persistent $N+1$-channel design and a quantified analytic class as in
Section~\ref{sec:v46-quantized-stability}.  The bounds include a common
holomorphic support strip, positive contact and density margins, finite-order
forward density bounds, and a finite nonvanishing harmonic witness for the
proper asymmetry of each obstacle.  There is an explicit conditional modulus
$\Omega(e_g,e_p+C_{\rm bin}\delta)$ tending to zero with its arguments such
that two tables whose onsets differ by at most $e_g$ and whose signed endpoint
histograms of mesh $\delta$ differ by at most $e_p$ in $\ell^1$ satisfy
\[
 \inf_{(U,t)\in\mathrm{SE}(2)}
 \left\{\|L-U\widetilde L\|+
 \max_a d_H(K_a,U\widetilde K_a+t)\right\}
 \le\Omega(e_g,e_p+C_{\rm bin}\delta).
\]
The same estimate controls the marked lattice Gram matrix.  Every positive
target accuracy has a finite prescription for the required jet order, grid
resolution and probability accuracy.  On a calibrated design, finitely many
successful even-flight observations give a confidence reconstruction with
explicit categorical sampling, finite-flight bias and preparation-cap bounds.
No longitudinal endpoint coordinate is used by this reconstruction.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:v46-quantized-inverse} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:v46-confidence} give the constants and proofs.  The inverse
is not assumed quantitatively: a cell-average estimate and an interior
interpolation inequality first control a finite density jet.  A finite Bellman
recursion evaluates the lower-order terms in the determinant-one contact
inverse.  Contact graphs then give anchored support jets.  A chain of
three-circles estimates continues these finite jets around the whole boundary,
and a B\'ezout formula for finitely many nonzero harmonics supplies stable
registration of all incidences.  The resulting modulus is an infimum of
fully specified finite-order bounds.  To attain a given error one uses one
finite order, without estimating the conditioning of an infinite triangular
system or appealing to an unspecified compactness separation constant.

The analytic width and its associated bounds are additional quantitative
priors; the earlier qualitative theorem retains its full statement.  The
number of histogram cells grows with accuracy, so the result is not exact
recovery from a fixed finite number of scalars.  Calibration is specified
separately from the reconstruction transcript.  The richer position experiment
and its direct interpolation benchmark remain distinct.  The continuation
and empirical-frequency estimates are classical mechanisms, and their severe
conditioning is not claimed as a new analytic-continuation principle.  The
conclusion here is their quantified composition with the nonlinear billiard
law-to-contact inverse and the intrinsic periodic gluing construction.
