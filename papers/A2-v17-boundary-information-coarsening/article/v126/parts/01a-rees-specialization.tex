\subsection*{Canonical specialization of the whole nonreduced scheme}

The associated graded algebra in Theorem~\ref{thm:main-depth} is not
only a bookkeeping device. It is the special fibre of a canonical
flat degeneration of the entire multiplication-failure scheme.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical Rees specialization]
\label{thm:canonical-rees-specialization}
Let \(R\in G_4^\circ\), let
\(\widehat{\mathcal N}_R\subset\mathcal O_{\widehat D_R}\)
be the nilradical, and form the extended Rees algebra
\[
 \mathscr R_R
 =
 \mathcal O_{\widehat D_R}
 [t,\widehat{\mathcal N}_R t^{-1}]
 \subset
 \mathcal O_{\widehat D_R}[t,t^{-1}].
\]
The affine-local spectra of \(\mathscr R_R\) glue to a canonical
scheme
\[
 \mathfrak D_R\longrightarrow\mathbb A^1
\]
that is flat over \(\mathbb A^1\). Over \(\mathbb G_m\),
\[
 \mathfrak D_R|_{\mathbb G_m}
 \simeq
 \widehat D_R\times\mathbb G_m,
\]
whereas the fibre over \(0\) is
\[
 \mathfrak D_{R,0}
 \simeq
 \operatorname{Spec}_{\widehat\Delta_R}
 \operatorname{gr}_{\widehat{\mathcal N}_R}
 \mathcal O_{\widehat D_R}
 =
 C_{\widehat\Delta_R/\widehat D_R}.
\]
Thus the special fibre is the intrinsic normal cone of the reduction
inside the multiplication-failure scheme. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:global-residual-colon}, its graded structure is
\[
 \mathcal O_{\widehat\Delta_R}
 \oplus
 \bigoplus_{j\ge1}
 \mathcal L^{\otimes j}\otimes\mathcal O_{W_j},
\]
with multiplication induced by the residual-colon tower.

The construction is functorial under abstract isomorphisms of
multiplication-failure schemes. It therefore requires neither a
chosen Grassmannian embedding nor a chosen projection chart.
\end{theorem}

The proof is given in Section~\ref{sec:rees-specialization}. On the
mixed-regular rank-two boundary its special fibre is the three-step
graded algebra of Theorem~\ref{thm:graded-nilpotent}; at the
decomposable mixed-kernel wall the degree-two support becomes the
length-three scheme of Theorem~\ref{thm:decomposable-kernel-wall}.
Hence the collision and tensor-rank boundary calculations are
restrictions of one global specialization mechanism, not independent
normal-form assertions.
