\section{Components in all dimensions}\label{sec:all-dimensions}
We retain the entire range $4\le c<k$ and $Lq\ge p$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:global-intro}. Let $\Phi_L$ be the closure of the
image of the incidence with nonsingular $H_\ell$. Let
$\Sigma_\varepsilon$ denote the signed secant image: for distinct $x,y$
and $t\ne0$ its points satisfy
\[
 U_\nu\subset\ker(\operatorname{ev}_x+
          \varepsilon_\nu t\operatorname{ev}_y),\qquad
 \varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}^L/\{\pm1\}.
\]
Only in the five triples in Table~\ref{tab:exceptions} do we need an
additional notation. For those triples, $\mathcal T_{c,k}$ is the
closure of the image of the rank-$r$ incidence indicated in the table.
Equivalently, on its dense distinct-support open set,
\[
 E=\operatorname{rad}H_\ell\subset U,\qquad
 U/E\text{ is a maximal isotropic subspace of }V_n/E.
\]
Thus these exceptions have one uniform geometric description; they are
not five unrelated numerical accidents.

\begin{theorem}[All-dimension component theorem]\label{thm:all-dimension}
Suppose $4\le c<k$ and $Lq\ge p$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $a<b$, the scheme $\mathscr D_L$ is integral and
Cohen--Macaulay of codimension $a$. Its support is $\Phi_L$, except at
$(c,k,L)=(5,8,1),(6,11,1)$, where its support is
$\mathcal T_{5,8},\mathcal T_{6,11}$, respectively.
\item If $a=b$, the scheme is reduced and Cohen--Macaulay of pure
codimension $a$. Its components are $\Phi_L$ and the $2^{L-1}$
varieties $\Sigma_\varepsilon$, except at $(6,10,1),(7,13,1)$,
where $\Phi_L$ is replaced by $\mathcal T_{6,10},\mathcal T_{7,13}$.
\item If $b<a$, the maximal-dimensional components are exactly the
$2^{L-1}$ signed secant varieties, except at $(5,7,1)$, where
$\mathcal T_{5,7}$ is one additional maximal-dimensional component.
Their codimension is $b$.
\end{enumerate}
Every component listed above has general multiplication corank one and
is generically smooth in the maximal-minor scheme. Each of its indicated
annihilator incidences is birational onto it. The generic Hankel rank is
$k$ on $\Phi_L$, two on $\Sigma_\varepsilon$, and the rank in
Table~\ref{tab:exceptions} on $\mathcal T_{c,k}$.
The signed components are rational. In the range $a\le b$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-dimension-cycle}
 [\mathscr D_L]=c_a\left(\left(\bigoplus_\nu\Sym^2\mathcal S_\nu\right)^*\right)
             \quad\hbox{in }A^a(X_L).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
The third assertion concerns maximal-dimensional components, not all
associated primes in the excess range. The theorem removes the stable
restriction from the component and expected-codimension scheme assertions;
it does not identify distinct notions of completeness.

\subsection{The complete equality analysis}
\begin{lemma}[All endpoint ties]\label{lem:all-ties}
Among the rank incidences in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:global-intro}, an incidence of dimension at least
$M-\min(a,b)$ is either the rank-two incidence with every $s_\nu=1$,
the full-rank incidence with every $s_\nu=c$, or one of the five
incidences in Table~\ref{tab:exceptions}. The dimension in each listed
case is exactly $M-\min(a,b)$ when its corresponding endpoint is minimal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use $D_{r,s}$ and $h_r$ in Lemmas~\ref{lem:hankel-strata} and
\ref{lem:isotropic}. For $r<k$ and $s=0$ the cost is
$(Lc-2)r+1>b$. For $1\le s\le c-2$, minimization in $r\ge2s$
uses the nonnegative coefficient $L(c-s)-2$ and gives
\[
 g(s)=1+s(Lc-4)-Ls(s-1)/2,
 \quad g(1)=b,
 \quad g(c-2)-b=(c-3)\{L(c+2)/2-4\}.
\]
If $L\ge2$, the last difference is strictly positive, and strict
concavity excludes the intervening integers. The coefficient for $s=1$
is positive, so equality there requires $r=2$.
For $s=c-1$, $L\ge2$, the cost at its minimum exceeds $b$ by
$(c-2)\{L(c+1)/2-4\}>0$. For $s=c$ and $r<k$, the cost is at
least $a+1$. Thus there are no extra ties for $L\ge2$.

It remains to take $L=1$. Saturation gives $k\le(q+1)/2$.
For $c=4$, this forces $k=5$ and feasibility permits only $s\le1$
when $r<k$. For $c=5$, the values at $s=1,2,3$ are $2,2,1$.
At $s=2$ equality requires $r=4$, and feasibility together with the
minimal endpoint gives $k=7$. At $s=3$ the coefficient of $r$ is zero;
feasibility forces $r=6,k=8$. For $c=6$, the only further lower-endpoint
tie is $s=4,r=8,k=10$.
For $c\ge7$, strict concavity and the displayed endpoint difference
exclude every $2\le s\le c-2$.

For $s=c-1$ the cost is decreasing in $r$ and is bounded below by
$a+k-2c+1$. Feasibility requires $k\ge2c-1$.
When $k\ge2c$, this is strictly greater than $a$.
When $k=2c-1$, feasibility forces $r=2c-2$, and
\[
 a-b=(c-7)(c-2)/2.
\]
Saturation permits this case for $c\ge6$; the minimal-endpoint ties
are precisely $c=6$ and $c=7$, giving $(6,11)$ and $(7,13)$.
For $c\ge8$ the cost $a$ is larger than $b$. The case $s=c$ was
already excluded. This proves the one-contact list.

Finally, if the contacts have different $s_\nu$, write their total cost
as the average of $LD_{r,s_\nu}-h_r$. Every summand is at least
$\min(a,b)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:optimization}; equality of the average
requires equality of every summand. The preceding analysis shows that
for $L\ge2$ only the identical endpoint choices remain. The exceptional
cases all have $L=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Three-dimensional residual subseries]\label{lem:three-plane-residual}
For $K\ge3$ and $L\ge1$, general three-planes in a $K$-dimensional
binary space have full quadratic product span whenever $6L\ge2K-1$,
provided either $K=3$ or $L\ge2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $K=3$ the plane is the entire binary space. Otherwise stratify the
annihilator incidence as before, now with $c=3$ and $L\ge2$.
For $r<K$, the costs for $s=0,1,2,3$ are respectively
\[
 (3L-2)r+1,\quad(2L-2)r-L+1,\quad(L-2)r+L+1,
 \quad6L-2r+1.
\]
On their feasible ranges they are positive: use $r\ge2$ for $s=1$,
$r\ge4$ for $s=2$, and $r\le K-1$ for $s=3$.
The full-rank cost, when feasible, is $6L-2K+2\ge1$.
The projective incidence therefore cannot dominate the Grassmannian
product. This proves generic surjectivity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Irreducibility and the exceptional geometry}
\begin{lemma}[Signed incidences in the full range]\label{lem:all-signed}
For $c\ge4$, the $2^{L-1}$ signed secant images are distinct,
irreducible and rational of codimension $b$. A general point of one
admits exactly one rank-two projective annihilator.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A general secant hyperplane has a unique unordered decomposition into
two evaluations: evaluations at four distinct points are independent
for $n\ge3$, and the limiting assertion follows by Hermite
interpolation. Its partner hyperplane and its rank-two quadratic
annihilator are therefore determined. The secant parameter space is
rational, being birational to a projective-line bundle over
$\operatorname{Sym}^2\Pj^1$. Normalize the first sign to be positive.
The incidence is a product of relative Grassmannians of dimension
$3+Lc(k-1-c)=M-b$, rational over this rational base.

For a fixed general first hyperplane $h$, planes admitting a second
secant hyperplane have dimension at most $3+c(k-2-c)$ in
$\Gr(c,\ker h)$, strictly less than $c(k-1-c)$. Thus the first plane
recovers $h$ generically. A general other plane lies in only its chosen
partner, proving distinctness of the sign sectors and birationality.
Every rank-two isotropic plane is contained in one of its isotropic
hyperplanes. The exceptional confluent secants have smaller base
dimension. These facts also give uniqueness of the general rank-two
annihilator.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Orthogonal incidences and their orientation covers]
\label{lem:orientation-incidences}
In the expected-codimension range, the nondegenerate incidence, when
feasible, is irreducible of dimension $M-a$ and contains points outside
all the signed images. Each exceptional incidence in
Table~\ref{tab:exceptions} is irreducible and its image also contains
points outside the signed images.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $2c<k$, the nondegenerate incidence is smooth over the irreducible
open set of nonsingular Hankel forms, with geometrically irreducible
fibres $\operatorname{OGr}(c,k)^L$. The dimension of each factor is
$c(k-c)-q$. For a fixed nonsingular form, isotropic $c$-planes in a
hyperplane have codimension at least $c$ in this Grassmannian: if the
restricted form has a one-dimensional radical, separate planes
containing that radical from those avoiding it. Their codimension drops
are respectively $k-c-1\ge c$ and $c$. Varying a secant hyperplane
contributes three parameters, fewer than $c$. Hence the first plane can
be chosen outside every secant hyperplane.

The inequalities $a\le b$ and $e\ge p$ imply $2c<k$, except for the
four expected-codimension entries of Table~\ref{tab:exceptions} and
$(c,k,L)=(7,14,1)$. This follows at once for $c\ge8$ from
$2k\ge L\binom c2+5$; for $4\le c\le7$ substitution gives exactly
these five possibilities. In the four table entries the full-rank
incidence is empty. For $(7,14,1)$ its maximal orthogonal fibres have
two components, but the total incidence is irreducible. Indeed the
orientation double cover adjoins a square root of the determinant of
the universal Hankel form. This determinant is not a square in the
function field: its zero divisor is the irreducible rank-at-most-$13$
Hankel hypersurface, and it vanishes simply at a general corank-one
point. Simplicity also follows by perturbing that point by one further
evaluation nonzero on its kernel. The connected orientation cover
exchanges the two maximal orthogonal families. Over it each family has
geometrically irreducible fibres, which proves irreducibility of the
total incidence. In a maximal orthogonal Grassmannian the condition of
containing a fixed isotropic line has codimension $c-1=6$; the same
three-parameter secant argument gives a nonsecant first plane.

For an exceptional incidence write $r=2(k-c)$ and $d=2c-k$.
On the dense distinct-support open set,
\[
 \ell=\sum_{i=1}^r\alpha_i\operatorname{ev}_{x_i}^{(2n)},
 \qquad E=gV_{d-1},\qquad
 V_n/E\simeq\C^r,
\]
with quotient form $\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_i)$ and all
$\alpha_i\ne0$. The product $\prod_i\alpha_i$ is nonsquare in the
function field of the ordered support and projective weight parameter
space (normalize the last weight to one). Thus its orientation cover
is connected. The same argument for maximal orthogonal fibres proves
irreducibility of this incidence. Its closure defines the incidence
used here; lower-dimensional support collisions do not add another
generic family.

To find a point outside the secant images, first suppose $d\ge2$.
A secant functional annihilating $gV_{d-1}$ must have both its support
points among the roots of $g$, since evaluations of $V_{d-1}$ at two
distinct points are independent. A confluent secant contributes only a
pure evaluation, because $g$ has simple roots. In the nondegenerate
quotient an admissible hyperplane must have isotropic dual vector.
Each of the finitely many secant lines through support points contains
only two such vectors; a pure evaluation has anisotropic dual.
A maximal isotropic $s$-plane containing any fixed isotropic line is a
proper subvariety of codimension $s-1>0$. Avoid this finite union.
If $d=1$, the secant functionals annihilating $g$ form a set of dimension
at most two. Here $s=5$ or $6$, and $s-1>2$, so the same incidence
bound again leaves a nonempty complement. This proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A corank-one point in the excess exception]\label{lem:excess-example}
The image $\mathcal T_{5,7}$ has dimension $M-b$ and general
multiplication corank one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Dehomogenize and choose a monic square-free polynomial $g$ of degree
four. In $V_6$ put
\[
 U=\Span\{1,t,g,tg,t^2g\}.
\]
For its four roots $x_i$, the nonzero barycentric weights
$\alpha_i=1/g'(x_i)$ satisfy
$\sum_i\alpha_i x_i^j=0$ for $j=0,1,2$. Thus
$\ell=\sum_i\alpha_i\operatorname{ev}_{x_i}^{(12)}$ has Hankel rank
four, radical $gV_2$, and annihilates $U^2$.
The twelve products
\[
 1,t,t^2,\ g,tg,t^2g,t^3g,\ g^2,tg^2,t^2g^2,t^3g^2,t^4g^2
\]
have distinct degrees, hence are independent. They span a hyperplane
of $V_{12}$. The annihilator is unique, so the incidence projection is
an isomorphism on a neighbourhood in its corank-one open set. Its
image has the incidence dimension $M-b$. This example is not in a
secant hyperplane. Since it contains $gV_2$, any secant annihilator
would have both support points among the roots of $g$, by the argument
in Lemma~\ref{lem:orientation-incidences}. Evaluations of $1,t$ at two
distinct such roots are independent; a pure evaluation is also excluded.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of the component theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:all-dimension}]
Assume first $a\le b$. Proposition~\ref{prop:expected-grade} makes
$\mathscr D_L$ pure of dimension $M-a$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:incidence-components} and Lemma~\ref{lem:all-ties},
its components must be dominated by the listed equal-dimensional
incidences. In the strict case only the nondegenerate incidence, or the
specified exceptional replacement, can dominate. It is irreducible,
so the nonempty failure scheme has just that support. At the wall the
signed images already have the component dimension. The nonsecant
point of Lemma~\ref{lem:orientation-incidences}, purity, and the same
rank-gap argument force one further component, dominated by the
nondegenerate incidence or its exceptional replacement. There can be
no others.

For each nondegenerate or exceptional component, its incidence and
image have the same dimension. In the nondegenerate case full rank is
open in its projective annihilator space. In an exceptional case the
rank $r=2(k-c)$ is the largest rank permitting an isotropic $c$-plane:
$\dim U\le k-r+\lfloor r/2\rfloor$ excludes every larger rank.
It too is an open rank condition in the annihilator space. A
zero-dimensional nonempty open subset of a projective space forces that
space to be a point. This proves corank one and birationality.

When $b<a$, the same incidence-to-component argument lists all possible
maximal-dimensional components. The signed images have the required
dimension, and Lemma~\ref{lem:excess-example} supplies the additional
component at $(5,7,1)$. No other rank incidence has sufficient dimension.

We verify generic corank one on the signed components without assuming
it in this dimension argument. At a general signed point the rank-gap
lemma excludes all strictly smaller incidences. If its annihilator
space had positive dimension and generic rank greater than two, its
open maximum-rank part would have positive dimension. The only
remaining possible incidences have dimension $M-b$, so cannot have a
positive-dimensional fibre over a dense subset of a signed component
of that same dimension. If the generic rank were two, the entire open
part would contradict uniqueness of the rank-two annihilator in
Lemma~\ref{lem:all-signed}. These alternatives exhaust a projective
space containing its known rank-two point.

The signed residual planes are general by
Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-dominance}. Since $b\le a$ is exactly
\[
 L\binom c2\ge2k-5,
\]
their multiplication is onto: apply the earlier codimension theorem
at $(k-2,c-1)$ when $c\ge5$. For $c=4$ use
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-plane-residual}; if $L=1$, saturation here forces
$k=5$ and the residual space is the entire three-dimensional binary
space. Corollary~\ref{cor:residual-singularity} therefore proves
generic scheme smoothness on the signed components.

For a nondegenerate component all $W_\nu$ are zero, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:residual-tangent} gives tangent dimension $M-a$.
For an exceptional component $L=1$, $W=E=gV_{d-1}$, and
$\dim W^2=2d-1$. Its tangent dimension is
\[
 M-a+\binom{d+1}{2}-(2d-1)
   =M-a+(d-1)(d-2)/2.
\]
For $d=1,2$ this is $M-a$. For the excess exception $d=3$, it is
$M-a+1=M-b$. In each case it is exactly the local dimension, proving
generic scheme smoothness. Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-reducedness}
now proves reducedness, and integrality in the strict case, of the
entire expected-codimension scheme.

Finally apply the Thom--Porteous formula to the dual map with source
$V_{2n}^*\otimes\mathcal O$ of rank $p$ and target
$(\bigoplus_\nu\Sym^2\mathcal S_\nu)^*$ of rank $e$, at rank bound
$p-1$ \cite{KL,Fulton}. Expected codimension has been proved and gives
\eqref{eq:all-dimension-cycle}. Reducedness makes this the sum of the
component cycles with multiplicity one. No excess-codimension cycle
formula is asserted.
\end{proof}
