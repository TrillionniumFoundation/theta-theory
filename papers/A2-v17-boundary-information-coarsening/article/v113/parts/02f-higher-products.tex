\section{Higher products and dihedral phase sectors}\label{sec:higher-products}
The two-hyperplane description has a higher-product counterpart, but
its monodromy is no longer exhausted by a simultaneous sign change.
For $m\ge2$ consider
\[
 \mu_m:\bigoplus_{\nu=1}^L\Sym^mU_\nu\longrightarrow V_{mn},
 \qquad p_m=mn+1,\quad e_m=L\binom{c+m-1}{m}.
\]
Here ``two-point annihilator'' refers to the family
\[
 [\ell]=[\operatorname{ev}_x^{(mn)}-t^m\operatorname{ev}_y^{(mn)}],
 \qquad x\ne y,\quad t\ne0,
\]
not to a purported classification of every higher catalecticant rank.
Let $\boldsymbol\mu_m$ be the group of $m$th roots of unity.

\begin{theorem}[Phase-sector classification for every symmetric power]
\label{thm:higher-phase}
Suppose $n\ge4$, $4\le c<n+1$, $L\ge1$, and $m\ge2$.
The reduced incidence of two-point annihilators of $\mu_m$ has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dihedral-count}
 N_{m,L}=\frac{m^{L-1}+\gcd(m,2)^{L-1}}2
\end{equation}
irreducible components on the distinct-support open base. The closure
of the image of each component in $X_L$ is irreducible and rational
of codimension $Lc-3$. Its incidence projects birationally, and its
general point has a unique two-point annihilator.
These sectors are indexed by relative phase patterns
\[
 (\xi_2,\ldots,\xi_L)\in\boldsymbol\mu_m^{L-1},
 \qquad (\xi_2,\ldots,\xi_L)\sim
                     (\xi_2^{-1},\ldots,\xi_L^{-1}).
\]
For $m=2$ this recovers $2^{L-1}$. In general the count is not
$m^{L-1}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By polarization in characteristic zero, $\ell$ annihilates
$\Sym^mU$ if and only if the degree-$m$ polynomial $\ell(f^m)$
vanishes identically for $f\in U$. Its factorization is
\[
 f(x)^m-t^mf(y)^m
 =\prod_{\zeta\in\boldsymbol\mu_m}
                \bigl(f(x)-\zeta t f(y)\bigr).
\]
Since the polynomial ring on $U$ is a domain, this holds precisely when
$U$ lies in one of the $m$ hyperplanes
$H_\zeta=\ker(\operatorname{ev}_x-\zeta t\operatorname{ev}_y)$.
The splitting cover of the two-point base labels all these hyperplanes.
Changing the chosen $m$th root $t$ acts by simultaneous cyclic
translation of phase labels. Interchanging the two support points acts
by inversion after that translation. Both operations occur: a loop in
the nonzero weight ratio realizes the cyclic action, and the connected
ordered-support double cover realizes the interchange. Thus the
monodromy orbits are those of the dihedral action on
$\boldsymbol\mu_m^L$.

Quotient first by simultaneous translation, normalizing the first phase
to one. The remaining involution inverts every relative phase. Its fixed
patterns have each entry of order dividing two; there are
$\gcd(m,2)^{L-1}$ of them. The two-element orbit formula gives
\eqref{eq:dihedral-count}. Over the splitting cover every phase sector
is a smooth irreducible product of relative Grassmannians; monodromy
orbits are therefore exactly the irreducible components after descent.

Each incidence has dimension $3+Lc(k-1-c)=M-(Lc-3)$.
The first plane determines its general secant hyperplane uniquely by
the dimension argument in Lemma~\ref{lem:all-signed}. That hyperplane
determines its unordered support points, weight ratio and resulting
two-point annihilator, and the other planes determine their phase
hyperplanes. This proves generic injectivity and distinctness of the
image sectors, hence birationality in characteristic zero.

For rationality, a relative pattern not fixed by inversion chooses an
ordering of the support points. Its parameterization uses an open
subset of $\Pj^1\times\Pj^1\times\mathbb G_m$, with rational
Grassmannian fibres trivialized on a dense open set, and is birational.
For an inversion-fixed pattern, its first hyperplane instead
parameterizes the unordered secant threefold, which is rational; all
other hyperplanes are rationally determined from it. The same
Grassmannian argument applies. Taking image closures does not change
these birational assertions.
\end{proof}
The theorem classifies the two-point incidence and its image sectors.
It does not assert that every such sector is an irreducible component
of the entire higher-product failure scheme, or that the full
higher-product failure codimension has been determined.

\begin{theorem}[Higher-product residual conormal]\label{thm:higher-residual}
On a phase sector suppose no $U_\nu$ is contained in
$E=gV_{n-2}$, and write $U_\nu=gK_\nu+\C a_\nu$.
Put
\[
 R_m=\sum_\nu(gK_\nu)^2U_\nu^{m-2}
     =g^2\sum_\nu K_\nu^2U_\nu^{m-2}\subset V_{mn},
 \qquad \delta_m=(p_m-4)-\dim R_m.
\]
If $e_m\ge p_m$ and $\mu_m$ has corank one, then the tangent dimension
of its maximal-minor scheme is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:higher-residual-dimension}
 M-(Lc-3)+\delta_m.
\end{equation}
In particular $\delta_m=0$ implies scheme smoothness of codimension
$Lc-3$ at this point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed contact split $U=W+\C a$, with $W=gK$.
The derivative with respect to the plane vanishes on every monomial
having at least two $W$ factors: after varying one factor another still
vanishes at both support points. On the quotient spanned by $a^m$ and
$wa^{m-1}$, $w\in W$, it is surjective. Indeed the nonzero quotient
linear functional
\[
 v\longmapsto v(x)a(x)^{m-1}-t^mv(y)a(y)^{m-1}
\]
allows the coordinates of the variation of each basis vector to solve
these $c$ equations independently; the coefficient for $a^m$ is the
nonzero integer $m$. Thus the plane derivative has rank $c$.
The remaining compatibility equations for $\dot\ell$ are precisely
annihilation of $R_m$. Independent solutions for the contacts give
\[
 \dim T\mathfrak I_m=M+(p_m-1)-Lc-\dim R_m,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:higher-residual-dimension}.
The corank-one Schur argument identifies this tangent space with that
of the maximal-minor scheme. Multiplication by $g^2$ embeds
$V_{mn-4}$, of dimension $p_m-4$, so $\delta_m\ge0$.
If it is zero, the tangent dimension equals the dimension $M-(Lc-3)$
of the local phase incidence, which is embedded by the corank-one
projection. Equality of tangent and local dimensions proves smoothness.
\end{proof}
For $m=2$ the residual span is independent of the lifts $a_\nu$ and
reduces to the multiplication of the $K_\nu$. For $m>2$ it retains
$U_\nu^{m-2}$. This difference is why a higher-product phase
classification alone does not imply the quadratic saturation formula
with $q$ replaced by $\binom{c+m-1}{m}$.
