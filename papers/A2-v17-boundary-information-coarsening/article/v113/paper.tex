\begin{abstract}
Quadratic multiplication of binary subseries governs recovery of a calibrated information metric from distance contacts. We determine its failure components in every dimension $4\le c<k$, including five exceptional maximal-isotropic incidences, and prove reducedness and Cohen--Macaulayness in expected codimension. An exact tangent sequence expresses the conormal through multiplication on the intersections with an annihilator's radical. On the rank-two stratum this gives a singularity recursion, and at the residual wall it produces a nonempty double-crossing divisor with local equation $uv=0$. The resulting local small-singular-value law acquires a logarithmic factor. We describe the normalization and all same-annihilator intersections of the signed incidence, and compute the square-zero embedded structure of its rank-two isotropy fibres. For every symmetric power, two-point annihilators yield dihedral phase sectors and a corresponding residual conormal formula. Exact contact fibres, positive native realization, statistical comparison and finite-precision results provide complementary consequences. The scheme assertions distinguish expected codimension, maximal excess components, and explicitly specified local strata.
\end{abstract}
