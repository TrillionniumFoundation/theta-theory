\begin{abstract}
We give a reduction and reconstruction theory for metric quotients of polynomial observations. For a polynomial fast map with an isolated central fibre, explicit discriminant and derivative inequalities imply a finite-sheet normal form; the sheets are extracted from the original map rather than prescribed. The conclusion is uniform relative to the exact residual, including cancellation and ramified branch collisions, and determines an intrinsic tensor envelope. For an arbitrary number of invisible nonnegative coordinates, we reconstruct every minimal representation of the profiled quadratic form on a fully visible class. Its local Hessians form a Boolean lattice; they determine the minimal endpoint dimension and the normalized coupling matrix, leaving only positive coordinate scales and permutations. This class is nonempty and open in every endpoint dimension. A native categorical polynomial family has an exact rank and secant classification in every retained dimension, and its sharp fixed and adaptive ray complexities equal the dimension of a rank-one span. A finite-alphabet local experiment identifies these ray costs with efficient Gaussian discrimination, without identifying query counts with sample counts. Real local factorization and positive-time accessibility are proved separately.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A singular inverse problem can lose information in three different operations: passage to a leading residual, minimization over coordinates, and removal of coordinates invisible at the target scale. These operations preserve more than an order of vanishing. They preserve a metric quotient, and the quotient can sometimes be reconstructed from finite intrinsic data.

The first question is when a finite affine description is forced by an observation map. Let
\[
 R(x,t)=(F(x)-a(t),\ G(x)-b(t)),
\]
where $F:\R^r\to\R^r$ and $G:\R^r\to\R^q$ are polynomial. Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} constructs inverse sheets from the critical values of $F$ and bounds their nonlinear distortion by rational derivatives of the original map. It does not assume independent monomial fast equations. Corollary~\ref{cor:newton} verifies the inequalities for coupled weighted initial maps, while Example~\ref{ex:collision} treats an arc approaching a genuine discriminant. The leading residual set and its tensor envelope are independent of the chosen inverse presentation.

The second question is whether a one-sided partial minimum remembers the quadratic form from which it came. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-profile}
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^{\mathsf T}&C\end{pmatrix}>0,
 \qquad G_Q(x)=\min_{z\in\R_+^e}(x,z)^{\mathsf T}Q(x,z),\quad x\in\R_+^k.
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:reconstruct} answers this question in every endpoint dimension on a fully visible class. The unlabelled local quadratic matrices of $G_Q$ recover a Boolean lattice of active sets, the number $e$, and a unique normalized matrix up to permutation. The complete fibre among $e$-endpoint representations consists of positive diagonal congruences and permutations. No representation with fewer endpoints exists. A simple positive-spanning condition makes this an open, nonempty class for every $e$, not a list of isolated examples. Proposition~\ref{prop:redundancy} gives a finite coordinate-deletion criterion without the visibility hypothesis.

The third question concerns the native observation metric. Theorem~\ref{thm:paired} classifies the quotient image and all normalized secants of paired-contrast polynomial probability models. The answer is the linear span of the contrast tensors; no determinant witness is required. Corollary~\ref{cor:every-native} places a native open family at every positive definite quotient, so the fully visible endpoint class and the finite-map metric reduction both occur in these genuine stochastic observations. Theorem~\ref{thm:experiment} identifies the variational quotient with the nuisance-free Gaussian limit experiment. Its scalar values are intrinsic local discrimination costs. Recovering such values through an exact oracle remains a different problem from estimating them from samples.

These results have distinct, stated hypotheses. We do not identify every polynomial singularity with a finite affine arrangement, nor identify the paired-contrast class with every binary polynomial mixture. The reduction theorem supplies a converse for a broad finite-map class; the endpoint theorem supplies an all-dimensional reconstruction result; and the native theorem supplies an all-dimensional exactly classified stochastic class. The earlier binary model, its whole-model spectral limit, its rational-moment bounds, and its sharp two-variance theorem are retained in full in the supporting volume. The present proofs are independent of that volume except where an earlier application is explicitly identified.

\subsection*{Background and organization}
Semialgebraic selection and one-variable preparation are used in the form developed in \cite{BCR}; proper real uniformization and normal crossings are used in precisely the forms of \cite[Theorem 0.1 and Corollary 4.9]{BM}. Convex separation, Schur complements, and quadratic optimization are classical \cite{BV}. The piecewise-quadratic active-set description of \eqref{eq:intro-profile} is part of parametric quadratic programming \cite{BMDP}, with its accompanying corrigendum \cite{BMDPc}; our reconstruction concerns the unlabelled Hessian lattice, minimal representations, and the entire fixed-dimensional fibre. Comparison by randomizations goes back to Blackwell \cite{Blackwell}; local asymptotic normality and efficient information are classical statistical constructions \cite{vdV}. We provide their finite-alphabet proof here so that the relation to the singular root scale does not rest on a regularity analogy.

Sections~\ref{sec:reduction}--\ref{sec:tensors} derive the finite quotient. Section~\ref{sec:endpoints} reconstructs its orthant profiling. Sections~\ref{sec:native}--\ref{sec:experiment} treat native metrics and local experiments. Appendix~\ref{app:real} supplies the real-analytic factorization and accessibility arguments. Appendix~\ref{app:inverse} corrects a clock-pair quantifier and displays the partial fractions needed for the retained binary inverse.
