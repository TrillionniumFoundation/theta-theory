\section{An exactly classified native stochastic family}\label{sec:native}
Fix $z_1,\ldots,z_m\in\R^k$ spanning $\R^k$. Consider a single categorical observation with $2m+1$ cells and probabilities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paired}
 p_{j,+}(a,\tau)=a_j/2+z_j^{\mathsf T}\tau,\quad
 p_{j,-}(a,\tau)=a_j/2-z_j^{\mathsf T}\tau,\quad
 p_0(a,\tau)=1-\sum_{j=1}^m a_j.
\end{equation}
The centre is $\tau=0$, where $a_j>0$ and $\sum_j a_j<1$. For all small $\tau$ in a fixed cone $\mathcal K$ with nonempty interior these probabilities are positive. The masses $a$ are free nuisance coordinates and $\tau$ are retained coordinates. The observation metric is the ordinary, unaltered Hellinger metric. Thus the construction specifies the probability model and not an independently adjustable quadratic metric.

\begin{theorem}[Native image, secants, and exact ray complexity]\label{thm:paired}
For \eqref{eq:paired}, profiling the free mass coordinates at the centre gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paired-Q}
 Q(a)=4\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{z_jz_j^{\mathsf T}}{a_j}>0.
\end{equation}
Put $\mathcal V=\operatorname{span}\{z_jz_j^{\mathsf T}:1\le j\le m\}$ and $s=\dim\mathcal V$. The native image $\{Q(a):a_j>0,\ \sum a_j<1\}$ is relatively open in $\mathcal V$, has dimension $s$, and its normalized nonzero secants are exactly the unit sphere in $\mathcal V$ for the Frobenius norm. In particular that secant set has dimension $s-1$.

Exactly $s$ legal ray values $v^{\mathsf T}Qv$, $v\in\mathcal K$, are necessary in the worst case to determine the unknown native quotient, for both fixed and deterministic adaptive exact queries. There is a fixed family of $s$ rays attaining the bound. The same count determines the quadratic cone ball, and hence any fixed root decoder from which that ball's radial gauge is recovered.

Taking the contrasts $e_i$, $1\le i\le k$, and $e_i+e_j$, $i<j$, gives $s=k(k+1)/2$. Thus full-dimensional native families with sharp polarization complexity exist in every retained dimension, without varying the observation metric or using a numerical rank witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At $\tau=0$, the two entries in pair $j$ have probability $a_j/2$. Their retained probability derivatives are $z_j$ and $-z_j$, while the mass derivative in that pair has entries $1/2,1/2$. Their Fisher cross product cancels. The retained information is therefore $4z_jz_j^{\mathsf T}/a_j$, summed over pairs. The remaining cell has zero retained derivative. The nuisance information is
\[
 I_{aa}=\diag(a_1^{-1},\ldots,a_m^{-1})+
 \frac{\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}}{1-\sum_j a_j}>0.
\]
Consequently its Schur complement is exactly \eqref{eq:paired-Q}. The spanning condition proves positive definiteness.

The coordinate change $a_j\mapsto c_j=4/a_j$ maps the mass domain diffeomorphically onto an open subset of $\R^m$. The linear map $(c_j)\mapsto\sum_jc_jz_jz_j^{\mathsf T}$ is onto $\mathcal V$, and every surjective linear map is open. Hence the image is relatively open with dimension $s$. All secants lie in $\mathcal V$. Fix an interior image point and a sufficiently small relative ball around it. Its differences contain a relative ball about zero, so every nonzero direction in $\mathcal V$ is the direction of a secant. This proves the precise sphere assertion, including $s=1$.

Evaluation by the rank-one forms $H\mapsto v^{\mathsf T}Hv$ with $v$ in the interior of $\mathcal K$ separates $\mathcal V$. Otherwise a nonzero quadratic polynomial would vanish on an open set. Finite-dimensional linear algebra therefore selects $s$ such evaluations forming a basis of $\mathcal V^*$. Their values determine $Q$.

For the adaptive lower bound, fix $Q_0$ in a relative open ball of the native image and run a proposed algorithm making fewer than $s$ queries. There is a nonzero $H\in\mathcal V$ annihilated by every recorded evaluation. Both $Q_0+\epsilon H$ and $Q_0-\epsilon H$ remain in the native image for sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$. They produce the same first answer as $Q_0$, hence the same second query and answer, and inductively the same transcript and stopping decision. They are distinct, so the algorithm cannot recover both. No continuity of the query rule is assumed. A cone ball with nonempty interior determines its positive quadratic form by its radial gauge, proving the fibre assertion.

Finally the listed contrast tensors form a basis of $\Sym_k$: the $e_ie_i^{\mathsf T}$ give the diagonal entries and subtracting two such diagonal tensors from $(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^{\mathsf T}$ gives the $(i,j)$ off-diagonal tensor. This proves the all-dimensional statement.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[Native realization at every positive quotient]\label{cor:every-native}
For every $Q_0\in\Sym_n^{++}$ there is a fixed paired-contrast experiment whose native quotient image contains a neighbourhood of $Q_0$ in $\Sym_n$. Its design and contrasts are fixed, and only its centre masses vary. Consequently the fully visible endpoint class of Section~\ref{sec:endpoints} occurs in a native polynomial probability model with the ordinary Hellinger metric.

Likewise, every finite-map reduction in Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} can be embedded, on a sufficiently small feasible neighbourhood of its central fibre, in a strictly positive polynomial probability observation. Given any $H>0$ on its residual space, that embedding may be chosen so that its minimum Hellinger distance has the two-sided relative comparison
\[
 \min_x h(P_{\rm rem}(x),P_{\rm base}(t))
 =\tfrac12\min_b q_b(H,t)\bigl(1+O(\eps(t)+\omega(t))\bigr),
\]
where $\omega(t)\to0$ and equality here denotes the corresponding two-sided bounds, including exact zeros. Thus the extracted finite sheets and the positive-metric envelope are native stochastic objects for this class.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_N$ whose rank-one tensors form a basis of $\Sym_n$, for example the vectors used in Theorem~\ref{thm:paired}. Choose $\eta>0$ so small that
$Q_0-\eta\sum_jv_jv_j^{\mathsf T}>0$, and factor this difference as $WW^{\mathsf T}$ with columns $w_1,\ldots,w_n$. Put $m=N+n$ and choose all centre masses $a_j=1/(2m)$. Use contrasts
\[
 z_j=\sqrt{a_j\eta/4}\,v_j\quad(j\le N),\qquad
 z_{N+i}=\sqrt{a_{N+i}/4}\,w_i\quad(i\le n).
\]
Formula \eqref{eq:paired-Q} gives $Q(a)=Q_0$. The contrast tensors span $\Sym_n$, so the image is open there. Apply this construction with $n=k+e$ to any fully visible matrix $Q_0$, marking its last $e$ retained coordinates as nonnegative endpoints. Profile the free masses first and then those endpoints. This gives exactly $G_{Q_0}$, and all nearby matrices remain in the same native experiment.

For the reduction, write $O(x)=(F(x),G(x))$, $o(t)=(a(t),b(t))$, and $o_0=(a_0,b_0)$. Apply the preceding construction to $H$ and set the paired model's retained coordinate to $O(x)-o_0$ or to $o(t)-o_0$, with the same fixed masses. Every probability is polynomial in $x$ on the remote side, and the marked base is semialgebraic. Positivity holds after restricting to sufficiently small neighbourhoods of the finitely many central inverse points. This restriction does not change the small-time minimizing germ, since the residual is bounded away from zero on the discarded compact part.

On a compact positive probability neighbourhood the square-root identity gives a uniform norm equivalence and, when both observations tend to the centre, the stronger relative comparison
\[
 h(P',P)^2=\tfrac14 R^{\mathsf T}HR\,(1+O(\omega)),
 \qquad R=O(x)-o(t),
\]
where $\omega$ bounds the two displacements from $o_0$. Trials at the inverse origins have residual $O(c(t))$. Norm equivalence therefore localizes Hellinger minimizers and quadratic trials to the same fast tube and bounds their residuals by $O(c(t))$. At those points one may take
$\omega(t)=K(|o(t)-o_0|+c(t))\to0$. Combining with \eqref{eq:relative} proves the asserted relative bounds, without dividing by a residual that may vanish. The contrasts span the observation space, so equality of the probability vectors is exactly equality of the observations. Exact cancellations are preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Rank loci and fixed root patterns]\label{cor:rank-loci}
For a varying contrast matrix $Z$, the native dimension is exactly the rank of the matrix with columns $\operatorname{vec}_{\rm sym}(z_jz_j^{\mathsf T})$. Its rank-drop loci are given by the corresponding minors. Positive mass changes cause no additional dimension drop. Under a prescribed retained-coordinate map $\tau=B\xi$, the formula is the same with contrasts $B^{\mathsf T}z_j$.

Every fixed combination of simple root shifts, inward endpoint sums, and repeated interior variances can be used as the coordinates $\tau$ in \eqref{eq:paired}. This gives polynomial probability observations with the same quotient classification on that pattern, and uniform conclusions on compact sets with positive cell margins and fixed contrast-rank bounds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The dimension assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:paired}; polynomial minors give exactly the rank loci. Substitution gives the pullback contrasts. A simple root displacement and an endpoint sum are linear polynomial functions of root coordinates. For a repeated interior cluster the function
\[
 X=\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^d\left(u_i-\tfrac1d\sum_hu_h\right)^2
\]
is polynomial, nonnegative, and realizes every small nonnegative value with zero mean. These choices independently realize the indicated product cone near separated clusters; the means of repeated interior clusters are held fixed, and bounded mass changes remain feasible. Substituting these polynomials into \eqref{eq:paired} proves the model assertion. Compact strict margins give the stated uniformity.
\end{proof}
For two roots at $r\pm h$, the variance is $X=h^2$. In the coordinate $h\ne0$ the pulled-back information is $4h^2Q_X$, which vanishes as $h\to0$. At the collision the nonzero variance coordinate is second order instead, and the local root scale is $n^{-1/4}$ rather than $n^{-1/2}$; this follows precisely from the local experiment below. Thus the rank formula distinguishes a coordinate transition from loss of the quadratic variance experiment. We do not extend compact fixed-pattern uniform constants across this degeneracy.

The class \eqref{eq:paired} is distinct from the two-component binary polynomial model. The latter retains its earlier rational-moment description and its proved sharp two-variance native family. The present theorem supplies a general classified native class; it does not assert that every higher-degree binary pattern has full dimension. Also, the unknown $a$ is not supplied as part of an exact-query problem. If the full numerical centre law is supplied, the masses and \eqref{eq:paired-Q} are directly known and no oracle reconstruction is needed.
