\appendix
\section{Real factorization and positive-time accessibility}\label{app:real}
We isolate two points at which complex or formal order calculations would not suffice. All analytic functions in this appendix are real analytic.

\begin{lemma}[Factors of a normal-crossings product]\label{lem:factor}
In the local analytic ring at a point with coordinates $(r_1,\ldots,r_s,z)$, suppose
\[
 f g=u\prod_{i=1}^s r_i^{a_i},\qquad u(0)\ne0.
\]
Then $f=u_f r^b$ and $g=u_g r^c$ with analytic units $u_f,u_g$ and $b+c=a$. If instead
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^M R_j^2=u r^a,
\]
then every $a_i$ is even and $R_j=r^{a/2}V_j$ with analytic $V_j$ and $\sum_jV_j^2=u$. In particular the $V_j$ have no common zero near the point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each coordinate ideal $(r_i)$ is prime: its quotient is the integral domain of analytic germs in the remaining coordinates. An analytic germ divisible by $r_i$ can be divided analytically, as seen by its convergent power series. Let $b_i$ and $c_i$ be the finite maximal orders of divisibility of $f$ and $g$ by $r_i$. The primality just noted gives $b_i+c_i=a_i$. Divide by all these coordinate powers. The remaining factors have product $u$, so their values at the point are nonzero and each is a unit. This proves the first assertion without an unstated factorization assumption.

For the second, let $b_i$ be the smallest $r_i$-divisibility order among the $R_j$. After dividing by $r_i^{b_i}$, at least one restricted germ on $r_i=0$ is nonzero. A sum of squares of real analytic germs is identically zero only if every germ is zero: evaluate on a real neighbourhood. Thus the restricted sum of squares is nonzero and its $r_i$-order is exactly $2b_i$. It follows that $a_i=2b_i$. Every $R_j$ is divisible by every $r_i^{b_i}$, hence by their product, by successive coordinate division. Dividing the identity proves the remaining assertions. Since $u$ is nonvanishing and equals a real sum of squares, it is positive on a sufficiently small neighbourhood.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Proper pruning and accessible faces]\label{lem:accessible}
Let $\Gamma$ be compact subanalytic and equal to the closure of $\Gamma\cap\{t>0\}$, where $t\ge0$ is analytic on an ambient neighbourhood. Let $\psi:M\to\Gamma$ be a proper analytic surjection from a nonsingular real analytic manifold. Retain the closure in $M$ of $\{t\circ\psi>0\}$. Its image is still all of $\Gamma$. The same statement holds after a further proper analytic surjection onto $M$.

Suppose after normal crossings a retained local orthant chart has
\[
 t\circ\psi=u(r,z)r^a,\qquad r_i\ge0,\quad u>0,
\]
and its full interior has positive time. For every coordinate face with $a_i>0$, every choice of sufficiently small fixed positive values of the other $r_j$ and an interior value of $z$ gives a feasible positive-time analytic arc transverse to that face, by setting $r_i=s\downarrow0$. Its time order is $a_i$. If $R\circ\psi=r^bV$ with $V$ nowhere zero, its residual norm has order $b_i$ on this arc.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $y\in\Gamma$ choose a sequence $y_n\in\Gamma$ with $t(y_n)>0$ and $y_n\to y$. Choose any lifts $x_n\in M$. Properness over the compact set $\Gamma$ supplies a convergent subsequence with limit $x$, and continuity gives $\psi(x)=y$. Its limit is in the retained closure. This proves surjectivity after pruning; the same argument proves the assertion after any further proper surjection.

On a connected analytic component on which $t\circ\psi$ is not identically zero, its nonnegative analytic function has a zero set with empty interior, so the positive-time part is dense. Components on which it vanishes identically contribute no retained interior and may be discarded. On a local normal-crossings chart choose orthants whose interiors have positive time, replace their signed coordinates by nonnegative $r_i$, and take smaller closed boxes. The transverse arcs specified in the statement belong to those boxes and their images lie in $\Gamma$. Holding the other coordinates positive leaves a nonzero constant times $s^{a_i}$ for time and a nonzero vector times $s^{b_i}$ for the residual, proving both orders. This is an explicit feasible arc, not an appeal to a complex divisor.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Proper real order presentation]\label{thm:real}
Let $\Gamma$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:accessible}, and let $R_1,\ldots,R_M$ be analytic on an ambient neighbourhood. Suppose there is no common residual zero on $\Gamma\cap\{0<t\le t_0\}$. On a sufficiently small compact time neighbourhood of the zero-time fibre there is a finite proper-surjective system of real corner charts with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-monomials}
 t=u(r,z)r^a,\qquad R=r^bV(r,z),\qquad r_i\ge0,\qquad
a_i=0\Longrightarrow b_i=0,
\end{equation}
where $u$ and $|V|$ have positive lower and finite upper bounds. Every face used in a ratio $b_i/a_i$ is reached by a feasible positive-time transverse arc. Assuming the zero-time fibre is nonempty, put
\[
 \rho=\max\{b_i/a_i:\hbox{retained chart faces with }a_i>0\}.
\]
Then, on the feasible germ, $|R|\ge c t^\rho$ for a constant $c>0$, and an accessible arc attains order ratio $\rho$. If the positive-time slices are all nonempty for small time, their minimum residual norm has order exactly $\rho$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The uniformization theorem of \cite[Theorem 0.1]{BM} gives a proper analytic surjection from a nonsingular manifold onto the compact subanalytic set. Pull back $t$ and $R$. Prune as in Lemma~\ref{lem:accessible}. On each remaining component the analytic function $t\sum_jR_j^2$ is not identically zero, since the residual is nonzero at positive time. Apply the proper normal-crossings theorem \cite[Corollary 4.9]{BM} to that function on a neighbourhood of the compact inverse image of the zero-time fibre. The resulting manifold and proper surjection preserve the feasible image. Prune once more.

In every resulting coordinate neighbourhood the product is a coordinate monomial times a unit. The first part of Lemma~\ref{lem:factor} factors the time function and the sum of squares separately; its second part factors the vector residual and proves that its remaining vector factor is nowhere zero. Shrink to finitely many closed corner boxes covering the compact inverse zero-time fibre. The positive-time orthants have $u>0$, and the units have uniform bounds on these boxes. Their images cover the feasible germ: otherwise positive-time points with times tending to zero outside the image would have lifts converging, by properness, to the inverse zero-time fibre covered by the boxes. Choose the boxes with interiors covering that fibre to obtain the contradiction.

If $a_i=0$ but $b_i>0$, take $r_i=0$ and all other divisor coordinates positive and sufficiently small. The time is positive whereas every residual coordinate vanishes, a contradiction. Thus \eqref{eq:real-monomials} holds. Lemma~\ref{lem:accessible} supplies all the stated transverse arcs with the correct orders, including the face realizing the finite maximum.

Rescale the boxes so that $0\le r_i\le1$. The inequalities $b_i\le\rho a_i$ imply $r^b\ge(r^a)^\rho$. Combining this with the unit bounds yields $|R|\ge c t^\rho$ on every chart and hence on the germ. On the maximizing transverse arc, $t$ is a positive unit times $s^{a_i}$ and $|R|$ is a positive unit times $s^{b_i}$. This gives an upper bound by a constant times $t^\rho$ on all sufficiently small time slices, since the arc's time is eventually strictly monotone. The matching lower bound proves the final statement.
\end{proof}
The theorem computes orders for an analytic residual on a subanalytic feasible graph. A continuous semialgebraic observation can be treated by taking its compact graph and using the analytic coordinate projections for time and residual. The polar-gap suprema in Proposition~\ref{prop:order-tests} likewise have semialgebraic graphs. Extracting their orders by these real charts and then testing the strict inequalities in \eqref{eq:gap} connects the resolution presentation to the finite-sheet theorem. The extra residues and limiting derivative matrices in \eqref{eq:intrinsic-sheets} are indispensable; monomial orders alone are not asserted to recover them.
