\section{The binary inverse: precise quantifiers and score columns}\label{app:inverse}
We state explicitly the correction to the earlier coefficient-inverse argument. Let
\[
 K(z)=\alpha_1 f_1(z)u_1v_1^{\mathsf T}+\alpha_2 f_2(z)u_2v_2^{\mathsf T},
\]
where $f_1,f_2$ are coprime monic degree-$d$ polynomials, $d\ge1$, with roots in $[0,D]$, $\alpha_a>0$, and $U=(u_1,u_2),V=(v_1,v_2)$ are invertible strictly positive column-stochastic matrices. Let $T_1,\ldots,T_\ell>D$ be distinct, with $\ell\ge2d+1$.

\begin{lemma}[Existence of a separating clock pair]\label{lem:clock}
For each fixed clock index $i$ there exists an index $j$ such that
\[
 \frac{f_1(T_j)}{f_1(T_i)}\ne\frac{f_2(T_j)}{f_2(T_i)}.
\]
No assertion is made for an arbitrary prescribed pair. The matrix $K(T_j)K(T_i)^{-1}$ for such a pair has distinct eigenvalues and its eigenvectors recover the columns of $U$ after normalization by their entry sums. On a compact full-rank fixed-pattern family a finite cover supplies locally chosen pairs with uniform eigenvalue gaps.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If the displayed equality held for every $j$, the degree-at-most-$d$ polynomial
$f_1-f_1(T_i)f_2/f_2(T_i)$ would vanish at every clock, hence identically. Monicity would make $f_1=f_2$, contradicting coprimality. A separating pair therefore exists. Matrix multiplication gives
\[
 K(T_j)K(T_i)^{-1}
 =U\diag\left(\frac{f_1(T_j)}{f_1(T_i)},\frac{f_2(T_j)}{f_2(T_i)}\right)U^{-1}.
\]
The two simple eigenlines and their stochastic normalizations are analytic locally. A chosen strict gap persists in a neighbourhood. Compactness supplies a finite cover by such neighbourhoods and the minimum of their finitely many positive gap bounds. This does not select one universal pair on the entire family.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Displayed partial-fraction independence]\label{lem:fractions}
For distinct cluster locations $r_j$ in one component, the first-order polynomial columns corresponding to cluster sums and repeated interior variances are, respectively,
\[
 -\frac{f_a(z)}{z-r_j},\qquad -\frac{f_a(z)}{(z-r_j)^2},
\]
with the second column retained only when the multiplicity is at least two and the cluster is interior. The inward sum at the upper endpoint reverses the sign of the first column. All these columns are linearly independent. Combined with the analytic coefficient/stochastic inverse, they imply injectivity of the full free-and-retained score matrix.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A linear dependence, divided by the nonzero polynomial $f_a$, is a rational identity
\[
 \sum_j\frac{a_j}{z-r_j}
 +\sum_{\substack{j\text{ repeated}\\\text{and interior}}}
 \frac{b_j}{(z-r_j)^2}=0.
\]
At each repeated pole, multiplying by $(z-r_j)^2$ and evaluating there gives $b_j=0$. Multiplying the remaining identity by $z-r_j$ and evaluating gives $a_j=0$ for every cluster. A sign reversal does not affect independence. Different components have separately recovered polynomial coefficients, so their columns are independent in that product coordinate space.

For completeness, coefficient recovery from the normalized clock laws uses the monic normalizer $q=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}K\mathbf1$. A homogeneous ambiguity has $\deg e<d$, $\deg H\le d$, and $H(T_j)=e(T_j)P_j$. Thus the polynomial $qH-eK$ of degree at most $2d$ vanishes identically. Taking a marginal and multiplying by the inverse channel gives $q\mid ef_1$ and $q\mid ef_2$. A Bezout identity for the coprime components implies $q\mid e$, hence $e=0$ and $H=0$. A nonsingular minor gives the analytic coefficient inverse. Lemma~\ref{lem:clock} then gives the component polynomials, weights, and both channels locally. Differentiating this inverse gives a left inverse in coefficient and stochastic coordinates. Applying it to a zero combined score reduces to the polynomial independence just proved and to zero stochastic coordinate increments. This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}
All compact-uniform statements here hold within the full-rank fixed-pattern set with the required gaps and probability margins. They do not assert uniformity through channel-rank loss, cluster merging, a vanishing design weight, or a discriminant not satisfying the polar-gap bounds.
