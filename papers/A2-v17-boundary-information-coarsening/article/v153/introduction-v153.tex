\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction-v153}
A multiplication-failure scheme records where a family of generators
ceases to generate an algebra. Its reduced support need not distinguish
the multiplication laws. The question here is whether the abstract
nonreduced scheme, with no ambient or tensor marking, retains those
laws. For quadratic pencils it does. The same first relation that gives
the inverse also defines a divisor incidence whose normalization and
singularities can be studied independently. We organize the paper around
these two aspects of the first relation: reconstruction on its exact
locus, and the geometry of its divisor boundary.

\subsection{Sharp unmarked reconstruction}
Let $V$ have dimension $n\geq3$, let $N=\binom{n+1}{2}$, and let
$R\subset\Sym^2V$ be a two-dimensional subspace. Put $p=N-2$ and
$d=n+2p=n^2+2n-4$. The cube-zero algebra
$\C\oplus V\oplus(\Sym^2V/R)$ has its natural quadratic multiplication.
For the universal matrix $T$ and the quotient map $\gamma_R$, its
multiplication-failure ideal on a graph chart is
\[
       (\det T) I_p(\gamma_R\Sym^2T).
\]
Its generators have degree $d$. Let $\mathfrak m$ be the ideal of the
matrix entries and write
\[
 \mathfrak A_R^{[d]}=
 \C[t_{ij}]/\bigl((\det T)I_p(\gamma_R\Sym^2T)+\mathfrak m^{d+1}\bigr).
\]
Theorems~\ref{thm:artin-local-inverse-v146} and
\ref{thm:sharp-finite-pencil} prove the equivalence
\[
 \mathfrak A_R^{[d]}\simeq\mathfrak A_{R'}^{[d]}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 R'=gR\text{ for some }g\in\PGL(V),
\]
where the isomorphism on the left is ungraded and unmarked. Every lower
truncation is independent of $R$. The statement includes singular
pencils. Its global and relative forms recover the pencil from a
Grassmannian-supported or curve-supported neighbourhood, and recover
moving pencil subbundles together with their actual source bundles.

The proof extracts the first relation intrinsically, reconstructs the
two rulings of its determinant support, and orients them by unequal
coefficient-support ranks. Cancellation of the intrinsic determinant
factor leaves a coefficient line. The irreducibility of
$\bigwedge^2\Sym^2V$ then identifies this line with the Pluecker line
of $R$. The moving-family inverse uses one constant left transformation
over the projective reduction; it is stronger than a collection of
independently marked fibrewise inverses. The detailed relative statements
and their complete proofs are retained in the appendices.

\subsection{Divisor geometry and ramification fibres}
For a vector space $E$, let $Y_g\subset\Gr(r,\Sym^D E)$ be the
scheme-theoretic image of multiplication
\[
 \nu_g:\Pj(\Sym^g E)\times\Gr(r,\Sym^{D-g}E)\longrightarrow Y_g.
\]
This finite map is the normalization. A divisor choice and its overlap
with the residual common factor distinguish separated branches from
ramified fibres. Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-normalization-v151} gives
the exact normal locus. We now determine considerably more than that
locus or a list of special fibres.

For binary forms, Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-fibres-v153} identifies the
normalization over the entire exact-gcd-degree-$s$ stratum with a finite
flat factorization morphism of degree $\binom{s}{g}$. It computes every
local fibre at every root partition, including simultaneous collisions,
as a tensor product of reciprocal complete intersections. Their lengths,
weighted Hilbert series, socles, and partition closure relations follow.
Theorem~\ref{thm:squarefree-v153} gives a global conductor theorem on
the squarefree-common-divisor open set $\mathcal U$:
\[
           \mathfrak c_{Y_g}|_{\mathcal U}
                       =\II_{Y_{g+1}/Y_g}|_{\mathcal U}.
\]
Its block monomial local form gives all multiple branch intersections,
seminormality, depth, multiplicity, and exact Cohen--Macaulay criteria,
for any number of divisor choices. Theorem~\ref{thm:atlas-v153}
provides a single completed incidence atlas for all binary collisions;
Theorem~\ref{thm:fitting-v153} extracts a closed singular Fitting ideal
in every double-root rank.

There is also a uniform result in arbitrary dimension. For a pure-power
common divisor, put $E=\C a\oplus W$, $g\geq k\geq1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:reciprocal-structure-v153}
computes the entire minimal relation module of its degree-$g$ divisor
fibre as
\[
             \bigoplus_{j=g+1}^{g+k}(\Sym^jW)^*.
\]
The fibre is a complete intersection exactly when $\dim W=1$.
This theorem applies to the ramified limiting algebra of
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-boundary-v152}. That point belongs to every
full $\operatorname{GL}(E)$ first-relation orbit closure. The passage
from a general pencil uses two specializations, first to a flag pencil
and then a scalar-matrix contraction. It is an orbit-incidence theorem
for full cotangent coordinate changes, distinct from a classification
of $\PGL(V)$ congruence-orbit boundaries. The chosen point is not
asserted to be a coordinate-free distinguished moduli point.

\subsection{Comparisons and organization}
Factorization and conductor constructions have substantial classical
precedents. The binary reciprocal rings are the classical rectangular
Schur quotients; we use the algebraic basis theorem of
\cite[Theorem~2.7]{Grinberg2019}. The comparisons with coincident-root
loci, tuple common-factor strata and conductor squares are given in
Appendix~\ref{sec:introduction-v132}, with
\cite{Chipalkatti2003,Kurmann2012,HuLinShao2007,Ferrand2003}.
We do not claim that these general mechanisms originate here. The
present conclusions concern the image of an unframed Grassmannian
subspace, its conductor and branch schemes, and the multivariate
minimal relation module of its divisor fibre.

The bibliographic record of \cite{Ballico93} concerns higher-order
failure loci. Its full theorem and proof text has not been obtained;
a theorem-level comparison of nonreduced structure, infinitesimal
order, family hypotheses, forgotten markings and inverse conclusions
therefore remains unavailable. No nonanticipation inference about that
paper is drawn from this documentary limitation.

The main text first proves the reconstruction mechanism and the local
pencil inverse, then treats normalization, conductor strata, collision
atlases and their occurrence in full first-relation orbit closures.
The appendices retain the detailed original formulations, moving-family
and curve proofs, recognition and rigidification results, covering-map
reconstruction, and spectral consequences. They are part of the article,
not replacements for missing arguments in the main text.
