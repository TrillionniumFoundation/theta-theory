\section{Finite remote entrances and the critical window}\label{sec:remote-general}
The preceding weight wall is an instance of a more general phenomenon.
A regular feasible corner, rather than invertibility of the ambient score,
is the relevant local hypothesis. We formulate the result in the
square-root probability embedding, so that the distance in its statement
is exactly the Hellinger distance and not a quadratic approximation to it.

Let $X$ be compact and semialgebraic, let $F$ be a continuous probability
map, and let $T$ be a continuous monic-polynomial root-multiset target of
fixed degree. Quotient a finite representation symmetry when necessary.
Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-exact-fibre}
 F^{-1}(P_0)=\{x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_m\}.
\end{equation}
The points are representatives in this quotient. Let $x_\delta\to x_0$
be a feasible centre path, $P_\delta=F(x_\delta)$, and suppose $P_0$ is
strictly positive. The design weights are fixed. Define
\[
 E(P)=(\sqrt{w_j}\sqrt{P_{je}})_{j,e},\qquad p_0=E(P_0),
 \qquad p_\delta=E(P_\delta)=p_0+\delta b+\delta^2c+o(\delta^2).
\]
The Euclidean distance between two embedded arrays is $h_w$.

For each $j\ge1$, assume that a full relative neighbourhood of $x_j$ in
$X$ has coordinates
\[
 \psi_j:K_j\cap B_{r_j}(0)\longrightarrow X,\qquad \psi_j(0)=x_j,
\]
where $K_j$ is a closed convex polyhedral cone and $\psi_j$ is a Nash
parametrization, a homeomorphism onto that relative neighbourhood. Its
inverse need not be differentiable at the vertex. Assume that
$E\circ F\circ\psi_j$ has a $C^2$ extension to an ambient neighbourhood
of zero. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-corner-jet}
 E(F(\psi_j(z)))=p_0+J_jz+Q_j(z,z)+o(\|z\|^2),\qquad
 \|J_jv\|\ge a_j\|v\|\quad(v\in K_j),\quad a_j>0.
\end{equation}
Here $Q_j(z,z)$ denotes one half of the second derivative. The score may
have a kernel in the ambient vector space; the last inequality is required
only on the feasible cone. Choose small disjoint closed neighbourhoods
$N_0,\ldots,N_m$, with boundaries avoiding the exact fibre, and put
\[
 d_j(\delta)=\min_{x\in N_j}h_w(F(x),P_\delta),\qquad
 \mu_j=\min_{v\in K_j}\|J_jv-b\|.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Remote entrance for regular corners]\label{thm:remote-general}
Assume \eqref{eq:finite-exact-fibre}--\eqref{eq:general-corner-jet} and
$\mu_j>0$ for $1\le j\le m$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-entrance}
 d_j(\delta)=\mu_j\delta+O(\delta^2)\qquad(\delta\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
For small $\delta$ the minimum is unchanged on replacing $N_j$ by any
fixed smaller relative neighbourhood of $x_j$. If $\lambda>0$ differs
from every $\mu_j$, the target image of the radius-$\lambda\delta$ ball
converges in Hausdorff distance to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entrance-staircase}
 \mathcal D_\lambda=\{T(x_0)\}\cup
                 \{T(x_j):\mu_j<\lambda\}.
\end{equation}
Its diameter consequently tends to $\diam\mathcal D_\lambda$.
For $\lambda<\min_j\mu_j$, the whole ball is the base local ball for
small $\delta$. Any compact-uniform local asymptotic law at the base
therefore remains valid in this regime.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a remote index and omit it from the notation. Taylor's theorem and
cone coercivity give, after reducing $r$,
\[
 \|E(F(\psi(z)))-p_0\|\ge a\|z\|/2\quad(z\in K,\ \|z\|<r).
\]
The competitor $z=0$ gives an upper bound $O(\delta)$ for $d(\delta)$.
Compactness and \eqref{eq:finite-exact-fibre} exclude the boundary of
$N$ from minimizing sequences. A minimizer thus has $z=O(\delta)$:
its distance to $p_0$ is at most $d(\delta)+\|p_\delta-p_0\|$.
Write $z=\delta v$. The vectors $v\in K$ are uniformly bounded, and
\[
 E(F(\psi(\delta v)))-p_\delta
   =\delta(Jv-b)+\delta^2\{Q(v,v)-c\}+o(\delta^2)
\]
uniformly on bounded sets. Minimizing the leading norm gives the lower
bound in \eqref{eq:general-entrance}. The function $v\mapsto\|Jv-b\|$
is coercive on $K$, so its minimum is attained; inserting a minimizer
in the exact chart gives the upper bound. The same localization shows
independence of the fixed neighbourhood.

Outside the union of the $N_j$ the continuous function
$h_w(F(x),P_0)$ has a positive minimum. This remains an exclusion gap
for balls of radius $\lambda\delta$ about $P_\delta$. In a remote
neighbourhood, membership is possible exactly when
$t\ge d_j(\delta)$, by compact attainment. Thus a remote neighbourhood
is excluded if $\lambda<\mu_j$ and contains a minimizer if
$\lambda>\mu_j$. Every sequence of remaining competitors converges,
after subselection, to an exact representative; its target converges to
that representative's target. Conversely $x_\delta$ supplies $T(x_0)$
in the limit and each included remote minimizer supplies $T(x_j)$.
Finiteness gives Hausdorff convergence to \eqref{eq:entrance-staircase}.
The diameter conclusion follows from the elementary bound by twice the
Hausdorff distance. When no remote neighbourhood is included, all
competitors are in $N_0$, which proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The coefficient of the next term can be determined without assuming a
unique minimizer or an invertible ambient score. This is useful at a
change of active face of a constrained Fisher program.

\begin{theorem}[The second-order entrance coefficient]\label{thm:critical-window}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:remote-general}, set
\[
 M_j=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{v\in K_j}\|J_jv-b\|.
\]
This is a nonempty compact set. The residual $r_j=J_jv-b$ is the same
for all $v\in M_j$, and has norm $\mu_j$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-coefficient}
 k_j=\frac1{\mu_j}\min_{v\in M_j}
                   \langle r_j,Q_j(v,v)-c\rangle.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-expansion}
 d_j(\delta)=\mu_j\delta+k_j\delta^2+o(\delta^2).
\end{equation}
At radius $t=\mu_j\delta+s\delta^2$, the $j$th remote neighbourhood
is excluded for $s<k_j$ and included for $s>k_j$, for all sufficiently
small positive $\delta$. Equality requires the next nonzero term or the
exact minimum inequality; it is not assigned to a regime by convention.
For algebraic Nash input, $\mu_j$ and $k_j$ are real algebraic numbers
computable by real quantifier elimination and real-root isolation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Coercivity bounds all minimizers. The image $J_jK_j$ is a closed convex
polyhedral cone. The squared distance to $b$ has a unique minimizing
image point: if two different image points minimized it, their midpoint
would have strictly smaller squared distance. This proves the assertion
about $r_j$ without requiring uniqueness of its preimage.

Again suppress $j$. Localization in the preceding proof bounds all
scaled minimizers in one compact subset of $K$, also containing $M$.
On that set the squared normalized distance has the uniform expansion
\[
 f_\delta(v)=\|Jv-b\|^2+
       2\delta\langle Jv-b,Q(v,v)-c\rangle+o(\delta).
\]
Every limit of minimizers of $f_\delta$ belongs to $M$, by comparison
with any point of $M$. The first term is at least $\mu^2$ everywhere.
The lower bound for the first-order increment of $\min f_\delta$ is
therefore the minimum of the displayed second term over $M$. For the
upper bound evaluate $f_\delta$ at a point of $M$ attaining that
minimum. Compactness and continuity justify both extrema. Consequently
\[
 \min f_\delta=\mu^2+
 2\delta\min_{v\in M}\langle r,Q(v,v)-c\rangle+o(\delta).
\]
Taking the positive square root and multiplying by $\delta$ proves
\eqref{eq:critical-expansion}. Comparing with the proposed radius gives
the strict cases. The minimum defining $M$ and the minimum in
\eqref{eq:critical-coefficient} have first-order polynomial formulas
with compact attainment; the latter may equivalently be restricted to
a sufficiently large algebraic ball. Elimination and isolation give
algebraic outputs. The formula is intrinsic to the distance and the fixed
centre path, since its value equals the coefficient in
\eqref{eq:critical-expansion}, independently of the chosen chart.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Application to the cubic weight wall]\label{cor:cubic-critical}
The remote point of Section~\ref{sec:wall} satisfies the preceding
hypotheses with $m=1$, $K_1=\mathcal T_*$ and $\mu_1=\mu_E$.
In particular its entrance has the expansion
\[
 d_{\rm rem}(\delta)=\mu_E\delta+k_E\delta^2+o(\delta^2),
\]
where $k_E$ is given by \eqref{eq:critical-coefficient} in the exact
remote chart of Proposition~\ref{prop:remote-cone}. This formula does
not assert a common sign for $k_E$ on the weight-wall family.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-fibre} gives the two exact representatives.
The remote chart and its converse feasible realization are
Proposition~\ref{prop:remote-cone}; its smooth coefficient formulas and
positive normalizers give the required $C^2$ observation embedding.
In these coordinates
\[
 J_1v=\left(\frac{\sqrt{w_j}}{2\sqrt{P_{0,je}}}(S_*v)_{je}\right)_{j,e},
 \qquad
 b=\left(\frac{\sqrt{w_j}}{2\sqrt{P_{0,je}}}(B_0)_{je}\right)_{j,e}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:mu-positive} is exactly cone coercivity and positive
separation in this embedding. Hence the first minimization is
\eqref{eq:mu}. The two second-order derivatives in
\eqref{eq:critical-coefficient} are taken from the rational coefficient
chart and the square-root probability map, not from root coordinates
with an unproved differentiability at a multiple root. Theorems
\ref{thm:remote-general} and \ref{thm:critical-window} apply.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Both signs of the critical correction]\label{ex:critical-signs}
Let $e_0=(1,1)/\sqrt2$, $e_1=(-1,1)/\sqrt2$ and
\[
 q(s)=\frac{(1-s^2)e_0+2se_1}{1+s^2},\qquad
 P(s)=(q_1(s)^2,q_2(s)^2).
\]
For sufficiently small $|s|$ both coordinates of $q(s)$ are positive.
Take a base interval with observation $P(s)$ and a disjoint remote
interval $0\le u\le e$ with observation $P(-u/2)$. Give the two
intervals distinct simple-root targets at zero, for example
$z-(1/4+s)$ and $z-(3/4+u)$, and choose $e$ small enough to keep
all roots in $[0,1]$. The exact fibre at $P(0)$ has two points.
Choose the centre $s_\delta=(\delta+a\delta^2)/2$, where $a$ is fixed.
At the remote point $K=\R_+$, $Jv=-ve_1$, $b=e_1$,
$c=ae_1-e_0/2$, $M=\{0\}$ and $\mu=1$. Formula
\eqref{eq:critical-coefficient} gives $k=a$.
Indeed the nearest remote point is $u=0$ for small $\delta>0$, since
on this short circle arc the chord distance increases with the separation
of the stereographic parameters. Directly,
\[
 d_{\rm rem}(\delta)=\frac{2s_\delta}{\sqrt{1+s_\delta^2}}
                 =\delta+a\delta^2+O(\delta^3).
\]
Thus either sign of the second-order correction occurs in an algebraic
probability experiment satisfying all hypotheses. For $a=0$ the
quadratic coefficient vanishes and the next term is $-\delta^3/8$.
\end{example}

These results apply in any coefficient dimension and to any fixed number
of latent components once their stated finite-fibre and feasible-cone
hypotheses have been verified. They are not a classification of all
rank-deficient singularities. The endpoint double root at the geometric
wall requires a different parametrization and a different separation
argument, supplied in Section~\ref{sec:geometric-wall}.
