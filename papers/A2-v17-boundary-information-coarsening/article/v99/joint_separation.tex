\section{Scalar envelopes do not determine the spectral invariant}\label{sec:separation}
The distinction between the scalar orders and the joint initial fibre
can be made exact. The following examples have not only the same optimal
orders but the same individual coefficient envelopes at every sufficiently
small radius. Their leading spectral diameters are nevertheless different.
The assertion concerns the scalar data of Corollary~\ref{cor:orders} and
separate coefficient envelopes; it does not concern the collection of
value functions of all possible combinations of the coefficients.

\begin{theorem}[Separation from marginal scalar data]\label{thm:scalar-separation}
There are two compact semialgebraic probability experiments with a unique
exact target, real quadratic targets with roots in $[0,1]$, and identical
coefficient orders and individual coefficient envelopes, for which
\[
 \omega_{\mathrm A}(t)=(1+\sqrt5)t,\qquad
 \omega_{\mathrm B}(t)=\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}2t
 \quad (0\le t\le 1/20).
\]
In both experiments the coefficient orders are $(\beta_1,\beta_2)=(1,2)$
and the spectral exponent is one. On each source arc the orders of
$(G,H_1,H_2)$ are $(2,2,4)$. Thus the order lists and the separate optimal
coefficient envelopes do not determine the joint initial root set or its
diameter.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $r=1/2$ and $e=1/20$. In each experiment the source is the disjoint
union of two closed intervals $0\le\rho\le e$. Its probability map and
datum are
\[
 F(\rho)=\bigl((1-\rho^2/2)^2,\ \rho^2(1-\rho^2/4)\bigr),
 \qquad P_0=(1,0).
\]
The entries are nonnegative and sum to one. Since
$\sqrt{F_1}=1-\rho^2/2$ on this interval,
\[
 h(F(\rho),P_0)^2=2-2\sqrt{F_1}=\rho^2.
\]
Associate with an interval labelled $(u,v)$ the target
\[
 A_{u,v,\rho}(z)=(z-r)^2+u\rho(z-r)+v\rho^2.
\]
Use the label sets
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm A}=\{(1,-1),(2,-4)\},\qquad
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm B}=\{(1,-4),(2,-1)\}.
\]
These are compact semialgebraic experiments in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:input}; the discrete label is simply an additional
source coordinate. All exact targets are $(z-r)^2$. The two root
increments on an interval are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separation-roots}
 \rho\,\ell(u,v),\quad\rho\,q(u,v),\qquad
 \ell(u,v)=\frac{-u-\sqrt{u^2-4v}}2<0<
 q(u,v)=\frac{-u+\sqrt{u^2-4v}}2.
\end{equation}
They lie in $[0,1]$ after translation by $r$, since the greatest absolute
increment is at most $(1+\sqrt5)/20<1/2$.

Write $c_1=u\rho$ and $c_0=v\rho^2$. In either experiment the exact
marginal images over the radius-$t$ ball are
\[
 \{c_1\}=[0,2t],\qquad \{c_0\}=[-4t^2,0].
\]
In particular $\max|c_1|=2t$ and $\max|c_0|=4t^2$, not just to leading
order. Moreover $G=\rho^2$, $H_1=u^2\rho^2$, and
$H_2=v^2\rho^4$. The order assertions follow. Even the unordered lists
of individual squared units agree: they are $\{1,4\}$ and $\{1,16\}$.
Their association across the two coordinates is different.

For $t>0$, put $\lambda=\rho/t$. The scaled coefficient image is already
independent of $t$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separation-joint}
 \cC_{0,\mathrm E}=
 \bigcup_{(u,v)\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm E}}
       \{(u\lambda,v\lambda^2):0\le\lambda\le1\},
 \qquad \mathrm E\in\{\mathrm A,\mathrm B\}.
\end{equation}
This is its joint initial coefficient set. The ordered root image consists
of $(\lambda\ell(u,v),\lambda q(u,v))$. Its first coordinate is
nonpositive, its second nonnegative, and the zero vector belongs to it.
The diameter in the maximum metric is the larger of its two coordinate
widths. Since $u>0$, $-\ell(u,v)>q(u,v)$ on each interval. Therefore
\[
 C_{\mathrm E}=\max_{(u,v)\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm E}}
                     \frac{u+\sqrt{u^2-4v}}2.
\]
For $\mathrm A$ the maximum is $1+\sqrt5$. For $\mathrm B$ it is
$(1+\sqrt{17})/2$, which exceeds $1+\sqrt2$.
The inequalities are strict and these two constants differ. Translation
and multiplication by $t$ in \eqref{eq:separation-roots} prove the asserted
exact diameter identities. No product approximation of
\eqref{eq:separation-joint} has been used.
\end{proof}

The example isolates the information carried by the joint specialization:
relations between weighted coefficients survive both real closure and the
root map. A finite list of scalar exponents cannot recover these relations,
even when augmented by the separate sharp envelopes. Conversely the full
joint set determines the diameter by Theorem~\ref{thm:joint}; no new scalar
resolution theorem is required for this distinction.
