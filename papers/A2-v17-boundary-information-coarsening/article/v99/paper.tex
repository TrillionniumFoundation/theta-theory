\begin{abstract}
We study unordered polynomial spectra observed through stochastic channels
and an unknown scalar normalization. For compact semialgebraic models,
a finite relative real monomial presentation determines a joint weighted
initial coefficient fibre, with the datum held fixed. Its root-multiset
image gives the exact leading bottleneck diameter. A finite Nash
stratification in fixed-format families supports constant rational
exponents, Nash leading diameters and compact-uniform power remainders;
algebraic presentations admit finite exact algorithms. Two real polynomial
experiments with identical individual coefficient envelopes have different
leading spectral diameters, separating the joint invariant from marginal
scalar data. We compute every multiplicity and boundary pattern in the
fixed-degree, full-rank, coprime binary model and classify the whole exact
fibre of a rank-deficient cubic family. For finite exact fibres with
cone-coercive remote parametrizations, we prove an entrance law and a
second-order formula, allowing nonunique first-order minimizers. Both the
stochastic weight wall and the geometric endpoint wall satisfy these
hypotheses. Their remote entrance scales are linear while the local Fisher
constants stay finite; on the opposite side their exact spectral diameters
open differently. Explicit rational examples decide the critical rays.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An unordered spectrum is not recovered by bounding its polynomial
coefficients independently. Near a multiple root, relations among the
coefficients survive rescaling and constrain the limiting root multiset.
In a normalized stochastic experiment the admissible relations also
include inequalities, rank constraints and an unobserved scalar
normalization. A second phenomenon is global: a component of the exact
fibre can enter from a positive distance in coefficient space while the
local inverse at the original component remains uniformly conditioned.
This article separates these two problems and connects them by a real
spectral atlas.

\subsection*{The joint invariant}
Let $X$ be a compact semialgebraic model, $F$ its continuous semialgebraic
probability map, and $A_x$ a continuous monic target polynomial. At a datum
$P$ with a unique exact target, write $c_{r,a}$ for the coefficient of
degree $a$ in the monic factor belonging to a root $r$ of multiplicity
$m_r$. Set $G=h_w(F(x),P)^2$ and $H_{r,a}=|c_{r,a}|^2$ on the actual
real observation graph. A real monomial presentation gives
\[
 G=u\prod_i|y_i|^{a_i},\qquad
 H_{r,a}=v_{r,a}\prod_i|y_i|^{b_{r,a,i}},\qquad
 \alpha=\min_{r,a,i}\frac{b_{r,a,i}}{a_i(m_r-a)}.
\]
Only divisors with an admissible real transversal enter the minimum.
The scalar divisor-ratio calculation is classical. The additional object
is obtained by the simultaneous substitutions, for $\alpha=p/q$,
\[
 R=\varepsilon^q U,\qquad
 c_{r,a}=\varepsilon^{p(m_r-a)}V_{r,a},\qquad \|U\|\le1,
\]
followed by real closure with $P$ fixed. Its coefficient projection
$\cC_0$ retains all joint relations and inequality faces. Theorem
\ref{thm:joint} identifies the exact leading diameter as
\[
 C(P)=\max_{V,V'\in\cC_0}\max_r d_\infty\left(
 Z\left(z^{m_r}+\sum_{a<m_r}V_{r,a}z^a\right),
 Z\left(z^{m_r}+\sum_{a<m_r}V'_{r,a}z^a\right)\right).
\]
In particular, the construction is neither a product of marginal
coefficient bounds nor a closure in which the centre is allowed to drift.

The distinction is quantitative. Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-separation}
gives two compact real-rooted quadratic experiments having identical
observation functions, identical scalar order triples $(2,2,4)$ on each
component, and identical exact individual coefficient ranges at every
small radius. Nevertheless their spectral diameters are respectively
\[
 (1+\sqrt5)t\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad \frac{1+\sqrt{17}}2\,t.
\]
The difference is the pairing of the leading coefficients. Thus even
sharp individual scalar envelopes do not determine the spectral constant.
This assertion concerns marginal data; it does not identify the joint
invariant with information irrecoverable from every possible scalar
function of all the coefficients.

\subsection*{Relative real geometry and effective output}
Theorem~\ref{lem:relative} constructs a finite fibrewise proper real
monomial cover in fixed-format families. Its proof keeps four interfaces
separate. A complete finite diagram, including inverse maps on the actual
successive complements of a closed chain, is spread over an algebraic
base. A single generic localization for each finite collection of charts
makes the Rees algebras commute with every fibre base change. Square-slack
lifts and selected real algebraic branches preserve the original graph,
including strict exclusions. Finally, real accessibility is a finite
first-order condition with an attaining transversal. Special algebraic
fibres are reconstructed by dimension descent; changes between real
chambers are handled by a separate finite semialgebraic partition.
Appendix~\ref{app:foundations} proves these interfaces explicitly.

The joint initial family is defined by a formula in which the parameter
is free but fixed inside the closure quantifiers. The semialgebraic error
between its root image and the scaled positive-radius image tends to zero
on each fibre. A finite support argument and positive continuous
threshold selection then give, on every compact subset $H$ of a finite
Nash stratum,
\[
 \omega_\theta(t)=C(\theta)t^\alpha+O_H(t^{\alpha+\nu}),\qquad
 \alpha,\nu>0,\quad \alpha\in\Q,\quad C\text{ positive and Nash}.
\]
Hardt triviality is used for topological types and proper restrictions,
not for a metric rate. Theorem~\ref{thm:effective} gives finite algorithms
for algebraic inputs. In particular, its order algorithm tests finitely
many rational candidates by quantifier elimination; it does not invoke an
unspecified procedure for choosing a Puiseux branch. The detailed
representation and termination arguments are in Appendix~\ref{app:effective}.

\subsection*{Explicit binary polynomial laws}
The experiment in Section~\ref{sec:model} has two monic degree-$d$
component polynomials with roots in $[0,D]$, positive mixture weights,
and binary column-stochastic channels. With coprime components, strictly
positive invertible channels and at least $2d+1$ exterior clocks,
Theorem~\ref{thm:mult-atlas} gives the whole-model leading set for every
multiplicity and endpoint/interior pattern. A repeated interior cluster
has exponent $1/2$ and leading constant
\[
 C=\max_j2\sqrt{(m_j-1)/m_j}\,\kappa_j^{-1/4},
\]
where each $\kappa_j$ is an explicit projected Fisher-cone minimum.
With no repeated interior cluster the exponent is one. Competitors
range over the entire closed model, not just the root-pattern stratum.
These are fixed-degree, full-rank, coprime binary assertions; no
classification of arbitrary multicomponent singularities is implicit.

Rank deficiency requires a separate inverse. For
$f=z(z-r)^2$, $g=(z-s)^3$, $0<s<r<D$, positive channels and invertible
second channel, put
\[
 B=(3D-r)s-2rD,\qquad
 E=(1-\alpha)(r+3s)(2r-3s)^2-4\pi r^3.
\]
The entire exact fibre is classified in Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-fibre}.
The identifying locus is
$\{\det U\ne0\}\cup\{B<0\}\cup\{E<0\}$.
A quantitative inverse through rank changes uses the second marginal
and two robust pencil-isolation inequalities, never division by
$\det U$, $B$ or $E$. The resulting local Fisher law remains coercive
through both identification walls. Global identification can fail there
because the exclusion radius of a remote component collapses, rather
than because the local constant diverges.

\subsection*{Remote entrances and two different walls}
For a compact model with a finite exact fibre, Theorem
\ref{thm:remote-general} describes the entrance of each remote
representative admitting an exact polyhedral-cone parametrization and a
cone-coercive first observation derivative. Ambient score injectivity is
not required. In the square-root probability embedding, write the remote
map as $p_0+Jz+Q(z,z)+o(\|z\|^2)$ and the centre path as
$p_0+\delta b+\delta^2c+o(\delta^2)$. The entrance coefficient is
$\mu=\min_{v\in K}\|Jv-b\|$. If $\mu>0$, then
\[
 d_{\rm rem}(\delta)=\mu\delta+k\delta^2+o(\delta^2),\qquad
 k=\frac1\mu\min_{v\in M}\langle Jv-b,Q(v,v)-c\rangle,
 \quad M=\arg\min_{v\in K}\|Jv-b\|.
\]
The residual $Jv-b$ is common to all $v\in M$, although the minimizer
itself need not be unique. Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-window} proves this
formula and the strict regimes in the second-order critical window.
Several remote representatives give a finite sequence of target-set
entrances. Both signs of $k$ occur in an elementary probability example.

At the cubic weight wall $E=0$, $B>0$, the remote pair is
$(f,(z-a)(z-b)^2)$ with $s<a<r<b<D$. Theorem~\ref{thm:wall} gives its
first entrance law; Corollary~\ref{cor:cubic-critical} gives the
second-order coefficient. At the geometric wall $B=0$, $E>0$, the
remote double root is instead at $D$. Ordered endpoint-root coordinates,
not the interior variance cone, give its complete feasible
parametrization. Theorem~\ref{thm:geometric-wall} proves the two-scale
entrance for a transverse fixed-design path through this wall. In both
cases the entrance is linear and the base local square-root law stays
uniformly conditioned. On the opposite side the weight-wall pencil
interval opens linearly and can produce a square-root increase in exact
spectral diameter. The geometric-wall interval opens quadratically, yet
its exact diameter remains $D-r$. These are two completed, distinct
mechanisms; their intersection and arbitrary higher-rank singularities
are not being identified with either local cone.

\subsection*{Classical inputs and scope of the contribution}
Resolution and principalization, scalar divisor ratios, quantifier
elimination, semialgebraic triviality and algebraic one-variable
asymptotics are established inputs \cite{BM88,BM97,BM08,BE09,BPR06,BCR98,Hardt80}.
Algorithmic equiresolution in families is studied in \cite{ENV03}.
The finite-diagram and real-cover arguments here specify how these inputs
apply to the actual constrained graph. They are not claims to a new
resolution algorithm. H\`a's current version \cite{Ha26} already gives
finite scalar testing in Theorem 5.8, a non-toric finite-candidate chamber
principle in Theorem 9.9, and a toric family wall--chamber statement under
the hypotheses of Theorem 12.3. A finite chamber vocabulary is therefore
not the novelty claimed here. The joint real leading set, its separation
from individual scalar envelopes, and the remote-entrance laws with
explicit weight and endpoint realizations are the additional metric
statements. No general leading set is asserted to be quadratic.

Sections~\ref{sec:real}--\ref{sec:separation} establish the general joint
invariant, after Section~\ref{sec:model} fixes the experiment. Sections
\ref{sec:multiplicity}--\ref{sec:wall} develop the explicit multiplicity,
rank-deficient cubic and weight-wall laws. Sections~\ref{sec:remote-general}
and \ref{sec:geometric-wall} give the structural entrance theorem and the
geometric wall. The proof appendices supply the complete relative and
algorithmic interfaces. Finite exact diagnostics accompany the displayed
algebraic examples; they are neither a substitute for these proofs nor a
machine verification of their universal quantifiers.
