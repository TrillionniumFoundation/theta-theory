\subsection{Exact critical-ray examples}\label{sec:critical-examples}
We record two algebraic specializations of the second-order formula.
Use the clocks $(6/5,7/5,8/5,9/5,2,5/2,3)$ with equal weights,
$u_1=u_2=(1/3,2/3)^{\mathsf T}$, and
$v_1=(1/4,3/4)^{\mathsf T}$, $v_2=(3/4,1/4)^{\mathsf T}$.

\begin{example}[An endpoint wall with a positive critical correction]\label{ex:geometric-exact}
Take $D=1$, $r=3/5$, $s_0=1/2$, $\alpha=2/5$, $\pi=1/10$.
The remote coordinates and the interval-opening coefficient are
\[
 a=\frac47,\qquad c_* =\frac{32}7,\qquad
 \beta=\frac{21}{160},\qquad
 v_f^*=\frac1{556}(289,267)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \dot b=8,\qquad \Lambda_B=\frac{38400}{49}.
\]
The projected three-variable program has the empty support as its only
successful independent-support test. Its linear vector is strictly
negative entrywise, so the minimizer has all three nonnegative
coordinates zero. The free score has rank seven; hence the first-order
minimizer is unique, including the endpoint split. Exact rational
arithmetic and positive-root isolation give
\begin{align*}
 0.000063237134157102&<\mu_B<0.000063237134157103,\\
 0.071857358263070&<k_B<0.071857358263071.
\end{align*}
Consequently the remote branch is excluded on $t=\mu_B\delta$ for
all sufficiently small positive $\delta$. On the opposite side the
exact spectral diameter is $2/5$, despite the nonzero quadratic
pencil-interval opening.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The weight-wall critical ray]\label{ex:weight-critical}
At the weight-wall datum of Example~\ref{ex:wall-exact}, the four entries
of the projected linear vector $g_*$ are strictly negative. The empty
cone support is therefore the unique first-order minimizer, with its
seven free coordinates recovered by the inverse free Gram matrix.
The second-order formula gives
\[
 0.011285405417542<k_E<0.011285405417543.
\]
Thus the critical ray $t=\mu_E\delta$ also excludes the remote branch
for this datum when $\delta>0$ is sufficiently small. This sign is a
property of this specialization, not a convention at an unresolved ray
and not a sign assertion for the whole wall family.
\end{example}

For reproducibility, the finite arithmetic behind both examples can be
specified without numerical differentiation. Write the exact remote and
base probability expansions at the selected first-order minimizer as
\[
 P_{\rm rem}(\delta)=P+\delta A+\delta^2C+O(\delta^3),\qquad
 P_{\rm base}(\delta)=P+\delta B+\delta^2D+O(\delta^3).
\]
The rational coefficients are obtained by multiplying the exact polynomial,
weight and channel jets and dividing by the normalizer. The square-root
expansion then gives
\begin{align}
 \mu^2&=\frac14\sum_{j,e}\frac{w_j(A_{je}-B_{je})^2}{P_{je}},\nonumber\\
 \mu k&=\sum_{j,e}w_j\left\{
 \frac{(A_{je}-B_{je})(C_{je}-D_{je})}{4P_{je}}
 -\frac{(A_{je}-B_{je})(A_{je}^2-B_{je}^2)}{16P_{je}^2}
 \right\}.\label{eq:exact-second-coefficient}
\end{align}
Strict empty-support KKT inequalities and positive semidefiniteness of
the projected Gram matrix prove uniqueness of the cone optimum even
when that matrix is singular: the difference of objectives is
$z^{\mathsf T}Qz-2g^{\mathsf T}z>0$ for every nonzero $z\ge0$.
The rational values of $\mu^2$, $\mu k$, the Gram data and isolating
intervals are retained with the exact regression. The interval tests
square rational endpoints and compare integers after clearing positive
denominators. They certify these finite specializations only.
