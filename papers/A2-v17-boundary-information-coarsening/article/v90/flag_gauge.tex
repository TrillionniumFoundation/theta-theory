\section{An additive gauge on noncommuting flag algebras}
\label{sec:flag-gauge}
The scalar normalization acts on a matrix polynomial by an entrywise
interpolation operator. It consequently preserves every constant
invariant flag of the normalized coefficient algebra. Commutativity
and semisimplicity are not needed for this preservation. The resulting
inverse estimate has two scales: scalar multiplicity governs the
normalization term, while paths along the flag govern general matrix
perturbations.

Consider square quotients $P=M/q$, with $M$ of degree $d$, invertible
leading coefficient $A_0$, and $q$ monic of degree $d$. Assume fixed
coefficient bounds for $q$ and its competitors, a positive lower bound
for $|q(T_j)|$, and a fixed disk containing the zeros of $\det M$.
The clock geometry and $d,k$ are fixed. The data norm is again the
maximum Frobenius norm at the clocks. Suppose $\tau(P)>0$ and the
coefficients of $C(z)=M(z)A_0^{-1}$ have a common complete invariant
flag over $\C$. An orthonormal basis adapted to that flag gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-triangularization}
 Q^*C(z)Q=D_f(z)+N(z),\qquad D_f=\diag(f_1,\ldots,f_k),
\end{equation}
where $Q$ is unitary, each $f_i$ is monic of degree $d$, and $N$ is
strictly upper triangular of degree at most $d-1$. The $f_i$ need not
be distinct, and the coefficients need not commute or be diagonalizable.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-certificate}
 n_Q=\|N\|_{\rm coef},\qquad
 B_Q=\|A_0^{-1}\|_\op\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}(1+n_Q)^h,
 \qquad B_\triangle(P)=\min_Q B_Q,
\end{equation}
where the minimum is over unitary simultaneous triangularizers of the
recovered coefficient matrices. This nonempty set is closed in the
unitary group; the minimum is attained. It is an observable finite
optimization over flags of a known matrix algebra, not over latent
stochastic factorizations. It is invariant under constant unitary
changes of row and column coordinates. The assertions below are
stability statements; no complexity bound for finding this flag is
required.

\begin{theorem}[Two-scale additive flag gauge]\label{thm:flag-gauge}
For every degree-$d$ competitor $M'/q'$ sufficiently close in the
sense $\delta(\tau(P)^{-1}+B_\triangle(P))\le c$, its numerator has
invertible leading coefficient and exactly $kd$ roots, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-additive}
 d_\infty(Z(M),Z(M'))
 \le C\left\{\left(\frac{\delta}{\tau(P)}\right)^{1/d}
                 +(B_\triangle(P)\delta)^{1/(kd)}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The competitor need not preserve a flag, commute, or be semisimple.
The constants depend on the declared degree, dimension, clock and
scalar-root bounds, not on $\tau$, $B_\triangle$, or a latent channel
condition.

There is a refined version for separated clusters. Suppose the roots
of all the $f_i$ lie in a fixed collection of mutually separated disks,
with a fixed margin between the roots and the disk boundaries. Let
$m_{is}$ be the number of roots of $f_i$ in disk $s$, counted with
multiplicity, and put $m=\max_{i,s}m_{is}$. In a chosen flag draw an
edge $i\to j$, $i<j$, when the polynomial $N_{ij}$ is nonzero, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-path-order}
 \nu_Q=\max_s\max_{\text{directed paths }\mathcal P}
                         \sum_{i\in\mathcal P}m_{is}.
\end{equation}
Single-vertex paths are included. Then $m\le\nu_Q\le kd$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-cluster}
 d_\infty(Z(M),Z(M'))
 \le C\left\{(\delta/\tau(P))^{1/m}
                         +(B_Q\delta)^{1/\nu_Q}\right\},
\end{equation}
with additional dependence on the fixed cluster separation and margin.
The path exponent is a valid observable upper certificate; cancellations
between paths may improve it, so it is not asserted to be the minimal
pole order in every flag algebra.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $r=q'-q$ and let $\mathcal H_r$ act on a scalar or matrix
polynomial $F$ by
\[
 \mathcal H_rF=I_d\big(r(T_j)F(T_j)/q(T_j)\big).
\]
It is linear, commutes with constant left and right multiplication,
and preserves every identically zero matrix entry. The normalization
equation and interpolation give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-error-split}
 \|r\|_{\rm coef}\le C\delta/\tau(P),\qquad
 M'=\overline M_r+H,\quad
 \overline M_r=M+\mathcal H_rM,\quad
 \|H\|_{\rm coef}\le C\delta.
\end{equation}
In the chosen flag,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-gauge-identity}
 Q^*\overline M_r A_0^{-1}Q
    =D_f+\mathcal H_rD_f+N+\mathcal H_rN
    =:\overline D+\overline N.
\end{equation}
In particular the diagonal perturbations
$h_i=\mathcal H_rf_i$ satisfy $\|h_i\|_{\rm coef}\le C\|r\|_{\rm coef}$,
whereas $\|\overline N\|_{\rm coef}\le Cn_Q$ for $\|r\|_{\rm coef}\le1$.
No inverse of $A_0$ or norm of $N$ occurs in the first bound.

On a fixed disk containing the roots put $\rho=\dist(z,Z(M))$.
Since $|f_i(z)|\ge\rho^d$, the inequalities
$\rho^d\ge C_1\|r\|_{\rm coef}$ give
$|f_i(z)+u h_i(z)|\ge\rho^d/2$ for $0\le u\le1$.
The preliminary homotopy $M+u\mathcal H_rM$ is triangular after the
constant transformations in \eqref{eq:flag-gauge-identity}; its
determinant has no zeros outside these scalar disks.
For the second homotopy use the terminating triangular inverse
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-inverse-series}
 (\overline D+\overline N)^{-1}
       =\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}(-\overline D^{-1}\overline N)^h
                                             \overline D^{-1}.
\end{equation}
It follows, for $0<\rho\le1$, that
\[
 \|\overline M_r(z)^{-1}\|\le CB_Q\rho^{-kd}.
\]
Thus $\overline M_r+sH$ is invertible whenever
\[
 \rho\ge C_2\{\|r\|_{\rm coef}^{1/d}
                                  +(B_Q\delta)^{1/(kd)}\},\quad 0\le s\le1.
\]
This is the decisive separation: the normalization error is used to
control diagonal scalar denominators before the inverse series is
applied to $H$. Inserting both errors into a single Neumann estimate
would unnecessarily apply the longest flag path to $r$ as well.

We verify that no degree or roots are lost at infinity. The leading
diagonal entries of the first homotopy are $1+u(h_i)_d$, bounded below
by $1/2$ when $C\|r\|_{\rm coef}\le1/2$. Its leading strictly upper
part has norm at most $C\|r\|_{\rm coef}n_Q$. The same finite series
bounds the inverse of its leading coefficient by $CB_Q$. If
$CB_Q\delta\le1/2$, adding $sH_d$ preserves invertibility. Every
homotopy determinant therefore has degree exactly $kd$.
For $|z|$ larger than a fixed multiple of the scalar root bound,
$|f_i(z)|\ge c|z|^d$, while
$|h_i(z)|\le C\|r\|_{\rm coef}|z|^d$ and
$\|\overline N(z)\|\le Cn_Q|z|^d$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:flag-inverse-series} then gives
$\|\overline M_r(z)^{-1}\|\le CB_Q|z|^{-d}$.
Since $\|H(z)\|\le C\delta|z|^d$, the same smallness excludes zeros
outside this fixed disk. Argument-principle counting on the connected
components of the remaining root disks and the fixed-permutation limit
of Lemma~\ref{lem:roots} give the matching bound. Choose a minimizing
flag in \eqref{eq:flag-certificate} to obtain \eqref{eq:flag-additive}.
This also proves leading invertibility for a competitor that was not
initially assumed regular.

For the refinement, near cluster $s$ the factors outside that cluster
are uniformly bounded below. Therefore
\[
 |f_i(z)|\ge c\min\{1,\rho^{m_{is}}\}.
\]
The scalar gauge is excluded by radius $C\|r\|_{\rm coef}^{1/m}$.
Expand each entry of \eqref{eq:flag-inverse-series} along increasing
index paths. A path has one diagonal denominator for each visited
vertex and only the corresponding off-diagonal entries in its
numerator. Zero edges remain zero under $\mathcal H_r$. On cluster
$s$ the denominator exponent along that path is
$\sum_{i\in\mathcal P}m_{is}$, at most $\nu_Q$.
The finite sum of its numerator bounds is at most $CB_Q$.
Consequently the second exclusion radius is $C(B_Q\delta)^{1/\nu_Q}$.
The leading-coefficient and counting arguments are unchanged. This
proves \eqref{eq:flag-cluster} uniformly even if roots within a cluster
coalesce.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A noncommuting positive quotient and a Jordan intersection}
The class in Theorem~\ref{thm:flag-gauge} is strictly larger than the
simultaneously semisimple stochastic model. Let $\epsilon>0$ and put
\[
 C(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z^2+7z+10&\epsilon z\\0&z^2+7z+12\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad L=R=\frac13\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad K(z)=\tfrac12LC(z)R^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Its entry sum is $q(z)=z^2+(7+\epsilon/2)z+11$, so $K(T)/q(T)$ is a
strictly positive probability matrix for $T>0$. The leading coefficient
is $LR^{\mathsf T}/2$, and $K(z)K_2^{-1}=LC(z)L^{-1}$. Before this
similarity the coefficient of $z$ is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}7&\epsilon\\0&7\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
which is not diagonalizable, and its commutator with
$\diag(10,12)$ has nonzero upper entry $2\epsilon$. Thus these
coefficients neither commute nor admit simultaneous diagonalization.
The diagonal polynomials have disjoint root sets, so their greatest
common divisor, and that of the entries of $K$, is one.
Theorem~\ref{thm:rational-module} gives $\tau>0$ at five clocks.
All hypotheses are therefore realized by a genuine positive rational
observation, not merely by a formal matrix example. It is a broader
quotient experiment, not a new representation of \eqref{eq:model}.

The difference between the two exponents is already visible for
\[
 C(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z&\beta\\0&z\end{pmatrix},\qquad \beta>0.
\]
With the same $L,R$ and $K=LC R^{\mathsf T}/2$, the normalizer is
$q=z+\beta/2$ and the observations are strictly positive for $T>0$.
A normalization-only gauge changes the two diagonal polynomials by
the same scalar affine perturbation; its repeated root moves by
$O(\|r\|)$. In contrast, replacing the lower zero entry of $C$ by
$t>0$ produces roots $\pm\sqrt{\beta t}$. The corresponding normalizer
changes to $z+(\beta+t)/2$, and the observation error at fixed positive
clocks is $O(t)$. Here $m=1$ but the flag path has $\nu_Q=2$.
Normalization and the nilpotent collision consequently contribute
linearly and by a square root, respectively. This establishes the
mechanism behind the two-scale sum without claiming a classification
of arbitrary intersections with normalization-kernel or channel-rank
boundaries.
