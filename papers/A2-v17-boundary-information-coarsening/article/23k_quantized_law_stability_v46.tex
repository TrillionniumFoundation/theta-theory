\section{Quantitative reconstruction from quantized transverse laws}
\label{sec:v46-quantized-stability}

The exact inverse admits a conditional quantitative version in an observation
norm which does not involve derivatives of a measured density.  The observation
is a finite histogram of signed transverse endpoints, together with an onset
estimate for each selected channel.  Its resolution is allowed to increase as
the required geometric error decreases.  In particular, no longitudinal
endpoint coordinate is retained.  We first give a deterministic estimate
between two realizable tables, and then a finite-sample confidence construction.
The analytic continuation estimate is deliberately conservative.  Its constants
record, rather than suppress, the deterioration with the continuation distance
and the analytic width; compare \cite{Trefethen2020}.

\subsection{Quantified classes and observation distances}

Fix a selected graph $E=\mathcal T\cup\{f_1,f_2\}$ supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v45-clear-skeleton}, with $|E|=N+1$.  Its obstacle labels,
original deck labels, root incidence, tree-path labels $m_a$, and the invertible
integer gain matrix $M=(\eta_{f_1}\ \eta_{f_2})$ are fixed.  The marked
Euclidean realization $L$ is not supplied.  Fix a positive offset $d_0$ below the common relative-law collar of the
selected channels, and
nested squares
\[
 Q_0=[-r_0,r_0]^2\Subset Q_1=[-r_1,r_1]^2,
 \qquad 0<|a_*|<r_0<r_1,
\]
in the signed contact coordinates of each measured law.  The two laws of a
channel always refer to the two same-type even bridges.  There are
$\mathcal L=2N+2$ laws in total.

A \emph{quantified analytic class} $\mathcal K$ consists of realizable tables
with this persistent design and the following bounds.  All gaps, contact
curvatures and clearances lie in prescribed positive bounded ranges; the
lattice and representative placements are bounded after the fixed root gauge
is chosen.  Each support function in its contact-centered proper frame extends
$2\pi$-periodically and holomorphically to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-strip-bound}
 |\operatorname{Im}z|<s,\qquad |h(z)|\le B,
 \qquad B\ge1,
\end{equation}
with prescribed $s>0$.  The contact frames use the outward normal and its
positively oriented tangent.  Thus $h(0)=h'(0)=0$ in each individual
contact-centered frame; the placement of the second body in a channel also
uses its observed gap.  For each limiting density $f$ on $Q_1$, assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-forward-bounds}
 f\ge m_*>0,\qquad q_f\ge q_*>0,\qquad
 \|f\|_{C^k(Q_1)}\le H_k\quad(k\ge1),
\end{equation}
where $q_f$ is the scalar anchor of \eqref{eq:v26-ratio-anchor},
with the fixed nonzero coordinate $a_*$.
Only the finitely many $H_k$ used in a chosen finite-order estimate are needed.
These are a priori forward bounds, not observations of the amplitude function
and not bounds on an unknown inverse modulus.  They can equally be furnished
by quantitative contact bounds and the weighted forward estimates.

For each obstacle label $a$ fix a finite set $F_a\subset\{2,3,\ldots\}$ and
integers $b_{a,k}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-witness}
 \sum_{k\in F_a} k b_{a,k}=1,\qquad
 |z_k(K_a)|\ge\zeta_*>0\quad(k\in F_a).
\end{equation}
The Fourier convention is that of \eqref{eq:v45-general-registration};
coefficients of order at least two are translation invariant.  The actual coefficients and phases are unknown.  No harmonic truncation of an obstacle
is imposed.

Every properly asymmetric analytic table has such a finite witness, by the
greatest-common-divisor argument in
Proposition~\ref{prop:v45-generic-asymmetry}.  It also has a positive analytic
width.  After the width is decreased, sufficiently small neighborhoods in
the corresponding bounded holomorphic-support norm have the bounds above.
This assertion does not give a common analytic width on a $C^2$ neighborhood.
The qualitative open-dense theorem retains its original topology and does
not acquire these quantitative hypotheses.

For two labelled realizations put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-table-distance}
 D(T,\widetilde T)=\inf_{(U,t)\in\mathrm{SE}(2)}
 \left\{\|L-U\widetilde L\|+
       \max_a d_H(K_a,U\widetilde K_a+t)\right\}.
\end{equation}
Here representatives and deck marks are fixed; there is no independent
change of representatives inside the infimum.  On the prescribed bounded
class the same estimates control the difference of lattice Gram matrices.

Partition $Q_1$ into a rectangular grid of cells with side lengths at most
$\delta$ and include its complement as one further category.  Half-open
cells remove overlap, and cell boundaries have zero probability.  Write
$\mathbf p_\delta(P)$ for the resulting probability vector.  Define
\begin{align}
 e_g&=\max_{e\in E}|g_e-\widetilde g_e|,\nonumber\\
 e_p&=\max_{e,b}\|\mathbf p_\delta(P_{e,b})-
                   \mathbf p_\delta(\widetilde P_{e,b})\|_1,
       \label{eq:v46-observation-error}\\
 \eta&=e_p+C_{\rm bin}\delta,
 \qquad C_{\rm bin}=4H_1|Q_1|.\nonumber
\end{align}
All laws in this display are limiting laws at $d_0$.  The inclusion of the
complement category keeps the observation a probability vector; the proof
uses density regularity only inside $Q_1$.

\subsection{From cells to a finite support jet}

\begin{lemma}[Interior interpolation from histogram probabilities]
\label{lem:v46-cell-interpolation}
Let $f,\widetilde f$ satisfy \eqref{eq:v46-forward-bounds}.  For every
integer $m\ge0$ there is a finite constant $I_m$, depending only on
$Q_0,Q_1,H_{m+1}$ and a fixed polynomial kernel, such that, when $\eta\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-interpolation}
 \|f-\widetilde f\|_{C^m(Q_0)}\le I_m\eta^{1/(m+3)}.
\end{equation}
The constant is obtainable by differentiation and integration of finite
polynomials and by the displayed forward bounds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\bar f_\delta$ be the cell-average function on $Q_1$.  The mean value
inequality gives
$\|f-\bar f_\delta\|_{L^1(Q_1)}\le2H_1|Q_1|\delta$.
The $L^1$ difference of two cell-average functions is the sum of the absolute
differences of their cell masses.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-cell-l1}
 \|f-\widetilde f\|_{L^1(Q_1)}\le\eta.
\end{equation}
Set $w=f-\widetilde f$.  Use the product kernel
$K_m(x_1,x_2)=c_m(1-x_1^2)_+^{m+2}(1-x_2^2)_+^{m+2}$,
normalized to have integral one.  It is sufficiently differentiable for
all integrations by parts below; its relevant derivatives and normalization
are finite polynomial expressions and beta integrals.  For
$h\le h_0:=\min\{1,(r_1-r_0)/2\}$ and $|\alpha|\le m$,
\[
 \|\partial^\alpha(K_{m,h}*w)\|_{L^\infty(Q_0)}
 \le\|\partial^\alpha K_m\|_\infty h^{-2-|\alpha|}\eta.
\]
All convolution points are inside $Q_1$.  Integration by parts and the
mean value inequality also give
\[
 \|\partial^\alpha w-K_{m,h}*\partial^\alpha w\|_{L^\infty(Q_0)}
 \le C H_{m+1}h.
\]
Choose $h=h_0\eta^{1/(m+3)}$ and add the two estimates.  Since $h\le1$,
the worst power in the first estimate is $h^{-m-2}$, proving
\eqref{eq:v46-interpolation}.  When $\eta=0$, let $h$ decrease to zero.
The a priori derivative bound is essential: the assertion is not an
unconditional embedding of total variation into $C^m$.
\end{proof}

The finite-jet inverse in Section~\ref{sec:v27-signed-rigidity} has an
additional useful description.  It makes the constants used here finite
computations, without replacing the smooth finite-remainder proof by
formal algebra.

\begin{lemma}[A finite Bellman recipe for the jet constants]
\label{lem:v46-bellman}
For each $m\ge2$, the finite action-to-graph inverse and the conversion
from a graph jet to its contact-centered support jet are finite smooth
formulas with denominators bounded away from zero on the prescribed
positive geometric ranges.  Their two-point Lipschitz bounds are obtained
by a finite arithmetic and differentiation recursion.  In particular there
is a computable finite $A_m\ge1$ such that the support jets recovered from
two realizable sets of channel data obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-support-jet-error}
 \sum_{k=0}^m\frac{r^k}{k!}
       |h^{(k)}(0)-\widetilde h^{(k)}(0)|
 \le A_m\{e_g+\eta^{1/(m+3)}\},
 \qquad r=\min\{s/8,1/8\}.
\end{equation}
Every incidence is covered by the same constant after taking the maximum
over the finite design.  The constants are not asserted to be bounded
uniformly in $m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The four-density identities and the scalar positive anchor give a finite
$C^m$ action estimate from \eqref{eq:v46-interpolation}.  This can be a
two-point estimate on the whole quantified realizable class: compare
products, reciprocals and the positive scalar roots directly, using the
lower bounds at both endpoints.  An interpolating density need not itself
factorize.  The exact reconstructed actions have zero constant and linear
terms.

For completeness, the lower-order remainders in the graph recursion can
be computed as follows.  Let $V_b(u)$ be the first interior coordinate of
the stationary half-line beginning at $u$.  Uniqueness of the decaying
half-line and the absolutely convergent action yield the local identities
\begin{align}
 S_b(u)&=\ell_b(u,V_b(u))-g+S_{1-b}(V_b(u)),
       \label{eq:v46-bellman}\\
 0&=\partial_2\ell_b(u,V_b(u))+S_{1-b}'(V_b(u)).\nonumber
\end{align}
The derivative of the second equation with respect to $V_b$ at zero is
$g^{-1}+\kappa_{1-b}+a_{1-b}>0$, where $a_b=S_b''(0)$.
Its linear solution is
\[
 V_b'(0)=\alpha_b=\mathfrak r_b e^{-\gamma},
 \qquad \alpha_0\alpha_1=e^{-2\gamma}<1.
\]
Recover the quadratic geometry by \eqref{eq:v22-leading-recovery}.
Suppose all orders below $n\ge3$ have been found.  In the common transverse channel coordinates the local length used in
this computation is explicitly
\[
 \ell_b(u,v)=\sqrt{\bigl(g+\psi_b(u)+\psi_{1-b}(v)\bigr)^2+(v-u)^2}.
\]
Set the order-$n$ graph and action coefficients provisionally to zero.  Solve the second
identity in \eqref{eq:v46-bellman} through degree $n-2$ for the Taylor
polynomial of $V_b$.  This uses only the already determined coefficients,
and divides at each step by the positive scalar just displayed.  Inserting
that polynomial into the first identity gives a known order-$n$ coefficient
$P_{b,n}$, expressed in derivative rather than divided-power normalization.
The true order-$n$ coefficients satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-bellman-block}
 (I-B_n)s_n=(I+B_n)q_n+P_n,
 \qquad
 B_n=\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha_0^n\\\alpha_1^n&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the new graph terms in the local flight length are
$(q_{b,n}u^n+q_{1-b,n}v^n)/n!$, and the new terminal action term is
$s_{1-b,n}v^n/n!$.  They can be evaluated at $v=\alpha_bu$.
Omitting the unknown degree-$n-1$ term of $V_b$ changes the stationary
value only by $O(u^{2n-2})$, which is of higher order than $n$.
This last assertion follows by Taylor expansion around the stationary
point: the first derivative there vanishes and the displacement is
$O(u^{n-1})$.  The finite-jet factorization and smooth remainders proved
in Lemma~\ref{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization} justify using these Taylor
identities for the actual functions.

Both $I-B_n$ and $I+B_n$ have determinant $1-e^{-2n\gamma}$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-bellman-inverse}
 q_n=(I+B_n)^{-1}\{(I-B_n)s_n-P_n\}.
\end{equation}
In particular $(I-B_n)^{-1}(I+B_n)$ is precisely the matrix
$M_n$ of \eqref{eq:v22-general-jet-block}.  Formula
\eqref{eq:v46-bellman-inverse}, including the construction of $P_n$,
involves only finitely many truncated series operations.  It is not a
numerical truncation of an uncontrolled infinite orbit.

In an individual outward-normal frame the boundary is
$(-\psi(y),y)$.  Its normal angle satisfies $\psi'(y)=\tan\theta$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-graph-support}
 h(\theta)=-\psi(y(\theta))\cos\theta+y(\theta)\sin\theta.
\end{equation}
The implicit scalar derivative is $\psi''(0)=\kappa>0$.
For order $n$, solve for $y$ through degree $n-2$; the stationary-value
identity in \eqref{eq:v46-graph-support} shows that the support coefficient
through order $n$ depends on the graph coefficients only through order $n$.
For $n=2$ it is $h''(0)=1/\kappa$; for $n\ge3$ the same quadratic
remainder argument as above applies.  The channel coordinates use a common transverse axis.  On the side whose
outward normal is opposite to that of the first side, the positively oriented
tangent is also reversed.  Its individual graph coefficients are therefore
$(-1)^nq_{b,n}$ when that reversal is required.  These known proper changes
fix the signs before \eqref{eq:v46-graph-support} is applied; they introduce
no unknown inter-channel pose.

Here is an explicit bounding prescription for $A_m$.  Bound the input
Taylor coefficients by the supplied forward bounds and the contact bounds
following from \eqref{eq:v46-strip-bound} and positive curvature.  Apply
truncated product, reciprocal, square-root and implicit-series recursions
to the four-density formula, \eqref{eq:v22-leading-recovery},
\eqref{eq:v46-bellman-inverse}, and \eqref{eq:v46-graph-support}.
At each finite operation use the product difference inequality and, for a
reciprocal, $|x^{-1}-y^{-1}|\le |x-y|/c^2$ when $|x|,|y|\ge c$.
The scalar roots use their positive lower bounds.  The other denominators
are $g$, positive leading action curvatures, $\kappa$,
$g^{-1}+\kappa_{1-b}+a_{1-b}$ and $1-e^{-2n\gamma}$; their lower bounds
are fixed, with the last at least $1-e^{-6\gamma_-}$.
Elementary hyperbolic functions and their finitely many derivatives are
bounded on the prescribed leading-geometry intervals.  Summing the
resulting finite coefficient bounds with weights $r^k/k!$ and including
$I_m$ gives $A_m$.  No supremum over an unknown infinite-dimensional
inverse or a compactness separation constant enters this prescription.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A quantified continuation of the recovered support jet}

\begin{lemma}[Continuation from a finite anchored jet]
\label{lem:v46-continuation}
Suppose $h,\widetilde h$ satisfy \eqref{eq:v46-strip-bound}, and put
\[
 r=\min\{s/8,1/8\},\quad
 J_s=\lceil\pi/r\rceil+1,\quad \beta_s=2^{-J_s}.
\]
If $\sum_{k=0}^m r^k|h^{(k)}(0)-\widetilde h^{(k)}(0)|/k!\le u$,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-continuation-bound}
 \|h-\widetilde h\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
 \le (2B)^{1-\beta_s}
 \left[\min\left\{2B,u+\frac{8B}{3}4^{-m-1}\right\}\right]^{\beta_s}.
\end{equation}
For a fixed derivative order $k$ the right side, multiplied by
$k!(2/r)^k$, also bounds $\|(h-\widetilde h)^{(k)}\|_\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $F=h-\widetilde h$ and $H=2B$.  The disk of radius $4r$ about any
real point lies strictly inside the given strip.  Cauchy's coefficient
bound at zero gives on $|z|\le r$
\[
 |F(z)|\le u+H\sum_{j=m+1}^\infty4^{-j}
           =u+\frac{8B}{3}4^{-m-1}.
\]
It is also at most $H$.  If the bound on a disk of radius $r$ centered
on the real line is $v\le H$, the three-circles inequality at radii
$r,2r,4r$ bounds the middle disk by $\sqrt{Hv}$.
One may obtain this inequality directly by comparing the subharmonic
function $\log|F|$ on the annulus with the affine function of $\log|z|$
having boundary values $\log v$ and $\log H$; regularize zeros and let
the regularization decrease to zero.
A disk of radius $r$ whose center is shifted by at most $r$ is contained
in that middle disk.  Iterating along the real line therefore bounds the
$j$th disk by $H^{1-2^{-j}}v^{2^{-j}}$.  In each of the two real directions,
$J_s$ steps suffice to cover $[-\pi,\pi]$, including a disk of radius
$r/2$ about every point there.  Periodicity and Cauchy's derivative bound
on the smaller disks prove the assertions.  When $v=0$, use the same
argument with $v+\epsilon$ and let $\epsilon$ decrease to zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Registration with arbitrary finite asymmetry witnesses}

\begin{lemma}[B\'ezout registration and cochain stability]
\label{lem:v46-registration}
For two congruent properly oriented copies $C_s,C_t$ of obstacle type $a$,
set $p_k(C)=z_k(C)/|z_k(C)|$.  Under \eqref{eq:v46-witness} their unique
proper congruence has rotation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-bezout-phase}
 U_{s\to t}=\prod_{k\in F_a}
          \left(\frac{p_k(C_s)}{p_k(C_t)}\right)^{b_{a,k}}
\end{equation}
and translation $s(C_t)-U_{s\to t}s(C_s)$.
Consequently the registration and cochain formulas
\eqref{eq:v45-displacement-cochain}--\eqref{eq:v45-cochain-recovery}
are uniformly Lipschitz in the complete channel-image supports and gaps
on the quantified class.  Denote one such finite constant by $C_{\rm reg}$.
It depends on the design, its integer marks, $\|M^{-1}\|$, the prescribed
size bounds, $\zeta_*^{-1}$ and $\sum_{a,k}|b_{a,k}|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $C_t=e^{i\omega}C_s+t$, then
$p_k(C_t)=e^{-ik\omega}p_k(C_s)$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:v46-witness} makes the product in
\eqref{eq:v46-bezout-phase} equal to $e^{i\omega}$, without choosing a
root or an argument branch.  Centering supplies the displayed translation.
The same identity proves uniqueness.
For $|z|,|w|\ge\zeta_*$,
\[
 \left|\frac z{|z|}-\frac w{|w|}\right|
 \le\frac{2|z-w|}{\zeta_*}.
\]
Fourier coefficients and the first-harmonic center are bounded linear
functionals of a support function.  A product of unit complex factors has
difference bounded by the sum of the factor differences, with the factor
$|b|$ for a power $b\in\mathbb Z$.  This proves an explicit uniform
bound for each rotation and translation.  Propagate these bounds through
the finitely many tree incidences.  The displacement cochain, its two
cycle defects and multiplication by the fixed $M^{-1}$ are finite linear
operations with bounded coefficients.  The representative correction
$c_a=p_a-Lm_a$ uses the same recovered $L$ for all labels.
The difference of two support functions equals the Hausdorff distance
of their convex bodies in the uniform norm: the support inequalities
characterize containment in the corresponding parallel body.  Rotating
an approximate support introduces at most its bounded first derivative
times the rotation error, and this derivative is bounded by Cauchy's
estimate from \eqref{eq:v46-strip-bound}.  Adding the gap error for the
second contact placement completes the claimed table estimate.
Finally
$\|L^tL-\widetilde L^t\widetilde L\|
 \le(\|L\|+\|\widetilde L\|)\|L-\widetilde L\|$
in the common proper frame.
\end{proof}

The choices $F_a=\{2,3\}$ and $(b_{a,2},b_{a,3})=(-1,1)$ recover the
earlier harmonic formula.  For example $F_a=\{4,9\}$ admits
$(b_{a,4},b_{a,9})=(-2,1)$ and needs neither a second nor a third harmonic.
The formula applies to the whole properly asymmetric locus through its
local finite witnesses, not only to the explicit two-harmonic sublocus.

\subsection{The deterministic finite-resolution inverse}

Define, for $e_g\ge0$ and $0\le\eta\le1$,
\begin{align}
 W_m(e_g,\eta)&=\min\left\{2B,
 A_m(e_g+\eta^{1/(m+3)})+\frac{8B}{3}4^{-m-1}\right\},\nonumber\\
 \mathfrak W(e_g,\eta)&=\inf_{m\ge2}W_m(e_g,\eta),
       \label{eq:v46-modulus}\\
 \Omega(e_g,\eta)&=C_{\rm reg}\left\{e_g+
              (2B)^{1-\beta_s}\mathfrak W(e_g,\eta)^{\beta_s}\right\}.
              \nonumber
\end{align}
The constants may be enlarged to cover all channel incidences and the Gram
matrix.  Each fixed-$m$ expression is already a valid upper bound; computing
an infinite infimum is not required.

\begin{theorem}[Quantized-law stability of the complete periodic table]
\label{thm:v46-quantized-inverse}
Let $T,\widetilde T\in\mathcal K$ have the same selected design and satisfy
\eqref{eq:v46-observation-error}, with $\eta\le1$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-main-bound}
 D(T,\widetilde T)\le\Omega(e_g,\eta).
\end{equation}
The same bound, after the stated enlargement of $C_{\rm reg}$, controls
their marked lattice Gram matrices.  Moreover
$\Omega(e_g,\eta)\longrightarrow0$ as $(e_g,\eta)\longrightarrow(0,0)$.
Thus finitely many scalar cell probabilities at every prescribed resolution,
and finitely accurate gaps, determine the entire table to a prescribed
positive error.  This conclusion uses no longitudinal positions, supplied
support coefficients, amplitude functions or inter-channel registration.
It is not exact determination from a fixed finite list of scalar measurements.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:v46-cell-interpolation} converts the finite cell discrepancy
into a fixed-order interior density error.  The finite inverse and support
conversion of Lemma~\ref{lem:v46-bellman} give the anchored support-jet
bound \eqref{eq:v46-support-jet-error} for every incidence.  Lemma
\ref{lem:v46-continuation} yields the complete channel-image bound with
$W_m$ on its right.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v46-registration} puts these images
in one root frame and recovers the table and lattice with error at most the
corresponding fixed-$m$ expression in \eqref{eq:v46-modulus}.
Take the infimum over $m$.  For convergence, first choose a fixed $m$
so large that its geometric tail is small, and then decrease $e_g,\eta$
with this $m$ held fixed.  This order of choices uses no uniform bound
on $A_m$ as $m$ increases.
\end{proof}

The estimate gives a finite accuracy prescription, not merely a separation
constant obtained by compactness.  Given $\varepsilon>0$, set
\[
 v=\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}
       {2C_{\rm reg}(2B)^{1-\beta_s}}\right\}^{1/\beta_s}.
\]
Choose a finite $m\ge2$ with $(8B/3)4^{-m-1}\le v/2$, and require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-accuracy-prescription}
 e_g\le\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{2C_{\rm reg}},
                          \frac v{4A_m}\right\},\qquad
 \eta\le\min\left\{1,\left(\frac v{4A_m}\right)^{m+3}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Then \eqref{eq:v46-main-bound} is at most $\varepsilon$.
Taking $C_{\rm bin}\delta\le\eta/2$ and $e_p\le\eta/2$ specifies a
finite grid and accuracy for its cell probabilities.  The prescription is
potentially very ill-conditioned.  No sharp H\"older exponent, analytic
minimax rate, or uniform Lipschitz law-to-boundary inverse is asserted.

\subsection{Finite samples, finite flights, and charged acquisition}

\begin{corollary}[Histogram confidence reconstruction]
\label{cor:v46-confidence}
Suppose $n$ independent successful marks are available for each of the
$\mathcal L$ calibrated same-type channels at a fixed positive offset, and
retain only their grid categories.  Let the grid, including the complement,
have $K_\delta$ categories.  If the marks have even-flight laws $P_{j,e,b}$
satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-finite-law-bias}
 \|P_{j,e,b}-P_{e,b}\|_{\rm TV}\le C_0\tau^j,
 \qquad 0<\tau<1,
\end{equation}
define
\[
 u_n=\sqrt{K_\delta/n}+
              \sqrt{2\log(\mathcal L/\alpha)/n},
 \qquad b_j=2C_0\tau^j.
\]
On the event that supplied gap estimates have error at most $v_g$, form the
set of candidate tables in $\mathcal K$ whose gaps are within $v_g$ of
those estimates and whose limiting histogram vectors are within $u_n+b_j$
of the empirical vectors in $\ell^1$.  With conditional-law sampling
probability at least $1-\alpha$, the true table belongs to this set, and
every candidate $\widetilde T$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-confidence-radius}
 D(T,\widetilde T)\le
 \Omega\bigl(2v_g,\,2(u_n+b_j)+C_{\rm bin}\delta\bigr)
\end{equation}
whenever the second argument is at most one.  If the gap event has failure
probability $\alpha_g$, the joint failure bound is $\alpha+\alpha_g$;
no independence from that event is needed when the sampling bound is uniform.
For a compact metrizable $\mathcal K$ on which the finite observation
vector is continuous, a measurable approximate minimum-excess-discrepancy
selection can be used instead, with its positive tolerance added to each
of the two arguments.  Neither compactness nor selection is used to define
the modulus \eqref{eq:v46-modulus}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a categorical law with vector $p$, the empirical vector satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widehat p-p\|_1
 \le\sum_{k=1}^{K_\delta}\sqrt{p_k(1-p_k)/n}
 \le\sqrt{K_\delta/n}.
\]
Changing one mark changes this norm by at most $2/n$.  Exposing the marks
successively and applying the elementary bounded-interval exponential
moment inequality to the resulting martingale differences gives
\[
 \Pr\{\|\widehat p-p\|_1>
        \sqrt{K_\delta/n}+t\}\le e^{-nt^2/2}.
\]
Here the moment inequality for a centered variable of range length $c$ is
$\mathbb E e^{\lambda X}\le e^{\lambda^2c^2/8}$; it follows by convexity
of the exponential and maximization over the endpoint Bernoulli mean.
A union bound proves the assertion simultaneously for all $\mathcal L$
laws.  Total variation contracts under the grid map, and the $\ell^1$
difference of probability vectors is twice their total variation.  Thus
\eqref{eq:v46-finite-law-bias} contributes at most $b_j$.
The true limiting vectors are within $u_n+b_j$ of their empirical vectors.
Every other candidate in the confidence set is therefore within
$2(u_n+b_j)$ of the true limiting vectors, and its gaps are within $2v_g$
of the true gaps.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v46-quantized-inverse}.

For the selection assertion, use a fixed countable dense sequence of the
compact metrizable candidate class.  Define the nonnegative excess residual
\[
 R(T)=\max\left\{0,\ \max_e|g_e(T)-\widehat g_e|-v_g,\
 \max_{e,b}\|\mathbf p_\delta(P_{e,b}(T))-\widehat p_{e,b}\|_1
                  -(u_n+b_j)\right\}.
\]
On the coverage event $R(T_{\rm true})=0$.  Continuity makes the infimum
of $R$ over the dense sequence zero as well.  The first element whose
residual is within a prescribed positive tolerance $\epsilon_{\rm sel}$
of the countable infimum is measurable.  Each of its individual gap and
histogram constraints is violated by at most $\epsilon_{\rm sel}$, proving
the stated addition to each argument.  On a compact holomorphic-support
subfamily of the persistent design, the uniform limiting density construction
gives the required total-variation continuity, including its moving boundary;
cell probabilities and the gap coordinates are therefore continuous.
This is an existence construction of a selector, not a claim that searching
an arbitrary analytic model class is computationally efficient.
\end{proof}

At fixed $d_0$, the uniform relative-law theorem and the normalization argument
in Corollary~\ref{cor:v26-finite-flight-inverse} give
\eqref{eq:v46-finite-law-bias}.  Thus the finite-resolution prescription
\eqref{eq:v46-accuracy-prescription} permits a finite even $j$, a finite
grid and a finite $n$.  Taking $j$ larger preserves the bias bound and allows
the minimum bridge length to diverge along any sequence of target accuracies.
Only a quantitative $C^0$ forward bias bound is needed here; a
flight-number estimate uniform over all derivative orders is not used.

There is also a direct charged version for an already calibrated design.
Suppose independent preparations have success probability at least
$p_*(j)>0$ for every selected law.  Run at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-cap}
 T_j=\left\lceil\frac{2n+8\log(\mathcal L/\alpha_c)}{p_*(j)}\right\rceil
\end{equation}
preparations per law, retaining the first $n$ successful grid categories
when possible.  The binomial lower-tail bound makes the probability of
any cap failure at most $\alpha_c$: the expected success count is at least
$2n+8\log(\mathcal L/\alpha_c)$ and a failure lies below half this mean.
The total charge is at most $\mathcal L T_j$.  Apply the preceding
concentration argument to the uncapped iid stream of successful categories,
then add the cap-failure event.  Conditioning on cap completion is neither
needed nor asserted to preserve an iid law.  On a uniform fixed-offset
bridge class one may use its forward lower bound
$p_*(j)=c_*e^{-\Gamma_*j}$.

The calibrated signed charts, selected labels and onset estimates remain
