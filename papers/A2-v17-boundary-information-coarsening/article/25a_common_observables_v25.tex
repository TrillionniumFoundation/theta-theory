\section{Common physical observations and charged channel calibration}
\label{sec:v25-common-observables}

A channel coordinate is not an observation until its origin and direction
have been supplied or estimated.  We construct the latter from the physical
records themselves.  The construction uses only even, same-type alternating
bridges; their flight numbers may be required to exceed any prescribed
integer.  In particular, no mixed-type one-flight experiment is used.

\subsection{The acquisition space}

Fix the marked channel labels \(e=(a_e,b_e,\ell_e)\) and their transverse
sign conventions.  A channel detector reports positions in one oriented
Euclidean sensor frame for that channel.  The same frame is used for both
endpoint types of the channel, but the rigid transformations relating
\emph{different} channel frames are neither supplied nor observed.  A sensor
reports both components of a collision position in physical length units.
This is the endpoint-position observation, before taking its transverse
projection.  Unit speed and the physical clock fix the length unit.  The
marked obstacle and deck labels are acquisition labels, not supplied deck
vectors.  As in the physical preparation model, each preparation is an
independent draw from normalized billiard phase volume.  Its distribution
may depend on the table; the instructions for taking and recording a
preparation do not.

The design and one-preparation record spaces are the standard Borel spaces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-design-record-spaces}
 \mathscr A=\{(e,b,j,t):e\in E,\ b\in\{0,1\},\ j\ge2
       \text{ even},\ t>0\},\qquad
 \mathscr X=\{\partial\}\sqcup(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2
                                      \times[0,\infty)).
\end{equation}
For a design \(a=(e,b,j,t)\), monitor the marked collision word up to the
programmed time \(t\).  If it is the specified alternating word of
\(j+1\) impacts beginning at type \(b\), record
\((X,Y,r)\), where \(X,Y\) are the first and last positions in that
channel's sensor frame and \(r\) is the time from preparation to the first
impact.  Otherwise record \(\partial\).  This is a single measurable map
of the marked timed trajectory, defined by labels, comparisons with \(t\),
and coordinate readings.  It contains no contact point, tangent, gap or
unknown boundary graph.  All failures, designs and stopping decisions are
retained in the transcript.  Extra trajectory information used by the
apparatus to select the word is then discarded.

The selected labels refer to the facing lifted obstacle pair, not to a
parameter-dependent spatial gate.  If detector gates are used, they are
fixed before observation and contain the persistent contact neighborhoods
for the declared class.  Near onset, the localization below makes the two
implementations agree.  Discovery of unlabelled channels is a different
observation problem and is not assumed as part of this record map.

Let \(\mathfrak K\) be the compact marked analytic class of
Section~\ref{sec:v25-signature-stability}.  In this section only its uniform
positive curvature, gap, area, separation and persistent-channel bounds are
needed.  Write \(p_{e,0},p_{e,1}\) for the two contact positions in the
channel sensor frame, and put
\[
 n_e=(p_{e,1}-p_{e,0})/g_e,\qquad t_e=R_{\pi/2}n_e.
\]
The prescribed sign convention fixes the possible simultaneous sign change
of the transverse axes.  These quantities are used in the analysis, not in
the acquisition instructions.  Bounds below are invariant under arbitrary
independent rigid motions of the channel sensor frames.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform physical localization and success mass]
\label{lem:v25-physical-localization}
There are \(h_0,c_*,C_*,\gamma_+>0\), uniform on \(\mathfrak K\), such
that a selected \(j\)-flight record is impossible for \(t\le jg_e\).
If \(0<t-jg_e\le2h\le2h_0\), each recorded endpoint of type \(b\)
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-endpoint-localization}
                 |X-p_{e,b}|+|Y-p_{e,b}|\le C_*\sqrt h.
\end{equation}
For every even \(j\ge2\) and \(h\le t-jg_e\le2h\le2h_0\), the
selected-record probability is at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-pilot-mass}
                   p_*(j,h):=c_*h^2e^{-j\gamma_+}>0.
\end{equation}
The constants can be decreased so that \(p_*(j,h)<1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every flight between the specified two lifted obstacles has length at least
\(g_e\).  Their unique closest pair is an interior nondegenerate minimum
of the two-endpoint distance.  In contact arclength coordinates its Hessian
is positive definite, uniformly on the compact class.  Taylor's formula
therefore gives
\[
 \ell_e(s,s')-g_e\ge c(s^2+(s')^2)
\]
on a common neighborhood.  On its complement a positive uniform distance
margin follows from uniqueness, channel persistence and compactness.  Thus
if the sum of the \(j\) flight lengths is less than \(jg_e+2h\), for
\(h\) sufficiently small every flight is in that neighborhood.  Applying
the displayed bound to the first and last flights proves
\eqref{eq:v25-endpoint-localization}; the constant does not grow with \(j\).
Nonnegative entrance and exit times prove the assertion before onset.

For the lower mass use the exact flux density in
\eqref{eq:v6-finite-experiment}, in unscaled coordinates.  Uniformly on the
contact box, \(E_j(u,v)\le C(u^2+v^2)\), \(b_j\ge c>0\), and
\(-W_{j,uv}(0,0)\ge c e^{-j\gamma_+}\).  Restrict \((u,v)\) to a ball
of radius \(c\sqrt h\) and \(r\) to an interval of length \(ch\), so
that \(r+E_j<h\).  Integrating gives \(ch^2e^{-j\gamma_+}/A\).
The uniform upper bound on the free area gives
\eqref{eq:v25-pilot-mass}.  These bounds follow from the finite bridge and
relative-law estimates with strict uniform curvature and hyperbolicity
margins; they require no finite-dimensional parametrization of
\(\mathfrak K\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite capped scan}

Choose known bounds \(0<g_-\le g_e\le g_+\) for the class.  Fix an even
flight number \(j\), a resolution \(h<h_0\), and an error probability
\(\beta\in(0,1)\).  All of these are chosen before the pilot.  Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-pilot-grid}
 t_l=jg_-+lh,\quad 0\le l\le L,
 \qquad L=\left\lceil j(g_+-g_-)/h\right\rceil+2,
 \qquad
 N=\left\lceil p_*(j,h)^{-1}\log(2|E|/\beta)\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
For each \((e,b)\), scan these times in increasing order, taking at most
\(N\) independent preparations at each time, and stop at the first
successful record.  Denote its time by \(T_{e,b}\) and its first endpoint
by \(\widehat p_{e,b}\).  If a scan exhausts the grid, report pilot failure.
The total preparation cap is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-pilot-cap}
                          B_{\rm pil}=2|E|(L+1)N.
\end{equation}
No intermediate failed preparation is omitted from this count.

\begin{theorem}[Observable contact-frame and onset calibration]
\label{thm:v25-observable-calibration}
\label{lem:v24-global-centering-pilot}
The preceding policy has, with probability at least \(1-\beta\) uniformly
on \(\mathfrak K\), a successful outcome in every scan and
\[
             jg_e<T_{e,b}\le jg_e+2h.
\]
On this event define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-pilot-estimators}
 \widehat g_e=\frac{\min_bT_{e,b}-h}{j},\qquad
 \widehat n_e=\frac{\widehat p_{e,1}-\widehat p_{e,0}}
                   {|\widehat p_{e,1}-\widehat p_{e,0}|},\qquad
 \widehat t_e=R_{\pi/2}\widehat n_e.
\end{equation}
For all sufficiently small \(h\), the denominator is at least \(g_-/2\)
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-pilot-error}
 \max_e j|\widehat g_e-g_e|\le h,\qquad
 \max_{e,b}|\widehat p_{e,b}-p_{e,b}|
       +\max_e|\widehat t_e-t_e|\le C\sqrt h.
\end{equation}
The policy and estimates are measurable functions of the transcript on
\eqref{eq:v25-design-record-spaces}.  They require no supplied contact
center, tangent, curvature, gap or inter-channel placement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each \((e,b)\) there is a grid point whose true excess is in
\([h,2h]\).  At that point the probability of \(N\) failures is at most
\((1-p_*)^N\le\exp(-Np_*)\le\beta/(2|E|)\).  If the scan has stopped
earlier, its first success cannot precede onset and its time is even smaller.
A union bound proves the claimed simultaneous event.  In particular, this
argument does not require a lower success bound at times far beyond the
near-onset collar, nor a decision that a finite run of failures proves zero
probability.

The gap bound follows by subtracting the midpoint \(h\) from a number in
\((0,2h]\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:v25-physical-localization} gives the two
position bounds.  For vectors \(v,w\) with \(|v|\ge g_-\) and
\(|v-w|\le g_-/2\),
\[
 \left|\frac v{|v|}-\frac w{|w|}\right|
                         \le 4|v-w|/g_-.
\]
Apply this with the true and estimated contact displacements.  Each operation
in \eqref{eq:v25-pilot-estimators} is Borel on the good event.  On a failed
scan or a small denominator assign a fixed default value and a failure flag.
This defines the policy and estimators on every transcript.  Rigidly moving
a channel sensor moves both estimated contact positions by the same rigid
motion and rotates the estimated tangent.  Hence the bounds are uniform in
these unsupplied sensor placements.
\end{proof}

The pilot flight number may be any prescribed even \(j\), however large.
Later we set it equal to the already chosen post-pilot flight number \(J\).
The pilot therefore controls \(J|\widehat g_e-g_e|\), not a different
quantity at an earlier, smaller flight number.  Both types are measured in
the same channel sensor frame; this does not register different channels.

\subsection{Observable normalization and continuity of the tested laws}

For a fresh successful same-type record set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-observable-normalization}
 \widehat C_{e,b}(X,Y,r)=
 \bigl(\widehat t_e\cdot(X-\widehat p_{e,b}),
       \widehat t_e\cdot(Y-\widehat p_{e,b}),r\bigr).
\end{equation}
Its ideal counterpart \(C_{T,e,b}\) replaces the estimates by the true
contact frame.  On the pilot event and a common positive-offset collar,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-coordinate-error}
             |\widehat C_{e,b}(X,Y,r)-C_{T,e,b}(X,Y,r)|
                                                   \le C\sqrt h.
\end{equation}
Indeed the projected displacement error is bounded by the position error
plus the tangent error times \(|X-p_{e,b}|\), which is uniformly bounded
on the selected collar.  No bound on the absolute sensor origin is needed.

Let \(Q_{T,e,b,d}^{\infty}\) be the centered successful endpoint--time law,
with density, extended by zero to a fixed box,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-centered-density}
 q_{T,e,b,d}(u,v,r)=Z_{T,e,b,d}^{-1}B_{e,b}(u)B_{e,b}(v)
 \mathbf1_{\{0<r<d-S_{e,b}(u)-S_{e,b}(v)\}}.
\end{equation}
For any compact positive-offset interval inside the common collar,
\(Z\) is bounded below by a positive constant.  If \(S,B,d\) and
\(\widetilde S,\widetilde B,\widetilde d\) are two such descriptions,
fiber integration and \(|x_+-y_+|\le|x-y|\) give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-centered-TV-continuity}
 \|Q-\widetilde Q\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C\bigl(\|S-\widetilde S\|_\infty+
             \|B-\widetilde B\|_\infty+|d-\widetilde d|\bigr).
\end{equation}
To verify this estimate, integrate the amplitude difference on the common
fibers and the uniformly bounded amplitudes on their symmetric difference;
the latter length is bounded by the action and offset differences.  The same
bound applies to the normalizers, after which division by their positive
lower bound proves the assertion.  The half-line contraction and relative
operator estimates imply continuous dependence of \(S,B\) on the table in
this fixed collar.  Thus the centered laws are continuous in total variation.
This is not a claim of total-variation continuity for ambient position laws
supported on different boundary curves.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform implementation of a finite family of tests]
\label{prop:v25-test-implementation}
Let \((e_r,b_r,d_r,\phi_r)_{r=1}^R\) be a fixed finite family with positive
offsets in the interior of the collar and bounded Lipschitz functions
\(|\phi_r|\le1\).  Program the post-pilot time
\(J\widehat g_{e_r}+d_r\), where \(J\) is chosen before a pilot with
\(j=J\).  Conditional on any good pilot history, a fresh successful record
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-test-bias}
 \left|\mathbb E_T\phi_r(\widehat C_{e_r,b_r}(X,Y,r))
              -Q_{T,e_r,b_r,d_r}^{\infty}\phi_r\right|
       \le C_{\mathscr F}(\sqrt h+h+\tau^J).
\end{equation}
The actual statistic and the programmed design are parameter independent
functions of the transcript.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the good history the true offset differs from \(d_r\) by at most \(h\).
Choose \(h\) below half the smallest offset and below the upper collar
margin.  The conditional physical-to-boundary comparison
\eqref{eq:v6-tv} costs at most \(2C\tau^J\) for a test bounded by one.
The same boundary-position embedding is used for both measures at the fixed
true table.  Applying the \emph{observable} map
\eqref{eq:v25-observable-normalization} cannot increase that distance.
Replacing this map by its ideal counterpart costs at most the Lipschitz
constant times \(C\sqrt h\).  Finally
\eqref{eq:v25-centered-TV-continuity} bounds the offset error by \(Ch\).
Summing proves \eqref{eq:v25-test-bias}.  The ideal map appears only in this
error calculation.  It is not a kernel that the experimenter must apply.
\end{proof}
