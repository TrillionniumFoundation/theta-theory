\subsection{Uniform control of the highest-degree interaction}

The matrices in \eqref{eq:v22-general-jet-block} are not arbitrary
members of a determinant-one family.  Their two shape factors come from
positive contact curvatures.  This admissibility gives the following
order-uniform bounds, observed in~\cite{A2ReviewV57}.  We also record
their consequences for actual fixed-lower-jet comparisons and for the
block-diagonal operator on Taylor coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform admissible blocks]
\label{prop:v58-uniform-blocks}
Let $g,\kappa_0,\kappa_1>0$, $c_b=1+g\kappa_b$,
$c=\sqrt{c_0c_1}=\cosh\gamma$ and
$\mathfrak r_b=\sqrt{c_b/c_{1-b}}$.  Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v58-block-constants}
 t=e^{-\gamma},\qquad \vartheta=\frac{1+t^2}{2}<1,\qquad
 C_\gamma=\frac{3+t^6}{1-t^6},\qquad K_\gamma=\frac4{1-t^6}.
\end{equation}
For the maximum absolute row-sum norm and every integer $n\ge3$,
\begin{align}
 \max\{\|M_n\|_\infty,\|M_n^{-1}\|_\infty\}&\le C_\gamma,
                                      \label{eq:v58-uniform-block-bound}\\
 \max\{\|M_n-I\|_\infty,\|M_n^{-1}-I\|_\infty\}
                                  &\le K_\gamma\vartheta^n.
                                      \label{eq:v58-identity-decay}
\end{align}
The constants and the exponential rate can be chosen uniformly whenever
$\gamma\ge\gamma_*>0$, without a further upper bound on the curvature
ratio.

If two actual smooth anchored graph pairs have this gap and equal graph
jets through order $n-1$, then their degree-$n$ graph and action differences
satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v58-actual-top-degree}
 C_\gamma^{-1}\|\Delta q_n\|_\infty
       \le \|\Delta s_n\|_\infty
       \le C_\gamma\|\Delta q_n\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
Here $C_\gamma$ is independent of $n$; the functional smooth bounds needed
to define and differentiate each action are still imposed at the requested
finite order.

For $R>0$, define the coefficient space
\[
 \mathcal A_R=\left\{x=(x_n)_{n\ge3},\ x_n\in\mathbb C^2:
       \|x\|_R=\sum_{n\ge3}\frac{R^n}{n!}\|x_n\|_\infty<\infty\right\}.
\]
The block-diagonal operator $(\mathcal D x)_n=M_nx_n$ is a bounded
isomorphism of $\mathcal A_R$, with both operator norms at most
$C_\gamma$.  Its corrections to the identity satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v58-coefficient-radius}
 \| (\mathcal D-I)x\|_{R/\vartheta}
 +\| (\mathcal D^{-1}-I)x\|_{R/\vartheta}
                            \le 2K_\gamma\|x\|_R.
\end{equation}
These statements concern the block-diagonal part, not the complete
nonlinear graph-to-action map.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $c_{1-b}>1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v58-admissible-contraction}
 \mathfrak r_b=\frac{c}{c_{1-b}}<c,
 \qquad \lambda_b:=\mathfrak r_bt<ct=\vartheta<1,
 \qquad \lambda_0\lambda_1=t^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the linear visit to the opposite contact has contraction factor
$\lambda_b$, even when $\mathfrak r_b>1$.  Summing the endpoint and
interior visits as in \eqref{eq:v22-own-sum}--\eqref{eq:v22-cross-sum}
gives, with the sign distinguishing the matrix from its inverse,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v58-explicit-signed-blocks}
 M_n^{\pm1}=\frac1{1-t^{2n}}
       \begin{pmatrix}1+t^{2n}&\pm2\lambda_0^n\\
                       \pm2\lambda_1^n&1+t^{2n}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The inverse formula uses $\lambda_0\lambda_1=t^2$ and determinant one.
Both matrices have the same absolute row sums.  Since $n\ge3$,
\[
 \|M_n^{\pm1}\|_\infty
 \le \frac{1+t^{2n}+2\vartheta^n}{1-t^{2n}}
 \le \frac{3+t^6}{1-t^6}.
\]
After subtracting the identity the diagonal numerator is $2t^{2n}$.
As $t^2<\vartheta$, the row sums are at most
$4\vartheta^n/(1-t^6)$.  This proves both bounds.  If
$\gamma\ge\gamma_*$, replace $t$ by $e^{-\gamma_*}$ in the upper
bounds and in $\vartheta$; all resulting constants are uniform.

For actual smooth pairs, Lemma~\ref{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization}
first makes each finite action jet a function of the corresponding
finite graph jet.  Proposition~\ref{prop:v22-last-jet-block} then gives
$\Delta s_n=M_n\Delta q_n$, since the common lower jets fix $R_n$.
Applying \eqref{eq:v58-uniform-block-bound} in the two directions proves
\eqref{eq:v58-actual-top-degree}.  No formal-series argument replaces
this smooth factorization.

For the coefficient-space assertions, multiply each of the block bounds
by $R^n/n!$ and sum.  The inverse is defined coefficient by coefficient
and belongs to the same space by the same bound, so it is an actual
bounded inverse on $\mathcal A_R$.  Multiplying
\eqref{eq:v58-identity-decay} by $(R/\vartheta)^n/n!$ proves
\eqref{eq:v58-coefficient-radius}.  In particular, truncating either
correction after degree $N$ makes an operator-norm error on
$\mathcal A_R$ of at most $K_\gamma\vartheta^{N+1}$.  Only absolutely
convergent coefficient sums have been used.
\end{proof}

The location of the remaining conditioning problem is consequently
precise.  With the leading geometry fixed, the derivative of the full
finite triangular map on degrees $3,\ldots,M$, $M\ge3$, has the form
$L_M=D_M+N_M$, where $D_M=\operatorname{diag}(M_3,\ldots,M_M)$ and
$N_M$ is strictly block lower triangular.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v58-triangular-factorization}
 L_M^{-1}=\left[\sum_{k=0}^{M-3}(-D_M^{-1}N_M)^k\right]D_M^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The diagonal inverse is bounded independently of $M$, but this assertion
alone does not bound the displayed sum.  It does not control the
lower-jet remainders and their derivatives, the finite smooth remainder
constants, the density-to-jet operation, or continuation away from the
contact.  The coefficient-radius gain in
\eqref{eq:v58-coefficient-radius} therefore belongs to the diagonal
correction only.  The full inverse and finite-flight reconstruction
retain their stated fixed-order hypotheses; no unrestricted all-order
noisy inverse is being inferred.
