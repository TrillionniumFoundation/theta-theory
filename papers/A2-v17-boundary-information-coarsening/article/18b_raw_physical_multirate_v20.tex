\section{A raw physical local experiment on fixed laboratory windows}
\label{sec:v20-raw-physical}

The purpose of this section is to place the boundary-information theorem on an
observation space and an acquisition rule which do not depend on the unknown
local alternative.  The distinction is essential.  A fixed excess coordinate
$d=t-jg$ is convenient for the forward geometry, but if $g$ is unknown then
holding $d$ fixed changes the physical time window with the parameter.  We
therefore keep physical time fixed and allow the gap direction to acquire its
natural faster local scale.

\subsection{Registered local geometry and the raw acquisition rule}

We work with a \emph{registered labelled channel}.  The two participating
contact labels and the common signed transverse coordinate used in
Section~\ref{sec:geometric-setup} are fixed once and for all.  Equivalently,
the Euclidean pose of the local channel and the label exchange are calibration
variables, not coordinates in the statistical parameter below.  The unknown
geometry consists of the gap and the contact geometry in these fixed frames.
This is exactly the fixed-frame hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-signed-rigidity}; no parameter-dependent re-centering of
the observed endpoints is used in the experiment below.

Let $\vartheta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb R^r$ parameterize a smooth
finite-dimensional family of registered contact geometries and let $g>0$ be
the gap coordinate.  The base geometry is $(g_0,0)$.  Write
$S_{b,g,\vartheta}$ and $B_{b,g,\vartheta}$ for the two half-line actions and
positive amplitudes.  All derivatives below are taken in this fixed registered
chart.  Set
\[
 \dot S_b(u)h=D_\vartheta S_{b,g_0,\vartheta}(u)|_{\vartheta=0}[h].
\]
As always, $S_{b,g,\vartheta}(0)=S'_{b,g,\vartheta}(0)=0$.

Fix finitely many pairs $(b_\ell,d_\ell)$ with
$b_\ell\in\{0,1\}$ and $0<d_\ell<d_*$, $1\le\ell\le L$.  At flight number
$j$ the $\ell$th physical acquisition window is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-fixed-physical-window}
                 t_{j,\ell}=j g_0+d_\ell.
\end{equation}
This time is programmed from the specified reference geometry and is the same
under every local alternative.  A fresh raw preparation returns either a
failure symbol or the selected physical endpoint record in the fixed signed
coordinates.  A batch stops when a preassigned number of successful endpoint
records has been obtained.  The stopping rule, the endpoint coordinate system
and the physical times in \eqref{eq:v20-fixed-physical-window} are therefore
parameter independent.

\subsection{The gap has a different local scale}

Let $j_n\to\infty$ and $\delta_n\downarrow0$.  For bounded
$(a,h)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^r$ define the local alternatives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-multirate-parameter}
 g_n(a)=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a,
 \qquad
 \vartheta_n(h)=\delta_n h.
\end{equation}
The excess of the fixed physical window is then exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-raw-excess}
 t_{j_n,\ell}-j_ng_n(a)=d_\ell-\delta_n a.
\end{equation}
Thus a gap perturbation of order $\delta_n/j_n$ produces an order-$\delta_n$
normal displacement of the support.  This is the raw physical source of the
faster gap scale; no onset value is supplied to the acquisition rule.

For the limiting same-type endpoint law at design $(b,d)$ put
\[
 w_{b,d}(u,v)=d-S_{b,g_0,0}(u)-S_{b,g_0,0}(v)
\]
and let $A_{b,d}(u,v)>0$ denote the normalized smooth amplitude, so that the
base conditional density is
\[
 f_{b,d}(u,v)=A_{b,d}(u,v)(w_{b,d}(u,v))_+.
\]
For $z=(a,h)$ define the support-velocity functional
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-raw-support-velocity}
 U_b(u,v)z
 =a+\dot S_b(u)h+\dot S_b(v)h.
\end{equation}
The sign is immaterial for the information matrix.  The associated matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-raw-information}
 \mathcal K_{b,d}
 =\int_{\{S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d\}}
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)\,U_b(u,v)^tU_b(u,v)}
      {|\nabla(S_b\oplus S_b)(u,v)|}\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}
Here $U_b(u,v)$ is viewed as the row vector representing
\eqref{eq:v20-raw-support-velocity} on
$\mathbb R^{1+r}$.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-window reduction to the boundary family]
\label{lem:v20-fixed-window-reduction}
Uniformly for $z=(a,h)$ in compact sets, the limiting endpoint density at the
fixed physical window \eqref{eq:v20-fixed-physical-window} has defining
function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-defining-expansion}
 w_{n,z}(u,v)
 =w_{b,d}(u,v)-\delta_nU_b(u,v)z+o(\delta_n)
\end{equation}
in $C^2$ on the common endpoint box.  The corresponding amplitudes are
uniformly $C^2$ and differ from the base amplitude by $O(\delta_n)$.  The
direct effect of the gap perturbation on the half-line actions is
$o(\delta_n)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:v20-raw-excess} supplies the term $-\delta_na$.
Smooth parameter dependence of the half-line actions gives
\[
 S_{b,g_n(a),\vartheta_n(h)}
 =S_{b,g_0,0}+\delta_n\dot S_bh
   +\frac{\delta_n a}{j_n}\,\partial_gS_b+o(\delta_n)
\]
uniformly with two endpoint derivatives and uniformly on compact $z$-sets.
Since $j_n\to\infty$, the displayed $g$-derivative term is
$o(\delta_n)$.  The same smooth dependence applies to the positive
amplitude and its normalizer.  Substitution proves
\eqref{eq:v20-defining-expansion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite positive-offset design]
\label{lem:v20-finite-positive-design}
Assume the finite-dimensional family is a local contact-jet family on which
$(g,\vartheta)$ are coordinates in the registered channel.  There are finitely
many same-type designs $(b_\ell,d_\ell)$ and positive weights
$\alpha_\ell$, $\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell=1$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-design-info}
 \mathcal K_{\rm design}
 =\sum_{\ell=1}^L\alpha_\ell\mathcal K_{b_\ell,d_\ell}
\end{equation}
is positive definite on $\mathbb R^{1+r}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $z=(a,h)$ belongs to the kernel of
$\mathcal K_{b,d}$ for every $b\in\{0,1\}$ and every sufficiently small
$d>0$.  Positivity of the amplitude in
\eqref{eq:v20-raw-information} implies, by continuity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-kernel-level}
 a+\dot S_b(u)h+\dot S_b(v)h=0
 \quad\text{whenever}\quad S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d.
\end{equation}
Take $v=0$ and $d=S_b(u)$.  Since $\dot S_b(0)h=0$,
\[
 a+\dot S_b(u)h=0
\]
for all sufficiently small nonzero $u$.  Letting $u\to0$ gives $a=0$,
and then $\dot S_b(u)h=0$ for both labels throughout a contact
neighborhood.  At fixed $g_0$, the leading formulas and the determinant-one
jet recursion in Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-signed-rigidity} imply successively that
every labelled graph-jet component of $h$ vanishes.  Hence $h=0$.

Thus every nonzero unit vector $z$ is detected by at least one pair $(b,d)$.
Strict positivity of $z^t\mathcal K_{b,d}z$ is open in $z$.  The unit sphere
is compact, so finitely many pairs suffice to cover it.  Any positive convex
combination of the corresponding matrices is then positive definite.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-window multirate endpoint LAN]
\label{thm:v20-fixed-window-lan}
Choose the finite design of Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-finite-positive-design}.  For
each $n$ let $k_{n,\ell}$ be the prescribed number of successful endpoint
records at design $\ell$, put $k_n=\sum_\ell k_{n,\ell}$, and assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-batch-rate}
 \frac{k_{n,\ell}}{k_n}\longrightarrow\alpha_\ell,
 \qquad
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1.
\end{equation}
At the fixed physical times \eqref{eq:v20-fixed-physical-window}, stop each
independent batch when $k_{n,\ell}$ selected endpoint records have been
obtained and retain those signed endpoints.

For the boundary experiment and the local alternatives
\eqref{eq:v20-multirate-parameter}, the endpoint-output experiment is LAN:
there are central sequences
$\Delta_n\Rightarrow N_{1+r}(0,\mathcal K_{\rm design})$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-physical-lan}
 \log\frac{d\mathsf Q_{n,z}}{d\mathsf Q_{n,0}}
 =z^t\Delta_n-rac12z^t\mathcal K_{\rm design}z+o_{\mathsf Q_{n,0}}(1)
\end{equation}
uniformly for $z=(a,h)$ in compact sets.  In particular the gap is a
statistical coordinate at rate $\delta_n/j_n$, while the registered contact
geometry is at rate $\delta_n$.  The matrix
$\mathcal K_{\rm design}$ is positive definite.

The Gaussian testing profile and the local asymptotic minimax conclusions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-vector-boundary-lan} hold with
$\mathcal J_\Sigma$ replaced by $\mathcal K_{\rm design}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For one design, Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-fixed-window-reduction} differs from the
fixed vector boundary family of Section~\ref{sec:v20-vector-information} by
a $C^2$ perturbation which is $o(\delta_n)$ at the defining-function level
and $O(\delta_n)$ in the regular amplitude.  Repeating the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-vector-likelihood} shows that the $o(\delta_n)$ support
perturbation contributes $o_P(1)$ to the local likelihood ratio, while the
regular amplitude contribution has accumulated quadratic information
$O(k_n\delta_n^2)=O(1/\log(1/\delta_n))$ and is therefore negligible at the
boundary scale.  Thus the one-design information is
$\mathcal K_{b,d}$.  Independent batches add their central sequences and
information matrices.  The allocation limits in
\eqref{eq:v20-batch-rate} give \eqref{eq:v20-physical-lan}.  Positive
definiteness is Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-finite-positive-design}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transfer to actual long finite bridges}

The preceding theorem is a boundary experiment.  We now state the finite
physical theorem on the same parameter-independent acquisition rule.  The
point is that the required transfer is already contained in the stopped
physical comparison of Section~\ref{sec:v16-adaptive}; what was missing in
v19 was a theorem that connected that comparison to the local alternatives
and fixed physical windows.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform stopped local-experiment transfer]
\label{thm:v20-stopped-local-transfer}
Let $\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}$ be the stopped transcript law generated by
actual finite bridges under the policy of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-fixed-window-lan}.  The transcript may retain every
failure symbol, every successful endpoint record and the stopping times.  Let
$\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}$ be the corresponding boundary transcript under
the same policy, on the same physical record space and at the same fixed
physical times.  Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-transfer-condition}
                   k_n\tau^{j_n}\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
where $\tau<1$ is the uniform relative-law rate.  Then for every compact
$K\subset\mathbb R^{1+r}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-lecam-transfer}
 \sup_{z\in K}
 \|\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}-\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}\|_{\rm TV}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The same conclusion holds after any common Markov coarsening of the stopped
transcript.  In particular, if the output keeps the successful signed
endpoints and erases the waiting transcript, the actual finite-bridge output
inherits the LAN expansion \eqref{eq:v20-physical-lan}, the Gaussian testing
profile and the local minimax bound.

No failed preparation is free.  If $T_{n,\ell}$ denotes the raw number of
preparations needed to obtain $k_{n,\ell}$ successes, then uniformly on
compact local parameter sets
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-physical-cost}
 \mathbb E T_{n,\ell}
 =\frac{k_{n,\ell}}{p_{n,\ell}(z)},
 \qquad
 p_{n,\ell}(z)\asymp d_\ell^2e^{-j_n\gamma_0},
\end{equation}
up to constants independent of $n$ and bounded local $z$.  Thus the entire
failure cost remains charged even when the inference output deliberately
coarsens away the waiting transcript.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For bounded local $z$, \eqref{eq:v20-raw-excess} remains in a fixed compact
subcollar of $(0,d_*)$ for all large $n$.  The local geometries remain in the
compact family of Theorems~\ref{thm:v6-transfer} and
\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer}.  The policy uses flight number $j_n$ and
stops after exactly $k_n$ successes in total.  Therefore
\eqref{eq:v16-success-stop} gives, uniformly in $z$,
\[
 \|\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}-\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le Ck_n\tau^{j_n},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:v20-lecam-transfer}.  Total variation cannot increase
under a common Markov kernel, so the endpoint-output experiments are also
asymptotically equivalent.  The LAN and risk conclusions then transfer from
Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-fixed-window-lan}.  Finally the stopping time in each
batch is negative binomial with mean $k_{n,\ell}/p_{n,\ell}(z)$, and the
uniform rare-event estimate of Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-transfer} gives
\eqref{eq:v20-physical-cost}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the full stopped transcript can contain more information]
The endpoint-output experiment above is a declared coarsening of the raw
stopped transcript, not an assertion that the waiting counts are ancillary.
Indeed the success probability contains the long-bridge factor
$\sinh(j\gamma)^{-1}$, so retaining waiting counts can produce a still faster
channel for directions which change the hyperbolic exponent.  The theorem
claims the sharp boundary LAN and minimax statement for the explicitly
specified endpoint-output experiment, while charging the complete raw
preparation cost.  Nothing in the argument discards additional information
available to a richer transcript.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A charged pilot justifies the centered formulation}

The fixed-window theorem needs no onset calibration.  For comparison with the
centered formulation used in v19, the historical self-calibration theorem also
gives an explicit two-stage route in which the unknown gap is estimated before
the endpoint experiment.

\begin{corollary}[Pilot-centered asymptotic equivalence]
\label{cor:v20-pilot-centered}
Assume the coarse-bracket hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration}.  Let $r_n\downarrow0$ be its calibration
bandwidth and let an independent charged pilot produce $\widehat g_n$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-pilot-gap}
 j_n|\widehat g_n-g|
 \le C r_n^{m+1}
\end{equation}
with probability $1-\eta_n$, where $\eta_n\to0$.  Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-pilot-smallness}
 k_n r_n^{2m+2}\log(1/r_n)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
After the pilot, use the data-dependent but parameter-free rule
$t_{n,\ell}=j_n\widehat g_n+d_\ell$ and stop at the same prescribed success
counts.  Conditional on a good pilot, the endpoint-output experiment is at
vanishing Le Cam distance from the oracle centered experiment using
$t_{n,\ell}=j_ng+d_\ell$.  The unconditional distance is $o(1)$.

The pilot cost is not suppressed: Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration}
gives, for fixed $m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-pilot-cost}
 \mathbb E T_{\rm pilot}
 \le C_m e^{j_n\gamma}r_n^{-(2m+2)}
             \log(C_m/\eta_n).
\end{equation}
Thus the centered endpoint LAN may be used after a quantitatively charged
onset calibration, rather than by treating the unknown gap as observed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On the good pilot event the displacement of every post-pilot excess from its
oracle value is at most $Cr_n^{m+1}$.  Apply the scalar Hellinger expansion of
Section~\ref{sec:v17-boundary-information} to the offset parameter, uniformly
on the finite design.  The one-record squared Hellinger distance is bounded by
\[
 C r_n^{2m+2}\log(1/r_n).
\]
For $k_n$ independent retained endpoints, tensorization and
\eqref{eq:v20-pilot-smallness} make the Hellinger, hence total-variation,
distance tend to zero.  On the bad pilot event bound the distance by one and
add $\eta_n$.  The finite-bridge replacement may be performed after the same
pilot by Corollary~\ref{cor:v16-pilot-transfer}; the additional error is
$Ck_n\tau^{j_n}$.  The cost bound is exactly the historical charged
self-calibration estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Registration scope]
Theorems~\ref{thm:v20-fixed-window-lan} and
\ref{thm:v20-stopped-local-transfer} estimate the gap and contact geometry in
a fixed labelled registered channel.  Absolute Euclidean pose, contact-label
exchange and an unknown construction of the endpoint frames are not silently
included in $\vartheta$.  If those quantities are also unknown, they must be
added as nuisance parameters on the common laboratory record space or supplied
by a pilot.  A pilot whose induced coordinate error is $\varepsilon_n$ is
negligible for the boundary experiment whenever
$k_n\varepsilon_n^2\log(1/\varepsilon_n)\to0$, by the same Hellinger argument.
This stability criterion is not a claim that every physical apparatus supplies
such a registration pilot for free.
\end{remark}
