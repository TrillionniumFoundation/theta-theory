\section{A raw physical local experiment on fixed laboratory windows}
\label{sec:v20-raw-physical}

The purpose of this section is to place the boundary-information theorem on an
observation space and an acquisition rule which do not depend on the unknown
local alternative.  A fixed excess coordinate $d=t-jg$ is convenient for the
forward geometry, but if $g$ is unknown then holding $d$ fixed changes the
physical time window with the parameter.  We therefore keep physical time
fixed and allow the gap direction to acquire its natural faster local scale.

\subsection{Registered local geometry and a common raw experiment}

We work with a \emph{registered labelled channel}.  The two participating
contact labels and the common signed transverse coordinate used in
Section~\ref{sec:v18-formal-setup} are fixed once and for all.  Equivalently,
the Euclidean pose of the local channel and the label exchange are calibration
variables, not coordinates in the statistical parameter below.  The unknown
geometry consists of the gap and the contact geometry in these fixed frames.
This is exactly the fixed-frame hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-signed-rigidity}; no parameter-dependent re-centering of
the observed endpoints is used in the experiment below.

Let $\vartheta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb R^r$ parameterize a smooth
finite-dimensional family of registered contact geometries and let $g>0$ be
the gap coordinate.  The base geometry is $(g_0,0)$.  Write
$S_{b,g,\vartheta}$ and $B_{b,g,\vartheta}$ for the two half-line actions and
positive amplitudes.  All derivatives below are taken in this fixed registered
chart.  Set
\[
 \dot S_b(u)h=D_\vartheta S_{b,g_0,\vartheta}(u)|_{\vartheta=0}[h].
\]
As always, $S_{b,g,\vartheta}(0)=S'_{b,g,\vartheta}(0)=0$.

Fix finitely many pairs $(b_\ell,d_\ell)$ with
$b_\ell\in\{0,1\}$ and $0<d_\ell<d_*$, $1\le\ell\le L$.  At flight number
$j$ the $\ell$th physical acquisition window is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-fixed-physical-window}
                 t_{j,\ell}=jg_0+d_\ell.
\end{equation}
This time is programmed from the specified reference geometry and is the same
under every local alternative.  A fresh raw preparation returns either a
failure symbol or the selected physical endpoint record in the fixed signed
coordinates.  The experiment will use deterministic preparation caps and
stop each batch at the target success count or at its cap, whichever occurs
first.  Thus the policy satisfies the bounded-stopping hypotheses of
Section~\ref{sec:v16-adaptive} exactly.

\subsection{The gap has a different local scale}

Let $j_n\to\infty$ and $\delta_n\downarrow0$.  For bounded
$(a,h)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^r$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-multirate-parameter}
 g_n(a)=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a,
 \qquad
 \vartheta_n(h)=\delta_nh.
\end{equation}
The excess of the fixed physical window is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-raw-excess}
 t_{j_n,\ell}-j_ng_n(a)=d_\ell-\delta_na.
\end{equation}
Thus a gap perturbation of order $\delta_n/j_n$ produces an order-$\delta_n$
normal displacement of the support.  No onset value is supplied to the
acquisition rule.

For the limiting same-type endpoint law at design $(b,d)$ put
\[
 w_{b,d}(u,v)=d-S_{b,g_0,0}(u)-S_{b,g_0,0}(v)
\]
and let $A_{b,d}(u,v)>0$ denote its normalized smooth amplitude, so that
$f_{b,d}=A_{b,d}(w_{b,d})_+$.  For $z=(a,h)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-raw-support-velocity}
 U_b(u,v)z=a+\dot S_b(u)h+\dot S_b(v)h.
\end{equation}
The sign is immaterial for the information matrix.  Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-raw-information}
 \mathcal K_{b,d}
 =\int_{\{S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d\}}
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)\,U_b(u,v)^tU_b(u,v)}
      {|\nabla(S_b\oplus S_b)(u,v)|}\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-window reduction to the boundary family]
\label{lem:v20-fixed-window-reduction}
Uniformly for $z=(a,h)$ in compact sets, the limiting endpoint density at the
fixed physical window \eqref{eq:v20-fixed-physical-window} has defining
function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-defining-expansion}
 w_{n,z}(u,v)=w_{b,d}(u,v)-\delta_nU_b(u,v)z+o(\delta_n)
\end{equation}
in $C^2$ on the common endpoint box.  The corresponding amplitudes are
uniformly $C^2$ and differ from the base amplitude by $O(\delta_n)$.  The
direct effect of the gap perturbation on the half-line actions is
$o(\delta_n)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:v20-raw-excess} supplies $-\delta_na$.  Smooth parameter
dependence gives
\[
 S_{b,g_n(a),\vartheta_n(h)}
 =S_{b,g_0,0}+\delta_n\dot S_bh
 +\frac{\delta_na}{j_n}\partial_gS_b+o(\delta_n)
\]
uniformly with two endpoint derivatives and on compact $z$-sets.  Since
$j_n\to\infty$, the displayed gap derivative is $o(\delta_n)$.  The same
smooth dependence applies to the positive amplitude and its normalizer.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite positive-offset design]
\label{lem:v20-finite-positive-design}
Assume that $\vartheta$ is a finite labelled contact-jet coordinate at fixed
gap in the registered channel.  There are finitely many same-type designs
$(b_\ell,d_\ell)$ and positive weights $\alpha_\ell$,
$\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell=1$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-design-info}
 \mathcal K_{\rm design}
 =\sum_{\ell=1}^L\alpha_\ell\mathcal K_{b_\ell,d_\ell}
\end{equation}
is positive definite on $\mathbb R^{1+r}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $z=(a,h)$ belongs to the kernel of $\mathcal K_{b,d}$ for both labels
and every sufficiently small $d>0$.  Positivity of the weight in
\eqref{eq:v20-raw-information} implies, by continuity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-kernel-level}
 a+\dot S_b(u)h+\dot S_b(v)h=0
 \quad\text{if}\quad S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d.
\end{equation}
Take $v=0$ and $d=S_b(u)$.  Since $\dot S_b(0)h=0$,
$a+\dot S_b(u)h=0$.  Sending $u\to0$ gives $a=0$ and hence
$\dot S_b(u)h=0$ for both labels in a contact neighborhood.  At fixed gap,
the leading reconstruction formulas and determinant-one recursion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v19-signed-rigidity} then force every labelled graph-jet
component of $h$ to vanish.  Thus the common kernel is trivial.

Every nonzero unit vector is therefore detected by some pair $(b,d)$.
Strict positivity is open in the unit vector and the unit sphere is compact,
so finitely many pairs suffice.  Any positive convex combination of the
corresponding matrices is positive definite.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The boundary local experiment}

Choose the finite design of Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-finite-positive-design}.  Let
$k_{n,\ell}$ be target success counts and put $k_n=\sum_\ell k_{n,\ell}$.
Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-batch-rate}
 \frac{k_{n,\ell}}{k_n}\to\alpha_\ell,
 \qquad
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1,
 \qquad
 j_n\delta_n\to0.
\end{equation}
The last condition is not part of the boundary singularity; it guarantees
that the rare-event prefactors and the deterministic cost bounds remain
uniformly comparable to their values at the reference table.  It is
compatible with the finite-bridge condition below, for example with
$j_n$ logarithmic in $k_n$.

Let $p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)$ be the one-preparation success probability in the
boundary model at the fixed physical window.  By the uniform threshold law and
\eqref{eq:v20-batch-rate}, for every fixed compact local set $K$ there are
constants $c_K,C_K>0$ such that, for all large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-uniform-success}
 c_Kd_\ell^2e^{-j_n\gamma_0}
 \le p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)
 \le C_Kd_\ell^2e^{-j_n\gamma_0},
 \qquad z\in K.
\end{equation}
Choose deterministic caps $N_{n,\ell}$, from a fixed compact parameter
neighborhood rather than from the unknown $z$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-cap-choice}
 \inf_{z\in K}N_{n,\ell}p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)
 \ge 2k_{n,\ell}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently one may use a single compact-neighborhood lower bound in place of
$K$.  A binomial Chernoff bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-cap-tail}
 \sup_{z\in K}\Pr_z\{\text{cap reached before }k_{n,\ell}
 \text{ successes}\}\le e^{-c k_{n,\ell}}.
\end{equation}
On that exceptional event we output a fixed cemetery symbol.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-window multirate endpoint LAN]
\label{thm:v20-fixed-window-lan}
Under \eqref{eq:v20-batch-rate}, the boundary endpoint-output experiment
produced by the capped policy above is LAN for the local coordinate
$z=(a,h)$.  There are central sequences
$\Delta_n\Rightarrow N_{1+r}(0,\mathcal K_{\rm design})$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-physical-lan}
 \log\frac{d\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\rm end}}{d\mathsf Q_{n,0}^{\rm end}}
 =z^t\Delta_n-\frac12z^t\mathcal K_{\rm design}z+o_P(1)
\end{equation}
uniformly on compact $z$-sets.  The gap is a statistical coordinate at rate
$\delta_n/j_n$ and the registered contact geometry at rate $\delta_n$.
The matrix $\mathcal K_{\rm design}$ is positive definite.  The Gaussian
testing profile and local asymptotic minimax conclusions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-vector-boundary-lan} hold with
$\mathcal J_\Sigma$ replaced by $\mathcal K_{\rm design}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Ignore the cap first.  For one design,
Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-fixed-window-reduction} differs from the fixed vector
boundary family of Section~\ref{sec:v20-vector-information} by an
$o(\delta_n)$ $C^2$ perturbation of the defining function and an
$O(\delta_n)$ regular amplitude perturbation.  The likelihood proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-vector-likelihood} is uniform under such perturbations.
The regular amplitude contributes only
$O(k_n\delta_n^2)=O(1/\log(1/\delta_n))$ quadratic information, whereas the
support term gives $\mathcal K_{b,d}$.  Independent batches add central
sequences and information matrices, so the allocation limits give
\eqref{eq:v20-physical-lan}.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-finite-positive-design}
gives positive definiteness.  Finally \eqref{eq:v20-cap-tail} shows that the
capped and uncapped endpoint-output experiments differ in total variation by
$o(1)$, uniformly on compact local sets.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transfer to actual long finite bridges}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform stopped local-experiment transfer]
\label{thm:v20-stopped-local-transfer}
Let $\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}$ be the complete stopped transcript law for the
actual finite bridges under the same deterministic caps, fixed physical times
and target success counts.  The transcript retains failures, successes,
designs and stopping times.  Let $\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}$ be the boundary
transcript generated by the identical policy.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-transfer-condition}
                         k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0,
\end{equation}
then for every compact local set $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-lecam-transfer}
 \sup_{z\in K}
 \|\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}-\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}\|_{\rm TV}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The conclusion survives every common Markov coarsening.  In particular the
finite-bridge endpoint-output experiment inherits
Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-fixed-window-lan}.

No failed preparation is free.  If $T_{n,\ell}$ is the number of raw
preparations used in batch $\ell$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-physical-cost}
 \mathbb E_zT_{n,\ell}
 \le \frac{k_{n,\ell}}{p_{n,\ell}(z)}
 \le C_K k_{n,\ell}d_\ell^{-2}e^{j_n\gamma_0}
\end{equation}
uniformly for $z\in K$ and all large $n$.  The deterministic cap provides the
corresponding absolute upper bound on $T_{n,\ell}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For bounded local $z$, \eqref{eq:v20-raw-excess} stays in a common compact
subcollar and the local geometries stay in the compact family of
Theorems~\ref{thm:v6-transfer} and \ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer}.  The
capped policy satisfies the deterministic stopping hypothesis of the latter
theorem and stops no later than the $k_n$th success in total.  Therefore
\[
 \|\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}-\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le Ck_n\tau^{j_n}
\]
uniformly on compact local sets.  This proves
\eqref{eq:v20-lecam-transfer}.  Total variation contracts under a common
Markov kernel, so the same bound applies to endpoint output.  The expectation
bound follows from domination by the uncapped negative-binomial stopping time
and the uniform success estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The waiting transcript is not declared ancillary]
The endpoint-output experiment is a declared coarsening of the raw stopped
transcript.  The success probability contains the long-bridge factor
$\sinh(j\gamma)^{-1}$, so waiting counts can carry additional, faster
information in directions which change the hyperbolic exponent.  The theorem
claims the logarithmic boundary LAN and its minimax statement for the
specified endpoint-output experiment while charging the complete preparation
cost.  It does not assert that a richer transcript has no additional
information.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A charged pilot justifies centered windows}

The fixed-window theorem needs no onset calibration.  To justify the elegant
centered formulation used in the preceding revision, one may instead estimate
the gap with an independent charged pilot and then center the physical
windows at the estimate.

\begin{corollary}[Pilot-centered asymptotic equivalence]
\label{cor:v20-pilot-centered}
Assume the coarse-bracket hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration}.  Let $r_n\downarrow0$ be the calibration
bandwidth and let an independent pilot produce $\widehat g_n$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-pilot-gap}
 j_n|\widehat g_n-g|\le Cr_n^{m+1}
\end{equation}
with probability $1-\eta_n$, where $\eta_n\to0$.  Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-pilot-smallness}
 k_nr_n^{2m+2}\log(1/r_n)\to0.
\end{equation}
After the pilot use the common data-dependent rule
$t_{n,\ell}=j_n\widehat g_n+d_\ell$ and the same capped success targets.
Then the endpoint-output experiment is at vanishing Le Cam distance from the
oracle centered endpoint experiment using $t_{n,\ell}=j_ng+d_\ell$.
Including the finite-bridge replacement adds at most
$\eta_n+Ck_n\tau^{j_n}+o(1)$.

The pilot cost is explicit:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-pilot-cost}
 \mathbb ET_{\rm pilot}
 \le C_me^{j_n\gamma}r_n^{-(2m+2)}\log(C_m/\eta_n).
\end{equation}
Thus centered windows may be used after quantitative onset calibration; the
unknown gap is not inserted as a free observation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On the good pilot event the post-pilot excess differs from its oracle value by
at most $Cr_n^{m+1}$.  The scalar boundary Hellinger expansion of
Section~\ref{sec:v17-boundary-information}, uniformly over the finite design,
gives one-record squared Hellinger distance at most
$Cr_n^{2m+2}\log(1/r_n)$.  Hellinger tensorization and
\eqref{eq:v20-pilot-smallness} give $o(1)$ for all retained endpoints.  Add
$\eta_n$ for the bad pilot event.  Corollary~\ref{cor:v16-pilot-transfer}
adds the finite-bridge error $Ck_n\tau^{j_n}$.  The cost bound is exactly the
charged self-calibration estimate of Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Registration scope]
Theorems~\ref{thm:v20-fixed-window-lan} and
\ref{thm:v20-stopped-local-transfer} estimate the gap and contact geometry in
a fixed labelled registered channel.  Absolute Euclidean pose, contact-label
exchange and an unknown construction of the endpoint frames are not silently
included in $\vartheta$.  If those quantities are also unknown, they must be
added as nuisance parameters on the common laboratory record space or supplied
by a pilot.  A pilot whose induced coordinate error is $\varepsilon_n$ is
negligible whenever
$k_n\varepsilon_n^2\log(1/\varepsilon_n)\to0$ by the same Hellinger
argument.  This stability criterion is not a claim that such registration is
free.
\end{remark}
