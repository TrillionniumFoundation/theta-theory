\begin{abstract}
We study which information metrics can be recovered from the second contact jets of synchronized root observations. Under injective quadratic measurements, the conormal union of the rank-one residual cone has a sharp dimension-dependent obstruction: with two shared parameters, precisely one quadratic matrix direction is invisible, whereas with at least three shared parameters no nonzero quadratic form is invisible. In calibrated polynomial binary experiments, interpolation forces inverse efficient information into a Hankel congruence class. We give explicit three-site relations characterizing that class and prove that they remove the two-parameter ambiguity exactly under a stated transversality condition. In the smallest nonlinear case, five directional contact curvatures recover the native metric; without its Hankel constraint an entire line remains indistinguishable to second order. More generally, sharp finite dual-jet recovery transports the fixed-exposure image to a convex region of observable data with singleton boundary fibres and circular interior design fibres. We also give an intrinsic effective minimal-representation theorem whose input is only a labelled endpoint value function. Complete companion results on finite-map residuals, critical weighted jets, endpoint reconstruction, and local experiments are retained with their proofs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A common perturbation of several double roots constrains their variance vector to a quadratic image
\[
 q_A(\eta)=((a_i^{\mathsf T}\eta)^2)_{i=1}^k.
\]
After the free score directions have been profiled out, the leading contact with a target variance $tb$, $b\ge0$, is the squared distance from $b$ to this cone in the efficient information metric. For a fixed cone, a natural inverse question is whether its observed contact geometry determines the metric. Knowing a metric and evaluating a distance is immediate; recovering that metric from contact is not.

Theorem~\ref{thm:general-tomography} answers this inverse question for injective quadratic measurements and native calibrated polynomial experiments. A normal vector $n$ at $q_A(\eta)$ satisfies
\[
 \Big(\sum_i n_i a_i a_i^{\mathsf T}\Big)\eta=0.
\]
Thus the conormal union is the inverse image of the singular symmetric matrices. The quadratic forms vanishing on this union have dimension one for two shared parameters, and dimension zero in all higher shared dimensions. Independently, the native scalar score inverse satisfies explicit three-site linear relations. Testing the sole invisible quadratic direction against those relations gives a necessary and sufficient recovery criterion. This is the interaction between residual geometry and Hankel information used throughout the principal theorem.

Corollary~\ref{thm:tomography} makes the criterion fully explicit for three root pairs and two shared parameters. Five scalar directional curvatures recover a binary quartic; its one-dimensional matrix ambiguity is removed by the native annihilator exactly when $\tau_A\ne0$. We exhibit both a rational transverse experiment and a nondegenerate exceptional loading family. The ambiguity statement concerns second jets, not equality of all higher contact terms. For higher codimension the finite oracle is a scalar value of the dual normal quadratic form, not an unqualified count of ordinary directional derivatives.

The fixed-budget conclusion is a consequence of this recovery map. In moment coordinates the reciprocal budget value has positive definite Hessian. The recovered dual jets are an invertible linear change of those coordinates, so the entire budget image and all its design fibres become observable contact-data geometry. The reciprocal-fibre argument itself is a general convex lemma; what is forced by the experiment is the inverse-information transformation and the corank two in the endpoint-free family.

Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-fibre} addresses a separate companion inverse problem. Its input is the graph of a labelled scalar endpoint profile, not a reference quadratic presentation or an optimizer map. It returns every normalized representation at any prescribed dimension and, under a finite-representability promise, the full minimal fibre. Explicit first-stratum reconstructions remain in the appendices; the general effective statement is not advertised as an explicit normal-form catalogue.

The organization follows this hierarchy. The native score inverse and uniform contact hypotheses precede the principal conormal theorem. The three-root formula and intrinsic endpoint companion follow it. The complete earlier geometric and statistical machinery is placed in the appendices rather than used as an additional centre of the paper.
