\section{Contact tomography and the Hankel constraint}\label{sec:tomography}
We consider three separated interior double roots and no endpoints in the native experiment of Section~\ref{sec:binary-hankel}. There are seven clocks. The roots, clocks, calibrated channels, central mixing probability, and component colours are known; the seven positive exposures are not supplied. The unknown retained inverse information belongs to the open native cone
\[
 \mathscr R=\{J_*H(\omega)J_*^{\mathsf T}:\omega\in\R_{>0}^7\}.
\]
Here and below ``open'' for this cone means open in its five-dimensional linear span. The loading vectors $a_1,a_2,a_3\in\R^2$ are pairwise linearly independent. Set
\[
 \ell_i(\eta)=a_i^{\mathsf T}\eta,\qquad
 \Delta_{ij}=\det(a_i,a_j),\qquad q(\eta)=(\ell_i(\eta)^2)_{i=1}^3.
\]
The loading tensors are a basis of $\Sym_2$. Thus the cone $\mathcal V_A=q(\R^2)$ is a linearly embedded rank-one positive cone, with its vertex at zero. For any $R>0$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tomography-contact}
 C_R(b)=\min_{\eta\in\R^2}(q(\eta)-b)^{\mathsf T}R^{-1}(q(\eta)-b),
 \qquad b\in\R_+^3.
\end{equation}
For $R\in\mathscr R$, Theorem~\ref{thm:synchronized} identifies this function with the leading Hellinger contact of the synchronized experiment. We first take that small-time limit; the second derivatives below are derivatives of the resulting function of $b$. No interchange of a finite-sample derivative with the small-time limit is asserted.

The following conormal field is fixed by the loading geometry:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conormal}
 n_A(\eta)=\big(\Delta_{23}\ell_2\ell_3,
              -\Delta_{13}\ell_1\ell_3,
               \Delta_{12}\ell_1\ell_2\big)^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
Let $B_A\in\Sym_3$ have zero diagonal and $(B_A)_{ij}=\Delta_{ij}^2$ for $i\ne j$. In physical root order $r_1<r_2<r_3$, let $d_i$ be the nonzero interpolation coefficients in \eqref{eq:cauchy-columns}, and define $K\in\Sym_3$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hankel-annihilator}
 K_{ii}=0,\quad K_{12}=\frac{r_2-r_1}{2d_1d_2},\quad
 K_{13}=-\frac{r_3-r_1}{2d_1d_3},\quad
 K_{23}=\frac{r_3-r_2}{2d_2d_3}.
\end{equation}
Our transversality invariant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau}
 \tau_A=\tr(KB_A)
 =\frac{(r_2-r_1)\Delta_{12}^2}{d_1d_2}
  -\frac{(r_3-r_1)\Delta_{13}^2}{d_1d_3}
  +\frac{(r_3-r_2)\Delta_{23}^2}{d_2d_3}.
\end{equation}
Its vanishing is unchanged by an invertible change of the two shared parameters: each squared determinant is multiplied by the same positive factor. This is a relation between the conormal of the synchronized cone and the native information hyperplane, not a positivity condition on a freely selected metric.

Choose five nonzero vectors $\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_5$ in distinct real projective directions, avoiding the three lines $\ell_i=0$. Then $b_j=q(\eta_j)>0$. Put $n_j=n_A(\eta_j)$ and $v_j=n_j/(n_j^{\mathsf T}n_j)$. The measured scalar contact jet and its reciprocal are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jet-data}
 c_j(R)=\lim_{\epsilon\to0}\epsilon^{-2}C_R(b_j+\epsilon v_j),
 \qquad h_j(R)=c_j(R)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Both signs of sufficiently small $\epsilon$ give admissible targets. These are exact limiting scalar data; their number is not a sample-complexity assertion.

\begin{corollary}[Five directional contact curvatures]\label{thm:tomography}
In the experiment and with the five projective directions just specified, the following statements hold.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(i) Sharp transversality.}
The five contact jets determine the native inverse information $R\in\mathscr R$ if and only if $\tau_A\ne0$. In that case they determine $C_R(b)$ for every $b\ge0$. More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-jet-recovery}
 h_j(R)=n_j^{\mathsf T}Rn_j,\qquad \tr(KR)=0.
\end{equation}
These six equations have one solution in $\Sym_3$. If $R_0$ is any solution of the first five equations, that solution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-recovery}
 R=R_0-\frac{\tr(KR_0)}{\tau_A}B_A.
\end{equation}
Five is the least number of such scalar contact-jet queries sufficient on the open native image, even for deterministic adaptive queries. It is also least on the interior of the fixed-total-exposure image.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(ii) Exact ambiguity without the native constraint.}
For arbitrary positive definite $R,R'$, all second contact jets at the smooth positive part of $\mathcal V_A$ coincide if and only if $R'-R\in\R B_A$. Thus arbitrary positive metrics have a one-dimensional ambiguity which the native constraint removes precisely when $\tau_A\ne0$. When $\tau_A=0$, the native fibre of any attainable jet data is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exception-fibre}
 (R_0+\R B_A)\cap\mathscr R.
\end{equation}
It contains a nontrivial open interval about every one of its points. At fixed total exposure one intersects this fibre with the budget image; a nontrivial interval persists at every budget-interior point, but need not persist at its boundary. Equality of second jets is not asserted to imply equality of the entire contact functions for the ambiguous metrics.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(iii) Geometry of the observable budget image.}
Suppose $\tau_A\ne0$ and $\sum_j\lambda_j=1$. Write $y=(y_0,\ldots,y_4)$ for the moment coordinates of $H$, and let $\chi$ be the reciprocal budget function of Theorem~\ref{thm:budget}. The linear map
\[
 \mathcal L:y\longmapsto\big(n_j^{\mathsf T}J_*H(y)J_*^{\mathsf T}n_j\big)_{j=1}^5
\]
is invertible. The attainable reciprocal contact-jet vectors form exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contact-budget-image}
 \{h\in\mathcal L(\mathscr M):\chi(\mathcal L^{-1}h)\le1\}.
\end{equation}
This is a convex region. Its relative boundary inside $\mathcal L(\mathscr M)$ is the analytic hypersurface $\chi(\mathcal L^{-1}h)=1$, with positive definite Hessian of its defining function. A boundary datum comes from one positive exposure vector. An interior datum comes from a fibre diffeomorphic to $S^1$. The circle is a differential-topological assertion, not an affine circle in exposure coordinates.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(iv) Stability.}
Let $\mathscr W=\{X\in\Sym_3:\tr(KX)=0\}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tomography-margin}
 \sigma_{\rm tom}=\min_{X\in\mathscr W,\ \|X\|_F=1}
    \left(\sum_{j=1}^5|n_j^{\mathsf T}Xn_j|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Then $\sigma_{\rm tom}>0$ if and only if $\tau_A\ne0$, and
\[
 \|R-R'\|_F\le\sigma_{\rm tom}^{-1}\|h(R)-h(R')\|_2
 \quad(R,R'\in\mathscr R).
\]
On compact positive-metric families, this estimate controls the entire contact function uniformly on compact target sets, including targets with nonunique minimizing root configurations. All constants are uniform on compact configurations with $\sigma_{\rm tom}$ bounded below.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Conormal measurements and their unique missing direction}
\begin{lemma}[Second contact jet]\label{lem:contact-hessian}
For $\eta\ne0$ with all $\ell_i(\eta)\ne0$, the function $C_R$ is smooth in a neighbourhood of $q(\eta)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contact-hessian}
 D^2C_R(q(\eta))=
 \frac{2n_A(\eta)n_A(\eta)^{\mathsf T}}{n_A(\eta)^{\mathsf T}Rn_A(\eta)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The map $S\mapsto(a_i^{\mathsf T}Sa_i)_i$ is a linear isomorphism on $\Sym_2$: a quadratic form vanishing on three distinct projective lines is zero. The rank-one positive matrices away from zero form a smooth embedded surface. Therefore $\mathcal V_A$ is smooth there. The elementary three-vector determinant identity gives $Dq(\eta)^{\mathsf T}n_A(\eta)=0$, and $n_A(\eta)\ne0$.

Nearest-point projection in a fixed positive metric is smooth in a sufficiently small tubular neighbourhood of any point of this surface. Here this can be proved directly by solving the stationary equations in two local surface coordinates: at a point on the surface the coordinate Hessian is twice the positive definite tangent Gram matrix, so the implicit function theorem applies. This local minimizer is the global one near the point. Indeed the trial at $q(\eta)$ makes the minimum tend to zero, hence positive definiteness forces every minimizing image point to tend to $q(\eta)$. Closedness and properness of the quadratic cone rule out escape; the local surface chart then contains every minimizer.

For a displacement $\delta b$, minimizing its quadratic cost modulo the tangent plane $n^{\mathsf T}u=0$ gives residual
$Rn(n^{\mathsf T}\delta b)/(n^{\mathsf T}Rn)$ and cost
$(n^{\mathsf T}\delta b)^2/(n^{\mathsf T}Rn)$. This is the quadratic term of the squared distance and proves the formula, including the factor two.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conormal kernel]\label{lem:conormal-kernel}
The map
\[
 \mathfrak p:\Sym_3\longrightarrow\R[\eta_1,\eta_2]_4,
 \qquad X\longmapsto n_A(\eta)^{\mathsf T}Xn_A(\eta)
\]
has rank five and kernel $\R B_A$. Evaluation at any five distinct real projective directions has the same kernel.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The three coordinates of $n_A$ are independent quadratic forms. To see independence, take a nonzero vector on $\ell_i=0$; exactly the $i$th coordinate of $n_A$ is nonzero. They are therefore a basis of binary quadratics. Products of binary quadratics span all binary quartics, a five-dimensional space. Hence $\mathfrak p$ is onto and has a one-dimensional kernel.

The matrix $S(\eta)=\sum_i n_i(\eta)a_i a_i^{\mathsf T}$ kills $\eta$, since $Dq(\eta)^{\mathsf T}n_A(\eta)=0$. Thus
\[
 0=2\det S(\eta)=2\sum_{i<j}\Delta_{ij}^2n_i(\eta)n_j(\eta)
 =n_A(\eta)^{\mathsf T}B_A n_A(\eta).
\]
As $B_A\ne0$, this identifies the kernel. A nonzero homogeneous binary quartic has at most four distinct real projective zeros. Five evaluations are therefore injective on its coefficient space and have precisely the same kernel on $\Sym_3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Native annihilator]\label{lem:native-annihilator}
The linear span of $\mathscr R$ is $\mathscr W=K^\perp$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $c(T)=(d_i/(T-r_i))_{i=1}^3$, multiplication by
$(T-r_1)(T-r_2)(T-r_3)$ gives
\[
 c(T)^{\mathsf T}Kc(T)\prod_i(T-r_i)
 =(r_2-r_1)(T-r_3)-(r_3-r_1)(T-r_2)+(r_3-r_2)(T-r_1)=0.
\]
Every native inverse information is a positive sum of these Cauchy tensors, so it lies in $K^\perp$. Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-hankel} gives span dimension five. Since $K\ne0$, its orthogonal hyperplane also has dimension five, proving equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{thm:tomography}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:contact-hessian} gives $c_j(R)=1/(n_j^{\mathsf T}Rn_j)>0$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:conormal-kernel}, two symmetric solutions of these data differ by a multiple of $B_A$. Lemma~\ref{lem:native-annihilator} shows that this line is transverse to the native hyperplane exactly when $\tr(KB_A)\ne0$. This proves the formula and both directions of the injectivity assertion, as well as the general-metric ambiguity. When the line is contained in the hyperplane, its intersection with the relatively open convex cone is a relatively open interval. A sufficiently small segment about an interior point remains in that cone. The same reasoning applies at a budget-interior point.

For the lower bound on the number of scalar queries, run a proposed deterministic adaptive procedure at an interior native metric. Each scalar answer, after reciprocation, imposes one linear equation on $\mathscr W$. Fewer than five queries leave a nonzero common kernel direction. Small opposite perturbations in this direction remain feasible and give the same transcript, including the adaptively chosen directions. The fixed-budget interior is also relatively open in $\mathscr W$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:budget}, so the argument is unchanged there. This lower bound concerns exactly the scalar oracle in \eqref{eq:jet-data}, not an oracle returning an arbitrary function.

The linear moment parametrization and the now injective five measurements give the isomorphism $\mathcal L$. At seven clocks the moment map has kernel dimension $7-5=2$. Theorem~\ref{thm:budget}, or the abstract reciprocal-fibre Lemma~\ref{lem:reciprocal-general} below, identifies the budget image by $\chi\le1$, its boundary by $\chi=1$, and its fibres by a point or $S^1$. Under the linear change $\mathcal L$ the defining Hessian remains positive definite. Euler's identity $y^{\mathsf T}\nabla\chi(y)=-\chi(y)$ makes the gradient nonzero on the boundary. This proves (iii).

The smallest-singular-value estimate gives (iv). For completeness, suppose the eigenvalues of $R,R'$ lie in $[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)$ and $|b_0|\le M$. A minimizer for $C_R(b_0)$ obeys
\[
 C_R(b_0)\le |b_0|^2/a,\qquad
 |q(\eta)-b_0|^2\le (b/a)M^2.
\]
Testing this minimizer in the other metric, and conversely, bounds the contact difference by $(b/a)M^2\|R^{-1}-R'^{-1}\|$, which is at most $(b/a^3)M^2\|R-R'\|$. This argument does not require uniqueness of the minimizer. Conversion between $c$ and $h=1/c$ is uniformly Lipschitz when the metric and the chosen nonzero normals range over compact nondegenerate sets. Continuity and compactness give the last uniform assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exceptional locus and an exact native example}
For one-colour roots the three $d_i$ have the same sign. With
$a_1=(1,0)$, $a_2=(0,1)$, and $a_3=(x,y)$, $xy\ne0$, the condition is
\[
 \tau_A=\frac{r_2-r_1}{d_1d_2}
       -\frac{(r_3-r_1)y^2}{d_1d_3}
       +\frac{(r_3-r_2)x^2}{d_2d_3}.
\]
It is not identically zero, and for every nonzero $x$ there is a nonzero $y$ making it zero. Thus the exceptional algebraic hypersurface is nonempty, and the transverse class is open and dense in this loading chart. An assertion of recovery for all spanning loadings would be false.

For a rational example, take roots $(1,2,3)$, $D=7/2$, clocks $(4,5,6,7,8,9,10)$, and all variable factors in component one. Use any distinct positive calibrated product tables, an interior mixing probability, and positive exposures. The interpolation factors are
\[
 (d_1,d_2,d_3)=(-45360,-40320,-1260).
\]
For loadings $(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)$ one obtains
\[
 n_A(u,v)=(-v(u+v),-u(u+v),uv),\qquad
 \tau_A=-\frac1{67737600}\ne0.
\]
The five directions $(1,j)$, $j=1,\ldots,5$, are admissible. In the variable order
\[ (R_{11},R_{22},R_{33},R_{12},R_{13},R_{23}), \]
the six linear equations \eqref{eq:linear-jet-recovery} have determinant $-1/58800$. Changing only the third loading to $(1,\sqrt{37}/8)$ gives $\tau_A=0$ and a genuine one-dimensional native ambiguity of second jets. Both assertions follow by substitution in the displayed formulas. The accompanying exact checks are finite algebraic checks of these examples, not certification of the universal theorem.

The coupling is now explicit: the conormal has one quadratic ambiguity, while interpolation gives one independent linear restriction on inverse information. Their transversality determines whether observable contact curvature recovers the native metric. Neither positivity alone nor the rank-one lifting identity alone yields this conclusion.
