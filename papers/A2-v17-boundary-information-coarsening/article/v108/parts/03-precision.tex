\section{Reciprocal fibres and uniform hypotheses}\label{sec:reciprocal-general}
We separate a general convex-fibre fact from the polynomial experiment that supplies its particular matrix and corank.

\begin{lemma}[General reciprocal-fibre geometry]\label{lem:reciprocal-general}
Let $T:\R^m\to\R^s$ be surjective. Assume there is a covector $u\in\R^s$ with $(T^{\mathsf T}u)_j>0$ for all $j$. Fix $a_j>0$, set $\mathscr M=T\R_{>0}^m$, and define
\[
 \phi(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{a_j}{\omega_j},\qquad
 \chi(y)=\min_{\omega>0,\ T\omega=y}\phi(\omega).
\]
Every $y\in\mathscr M$ has a unique minimizer $\omega_*(y)$. The functions $\omega_*$ and $\chi$ are analytic, $\chi(ty)=\chi(y)/t$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-reciprocal-hessian}
 D^2\chi(y)=(TD^{-1}T^{\mathsf T})^{-1}>0,
 \qquad D=\diag(2a_j/\omega_{*j}^3).
\end{equation}
Put $d=m-s$. If $d=0$, the image of $\{\omega>0:\phi(\omega)=1\}$ is $\{\chi=1\}$, with singleton fibres. If $d>0$, its image is $\{\chi\le1\}$; fibres are singletons when $\chi=1$, and are diffeomorphic to $S^{d-1}$ when $\chi<1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The closure of a positive affine fibre is bounded, since
$\sum_j(T^{\mathsf T}u)_j\omega_j=u^{\mathsf T}y$. The function $\phi$ tends to infinity at any coordinate face and has positive definite Hessian. Existence and uniqueness follow by compact sublevel sets and strict convexity. At the minimizer, $\nabla\phi(\omega_*)=T^{\mathsf T}\nu$. The bordered linearization of these equations and $T\omega_*=y$ is nonsingular: a homogeneous solution obeys $D\delta\omega=T^{\mathsf T}\delta\nu$ and $T\delta\omega=0$, hence $\delta\omega=0$ and then $\delta\nu=0$. The analytic implicit function theorem applies. Differentiating gives
$D\omega_*=D^{-1}T^{\mathsf T}(TD^{-1}T^{\mathsf T})^{-1}$ and $D\chi=\nu^{\mathsf T}$, proving the Hessian formula. Homogeneity follows by scaling the affine fibre.

When $d=0$ each affine fibre is a point. Otherwise, within the $d$-dimensional kernel directions, each ray from $\omega_*$ reaches the boundary of the bounded positive affine fibre. Along it $\phi$ starts at its minimum, increases strictly, and tends to infinity. If $\chi<1$ it crosses level one exactly once. The crossing radius is smooth in the unit direction by the implicit function theorem, since the radial derivative is strictly positive. This radial parametrization and its inverse are smooth and give $S^{d-1}$. If $\chi=1$ strict convexity leaves only the minimizer; if $\chi>1$ the level is empty.
\end{proof}

The binary experiment forces $T_{rj}=T_j^r$, $s=2n-1$, $m=2k+e+1$, $a_j=\xi_j^2/(p_*(T_j)^2\kappa_j)$, and therefore $d=2-e$. The zeroth-moment covector supplies the positive covector hypothesis. Theorem~\ref{thm:budget} is its native specialization, with all the explicit rational interpolation and rectangular-clock calculations retained. The function $\chi$ is a reciprocal budget value of degree minus one, not a degree-one norm gauge. No claim is made that the general strict-convexity argument is peculiar to binary observations.

At a fixed unit budget $\lambda_j>0$, $\omega_j=a_j/\lambda_j$ and $\lambda_j\le1$, so $\omega_j\ge a_j$. Letting any exposure tend to zero sends $\omega_j$, and hence the zeroth moment, to infinity. It does not approach a finite boundary point of the open moment cone. The hypersurface $\chi=1$ is the relative boundary of the positive-exposure image inside that open cone; its exposures are all strictly positive. A boundary of the closed finite moment cone obtained by letting some $\omega_j$ vanish is a third, different boundary. These distinctions also apply after the observable linear transformation in \eqref{eq:contact-budget-image}.

\subsection{Explicit uniform class for synchronized contact}\label{sec:uniform-class}
In every compact-uniform assertion of Theorem~\ref{thm:synchronized}, use the measurement margin
\[
 \mu(A)=\min_{S\in\Sym_d,\ \|S\|_F=1}\|\mathcal A_A(S)\|_2\ge\mu_0>0.
\]
Keep the dimensions fixed, loadings bounded, separated roots and clocks in compact sets, positive probabilities and exposures bounded away from zero, and metric eigenvalues in a fixed compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$. Targets always satisfy $b\in\R_+^k$ and range in a bounded set. The sufficient margin is not a sharp phase-retrieval condition.

For the nonlinear replacement of a loading, the precise perturbation class consists of functions on one fixed ball $|\eta|\le\rho$, with $\ell_i(0)=0$, $D\ell_i(0)=a_i^{\mathsf T}$ and a common bound on $\|D^2\ell_i\|$. Require $C^2$ regularity there, and analytic or semialgebraic regularity when that category is invoked. Taylor's theorem gives a uniform quadratic remainder. Moreover
\[
 \|q_A(\eta)\|\ge\mu_0|\eta|^2,\qquad
 \|((\ell_i(\eta)^2)_i)-q_A(\eta)\|\le C|\eta|^3.
\]
After one common shrinking of $\rho$, the perturbed variance norm is at least $(\mu_0/2)|\eta|^2$. This proves the uniform localization used in the synchronized theorem rather than assuming it through the word ``analytic''. At $\eta=\sqrt t\,\zeta$ the remainder divided by $t$ is uniformly $O(\sqrt t)$ on bounded $\zeta$. The inherited limiting residual and minimum proof therefore applies to exactly this class. The full residual includes the free score space and endpoint orthant; only its free-score quotient is the translated linear image of $\mathcal V_A\times\R_+^e$.

\subsection{Conventions for the retained companion proofs}
For Proposition~\ref{prop:simultaneous}, the source is compact semialgebraic, the observation maps are polynomial or analytic and semialgebraic on the neighbourhoods used, and the marked arcs and tube data are semialgebraic with the stated positive-time analytic regularity. The determinant $J$ is nonzero on the positive-time tube. The object being uniformized is the compact closure of its positive-time graph, not the closure of one selected inverse sheet. Every pruning retains a surjective preimage of that graph germ. These are hypotheses of that proposition; its proof uses them to compare full tube suprema. A proper weighted initial map in Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-jets} is a supporting sufficient normal form, not a classification of arbitrary critical germs.

In the missing-singleton proof, at $e=3$ the two other independent columns give the required two distinct pair ranges. In arbitrary dimension, equality of a pair span with another selected pair containing a different known column would give a linear dependence among the independent columns, so the criterion cannot choose a spurious pair. In the planar exhaustion proof, a rank-one difference determines a linear form only up to sign; the sign is fixed by the labelled open regions carrying the two distinct local quadratics. Reversing the sign interchanges these regions and changes the function on an open set. Coincident quadratics are instead a visibility degeneration, which is handled separately and cannot justify the sign selection. Theorem~\ref{thm:all-strata} remains a reference-based equality test; the function-input inverse statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-fibre}.

The seven-clock overidentification example in Proposition~\ref{prop:overidentified} excludes extension of the specified six-clock congruence, not every possible exposure-independent congruence. In particular none of the contact-tomography statements extrapolates the exact square-clock Hankel constraint to an overidentified experiment.
