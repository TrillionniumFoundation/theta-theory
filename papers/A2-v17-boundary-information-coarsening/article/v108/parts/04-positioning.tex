\subsection{Relation to inverse optimization and design equivalence}
Inverse parametric optimization is directly relevant to the companion endpoint problem. Hempel, Goulart, and Lygeros \cite{HGL} construct optimization representations of supplied piecewise-affine maps in hybrid control. Nguyen, Olaru, Rodriguez-Ayerbe, Hovd, and Necoara \cite{NORHN} use convex lifting to represent continuous piecewise-affine functions on polytopic partitions as solutions of parametric linear or quadratic programs. Thus inverse synthesis of a parametric program is not a new problem introduced here. In those formulations the given object is an optimizer map (or its linear output), with its piecewise-affine data; constraints and objective are constructed. They do not start with only our scalar labelled optimal-value function. In particular one must not describe their inputs as a known hidden quadratic-program active fan.

Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-fibre} instead starts with the exact scalar graph, searches within positive block quadratic forms with a fixed endpoint orthant, determines the least endpoint dimension, and returns all normalized minimal representations. The earlier explicit first-stratum theorems additionally identify uniqueness modulo endpoint gauges or scalar moduli. These are different outputs from constructing one optimizer representation, not a claim that our restricted class subsumes the general inverse-optimization constructions. An optimizer alone does not determine the observed value: adding a parameter-only quadratic to the objective leaves every minimizer unchanged while changing that value. Conversely a value function need not specify a hidden optimizer or its dimension. Hence applying optimizer-map synthesis to an arbitrarily chosen map cannot by itself establish the value equality, positivity, and minimal-fibre assertions here. Quantifier elimination is the effective general tool; explicit first-stratum formulas remain separately valuable.

There is also a concrete distinction from direct information-matrix design equivalence. Harman, Filov\'a, and Rosa \cite[Lemma 1]{HFR} describe fixed-information approximate designs by nonnegative weights satisfying linear moment equations, hence by a polytope. Our direct information is not being redescribed as something nonlinear. After nuisance elimination, rational interpolation forces the \emph{inverse} efficient information to have weights $\omega_j=a_j/\lambda_j$. Unit exposure therefore becomes $\sum_j a_j/\omega_j=1$, a reciprocal level inside an affine moment fibre, rather than the affine simplex slice of direct design equivalence. Lemma~\ref{lem:reciprocal-general} deliberately states the convex argument in its general form: it uses a positive covector and not polynomial observations. The native content is the forced reciprocal transformation, the corank $2-e$, and, in Theorem~\ref{thm:general-tomography}, the exact transport of this geometry into observed dual contact jets. No new general equivalence theorem for optimal designs is claimed.

The principal result is different from both inverse-program synthesis and design equivalence. It classifies the quadratic forms invisible on the conormal union of a synchronized rank-one cone, then tests that invisible space against the three-site relations forced by the native Hankel congruence. For two shared parameters the test is essential: it eliminates an ambiguity present for arbitrary positive metrics. For higher shared dimension the determinantal obstruction has no quadratic part. This precise mechanism, rather than a claim of exhaustive priority over every use of conormal geometry, is the centre of the paper.
