\section{From one-channel germs to finite-channel analytic table rigidity}
\label{sec:v22-multichannel-rigidity}

The preceding theorem is local in phase space but, in the analytic category,
its geometric consequence need not remain local on the table.  One recovered
contact germ determines the entire connected analytic boundary carrying that
contact.  A finite collection of labelled channels which meets every obstacle
therefore determines the whole labelled table in a fixed periodic cell.  This
provides a table-level consequence of the determinant-one mechanism without
replacing the endpoint data by a marked-length spectrum.

Fix a periodic lattice and a fundamental cell containing finitely many
labelled strictly convex obstacles
\[
                 \mathcal O_1,\ldots,\mathcal O_N.
\]
Assume each $\partial\mathcal O_r$ is a connected real-analytic embedded
curve.  A \emph{registered channel} means a selected closest-pair alternating
channel together with the labels of its two participating obstacles, the two
contact points, and the oriented contact frames in one common Euclidean
laboratory registration.  Equivalently, all signed endpoint coordinates are
reported in registrations whose Euclidean placements in the fixed cell are
known.

\begin{definition}[Spanning channel cover]
\label{def:v22-channel-cover}
A finite family $\mathscr C$ of registered channels is a spanning channel
cover if every obstacle label $r\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$ occurs as an endpoint of
at least one channel in $\mathscr C$.
\end{definition}

For a channel $e\in\mathscr C$, let
$\Pi^{e,\mathrm{end},\infty}_{00,d}$ and
$\Pi^{e,\mathrm{end},\infty}_{11,d}$ denote the two same-type signed endpoint
laws in the two orientations, and let the physical onset of that channel be
retained.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-channel analytic table rigidity]
\label{thm:v22-multichannel-table-rigidity}
Let two periodic dispersing billiard tables have the same fixed lattice, the
same finite obstacle labels, and connected real-analytic strictly convex
obstacle boundaries.  Suppose they admit the same spanning channel cover
$\mathscr C$ in one common laboratory registration.  Assume that for every
$e\in\mathscr C$ the two tables have identical channel onset and identical
signed endpoint-law germs
\[
 \bigl\{\Pi^{e,\mathrm{end},\infty}_{00,d}:0<d<d_e\bigr\},
 \qquad
 \bigl\{\Pi^{e,\mathrm{end},\infty}_{11,d}:0<d<d_e\bigr\}
\]
for some $d_e>0$.  Then every labelled obstacle boundary image agrees in the
fixed fundamental cell.  Hence the two labelled billiard tables are
identical.

More generally, if the common laboratory registration is forgotten but one
registered channel frame is fixed, the same data determine every obstacle
met by the cover up to the single Euclidean motion used to place that initial
frame.  No reflection ambiguity remains once the transverse orientation of
the initial frame is fixed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $e\in\mathscr C$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity} applies to the
two participating labelled contacts.  Equality of the channel onset gives
the gap, and equality of the two signed endpoint-law germs gives equality of
both unsymmetrized half-line actions.  The determinant-one recursion then
gives equality of every labelled graph jet at the two contact points.
Analyticity converts equality of the jets into equality of the two graph
germs.

Now take one participating obstacle, say $\mathcal O_r$.  Parametrize the two
candidate boundaries by oriented arclength from the recovered contact point
and tangent frame.  Their curvature functions are real analytic and agree on
a nonempty interval.  The identity theorem makes them agree along the entire
connected analytic boundary.  The planar Frenet equations with the same
initial point and tangent then give equality of the complete boundary images.
Thus one measured channel determines globally each of its two participating
analytic obstacle boundaries in the registered frame.

Because $\mathscr C$ is a spanning channel cover, every obstacle label occurs
in at least one measured channel.  Applying the preceding argument to one
incident channel for each label determines every obstacle boundary image in
the common laboratory cell.  The fixed lattice then determines all periodic
translates, proving equality of the tables.

If an absolute laboratory placement is not supplied, choose one channel frame
as the initial frame.  Its arbitrary placement accounts for one common
Euclidean motion.  Every subsequent recovered boundary is placed relative to
its registered channel frame; where channels share already reconstructed
labels, the registrations are compatible by hypothesis.  The fixed initial
orientation removes a global reflection ambiguity.  No stability of analytic
continuation from noisy data is claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite data architecture for global analytic recovery]
\label{cor:v22-finite-channel-global-recovery}
For a fixed analytic labelled table, any spanning channel cover supplies a
finite collection of channel families whose signed endpoint support germs and
onsets determine the entire table.  The number of channel families depends on
the chosen cover, not on the jet order: each channel germ contains the whole
all-order determinant-one hierarchy.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Relation to global orbit rigidity]
The theorem is a global geometric conclusion from a data set different from a
marked or enriched length spectrum.  Marked-length rigidity organizes global
periodic-orbit lengths; the present theorem uses finitely many labelled
channels but retains continuum signed endpoint-support germs in collision
coordinates.  Neither observation scheme is claimed to dominate the other.
The point is that the local boundary-law mechanism now has an explicit
finite-channel table-level consequence.
\end{remark}
