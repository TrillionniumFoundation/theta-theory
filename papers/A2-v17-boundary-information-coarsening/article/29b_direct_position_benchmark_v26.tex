\section{Direct geometric sampling in the same long-even experiment}
\label{sec:v26-position-benchmark}

The restriction to long even bridges does not by itself make the half-line
inverse necessary when exact planar endpoint positions are observed.
We give a comparison within the physical record space
\eqref{eq:v25-design-record-spaces}, using its charged pilot and no short
or mixed-type flight.  The comparison preserves the global reconstruction
problem; it identifies the additional content of the single-offset
\emph{transverse-law} inverse.

\begin{proposition}[Charged direct acquisition of contact jets]
\label{prop:v26-position-jets}
Fix an integer \(M\ge2\) and a contact class with uniform \(C^{M+1}\)
graph bounds and the uniform localization and flux bounds of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v25-physical-localization}.  In particular, the compact
analytic class \(\mathfrak K\) has these properties.  Fix a target
error probability \(\beta\in(0,1)\), and any prescribed even flight number
\(J\ge2\).  For every sufficiently small \(a>0\) and pilot-frame tolerance
\(\varepsilon>0\) satisfying \(\varepsilon\le c_Ma\), there is a finite
capped policy, every preparation of which uses \(J\) flights, that produces
estimates of the two graph jets with, uniformly on the class,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-interpolation-error}
 \max_{e,b}\left|\widehat\psi_{e,b}^{(m)}(0)
                   -\psi_{e,b}^{(m)}(0)\right|
 \le C_M\{a^{M+1-m}+\varepsilon a^{-m}\},\qquad 0\le m\le M,
\end{equation}
except on an event of probability at most \(\beta\).  All failed
preparations and discarded off-bin successes are charged.  No half-line
action is estimated or inverted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(M+1\) distinct fixed numbers \(\xi_i\) in a bounded interval and
pairwise disjoint closed bins \(I_i\) about them.  The bins have positive
length and a positive minimum separation.  The observed transverse bins
will be \(aI_i\).  Constants below may depend on these fixed choices and
on \(M\).

Run Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-observable-calibration} with \(j=J\), error
probability \(\beta/2\), and a resolution \(h\) such that
\[
 C\sqrt h\le\varepsilon,\qquad h\le c_Ma^2.
\]
On its good event the estimated contact frames have error at most
\(\varepsilon\), and \(J|\widehat g_e-g_e|\le h\).
The pilot is charged and its flight number is never increased afterwards.

Choose a fixed sufficiently large \(D_M\) and program
\(t_e=J\widehat g_e+D_Ma^2\).  For small \(a\) these windows lie in the
common positive-offset collar.  The finite bridge action obeys
\(E_J(u,v)\le C(u^2+v^2)\) on a common contact box.  In each target bin
restrict the true first endpoint to a smaller interior sub-bin of length
comparable to \(a\), the second endpoint to an interval of length comparable
to \(a\), and the residual time to an interior interval of length comparable
to \(a^2\).  Increasing \(D_M\) first ensures
\(r+E_J<t_e-Jg_e\) throughout these sets; taking \(a\) small afterwards
keeps them in the collar.  The frame error, after reducing \(c_M\), ensures
that each smaller true-coordinate bin lies in the corresponding observed
bin.  Positivity of the exact flux and its uniform lower bound give a
one-preparation probability at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v26-bin-probability}
                      p_M(J,a)=c_Ma^4e^{-J\gamma_+}>0
\end{equation}
for every target bin, conditional on any good pilot history.  Alternatively,
for a fixed \(J,a,M\), compactness and strict positivity alone give a
positive lower bound; no effective uniform-in-\(J\) estimate is needed for
the existential comparison.

For each \((e,b,i)\), take at most
\[
 N_M=\left\lceil p_M(J,a)^{-1}
             \log\frac{4|E|(M+1)}\beta\right\rceil
\]
fresh preparations at the stated window, stopping when the first endpoint
lies in its observed bin.  The chance of exhausting any one cap is at most
\(e^{-N_Mp_M}\).  A union bound over \(2|E|(M+1)\) bins bounds their total
failure probability by \(\beta/2\).  The post-pilot preparation cap is
\(2|E|(M+1)N_M\); success outside the target bin does not evade this count.
Every decision is a Borel function of the physical records and the pilot.

For a retained point \(X_i\), form its measured transverse coordinate
\(\widehat y_i=\widehat t_e\cdot(X_i-\widehat p_{e,b})\) and signed normal
coordinate \(\widehat z_i\) in the estimated frame, with the prescribed
facing-side sign.  The true graph representation and the frame bounds give
\[
 |\widehat z_i-\psi_{e,b}(\widehat y_i)|\le C\varepsilon.
\]
Indeed the transverse-coordinate error is \(O(\varepsilon)\), the normal
error is \(O(\varepsilon)\), and the graph has a uniform local Lipschitz
bound.  The normalized observed nodes \(\widehat y_i/a\) belong to the
fixed separated bins \(I_i\).

Let \(P_M\) interpolate the \(M+1\) measured pairs.  The scaled
Vandermonde matrix has a uniformly bounded inverse: its entries vary
continuously on the compact product of the bins, and its determinant is a
product of nonzero node differences with a uniform lower bound.  Taylor's
formula on the common \(C^{M+1}\) graph patch shows that the discrepancy
between the observations and the Taylor polynomial of degree \(M\) is at
most \(C_M(a^{M+1}+\varepsilon)\) at every node.  Inverting the scaled
matrix and converting its degree-\(m\) coefficient into a derivative gives
\[
 |P_M^{(m)}(0)-\psi_{e,b}^{(m)}(0)|
       \le C_Ma^{-m}(a^{M+1}+\varepsilon).
\]
This is \eqref{eq:v26-interpolation-error}.  Default estimates on flagged
failures make the rule defined on every transcript.  Adding pilot and bin
failures completes the proof.
\end{proof}

Taking \(\varepsilon\le a^{M+1}\) gives error \(O_M(a)\) simultaneously
through order \(M\).  The pilot can achieve this precision at finite cost
by reducing \(h\); its cost is included rather than supplied externally.
Thus, at stage \(s\), one may first choose any even \(J_s\ge s\), then
choose the geometric resolution and its pilot, all at that same \(J_s\).
The contact graph jets and gaps determine the action jets by the finite
smooth forward map.  Alternatively their exact infinite collection is
injective on the same signature-rigid analytic class.  The compact inverse
modulus, finite-template matching, and increasing-order diagonal argument
therefore give global consistency from these direct graph samples as well.
No claim of an effective analytic-continuation rate is needed for this
conclusion.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-single-offset-global} proves a different assertion.
Its successful observations retain \((u,v)\), not
\((u,\psi_b(u),v,\psi_b(v))\).  The four-density identity reconstructs the
unsymmetrized half-line actions from the distribution of those two scalar
coordinates, and the determinant-one recursion recovers the contact geometry
encoded by repeated alternating returns.  Graph interpolation has no graph
ordinates to use in that data map.  The two results are compared under their
actual observations; a diverging flight number is not offered as an argument
excluding direct interpolation in the richer position experiment.
