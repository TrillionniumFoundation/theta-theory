\section{Rank-two holonomy and recovery of the periodic lattice}
\label{sec:v24-rank-two-lattice}

The preceding intrinsic reconstruction theorem was stated relative to a marked
abstract lattice carrying a prescribed Euclidean Gram form.  The holonomy
identity in fact contains more information.  With two independent deck
cycles based in one channel frame, the Euclidean realization of the marked
lattice, including its metric and absolute scale, is determined by the
channel data.  We record that stronger form here and use it in the global
statistical theorem below.

Let \(\Lambda_{\rm top}\simeq\mathbb Z^2\) be only an oriented marked free
abelian group of rank two.  No inner product on
\(\Lambda_{\rm top}\otimes\mathbb R\) is supplied.  A periodic Euclidean
realization is an orientation-preserving linear isomorphism
\[
 L:\Lambda_{\rm top}\otimes\mathbb R\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2.
\]
It induces the Gram form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-recovered-gram}
 G_L(\eta,\zeta)=\langle L\eta,L\zeta\rangle_{\mathbb R^2}.
\end{equation}
The lifted channel label \(e=(a,b,\ell)\), with
\(\ell\in\Lambda_{\rm top}\), remains part of the marked acquisition
protocol.  Thus the deck class is known combinatorially, but neither its
Euclidean length nor its direction is supplied.

An algebraic gluing is now defined exactly as in
Section~\ref{sec:v23-intrinsic-multichannel-rigidity}, except that the unknown
lattice variable is \(L\in\mathrm{GL}^+(2,\mathbb R)\), rather than an
isometry from a pre-metrized lattice.  The incidence relation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-unmetrized-gluing}
 \tau_{-L\nu(e,\sigma)}A_eC_{e,\sigma}
 =\tau_{-L\nu(f,\sigma')}A_fC_{f,\sigma'}
\end{equation}
whenever the two incidences carry the same quotient-obstacle label.
Admissibility again means that the periodic translates form a dispersing
billiard and that the declared measured edges are the prescribed facing
channels.  Simultaneous Euclidean motion acts on the whole realization in the
usual way.

\begin{definition}[Rank-two anchoring pair]
\label{def:v24-rank-two-anchoring}
Two signature-rigid closed channel cycles \(c_1,c_2\), based at the same
oriented channel frame, form a \emph{rank-two anchoring pair} if their marked
deck displacements
\[
 \eta_i=\sum_{e\in c_i}\epsilon_e\ell_e\in\Lambda_{\rm top},
 \qquad i=1,2,
\]
are linearly independent in \(\Lambda_{\rm top}\otimes\mathbb R\).
\end{definition}

The incidence congruences are determined entirely by the recovered analytic
curve copies.  Hence, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-cycle-holonomy}, their
composition around \(c_i\) is a data-determined Euclidean motion
\(H_i=\tau_{v_i}\).  The next proposition is the metric-free version of the
cycle-holonomy identity.

\begin{proposition}[Metric-free cycle holonomy]
\label{prop:v24-metric-free-holonomy}
For realizable intrinsic data and every signature-rigid cycle \(c\) based at
an edge frame whose placement has rotational part \(R_0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-metric-free-holonomy}
 R_0v_c=L\eta_c.
\end{equation}
In particular, no Euclidean structure on the abstract deck group is used in
deriving the identity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-cycle-holonomy} uses only the deck-addition
law and Euclidean translations of the realized table.  Replacing
\(\iota(\nu)\) there by \(L\nu\) leaves the telescoping argument unchanged:
\[
 A_{e_0}H_cA_{e_0}^{-1}=\tau_{L\eta_c}.
\]
The rotational part of \(H_c\) is therefore the identity, and comparison of
translation vectors gives \eqref{eq:v24-metric-free-holonomy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Recovery of lattice shape, scale and Gram form]
\label{thm:v24-lattice-gram-recovery}
Assume the intrinsic signed channel data contain a rank-two anchoring pair
\(c_1,c_2\).  Then the data determine the full Euclidean realization of the
marked lattice relative to the common starting channel frame, up to the one
global rotation already contained in the global \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\) gauge.
More precisely, after fixing that gauge so that the starting-frame rotation is
the identity, there is a unique linear map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-L-from-holonomy}
 L:\Lambda_{\rm top}\otimes\mathbb R\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2,
 \qquad L\eta_i=v_i\quad(i=1,2).
\end{equation}
Consequently the full marked Gram matrix, including absolute scale, is the
data-determined matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v24-gram-matrix}
 [G_L]_{\mathcal B}=M_{\mathcal B}^{-T}V^TVM_{\mathcal B}^{-1},
\end{equation}
where \(M_{\mathcal B}\) has as columns the coordinates of
\(\eta_1,\eta_2\) in the marked basis \(\mathcal B\), and
\(V=(v_1\ v_2)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The vectors \(\eta_1,\eta_2\) form a basis of
\(\Lambda_{\rm top}\otimes\mathbb R\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:v24-metric-free-holonomy} therefore prescribes a unique linear map
\(L\) once the common starting-frame rotation has been fixed.  Realizability
implies that \(L\) is invertible and orientation preserving, so
\(v_1,v_2\) are linearly independent with the compatible orientation.
Formula \eqref{eq:v24-gram-matrix} is simply
\(G_L=L^TL\) written in the marked basis.  A change of the global Euclidean
gauge left-multiplies \(L\) by a rotation and hence leaves \(G_L\) unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uncalibrated intrinsic periodic rigidity]
\label{thm:v24-uncalibrated-periodic-rigidity}
Let two labelled periodic dispersing billiard tables have the same oriented
marked deck group \(\Lambda_{\rm top}\simeq\mathbb Z^2\), the same
obstacle-orbit labels and the same connected measured lifted-channel graph.
No lattice Gram form, lattice scale, lattice direction, common channel
registration or absolute contact frame is supplied.  Assume every obstacle
boundary is connected, real analytic and strictly convex, the intrinsic signed
channel data agree, the measured graph contains a rank-two anchoring pair, and
a rooted signature-rigid spanning tree reaches every obstacle orbit.
Then the complete marked periodic table and the Euclidean Gram form of its
lattice are determined by the observations up to one simultaneous global
element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The signed one-channel inverse and analytic continuation recover every
incident obstacle curve in its own channel frame.  Theorem
\ref{thm:v24-lattice-gram-recovery} fixes the complete lattice realization in
the chosen root-frame gauge.  The rooted signature-rigid spanning tree then
propagates the placements of all quotient obstacle images exactly as in
Corollary~\ref{cor:v23-signature-rigid-global}.  Thus there is at most one
algebraic gluing.  Existence is not hidden in the word ``admissible'': it is
provided by the standing hypothesis that the data are generated by an actual
periodic dispersing table.  Hence that algebraic gluing is admissible and is
the unique realization modulo the initial global Euclidean gauge.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is marked and what is recovered]
\label{rem:v24-deck-operational}
The deck label \(\ell\) is a combinatorial lift label in the marked covering
and is part of the acquisition design; its Euclidean vector is not.  Unknown
or corrupted deck-class labels constitute a different inverse problem and are
not silently inferred here.  The classification of all admissible gluings is
a bookkeeping theorem; the rigidity content is the rank-two holonomy recovery
plus signature-rigid propagation.  Theorem
\ref{thm:v24-uncalibrated-periodic-rigidity} therefore removes the metric
calibration singled out in the preceding formulation while keeping the
measurement protocol explicit.
\end{remark}
