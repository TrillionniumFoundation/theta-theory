\section{Uniform constants and the nuisance invariant}
\label{sec:uniform-constants}
We record explicit constants for the Poisson step-density estimate in
Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-TV}. They concern the same experiment and do not
replace its finite-precision protocol.
\begin{proposition}[An explicit central-region threshold]
\label{prop:poisson-constants}
Let $K\sim\operatorname{Pois}(N\lambda)$, let
$U\sim\operatorname{Unif}(-1/2,1/2)$ independently, and suppose
$0<\lambda_-\le\lambda\le\lambda_+$. Let $f_{N,\lambda}$ be the
density of $(K-N\lambda+U)/\sqrt N$, and let $\varphi_\lambda$ be
the centred Gaussian density of variance $\lambda$. Set
\[
 C_0=10(1+\lambda_-^{-1}+\lambda_-^{-2}),\qquad
 N_0=\left\lceil\max\{1,2/\lambda_-,(4/\lambda_-)^{5/2},C_0^5\}\right\rceil.
\]
For every integer $N\ge N_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-poisson-bound}
 \|f_{N,\lambda}-\varphi_\lambda\|_1
 \le (3C_0+2\lambda_++1)N^{-1/5}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $z_j=(j-N\lambda)/\sqrt N$ and
$u_j=z_j/(\sqrt N\lambda)$. If $|z_j|\le2N^{1/10}$ and
$N\ge N_0$, then $|u_j|\le1/2$ and $j\ge N\lambda_-/2\ge1$.
For $F(u)=(1+u)\log(1+u)-u$, Taylor's theorem gives
$|F(u)-u^2/2|\le(2/3)|u|^3$ on this interval.
Stirling's remainder bound $0<r_j<1/(12j)$ therefore yields
\[
 \left|\log\frac{\sqrt N\Pr(K=j)}{\varphi_\lambda(z_j)}\right|
 \le\frac{2|z_j|^3}{3\sqrt N\lambda_-^2}
       +\frac{|z_j|}{\sqrt N\lambda_-}
       +\frac1{6N\lambda_-}.
\]
Within a cell of half-width $1/(2\sqrt N)$ the change of the Gaussian
log density is at most
$|z_j|/(2\sqrt N\lambda_-)+1/(8N\lambda_-)$.
On $|z|\le N^{1/10}$ its cell centre satisfies
$|z_j|\le2N^{1/10}$; the sum of these bounds is at most
$C_0N^{-1/5}\le1$. Thus the density ratio differs from one by at
most $3C_0N^{-1/5}$, which bounds the central integral.
The second moments of the step density and the Gaussian are
$\lambda+1/(12N)$ and $\lambda$. Chebyshev's inequality bounds the
sum of their outside integrals by $(2\lambda_++1)N^{-1/5}$.
Adding proves the result.
\end{proof}
For the local exposure model, let
$H=\sup_h\|B_*h\|_\infty$ on the prescribed compact index set.
Taking also $N\ge(2H/\min_j\lambda_j^0)^2$ puts every exposure in
$[\min_j\lambda_j^0/2,\max_j\lambda_j^0+\min_j\lambda_j^0/2]$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:explicit-poisson-bound} then supplies the uniform
coordinate constants; product total variation is at most the sum of
the coordinate total variations. The covariance replacement and
quantization bounds in the preceding appendix remain unchanged.

\begin{definition}[Nuisance translation invariant]\label{def:nuisance-invariant}
In the limiting Gaussian experiment of Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary},
use the independent nuisance score and efficient observation as
coordinates. Let $\R^{k+1}$ act by translation on the nuisance score
coordinate and trivially on the efficient observation $Y$.
The invariant observation is the measurable maximal invariant of this
action, namely $Y$.
\end{definition}
Thus the phrase ``quotienting out nuisance translation'' in that theorem
means passage to this explicitly defined invariant observation.
Independence of the Gaussian blocks follows from their zero cross
covariance, and arbitrary nuisance translation removes any information
in the first block about the retained parameter for invariant
procedures. It is not an equivalence of the full experiment, in which
the nuisance itself remains an unknown parameter, to $Y$ alone.
