\section{The classical incidence surface and the new invariant}
\label{sec:classical}
This section distinguishes three geometric objects associated with the
same relation system. Let $e=4$ and let $q_i(x,y)$ denote the symmetric
bilinear polarization of $q_i(x)$, normalized by $q_i(x,x)=q_i(x)$.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bilinear-incidence}
 Z_R=\{([x],[y])\in\Pj(V^*)\times\Pj(V^*):q_i(x,y)=0\text{ for all }i\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:incidence-bridge}
For $R\in G_4^\circ$, projection on the first factor identifies
$Z_R$ with $Y_R$. Transposition of the two factors induces a fixed-point-free
involution on this K3 surface. The polarization recovered in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-reconstruction} is the first projection's quartic
polarization.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The bilinear equations at $x$ are $J_R(x)y=0$, up to the common
nonzero factor two. Off $Y_R$ there is no projective solution; on
$Y_R$ the matrix has rank exactly three because $Y_R$ is smooth.
Inverting a nonzero three-minor gives a unique kernel line, with its
scheme structure, and these kernel lines glue to an inverse for the
projection $Z_R\to Y_R$. The equations are symmetric in $x,y$,
so transposition acts. A fixed point would have $[x]=[y]$ and hence
$q_i(x)=0$ for every $i$, contrary to basepoint-freeness. The final
assertion follows from the first projection and its hyperplane bundle.
\end{proof}

The discriminant of the web is a different embedded hypersurface. If
$q_i(x)=x^tA_i x$, its equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discriminant-distinction}
 \det\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^3\lambda_i A_i\Bigr)=0
       \quad\text{in }\Pj(R),
\end{equation}
whereas the ramification equation is $\det J_R(x)=0$ in
$\Pj(V^*)$. The former varies the quadric in the web; the latter
varies a point of the source of the quadratic morphism. The two
determinants must not be identified simply because both have degree
four. The classical construction recalled in \cite[\S4]{IK} relates
the bilinear K3 surface to the resolution of the web's symmetroid and
to a nodal Enriques quotient; see also the account of Reye congruences
in \cite{DK}. Proposition~\ref{prop:incidence-bridge} places our
ramification surface in that construction by an explicit kernel-line
isomorphism. No claim is made that the existence of that K3 surface,
its involution, or webs of quadrics is new.

The invariant used here starts instead with the abstract
multiplication-failure scheme. It obtains $E$ as the annihilator of
its nilradical, constructs
$\omega_E\otimes\NN^{-9}$ without the web or either projective
space, and recovers the section ring of $\OO_{Y_R}(4)$.
The corank-two theorem further recovers an additional associated
support from the square of the nilradical. These are statements about
nonreduced multiplication-failure schemes, not merely about the
classical incidence surface or the discriminant cycle.
