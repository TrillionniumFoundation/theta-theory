\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

This paper studies an inverse problem in which the observable objects are not
periodic lengths but physical laws attached to long alternating collision
bridges in a periodic dispersing billiard.  The small mixed twist of such a
bridge is exponentially weak in its flight number.  After normalization by
that exact twist, however, the bridge retains a nonlinear boundary profile.
Our first result is a relative limit on a fixed endpoint box and a positive
offset collar independent of the flight number.  Its two half-line actions
encode the signed contact geometry, while its two positive determinant
amplitudes are the scalar linearizing densities of the physical stable
returns.

The paper has two main theorems.  The deterministic theorem reconstructs an
analytic periodic table, including the metric of its period lattice, from
unregistered signed channel laws.  The statistical theorem constructs one
sequence of actual finite-bridge acquisition procedures which reconstructs
the table uniformly on a quantitatively nondegenerate compact analytic class.
The local Poisson and Gaussian experiments developed between these two
results describe the information lost under successive coarsenings of the
physical transcript; they are not assumptions in the global consistency
theorem.

\subsection{The deterministic theorem}

A measured oriented channel is labelled by
\[
                         e=(a,b,\ell),
 \qquad \ell\in\Lambda_{\rm top}\simeq\mathbb Z^2,
\]
where \(a,b\) are quotient-obstacle labels and \(\ell\) is the marked deck
class.  The deck class is a combinatorial lift label in the acquisition
protocol.  Its Euclidean vector, length and direction are not supplied.  A
channel record is expressed only in its own oriented channel frame and
retains the physical onset and the two signed endpoint-law germs.  No common
laboratory placement of different channel frames is part of the data.

A signature-rigid cycle is a closed sequence of measured channels for which
the required incidences on each repeated analytic obstacle are determined by
the curvature signature.  Composition of the incidence congruences gives a
data-determined translation vector \(v_c\), while the marked deck labels give
a deck displacement \(\eta_c\in\Lambda_{\rm top}\).  Two cycles based in one
channel frame form a rank-two anchoring pair when their deck displacements are
linearly independent.

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic flagship theorem]
\label{thm:intro-deterministic-flagship}
Let a labelled periodic dispersing billiard in the plane have connected,
real-analytic, strictly convex obstacle boundaries.  Assume that a connected
measured lifted-channel graph contains a rank-two signature-rigid anchoring
pair and, from an obstacle met by that pair, a rooted signature-rigid spanning
tree reaching every quotient obstacle.  Then the intrinsic signed channel
laws determine
\begin{enumerate}
\item the two complete labelled contact jets on every measured channel;
\item the complete analytic boundary of every incident obstacle lift;
\item the Euclidean realization of the marked deck group, hence the full
period-lattice Gram form including shape and absolute scale; and
\item the placement of every labelled quotient obstacle and every measured
channel frame.
\end{enumerate}
The complete marked periodic table is therefore determined up to one
simultaneous global element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).  No period-lattice metric,
common inter-channel registration, absolute contact point or absolute tangent
frame is supplied.
\end{theorem}

The proof has three rigid steps.  First, signed support recovers separately
the two half-line actions.  If
\(a_b=S_b''(0)\), the physical onset and quadratic action jets give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v23-leading}
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}),
 \qquad
 c_0=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_0/a_1},
 \qquad
 c_1=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_1/a_0},
 \qquad
 \kappa_b=\frac{c_b-1}{g}.
\end{equation}
At each order \(n\ge3\), after lower graph jets have been fixed, the next
labelled pair \(q_n=(q_{0,n},q_{1,n})^t\) enters the next action-jet pair
through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-jet-block}
 D_{q_n}s_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1=1,
 \qquad \det D_{q_n}s_n=1.
\end{equation}
The determinant-one block is not a formal Taylor inversion: it follows from a
weighted inverse for the nonlinear half-line stationarity operator, a
finite-truncation envelope identity and a homogeneous action-jet filtration.
Thus odd and even labelled contact jets are recovered without reflection
symmetry, equal contacts or supplied curvatures.

Second, exact analytic continuation globalizes each recovered contact germ to
its connected analytic obstacle boundary.  This is an exact-data rigidity
statement; no numerical stability of unrestricted analytic continuation is
claimed.

Third, the channel copies are glued intrinsically.  If \(L\) denotes the
unknown Euclidean realization of the marked deck group and \(c_i\) are the
two anchoring cycles, their holonomies satisfy
\[
                         L\eta_i=v_i,
                         \qquad i=1,2,
\]
after fixing the root-frame rotation by the global gauge.  Since
\(\eta_1,\eta_2\) are independent, these two equations determine the entire
linear map \(L\).  Hence the lattice Gram form is \(L^TL\) and is observed,
not calibrated.  The signature-rigid spanning tree then propagates the
remaining obstacle placements.  The formal gluing classification records all
compatible realizations; the rigidity content is the rank-two holonomy and
the unique signature propagation.

\subsection{The statistical theorem}

The global statistical statement is made on an explicit robust analytic
class.  We fix the measured graph and its rank-two anchoring pair and spanning
tree, a common holomorphic collar and bound for the analytic curvatures,
uniform positive curvature and obstacle separation, a positive margin
preventing the declared closest-pair channels from switching, a positive
finite-signature separation at every incidence used for gluing, and a lower
singular-value bound for the two observed holonomy vectors.  This defines a
compact class \(\mathfrak K\) modulo one global Euclidean motion.  The
complete intrinsic data map is injective on \(\mathfrak K\), and its inverse
has a uniform modulus \(\omega_q\) in every fixed labelled \(C^q\) metric.
The compact class is essential: exact analytic continuation by itself is not a
stable reconstruction algorithm.

The physical procedure is also explicit at the level relevant for
implementability.  It uses only the fixed marked channel labels, predetermined
laboratory timing grids, charged onset-centering pilots, finite positive
offset lists, success stopping targets and deterministic preparation caps.
After a pilot, later times may be measurable functions of the retained pilot
transcript.  Unknown gaps and unknown local charts are never supplied to the
policy.  Every failure, waiting count, successful endpoint--time record and
stopping time remains part of the complete transcript.

\begin{theorem}[Statistical flagship theorem]
\label{thm:intro-statistical-flagship}
On every uniformly nondegenerate compact analytic class \(\mathfrak K\) as
above, there exist a deterministic sequence of finite, implementable,
deterministically capped finite-bridge acquisition policies \(\pi_s\), jet
orders \(M_s\uparrow\infty\), and measurable estimators
\(\widehat T_s\in\mathfrak K\) such that for every fixed \(q\ge0\) and every
\(\varepsilon>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-global-consistency}
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}
 \Pr_T^{\pi_s}\{d_q(\widehat T_s,T)>\varepsilon\}
                         \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The procedure is based on the actual finite-bridge experiment.  At each stage
its finite design list, success allocation, flight numbers and caps are chosen
before the post-pilot observations, and the total finite-to-boundary
comparison error tends to zero.  No uniform-in-order inverse condition number
is assumed.
\end{theorem}

The proof first fixes \(M\).  The tangent inverse and compactness of the unit
tangent bundle give a finite positive design common to all
\(T\in\mathfrak K\).  More globally, compactness of the set of pairs whose
finite data differ by a prescribed amount gives finitely many centered
boundary laws and bounded test functions which separate every such pair.  A
charged physical pilot locates each channel onset uniformly, after which these
centered designs become implementable adaptive laboratory windows.  Empirical
separator means are uniformly consistent.  The stopped physical-to-boundary
coupling controls the complete post-pilot transcript by a success-weighted
error of the form
\[
                         C L k\tau^J.
\]
Choosing the common flight number \(J\) after the finite design size \(L\) and
success target \(k\) are known makes this error arbitrarily small.  This gives,
for every fixed \(M\), a uniform estimator of the finite action-jet vector
from actual finite bridges.  Finally one chooses
\(M_s,L_s,k_s,J_s\) diagonally so that the finite-data inverse modulus and the
physical comparison error both vanish.  The theorem proves consistency, not a
sharp analytic minimax rate.

\subsection{The three local information levels}

The global consistency theorem does not identify the information geometry of
a fixed finite-dimensional local model.  For that purpose we keep three
nested observation sigma-fields distinct.

At the richest level treated here, a successful preparation retains signed
endpoints and residual preparation-to-first-impact time; failures, designs,
waiting sequence and stopping time are retained in the stopped transcript.
The conditional successful boundary density at design \(\ell\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-et-density}
 q_{\ell,0}(u,v,r)=
 \rho_\ell(u,v)\mathbf1_{\{0<r<w_\ell(u,v)\}},
 \qquad
 w_\ell=d_\ell-S_{b_\ell}(u)-S_{b_\ell}(v).
\end{equation}
The density has a positive trace at the moving ceiling.  With \(k_n\)
successful records, shape perturbations of order \(k_n^{-1}\) and gap
perturbations of order \((j_nk_n)^{-1}\) produce a generally non-dominated
Poisson boundary-shift experiment.  The proof is in Le Cam distance: a
parameter-independent forward kernel extracts a fixed reference ceiling
layer, while a reverse kernel reconstructs the sample from the Poisson layer
and the reference bulk conditional law.  The joint layer count, locations and
bulk are controlled uniformly, including the moving-support remainder.
Under \(j_n=o(\sqrt{k_n})\) the waiting count is ancillary at this fastest
scale, and the complete stopped endpoint--time experiment transfers to
finite bridges when \(k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0\).

After residual time is discarded but waiting counts are retained, two slower
Gaussian channels remain.  Intrinsically, if \(V\) is the finite shape
tangent space and \(\lambda=d\gamma\), then
\[
                         H=\ker\lambda,
 \qquad N=V/H.
\]
The count information lives on the one-dimensional normal quotient \(N\),
where \([v]\mapsto|\lambda(v)|^2\), while the endpoint information is the
restriction of the boundary quadratic form to
\(\mathbb R_{\rm gap}\oplus H\).  Formula
\eqref{eq:intro-v23-leading} implies
\[
 \frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\kappa_0}
 =\frac{gc_1}{2\sqrt{c_0c_1}\sinh\gamma}>0,
 \qquad
 \frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\kappa_1}
 =\frac{gc_0}{2\sqrt{c_0c_1}\sinh\gamma}>0,
\]
so this quotient is genuinely one dimensional.  Choosing a vector
\(v_\gamma\) with \(d\gamma(v_\gamma)=1\) is only a coordinate splitting; the
product experiment is invariant under changing that representative or
reparameterizing the finite jet model.

Discarding waiting counts as well gives the endpoint-only moving-boundary
Gaussian experiment.  Its intrinsic information is the boundary quadratic
form
\[
 \mathcal J_\Sigma=
 \int_\Sigma\frac{a_0VV^t}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma.
\]
These Poisson, count--endpoint Gaussian and endpoint-only Gaussian limits are
limits of different sigma-fields.  None is a relabelling of another, and no
claim is made that the stopped endpoint--time transcript exhausts the still
richer full growing collision array.

\subsection{Organization of the proof}

The first part proves the relative boundary law, its differentiated uniform
version and the all-order signed contact inverse, then globalizes the germs and
establishes intrinsic holonomy rigidity, rank-two lattice recovery and the
uniform gluing modulus.  The second part analyzes the three physical local
experiments, proves the two-sided Poisson deficiency bound and the intrinsic
normal--tangent form of the count--endpoint limit.  The final section builds
the common finite physical acquisition atlas and proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-statistical-flagship}.  Auxiliary transfer,
regularization and comparison arguments are collected after the principal
theorem chain.
