\section{Intrinsic channel graphs and analytic table rigidity}
\label{sec:v23-intrinsic-multichannel-rigidity}

The signed one-channel inverse is formulated in a channel-adapted oriented
frame.  A global inverse theorem should therefore distinguish geometry that is
contained in the channel data from geometry that is supplied by an external
laboratory registration.  We do so here.  No common Euclidean placement of
distinct measured channels is assumed.

Fix an abstract rank-two periodic lattice and finitely many labelled connected
real-analytic strictly convex obstacles in one fundamental quotient.  When an
absolute laboratory registration is not part of the data, the lattice and all
obstacle images are acted on simultaneously by \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\); this is
the single unavoidable global gauge.  The \emph{measured channel graph} is the
graph \(G=(V,E)\), with \(V=\{1,\ldots,N\}\), whose edge \(e=(r,s)\) is a
selected closest-pair alternating channel joining the labelled obstacles
\(r\) and \(s\).  Multiple edges are allowed.  Each edge is oriented once and
for all.

\begin{definition}[Intrinsic signed channel datum]
\label{def:v23-intrinsic-channel-datum}
For an oriented channel \(e=(r,s)\), its intrinsic datum consists of
\begin{enumerate}
\item the ordered obstacle labels \((r,s)\);
\item the physical onset \(g_e\);
\item the two orientations of the same-type signed endpoint-law germs
\[
 \{\Pi^{e,\mathrm{end},\infty}_{00,d}:0<d<d_e\},\qquad
 \{\Pi^{e,\mathrm{end},\infty}_{11,d}:0<d<d_e\};
\]
\item the sign convention for the transverse endpoint coordinate.
\end{enumerate}
The datum is recorded in the channel-adapted frame in which the first contact
is \((0,0)\), the second contact is \((g_e,0)\), and the two oriented tangent
axes are the transverse axes used by the signed endpoint laws.  Frames
belonging to distinct edges are \emph{not} supplied with relative Euclidean
placements.
\end{definition}

Thus each edge carries the relative placement of its own two contact frames,
as part of the channel-adapted geometry, but there is no externally registered
map between two unrelated edge frames.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity}, the intrinsic datum of \(e=(r,s)\)
recovers two complete labelled analytic boundary images in the edge frame,
which we denote by
\[
                C_{e,r},\qquad C_{e,s}.
\]
Each image carries its distinguished channel contact and oriented tangent
frame.  Different incident edges recover different framed copies of the same
labelled obstacle.  The remaining global inverse problem is precisely to glue
those copies.

\subsection{Analytic signatures and the only local gluing ambiguity}

Let \(C\) be a connected oriented real-analytic strictly convex closed curve,
parametrized by oriented arclength \(s\in\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z\), with signed
curvature \(\kappa_C(s)>0\).  At \(p=C(s_0)\) define its oriented analytic
curvature signature by
\[
 \mathfrak j_C(p)
 =\bigl(\kappa_C(s_0),\kappa_C'(s_0),\kappa_C''(s_0),\ldots\bigr).
\]
Let \(\mathrm{Sym}^+(C)\subset\mathrm{SE}(2)\) be the orientation-preserving
Euclidean symmetry group of the boundary image.

\begin{lemma}[Coincident analytic signatures are exactly symmetry orbits]
\label{lem:v23-signature-symmetry}
Let \(p,q\in C\), with their oriented tangent frames.  Then
\[
                    \mathfrak j_C(p)=\mathfrak j_C(q)
\]
if and only if an element of \(\mathrm{Sym}^+(C)\) carries the framed point
\(p\) to the framed point \(q\).

Consequently, once one framed copy of \(C\) has been placed in the plane, an
independently recovered analytic contact germ of the same labelled obstacle
locates its contact point on the placed curve uniquely modulo
\(\mathrm{Sym}^+(C)\).  In particular the location is unique whenever that
signature has trivial stabilizer on the oriented curve.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reverse implication is immediate.  For the forward implication choose
arclength so that \(p=C(0)\) and \(q=C(a)\).  Equality of the complete
curvature jets gives equality of the two real-analytic germs
\(\kappa_C(s)\) and \(\kappa_C(s+a)\).  The identity theorem gives
\[
                       \kappa_C(s)=\kappa_C(s+a)
                       \qquad\text{for every }s.
\]
Let \(A\in\mathrm{SE}(2)\) be the unique orientation-preserving Euclidean
motion sending the point and oriented tangent frame at \(0\) to those at
\(a\).  The planar Frenet system
\[
 X'=T,\qquad T'=\kappa N,\qquad N'=-\kappa T
\]
has unique solutions for prescribed initial framed point.  The displayed
curvature identity therefore gives \(AC(s)=C(s+a)\) for all \(s\).  Hence
\(A\in\mathrm{Sym}^+(C)\).
\end{proof}

The lemma identifies the obstruction rather than hiding it in a registration
hypothesis.  A circle genuinely has a continuous phase ambiguity; a curve
with a nontrivial cyclic symmetry has the corresponding finite ambiguity.
Those ambiguities cannot be removed from intrinsic one-contact data by
notation.

\subsection{The intrinsic gluing space}

For every edge \(e\), let \(F_e\) denote its recovered channel frame.  A
\emph{gluing} of the intrinsic data is a family
\[
                         (A_e)_{e\in E},\qquad A_e\in\mathrm{SE}(2),
\]
such that for every obstacle label \(r\) and every two incident edges
\(e,f\ni r\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-gluing-constraint}
                         A_e C_{e,r}=A_f C_{f,r}
\end{equation}
as oriented labelled boundary images.  The same maps place the abstract
periodic lattice; equivalently one may fix one lattice frame after taking the
quotient below.  Two gluings are equivalent if all \(A_e\) are left-composed
by the same element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).  Denote the quotient set by
\(\mathfrak G(\mathscr D)\), where \(\mathscr D\) is the collection of
intrinsic channel data.

The set \(\mathfrak G(\mathscr D)\) is determined by the observed data: the
curves \(C_{e,r}\) are recovered by the all-order inverse, and all transition
sets in \eqref{eq:v23-gluing-constraint} are determined by analytic curve
matching.  For a realizable data set it is nonempty.  Its residual ambiguity
is exactly the symmetry/holonomy ambiguity produced by matching the same
labelled analytic obstacle through different incident edges.

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic finite-channel reconstruction and holonomy
classification]
\label{thm:v23-intrinsic-table-rigidity}
Let two labelled periodic dispersing billiard tables have congruent periodic
lattices and the same connected measured channel graph \(G\).  Assume all
obstacle boundaries are connected, real analytic and strictly convex.  Suppose
the two tables have identical intrinsic signed channel data in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:v23-intrinsic-channel-datum} for every edge of \(G\).
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every edge and every incident obstacle, the complete analytic
boundary image is recovered in that edge's own frame;
\item the intrinsic data determine the gluing space
\(\mathfrak G(\mathscr D)\) without any common laboratory registration;
\item labelled table--lattice pairs realizing \(\mathscr D\), modulo one
simultaneous global orientation-preserving Euclidean motion, are in bijection
with \(\mathfrak G(\mathscr D)\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular, if \(\mathfrak G(\mathscr D)\) is a singleton, the intrinsic
channel data determine the complete labelled periodic table up to one global
element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).

If the sign of every transverse coordinate is simultaneously forgotten, the
last quotient is by \(\mathrm E(2)\) and one global reflection is unavoidable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity} followed by
analytic continuation and uniqueness for the Frenet equations.  Hence all
objects entering \eqref{eq:v23-gluing-constraint} are data-determined.

A realization of the data in a common Euclidean plane assigns to each edge
frame its actual placement; these placements satisfy
\eqref{eq:v23-gluing-constraint}.  Changing the ambient point and oriented
orthonormal frame by one common element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\), simultaneously
for the periodic lattice and all obstacles, changes no intrinsic datum.  Thus
a realization determines a class in \(\mathfrak G(\mathscr D)\).

Conversely take a gluing \((A_e)\).  For a fixed obstacle label \(r\),
constraint \eqref{eq:v23-gluing-constraint} makes
\(A_eC_{e,r}\) independent of the chosen incident edge.  Call the resulting
boundary image \(C_r\).  Every measured edge \(e=(r,s)\) was reconstructed in
a channel frame with its two distinguished contacts separated by the observed
onset \(g_e\) and with the observed oriented transverse convention.  Applying
\(A_e\) therefore places \(C_r\) and \(C_s\) with exactly that channel
geometry.  Since the datum is realizable by a periodic dispersing table, the
resulting family has the prescribed disjointness and periodic incidence.  A
choice of one global frame places the congruence class of the lattice.  Thus a
gluing reconstructs a labelled table--lattice pair.  Equivalent gluings differ
by one global Euclidean motion and give the same class.  The two constructions
are inverse.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Signature-rigid spanning tree]
\label{def:v23-signature-rigid-tree}
A connected measured channel graph has a signature-rigid spanning tree if its
tree edges can be ordered \(e_1,\ldots,e_{N-1}\) so that \(e_1\) places two
vertices and, for every \(m\ge2\), the edge \(e_m\) joins one already placed
obstacle to one new obstacle, while the contact of \(e_m\) on the already
placed obstacle has an oriented analytic curvature signature occurring at a
single framed point of that obstacle.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Intrinsic analytic table rigidity from a connected channel
network]
\label{cor:v23-signature-rigid-global}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-intrinsic-table-rigidity}, assume
the measured channel graph contains a signature-rigid spanning tree.  Then the
intrinsic signed channel data determine the entire labelled periodic billiard
table, together with its lattice congruence class, up to one simultaneous
global element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).  No absolute contact point, no absolute
tangent frame, and no relative Euclidean placement between distinct channels
is supplied in advance.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Place the first channel frame arbitrarily; this uses the single global
\(\mathrm{SE}(2)\) freedom and places the two incident analytic obstacles.
Suppose the first \(m-1\) tree edges have been placed.  Edge \(e_m\) has one
contact on an already placed obstacle.  Its recovered contact germ has, by
assumption and Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-signature-symmetry}, a unique matching framed
point on that placed boundary.  There is therefore a unique
orientation-preserving Euclidean motion placing the frame of \(e_m\).  The
known within-channel gap and oriented frame relation then places the new
obstacle.  Induction places every vertex.  Hence the gluing space is a
singleton.  The lattice is then placed by the same single global choice.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Genericity and cycles]
\label{rem:v23-generic-holonomy}
The signature-rigid hypothesis is sufficient, not necessary.  If an obstacle
has a finite symmetry group, additional incident channels and cycle
constraints can eliminate the individual symmetry choices.  The general
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-intrinsic-table-rigidity} records this through the gluing
space \(\mathfrak G(\mathscr D)\).  The correct obstruction is a finite or
compact symmetry holonomy, not the absence of a preassigned laboratory frame.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Registered recovery as a special case]
\label{cor:v23-registered-special-case}
If the relative Euclidean placement of every measured channel frame is
supplied externally, then the gluing is fixed a priori.  A channel family
meeting every obstacle label therefore determines the whole analytic table in
that registration, recovering the registered v22 theorem as a special case.
\end{corollary}
