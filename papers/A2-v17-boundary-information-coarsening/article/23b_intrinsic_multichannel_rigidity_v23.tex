\section{Intrinsic lifted-channel graphs and analytic table rigidity}
\label{sec:v23-intrinsic-multichannel-rigidity}

The signed one-channel inverse is formulated in a channel-adapted oriented
frame.  In a periodic billiard, however, a channel also carries a deck
translation.  A global inverse theorem must therefore reconstruct not only the
relative placement of distinct channel frames but also their placement
relative to the periodic lattice.  We make both issues explicit.

Let \(\Lambda_{\rm abs}\simeq\mathbb Z^2\) be a marked abstract lattice with
its fixed Euclidean Gram form.  An oriented Euclidean realization is an
orientation-preserving linear isometry
\[
 \iota:\Lambda_{\rm abs}\otimes\mathbb R\longrightarrow\mathbb R^2.
\]
Changing \(\iota\) by a common planar rotation and translating all obstacle
images is the unavoidable global Euclidean gauge.  The quotient obstacle
orbits are labelled \(1,\ldots,N\).

An oriented measured channel is indexed exactly as in
Section~\ref{sec:v18-formal-setup}:
\[
                         e=(a_e,b_e,\ell_e),
 \qquad \ell_e\in\Lambda_{\rm abs},
\]
and joins a lift of obstacle \(a_e\) to the lift of obstacle \(b_e\) translated
by \(\ell_e\).  Its two incidences are denoted \((e,-)\) and \((e,+)\), with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-incidence-labels}
 r(e,-)=a_e,\quad \nu(e,-)=0,
 \qquad
 r(e,+)=b_e,\quad \nu(e,+)=\ell_e.
\end{equation}
Reversing the channel replaces \((a,b,\ell)\) by
\((b,a,-\ell)\).

\begin{definition}[Intrinsic signed lifted-channel datum]
\label{def:v23-intrinsic-channel-datum}
For an oriented channel \(e=(a,b,\ell)\), its intrinsic datum consists of
\begin{enumerate}
\item the lifted channel label \((a,b,\ell)\) in the marked abstract lattice;
\item the physical onset \(g_e\);
\item the two orientations of the same-type signed endpoint-law germs
\[
 \{\Pi^{e,\mathrm{end},\infty}_{00,d}:0<d<d_e\},\qquad
 \{\Pi^{e,\mathrm{end},\infty}_{11,d}:0<d<d_e\};
\]
\item the sign convention for the transverse endpoint coordinate.
\end{enumerate}
The law is recorded in the channel-adapted frame in which the first contact is
\((0,0)\), the second contact is \((g_e,0)\), and the oriented transverse axes
are those used by the signed endpoint coordinates.  No Euclidean placement of
this frame relative to the lattice or to any other measured channel is
supplied.
\end{definition}

Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-analytic-germ-globalization} recover from the datum two
complete oriented analytic boundary images in the edge frame,
\[
                         C_{e,-},\qquad C_{e,+}.
\]
They represent the lifted boundaries at deck offsets \(0\) and \(\ell_e\),
respectively.  The remaining inverse problem is a periodic Euclidean gluing
problem.

\subsection{Analytic signatures and local matching}

Let \(C\) be a connected oriented real-analytic strictly convex closed curve,
parametrized by oriented arclength \(s\in\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z\), with signed
curvature \(\kappa_C(s)>0\).  At \(p=C(s_0)\) define the oriented analytic
curvature signature
\[
 \mathfrak j_C(p)=
 \bigl(\kappa_C(s_0),\kappa_C'(s_0),\kappa_C''(s_0),\ldots\bigr).
\]
Let \(\mathrm{Sym}^+(C)\subset\mathrm{SE}(2)\) be the
orientation-preserving Euclidean symmetry group of the boundary image.

\begin{lemma}[Coincident analytic signatures are exactly symmetry orbits]
\label{lem:v23-signature-symmetry}
Let \(p,q\in C\), with their oriented tangent frames.  Then
\[
                         \mathfrak j_C(p)=\mathfrak j_C(q)
\]
if and only if an element of \(\mathrm{Sym}^+(C)\) carries the framed point
\(p\) to the framed point \(q\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reverse implication is immediate.  For the forward implication choose
arclength so that \(p=C(0)\) and \(q=C(a)\).  Equality of the complete
curvature jets gives equality of the real-analytic germs
\(\kappa_C(s)\) and \(\kappa_C(s+a)\).  The identity theorem gives
\(\kappa_C(s)=\kappa_C(s+a)\) for every \(s\).  Let
\(A\in\mathrm{SE}(2)\) send the oriented point--tangent frame at \(0\) to
that at \(a\).  Uniqueness for the planar Frenet system then gives
\(AC(s)=C(s+a)\) for all \(s\), so \(A\in\mathrm{Sym}^+(C)\).
\end{proof}

Thus a circle has a genuine continuous phase ambiguity and a cyclically
symmetric analytic obstacle may have a finite one.  The global theorem below
records these ambiguities instead of eliminating them by a registration
convention.

\subsection{Periodic gluing and lattice holonomy}

Let \(\tau_v(x)=x+v\).  A \emph{periodic gluing} of the intrinsic channel data
is a pair
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-periodic-gluing}
 \left(\iota,(A_e)_{e\in E}\right),
 \qquad
 \iota:\Lambda_{\rm abs}\otimes\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^2,
 \qquad A_e\in\mathrm{SE}(2),
\end{equation}
where \(\iota\) is an oriented Euclidean realization of the marked lattice,
and such that whenever two incidences \((e,\sigma)\) and
\((f,\sigma')\) carry the same quotient obstacle label,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-periodic-gluing-constraint}
 \tau_{-\iota(\nu(e,\sigma))}
       A_e C_{e,\sigma}
 =
 \tau_{-\iota(\nu(f,\sigma'))}
       A_f C_{f,\sigma'}
\end{equation}
as oriented labelled boundary images.  In other words, after each recovered
lift is translated back by its deck vector, all copies of the same quotient
obstacle must coincide.

The group \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\) acts on a gluing by
\[
 B=(R,t):\quad
 \iota\mapsto R\iota,
 \qquad A_e\mapsto B\circ A_e.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:v23-periodic-gluing-constraint} is invariant under this
action.  Let \(\mathfrak G_{\rm per}(\mathscr D)\) be the quotient of the set
of periodic gluings by this common action.

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic periodic reconstruction and holonomy classification]
\label{thm:v23-intrinsic-table-rigidity}
Let two labelled periodic dispersing billiard tables have the same marked
abstract Euclidean lattice, the same finite obstacle-orbit labels, and the same
connected measured lifted-channel graph.  Assume all obstacle boundaries are
connected, real analytic and strictly convex.  Suppose the two tables have
identical intrinsic signed lifted-channel data in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:v23-intrinsic-channel-datum} for every measured edge.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every incident lifted obstacle boundary is recovered as a complete
analytic image in its own channel frame;
\item the data determine the periodic gluing space
\(\mathfrak G_{\rm per}(\mathscr D)\) without a common laboratory registration;
\item labelled periodic tables realizing the data, modulo one simultaneous
orientation-preserving Euclidean motion of the lattice and all obstacle
images, are in bijection with
\(\mathfrak G_{\rm per}(\mathscr D)\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the intrinsic channel data determine the complete labelled
periodic table up to one global \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\) motion whenever
\(\mathfrak G_{\rm per}(\mathscr D)\) is a singleton.

If every transverse sign is simultaneously forgotten, the corresponding
statement is modulo \(\mathrm E(2)\), and one global reflection is
unavoidable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-analytic-germ-globalization}.  Hence every curve entering
\eqref{eq:v23-periodic-gluing-constraint} is determined by the observations.

A realization of the periodic table supplies an oriented Euclidean realization
\(\iota\) of its marked lattice and a placement \(A_e\) of every measured
channel frame.  The incidence \((e,\sigma)\) lies on the lift
\(D_{r(e,\sigma)}+\iota(\nu(e,\sigma))\), so translating by the negative deck
vector gives the same quotient obstacle image for every incidence with that
label.  Thus every realization defines a periodic gluing.  Simultaneously
moving the lattice and table by one element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\) changes the
gluing only within its quotient class.

Conversely let \((\iota,(A_e))\) satisfy
\eqref{eq:v23-periodic-gluing-constraint}.  For a quotient label \(r\), define
\[
 C_r=	au_{-\iota(\nu(e,\sigma))}A_eC_{e,\sigma}
\]
using any incidence with \(r(e,\sigma)=r\).  The gluing constraint makes this
independent of the choice.  Its periodic lifts are
\(C_r+\iota(\lambda)\), \(\lambda\in\Lambda_{\rm abs}\).  For a measured
edge \(e=(a,b,\ell)\), the two lifted images in the placed edge frame are
exactly \(C_a\) and \(C_b+\iota(\ell)\), with their observed onset, contact
frames and signed transverse convention.  Since the datum is assumed
realizable by a dispersing table, disjointness, convexity and the prescribed
channel incidence are inherited.  Equivalent gluings give globally congruent
periodic tables.  The two constructions are inverse.
\end{proof}

The theorem is an exact classification, including periodic deck information.
We next give a concrete sufficient condition for the gluing space to be a
singleton.

\subsection{Signature-rigid propagation and a lattice-anchoring cycle}

Suppose an incidence of a measured edge on an already reconstructed obstacle
has a curvature signature occurring at a unique framed point of that obstacle.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v23-signature-symmetry} then determines the relative
orientation-preserving Euclidean frame change uniquely.  Such transitions can
be composed along the measured channel graph.

\begin{definition}[Signature-rigid spanning tree]
\label{def:v23-signature-rigid-tree}
A spanning tree of the quotient obstacle graph is \emph{signature-rigid} if
its edges can be ordered \(e_1,\ldots,e_{N-1}\) so that \(e_1\) meets the first
two vertices and, for every \(m\ge2\), the edge \(e_m\) joins one already
reached obstacle orbit to one new orbit, with the attaching contact on the
already reached orbit having a curvature signature occurring at a unique
framed point of that obstacle.
\end{definition}

A closed oriented channel cycle
\[
                         c=e_1^{\epsilon_1}\cdots e_m^{\epsilon_m},
 \qquad \epsilon_i\in\{\pm1\},
\]
has total deck displacement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-cycle-deck}
                         \eta_c=\sum_{i=1}^m\epsilon_i\ell_{e_i}
                         \in\Lambda_{\rm abs}.
\end{equation}
When every transition along the cycle is signature-rigid, the corresponding
unique frame changes can be composed in the starting channel frame.  The
result has identity rotational part and is a translation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-cycle-observed-translation}
                         x\longmapsto x+v_c.
\end{equation}
For realizable data, a periodic gluing satisfies, after conjugating by the
starting frame,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-cycle-holonomy}
                         \iota(\eta_c)=v_c.
\end{equation}
Here we have used the global \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\) gauge to identify the starting
channel frame with the plane; in another gauge both sides are rotated by the
same rotation.

\begin{definition}[Lattice-anchoring cycle]
\label{def:v23-lattice-anchoring-cycle}
A signature-rigid cycle is \emph{lattice-anchoring} if
\(\eta_c\ne0\).  Equivalently, its observed translation
\(v_c\) is the nonzero Euclidean realization of a nonzero marked deck vector.
Because the lattice Gram form is fixed, one nonzero identity
\eqref{eq:v23-cycle-holonomy} determines the orientation-preserving lattice
realization relative to the starting channel frame.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Fully intrinsic periodic table rigidity]
\label{cor:v23-signature-rigid-global}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-intrinsic-table-rigidity}, assume
that the measured network contains a signature-rigid spanning tree and a
signature-rigid lattice-anchoring cycle.  Then
\(\mathfrak G_{\rm per}(\mathscr D)\) is a singleton.  Hence the intrinsic
signed lifted-channel data determine the complete labelled periodic billiard
table up to one simultaneous global element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).  No
absolute contact point, absolute tangent frame, inter-channel Euclidean
placement, or lattice orientation relative to a channel frame is supplied in
advance.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the global Euclidean gauge to place the starting frame of the anchoring
cycle.  Equation \eqref{eq:v23-cycle-holonomy} with \(\eta_c\ne0\) fixes the
orientation-preserving realization \(\iota\) of the marked Euclidean lattice:
an orientation-preserving plane isometry is determined by the image of one
nonzero vector of prescribed length.  With \(\iota\) fixed, place the first
tree edge.  At every subsequent tree edge, the unique curvature signature on
the already placed quotient obstacle fixes the edge-frame transform.  The
deck correction \(\iota(\ell_e)\) then fixes the new quotient obstacle image.
Induction places every obstacle orbit.  All additional edges satisfy the cycle
constraints because the data are realizable.  Therefore the periodic gluing
is unique modulo the initial global gauge.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Known ambient lattice frame]
\label{cor:v23-known-lattice-frame}
If an oriented Euclidean realization of the periodic lattice is supplied as
known ambient structure, a signature-rigid spanning tree alone determines all
obstacle images up to the residual global translation convention.  Relative
placements of the measured channel contact frames are still reconstructed,
not supplied.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Symmetry and cycle holonomy]
\label{rem:v23-generic-holonomy}
The two sufficient conditions above are not necessary.  Finite obstacle
symmetries produce finitely many possible incidence matches, and several
cycles can eliminate those choices.  The exact object controlling uniqueness
is the data-determined periodic gluing space
\(\mathfrak G_{\rm per}(\mathscr D)\).  The theorem therefore distinguishes
three genuine sources of ambiguity: obstacle symmetries, channel-network
connectivity, and periodic lattice holonomy.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Common laboratory registration as a special case]
\label{cor:v23-registered-special-case}
If the Euclidean placement of the lattice and of every measured channel frame
is supplied externally, then the periodic gluing is fixed a priori.  A family
of measured channels meeting every obstacle orbit therefore determines the
whole analytic table in that registration, recovering the registered v22
statement as a special case.
\end{corollary}
