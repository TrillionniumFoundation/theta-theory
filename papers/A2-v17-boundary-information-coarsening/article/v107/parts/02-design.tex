\section{Interpolation and fixed-total-exposure geometry}\label{sec:design}
The finite moment cone in Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-hankel} has fixed support. It is therefore polyhedral in its moment coordinates, although its linear span is the Hankel space. We first isolate the coefficient inverse used to derive it, and then impose the exposure constraint.

\begin{lemma}[The rational coefficient inverse]\label{lem:rational-inverse}
Let $p=2k+e+1$, let $h$ be the degree-$p-1$ polynomial in Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-hankel}, and let $T_1,\ldots,T_p$ be distinct clocks outside its zeros. The rational functions
\[
 1,\quad -\frac{\sigma_i}{T-r_i}\ (1\le i\le k),\quad
 \frac{\epsilon_a}{(T-\rho_a)^{\mu_a}}\ (1\le a\le n)
\]
form a basis of the $p$-dimensional space of rational functions with denominator $h$ and numerator degree at most $p-1$. Their evaluation matrix $V\in\R^{p\times p}$ is invertible. If $y\in\R^p$ is the vector of evaluations, the retained coefficient vector is $Zy$, where $Z\in\R^{n\times p}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isolated-inverse}
 Z_{aj}=\frac{h(T_j)}{P'(T_j)}
 \frac{-P(\rho_a)}{\epsilon_a h_a(\rho_a)(T_j-\rho_a)}.
\end{equation}
The polynomial coefficient matrix $E\in\R^{n\times n}$, whose rows are $p_*(T)/(T-\rho_a)$, is invertible, and
\[
 Z_{\cdot j}=\frac{\xi_j}{p_*(T_j)}\diag(d_a)Ew(T_j).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A linear relation among these rational functions has zero constant part at infinity. At an interior site its highest principal part forces the retained double-pole coefficient to vanish; its remaining simple-pole coefficient then vanishes. At an endpoint the simple principal part forces the endpoint coefficient to vanish. This proves independence, and the numerator space has the same dimension $p$.

For a coefficient vector $\theta$, put $g=h(V\theta)$, interpreting $V\theta$ here as the rational function whose evaluations are prescribed. Polynomial interpolation gives
\[
 g(T)=\sum_{j=1}^p h(T_j)y_j\frac{P(T)}{(T-T_j)P'(T_j)}.
\]
At a retained site, the coefficient of its highest pole is $g(\rho_a)/(\epsilon_a h_a(\rho_a))$. In evaluating the displayed interpolation formula, $\rho_a-T_j=-(T_j-\rho_a)$ supplies the minus sign in \eqref{eq:isolated-inverse}. This proves every entry of the $n\times p$ inverse block. Evaluation at $T=\rho_b$ makes the $a$th polynomial row of $E$ vanish unless $a=b$, when its value is $\prod_{c\ne a}(\rho_a-\rho_c)\ne0$. Thus these rows form a basis. Multiplying their evaluation vector by $\diag(d_a)$ proves the final identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[An exact four-clock inverse]\label{ex:four-clock-inverse}
Take one interior site $r=1$, a lower endpoint at $0$, one colour, and clocks $2,3,4,5$. In column order mixing, interior sum, endpoint, variance,
\[
 V=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&-1&-1/2&-1\\
 1&-1/2&-1/3&-1/4\\
 1&-1/3&-1/4&-1/9\\
 1&-1/4&-1/5&-1/16
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Z=\begin{pmatrix}-20&240&-540&320\\-8&72&-144&80\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives $ZV=[0_{2\times2},I_2]$. This illustrates the dimensions and signs; Lemma~\ref{lem:rational-inverse}, rather than this finite instance, proves the general formula.
\end{example}

Fix the roots, clocks, channels, and central mixing. Set
\[
 s=2n-1,\qquad m=2k+e+1,\qquad d_0=m-s=2-e,
 \qquad a_j=\frac{\xi_j^2}{p_*(T_j)^2\kappa_j}>0.
\]
Use moment coordinates $y=(y_0,\ldots,y_{s-1})$ for a Hankel matrix, so that $H_{ab}=y_{a+b}$ with indices $0\le a,b<n$. Let
\[
 T_{rj}=T_j^r\quad(0\le r<s),\qquad
 \mathscr M=T(0,\infty)^m,\qquad
 \phi(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{a_j}{\omega_j}.
\]
The letter $T$ in this paragraph denotes the moment matrix, not an individual clock. Its rank is $s$. For $y\in\mathscr M$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget-gauge}
 \chi(y)=\min\{\phi(\omega):\omega>0,\ T\omega=y\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-budget image and all exposure fibres]\label{thm:budget}
The minimum in \eqref{eq:budget-gauge} exists and has a unique minimizer $\omega^*(y)$. The function $\chi$ is real analytic and strictly convex on the open moment cone $\mathscr M\subset\R^s$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget-hessian}
 \chi(ty)=t^{-1}\chi(y),\qquad
 \nabla^2\chi(y)=(TD_y^{-1}T^{\mathsf T})^{-1}>0,
 \quad D_y=\diag\left(\frac{2a_j}{\omega_j^*(y)^3}\right).
\end{equation}
Under the fixed budget $\sum_j\lambda_j=1$, the native inverse-information image is obtained from the following exact moment image by $y\mapsto J_*H(y)J_*^{\mathsf T}$.

If $e=2$, the image is the smooth hypersurface $\chi(y)=1$, of dimension $s-1$. Each of its points has exactly one positive exposure vector.

If $e=0$ or $1$, the image is the convex region
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget-region}
 \{y\in\mathscr M:\chi(y)\le1\}.
\end{equation}
Its relative boundary in $\mathscr M$ is the smooth hypersurface $\chi=1$. A boundary point has exactly one exposure vector. At an interior point $\chi<1$, the entire positive-exposure fibre is diffeomorphic to $S^{d_0-1}$. Thus it consists of two points for $e=1$, and is a circle for $e=0$. These statements also describe the native information image after matrix inversion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The zeroth moment gives $\sum_j\omega_j=y_0$. Hence the closure of the positive weight fibre is compact. The function $\phi$ tends to infinity at its boundary because every $a_j$ is positive. It attains a minimum in the positive fibre. Its Hessian is the positive diagonal matrix in \eqref{eq:budget-hessian}; strict convexity proves uniqueness.

The Lagrange equations have the form
\[
 \nabla\phi(\omega^*)+T^{\mathsf T}\nu=0,
 \qquad T\omega^*=y.
\]
Their bordered derivative matrix is invertible: if $D_y u+T^{\mathsf T}v=0$ and $Tu=0$, then $u^{\mathsf T}D_yu=0$, so $u=0$, and full row rank of $T$ gives $v=0$. The analytic implicit function theorem makes $\omega^*$ and $\nu$ analytic. Differentiating these equations yields
\[
 d\omega^*=D_y^{-1}T^{\mathsf T}(TD_y^{-1}T^{\mathsf T})^{-1}dy,
 \qquad d\nu=-(TD_y^{-1}T^{\mathsf T})^{-1}dy.
\]
Since $\nabla\chi=-\nu$, this proves the Hessian formula and strict convexity. Rescaling the weight fibre proves the homogeneity formula. Euler's identity $\nabla\chi(y)\cdot y=-\chi(y)$ makes every positive level regular.

The exposure-to-weight change is a diffeomorphism of positive orthants:
$\omega_j=a_j/\lambda_j$. Its budget equation is exactly $\phi(\omega)=1$. If $d_0=0$, the moment map is invertible and the fibre is a point, proving the $e=2$ assertion.

Suppose $d_0>0$. Identify $\ker T$ with $\R^{d_0}$ by an orthonormal basis. The positive fibre is a bounded open convex set in this affine space, containing its unique minimizer $\omega^*$. On every ray $\omega^*+ru$, $|u|=1$, the first derivative of $\phi$ vanishes at $r=0$ and its second derivative is strictly positive. Its value therefore increases strictly from $\chi(y)$ to infinity at the first boundary point of the ray. Consequently the level $\phi=1$ is empty when $\chi>1$, is the singleton minimizer when $\chi=1$, and otherwise meets every ray in exactly one point. The derivative at that intersection is positive, so the implicit function theorem makes its radius a smooth function of $u$. Radial parametrization gives the asserted diffeomorphism with $S^{d_0-1}$. The coordinatewise inversion from weights to exposures preserves it. Convexity and the boundary description of \eqref{eq:budget-region} follow from the strict convexity and homogeneity of $\chi$. Congruence and inversion are diffeomorphisms on the positive definite matrix cone, proving the last statement.
\end{proof}

The dimension loss at two endpoints is therefore structural: the moment map has no weight kernel. With fewer endpoints, changing the exposure distribution inside an unobserved moment fibre allows the reciprocal budget to increase from its minimum. The region in \eqref{eq:budget-region} is a statement about fixed-clock moment coordinates; its nonlinear image under inversion need not be convex.

\begin{corollary}[A scalar budget optimum]\label{cor:scalar-budget}
For a fixed retained vector $u$, put $b_j=(Z_{\cdot j}^{\mathsf T}u)^2/\kappa_j$. Then
\[
 \inf_{\lambda>0,\ \sum\lambda_j=1}u^{\mathsf T}Q^{-1}u
 =\left(\sum_j\sqrt{b_j}\right)^2.
\]
If every $b_j>0$, the unique optimum is $\lambda_j=\sqrt{b_j}/\sum_i\sqrt{b_i}$. If some but not all $b_j$ vanish, the infimum is approached on the boundary and is not attained with all exposures positive. If all vanish, the objective is identically zero.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The inverse-information identity gives the objective $\sum_j b_j/\lambda_j$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives the lower bound and its equality conditions. Approximate the indicated boundary design when necessary.
\end{proof}
This scalar allocation identity is a classical design calculation, not a separate novelty claim. The image and complete design fibres in Theorem~\ref{thm:budget} are the additional geometric statements.

\begin{proposition}[More than the minimal number of clocks]\label{prop:overidentified}
Let the same root pattern be observed at $m>p=2k+e+1$ distinct clocks, with positive weights $W=\diag(\lambda_j\kappa_j)$. Then the rectangular score matrix $V\in\R^{m\times p}$ has full column rank and the retained Schur complement is positive. If $E_0\in\R^{p\times n}$ selects the retained coordinates, its inverse is
\[
 R=E_0^{\mathsf T}(V^{\mathsf T}WV)^{-1}E_0,
 \qquad
 u^{\mathsf T}Ru=\min_{V^{\mathsf T}a=E_0u}a^{\mathsf T}W^{-1}a.
\]
The minimizer is $a=WV(V^{\mathsf T}WV)^{-1}E_0u$. At fixed scores the native image is a rational semialgebraic image of the positive weights. Sending the weights at all but a selected $p$-clock subdesign to zero recovers its square-design inverse information.

The fixed congruence to a Hankel space supplied by a selected square subdesign does not, in general, persist after additional clocks receive positive weight.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Any $p$ rows form an invertible matrix by Lemma~\ref{lem:rational-inverse}, proving full column rank. Complete the square in the constrained quadratic minimization, or use its multiplier equations, to obtain the displayed optimizer and value. Positivity, rationality, and the boundary limit follow from the Gram formula and continuity of inversion at the positive definite subdesign matrix.

For the final assertion take two interior sites $1,2$, a lower endpoint $0$, one colour, and clocks $4,5,6,7,8,9,10$. Choose positive exposures so that $W=I$. In column order mixing, the two sums, endpoint, and the two variances, the row at clock $T$ is
\[
 (1,-(T-1)^{-1},-(T-2)^{-1},-T^{-1},-(T-1)^{-2},-(T-2)^{-2}).
\]
Let $J_*$ be the square-design congruence from the first six clocks. Its diagonal factors are $(15120,20160,2520)$ and its polynomial matrix is
\[
 E=\begin{pmatrix}2&-3&1\\0&-2&1\\0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Exact rational inversion of the displayed seven-row Gram matrix gives, for $H=J_*^{-1}RJ_*^{-\mathsf T}$ with indices $0,1,2$,
\[
 H_{02}-H_{11}=-\frac{473241649}{108060048}\ne0.
\]
These formulas constitute a rational counterexample which can also be checked by clearing denominators. A common normalization of the exposures only multiplies $R$ and this nonzero difference by the same positive scalar. Thus it also supplies a fixed-budget counterexample. No assertion that every possible alternative congruence is excluded is needed here.
\end{proof}
