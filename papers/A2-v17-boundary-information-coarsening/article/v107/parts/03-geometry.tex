\section{Critical initial maps and marked derivative certificates}\label{sec:critical-jets}
The residual-scale certificate proves affine asymptotics when its strict inequalities hold. At a critical scale, the full weighted leading map can instead survive. The next theorem supplies a normal form in that regime without postulating an affine residual or a small derivative certificate.

For positive rational weights $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_r)$ write
$\delta_t x=(t^{w_1}x_1,\ldots,t^{w_r}x_r)$ and
$\nu_w(x)=\max_i|x_i|^{1/w_i}$. A closed cone $K$ below is invariant under all $\delta_t$, and its weighted unit section is compact. Examples include $\R^r$ and coordinate orthants.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted critical residual and coefficient stability]\label{thm:critical-jets}
Let $O(x)=P(x)+H(x)$ be a polynomial observation near zero on $K$. Suppose that $P(\delta_t x)=tP(x)$, that $P^{-1}(0)\cap K=\{0\}$, and that every monomial of $H$ has weighted degree greater than one. Let the marked target be $o(t)=tb+\epsilon(t)$, where $|\epsilon(t)|/t\to0$. On a sufficiently small fixed weighted neighbourhood in $K$, the complete normalized residual set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-initial}
 E_b=P(K)-b.
\end{equation}
It is closed, and for every positive definite observation metric $G$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-minimum}
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}t^{-2}\min_x\|O(x)-o(t)\|_G^2
 =\min_{\xi\in K}\|P(\xi)-b\|_G^2.
\end{equation}
The right side is positive precisely when $b\notin P(K)$. In that case the minimum residual norm has exact order one. With time reparametrized by $t=\tau^q$, its order is $q$.

These conclusions, including convergence of the minima, are uniform on compact coefficient families with fixed monomial support and weights, uniformly bounded targets, metric eigenvalues in a fixed compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$, a uniform target remainder, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-margin}
 \inf_{\nu_w(\xi)=1,\ \xi\in K}|P(\xi)|\ge c_0>0.
\end{equation}
In particular the leading polynomials themselves may vary. The limiting minimum is continuous in these coefficients, targets, and metrics. Its positivity is an open condition; on compact subfamilies with a positive lower bound for that minimum, the exact order-one conclusion and its positive leading coefficient are uniform. No simultaneous resolution or constancy of divisorial orders is required for these stability assertions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compactness of the weighted unit section and the absence of a nonzero zero give $|P(x)|\ge c_0\nu_w(x)$ on $K$, by homogeneity. Let $\delta>0$ be the least excess over one of a weighted degree occurring in $H$; if $H=0$, omit the corresponding bounds. On $\nu_w(x)\le\epsilon$,
\[
 |H(x)|\le C\nu_w(x)^{1+\delta}.
\]
Choose $\epsilon$ so that $C\epsilon^\delta\le c_0/2$. Then $|O(x)|\ge(c_0/2)\nu_w(x)$. If $(O(x)-o(t))/t$ stays bounded, so does $O(x)/t$, and consequently $\nu_w(x)=O(t)$. Thus $x=\delta_t\xi$ with bounded $\xi\in K$. On such sets,
\[
 t^{-1}(O(\delta_t\xi)-o(t))=P(\xi)-b+o(1)
\]
uniformly. Subsequences give one inclusion in \eqref{eq:critical-initial}, and the substitution $x=\delta_t\xi$ gives the other. The same lower bound for $P$ proves its properness on $K$, hence the closedness of its image.

The trial $x=0$ bounds the minimum by $O(t^2)$. Norm equivalence and the preceding coercive estimate therefore bound every rescaled minimizing sequence. Uniform expansion on this bounded set proves the lower bound in \eqref{eq:critical-minimum}; testing a minimizer of the proper limiting problem proves the upper bound. Its minimum is zero exactly on $P(K)$.

For a compact coefficient family, the degree gap $\delta$, the higher-order constant $C$, the radius $\epsilon$, and all metric constants can be chosen uniformly. Bounded targets and the trial at zero give a common bound for the rescaled minimizers. The same expansion proves uniform convergence. The limiting minimizers also stay in a common compact subset of $K$. Uniform continuity of the polynomial objective on that compact set proves continuity of its minimum under coefficient, target, and metric changes. Positivity is consequently open, and a positive uniform lower bound allows taking square roots in the uniform minimum limit. The margin \eqref{eq:initial-margin} is itself open in the leading coefficients, and higher-weight coefficients can be perturbed independently inside bounded sets. This proves genuine coefficient stability, including variations that need not preserve a resolution type.
\end{proof}

The theorem allows coupled leading polynomials of the same weighted order. It is not a claim that every critical germ is weighted homogeneous. On a resolved chart with a proper weighted leading map, it applies to that chart's leading jets; feasibility and coverage of the charts must still be checked.

For synchronized roots, use weight $1/2$ on the shared splitting variables and weight one on sums, mixing, and endpoints. The leading probability map is $\mathsf La+\mathsf D_xq_A(\eta)+\mathsf D_z z$. Its injective ambient score and the coercivity of $q_A$ verify the hypotheses directly on $\R^{k+1}\times\R^d\times\R_+^e$. The model is analytic rather than polynomial in these coordinates, but its Taylor remainder has the same uniform higher-weight estimate used in the proof. Thus the proof applies verbatim. This explains why its critical contact depends on the quadratic splitting map, rather than merely on a polar order. For the three-cluster example, $b=(1,1,1)$ does not satisfy the cone equation. It therefore has a strictly positive contact coefficient, persisting under sufficiently small loading, target, and native-metric perturbations within the stated class.

\subsection{Changing a fast presentation}
The following chain rule states precisely a useful invariance category for derivative certificates. It also makes clear why an arbitrary replacement of the fast observables is not harmless.

\begin{proposition}[Transverse fast-coordinate transition]\label{prop:fast-switch}
Let an observation sheet be $(U,g(U))$, with $A=Dg$ and $M=\sup\|D^2g\|$ on a specified source region. Let $\mathcal T=(\mathcal T_F,\mathcal T_G)$ be a $C^2$ observation-coordinate diffeomorphism, and set
\[
 H_F=D\mathcal T_F\binom{I}{A},\qquad
 H_G=D\mathcal T_G\binom{I}{A}.
\]
Assume that $A$, the first two derivatives of $\mathcal T$ and $\mathcal T^{-1}$, and $H_F^{-1}$ are uniformly bounded there. Wherever the transformed sheet is expressed in its new fast coordinates, its slope and second fast derivative satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fast-transition}
 \widehat A=H_GH_F^{-1},\qquad
 \|\widehat A\|\le C,\qquad \widehat M\le C(1+M).
\end{equation}
If the corresponding inverse transition obeys the same bounds, these estimates hold in both directions on the same transported source region. Consequently residual-scale second-derivative smallness is equivalent when the compared residual scales are uniformly comparable and tend to zero. Localization and tube-covering hypotheses must also hold for each marked presentation; they are not consequences of the displayed chain rule alone.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate $\widehat U=\mathcal T_F(U,g(U))$ and $\widehat g=\mathcal T_G(U,g(U))$. Their derivatives are $H_F$ and $H_G$, giving the first identity. The derivative of either $H$ in $U$ is bounded by a constant times $1+\|D^2g\|$: its terms contain a second derivative of $\mathcal T$ applied to two bounded graph tangents, or a first derivative of $\mathcal T$ applied to $(0,D^2g)$. Differentiate $H_GH_F^{-1}$, use
$D(H_F^{-1})=-H_F^{-1}(DH_F)H_F^{-1}$, and multiply by $H_F^{-1}$ to change the last derivative to $\widehat U$. This proves \eqref{eq:fast-transition}. Apply the same computation to the inverse transition. If $\widehat c\asymp c\to0$, the two inequalities imply $cM\to0$ if and only if $\widehat c\widehat M\to0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[One residual germ, two unequal certificates]\label{ex:marked-certificate}
Consider $O(x)=(x,x^3)$ with target $(t,t^3+t^4)$. Marking $F=x$ gives $A_F=3x^2$, $M=6|x|$ on a tube of radius $t^2$, and $c=t^4$. The residual-scale certificate holds. Instead mark $\widehat F=x^3$ and use $x$ as slow observable. A tube of radius $t^4$ around $t^3+t^4$ avoids the critical value for small time, but the slow slope is $1/(3x^2)\asymp t^{-2}$ and is unbounded. The coordinate swap preserves the full residual germ and transports its metric, while this derivative certificate fails. The first transition has no uniformly bounded inverse fast Jacobian. This example proves that certificate invariance without a transversality condition would be false; it does not affect invariance of the normalized residual image itself.
\end{example}

\subsection{A simultaneous proper-real presentation}
The next statement makes the resolution dependency of the divisorial criterion explicit. All suprema are over the original tube; no selected inverse branch replaces that tube.

\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous resolution of the tube certificate]\label{prop:simultaneous}
Let $\Gamma$ be the compact closure of the positive-time graph
\[
 \{(t,x,a(t),b(t),r(t),c(t)):0<t\le t_0,\ x\in\Omega_t\},
\]
where $c(t)>0$ and the tube satisfies the geometric hypotheses preceding Definition~\ref{def:gap}. The graph is considered only near its zero-time fibre. Let $J=\det DF$, $P=DG\operatorname{adj}(DF)$, and
$N_2=(JD_xP-PD_xJ)\operatorname{adj}(DF)$.
There is a proper real analytic uniformization followed by a proper normal-crossings modification and a finite collection of retained closed corner boxes covering the germ. In each such box the pullbacks have the forms
\[
 t=u_tz^a,\quad r=u_rz^s,\quad c=u_cz^h,\quad J=u_Jz^j,
 \quad P=z^pP_0,\quad N_2=z^qN_0,
\]
with all scalar units bounded away from zero, and all displayed nonzero vector units having norms bounded above and away from zero. Coordinates $z_i$ are nonnegative; nondivisorial coordinates range in compact boxes. A numerator which vanishes identically on an analytic component is retained as the zero block, not assigned a fictitious order.

The images of the boxes cover the entire tube germ. Every coordinate face used in a necessary bound is reached by holding the other divisor coordinates positive and approaching that face inside a positive-time orthant. For every nonnegative function of the recorded coordinates, its supremum on the tube equals its supremum on the full retained preimage. Consequently boundedness of $P/J$, convergence of $c/r$ to zero, and convergence of $cN_2/J^3$ to zero are equivalent to the finite coordinatewise tests in Theorem~\ref{thm:divisorial}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The graph coordinates are analytic functions on the ambient space of the compact semialgebraic graph. Apply real uniformization \cite[Theorem 0.1]{BM} and pull back all these coordinates. Prune to the closure of the positive-time part. Lemma~\ref{lem:accessible} proves that this remains surjective onto $\Gamma$. On every retained analytic component, $t,r,c,J$ are not identically zero: they are nonzero at positive time, including $J$ by the tube condition. A numerator block may vanish identically; in that case its norm is identically zero and its associated boundedness or smallness condition is automatic on that component.

On each component resolve the product of the nonzero factors among
\[
 t\,r\,c\,J^2\left(\sum_{a,b}P_{ab}^2\right)
                \left(\sum_{a,b,c}(N_2)_{abc}^2\right).
\]
Normal crossings follows from \cite[Corollary 4.9]{BM}, applied near the compact inverse image of the zero-time fibre. Prune again. The scalar factorization and sum-of-squares statements of Lemma~\ref{lem:factor} give precisely the asserted scalar monomials and nonvanishing vector units. In particular no separate product of norms discards cancellation inside a numerator.

Choose finitely many coordinate neighbourhoods of the compact inverse zero-time fibre and smaller closed boxes whose relative interiors cover it. Retain all orthants with positive-time interior and replace signed divisor coordinates by nonnegative ones. The units have the required bounds after shrinking. Properness proves that their images cover a full sufficiently small time germ: a sequence of omitted tube points with time tending to zero would have lifts accumulating at the covered inverse zero-time fibre. The transverse arcs in the statement lie in the retained boxes and map to feasible tube points. This also handles faces with zero time exponent: their interior points have positive time when all time-divisor coordinates are held positive.

Surjectivity gives equality of suprema before any estimates. For a nonzero monomial ratio $uz^d$ with bounded invertible unit, boundedness holds exactly when every $d_i\ge0$. Uniform convergence to zero at $t=0$ holds exactly when $d_i>0$ on time-divisor coordinates $a_i>0$ and $d_i\ge0$ on the others. Negative exponents or a zero exponent at a time face are excluded by the transverse arcs. Conversely, with
$\delta=\min_{a_i>0}d_i/a_i>0$, boxes rescaled to $z_i\le1$ give $|uz^d|\le Ct^\delta$. Apply this to $P/J$, $c/r$, and $cN_2/J^3$, omitting identically zero numerator blocks. Their exponents are respectively $p-j$, $h-s$, and $h+q-3j$. The last tube supremum is $c(t)M(t)$, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Order faces and the meaning of persistence]\label{rem:order-faces}
The admissible exponents of the divisorial criterion satisfy both weak and strict linear inequalities. The whole admissible set need not be relatively open in the full exponent space. Partition the weak-inequality cone into faces according to which weak inequalities are equalities. On each such face, the additional strict tests cut out a relatively open rational polyhedral subset, possibly empty. Whenever a fixed-order region is called relatively open below, this facewise meaning is intended. Refinement invariance is equivalence to bounds on the same marked tube. Persistence of strict margins with fixed orders and uniform units is not a simultaneous-resolution theorem for arbitrary parameter changes. The coefficient stability of Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-jets} is a distinct positive stability result which does allow the leading map to vary.
\end{remark}
