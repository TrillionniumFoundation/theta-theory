\subsection{Theorem-level relation to earlier work}
The contribution is the relation among a particular singular feasible set, a model-forced information matrix, and an inverse endpoint object. None of the classical ingredients listed below is claimed as a new construction. The distinctions are part of the statements, not qualifications to be supplied after applying them.

\paragraph{Quadratic measurements and singular statistical models.}
The map $\eta\mapsto((a_i^{\mathsf T}\eta)^2)_i$ is the real quadratic measurement map familiar from phase retrieval. Its sign ambiguity and identifiability are studied by Balan, Casazza, and Edidin \cite{BCE}. The representation as linear measurements of $\eta\eta^{\mathsf T}$ is also the starting point of PhaseLift \cite{CSV}; dropping the rank constraint produces a different feasible set. We use the elementary sufficient hypothesis that the measurement tensors span $\Sym_d$, rather than claiming a sharp phase-retrieval measurement count. Drton \cite{Drton} explains why tangent cones, including nonconvex ones at singularities, govern likelihood-ratio limits in semialgebraic statistical models. Theorem~\ref{thm:synchronized} is not a new general tangent-cone principle. It proves that synchronized polynomial roots give this particular cone in a calibrated binary experiment, computes its deterministic Hellinger contact in the experiment's native Hankel metric, and proves that a reconstructed endpoint profile determines that contact for every loading family and target. The finite-root-label quotient of Theorem~\ref{thm:unification} is a regular companion result; it is not used to disguise this cone as an affine sheet.

\paragraph{Moment spaces and the location of the inverse.}
Moment spaces and their statistical design role are classical; see Dette and Studden \cite{DS}. At fixed clocks, $\sum_j\omega_jw(T_j)w(T_j)^{\mathsf T}$ is simply the linear image of a positive weight orthant. It is a finite polyhedral moment cone, not the full truncated moment cone with arbitrary support. Vandermonde rank explains its span and dimension. Lemma~\ref{lem:rational-inverse} likewise uses classical polynomial interpolation and extraction of principal-part coefficients. The model-specific content of Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-hankel} is that the calibrated polynomial log-odds scores force those coefficients and therefore force the \emph{inverse nuisance-efficient information} into the stated fixed congruence class. A generic information matrix written as a weighted sum of regression tensors does not establish this inverse-information statement. Proposition~\ref{prop:overidentified} identifies its square-design boundary: the same subdesign congruence can fail once an extra clock has positive weight.

\paragraph{Design geometry with a fixed budget.}
The equivalence of design extremum problems in Kiefer and Wolfowitz \cite{KW}, and the information-matrix viewpoint developed systematically by Pukelsheim \cite{Pukelsheim}, concern a much broader optimization theory. Our scalar allocation Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar-budget} is a direct Cauchy--Schwarz calculation within that tradition. Theorem~\ref{thm:budget} instead describes the entire image of the positive exposure simplex in moment coordinates and every exposure vector over each image point. The reciprocal budget function is minimized on an affine moment fibre. Its explicit Hessian and the dimension $\dim\ker T=2-e$ then distinguish a hypersurface with unique designs from a convex region with two-point or circular design fibres. This is the precise fixed-budget conclusion used in the nonlinear contact theorem; no unnormalized-cone secant assertion is transferred to the budget image.

\paragraph{Total positivity and endpoint visibility.}
The Cauchy determinant formula, Cauchy--Binet formula, complementary minors, and total positivity are classical; Pinkus \cite{Pinkus} provides their systematic matrix setting. We use a single ordered-site sign matrix $\Sigma_{aa}=(-1)^a$ after forming $Q=R^{-1}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:native-visibility} translates those matrix signs into positive-kernel and active-region assertions for the polynomial binary family. The novelty claimed is not strict total positivity of a Cauchy Gram matrix, but the explicit consequence for which native endpoint cells occur and which inverse fibre theorem applies. These conclusions depend on the one-colour and endpoint-count hypotheses as stated.

\paragraph{Forward complementarity and inverse value functions.}
Positive definite quadratic minimization over an orthant is a monotone linear complementarity problem, treated in Cottle, Pang, and Stone \cite{CPS}. Its piecewise linear optimizer and piecewise quadratic value are the familiar forward objects of parametric quadratic programming, including Bemporad, Morari, Dua, and Pistikopoulos \cite{BMDP,BMDPc}. Schur complementation and convex duality are also standard \cite{BV}. The inverse object here is a labelled value function with neither endpoint count nor active-set names supplied. Theorems~\ref{thm:reconstruct}, \ref{thm:one-missing}, and \ref{thm:three-cells} recover or classify all minimal positive representations under their respective visibility hypotheses. The planar table in Lemma~\ref{lem:planar-fan} explicitly supplies the gluing and exhaustion needed for the last assertion. Theorem~\ref{thm:all-strata} gives a general equality test and then applies classical quantifier elimination and semialgebraic triviality \cite{BCR,Hardt}. Its finite stratification is not presented as a new triviality theorem or as an explicit normal-form list for every higher-rank degeneration.

\paragraph{Residual geometry and resolution.}
Proper real uniformization and normal crossings are taken from Bierstone and Milman \cite{BM}. The scalar and sum-of-squares factorization arguments are included to expose which real faces are accessible. The new use of these tools is an exact marked-tube derivative certificate preserving the polar numerators' cancellations, together with the residual-scale rather than radius-scale comparison. Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-jets} is an explicit proper weighted-initial-map statement with a localization proof and coefficient-uniform constants. It does not classify every singularity by its order vector. The transformed residual image and its metric are intrinsic to the feasible observation germ, whereas the fast derivative bounds also depend on the selected transverse presentation. Proposition~\ref{prop:fast-switch} and Example~\ref{ex:marked-certificate} make this distinction testable.

\paragraph{Efficient information, identifiability, and exact queries.}
Nuisance-efficient information is obtained by a Schur complement; local asymptotic normality and comparison of labelled experiments use the classical frameworks of van der Vaart and Blackwell \cite{vdV,Blackwell}. Those operations are not new information-theoretic principles. Allman, Matias, and Rhodes \cite{AMR} establish broad latent-structure identifiability results by algebraic and tensor methods. Our calibrated scalar-clock experiment has a different, explicitly interpolated score inverse, and it is kept distinct from the inherited unknown-channel inverse in Appendix~\ref{app:inverse}. Finally, the exact recovery count for a deterministic noiseless dual-value oracle is a linear-algebra consequence of the native inverse-information span. It is secondary to the geometric classification and is neither a statistical sample complexity nor a guarantee for the different $Q$-value oracle.

These comparisons delimit the theorem-level contribution. They do not assert an exhaustive negative priority result over every formulation of inverse complementarity or singular design. The proofs, rather than a claim that no related theorem exists, specify the advance submitted for independent review.
