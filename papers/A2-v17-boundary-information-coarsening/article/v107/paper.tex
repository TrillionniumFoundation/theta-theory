\begin{abstract}
We determine the nonlinear contact geometry of calibrated binary polynomial observations whose root pairs split under shared parameters. The complete leading residual is a linear image of a rank-one quadratic measurement cone, not a union of affine sheets. Its metric cost is obtained by minimizing an endpoint profile over that cone. The same observation model forces the inverse efficient information into a fixed-clock Hankel cone; in a one-colour class the endpoint profile reconstructs the metric needed for every such nonlinear contact. We also classify the fixed-total-exposure image and its design fibres. With two endpoints the image is a smooth hypersurface; with one or no endpoints it is a convex region whose interior fibres are, respectively, two points or a circle. A weighted initial-map theorem treats critical residuals beyond the affine derivative regime and gives coefficient-uniform stability. We distinguish invariants of residual images from certificates attached to fast presentations, give an explicit simultaneous real resolution proposition, and complete the planar endpoint-fibre arguments. A finite semialgebraic presentation covers arbitrary visibility and rank strata. The earlier finite-sheet, tensor-envelope, endpoint-reconstruction, and experiment theorems are retained with their proofs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A common perturbation can split several repeated roots without making their variances independent. This elementary constraint changes the leading geometry of an observation. If the two roots at site $r_i$ are displaced by $\pm\ell_i(\eta)$, where $\ell_i$ is a prescribed linear form, the variance vector is
\[
 q(\eta)=(\ell_1(\eta)^2,\ldots,\ell_k(\eta)^2).
\]
For $\ell_1(s,t)=s$, $\ell_2(s,t)=t$, and $\ell_3(s,t)=s+t$, its image satisfies
\[
 (q_3-q_1-q_2)^2=4q_1q_2.
\]
Thus the singularity persists after root permutations have been quotiented out. It is not the multiplicity of names assigned to a regular observation sheet.

The main theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:synchronized}, places this singularity in the calibrated binary polynomial experiment of Section~\ref{sec:binary-hankel}. For prescribed loadings, roots, clocks, and exposures, none chosen from a desired quadratic form, it proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-contact}
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac4{t^2}\inf\sum_j\lambda_j h^2(p_j,p_j^{\rm target}(t))
 =\min_{\eta\in\R^d}\widehat G_Q(q(\eta)-b),
 \qquad \widehat G_Q(y)=\min_{z\ge0}(y,z)^{\mathsf T}Q(y,z).
\end{equation}
Here $Q^{-1}=J_*H(\omega)J_*^{\mathsf T}$ is forced by the same scalar score system. In the one-colour fully visible class, the labelled restriction of $\widehat G_Q$ to the positive orthant determines the right side for every loading family and every target $b$. This is the structural link: a reconstructed endpoint object determines nonlinear root contact in its native information metric. The theorem does not claim a new rank-one lifting identity or a new general tangent-cone principle.

The fixed-budget geometry is part of this link rather than an unconstrained surrogate. Theorem~\ref{thm:budget} determines both the image and all exposure fibres at fixed clocks. Its proof minimizes a strictly convex reciprocal function on moment fibres. The number of unobserved weight directions is $2-e$, giving a hypersurface for $e=2$, and a region with spherical design fibres for $e=0,1$. The rectangular-clock extension in Proposition~\ref{prop:overidentified} states separately what remains true beyond the square interpolation experiment.

Two complementary geometric results delimit the scope of the main theorem. Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-jets} identifies a broad class of critical residuals from weighted leading polynomials, including coupled maps. Proposition~\ref{prop:fast-switch} gives an explicit transition law for regular transverse changes of fast presentation and an example where a nontame change destroys the derivative certificate without changing the residual germ. The simultaneous-resolution statement in Proposition~\ref{prop:simultaneous} concerns that marked certificate, not a canonical classification of every observation germ. The coefficient-uniform statement in Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-jets} is a parameter-space stability theorem; persistence of divisorial inequalities in a fixed order type is a different assertion.

The endpoint reconstruction theorems are developed in Sections~\ref{sec:endpoints}--\ref{sec:endpoint-completion}. The added planar lemma exposes every KKT sector and its gluing. Separate reconstruction lemmas handle the missing singleton and the missing extreme vertices. Theorem~\ref{thm:all-strata} supplies a finite semialgebraic presentation of minimal fibres for arbitrary visibility and rank patterns. The explicit first-stratum normal forms are stronger than that general effective presentation; the latter does not pretend to replace them by a universal list of scalar moduli.
