\section{Intrinsic recovery of curves and K3 surfaces}
\label{sec:intrinsic-recovery}
The exact nilpotent structure has a consequence not available from
a transport of the reduced finite-algebra locus to global sections.
We first record the birational cancellation used to state it without
an ambient marking.

\begin{lemma}[Cancellation over a non-uniruled base]
\label{lem:nonuniruled-cancellation}
Let $Y,Y'$ be smooth projective complex varieties which are not
uniruled. If $Y\times\Pj^r$ and $Y'\times\Pj^{r'}$ are
birational, then $Y$ and $Y'$ are birational. For smooth genus-one
curves, or for K3 surfaces, they are then isomorphic.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A dominant rational map $Y\times\Pj^r\dashrightarrow Y'$ must
be constant on the general projective-space fibre. Indeed, if it
were nonconstant on such fibres, restrictions to lines on these
fibres would give a family of nonconstant rational curves covering
a dense open of $Y'$. This would make $Y'$ uniruled. Constancy on
the general fibre gives a rational factorization through $Y$:
on function fields, the pullbacks lie in the constant subfield
$\C(Y)\subset\C(Y)(z_1,\ldots,z_r)$. Apply this to a birational
map and its inverse. Their induced maps on $Y,Y'$ compose to the
identity, proving birationality. Equivalently one may take the
space of lines meeting the domains of the rational maps; a
nonconstant generic restriction would supply the excluded covering
family directly.

A genus-one curve is not uniruled, and a birational map between
smooth projective curves is an isomorphism. A complex K3 surface
is not uniruled either: a generically finite dominant map from a
ruled surface would inject its nonzero global two-form into the
zero space of global two-forms on a resolution of the ruled
surface. This is the usual characteristic-zero differential-form
argument; see \cite[Chapter 9, proof of Corollary 4.4]{HuyK3}.
For completeness, birational K3 surfaces are isomorphic as follows.
Resolve a birational map by point blowups
$Y\xleftarrow{p}Z\xrightarrow{q}Y'$. The pullbacks of their
nowhere-vanishing two-forms span the same one-dimensional space
$H^0(Z,\omega_Z)$. Their zero divisors are therefore equal.
By the blowup formula for the canonical divisor, their supports
are exactly the $p$-exceptional and $q$-exceptional curves,
respectively, every coefficient being positive. Thus the two
exceptional sets coincide. The morphism $q$ is constant on every
fibre of $p$, and conversely. Descending successively through the
point blowups, or using their graphs and connected fibres, gives
inverse morphisms $Y\to Y'$ and $Y'\to Y$. They are the desired
isomorphisms.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The ramification variety is intrinsic to the failure scheme]
\label{thm:intrinsic-ramification-recovery}
For $e\in\{3,4\}$ and $R,R'\in G_e^\circ$, an abstract complex-scheme
isomorphism $D_R\simeq D_{R'}$ implies $Y_R\simeq Y_{R'}$.
It is not necessary for the isomorphism to respect the Grassmannian,
the projection to $\Pj(V^*)$, the algebra, or any chosen frames.

The embedded support has the smooth projective compactification
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ramification-compactification}
 \overline E_R=
 \Pj_{Y_R\times\Pj(S_R)}
 \bigl(\mathcal O\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(H,S_R/\Lambda)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For all $i\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ramification-cohomology}
 H^i(\overline E_R,\mathcal O_{\overline E_R})
       \simeq H^i(Y_R,\mathcal O_{Y_R}).
\end{equation}
In particular $h^1=1$ for $e=3$, whereas $h^1=0$ and $h^2=1$
for $e=4$. The smooth projective compactification of the reduced
divisor obtained from \eqref{eq:delta-graph-bundle} is rational
and has no higher coherent cohomology. Its isomorphism type is
independent of $R$ after identifying vector spaces of the same
ranks. Thus the recovery statement uses the embedded structure
of $D_R$, not an invariant of its reduction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An isomorphism of schemes preserves the nilradical and its
annihilator. Theorem~\ref{thm:primary-powers-intrinsic}, with
$n=1$, consequently recovers $E_R$ as a closed subscheme of
$D_R$ and carries it isomorphically to $E_{R'}$. No primary
representative is chosen at this step.

The graph-bundle description gives the indicated compactification
and shows that
\[
 E_R\ \text{is birational to}\ 
 Y_R\times\Pj^{(p-1)+(e-1)(p-1)}.
\]
Indeed a vector bundle is trivial on a dense open of its integral
base. The same holds for $R'$. The isomorphism of the embedded
supports therefore makes $Y_R$ and $Y_{R'}$ stably birational.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:admissible-ramification} they are smooth
genus-one curves or K3 surfaces. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:nonuniruled-cancellation} to conclude that they
are isomorphic as abstract varieties.

For a projective bundle the direct image of its structure sheaf
is the structure sheaf of the base and its higher direct images
vanish, as is seen on trivializations from the cohomology of
projective space. Apply this first to
\eqref{eq:ramification-compactification} and then to
$Y_R\times\Pj(S_R)\to Y_R$. The Leray sequence proves
\eqref{eq:ramification-cohomology}. The genus-one and K3 values
follow from the hypersurface calculation in
Proposition~\ref{prop:admissible-ramification}. Replacing $Y_R$
by $\Pj(V^*)$ in the same construction compactifies $\Delta_R$.
Its base is a product of projective spaces, so it is rational
and the same direct-image calculation gives vanishing of all
higher coherent cohomology. Neither the vector-bundle description
of $\Delta_R$ nor this calculation uses $R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Nonisomorphic failure schemes with a fixed reduction]
\label{cor:intrinsic-elliptic-distinction}
For the family $B_\lambda$ and open $\Omega$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:elliptic-boundary}, distinct values of
$j(C_\lambda)$ give nonisomorphic abstract failure schemes on
the projection-corank-at-most-one open. All their reductions
are the same determinant divisor. Their relative family is flat.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Here the three-dimensional relation space is spanned by
$xy-\lambda z^2$, $yz-\lambda x^2$, and $zx-\lambda y^2$.
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:elliptic-boundary} verifies
basepoint-freeness and smoothness on $\Omega$; thus these are
points of $G_3^\circ$. Their restriction determinant is
$C_\lambda$, and its nonconstant $j$-invariant is computed in
\eqref{eq:elliptic-j}. The recovery theorem gives the asserted
nonisomorphism. Flatness is a special case of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ramification-flat-family}, and also follows
from the retained direct proof in Corollary~\ref{cor:elliptic-family}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:intrinsic-recovery-limits}
The recovered object is the abstract ramification variety. We do
not assert reconstruction of its polarization, the finite quadratic
map, or the input algebra from $D_R$. Nor does the orbit-dimension
estimate for the $(1,4,6)$ algebras assert that every quartic K3
surface occurs, or compute the dimension of the image in K3 moduli.
The theorem asserts recovery for every member of the nonempty
open class which is proved to exist. The elliptic subfamily gives
an explicit nonconstant invariant of the actual failure schemes.
These distinctions separate an intrinsic geometric conclusion
from stronger Torelli or dominance statements not used in the proof.
\end{remark}
