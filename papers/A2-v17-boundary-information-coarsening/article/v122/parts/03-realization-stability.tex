\section{Native realization and global separation}\label{sec:realization}
All spaces in this section are real. The following result holds for any fixed $J\in\mathrm{GL}_k(\R)$, not just the interpolation matrix.
\begin{theorem}[Exact fixed-space realization]\label{thm:fixed-realization}
Let $1\le d<k$, $c=k-d$, $U\in\Gr(c,k)$, and $T=JU^\perp$. The space $U$ is the normal-polynomial space of a local immersed image $q_A$ at $q_A(e_1)>0$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive}
 T\cap\R^k_{>0}\ne\varnothing.
\end{equation}
There is such a realization with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separated-fibre}
 q_A^{-1}(q_A(e_1))=\{e_1,-e_1\}
\end{equation}
if and only if, in addition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:no-split}
 D_sT\ne T\quad\text{for every }s\in\{\pm1\}^k\setminus\{\mathbf1,-\mathbf1\}.
\end{equation}
Here $D_s=\diag(s)$. On each sign chamber of the full-rank loading domain with $Ae_1$ nowhere zero, the map
\[
 A\longmapsto U(A)=J^{\mathsf T}\big(\ran(\diag(Ae_1)A)\big)^\perp
\]
is a submersion onto its positive realizable image. That image is nonempty, Euclidean open, and Zariski dense.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $z=Ae_1$. The tangent space is $T=\ran(\diag(z)A)$ and contains $t=z^2>0$. Taking orthogonal complements in $U=J^{\mathsf T}T^\perp$ gives $T=JU^\perp$ and proves necessity of \eqref{eq:positive}. Conversely choose $t>0$ in $T$, extend it to a basis $t,t_2,\ldots,t_d$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:construct-A}
 z_i=\sqrt{t_i},\qquad
 A=[z,\diag(z)^{-1}t_2,\ldots,\diag(z)^{-1}t_d].
\end{equation}
Then $\diag(z)A$ has this prescribed basis, and $q_A(e_1)=t$.

Equality of coordinatewise squares is equivalent to $A\eta=D_sz$ for a sign vector $s$. Because $D_s$ commutes with $\diag(z)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sign-test}
 D_sz\in\ran A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D_st\in T.
\end{equation}
If $D_sT=T$ for a nonconstant $s$, the right side holds for every $t\in T$; positivity ensures $D_st\ne\pm t$, giving another preimage. Conversely, under \eqref{eq:no-split}, each $T\cap D_sT$ is a proper linear subspace of $T$. Their finite union cannot cover the relatively open positive cone in \eqref{eq:positive}. Choose $t$ outside that union and use \eqref{eq:construct-A}. Equation~\eqref{eq:sign-test} leaves only the constant signs, hence exactly the two preimages in \eqref{eq:separated-fibre}.

For the submersion statement, on a fixed sign chamber the change of frames $A\mapsto B=\diag(z)A$ is a diffeomorphism onto full-rank frames whose first column is strictly positive. Its inverse has $z_i=\pm\sqrt{B_{i1}}$ with the fixed signs and $A_{ij}=B_{ij}/z_i$. The ordinary frame-to-plane map is a submersion. Orthogonal complement and multiplication by fixed $J$ preserve that property. Positivity persists under small changes of a frame, and planes containing $\mathbf1$ show nonemptiness. A polynomial vanishing on a nonempty real open subset of an affine Grassmannian chart vanishes identically, proving Zariski density.
\end{proof}
The obstruction in \eqref{eq:no-split} has a concrete interpretation. If $I$ is the set of positive signs of $s$, then $D_sT=T$ precisely when
\[
 T=(T\cap\R^I)\oplus(T\cap\R^{I^c}).
\]
Thus a prescribed normal space may be locally realizable yet admit no globally separated realization at that same space. Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-realization} classifies exactly this distinction.

\begin{lemma}[Generic global distance]\label{lem:global}
A nonempty Zariski-open subset of the full-rank loading domain with $Ae_1$ nowhere zero satisfies \eqref{eq:separated-fibre}. For each such fixed $A$, the global image has one embedded analytic germ at $b_0=q_A(e_1)$. For every compact set $\mathcal R\subset\Sym_k^{>0}$ there is a neighbourhood of $b_0$ on which the distance to that germ equals the global distance, and the minimizing projection is analytic with uniform bounds for $R\in\mathcal R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a nonconstant sign vector, the condition $D_sAe_1\in\ran A$ is algebraic, defined by augmented $(d+1)$-minors. It is proper: choose a vector $z$ with no zero entries and a $d$-plane containing $z$ but not $D_sz$, then take a frame with first column $z$. Removing the finitely many such proper sets proves the first assertion. The ambient loading space is the irreducible affine space $\R^{kd}$; full rank and nonzero first coordinates specify a Zariski-open domain, so these finite open intersections remain dense.

Full rank gives
\[
 \|q_A(\eta)\|_2\ge k^{-1/2}\|A\eta\|_2^2
                   \ge k^{-1/2}\sigma_{\min}(A)^2\|\eta\|_2^2,
\]
so $q_A$ is proper. Its derivative is injective at both preimages, and their local image germs coincide by evenness. Properness excludes a remote sheet accumulating at $b_0$. The trial image $b_0$ bounds the distance by $C\|b-b_0\|^2$. Uniform positive metric bounds on $\mathcal R$ therefore force all minimizing images into this germ when $b$ is close enough to $b_0$. The positive tangent Hessian and the implicit function theorem give the projection tube. Its radius and derivative constants depend on $A$, the local germ and $\mathcal R$; no uniformity over all generic loadings is claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold}]
Necessity is the dimension bound in Section~\ref{sec:contact}. If it holds and $d\ge2$, then $c=k-d\ge5$. Theorem~\ref{thm:global-intro} with $L=1$ gives a nonempty complex Zariski-open full-product locus. Its defining minors have real coefficients and some minor is a nonzero polynomial on a real affine chart. A nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on any nonempty real open set. Thus the full-product locus meets the positive realizable image of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-realization}.

On pivot charts normal frames are rational functions of $A$, so full product rank pulls back to a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the irreducible loading domain. Intersect it with Lemma~\ref{lem:global}'s dense open set. Theorem~\ref{thm:fibre} gives recovery from the global distance for every $\theta\in\Omega$ with the same $J$. Writing $k=d+c$ gives $c^2-3c-4d+2\ge0$, whose positive root yields \eqref{eq:threshold}. Formula~\eqref{eq:ambient} gives the unrestricted ambiguity.
\end{proof}
For $d=2,3,4,5,6$, the optimal $k$ are $7,8,10,11,13$. At the optimum the full symmetric measurement $S\mapsto(a_i^{\mathsf T}Sa_i)_i$ is necessarily noninjective for $d\ge5$: check $d=5,6$ directly, and use $k_{\min}\le2d<d(d+1)/2$ for $d\ge7$. This is not full-symmetric phase retrieval in disguise.

\begin{corollary}[Independent native designs and the real secant family]\label{cor:native-multiple}
Fix $4\le c<k$ and a native interpolation matrix $J$. For $L$ independently chosen loading matrices of size $k\times(k-c)$, generic globally separated contacts recover the common native moment parameter if and only if $Lq\ge p$. The common-secant family $\Sigma_L$ contains real, positive, globally separated native realizations. At its general such points its loading pullback has real local codimension $Lc-3$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the product of the loading submersions and generic global-separation opens to Theorem~\ref{thm:global-intro}, using real nonvanishing of minors as above. For the second assertion choose distinct $x,y>D$ and $h=\operatorname{ev}_x+\eps\operatorname{ev}_y$ with $\eps>0$. By \eqref{eq:nativeconstants}, every coordinate of $Jw_n(t)$ is negative for $t>D$. Thus $t=-Jh>0$. A general $d$-plane $T$ containing $t$ avoids each of the finitely many vectors $D_st$ for nonconstant $s$, since none is proportional to $t$ and $d<k$. Such a $T=JU^\perp$ has $U\subset\ker h$ and, by \eqref{eq:construct-A}--\eqref{eq:sign-test}, a globally separated realization. Choose the $T_\nu$ independently. These choices form real open subsets in the secant incidence. Its generically one-to-one projection is birational in characteristic zero and is an isomorphism on dense open subsets. The loading submersion consequently preserves its codimension there.
\end{proof}
The exact formula \eqref{eq:codimension} is an algebraic statement over $\C$. The corollary supplies a real native component of that dimension when the secant term is minimal. It does not infer real points on every complex component in the other regime. It also does not replace the independent-design assumption by multiple arbitrary points on one fixed quadratic image.

\section{Rank strata and quantitative inversion}\label{sec:stability}
Let $\Delta_r$ be the determinantal scheme on $\Gr(c,V_n)$ where multiplication has rank at most $r$. At a point of rank \emph{exactly} $r$, put $K_U=\ker\mu_U$ and $Q_U=V_{2n}/U^2$.
\begin{proposition}[Infinitesimal rank map]\label{prop:tangent}
The tangent space to $\Delta_r$ is the kernel of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent}
 \begin{split}
 \beta_U:\operatorname{Hom}(U,V_n/U)&\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(K_U,Q_U),\\
 \beta_U(\phi)\left(\sum_i f_i\odot g_i\right)
    &=\sum_i\{\phi(f_i)g_i+f_i\phi(g_i)\}\pmod{U^2}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
If $\beta_U$ is onto, the scheme is smooth there of codimension $(q-r)(p-r)$. The corresponding native contact fibre has dimension $p-r$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Express nearby planes as graphs and differentiate products. A different lift of $\phi$ changes the result only by an element of $U^2$. Near a nonzero $r$-minor, invertible block operations reduce multiplication to an invertible $r$-block and a Schur complement. Rank at most $r$ is equivalent to the vanishing of that complement, whose differential is \eqref{eq:tangent}. Its kernel is the scheme tangent space, and surjectivity gives smoothness with the displayed codimension. The fibre assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:fibre}.
\end{proof}
The same construction on $X_L$ uses the direct sum of the graph derivatives. The assertion is about the scheme defined by minors, and does not presuppose its reducedness.

Use coefficient Euclidean norms, the induced symmetric tensor norm on multiplication, direct-sum Frobenius norms on blocks, and the standard Grassmannian Riemannian distance. Let $\Delta_\R$ denote the real failure set. Let $\gamma$ be the least singular value of an injective $\LL$.
\begin{theorem}[Stability and transverse degeneracy]\label{thm:stability}
Suppose $m_0I\le B_\nu\le M_0I$ and $\widehat G_\nu=G_\nu+E_\nu$, with $\max_\nu\|E_\nu\|_{\rm op}\le(2M_0)^{-1}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stable}
 \left\|\LL^\dagger(\widehat G_\nu^{-1})_\nu-\theta\right\|_2
 \le\frac{2M_0^2}{\gamma}\left(\sum_\nu\|E_\nu\|_F^2\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The least multiplication singular value is bounded above by a constant times distance to $\Delta_\R$. Near a real corank-one point at which the corresponding kernel-to-cokernel differential is onto, it is bounded above and below by positive local constants times that distance. The same local comparison holds in loading space for distance to the pulled-back failure set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The inverse identity gives
$\widehat G_\nu^{-1}-B_\nu=-\widehat G_\nu^{-1}E_\nu B_\nu$.
The assumed bounds make its Frobenius norm at most $2M_0^2\|E_\nu\|_F$. Apply $\LL^\dagger$, whose norm is $\gamma^{-1}$.

In finitely many compact orthonormal frame charts, multiplication is Lipschitz in the plane coordinates. Singular values are Lipschitz in matrix entries and vanish on the real failure set, giving the upper estimate. At corank one, the Schur complement is a row of length $Lq-p+1$. Bounded invertible block operations compare the least singular value with the norm of this row. Surjectivity of its differential gives local coordinates in which the row is a coordinate projection, proving the lower estimate as well. For fixed $J$, passage from an orthonormal polynomial frame to $J^{\mathsf T}Z$ changes norms by bounded positive factors. The loading submersion gives local product coordinates and transfers the distance comparison.
\end{proof}
No linear distance law is asserted at nontransverse or singular points. Compact design sets disjoint from failure have a positive lower stability constant by continuity; this alone is not a dimension-uniform numerical estimate or a random-loading tail bound.

\section{Structured compression beyond Hankel matrices}\label{sec:structured}
Let $\mathcal K\subset\Sym_k$ have dimension $s$ and contain a positive matrix. Write $\rho(\mathcal K,c)$ for the maximum rank of $X\mapsto X|_{N\times N}$ over $N\in\Gr(c,k)$.
\begin{proposition}[Generic transfer]\label{prop:transfer}
For generic full-rank native loadings with $Ae_1$ nowhere zero, compression has rank $\rho(\mathcal K,c)$ and the global contact fibre in an open positive subset of $\mathcal K$ has dimension $s-\rho(\mathcal K,c)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Maximal compression rank is a nonempty Zariski-open condition. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-realization} with $J=I$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:global}. Positive block inversion preserves fibres, and rank-nullity gives the dimension.
\end{proof}
This is a transfer principle, not a computation of $\rho$ for an unspecified family. The following computation includes multiplicities in a fixed spectral decomposition.
\begin{theorem}[Block-spectral rank]\label{thm:spectral}
Let $P_1,\ldots,P_s$ be nonzero mutually orthogonal real symmetric projections with sum $I_k$, and let $\mathcal K=\Span\{P_1,\ldots,P_s\}$. Then
\[
 \rho(\mathcal K,c)=\min\{s,\tbinom{c+1}{2}\}.
\]
For the diagonal family the sharp threshold is $\binom{c+1}{2}\ge k$, with least ambient dimension $d+\lceil(1+\sqrt{1+8d})/2\rceil$ at fixed $d=k-c$. For the family of real symmetric circulant matrices it is $\binom{c+1}{2}\ge\lfloor k/2\rfloor+1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper bound is dimensional. Suppose $c\le s$. Choose one unit vector in each $\ran P_i$. In their span, a normal frame with rows $u_i^{\mathsf T}$ gives compressed matrices $u_iu_i^{\mathsf T}$. The $c$ vectors $e_a$ followed by the $c(c-1)/2$ vectors $e_a+e_b$ have linearly independent outer products: first inspect off-diagonal entries, then diagonal entries. Select the first $s$ when $s\le q$, or retain all $q$ and add rows when $s>q$. Starting with the $e_a$ gives full column rank. Column-orthonormalization transforms every compressed matrix by the same invertible congruence and therefore preserves linear independence. If $c>s$, take a $c$-plane containing one unit vector from every $\ran P_i$ and extend their span. Evaluation on these distinguished vectors separates the $s$ coefficients. This proves the rank formula in all cases.

For diagonal matrices, $s=k$ and substitution $k=d+c$ gives $c^2-c-2d\ge0$. A real symmetric circulant matrix is diagonal in the real Fourier decomposition: the zero frequency, the Nyquist frequency when present, and the paired sine--cosine frequencies give $\lfloor k/2\rfloor+1$ orthogonal spectral projections. Its eigenvalue is arbitrary on each such block, so the preceding formula applies.
\end{proof}
Independent Bernoulli log-odds coordinates with information weights $\lambda_i$ have inverse information $\diag((\lambda_ip_i(1-p_i))^{-1})$, providing a genuine positive diagonal information family. The block-spectral theorem concerns the stated matrix families and does not claim that every such family is generated by the calibrated polynomial experiment, or compute ranks for all Toeplitz or graph-sparse families.
