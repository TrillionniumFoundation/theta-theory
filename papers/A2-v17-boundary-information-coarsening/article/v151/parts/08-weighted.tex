\section{Weighted determinantal flags and the classical order profile}
\label{sec:weighted}
We record explicitly how the retained weighted-matrix calculation
fits the classical determinantal theory. This comparison concerns the
generic matrix only; the fixed-tensor theorem of
\S\ref{sec:coranktwo} was proved independently.

Let $A=(a_{ij})$ be a generic $q\times p$ matrix over a
characteristic-zero field $k$, with $p\ge q$, and put
$R_0=k[a_{ij}]$, $I_0(A)=R_0$. For a nonincreasing sequence
$\rho=(r_1,\ldots,r_s)$ write
\[
 I^\rho=\prod_{\nu=1}^s I_{r_\nu}(A),\qquad
 \gamma_j(\rho)=\sum_{\nu=1}^s\max(r_\nu-j+1,0).
\]
The classical formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical-product}
 I^\rho=\bigcap_{j=1}^{r_1} I_j(A)^{(\gamma_j(\rho))}.
\end{equation}
Here the superscript in parentheses denotes symbolic power.
See De Concini--Eisenbud--Procesi \cite{DCEP}, and its statement as
Theorem 2.4 in Bruns--Conca \cite{BC}, together with Proposition 2.2
there for the symbolic-order basis. The characteristic hypotheses and
attribution are stated on printed pages 13--14 of that exposition.

\begin{proposition}[Coefficientwise suspension]\label{prop:weighted}
In $R_0[t]$, put
\[
 F=t\sum_{i=0}^q t^{q-i}I_i(A),\qquad P_j=(t,I_j(A)).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted}
 F=(t)\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^qP_j^{(q-j+2)}.
\end{equation}
This is an irredundant primary decomposition. At a point with $t=0$ where $A$
has rank $a$, the nilradical of the corresponding local quotient has
index $q-a+1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the regular local ring $(R_0)_{I_j}$, the symbolic order of an
$i$-minor is $\max(i-j+1,0)$. Adjoining $t$ gives the coefficient
rule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-rule}
 \sum_{u\ge0}t^uf_u\in P_j^{(b)}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f_u\in I_j^{(\max(b-u,0))}\ \text{for every }u.
\end{equation}
For precision, the associated graded ring of the maximal ideal
$(t,I_j(R_0)_{I_j})$ is a polynomial ring over its residue field;
its homogeneous $t$-degrees are independent. The order of the displayed
sum is therefore the minimum of $u+\operatorname{ord}_{I_j}(f_u)$.
Contracting the maximal-ideal power proves \eqref{eq:coefficient-rule},
including the assertion that each contracted symbolic power is primary.

Intersecting with $(t)$ deletes coefficient degree zero. For
$1\le u\le q$, put $k=q+1-u$. The remaining coefficient conditions
on the right of \eqref{eq:weighted} are
\[
 f_u\in\bigcap_{j=1}^k I_j^{(k-j+1)}=I_k,
\]
the one-factor case $\rho=(k)$ of
\eqref{eq:classical-product}. For $u\ge q+1$ there is no condition.
These are exactly the coefficients of $F$, proving the equality.

For irredundancy, let $d_{j-1}$ be a fixed $(j-1)$-minor, with
$d_0=1$. The element $t^{q-j+1}d_{j-1}^2$ belongs to every component
other than $P_j^{(q-j+2)}$. Indeed, its order along $P_j$ is
$q-j+1$, while at $P_k$ for $k<j$ its order is
$q-j+1+2(j-k)\ge q-k+2$, and for $k>j$ its $t$-order suffices.
The element $d_q^2$ witnesses the necessity of $(t)$. The associated
primes are therefore exactly the displayed radicals.

After inverting an $a$-minor and Schur reducing, the localized
coefficient ideal contains $t^{q-a+1}$, whereas $t^{q-a}$ has
coefficient one in degree $q-a$, where membership would require a
one-minor of the residual matrix. That coefficient ideal is proper
at the point. Thus the radical $(t)$ has the asserted exact index.
\end{proof}

The exponent $q-j+2$ is therefore the scalar-variable suspension of
the classical minor order $k-j+1$, taken coefficientwise. This is a
direct consequence of the classical profile plus
\eqref{eq:coefficient-rule}; it is not a new straightening theorem.
The presentation $I_q[A,tI_q]=\sum_i t^{q-i}I_i(A)$ can also be
viewed as substituting a scalar identity block in a larger generic
matrix. Such substitution alone does not justify preservation of
primary components or flatness. The coefficient proof is what supplies
the exact intersection here, while the ideal $\delta\mathfrak m+
(g_0,\ldots,g_4)$ in \S\ref{sec:coranktwo} requires its separate
fixed-tensor analysis.
