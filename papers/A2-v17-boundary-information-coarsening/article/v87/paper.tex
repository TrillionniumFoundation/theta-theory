\begin{abstract}
We study recovery of an unordered action spectrum from normalized
matrix observations with two unknown stochastic channels. For every
fixed latent rank, three clocks identify every nonconstant spectrum
with full-rank channels, whereas two clocks admit a nontrivial exact fibre. A one-sided inverse estimate
holds against the full closed model, including rank-deficient
competitors, without a lower bound on individual action gaps. Its
conditioning separates the product of the two smallest channel
singular values from the observable contrast of the projective curve.
Thus partial action collisions and collapse of the entire spectrum
have different consequences. Residual confidence sets are honest
without supplied rank or contrast floors. Explicit positive families
with unchanged marginals, and a second family with a collapsing
spectrum, give matching risk and confidence-diameter orders in every
latent rank. The lower bounds also hold under adaptive clock selection.
The binary determinant law is recovered as a separated-spectrum case.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A selective observation can erase the total mass of an experiment
without erasing the relative frequencies of two conditionally
independent readings. The resulting datum is a probability matrix.
When an unobserved component with action $\xi_b$ contributes a factor
$T-\xi_b$ at clock $T$, the matrix is a rational curve with an unknown
common pole. The action spectrum is a feature of this curve, not of
a particular naming of the hidden components.

The exact matrix-pencil inverse is well understood when the channels
are well conditioned and the actions are separated. The question here
is different: how much information about the \emph{actions} remains
when the channels approach rank loss, some actions coincide, or the
whole clock curve approaches a constant? These limits should not be
combined into an unspecified inverse-function constant. Nor should
conditioning for recovering eigenvectors be charged to a problem
whose target is only the unordered spectrum.

We prove one estimate which separates the two relevant losses. The
first is bilinear: a weak direction in each readout channel produces
the product of the two weaknesses. The second is projective: a nearly
constant normalized curve conceals the location of its common pole.
A partial collision of actions causes neither loss by itself. The
same estimate yields exact identification, honest confidence regions,
and minimax upper bounds. Two explicit families show that its two
singular orders cannot in general be improved.

\subsection{The experiment}
Fix integers $2\le k\le\min(m_1,m_2)$, real numbers
$L<D<T_1<T_2<T_3$, and $0<\alpha_*<1/k$.
Let $\cK$ be the compact set of parameters
\[
 \omega=(U,V,\alpha,\xi),\qquad
 U\in\R^{m_1\times k},\quad V\in\R^{m_2\times k},
\]
where the columns of $U,V$ are probability vectors,
$\alpha_b\ge\alpha_*$, $\sum_b\alpha_b=1$, and
$L\le\xi_1\le\cdots\le\xi_k\le D$.
Rank-deficient channels and repeated actions are included. Define
\begin{align}
 h&=\sum_b\alpha_b\xi_b,&
 p_b(T)&=\frac{\alpha_b(T-\xi_b)}{T-h},\nonumber\\
 P_\omega(T)&=U\diag(p_b(T))V^{\mathsf T},&
 \mathcal P(\omega)&=(P_\omega(T_1),P_\omega(T_2),P_\omega(T_3)).
 \label{eq:v87-model}
\end{align}
Every $P_\omega(T)$ is a probability matrix. All weights $p_b(T)$
have a positive lower bound on $[T_1,T_3]$. The normalization of raw
matrices $c(T)U\diag(a_b(T-\xi_b))V^{\mathsf T}$ gives
\eqref{eq:v87-model}, with $\alpha_b=a_b/\sum_ca_c$, for every
unknown positive scalar detector $c(T)$. Moreover
\[
 \log\frac{p_b(T)}{p_c(T)}
 =\log\frac{\alpha_b}{\alpha_c}+\log(T-\xi_b)-\log(T-\xi_c).
\]
Thus the original logarithmic action law is retained. Componentwise
clock-dependent detectors are a different experiment.

The target is the spectrum with multiplicity, metrized by
\[
 d_\infty(\xi,\xi')=\min_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_k}
          \max_b|\xi_b-\xi'_{\pi(b)}|
        =\max_b|\xi_b-\xi'_b|.
\]
The last equality holds for the displayed increasing order. We use
$\|\mathcal P-\mathcal P'\|_*=\max_j\|P_j-P'_j\|_F$.
All unspecified constants depend only on the fixed dimensions,
intervals, weight floor and clocks.

Put
\begin{align}
 A&=U\diag(\alpha_b)V^{\mathsf T},&
 B&=U\diag(\alpha_b(h-\xi_b))V^{\mathsf T},\nonumber\\
 \kappa(\omega)&=\sigma_k(U)\sigma_k(V),&
 \gamma(\omega)&=\|B\|_F,\qquad
 \sig(\omega)=\frac{\kappa\gamma}{\kappa+\gamma}.
 \label{eq:v87-signal}
\end{align}
Set $\sig=0$ when either factor vanishes. In particular $\sig$ is
continuous on the closed model. The identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-curve}
                      P_\omega(T)=A+\frac{B}{T-h}
\end{equation}
is the starting point of the argument.

\begin{theorem}[Projective spectrum and singular modulus]
\label{thm:v87-main}
If $\sig(\omega)>0$, then for every $\omega'\in\cK$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-main-bound}
 d_\infty(\xi,\xi')+|h-h'|
 \le C\min\left\{1,
          \frac{\|\mathcal P(\omega)-\mathcal P(\omega')\|_*}
               {\sig(\omega)}\right\}.
\end{equation}
There is no rank, contrast, or individual action-gap assumption on the
competitor. No individual action-gap constant enters $C$.
Consequently three clocks identify the spectrum and $h$ whenever the
channels have full column rank and not all actions coincide. Two
clocks do not identify them, even with fixed positive full-rank
channels and interior, distinct actions.

If $\xi_k-\xi_1\ge\Delta>0$, then
$\sig(\omega)\ge c_\Delta\kappa(\omega)$, so
\eqref{eq:v87-main-bound} holds with $\kappa$ in place of $\sig$ and
a constant depending additionally on $\Delta$, but on no smaller gap.
\end{theorem}

The theorem extends the earlier arbitrary-rank exact clock-pencil
inverse by a uniform, one-sided singular estimate. At simple spectra
it also identifies the channels and relative weights up to a common
permutation; at repeated spectra these factors can have additional
gauges. The estimate concerns the spectrum, and does not assert
uniform recovery of those factors through an eigenvalue collision.

For an intrinsic formulation, define on the model image
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-envelope}
 \sig_*(P)=\max\{\sig(\omega):\mathcal P(\omega)=P\}.
\end{equation}
Compactness makes the maximum attainable. This is invariant under
all latent representations of the same datum, not merely under
permutation. If it is positive, Theorem~\ref{thm:v87-main} shows that
all representatives have the same spectrum and pole. The estimate
then holds with $\sig_*(P)$, by using a maximizing representative.
For a simple spectrum the stochastic spectral factors determine
$\kappa$ up to permutation, and hence $\sig_* = \sig$.
For repeated spectra \eqref{eq:v87-envelope} is a compact
factorization problem; no unproved closed formula is assumed.

\subsection{Statistical consequence}
At each of the three clocks observe $n$ independent draws from the
$m_1m_2$-cell law, and let $N=3n$.
Section~\ref{sec:v87-statistics} constructs a residual estimator and
an honest confidence region over the entire closed model, without
supplying either conditioning factor. For each true observation $P$
with $\sig_*(P)>0$, their squared risk and expected diameter satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-stat-summary}
 \E d_\infty(\widehat\xi,\xi)^2
       \le C\min\{1,(N\sig_*(P)^2)^{-1}\},\qquad
 \E\diam_\infty\cC_\xi
       \le C_\zeta\min\{1,(\sqrt N\sig_*(P))^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}
These orders are minimax sharp on the classes
$\{\omega:\sig_*(\mathcal P(\omega))\ge s\}$ for all sufficiently
small $s>0$. They are attained on two different positive families:
one with weak channel directions and fixed action spread, and one
with full-rank channels and a collapsing action spread. The same
lower bounds hold for adaptive selection of clocks in $[T_1,T_3]$.

\subsection{Predecessors and the contribution}
Observable diagonalization and latent-structure identification are
classical; see Allman--Matias--Rhodes \cite{AMR} and
Bonhomme--Jochmans--Robin \cite{BJR}. In this experiment a clock is a
controlled covariate, not an additional conditionally independent
readout. We use the usual rank-one spectral decomposition, rather
than claim a new general tensor-identifiability theorem. The exact
three-clock reconstruction was already available in the earlier
clock-pencil derivation. The present assertion is its gap-free
singular modulus and its finite-experiment lower bounds.

Identification modulo nuisance transformations has a general
bilinear formulation in Li--Lee--Bresler \cite{LLB}. Here the
normalization nuisance has a specific one-parameter two-clock fibre,
and three observations remove it. The perturbation proof below is
related to the classical matrix-pencil perspective of Bauer--Fike
\cite{BF}; we include the required eigenvalue argument. Applying a
condition number to an observed inverse and then another to its
eigenvectors would not establish the stated product law. The essential
step is to keep the pole perturbation diagonal after multiplication
by the \emph{true} two channel inverses.

The relation between moduli and inference is classical
\cite{Donoho,CaiLow}; weak identification and nonshrinking honest
sets have important precedents in \cite{Dufour}. Ho--Nguyen
\cite{HN} study singularity structures and rates for finite mixtures.
The calculation here is for a controlled categorical experiment with
unknown channels. Partial coalescence of action values does not cause
a slower action-spectrum rate: the full matrix curve still retains
the spectrum. Loss of a channel direction and loss of the whole
projective contrast do cause singular rates, as the two exact
families demonstrate. This distinction is a statement about the
observation map and the chosen target, not about finite mixtures in
general.

\section{The projective fibre and its third-clock resolution}
\label{sec:v87-clock}
\begin{lemma}[Pole recovery]\label{lem:v87-pole}
If $\gamma>0$, set $X=P_1-P_2$, $Y=P_2-P_3$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-ratio}
 \frac{\langle X,Y\rangle_F}{\|Y\|_F^2}
 =\frac{T_2-T_1}{T_3-T_2}\frac{T_3-h}{T_1-h},\qquad
 c\gamma\le\|Y\|_F\le C\gamma.
\end{equation}
If $\delta=\|\mathcal P-\mathcal P'\|_*\le c_0\gamma$ for a
sufficiently small fixed $c_0$, the candidate also has $\gamma'>0$,
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-pole-error}
                         |h-h'|\le C\delta/\gamma.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Subtract \eqref{eq:v87-curve} at consecutive clocks. The differences
are scalar multiples of $B$, which gives \eqref{eq:v87-ratio}.
All scalar multipliers and their relevant reciprocals are bounded
because $h\in[L,D]$. If $\delta\le c_0\gamma$, then
$\|Y'\|_F\ge\|Y\|_F-2\delta\ge c\gamma/2$; in particular $B'$
is nonzero. The difference of the two ratios is at most
$C\delta/\gamma$: expand their numerators and denominators, using
$\|X\|_F,\|Y\|_F,\|X'\|_F,\|Y'\|_F\le C\gamma$ and the
lower bounds on the denominators. The derivative of the right side
of \eqref{eq:v87-ratio} is
\[
 \frac{T_2-T_1}{T_3-T_2}\frac{T_3-T_1}{(T_1-h)^2},
\]
which is positive and bounded below on $[L,D]$. This proves
\eqref{eq:v87-pole-error}.
\end{proof}
Once $h$ is recovered, two values of $(T-h)P(T)$ give the affine
polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-cleared}
                  K(T)=U\diag(\alpha_b(T-\xi_b))V^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
Its reduced generalized eigenvalues are the actions, with
multiplicity. Section~\ref{sec:v87-inverse} proves this and its
stability without first recovering individual eigenvectors.

\begin{proposition}[The exact two-clock fibre]\label{prop:v87-fibre}
Fix an interior parameter with positive full-rank channels and distinct
actions. Its two-clock fibre contains a smooth nonconstant curve with
these same channels. With $H=(T_1-h)(T_2-h)$ and sufficiently small
real $u$, this curve is
\begin{align}
 h_u&=h+u,\nonumber\\
 \alpha_{b,u}&=\alpha_b\left(1+\frac{u(h-\xi_b)}{H}\right),
                                                        \label{eq:v87-gauge}\\
 \xi_{b,u}&=\frac{\xi_b+u\{1+(h-\xi_b)(T_1+T_2-h)/H\}}
                  {1+u(h-\xi_b)/H}.\nonumber
\end{align}
Both observed matrices are exactly unchanged. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-gauge-derivative}
 \left.\frac{d\xi_{b,u}}{du}\right|_{u=0}
                =\frac{(T_1-\xi_b)(T_2-\xi_b)}{H}>0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $q_b(T)$ be the affine interpolant of $p_b(T_1),p_b(T_2)$.
Direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-q}
 q_b(T)=\alpha_b\left[1+(h-\xi_b)
                           \frac{T_1+T_2-h-T}{H}\right].
\end{equation}
The affine function
$\alpha_b(T-\xi_b)-u q_b(T)$ has slope $\alpha_{b,u}$ and root
$\xi_{b,u}$. At $T_j$, $j=1,2$, it equals
$(T_j-h-u)p_b(T_j)$. Summing over $b$ gives the polynomial
$T-h-u$. Thus $\sum_b\alpha_{b,u}=1$ and
$\sum_b\alpha_{b,u}\xi_{b,u}=h+u$, and the two normalized weights
are unchanged. Positivity, the weight floor, distinctness and action
bounds persist for small $u$ by the interior assumptions.
Differentiation gives \eqref{eq:v87-gauge-derivative}.
\end{proof}
The obstruction is present even when the channels are fixed. It is
therefore a normalization fibre, not a failure of a spectral algorithm.

\section{A gap-free one-sided inverse}
\label{sec:v87-inverse}
We first record an elementary spectral fact. The use of a multiset
rather than individually labelled eigenvectors is essential.

\begin{lemma}[Matching a perturbed real diagonal spectrum]
\label{lem:v87-match}
Let $D_0=\diag(t_1,\ldots,t_k)$ with increasing real $t_b$.
If a real matrix $D_0+E_0$ has real eigenvalues
$t'_1\le\cdots\le t'_k$, counted with algebraic multiplicity, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-match}
             \max_b|t_b-t'_b|\le(2k-1)\|E_0\|_\op.
\end{equation}
Repeated eigenvalues in either spectrum are allowed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\varepsilon=\|E_0\|_\op$. For every $0\le s\le1$, the
spectrum of $D_0+sE_0$ is in the union of the complex disks of radius
$\varepsilon$ about $t_b$: outside this union the diagonal resolvent
has norm less than $\varepsilon^{-1}$, so a Neumann series proves
invertibility. Use slightly larger disks first, choosing their radius
so distinct connected components have disjoint boundaries. The number
of eigenvalues in each component is constant along the homotopy,
by the argument principle for the characteristic polynomial. It is
the number of its centres, counted with multiplicity. A component
with $r$ centres has diameter between extreme centres at most
$2(r-1)\varepsilon$, in the limiting radius. A root in the component
is consequently at distance at most $(2r-1)\varepsilon$ from every
one of those centres. Match within each component and let the
additional radius decrease to zero. For real ordered spectra an
increasing matching minimizes the maximum distance: an inverted pair
can be uncrossed without increasing that distance. This proves
\eqref{eq:v87-match}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v87-main}]
It suffices first to consider $\delta\le c_0\sig$.
Let $L_U=U^\dagger$ and $R_V=(V^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}$ be the
true channel inverses. They are used only in the proof, not supplied
to the estimator. Their norms have product $\kappa^{-1}$.
Let $K'(T)=(T-h')P_{\omega'}(T)$, an affine matrix polynomial even
when the candidate channels are singular, and write
\[
                         M(T)=L_UK'(T)R_V.
\]
At $T_j$, $j=1,2$,
\begin{align}
 M(T_j)&=\diag(\alpha_b(T_j-\xi_b))
        -(h'-h)\diag(p_b(T_j))+F_j,\nonumber\\
 F_j&=(T_j-h')L_U(P'_j-P_j)R_V,
              \qquad \|F_j\|_\op\le C\delta/\kappa.
                                                    \label{eq:v87-whiten}
\end{align}
Affinely interpolate this identity. The coefficient norms of the
interpolant of $F_1,F_2$ are bounded by $C\delta/\kappa$.
The pole term is diagonal, with scalar affine entries $q_b$ in
\eqref{eq:v87-q}; their coefficients are uniformly bounded. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-coefficients}
 M(T)=(D_\alpha+E_1)T-D_\alpha D_\xi+E_0,
 \qquad \|E_0\|_\op+\|E_1\|_\op
       \le C\delta(\kappa^{-1}+\gamma^{-1})=C\delta/\sig.
\end{equation}
Here $D_\alpha=\diag(\alpha_b)$ and $D_\xi=\diag(\xi_b)$.
This is the cancellation in the proof: the pole error is \emph{not}
multiplied by a second channel inverse bound.

For small enough $c_0$, $D_\alpha+E_1$ is invertible, with inverse
norm at most $2/\alpha_*$. The roots of $\det M(T)$ are the
spectrum of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-root-matrix}
 Z=(D_\alpha+E_1)^{-1}(D_\alpha D_\xi-E_0),\qquad
            \|Z-D_\xi\|_\op\le C\delta/\sig.
\end{equation}
To identify those roots without a hypothesis on the candidate, observe
that the leading coefficient is also
\[
                 (L_UU')\diag(\alpha'_b)(V'^{\mathsf T}R_V).
\]
Its invertibility forces both outer square factors to be invertible.
Thus
\[
 \det M(T)=\det(L_UU')\det(V'^{\mathsf T}R_V)
                         \prod_b\alpha'_b(T-\xi'_b).
\]
In particular $Z$ has exactly the real candidate spectrum, with
multiplicity. Lemma~\ref{lem:v87-match} and
\eqref{eq:v87-root-matrix} prove the action bound.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v87-pole} proves the pole bound. When
$\delta>c_0\sig$, compactness of the action interval proves
\eqref{eq:v87-main-bound} after increasing $C$.

If the channels have full rank, $B=0$ is equivalent to
$\alpha_b(h-\xi_b)=0$ for every $b$, by multiplying by the two
channel inverses. It is therefore equivalent to all actions being
equal. This proves the stated domain of exact identification; the
necessity of three clocks follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:v87-fibre}.
Finally,
\[
 \gamma=\|U\diag(\alpha_b(h-\xi_b))V^{\mathsf T}\|_F
 \ge\kappa\Big(\sum_b\alpha_b^2(h-\xi_b)^2\Big)^{1/2}
 \ge\tfrac12\alpha_*\Delta\kappa
\]
if $\xi_k-\xi_1\ge\Delta$, because at least one endpoint is at
distance $\Delta/2$ from $h$. The formula for $\sig$ proves the
last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Simple and repeated spectral factors]
\label{prop:v87-factors}
For full-rank channels and a nonconstant spectrum, the three matrices
determine the spectrum with multiplicity. If the actions are distinct,
they also determine $U,V,\alpha$ up to a simultaneous permutation.
For a repeated action, its total matrix coefficient is determined;
individual rank-one factors within that coefficient need not be.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The pole lemma determines $h$, so the slope $A$ and constant
coefficient of $K(T)$ are observed. Take row and column index sets
$I,J$ such that $A_{IJ}$ is invertible. Then
\[
 K(T_1)_{IJ}A_{IJ}^{-1}
                  =U_I\diag(T_1-\xi_b)U_I^{-1}.
\]
For a simple eigenvalue its spectral projector $\Pi_b$ yields
\[
 E_b=A_{:J}A_{IJ}^{-1}\Pi_b A_{I:}
                          =\alpha_b U_{:b}V_{:b}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Summing all entries gives $\alpha_b$; dividing the row and column
sums by $\alpha_b$ gives the two stochastic columns. A repeated
eigenvalue has a unique projector onto its whole eigenspace, which
yields $\sum_{\xi_b=t}\alpha_bU_{:b}V_{:b}^{\mathsf T}$ instead.
Different positive factorizations of this matrix can give the same
curve. The spectrum statement does not require choosing one.
\end{proof}
For example, with three columns in each full-rank channel, the spectra
$(a,a,b)$ with $a\ne b$ are covered uniformly as two nearby actions
approach $a$. In contrast, $(a,a,a)$ produces a constant normalized
curve; varying $a$ leaves it unchanged. Individual gaps and whole
spectrum collapse are mathematically different limits.

\section{The observation modulus and honest inference}
\label{sec:v87-statistics}
The next elementary principle isolates what is common to exact fibres
and finite-sample uncertainty. Its statistical ingredients are
classical; the preceding section supplies the model-specific modulus.

\begin{lemma}[Residual inversion of an observation quotient]
\label{lem:v87-modulus}
Let a compact parameter space $\Theta$ have continuous observation map
$F$ into a finite product of probability simplices and continuous target
$g$ in a compact metric space. For a true $\theta$, define
\[
 \Omega_\theta(t)=\sup\{d(g(\theta),g(\theta')):
                                  \|F(\theta')-F(\theta)\|\le t\}.
\]
Then $\Omega_\theta(0)=0$ precisely when the target is constant on
the exact observation fibre. In that case $\Omega_\theta(t)\to0$
as $t\downarrow0$. If $e=\|\widehat F-F(\theta)\|$, a residual
minimizer has target error at most $\Omega_\theta(2e)$.
The target image of $\{\theta':\|F(\theta')-\widehat F\|\le\eta\}$
has diameter at most $2\Omega_\theta(e+\eta)$, whenever nonempty.
On $e\le\eta$ it contains the true target.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first equivalence is the definition at zero. If continuity at
zero failed, choose a sequence of competitors at observation distance
tending to zero and target distance bounded below; a compact
subsequence contradicts the equivalence. A residual minimizer has
residual at most $e$, since the truth is feasible, and the triangle
inequality gives distance at most $2e$ from the true observation.
Every feasible candidate in the confidence set is at distance at most
$e+\eta$ from it. Applying the modulus to each candidate proves
the diameter bound. Inclusion of the truth is immediate.
\end{proof}

For \eqref{eq:v87-model}, let $q=m_1m_2$ and let $\widehat P_j$
be the empirical matrices. Given $0<\zeta<1$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-radius}
 \eta=\sqrt{\frac{q}{2n}\log\frac{6q}{\zeta}},\qquad
 \cC=\{\omega\in\cK:\max_j\|P_\omega(T_j)-\widehat P_j\|_F\le\eta\}.
\end{equation}
Report the set $\cC_\xi$ of its action spectra. If the set is empty,
report model inconsistency and the empty set, not an assigned action.
Its diameter is assigned zero only for the loss calculation. Coordinate
intervals from the smallest and largest ordered actions in $\cC$
give simultaneous bands with the same maximum-coordinate width bound.
A minimum-residual estimate is defined over the full compact model.

\begin{theorem}[Honesty and adaptation without signal floors]
\label{thm:v87-confidence}
The region \eqref{eq:v87-radius} has coverage at least $1-\zeta$
uniformly over $\cK$, including both singular boundaries. For every
true observation $P$ with $s=\sig_*(P)>0$, on the coverage event
\[
                    \diam_\infty\cC_\xi\le C\min\{1,\eta/s\}.
\]
For fixed $\zeta$ the expectation and risk estimates
\eqref{eq:v87-stat-summary} hold. At $s=0$ the assertions are coverage
and bounded-range losses, not shrinking diameter.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An entrywise error bound $t$ implies a Frobenius error bound
$\sqrt q\,t$. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over the
$3q$ entries give
\[
 \Pr\{\max_j\|\widehat P_j-P_j\|_F>\eta\}
 \le 6q\exp(-2n\eta^2/q)=\zeta.
\]
For $q=4$ the conversion is exactly $2t$, not $t$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v87-main}, applied to a maximizing representative
in \eqref{eq:v87-envelope}, bounds the action modulus by
$C\min\{1,t/s\}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:v87-modulus} proves coverage
and the eventwise diameter bound.

No exceptional event is discarded in the expectation. On every
outcome with nonempty $\cC$ its diameter is at most
$C\min\{1,(e+\eta)/s\}$. Also
\[
 \E e^2\le\sum_{j=1}^3\E\|\widehat P_j-P_j\|_F^2
     =\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^3\left(1-\sum_{a,b}(P_j)_{ab}^2\right)
     \le3/n.
\]
The empty-set convention preserves this bound. Taking expectations
proves the diameter assertion. The residual estimator has error
bounded by $C\min\{1,e/s\}$ on every outcome; squaring and using
the same second moment proves the risk assertion. Compactness gives
minimizers and extrema. Measurable choices exist without a selection
issue, since the fixed-$n$ categorical sample space is finite.
\end{proof}
Coverage requires independence within each clock. Independence between
clocks is used below for the simple fixed-design likelihood argument.
Finite forward nets of $\cK$ give an exhaustive implementation:
if the mesh in parameter space is $r$, uniform forward Lipschitz
continuity allows padding the residual radius by $C r$ and the action
bands by $r$. The preceding proofs then replace $\eta$ by
$\eta+C r$. Neither inverse conditioning nor a guessed latent rank
floor is needed; the capacity $k$ is part of the declared experiment.

\section{Product singularities in every latent rank}
\label{sec:v87-product}
The following family exposes the loss which remains after both
observed marginals have been held fixed. It also shows why a product
rather than an individual channel weakness occurs.

Let $u_0=m_1^{-1}\one$ and $v_0=m_2^{-1}\one$. Choose unit zero-sum
vectors $e\in\R^{m_1}$, $f\in\R^{m_2}$. If $k>2$, choose
orthonormal zero-sum vectors $e_3,\ldots,e_k$ orthogonal to $e$,
and $f_3,\ldots,f_k$ orthogonal to $f$. Their existence follows from
$m_i\ge k$. Fix a small $c>0$. Choose distinct interior actions,
with the first two $\xi_1=s_0-d$, $\xi_2=s_0+d$, and all others
strictly above $\xi_2$ and separated from this pair. Take $\alpha_b=1/k$. For
$0\le t\le t_0<d/2$, put $\lambda=1-t/d$ and
\begin{align}
 \xi_{1,t}&=\xi_1+t,& \xi_{2,t}&=\xi_2-t,
       &\xi_{b,t}&=\xi_b\quad(b\ge3),\nonumber\\
 U_{t,:1}&=u_0+\frac{\epsilon_u e}{2\lambda},&
 U_{t,:2}&=u_0-\frac{\epsilon_u e}{2\lambda},&
 U_{t,:b}&=u_0+c e_b\quad(b\ge3),\label{eq:v87-lf}\\
 V_{t,:1}&=v_0+\frac{\epsilon_v f}{2\lambda},&
 V_{t,:2}&=v_0-\frac{\epsilon_v f}{2\lambda},&
 V_{t,:b}&=v_0+c f_b\quad(b\ge3).\nonumber
\end{align}
Take $0\le\epsilon_u,\epsilon_v\le\epsilon_*$ with $\epsilon_*$
sufficiently small. All channel and joint cell probabilities have
positive lower bounds independent of $t,\epsilon_u,\epsilon_v$.
The pole $h$ is constant along the family.

\begin{lemma}[Exact marginal cancellation]\label{lem:v87-lf}
Both readout marginals in \eqref{eq:v87-lf} are independent of $t$
at every clock in $[T_1,T_3]$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-lf-identity}
 P_t(T)-P_0(T)=
 \frac{T-s_0}{2k(T-h)}\epsilon_u\epsilon_v(\lambda^{-2}-1)ef^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
For positive contrasts,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-lf-signal}
 \kappa(\omega_t)=\frac{\epsilon_u\epsilon_v}{2\lambda^2},
 \qquad \sig_*(\mathcal P(\omega_t))
            =\sig(\omega_t)\asymp\epsilon_u\epsilon_v.
\end{equation}
The observation distance is comparable to
$\epsilon_u\epsilon_v t$. If either contrast is zero, all these
laws are exactly equal although the spectrum changes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In the pair contribution, expand
\[
 (T-s_0+\lambda d)
    (u_0+\epsilon_u e/(2\lambda))(v_0+\epsilon_v f/(2\lambda))^{\mathsf T}
 +(T-s_0-\lambda d)
    (u_0-\epsilon_u e/(2\lambda))(v_0-\epsilon_v f/(2\lambda))^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Its constant part is $2(T-s_0)u_0v_0^{\mathsf T}$, its linear parts
are $d\epsilon_u ev_0^{\mathsf T}+d\epsilon_v u_0f^{\mathsf T}$,
and its bilinear part is
$(T-s_0)\epsilon_u\epsilon_v ef^{\mathsf T}/(2\lambda^2)$.
Divide by $k(T-h)$ and subtract $t=0$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:v87-lf-identity}. Zero sums of $e,f$ prove the marginal
assertion directly.

The vector $(1,-1,0,\ldots,0)/\sqrt2$ is a right singular vector
of $U_t$ with singular value $\epsilon_u/(\sqrt2\lambda)$.
On its orthogonal complement the map is independent of $\epsilon_u$
and has a positive smallest singular value: the independent output
directions are $u_0,e_3,\ldots,e_k$. These directions are orthogonal
to $e$. Choosing $\epsilon_*$ below that fixed singular value gives
the stated exact smallest singular value. The same argument applies
to $V_t$, proving the first identity in \eqref{eq:v87-lf-signal}.
Taking marginals of $B$ shows that its $e$ and $f$ components have
magnitudes proportional to $\epsilon_u$ and $\epsilon_v$,
respectively; hence $\gamma\ge c\max(\epsilon_u,\epsilon_v)$.
Consequently $\sig\asymp\epsilon_u\epsilon_v$.
All spectra in this family are simple and the channels are full rank
when both contrasts are positive. Proposition~\ref{prop:v87-factors}
therefore gives $\sig_* = \sig$.
Finally $T-s_0$ and $T-h$ have uniform positive bounds, and
$\lambda^{-2}-1\asymp t$ on the fixed interval. This proves the
distance comparison and the exact singular statement.
\end{proof}
The vanishing interaction is the tensor product of the two contrast
directions. There are no first-order marginal changes left to exploit.
The calculation is not restricted to binary alphabets, square matrices,
or two latent components.

\section{Collapse of the whole projective spectrum}
\label{sec:v87-collapse}
Fix strictly positive full-column-rank channels, positive weights, and
distinct real numbers $z_b$ with $\sum_b\alpha_bz_b=0$.
Take an interior $a$ and sufficiently small $s\ge0$. Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-collapse}
             \xi_b(t)=a+t+s z_b,\qquad 0\le t\le t_0,
\end{equation}
with $t_0$ fixed so all actions remain in $(L,D)$.
Here $h_t=a+t$ and
\[
 A=U\diag(\alpha_b)V^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 B=-sU\diag(\alpha_bz_b)V^{\mathsf T}
\]
are independent of $t$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-collapse-identity}
 P_t(T)-P_0(T)
       =\frac{tB}{(T-a-t)(T-a)}.
\end{equation}
For $s>0$, $\gamma\asymp s$, $\kappa$ is bounded below, and
$\sig_* = \sig\asymp s$, by simple-spectrum uniqueness. The
observation distance is comparable to $st$, whereas the action
distance is exactly $t$. At $s=0$ the laws are constant in both
$T$ and $t$, although the common action changes. Thus the contrast
term in Theorem~\ref{thm:v87-main} is necessary even for perfectly
well-conditioned channels. This is a second singular boundary, not
the channel-rank boundary in Section~\ref{sec:v87-product}.

\section{Sharp statistical orders and adaptive clocks}
\label{sec:v87-lower}
\begin{theorem}[Minimax transition and honest-diameter lower bounds]
\label{thm:v87-minimax}
For each fixed admissible $k,m_1,m_2$ there is $s_*>0$ such that,
for $0<s\le s_*$ and $N=3n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-minimax}
 c\min\{1,(Ns^2)^{-1}\}
 \le\inf_{\widehat\xi}\sup_{\omega:\sig_*(\mathcal P(\omega))\ge s}
       \E_\omega d_\infty(\widehat\xi,\xi)^2
 \le C\min\{1,(Ns^2)^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}
The upper bound uses the balanced three-clock design. The lower bound
remains valid if the infimum also includes arbitrary randomized,
predictable choices of each of $N$ clocks in $[T_1,T_3]$.

Let a random spectrum region $J$ be honest at level $1-\zeta$ over
$\cK$, with $0<\zeta<1/4$. At the base point of either positive
family in Sections~\ref{sec:v87-product} and
\ref{sec:v87-collapse},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v87-diameter-lower}
 \E_0\diam_\infty J\ge c_\zeta
                      \min\{1,(\sqrt N\sig_*(P_0))^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}
This holds for any such clock strategy. At the exact singular limits
of these families there are nonvanishing risk and honest-diameter
lower bounds. Equal channel contrasts $\epsilon$ give the transition
$N\epsilon^4\asymp1$; full-spectrum spread $s$ with fixed channels
gives $Ns^2\asymp1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper bound follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:v87-confidence}.
In either lower-bound family write $\beta=\epsilon_u\epsilon_v$
or $\beta=s$, respectively. The exact identities and uniform
positive cell floors give, uniformly for $T\in[T_1,T_3]$,
\[
 \KL(P_t(T),P_0(T))
       \le\sum_{a,b}\frac{((P_t)_{ab}-(P_0)_{ab})^2}{(P_0)_{ab}}
       \le C\beta^2t^2.
\]
For a predictable randomized clock strategy, condition on the entire
past and the internal randomization at each step. The policy has the
same conditional rule under both parameters, and only the next
categorical law contributes to relative entropy. The chain rule
therefore bounds the entropy of the complete transcript by
$CN\beta^2t^2$. This includes fixed and uneven clock allocations.

Choose $t=c_1\min\{t_0,(\sqrt N\beta)^{-1}\}$ with $c_1$ small.
Pinsker's inequality makes the total variation distance of the
transcript laws at most $1/2$. The spectra are distance $t$ apart.
Choosing the nearer spectrum converts any estimator into a test, so
its larger squared risk is at least $t^2/16$.

To obtain the specified class in \eqref{eq:v87-minimax}, use the
product family with $\epsilon_u=\epsilon_v$ and choose their product
to be $M s$ for a fixed sufficiently large $M$. Uniformly on the
small $t$ interval, Lemma~\ref{lem:v87-lf} gives
$\sig_*\ge c\epsilon_u\epsilon_v\ge s$.
The upper contrast restriction holds for $s\le s_*$.
The two-point bound is then the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:v87-minimax}. The collapse family supplies the second
attained singular order as well.

For an honest set $J$, transfer coverage under the alternative to
the base law. Then
\[
 \Pr_0\{\xi(0),\xi(t)\in J\}
        \ge1-2\zeta-\TV(\mathbb P_t,\mathbb P_0).
\]
Decrease $c_1$, depending on $\zeta$, so the last expression is
bounded below by a positive constant. On this event the diameter
is at least $t$, which proves \eqref{eq:v87-diameter-lower}.
At a zero contrast, or at zero full-spectrum spread, choose fixed
distinct $t$; the exact identities make the transcript laws identical
for every policy. The same testing and coverage arguments give the
nonvanishing limits.
\end{proof}
The theorem is a uniform order statement and gives pointwise
lower bounds on the displayed families. It does not identify the
exact directional condition number of every anisotropic latent
factorization. In particular a small product computed from an
arbitrary repeated-spectrum representative is not declared to be
an intrinsic obstruction; \eqref{eq:v87-envelope} removes precisely
that dependence on representation.

\section{Relation to the other observation quotients}
\label{sec:v87-relations}
The result reorganizes two earlier mechanisms: the arbitrary-rank
projective clock pencil and the binary determinant-product modulus.
For $k=m_1=m_2=2$, stochastic columns imply
\[
 \frac{|\det U|}{\sqrt2}\le\sigma_2(U)\le|\det U|,
 \qquad
 \frac{|\det V|}{\sqrt2}\le\sigma_2(V)\le|\det V|.
\]
Indeed $1\le\sigma_1(U),\sigma_1(V)\le\sqrt2$, and the product
of singular values is the absolute determinant. On the separated
action/reference class, Theorem~\ref{thm:v87-main} thus gives the
same single inverse power of $|\det U\det V|$. Also
$\alpha_1=(\xi_2-h)/(\xi_2-\xi_1)$, so a fixed action/reference
gap recovers the earlier throughput estimate. No determinant of
a higher-degree scalar polynomial or gap-dependent root derivative
is needed for the general spectrum theorem.

The shared two-clock spatial experiment has a different nuisance
space: its clock factors are common across sites. Spatial calibration
can then replace a third clock. The exact shared theorem, including
its one-clock geometric alternatives, is retained in the companion.
Its local correctly-labelled calibration estimate is not a global
noise bound through profile collisions. The new theorem supplies a
singular theory for the projective affine experiment; it does not
silently transfer its modulus to that different shared experiment.

Likewise, the polynomial, rational and compact-surface detector
quotients retain their own secant and tangent tests and clock designs.
They are preserved in full, together with the branched-clock version
of the unchanged logarithmic law. Their divisor counts remain valid
sufficient counts. The sharp count here is governed by the affine
numerator and one projective normalization parameter, not by a claim
that an ambient meromorphic pole budget is always optimal.

The companion also retains finite-record geometric certificates,
component coverings, return-lift constructions, and raw-acquisition
laws. Metric rigidity there is supplied by the stated classical
boundary-rigidity theorems. The present theorem is an upstream
observation result: it adds neither a partial-data boundary-rigidity
claim nor a new geometric hypothesis. Separating these logical roles
allows the action inverse and its singular statistical transition to
form a self-contained theorem, while retaining the other mathematical
regimes with all their assumptions.
