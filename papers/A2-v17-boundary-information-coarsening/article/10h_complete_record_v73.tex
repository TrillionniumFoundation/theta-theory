\section{The complete invariant of the charged local experiment}
\label{sec:v73-complete-record}

The conditional laws determine the reflecting germs and the offsets, but
conditioning removes the normalization of phase volume.  The charged
experiment retains it.  We identify the exact information added by the
success probabilities and prove a finite-preparation estimate for the
resulting invariant.  In particular the reconstructed area will belong
to the same actual table as the reconstructed contacts and offsets.

Throughout this section the marked lattice and polygon are fixed, all
physical contacts are distinct, and $p_b\ne0$.  Offsets $d_b>0$ are
fixed nuisance parameters as in Section~\ref{sec:v72-one-law}.  Put
$\vartheta=(T,d)$ and write $\mathcal A(T)>0$ for the free area of its
marked cell.  A failure records only $\dagger$.  Every preparation is an
independent draw from normalized Liouville measure.  These conventions
are unchanged when a success probability is used as inverse data.

\subsection{An exact anchor for phase volume}

For a returning $N=nP$, denote the success probability at phase $b$ by
$\pi^\vartheta_{N,b}$ and its conditional endpoint density by
$f^\vartheta_{N,b}$.  The density is normalized on the actual success
gate $Q_+$, not on a later interior restriction.  The superscript will
be suppressed when there is no ambiguity.  The reference twist $D_{N,b}$
is computed in the returning signed coordinates of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v65-signed-jacobi}.

\begin{proposition}[The phase-volume anchor]\label{prop:v73-area-anchor}
For every permitted positive-residual gate containing the origin,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v73-area-anchor}
 \pi_{N,b}f_{N,b}(0,0)
   =\frac{D_{N,b}d_b}{2\pi\mathcal A(T)},\qquad
 \mathcal A(T)=\frac{D_{N,b}d_b}
 {2\pi\pi_{N,b}f_{N,b}(0,0)}.
\end{equation}
The identities hold at finite $N$, without a limiting approximation.
Once the limiting conditional laws have determined the contact germs
and offsets, the right side contains no supplied curvature or amplitude.
It uses the success probability and central conditional density of one
finite experiment.

Alternatively, at any one fixed phase the additional scalar
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v73-count-scalar}
 \ell_b:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{nP,b}}{D_{nP,b}}
       =\frac{d_b}{2\pi\mathcal A(T)f_b(0,0)}
\end{equation}
exists and determines the area together with the limiting laws.  Here
$D_{nP,b}$ is reconstructed from the contact curvatures, not supplied as
additional data.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The success subdensity in \eqref{eq:v71-local-measure} is
\[
 \frac{D_{N,b}}{2\pi\mathcal A(T)}
       \beta_{N,b}(u,v)r_{N,b,d_b}(u,v).
\]
At the origin $\beta_{N,b}(0,0)=1$ exactly, by the definition of the
reference twist.  The gauged action is zero there, so
$r_{N,b,d_b}(0,0)=d_b$.  The subdensity is also
$\pi_{N,b}f_{N,b}$, proving \eqref{eq:v73-area-anchor}.
The relative law gives $f_{nP,b}(0,0)\to f_b(0,0)>0$; division in
the first identity proves \eqref{eq:v73-count-scalar}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v72-rigidity} determines $d_b$ and the actual contact
germs.  Their curvatures and the marked flight lengths determine the
Jacobi matrices and hence $D_{N,b}$ by
\eqref{eq:v64-reference-twist}.  No unknown normalization survives.
\end{proof}

There is an equivalent raw exponential normalization.  With $\chi$ and
$\mathcal M_b$ reconstructed from those same curvatures,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v73-raw-count}
 \lim_{n\to\infty} e^{n\chi}\pi_{nP,b}
 =\frac{d_b\sinh\chi}
 {\pi\mathcal A(T)(\mathcal M_b)_{12}f_b(0,0)}.
\end{equation}
This follows because $e^{n\chi}D_{nP,b}\to
2\sinh\chi/(\mathcal M_b)_{12}$.  The prefactor, not just the
exponential decay exponent, is needed for area recovery.  Neither
\eqref{eq:v73-count-scalar} nor \eqref{eq:v73-raw-count} is an
observation of an infinite limit from a finite sample.  A charged
finite-sample construction is given below.

\subsection{The fibers of the observation map}

We use germs of the local experiment in the precise sense of
Section~\ref{sec:v71-local-observation}.  Two experiments agree as germs
when they agree on all sufficiently small common positive gates and all
sufficiently long returning lengths, with the gate normalization specified
anew.  Equivalently one may restrict to a cofinal family of such gates.
This qualification does not assert that equality of contact germs identifies
trajectories whose endpoints lie outside their common representatives.
Write
\[
 \mathfrak I(\vartheta)=
    \bigl((F_b^T)_b\text{ as germs},\ (d_b)_b,\ \mathcal A(T)\bigr).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Complete local observation invariant]
\label{thm:v73-fibers}
For actual marked tables in the stated class, equality of the germs of
all charged reset records is equivalent to equality of
$\mathfrak I(\vartheta)$.  Equality of the single-offset limiting
conditional law germs alone is equivalent to equality of the first two
components of this invariant.

In addition to the limiting law germs, the scalar
\eqref{eq:v73-count-scalar} at one phase and one fixed common gate
suffices to determine the remaining component.  The finite alternative
\eqref{eq:v73-area-anchor} also suffices.  No remote boundary profile is
included in this conclusion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equality of the charged records gives equality of their success masses
and, since these are positive, equality of their conditional densities.
Taking the relative limit on each common gate and applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:v72-rigidity} identifies the offsets and all contact
germs.  In particular both candidates have the same quadratic Jacobi
operator, and therefore the same $D_{N,b}$.  Their common success
subdensity at the origin in any one finite experiment identifies their
areas by \eqref{eq:v73-area-anchor}.

Conversely, suppose the three components of $\mathfrak I$ agree.
Choose representatives of the common contact germs and shrink the gates
so that all stationary bridges and allowed end segments stay in these
collars and their clear trajectory tubes.  The common margins and weighted
uniqueness used in Proposition~\ref{prop:v71-locality} apply.  That
proposition's proof uses only equality of the offset in each experiment,
not that the offsets were reported to the observer.  It therefore gives
identical success submeasures at every phase and returning length.
Equal areas remove its sole multiplicative factor.  The failure masses
then agree as well.  This proves the charged-record assertion.

For limiting conditional laws, the forward implication is exactly
Theorem~\ref{thm:v72-rigidity}.  The converse follows from the same
locality calculation: the area factor cancels upon conditioning, whether
or not the areas agree.  Finally
Proposition~\ref{prop:v73-area-anchor} proves both assertions about
one additional scalar record.
\end{proof}

The area component is independent of the conditional one.  A smooth bump
on an unvisited boundary arc, disjoint from the fixed collars and trajectory
tubes, can have nonzero first area variation.  For sufficiently small
parameter it preserves strict convexity, clearance and all marks, while
changing free area.  The conditional experiments remain identical, but
their charged success masses differ by the exact area ratio.  With the
second compensating bump of Corollary~\ref{cor:v71-local-completion},
the area can instead be held exactly fixed.  That nonconstant family
has the same complete charged-record germ.  Thus the theorem describes
both the recovered global scalar and the precise surviving freedom in
remote completion; it does not infer a whole smooth table from local data.

The equivalence also holds for every fixed finite adaptive composition
whose settings are chosen from these local experiments by a common
record-based rule and independent randomization.  Equality of the one-step
record kernels proves this by conditioning on the past.  The deterministic
one-step experiments are among these compositions, so the converse is
unchanged.  This statement is about equality of experiment families, not
about a sufficient statistic for one fixed finite sample.

\subsection{Sensitivity of the rare normalization}

Area reconstruction requires relative control of the reference twist.
An error in curvature cannot be propagated through $N$ flights with a
constant independent of $N$.  The following estimate retains that cost.
Write $c_i=F_i''(0)$, fix $k_i=L_i^{-1}$, and use the sign-conjugated
transfer matrices with masses $2c_i$.  All derivatives below are with
respect to the finite vector $c$.

\begin{lemma}[Logarithmic twist comparison]\label{lem:v73-log-twist}
On a fixed compact positive curvature box there is $C$ such that, for
all its $c,\widetilde c$, phases $b$, and $N=nP$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v73-log-twist}
 \left|\log\frac{D_{N,b}(\widetilde c)}{D_{N,b}(c)}\right|
 \le C(1+N)\|\widetilde c-c\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v73-log-twist-derivative}
 \nabla\log D_{N,b}
 =\{\coth\chi-n\coth(n\chi)\}\nabla\chi
               -\nabla\log(\mathcal M_b)_{12}.
\end{equation}
Along a smooth curvature path, if $\dot\chi\ne0$, then
$n^{-1}\partial_t\log D_{nP,b}\to-\dot\chi$.  Thus the flight-length
factor cannot in general be deleted from this normalization estimate.
This last assertion is not a minimax lower bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every matrix
\[
 Q_i(c_i)=
 \begin{pmatrix}1+2c_i/k_i&1/k_i\\2c_i&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
has determinant one and positive entries.  On the compact positive box,
$\chi(c)=\operatorname{arcosh}(\operatorname{tr}\mathcal M_b(c)/2)$
is smooth with a positive lower bound, and $(\mathcal M_b)_{12}$ has
a positive lower bound.  Their first derivatives are bounded.
Taking the logarithm of \eqref{eq:v64-reference-twist} and
differentiating proves \eqref{eq:v73-log-twist-derivative}.
Since $\coth(n\chi)\le\coth\chi_*$ for $n\ge1$, the gradient is
bounded by $C(1+n)$.  Integrating over the straight segment between the
curvature vectors proves \eqref{eq:v73-log-twist}.  Dividing the
derivative identity by $n$ proves the final limit.
There are admissible directions with $\dot\chi\ne0$: increasing one
positive curvature makes the trace of the positive monodromy increase
strictly.  This follows directly by differentiating the displayed
positive matrix product.  A sufficiently small local curvature bump
realizes such a direction while preserving the marked polygon and
its open margins.
\end{proof}

We next turn the identity into an estimate on realizable finite records.
Use the bounded class $\mathscr U$ of Section~\ref{sec:v72-one-law},
with $m\ge3$ chosen so that $6a^m/(1-a^m)<1$ on the enlarged positive
quadratic class, and the same finite forward derivative bounds.  Choose
$Q\Subset Q_+$ within the common stability collar, with $0\in\operatorname{int}Q$.
Densities below are normalized on $Q_+$ and restricted to $Q$ without
renormalization.  Fix one phase $b_0$.  The forward density floors are
uniform for $N\ge N_0$, after increasing $N_0$ if necessary.

\begin{proposition}[Stability on the finite-record image]
\label{prop:v73-finite-record-stability}
For $\vartheta=(T,d)$ and $\widetilde\vartheta=(\widetilde T,\widetilde d)$
in $\mathscr U$, put
\begin{align*}
 \Delta_f&=\max_b\|f^\vartheta_{N,b}-
                       f^{\widetilde\vartheta}_{N,b}\|_{C^m(Q)},\\
 \Delta_\pi&=\left|\log\frac{\pi^{\widetilde\vartheta}_{N,b_0}}
                                      {\pi^\vartheta_{N,b_0}}\right|,
 \qquad e_N=\Delta_f+e^{-\omega N}.
\end{align*}
There are class constants $C,\eta_1>0$ such that $e_N\le\eta_1$ implies
\begin{align}
 \|F^T-F^{\widetilde T}\|_{C^0(I)}
 +\|d-\widetilde d\|_\infty+\|c-\widetilde c\|_\infty
                         &\le Ce_N,\label{eq:v73-local-record-stability}\\
 \left|\log\frac{\mathcal A(\widetilde T)}{\mathcal A(T)}\right|
                         &\le C(1+N)e_N+\Delta_\pi.
                         \label{eq:v73-area-stability}
\end{align}
An upper bound for the areas also gives the corresponding absolute area
estimate.  No absolute error is divided by an exponentially small twist.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The uniform relative comparison bounds the distance between the two
limiting law tuples by $\Delta_f+2C_0e^{-\omega N}$.  Make $\eta_1$
small enough that Theorem~\ref{thm:v72-rigidity} applies.  Its proof
first applies the fixed-slice separation to obtain $C^m$ control of the
future actions.  Their second derivatives, followed by the global
positive quadratic estimate \eqref{eq:v66-pair-lipschitz}, control
$c-\widetilde c$ as well.  This proves
\eqref{eq:v73-local-record-stability}.

Apply the finite identity \eqref{eq:v73-area-anchor} at $b_0$ to
both actual candidates.  Subtraction of its logarithms gives
\begin{align*}
 \log\frac{\mathcal A(\widetilde T)}{\mathcal A(T)}
 ={}&\log\frac{D_{N,b_0}(\widetilde c)}{D_{N,b_0}(c)}
       +\log\frac{\widetilde d_{b_0}}{d_{b_0}}
       -\log\frac{\pi^{\widetilde\vartheta}_{N,b_0}}
                       {\pi^\vartheta_{N,b_0}}\\
    &-\log\frac{f^{\widetilde\vartheta}_{N,b_0}(0,0)}
                       {f^\vartheta_{N,b_0}(0,0)}.
\end{align*}
The positive offset and density floors control the second and fourth
terms by $Ce_N$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v73-log-twist} and
\eqref{eq:v73-local-record-stability} control the first term.
This proves \eqref{eq:v73-area-stability}.  If both areas are at most
$\mathcal A_+$, the mean value theorem for the exponential gives
$|\mathcal A(\widetilde T)-\mathcal A(T)|\le
\mathcal A_+|\log\mathcal A(\widetilde T)-\log\mathcal A(T)|$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A jointly realizable estimator}

The count at phase $b_0$ is already part of the charged record; it does
not require another preparation group.  To keep the area and contacts
compatible, use the finite-record image
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v73-record-image}
 \mathcal J_N(\vartheta)=
   \bigl((f^\vartheta_{N,b}|_Q)_b,\log\pi^\vartheta_{N,b_0}\bigr)
 \in C^m(Q)^J\times\mathbb R,\qquad J=r,
\end{equation}
with the sum of the maximum density norm and the scalar absolute value.
For each returning $N$ choose a countable dense subset of this image
and retain one actual representative $\vartheta_j\in\mathscr U$ at
each point.  Such a subset exists because the ambient metric space is
separable.  The representatives are not required to exhaust the tables.

For $B$ resets in each phase, let $S_b$ be its success count and put
$q_N=c_0e^{-\Gamma N}$ and $n=\lfloor Bq_N/2\rfloor$.
On the event $S_b\ge n$ for every phase, form the interior kernel
estimates $\widehat f_{N,b}$ from the first $n$ successful marks and
put $\widehat\pi=S_{b_0}/B$.  Select the first representative whose
distance from $((\widehat f_{N,b})_b,\log\widehat\pi)$ is within
$n^{-2}$ of the infimum over the countable image.  On the complementary
event choose one fixed member of $\mathscr U$.  This defines a measurable
estimator $(\widehat T,\widehat d)$ for every record, with its area
$\mathcal A(\widehat T)$ taken from that same representative.
It is not an efficient enumeration algorithm or a projection onto an
arbitrary factor-model image.

\begin{theorem}[Charged recovery of the complete invariant]
\label{thm:v73-charged-invariant}
In the stated bounded class, set
\[
 \alpha=\frac{s}{2(m+s)+2},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{s}{m+s+3},\qquad
 L_n=\log(C_1Jn/\zeta),\qquad 0<\zeta<\tfrac12.
\]
Suppose $N\ge N_0$, $n\ge2$, $Bq_N\ge C_2\log(4J/\zeta)$ and
$L_n/n\le1$.  Use
\[
 h=\max\{(L_n/n)^{1/[2(m+s)+2]},
                    \delta^{1/(m+s+3)},n^{-1}\}\le h_0.
\]
For $e^{-\omega N}+(L_n/n)^\alpha+\delta^\gamma$ sufficiently small,
the estimator above satisfies, with probability at least $1-2\zeta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v73-charged-invariant}
 \begin{split}
  &\|F^{\widehat T}-F^T\|_{C^0(I)}
    +\|\widehat d-d\|_\infty
    +\left|\log\frac{\mathcal A(\widehat T)}{\mathcal A(T)}\right|\\
  &\hspace{12mm}\le C(1+N)
          \{e^{-\omega N}+(L_n/n)^\alpha+\delta^\gamma\}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
All $JB=rB$ resets, including failures, are charged.  Readout errors
are bounded by $\delta$ after acceptance; the tags and gates remain exact.
Only the count at $b_0$ is used as additional geometric information.
The estimate is a sufficient upper bound, not an optimality assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a binomial count with success probability $\pi_b\ge q_N$,
Bernstein's inequality gives, for $t>0$, a failure probability at most
$2e^{-t}$ for
\[
 \left|\frac{S_b/B}{\pi_b}-1\right|
 \le \sqrt{\frac{2t}{B\pi_b}}+\frac{2t}{3B\pi_b}.
\]
Take $t=\log(4J/\zeta)$.  Increasing the absolute constant $C_2$
makes the right side at most $1/2$ simultaneously for all phases,
except on an event of probability at most $\zeta$.  On that event
$S_b\ge B\pi_b/2\ge n$, and $|\log(1+x)|\le2|x|$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v73-log-count}
 |\log\widehat\pi-\log\pi_{N,b_0}|
                \le C\sqrt{t/(Bq_N)}.
\end{equation}
Independent resetting admits the Bernoulli/conditional-mark
representation from Section~\ref{sec:v69-sampled-contact}.  Thus the
first $n$ successful latent marks have the required conditional law.
The density estimate can be justified on their infinite extension even
when the finite record has fewer successes; the count event ensures
that only actually observed marks are used.  No independence between
the count error and the density error is required for the union bound.

Lemma~\ref{lem:v69-density-estimation}, before adding any physical
limit bias, gives a simultaneous finite-density error at most
\[
 C\left\{h^s+\sqrt{\frac{L_n}{nh^{2m+2}}}
       +\frac{L_n}{nh^{m+2}}+n^{-2}+\delta h^{-m-3}\right\}
\]
except on an event of probability at most $\zeta$.
The displayed bandwidth bounds this by
$C\{(L_n/n)^\alpha+\delta^\gamma\}$.
Because $n$ is comparable to $Bq_N$ under $n\ge2$, and $t\le CL_n$,
\eqref{eq:v73-log-count} has this same upper bound: $\alpha<1/2$
and $L_n/n\le1$.  The fitted finite image is consequently within
$C\{(L_n/n)^\alpha+\delta^\gamma\}$ of the true image.

Density of the countable image and the near-minimum selection show that
the distance between the selected and true images is at most twice this
error plus $n^{-2}$.  Both images correspond to actual pairs in
$\mathscr U$, including their actual areas.  Apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:v73-finite-record-stability}, adding only now
the forward term $e^{-\omega N}$.  The stipulated small-error condition
ensures its applicability.  The two exceptional events have total
probability at most $2\zeta$, proving the theorem.  There is no
independent numerical area estimate attached to an unrelated completion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A budget and readout dependent returning length]
\label{cor:v73-budget}
Let
\[
 \beta=\frac{\alpha\omega}{\omega+\alpha\Gamma},\qquad
 L_B=\log(C_*JB/\zeta),\qquad
 t_{B,\delta}=(L_B/B)^\beta+\delta^\gamma<1,
\]
and choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v73-budget-design}
 N=P\left\lfloor\frac{\log(1/t_{B,\delta})}{P\omega}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad n=\lfloor Bc_0e^{-\Gamma N}/2\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Whenever the count, bandwidth, returning-length and small-error conditions
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v73-charged-invariant} hold, its right side is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v73-budget-rate}
                C t_{B,\delta}\log(e/t_{B,\delta}).
\end{equation}
At zero readout error the returning-length choice is the same as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v72-sampling}.  At positive readout error it stops the
length before curvature error is unnecessarily amplified.  The original
profile-and-offset rate \eqref{eq:v72-rate} is retained separately;
the logarithmic factor here pays for the additionally reconstructed area.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write $x=(L_B/B)^\beta$ and $t=t_{B,\delta}\ge x$.  The floor gives
$e^{-\omega N}\le e^{\omega P}t$ and $1+N\le C\log(e/t)$.
Also $e^{-\Gamma N}\ge t^{\Gamma/\omega}$, so the lower-count
condition gives $n\ge cB t^{\Gamma/\omega}$.  Since $n\le B$,
$L_n\le CL_B$, and hence
\[
 (L_n/n)^\alpha
 \le C(L_B/B)^\alpha t^{-\alpha\Gamma/\omega}
 \le C(L_B/B)^\alpha x^{-\alpha\Gamma/\omega}=Cx\le Ct.
\]
The equality is the defining identity for $\beta$.
Finally $\delta^\gamma\le t$.  Substitution into
\eqref{eq:v73-charged-invariant} proves \eqref{eq:v73-budget-rate}.
When $\delta=0$, $(\log(1/x))/\omega=
\alpha\log(B/L_B)/(\omega+\alpha\Gamma)$, giving the last comparison.
\end{proof}

The complete invariant is therefore accessible without numerical clock
calibration, curvature data or a flux model.  Its contact part uses the
actual smooth inverse, its volume part uses the unconditioned phase
normalization, and their finite reconstruction uses relative rather than
absolute rare-event control.  The classification and estimate concern
this specified marked reset experiment.  They neither identify it with
an exterior scattering observation nor turn its nontrivial equal-area
completion fibers into determined boundary arcs.
