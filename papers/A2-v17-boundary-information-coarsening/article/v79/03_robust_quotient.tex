\section{A split quotient of arbitrary clocked observations}
\label{sec:v79-quotient}

The exact inverse has a useful extension to observations outside the
shared-weight model.  It is important here to quotient the whole
observation space, not only its model image.  Throughout this section
$Q\subset\R^d$ is a compact rectangle with nonempty interior,
$m\ge0$ is an integer, and $T_1<\cdots<T_J$, $J\ge3$, are fixed.
The $C^m$ norm is the maximum of the suprema of the derivatives of
order at most $m$.  Functions are understood as restrictions from a
neighborhood of $Q$.  Equivalent standard Banach norms give the same
statements.

Let $\mathcal D_m$ be the space of $J$ strictly positive $C^m$
probability densities on $Q$.  Give it the relative product $C^m$
topology.  The group $G_m$ of strictly positive $C^m$ functions modulo
positive constants acts by simultaneous reweighting and normalization,
as in~\eqref{eq:v78-reweight}.  Fix a scalar point $z_0\in Q$ and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-log-contrast}
 Y_j(f)(z)=\log\frac{f_j(z)}{f_1(z)}
             -\log\frac{f_j(z_0)}{f_1(z_0)},\qquad 2\le j\le J.
\end{equation}
Write $C^m_0(Q)=\{h\in C^m(Q):h(z_0)=0\}$ and
$\mathcal Y_m=(C^m_0(Q))^{J-1}$.

\begin{proposition}[The ambient observation quotient]
\label{prop:v79-ambient}
The map $Y$ induces a homeomorphism from the topological quotient
$\mathcal D_m/G_m$ onto the Banach space $\mathcal Y_m$.  In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-quotient-metric}
 d_m([f],[\widetilde f])
       =\max_{2\le j\le J}\|Y_j(f)-Y_j(\widetilde f)\|_{C^m}
\end{equation}
is a complete metric inducing that quotient topology.  The quotient
has the corresponding global Banach coordinate, independently of any
shared-weight assumption.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Common reweighting changes $f_j/f_1$ by a constant depending on $j$;
anchoring removes that constant.  If $Y(f)=Y(\widetilde f)$, let
$b=\widetilde f_1/f_1$.  Then $\widetilde f_j$ is a positive constant
multiple of $bf_j$ for every $j$.  Their integrals are one, so
$\widetilde f=b\cdot f$.  Conversely this relation implies equality
of $Y$.  For arbitrary $y\in\mathcal Y_m$ a representative is
\[
 f_1=|Q|^{-1},\qquad f_j=\frac{e^{y_j}}{\int_Qe^{y_j}}.
\]
This is a continuous section of $Y$.  The map $Y$ is continuous by
positivity, compactness and continuity of logarithms on bounded
positive ranges.  The induced bijection on the quotient and the section
are continuous in opposite directions.  This proves the homeomorphism,
and completeness follows from completeness of $\mathcal Y_m$.
The assertion does not put a uniform lower bound on all representatives
of an orbit; representative-level estimates still require such bounds.
\end{proof}

For an action $W<T_1$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-model-chart}
 k_j(w)=\log(T_j-w)-\log(T_1-w),\qquad
 \Gamma_j(W)=k_j(W)-k_j(W(z_0)).
\end{equation}
Thus $Y(\mathcal F(W,[a]))=\Gamma(W)$ independently of $a$.
Constant actions all give $\Gamma(W)=0$ and are excluded from the
inverse charts.  Choose a second scalar point $z_1\in Q$, distinct
from $z_0$, and put
\[
 \mathcal W_{01}^m
   =\{W\in C^m(Q):\max_Q W<T_1,\ W(z_1)\ne W(z_0)\}.
\]
The choice specifies evaluation labels, not an action value.

For arbitrary $y\in\mathcal Y_m$ define $c_j=e^{y_j}$,
$\tau_j=T_j-T_1$, and, whenever the following expressions exist,
\begin{align}
 D(y)&=c_2(z_1)-c_3(z_1),\label{eq:v79-inverse-D}\\
 u_0(y)&=\frac{(1-c_2(z_1))/\tau_2-(1-c_3(z_1))/\tau_3}{D(y)},
       \label{eq:v79-inverse-u0}\\
 u_y(z)&=\frac{c_2(z)(1+\tau_2u_0(y))-1}{\tau_2},
       &\mathcal R(y)(z)&=T_1-\frac1{u_y(z)}.
       \label{eq:v79-inverse-u}
\end{align}
Let $\mathcal U_{01}^m$ be the open subset of $\mathcal Y_m$ on which
$D(y)\ne0$ and $\min_Q u_y>0$.  Finally let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-normal-space}
 \mathcal N_{01}^m
   =\{r\in\mathcal Y_m:r_2\equiv0,\ r_3(z_1)=0\}.
\end{equation}
No constraint beyond anchoring is placed on $r_j$ for $j\ge4$.

\begin{theorem}[Global split coordinates on an inverse chart]
\label{thm:v79-split}
The map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-split-map}
 y\longmapsto
 \bigl(\mathcal R(y),\,\mathcal S(y)\bigr),\qquad
 \mathcal S(y)=y-\Gamma(\mathcal R(y)),
\end{equation}
is a $C^\infty$ diffeomorphism
\[
 \mathcal U_{01}^m\ \longrightarrow\
              \mathcal W_{01}^m\times\mathcal N_{01}^m.
\]
Its inverse is $(W,r)\mapsto\Gamma(W)+r$.  In particular
$\Pi=\Gamma\mathcal R$ is a smooth retraction onto the
shared-weight model in this chart, and
\[
 y\hbox{ is represented by shared-weight laws}
           \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathcal S(y)=0.
\]
The inverse loses no spatial derivatives.  Its fixed-order bounds are
uniform on bounded subsets with positive lower bounds for $|D|$ and
$u_y$ and with bounded $u_y$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a model action put $u=(T_1-W)^{-1}$ and $u_0=u(z_0)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-rational-coordinate}
 e^{\Gamma_j(W)(z)}
           =\frac{1+\tau_j u(z)}{1+\tau_j u_0}.
\end{equation}
Evaluating at $z_1$ and eliminating $u(z_1)$ gives
\eqref{eq:v79-inverse-u0}; the second clock gives
\eqref{eq:v79-inverse-u}.  Moreover
\[
 D(\Gamma(W))=
 \frac{(\tau_2-\tau_3)(u(z_1)-u_0)}
 {(1+\tau_2u_0)(1+\tau_3u_0)}\ne0.
\]
Consequently $\mathcal R\Gamma(W)=W$.

For arbitrary $y\in\mathcal U_{01}^m$, the anchoring $c_2(z_0)=1$
gives $u_y(z_0)=u_0(y)>0$.  Formula~\eqref{eq:v79-inverse-u} shows
that $\Gamma_2(\mathcal R(y))=y_2$ everywhere.  The definition of
$u_0$ is exactly the equation
\[
 \frac{c_2(z_1)(1+\tau_2u_0)-1}{\tau_2}
   =\frac{c_3(z_1)(1+\tau_3u_0)-1}{\tau_3}.
\]
It follows that $\Gamma_3(\mathcal R(y))(z_1)=y_3(z_1)$.
Also $u_y(z_1)\ne u_0$: equality would force both $c_2(z_1)$ and
$c_3(z_1)$ to equal one, contradicting $D\ne0$.  Thus
$\mathcal R(y)\in\mathcal W_{01}^m$ and
$\mathcal S(y)\in\mathcal N_{01}^m$.

Conversely, adding $r\in\mathcal N_{01}^m$ to $\Gamma(W)$ changes
neither the second contrast nor the third contrast at $z_1$.
All quantities in~\eqref{eq:v79-inverse-D}--\eqref{eq:v79-inverse-u}
are therefore unchanged.  Hence $\mathcal R(\Gamma(W)+r)=W$,
$\mathcal S(\Gamma(W)+r)=r$, and the resulting $y$ is in the open
chart.  This proves both inverse identities globally on the displayed
sets.  Point evaluations, products and exponentials are smooth maps of
$C^m$ spaces, and reciprocals are smooth on the positive open cone.
This proves the smoothness and uniform local estimates.  In particular
no derivative of $y$ is used to recover an action derivative of the
same order beyond the derivatives already present in its $C^m$ norm.
\end{proof}

A different pair of anchors gives another chart with a smooth transition
map on its overlap.  These charts cover all nonconstant model actions.
They supply the topology and quantitative meaning of the exact orbit
identification in Theorem~\ref{thm:v78-quotient}; they are not a claim
of statistical sufficiency for finite records.

\begin{corollary}[Residuals measure distance from the model]
\label{cor:v79-residual-distance}
On a sufficiently small bounded chart neighborhood, let
$\operatorname{dist}_m(y,\Gamma(\mathcal V))$ denote the distance to
model points in a slightly larger fixed chart neighborhood $\mathcal V$
containing $\mathcal R(y)$.  There is a constant $C$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_m(y,\Gamma(\mathcal V))
       \le\|\mathcal S(y)\|_{C^m}
       \le C\operatorname{dist}_m(y,\Gamma(\mathcal V)).
\]
Already three clocks have a functional model-validation residual:
$r_3$ away from $z_0,z_1$.  Each additional clock contributes another
unconstrained anchored residual function.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The point $\Pi y$ is an allowed model point, giving the first inequality.
The formulas in the theorem make $\Pi$ Lipschitz on the indicated
neighborhood, say with constant $L$.  For any allowed $\Gamma(V)$,
\[
 \|y-\Pi y\|\le\|y-\Gamma(V)\|
                  +\|\Pi\Gamma(V)-\Pi y\|
              \le(1+L)\|y-\Gamma(V)\|.
\]
Take the infimum.  The description of residual coordinates is
precisely~\eqref{eq:v79-normal-space}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Approximate sharing and exact confounding}

Consider the larger observation model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-misspecified}
 f_j=\frac{a(T_j-W)e^{\eta_j}}
              {\int_Qa(T_j-W)e^{\eta_j}},\qquad a>0,
\end{equation}
where clock-dependent nuisance functions $\eta_j$ are now allowed.
Define their observable contrasts by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-nuisance-contrast}
 E_j=(\eta_j-\eta_1)-(\eta_j(z_0)-\eta_1(z_0)).
\end{equation}
Then $Y(f)=\Gamma(W)+E$ exactly.  This identity separates normalization,
common nuisance, and clock-dependent nuisance before an estimate is made.

\begin{theorem}[Robust reconstruction and complete ambiguity formula]
\label{thm:v79-nuisance}
Fix a bounded inverse chart with strict margins.  For
$\|E\|_{C^m}$ sufficiently small the reconstructed action
$W^\dagger=\mathcal R(Y(f))$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-bias}
 \|W^\dagger-W\|_{C^m}
          +\|\mathcal S(Y(f))\|_{C^m}\le C\|E\|_{C^m}.
\end{equation}
In particular weights $a(1+\epsilon_j)$ with
$\max_j\|\epsilon_j\|_{C^m}\le\epsilon$ and
$\max_j\|\epsilon_j\|_\infty\le1/2$ induce action bias at most
$C\epsilon$.

The laws in~\eqref{eq:v79-misspecified} coincide with exact
shared-weight laws for another action $V<T_1$ if and only if there
exist a common function $b$ and constants $c_j$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-exact-confounding}
 \eta_j(z)=b(z)+c_j+
           \log\frac{T_j-V(z)}{T_j-W(z)},\qquad 1\le j\le J.
\end{equation}
For $V$ and $W$ in an inverse chart this is the complete ambiguity:
there are no other off-model nuisance functions whose residual vanishes.
The formula holds for any number of clocks.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the Lipschitz estimate for $\mathcal R$ and
$\mathcal S$ at $\Gamma(W)$, where their values are $W$ and zero.
For multiplicative errors take $\eta_j=\log(1+\epsilon_j)$;
the $C^m$ algebra and reciprocal bounds give
$\|E\|_{C^m}\le C\epsilon$.

If the normalized laws equal those generated by $(V,\widetilde a)$,
their unnormalized densities differ by positive constants.  Taking
logarithms gives~\eqref{eq:v79-exact-confounding} with
$b=\log(\widetilde a/a)$.  Conversely this formula cancels the action
ratio, leaving $ae^b(T_j-V)$ times a clock constant; normalization
removes the latter.  Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:v79-split} proves that
zero residual is equivalent to an exact shared-weight representation,
so the equivalence just established is exhaustive in that chart.
\end{proof}

Thus extra clocks test departures transverse to the model, not all
clock-dependent efficiencies.  A departure of the form
\eqref{eq:v79-exact-confounding} is indistinguishable from a genuine
change of action even with exact laws.  This statement concerns the
weighted-action experiment; no pair of noncongruent physical billiards
is asserted by it.

\begin{proposition}[Tangent and normal components of nuisance]
\label{prop:v79-tangent}
At a model point let $L_W=D\mathcal R_{\Gamma(W)}$.  Then
\begin{align*}
 (D\Gamma_W h)_j
   &=k_j'(W)h-k_j'(W(z_0))h(z_0),\\
 L_WD\Gamma_W&=\mathrm{Id},\\
 P_W&=D\Gamma_WL_W,\\
 \mathcal Y_m&=\operatorname{ran}D\Gamma_W\oplus\mathcal N_{01}^m.
\end{align*}
Here $P_W$ is the bounded projection onto the first summand and
$\ker L_W=\mathcal N_{01}^m$.  Uniformly on bounded smaller charts,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal R(\Gamma(W)+E)-W&=L_WE+O(\|E\|_{C^m}^2),
       \label{eq:v79-tangent-bias}\\
 \mathcal S(\Gamma(W)+E)&=(\mathrm{Id}-P_W)E
                                      +O(\|E\|_{C^m}^2).
       \label{eq:v79-normal-bias}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate the two inverse identities of Theorem~\ref{thm:v79-split}.
Differentiation in the residual variable gives $L_Wr=0$ for
$r\in\mathcal N_{01}^m$, while differentiating the whole inverse gives
$\mathrm{Id}=P_W+D\mathcal S_{\Gamma(W)}$ and the asserted direct sum.
The displayed derivative of $\Gamma$ follows from the chain rule.
The smooth formulas have bounded second derivatives on the indicated
sets; the integral Taylor remainder proves the two quadratic estimates.
\end{proof}

The residual therefore identifies the normal component to first order,
whereas the tangential component changes the reconstructed action.
Neither component is inferred from the mere smallness of a fitting
error; the split and the ambiguity formula state what is actually
observable.
