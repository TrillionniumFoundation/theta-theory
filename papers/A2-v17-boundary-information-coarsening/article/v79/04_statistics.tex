\section{Finite records, validation, and sharp action risk}
\label{sec:v79-statistics}

The quotient identifies a function from exact laws, whereas records
require estimation of those laws.  In this section the loss is imposed
on the action itself.  The subsequent passage to billiard geometry has
additional conditioning and derivative loss, and its optimal risk is
not inferred from the result below.

\begin{proposition}[A finite-record residual test]
\label{prop:v79-test}
Suppose the true contrast $y$ lies in a fixed bounded smaller inverse
chart and an estimator $\widehat y$ satisfies
\[
 \Pr\{\|\widehat y-y\|_{C^m}\le\rho\}\ge1-\zeta.
\]
For $\rho$ sufficiently small, put
$\widehat r=\mathcal S(\widehat y)$ when the chart formulas are defined.
There is a fixed $C$ for which rejecting the shared-weight model when
$\|\widehat r\|_{C^m}>C\rho$ has type-I error at most $\zeta$.
It rejects with probability at least $1-\zeta$ whenever
$\|\mathcal S(y)\|_{C^m}>2C\rho$.
Undefined chart outputs may be declared rejections.  They occur only
on the exceptional event under the stated chart and error bounds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the stated event the chart margins persist and the Lipschitz bound
for $\mathcal S$ gives
$\|\widehat r-\mathcal S(y)\|_{C^m}\le C\rho$.
Under the null the true residual is zero.  Under the separated
alternative the reverse triangle inequality proves rejection.
The chart persistence statement follows from openness with uniform
positive margins.  No assertion of optimal testing separation is used.
\end{proof}

Three clocks suffice for this functional test, since their third
contrast has a residual away from the two anchor points.  Additional
clocks supply further residual coordinates but do not overcome the
exact confounding in~\eqref{eq:v79-exact-confounding}.

\subsection{The statistical class}

Fix rectangles $Q_0\Subset Q_+\subset\R^d$ with nonempty interiors,
anchors $z_0,z_1\in\operatorname{int}Q_0$, and an integer $m\ge0$.
Let $\beta>m$ be nonintegral.  The $C^\beta$ norm denotes the usual
H\"older norm, with the H\"older seminorm on derivatives of order
$\lfloor\beta\rfloor$.  A fixed class $\mathcal A_\beta(\epsilon)$
consists of triples $(W,a,(\eta_j)_{j=1}^J)$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:v79-misspecified}, normalized on $Q_+$, with the following
bounds.  The $C^\beta(Q_+)$ norms of $W,a,\eta_j$ are bounded by a
fixed $A$; $a$ is bounded above and below by fixed positive constants;
$T_j-W$ belongs to a fixed positive compact interval; and
$|W(z_1)-W(z_0)|$ is bounded below by a fixed positive number.
Finally $\max_j\|\eta_j\|_{C^m(Q_+)}\le\epsilon$.
All constants, clocks, rectangles, $m,\beta$ and $J$ are independent
of $n,\epsilon$.  For small fixed $\epsilon_0$, these bounds imply
uniform positive density and inverse-chart margins on $Q_0$.
Contrasts of density restrictions can be used without renormalization,
since all normalization constants disappear from~\eqref{eq:v79-log-contrast}.

For the lower bound we assume a nondegenerate class, in the precise
sense that it contains an exact member $(W_*,1,0)$ with strict room in
all the above inequalities and in its $C^\beta$ norm bound.  Choose
an open rectangle $B\Subset\operatorname{int}Q_0$ disjoint from the
two anchors.  We require this strict room on $B$ as well.  Thus small
$C^\beta$ perturbations of $W_*$ supported in $B$ are in the class.
This condition rules out a singleton class described by saturated
bounds; it is satisfied, for example, by a nonconstant affine action
with sufficiently generous fixed bounds.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp local risk with clock-dependent misspecification]
\label{thm:v79-minimax}
Observe $n$ independent draws from each of the $J$ conditional laws,
independently across clocks.  For the class just specified there are
constants $0<c<C<\infty$ and $\epsilon_0>0$ such that, for sufficiently
large $n$ and $0\le\epsilon\le\epsilon_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-minimax}
 c\left\{\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^\frac{\beta-m}{2\beta+d}
                      +\epsilon\right\}
 \le \inf_{\widehat W}\sup_{\mathcal A_\beta(\epsilon)}
       \E\|\widehat W-W\|_{C^m(Q_0)}
 \le C\left\{\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^\frac{\beta-m}{2\beta+d}
                      +\epsilon\right\}.
\end{equation}
The infimum is over measurable estimators with $C^m(Q_0)$ values.
The result is for the unrestricted weighted-action class on the stated
chart, not a minimax theorem for geometrically realizable billiard actions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We give both statistical and misspecification bounds.  The underlying
kernel and packing methods are classical; see
\cite{GineGuillou2002,GineNickl2009,Tsybakov2009}.  The point here is to
carry them through a fully specified quotient and its nuisance class.

For the upper bound use a smooth compactly supported kernel with integral
one and vanishing moments through order $\lfloor\beta\rfloor$.
Such kernels are obtained by a finite linear combination of dilates
of a smooth kernel, solving the finite moment system.  Estimate the
densities on $Q_0$ using samples on $Q_+$.  For sufficiently small
bandwidth $h$ the support of each kernel remains inside $Q_+$.
Taylor expansion, or convolution with each derivative of $f$, gives
bias at most $Ch^{\beta-m}$ in $C^m(Q_0)$.  Uniform $C^\beta$ bounds
and density floors follow from the class definition and the product,
exponential and reciprocal estimates on bounded sets.

For a multi-index $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\le m$, the centered
kernel summand has envelope $Ch^{-d-|\alpha|}$, variance
$Ch^{-d-2|\alpha|}$, and spatial Lipschitz bound
$Ch^{-d-|\alpha|-1}$.  A grid of mesh $n^{-q}h^{d+m+1}$ on $Q_0$,
with fixed sufficiently large $q$, reduces the supremum to polynomially
many pointwise inequalities when $h$ is a fixed power of $n$ times a
logarithmic factor.  Bernstein's inequality, a union bound over the
grid, derivatives and clocks, and the Lipschitz bound between grid
points give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v79-kernel}
 \max_j\|\widehat f_j-f_j\|_{C^m(Q_0)}
 \le C\left\{h^{\beta-m}
       +\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{nh^{2m+d}}}
       +\frac{\log n}{nh^{m+d}}+n^{-q}\right\}
\end{equation}
except on an event of probability at most $n^{-q'}$, where $q'$ can
be made arbitrarily large by increasing the constant.  Choose
$h=(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+d)}$.  The square-root and bias terms then
have size
$r_n=(\log n/n)^{(\beta-m)/(2\beta+d)}$; the envelope term is
smaller, since its exponent is $(2\beta-m)/(2\beta+d)$.

On this event density floors persist, hence the anchored logarithms
and the reconstruction are defined.  Theorems~\ref{thm:v79-split}
and~\ref{thm:v79-nuisance} give error at most $C(r_n+\epsilon)$.
To define an estimator on all samples, test the density and chart
margins and a fixed $C^m$ output bound larger than the class bound;
when a test fails return a fixed $C^m$ function.  The output is then
uniformly bounded.  Suprema of continuous derivatives on compact
rectangles are measurable (they equal suprema on a countable dense
set), so these tests and the formulas give a measurable estimator.
On the good event all tests pass for large $n$ and small $\epsilon$;
on its complement the bounded error contributes only $O(n^{-q'})$
to expectation.  This proves the upper bound.

For the statistical lower bound take $a=1$ and $\eta_j=0$.  In $B$
choose $M\asymp h^{-d}$ disjoint cubes of side comparable to $h$.
For a fixed nonzero smooth bump $\psi$ supported in the unit cube set
\[
 W_l(z)=W_*(z)+a_0h^\beta\psi((z-z_l)/h),
                  \qquad 1\le l\le M.
\]
Choose $\psi$ with a nonzero derivative of some order $m$ (for $m=0$
use its nonzero value).  The fixed number $a_0>0$ can be chosen small
enough that every $W_l$ is in the class for all small $h$.  The anchor
values are unchanged.  Disjoint supports imply pairwise $C^m(Q_0)$
separation at least $c a_0h^{\beta-m}$.

Let $P_l$ be the law of all $Jn$ records and $P_*$ the corresponding
reference law.  The exact densities are
$f_{j,W}=(T_j-W)/\int_{Q_+}(T_j-W)$.  Positivity and uniform
normalizer bounds give
\[
 \|f_{j,W_l}-f_{j,W_*}\|_{L^2(Q_+)}
                 \le C\|W_l-W_*\|_{L^2(Q_+)},
 \qquad
 \mathrm{KL}(f_{j,W_l},f_{j,W_*})
                 \le C a_0^2h^{2\beta+d}.
\]
For the first inequality, subtract the two normalized numerators and
bound the normalizer difference by
$|Q_+|^{1/2}\|W_l-W_*\|_2$.  For the second use
$\mathrm{KL}(f,g)\le\int(f-g)^2/g$, valid for probability densities
with $g>0$.  Independence gives
$\mathrm{KL}(P_l,P_*)\le C Jn a_0^2h^{2\beta+d}$.

For clarity, the information inequality used here is the following.
For a uniform index on $\{1,\ldots,M\}$, every classifier has error
probability at least
$1-(M^{-1}\sum_l\mathrm{KL}(P_l,P_*)+\log2)/\log M$.
Indeed the mutual information is at most that average relative entropy;
conditional entropy is at most $\log2+p_e\log M$, and subtracting it
from $\log M$ proves the bound.  Taking
$h=c_0(\log n/n)^{1/(2\beta+d)}$ with sufficiently small fixed $c_0$
makes the error probability bounded below by a positive constant.
Nearest-neighbor classification of any function estimate would be
correct whenever its error is less than half the pairwise separation.
Thus its maximal expected $C^m$ error is at least $c r_n$.

For the misspecification lower bound fix a nonzero smooth function
$\phi$ supported in $B$ and compare $W_*$ with
$W_1=W_*+c_1\epsilon\phi$.  Under $W_1$ choose
\[
 a=1,\qquad
 \eta_{1,j}=\log\frac{T_j-W_*}{T_j-W_1}.
\]
For small fixed $c_1$ these functions have $C^m$ norm at most
$\epsilon$ and satisfy the uniform $C^\beta$ bounds.  The observed
densities are exactly the reference densities at every clock, but the
actions are separated by $c\epsilon$ in $C^m$.  The triangle inequality
under this common data law gives maximal risk at least $c\epsilon/2$.
The maximum of the statistical and misspecification lower bounds is
at least half their sum.  This completes~\eqref{eq:v79-minimax}.
\end{proof}

The bound shows that approximate sharing is not a discontinuous
identification assumption on these charts: the action error changes
linearly with its violation.  At the same time that linear term cannot
be removed by more data.  Both statements use the exact normalized
observation law, rather than a likelihood written after eliminating
an uncharged nuisance parameter.
