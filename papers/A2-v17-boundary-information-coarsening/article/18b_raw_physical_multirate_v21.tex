\section{A registered physical local experiment on fixed laboratory windows}
\label{sec:v20-raw-physical}

The abstract moving-support theorem is useful for billiards only after the
observation space has been fixed independently of the unknown local parameter.
There are two distinct issues.  First, the acquisition time must not be
recentered by an unknown gap.  Second, the signed endpoint coordinate itself
must be a common laboratory coordinate, rather than a coordinate redefined at
the perturbed contact.  We make both points part of the hypotheses of the
physical theorem and quantify the remaining passage from the exact billiard
endpoint family to the ideal moving-boundary experiment.

\subsection{An anchored registered family in one laboratory chart}

Fix one Euclidean chart, one oriented transverse axis, and the two contact
labels.  The statistical family in this section consists of billiard tables
whose two participating boundary patches, in that \emph{same} chart for every
parameter value, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-anchored-family}
 \Gamma_{0,\vartheta}=\{(-\psi_{0,\vartheta}(u),u):|u|<u_*\},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{1,g,\vartheta}=\{(g+\psi_{1,\vartheta}(v),v):|v|<u_*\},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-anchored-normalization}
 \psi_{b,\vartheta}(0)=\partial_u\psi_{b,\vartheta}(0)=0,
 \qquad
 \partial_u^2\psi_{b,\vartheta}(0)\ge\kappa_*>0
\end{equation}
throughout a fixed finite-dimensional parameter neighborhood.  The smooth
extensions away from these patches range over a compact billiard family for
which the selected channel keeps its clearance and hyperbolicity margins.
Thus the two contact origins and tangent axes are \emph{anchored model data};
the unknown coordinates are the gap and the two graph shapes in the anchored
frames.  No parameter-dependent recentering or rotation is applied to a
recorded endpoint.

This hypothesis deliberately overrides, for the statistical family in this
section, the default channel-centered differentiation convention
\eqref{eq:v19-centered-coordinate-convention}.  Here all derivatives are taken
at fixed laboratory endpoint coordinates $(u,v)$ and at fixed programmed
physical time.  The centered excess is used only as an algebraic variable in
the proof.  In particular the raw observation map is parameter independent:
a preparation returns a failure symbol or the selected pair of signed
laboratory endpoint coordinates, together with the prescribed design label;
when the complete stopped transcript is considered below, every failure and
stopping time is retained as well.

Let $\vartheta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb R^r$ parameterize a finite labelled
anchored graph family and let $(g_0,0)$ be the reference geometry.  Write
$S_{b,g,\vartheta}$ and $B_{b,g,\vartheta}$ for the associated half-line
actions and positive amplitudes, all represented in the fixed chart
\eqref{eq:v21-anchored-family}.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-dot-S}
 \dot S_b(u)h=
 D_\vartheta S_{b,g_0,\vartheta}(u)|_{\vartheta=0}[h].
\end{equation}
The anchored normalization gives
$S_{b,g,\vartheta}(0)=S'_{b,g,\vartheta}(0)=0$ throughout the family.

Fix finitely many same-type designs $(b_\ell,d_\ell)$ with
$b_\ell\in\{0,1\}$ and $0<d_\ell<d_*$, $1\le\ell\le L$.  At flight number
$j$ the programmed physical time is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-fixed-physical-window}
                         t_{j,\ell}=jg_0+d_\ell.
\end{equation}
It is the same number under every local alternative.

\subsection{The multirate gap direction}

Let $j_n\to\infty$ and $\delta_n\downarrow0$.  For bounded
$z=(a,h)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^r$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-multirate-parameter}
 g_n(a)=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a,
 \qquad
 \vartheta_n(h)=\delta_nh.
\end{equation}
Then the physical window \eqref{eq:v20-fixed-physical-window} has exact excess
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-raw-excess}
 t_{j_n,\ell}-j_ng_n(a)=d_\ell-\delta_na.
\end{equation}
Thus a gap perturbation of order $\delta_n/j_n$ creates an order-$\delta_n$
normal support displacement without inserting the unknown gap into the
acquisition rule.

At the reference design $(b,d)$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-reference-defining-function}
 w_{b,d}(u,v)=d-S_{b,g_0,0}(u)-S_{b,g_0,0}(v)
\end{equation}
and let $A_{b,d}(u,v)>0$ be the normalized smooth amplitude of the successful
endpoint density.  Define the linear support velocity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-raw-support-velocity}
 U_b(u,v)z=a+\dot S_b(u)h+\dot S_b(v)h
\end{equation}
and the intrinsic boundary information matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-raw-information}
 \mathcal K_{b,d}
 =\int_{\{S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d\}}
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)\,U_b(u,v)^tU_b(u,v)}
      {|\nabla(S_b\oplus S_b)(u,v)|}\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exact fixed-window support expansion]
\label{lem:v20-fixed-window-reduction}
For every compact local set $K\subset\mathbb R^{1+r}$, the exact boundary
endpoint density at design $(b,d)$ and local parameter $z\in K$ can be written
on one fixed endpoint box as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-exact-boundary-density}
 f^{\rm ex}_{n,z}(u,v)=A^{\rm ex}_{n,z}(u,v)
                  (w^{\rm ex}_{n,z}(u,v))_+,
\end{equation}
where the densities are normalized and
\begin{align}
 w^{\rm ex}_{n,z}
 &=w_{b,d}-\delta_nU_bz+R_{n,z},
 &\sup_{z\in K}\|R_{n,z}\|_{C^2}&=:r_n(K)=o(\delta_n),
                                      \label{eq:v20-defining-expansion}\\
 \sup_{z\in K}
 \|A^{\rm ex}_{n,z}-A_{b,d}\|_{C^1}
 &\le C_K\delta_n.                 \label{eq:v21-amplitude-expansion}
\end{align}
The direct effect of $g_n(a)-g_0$ on the half-line actions is
$O_K(\delta_n/j_n)=o(\delta_n)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:v20-raw-excess} contributes the term $-\delta_na$.
Smooth dependence of the anchored half-line construction, including two
endpoint derivatives, gives
\[
 S_{b,g_n(a),\vartheta_n(h)}
 =S_{b,g_0,0}+\delta_n\dot S_bh
 +\frac{\delta_na}{j_n}\partial_gS_b+o_K(\delta_n)
\]
in $C^2$.  Since $j_n\to\infty$, the gap derivative of the action belongs to
the remainder.  The relative-law amplitude and its normalization are smooth
on the compact family; their displacement is $O_K(\delta_n)$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:v20-defining-expansion}--\eqref{eq:v21-amplitude-expansion}.
\end{proof}

We now turn the preceding first-order expansion into a comparison of
experiments.  For each $(b,d)$ define the \emph{ideal} normalized density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-ideal-density}
 f^{\rm id}_{n,z}(u,v)=c_{n,z}A_{b,d}(u,v)
             (w_{b,d}(u,v)-\delta_nU_b(u,v)z)_+,
\end{equation}
where $c_{n,z}$ is the normalizing constant.  Smooth tubular integration gives
$c_{n,z}=1+O_K(\delta_n)$ with all fixed local derivatives.  Hence
\eqref{eq:v21-ideal-density} is exactly an instance of the vector moving
boundary family in Section~\ref{sec:v20-vector-information}; its regular
amplitude variation carries no logarithmic information.

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative exact-to-ideal endpoint transfer]
\label{prop:v21-exact-ideal-transfer}
For every fixed design $(b,d)$ and compact local set $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-one-record-transfer}
 \sup_{z\in K}
 H^2(f^{\rm ex}_{n,z}\,du\,dv,f^{\rm id}_{n,z}\,du\,dv)
 \le C_K\left\{r_n(K)^2\log\frac e{r_n(K)}+\delta_n^2\right\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, if $k_n$ successful endpoint records are divided among a fixed
finite collection of designs and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-critical-record-scale}
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1,
\end{equation}
then the product experiment of the exact boundary endpoints and the product
experiment of the ideal densities \eqref{eq:v21-ideal-density} have Le Cam
distance tending to zero uniformly on compact local parameter sets.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-fixed-window-reduction} and the smooth normalization of
\eqref{eq:v21-ideal-density} give a $C^1$ defining-function discrepancy at
most $r_n(K)$ and a $C^0$ amplitude discrepancy $O_K(\delta_n)$.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-support-stability} to obtain
\eqref{eq:v21-one-record-transfer}.  Hellinger tensorization gives for the
product experiment
\[
 H^2_{\rm prod}
 \le C_Kk_n\left\{r_n(K)^2\log\frac e{r_n(K)}+\delta_n^2\right\}.
\]
Because $r_n(K)=o(\delta_n)$,
\[
 \frac{r_n(K)^2\log(e/r_n(K))}
      {\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)}\longrightarrow0,
\]
while \eqref{eq:v21-critical-record-scale} also gives
$k_n\delta_n^2\to0$.  Thus the product Hellinger distance tends to zero.
The identity maps in the two directions then give vanishing Le Cam distance.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Tangent separation and finite positive designs}

\begin{lemma}[Finite positive-offset design]
\label{lem:v20-finite-positive-design}
Fix a finite labelled contact-jet order $M$ in the anchored chart
\eqref{eq:v21-anchored-family}.  There are finitely many same-type designs
$(b_\ell,d_\ell)$ and positive weights $\alpha_\ell$, depending in general on
$M$, with $\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell=1$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-design-info}
 \mathcal K_{\rm design}
 =\sum_{\ell=1}^L\alpha_\ell\mathcal K_{b_\ell,d_\ell}
\end{equation}
is positive definite on the local coordinate
$(a,h)\in\mathbb R\times T\Theta_M$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $z=(a,h)$ belongs to the kernel of $\mathcal K_{b,d}$ for both labels
and every sufficiently small $d>0$.  Positivity of the density in
\eqref{eq:v20-raw-information} and continuity imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-kernel-level}
 a+\dot S_b(u)h+\dot S_b(v)h=0
 \quad\text{whenever}\quad S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d.
\end{equation}
Choose $v=0$ and $d=S_b(u)$.  Since the anchored normalization is fixed for the
whole family, $\dot S_b(0)h=0$, so
$a+\dot S_b(u)h=0$.  Letting $u\to0$ gives $a=0$ and hence
$\dot S_b(u)h=0$ for both labels on a contact neighborhood.  Proposition
\ref{prop:v21-tangent-signed-inverse}, which is a differential statement on
the exact finite jet chart, now gives $h=0$.  Thus the common kernel is
trivial.

Every nonzero unit vector is detected by at least one pair $(b,d)$.  Strict
positivity is open in the unit vector, and the unit sphere of the fixed
finite-dimensional chart is compact.  A finite subcover gives finitely many
designs.  Any positive convex combination of their information matrices is
positive definite.  The compactness argument is deliberately made separately
for each fixed $M$; no universal finite design for the entire infinite jet is
claimed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The endpoint-output limit experiment}

Choose the finite design of Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-finite-positive-design}.  Let
$k_{n,\ell}$ be the target number of successful endpoint outputs in design
$\ell$ and put $k_n=\sum_\ell k_{n,\ell}$.  Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-batch-rate}
 \frac{k_{n,\ell}}{k_n}\to\alpha_\ell,
 \qquad
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1,
 \qquad
 j_n\delta_n\to0.
\end{equation}
The last condition keeps the rare-event success prefactors uniformly
comparable to those of the reference geometry; it is not part of the
moving-boundary singularity.

Let $p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)$ be the one-preparation success probability in the
boundary model at the programmed physical window.  On every compact local set
$K$, the uniform threshold law gives constants $c_K,C_K>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-uniform-success}
 c_Kd_\ell^2e^{-j_n\gamma_0}
 \le p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)
 \le C_Kd_\ell^2e^{-j_n\gamma_0},
 \qquad z\in K.
\end{equation}
Choose a deterministic cap $N_{n,\ell}$ from a fixed compact-neighborhood
lower bound so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-cap-choice}
 \inf_{z\in K}N_{n,\ell}p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)
 \ge2k_{n,\ell}.
\end{equation}
Then a binomial Chernoff bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-cap-tail}
 \sup_{z\in K}\Pr_z\{\text{cap is reached before }k_{n,\ell}
 \text{ successes}\}\le e^{-c k_{n,\ell}}.
\end{equation}
On this exceptional event the endpoint-output experiment returns a cemetery
symbol.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-window multirate endpoint Gaussian experiment]
\label{thm:v20-fixed-window-lan}
Under \eqref{eq:v20-batch-rate}, for every compact local set $K$ the exact
boundary endpoint-output experiment $\mathsf Q^{\rm end}_{n,z}$ is at Le Cam
distance $o(1)$, uniformly for $z\in K$, from a dominated experiment
$\widetilde{\mathsf Q}^{\rm end}_{n,z}$ for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-physical-lan}
 \log\frac{d\widetilde{\mathsf Q}^{\rm end}_{n,z}}
          {d\widetilde{\mathsf Q}^{\rm end}_{n,0}}
 =z^t\Delta_n-\frac12z^t\mathcal K_{\rm design}z+o_P(1)
\end{equation}
uniformly on $K$, and
$\Delta_n\Rightarrow N_{1+r}(0,\mathcal K_{\rm design})$ under the reference
law.  Hence the \emph{original}, possibly non-dominated endpoint-output
experiment converges to the Gaussian shift with information
$\mathcal K_{\rm design}$.  Its bounded local alternatives are mutually
contiguous with the reference sequence, and it inherits the Gaussian testing
profile and local asymptotic minimax conclusions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-vector-boundary-lan}.

The gap is visible at rate $\delta_n/j_n$ and the anchored registered contact
geometry at rate $\delta_n$.  The matrix $\mathcal K_{\rm design}$ is positive
definite on every fixed finite labelled graph-jet chart.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Ignore the deterministic caps first.  Proposition
\ref{prop:v21-exact-ideal-transfer} replaces the exact successful endpoint
laws by the product of the ideal families \eqref{eq:v21-ideal-density} at
vanishing Le Cam distance.  Apply the parameter-independent collar-collapse
construction of Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-collar-collapse} separately to the finitely
many designs.  The collapsed product family is dominated, and Lemma
\ref{lem:v20-vector-likelihood} gives its uniform LAN expansion.  Independent
design blocks add their central sequences and information matrices; the
allocation limits in \eqref{eq:v20-batch-rate} therefore give
$\mathcal K_{\rm design}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v20-finite-positive-design} gives
positive definiteness.

The sum of the exact-to-ideal and collar-collapse Le Cam distances is $o(1)$,
uniformly on $K$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-vector-boundary-lan} then transfers
Gaussian convergence, contiguity, testing and local minimax statements back to
the exact endpoint-output family.  Finally \eqref{eq:v20-cap-tail} shows that
adding the deterministic caps changes the endpoint-output experiment by
$o(1)$ in total variation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transfer to actual finite bridges}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform stopped physical-transcript transfer]
\label{thm:v20-stopped-local-transfer}
Let $\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}$ be the complete stopped transcript law of the
actual finite bridges under the deterministic caps, fixed physical times and
target success counts above.  The transcript retains every failure, success,
design and stopping time.  Let $\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}$ be the boundary
transcript generated by the identical parameter-independent policy.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-transfer-condition}
                         k_n\tau^{j_n}\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
then for every compact local set $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-lecam-transfer}
 \sup_{z\in K}
 \|\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}-\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Every common Markov coarsening inherits the bound.  In particular, the actual
finite-bridge endpoint-output experiment inherits
Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-fixed-window-lan}.

No failed preparation is free.  If $T_{n,\ell}$ is the number of physical
preparations used in batch $\ell$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-physical-cost}
 \mathbb E_zT_{n,\ell}
 \le\frac{k_{n,\ell}}{p_{n,\ell}(z)}
 \le C_K k_{n,\ell}d_\ell^{-2}e^{j_n\gamma_0}
\end{equation}
uniformly for $z\in K$, and the deterministic cap gives an absolute upper
bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For bounded local $z$, the exact excess \eqref{eq:v20-raw-excess} stays in a
common compact subcollar and the billiard tables stay in the compact family of
Theorems~\ref{thm:v6-transfer} and \ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer}.  The
capped policy satisfies their deterministic stopping hypothesis and stops no
later than the $k_n$th success in total.  Their common-record-space coupling
therefore gives the first inequality in \eqref{eq:v20-lecam-transfer}.
Total variation contracts under a common Markov kernel, giving the endpoint
statement.  The expectation bound is dominated by the uncapped
negative-binomial stopping time together with \eqref{eq:v20-uniform-success}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Endpoint information and complete-transcript information]
\label{rem:v21-coarsening-scope}
The Gaussian experiment and minimax bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-fixed-window-lan} concern the declared
\emph{endpoint-output coarsening}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-stopped-local-transfer}
is stronger in a different direction: it compares the complete finite and
boundary stopped transcripts.  It does not identify the complete transcript
with its endpoint coarsening.  In particular the waiting counts need not be
ancillary.  Their success probability contains the long-bridge factor
$\sinh(j\gamma)^{-1}$ and may carry faster information in directions changing
$\gamma$.  Preparation cost is charged in the endpoint theorem, but cost
accounting is not an efficiency theorem for the richer transcript.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A charged pilot and the exact compared sigma-field}

The fixed-window theorem itself uses no onset calibration.  If centered
windows are desired, a charged pilot can be included without changing the
endpoint-output limit experiment.  We state the comparison on an explicit
sample space.

\begin{corollary}[Pilot-centered endpoint-output equivalence]
\label{cor:v20-pilot-centered}
Assume the coarse-bracket hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration}.  Let an independent pilot transcript
$\mathcal P_n$ produce $\widehat g_n$ such that, with probability
$1-\eta_n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-pilot-gap}
 j_n|\widehat g_n-g|\le Cr_n^{m+1},
 \qquad \eta_n\to0,
\end{equation}
and suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-pilot-smallness}
 k_nr_n^{2m+2}\log(1/r_n)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Consider the experiment whose observed sigma-field is generated by the
\emph{pilot transcript together with the post-pilot successful endpoint
outputs}; programmed times and waiting counts after the pilot are not added to
this endpoint-output sigma-field.  In the data-driven experiment the common
rule is
\[
 t_{n,\ell}=j_n\widehat g_n+d_\ell.
\]
Its oracle companion has the \emph{same pilot transcript} and, conditionally on
that transcript, replaces the post-pilot endpoint kernel by the one using
$t_{n,\ell}=j_ng+d_\ell$.  The Le Cam distance between these two experiments
tends to zero.  Replacing the boundary endpoint kernels by actual finite
bridges adds at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-pilot-total-error}
 \eta_n+C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}+o(1).
\end{equation}
The pilot preparation cost remains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v20-pilot-cost}
 \mathbb ET_{\rm pilot}
 \le C_me^{j_n\gamma}r_n^{-(2m+2)}
          \log(C_m/\eta_n).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Condition on the pilot transcript.  On the good event, every post-pilot support
is shifted relative to its oracle counterpart by at most $Cr_n^{m+1}$ in the
normal direction.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-support-stability}, uniformly over the
finite design, gives one-endpoint squared Hellinger distance at most
$Cr_n^{2m+2}\log(1/r_n)$.  Tensorization and
\eqref{eq:v20-pilot-smallness} give $o(1)$ conditionally on the good event.
Integrating over the common pilot transcript and adding $\eta_n$ proves the
boundary comparison.  Corollary~\ref{cor:v16-pilot-transfer} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v20-stopped-local-transfer} give the finite-bridge term.  The
cost is exactly the charged self-calibration estimate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Registration outside the anchored primary model]
The primary physical theorem is stated on the anchored registered family
\eqref{eq:v21-anchored-family}, so its observation map is literally fixed.
Absolute Euclidean pose, contact-label exchange and unknown construction of the
endpoint frames are not hidden coordinates in $\vartheta$.  If they are to be
estimated as well, they must be represented on the same laboratory record
space as nuisance coordinates; their derivatives then enter the support
velocity in exactly the same way as any other parameter.  Alternatively, a
charged registration pilot whose induced endpoint-coordinate error is
$\varepsilon_n$ is asymptotically negligible for the endpoint-output
experiment whenever
\[
 k_n\varepsilon_n^2\log(1/\varepsilon_n)\to0,
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-support-stability}.  This criterion is a stability
statement, not an assertion that registration is free.
\end{remark}
