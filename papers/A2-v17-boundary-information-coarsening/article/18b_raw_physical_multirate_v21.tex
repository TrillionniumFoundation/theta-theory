\section{A physical endpoint-output experiment on fixed laboratory windows}
\label{sec:v21-raw-physical}

We now realize the boundary-information theorem on a parameter-independent
physical observation space.  Two distinctions are essential.  First, the
acquisition times are fixed from the reference channel and do not depend on
the unknown alternative.  Second, the statistical theorem is formulated for
an anchored registered channel in one laboratory chart, so the observed
endpoint coordinates are literal coordinates of the raw collision record,
not contact-centered coordinates reconstructed separately at each parameter.
The deterministic signed-rigidity theorem of
Section~\ref{sec:v21-signed-rigidity} is more general; the anchoring below is
a hypothesis of the physical statistical experiment, not of the geometric
inverse theorem.

\subsection{An anchored registered channel in one laboratory chart}

Fix once and for all a Euclidean chart $(x,y)$, two obstacle labels, and two
facing patches.  In this chart the admissible local family has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-anchored-graphs}
 \Gamma_{0,\vartheta}=\{(-\psi_{0,\vartheta}(u),u):|u|<u_*\},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{1,g,\vartheta}=\{(g+\psi_{1,\vartheta}(v),v):|v|<u_*\},
\end{equation}
where, for every parameter in the family,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-anchor-normalization}
 \psi_{b,\vartheta}(0)=\psi'_{b,\vartheta}(0)=0,
 \qquad
 \psi''_{b,\vartheta}(0)>0.
\end{equation}
The labels, the transverse origin $y=0$, and the tangent direction are fixed
throughout the family.  Varying $g$ translates the second anchored graph in
the normal $x$ direction; it does not translate or rotate the observed
transverse coordinate.  The local parameter $\vartheta\in\Theta\subset
\mathbb R^r$ changes only the anchored graph geometry in this fixed chart.
For the finite-dimensional theorem below we take $\vartheta$ to be a finite
labelled contact-jet coordinate.

A raw preparation is observed in a common record space.  It returns the
collision count, physical time, obstacle labels and physical collision
points.  For the selected channel, the endpoint map is the same measurable
map at every parameter:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-laboratory-endpoint-map}
 \mathcal O_{bb}(\mathcal R)=
 \begin{cases}
 (y_{\rm first},y_{\rm last}),&\text{if the prescribed $bb$ bridge succeeds},\\
 \dagger,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus the signed endpoint coordinates are laboratory $y$ coordinates, not
parameter-dependent coordinates centered at moving contacts.  The channel
patches are selected by the fixed labels and the fixed inequalities
$|y|<u_*$.  These conventions give a single sample space and a single
observation map for the entire local family.

Let $S_{b,g,\vartheta}$ and $B_{b,g,\vartheta}$ denote the half-line actions
and positive amplitudes constructed in the same fixed transverse coordinate.
At the reference geometry $(g_0,0)$ write
\[
 S_b=S_{b,g_0,0},\qquad B_b=B_{b,g_0,0},\qquad
 \dot S_b(u)h=D_\vartheta S_{b,g_0,\vartheta}(u)|_{0}[h].
\]
The anchoring gives $S_{b,g,\vartheta}(0)=S'_{b,g,\vartheta}(0)=0$ throughout
the family.

Fix finitely many designs $(b_\ell,d_\ell)$ with
$b_\ell\in\{0,1\}$ and $0<d_\ell<d_*$, $1\le\ell\le L$.  At flight number
$j$ the $\ell$th acquisition time is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-fixed-physical-window}
                         t_{j,\ell}=jg_0+d_\ell.
\end{equation}
It is programmed from the reference experiment and is unchanged under every
local alternative.

\subsection{The multirate local parameter and its exact support expansion}

Let $j_n\to\infty$ and $\delta_n\downarrow0$.  For bounded
$z=(a,h)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^r$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-multirate-parameter}
 g_n(a)=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a,
 \qquad
 \vartheta_n(h)=\delta_nh.
\end{equation}
The physical excess of the fixed window is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-raw-excess}
 t_{j_n,\ell}-j_ng_n(a)=d_\ell-\delta_na.
\end{equation}
Thus the gap has its natural faster local scale $\delta_n/j_n$: an order
$\delta_n/j_n$ gap displacement produces an order-$\delta_n$ support shift
after $j_n$ flights.

For a same-type limiting endpoint law at design $(b,d)$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-base-defining-function}
 w_{b,d}(u,v)=d-S_b(u)-S_b(v).
\end{equation}
Let $A_{b,d}>0$ be its normalized smooth amplitude, so the base conditional
endpoint density is
\[
 f_{b,d}(u,v)=A_{b,d}(u,v)(w_{b,d}(u,v))_+.
\]
For $z=(a,h)$ define the linear support velocity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-support-velocity}
 U_b(u,v)z=a+\dot S_b(u)h+\dot S_b(v)h
\end{equation}
and its boundary information matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-information-matrix}
 \mathcal K_{b,d}
 =\int_{\{S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d\}}
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)\,U_b(u,v)^tU_b(u,v)}
 {|\nabla(S_b\oplus S_b)(u,v)|}\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}

The following proposition replaces the informal stability step of the
preceding revision by an explicit comparison of experiments at the critical
nonregular scale.

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative fixed-window reduction]
\label{prop:v21-fixed-window-reduction}
For every compact local set $K\subset\mathbb R^{1+r}$, the exact limiting
boundary endpoint density at the fixed physical window
\eqref{eq:v21-fixed-physical-window} can be written
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-exact-endpoint-density}
 f^{\rm ex}_{n,z}(u,v)
 =A^{\rm ex}_{n,z}(u,v)(w^{\rm ex}_{n,z}(u,v))_+,
 \qquad z\in K,
\end{equation}
where, uniformly on the common endpoint box,
\begin{align}
 \bigl\|w^{\rm ex}_{n,z}
 -(w_{b,d}-\delta_nU_bz)\bigr\|_{C^2}
 &\le C_Kr_n,
 &r_n&:=\delta_n^2+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n},
                                      \label{eq:v21-support-remainder}\\
 \|A^{\rm ex}_{n,z}-A_{b,d}\|_{C^1}
 &\le C_K\delta_n.                  \label{eq:v21-amplitude-remainder}
\end{align}

Let $\widetilde f_{n,z}$ be the normalized ideal density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-ideal-endpoint-density}
 \widetilde f_{n,z}(u,v)=
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)(w_{b,d}(u,v)-\delta_nU_b(u,v)z)_+}
 {\displaystyle\int A_{b,d}
  (w_{b,d}-\delta_nU_bz)_+\,du\,dv}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-exact-ideal-hellinger}
 \sup_{z\in K}H^2(f^{\rm ex}_{n,z},\widetilde f_{n,z})
 \le C_K\left\{r_n^2\log\frac e{r_n}+\delta_n^2\right\}.
\end{equation}
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-critical-success-scale}
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-product-exact-ideal}
 k_n\sup_{z\in K}
 H^2(f^{\rm ex}_{n,z},\widetilde f_{n,z})\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Consequently the exact and ideal $k_n$-record endpoint experiments have
Le Cam distance $o(1)$, uniformly on $K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:v21-raw-excess}, the time-window contribution to the defining
function is exactly $-\delta_na$.  Smooth dependence of the half-line action
on $(g,\vartheta)$ gives, uniformly with two endpoint derivatives,
\[
 S_{b,g_n(a),\vartheta_n(h)}
 =S_b+\delta_n\dot S_bh
 +\frac{\delta_na}{j_n}\partial_gS_b
 +O_K(\delta_n^2+\delta_n^2/j_n^2).
\]
Substitution at both endpoints proves
\eqref{eq:v21-support-remainder}.  Smooth dependence of the flux amplitude
and of its normalizing constant gives
\eqref{eq:v21-amplitude-remainder}.

The normalizing denominator in \eqref{eq:v21-ideal-endpoint-density} equals
$1+O_K(\delta_n)$, because the first variation of the integrand is integrable.
Thus the ideal density still has a regular amplitude differing from
$A_{b,d}$ by $O_K(\delta_n)$.  Apply the hypersurface-stability estimate
of Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-hypersurface-stability} with support error $C_Kr_n$
and amplitude error $C_K\delta_n$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:v21-exact-ideal-hellinger}.

It remains to check that this one-record error is negligible after
$k_n$ observations.  The amplitude term satisfies
$k_n\delta_n^2=O(1/\log(1/\delta_n))$.  For the support term, if
$j_n^{-1}\le\delta_n$, then $r_n\le2\delta_n^2$ and
\[
 k_nr_n^2\log(e/r_n)
 \le Ck_n\delta_n^4\log(1/\delta_n)=O(\delta_n^2).
\]
If $j_n^{-1}>\delta_n$, then $j_n<\delta_n^{-1}$ and
$r_n\le2\delta_n/j_n$, while
$\log(e/r_n)\le C\log(1/\delta_n)$; hence
\[
 k_nr_n^2\log(e/r_n)\le Cj_n^{-2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:v21-product-exact-ideal}.  Hellinger distance
subadditivity for products and the standard comparison of Hellinger and total
variation give the experiment-level conclusion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite positive design at the tangent level}

\begin{lemma}[Finite positive-offset design]
\label{lem:v21-finite-positive-design}
Fix a finite labelled contact-jet order $M$.  Let $\vartheta$ consist of the
two labelled contact jets through order $M$ at fixed gap in the anchored
chart.  There exist finitely many same-type designs $(b_\ell,d_\ell)$ and
positive weights $\alpha_\ell$, $\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell=1$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-design-information}
 \mathcal K_{\rm design}
 =\sum_{\ell=1}^L\alpha_\ell\mathcal K_{b_\ell,d_\ell}
\end{equation}
is positive definite on the full local coordinate $(a,h)$.  The finite set
may depend on $M$; no universal finite design for the infinite jet is
asserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $z=(a,h)$ lies in the kernel of $\mathcal K_{b,d}$ for both labels
and every sufficiently small $d>0$.  Positivity of the weight in
\eqref{eq:v21-information-matrix} and continuity imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-kernel-level}
 a+\dot S_b(u)h+\dot S_b(v)h=0
 \quad\text{whenever}\quad S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d.
\end{equation}
Take $v=0$ and $d=S_b(u)$.  Since the anchoring gives
$\dot S_b(0)h=0$, we obtain
$a+\dot S_b(u)h=0$.  Letting $u\to0$ gives $a=0$, hence
$\dot S_b(u)h=0$ for both labels near the contact.  Therefore all action-jet
derivatives through order $M$ vanish.  Proposition
\ref{prop:v21-tangent-jet-isomorphism} now gives $h=0$.  Thus the common
kernel is trivial at the differential level, with no appeal to nonlinear
injectivity.

Every unit vector is detected by some positive design.  Positivity remains
strict in a neighborhood of that vector; compactness of the unit sphere
selects finitely many designs.  Any convex combination with strictly positive
weights is positive definite.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The boundary endpoint-output Gaussian experiment}

Choose a finite design from Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-finite-positive-design}.  Let
$k_{n,\ell}$ be target success counts and put
$k_n=\sum_\ell k_{n,\ell}$.  Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-batch-rate}
 \frac{k_{n,\ell}}{k_n}\to\alpha_\ell,
 \qquad
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1,
 \qquad
 j_n\delta_n\to0.
\end{equation}
The final condition keeps the long-bridge success prefactors uniformly
comparable across compact local parameter sets; it is independent of the
moving-support singularity and is compatible with logarithmic choices of
$j_n$.

Let $p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)$ be the one-preparation success probability in
the boundary model.  The uniform threshold law gives, for every fixed compact
local set $K$, constants $c_K,C_K>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-uniform-success}
 c_Kd_\ell^2e^{-j_n\gamma_0}
 \le p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)
 \le C_Kd_\ell^2e^{-j_n\gamma_0},
 \qquad z\in K.
\end{equation}
Choose deterministic caps $N_{n,\ell}$ from one fixed compact parameter
neighborhood, not from the unknown $z$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-cap-choice}
 \inf_{z\in K}N_{n,\ell}p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)
 \ge2k_{n,\ell}.
\end{equation}
A binomial Chernoff bound yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-cap-tail}
 \sup_{z\in K}\Pr_z\{\text{cap reached before }k_{n,\ell}
 \text{ successes}\}\le e^{-c k_{n,\ell}}.
\end{equation}
On this exceptional event the endpoint-output experiment returns a fixed
cemetery symbol.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-window multirate endpoint Gaussian experiment]
\label{thm:v21-fixed-window-gaussian}
Under \eqref{eq:v21-batch-rate}, the capped boundary endpoint-output
experiment for the local coordinate $z=(a,h)$ is, uniformly on every compact
local parameter set, at $o(1)$ Le Cam distance from a dominated uniformly LAN
experiment with information matrix $\mathcal K_{\rm design}$.  Equivalently,
the original, generally non-dominated endpoint-output experiment converges to
the Gaussian shift with that information matrix.

More precisely, after applying to each design the common-collar censoring of
Section~\ref{sec:v21-vector-information}, the dominated representative has
central sequence
$\Delta_n\Rightarrow N_{1+r}(0,\mathcal K_{\rm design})$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-physical-lan-representative}
 \log\frac{d\overline{\mathsf Q}_{n,z}^{\rm end}}
          {d\overline{\mathsf Q}_{n,0}^{\rm end}}
 =z^t\Delta_n-\frac12z^t\mathcal K_{\rm design}z+o_P(1)
\end{equation}
uniformly on compact $z$-sets.  No global likelihood ratio is asserted for
$\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\rm end}$ itself when its support extends beyond the null
support.

The gap is visible at rate $\delta_n/j_n$ and the anchored registered contact
geometry at rate $\delta_n$.  The matrix $\mathcal K_{\rm design}$ is positive
definite.  The Gaussian testing profile, compact-local central-sequence
estimator, and local asymptotic minimax conclusions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-vector-boundary-gaussian} hold with
$\mathcal J_\Sigma$ replaced by $\mathcal K_{\rm design}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First ignore the cap.  Proposition~\ref{prop:v21-fixed-window-reduction}
shows that, for each design, the exact boundary endpoint experiment and the
ideal linearly moving-support experiment
\eqref{eq:v21-ideal-endpoint-density} are at $o(1)$ Le Cam distance after the
prescribed number of successful records.  The ideal family satisfies the
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-vector-boundary-gaussian}, with boundary
velocity $U_b$ and information matrix $\mathcal K_{b,d}$.  Hence it is at
$o(1)$ Le Cam distance from its dominated common-collar uniformly LAN
representative.

The design batches are independent.  Their central sequences and information
matrices add, and $k_{n,\ell}/k_n\to\alpha_\ell$ gives
$\mathcal K_{\rm design}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-finite-positive-design} gives
positive definiteness.  Finally \eqref{eq:v21-cap-tail} changes the experiment
by $o(1)$ in total variation, uniformly on compact local sets.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transfer to actual long finite bridges}

The previous theorem concerns the limiting boundary endpoint experiment.  We
now transfer it to actual long finite bridges at the level of the complete
stopped acquisition transcript before any statistical coarsening.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform stopped finite-to-boundary transfer]
\label{thm:v21-stopped-local-transfer}
Let $\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}$ be the complete stopped transcript law for the
actual finite bridges under the deterministic caps, fixed physical times and
target success counts above.  The transcript retains failures, successes,
design labels, raw endpoint records and stopping times.  Let
$\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}$ be the boundary transcript generated by the same
policy on the same registered laboratory coordinate space.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-transfer-condition}
                         k_n\tau^{j_n}\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
then, for every compact local set $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-lecam-transfer}
 \sup_{z\in K}
 \|\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}-\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}=o(1).
\end{equation}
The bound survives every common Markov coarsening.  In particular, the actual
finite-bridge endpoint-output experiment inherits
Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-fixed-window-gaussian}.

No failed preparation is free.  If $T_{n,\ell}$ denotes the number of raw
preparations in batch $\ell$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-physical-cost}
 \mathbb E_zT_{n,\ell}
 \le \frac{k_{n,\ell}}{p_{n,\ell}(z)}
 \le C_Kk_{n,\ell}d_\ell^{-2}e^{j_n\gamma_0}
\end{equation}
uniformly for $z\in K$ and all large $n$; the deterministic cap gives the
corresponding absolute upper bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For bounded local $z$, \eqref{eq:v21-raw-excess} remains in a common compact
subcollar and the anchored graphs remain in a compact smooth family.  The
policy satisfies the deterministic bounded-stopping hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer}.  That theorem couples the actual and
boundary stopped paths under the identical policy and gives the
success-weighted bound $C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}$, uniformly in the local family.
This proves \eqref{eq:v21-lecam-transfer}.  Total variation contracts under a
common Markov kernel, so the same estimate applies to endpoint output.  The
cost estimate follows from domination by the uncapped negative-binomial
stopping time and \eqref{eq:v21-uniform-success}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Endpoint information is not complete-transcript efficiency]
\label{rem:v21-endpoint-coarsening}
The Gaussian information matrix $\mathcal K_{\rm design}$ characterizes the
declared endpoint-output experiment.  It is not asserted to be the efficiency
bound for every procedure based on the complete stopped transcript.  In
particular, the success probability contains the factor
$\sinh(j\gamma)^{-1}$, so waiting counts can carry an additional and possibly
faster information channel in directions which change the hyperbolic
exponent.  The complete preparation cost is charged above, but cost accounting
is not statistical equivalence between the complete transcript and its
endpoint coarsening.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A charged pilot for centered windows}

The fixed-window theorem requires no onset calibration.  A centered-window
formulation can nevertheless be recovered after an independent charged pilot,
provided one states exactly which output sigma-field is being compared.

\begin{corollary}[Pilot-centered endpoint-output equivalence]
\label{cor:v21-pilot-centered}
Assume the coarse-bracket hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration}.  Let $r_n\downarrow0$ and let an
independent pilot produce $\widehat g_n$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-pilot-gap}
 j_n|\widehat g_n-g|\le Cr_n^{m+1}
\end{equation}
with probability $1-\eta_n$, where $\eta_n\to0$.  Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-pilot-smallness}
 k_nr_n^{2m+2}\log(1/r_n)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
After the pilot use the common data-dependent rule
$t_{n,\ell}=j_n\widehat g_n+d_\ell$ and the same capped success targets.
Consider the post-pilot endpoint-output sigma-field consisting only of the
selected endpoint records and the cap cemetery symbol; the pilot transcript
and the numerical programmed times are used for acquisition and their costs
are charged, but they are not included in this comparison experiment.  Then
that endpoint-output experiment is at vanishing Le Cam distance from the
oracle centered endpoint-output experiment using
$t_{n,\ell}=j_ng+d_\ell$.  Including the finite-bridge replacement adds at
most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-pilot-total-error}
                    \eta_n+C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}+o(1).
\end{equation}
The pilot cost is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-pilot-cost}
 \mathbb ET_{\rm pilot}
 \le C_me^{j_n\gamma}r_n^{-(2m+2)}\log(C_m/\eta_n).
\end{equation}
No equivalence is claimed here for a richer experiment which also retains the
pilot transcript or the realized design times; such an experiment contains
additional random coordinates.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On the good pilot event the post-pilot excess differs from its oracle value by
at most $Cr_n^{m+1}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-hypersurface-stability}, uniformly
over the finite design, gives one-record squared Hellinger distance at most
$Cr_n^{2m+2}\log(1/r_n)$.  Product Hellinger bounds and
\eqref{eq:v21-pilot-smallness} give $o(1)$ for the retained endpoint output.
Add $\eta_n$ for the bad pilot event and use
Theorem~\ref{thm:v21-stopped-local-transfer} for the finite-bridge
replacement.  The cost bound is the charged self-calibration estimate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What changes for generic nearby tables}

The anchored model above is not a disguised parameter-dependent coordinate
choice.  It is a physical submodel specified by
\eqref{eq:v21-anchored-graphs}.  The statistical theorem therefore estimates
an unknown gap and unknown anchored contact geometry in a genuinely common
laboratory record space.

A generic nearby-table family can also be written on a fixed laboratory
record space, but contact translations and frame rotations then become
additional nuisance directions.  If $\chi_{b,\eta}$ is the map from a fixed
laboratory transverse coordinate $y$ to the contact-centered coordinate for
the table parameter $\eta$, the laboratory action is
\[
 \mathscr S_{b,\eta}(y)=S_{b,\eta}(\chi_{b,\eta}(y)),
\]
and its first variation contains the coordinate-motion term
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v21-frame-motion-term}
 D_\eta\mathscr S_b(y)[h]
 =D_\eta S_b(y)[h]+S_b'(y)D_\eta\chi_b(y)[h]
\end{equation}
at a reference chart with $\chi_{b,0}(y)=y$.  Translation of the contact
origin, rotation of the tangent frame, and onset motion contribute through
this full laboratory support velocity.  The non-dominated boundary theorem
of Section~\ref{sec:v21-vector-information} applies to such a fixed-space
family once these nuisance coordinates and the resulting velocity are
included.  What is not asserted here is that the anchored information matrix
\eqref{eq:v21-design-information} remains the full information matrix after
silently discarding those nuisance directions.  A charged registration pilot
with coordinate error $\varepsilon_n$ is negligible for the endpoint-output
experiment whenever
\[
 k_n\varepsilon_n^2\log(1/\varepsilon_n)\longrightarrow0,
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:v21-hypersurface-stability}.
