\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:v25-introduction}

Long alternating trajectories near a closest pair of convex scatterers
contain two kinds of geometric information.  Their probability is
exponentially small in the number of flights, with an exponent determined
by the linearized collision dynamics.  Conditional on observing such a
trajectory, its signed endpoints retain a nonlinear boundary profile.  The
first quantity alone does not determine the two contacts.  The second is
not simply the action of one finite flight: it is the sum of two stationary
half-line actions, and its inversion must account for every return to both
boundaries.

We study these boundary profiles for periodic planar dispersing billiards.
The local forward results apply to smooth strictly convex contacts and do
not require finite horizon, reflection symmetry, equal scatterers or equal
contact curvatures.  At each fixed derivative order we obtain a relative
limit on an offset collar independent of the number of flights.  We then
recover the two unsymmetrized contact germs, solve the intrinsic marked
periodic gluing problem under explicit uniqueness assumptions, and construct
consistent global estimators from a common physical observation protocol.
A separate sequence of local experiments identifies what is lost when the
residual preparation time and the waiting counts are discarded.

\subsection{The relative law and the intrinsic inverse}

For a selected closest-pair channel let \(g>0\) be its gap, \(\kappa_0\)
and \(\kappa_1\) its labelled contact curvatures, and
\[
 c_b=1+g\kappa_b,\qquad
 \gamma=\operatorname{arcosh}\sqrt{c_0c_1}.
\]
The integer \(j\) counts flights.  If \(W_j(u,v)\) is the stationary
length with prescribed signed transverse endpoints, put
\[
 E_j=W_j-jg,\qquad b_j=\frac{-W_{j,uv}}{-W_{j,uv}(0,0)}.
\]
The relative theorem constructs positive functions \(B_b\), normalized
by \(B_b(0)=1\), and strictly convex functions \(S_b\), normalized by
\(S_b(0)=S_b'(0)=0\), such that, with \(p=j\bmod2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-intro-relative}
 \|E_j-S_0\oplus S_p\|_{C^k}
       +\|b_j-B_0\otimes B_p\|_{C^k}\le C_k\tau^j,
       \qquad 0<\tau<1.
\end{equation}
Here \(k\) is any fixed order, including the stated mixed geometric and
offset derivatives.  Constants may depend on that order.  The construction
and its quantitative dependencies are given in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-main-relative} and Part I.

At excess time \(d>0\), the limiting successful endpoint--residual-time
law has density proportional to
\[
       B_0(u)B_p(v)\mathbf1_{\{0<r<d-S_0(u)-S_p(v)\}}.
\]
After \(r\) is removed, its support still identifies
\(S_b(u)+S_b(v)\) in each same-type even-flight experiment.  The slice
\(v=0\) determines \(S_b\).  The physical onset, which is retained as a
separate datum, determines \(g\).  With \(a_b=S_b''(0)\), the formulas
\[
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}),\qquad
 c_b=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_b/a_{1-b}},\qquad
 \kappa_b=(c_b-1)/g
\]
recover the leading contact geometry.  At degree \(n\ge3\), the new graph
jets enter the new action jets through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-intro-jet-block}
 M_n=\begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\operatorname{csch}(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\operatorname{csch}(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \mathfrak r_b=\sqrt{c_b/c_{1-b}},\qquad \det M_n=1.
\end{equation}
The nonlinear terms of lower degree are already determined.  The proof uses
a weighted inverse of the half-line stationarity operator, finite-truncation
envelope differentiation, and homogeneous degree isolation.  It does not
infer an infinite-order condition number from \(\det M_n=1\).

For the global problem the lattice is a marked abstract copy of
\(\mathbb Z^2\), without a supplied Euclidean Gram form.  A channel label
\((a,b,\ell)\) identifies its obstacle orbits and abstract deck displacement.
Its two contact laws are recorded in its own oriented channel frame; the
relative placements of distinct channel frames are not supplied.  A
signature-rigid transition means that the relevant complete oriented
curvature signature occurs at a unique framed point.  General analytic
symmetries give a gluing space rather than an arbitrarily chosen phase.

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic contact and marked periodic determination]
\label{thm:v25-intro-rigidity}
The physical onset and the two same-type signed limiting endpoint-law germs
of a smooth strictly convex channel determine every finite labelled contact
jet.  If the incident boundaries are connected and real analytic, they
determine their complete boundary images in the channel frame.

For a labelled periodic table, assume the measured channel graph contains
two signature-rigid cycles with linearly independent marked deck
displacements, and a rooted signature-rigid spanning tree reaching every
obstacle orbit.  Then the intrinsic channel data determine the entire
marked periodic table, including the Euclidean realization of its lattice,
up to one simultaneous element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The contact statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity} and its
analytic continuation consequence.  The incidence classification is
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-intrinsic-table-rigidity}; the rank-two reconstruction
is Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-lattice-gram-recovery}.  In the root frame its final
lattice step is \(L=VM^{-1}\), where \(V\) consists of the two recovered
translation holonomies and \(M\) of their marked deck displacements.
The remaining placements are propagated along the signature-rigid tree.
\end{proof}

Thus the metric-free lattice conclusion is not a discovery of unknown deck
labels.  The labels are part of the marked observation.  Nor is the last
matrix identity the difficult analytic inverse: its input is supplied by
contact recovery, analytic continuation and unique incidence matching.

\subsection{An observable long-bridge reconstruction theorem}

The statistical acquisition must distinguish two coordinate questions.
Within each channel, a transverse origin and tangent must be acquired before
a signed test can be evaluated.  Between channels, the relative Euclidean
placement must subsequently be reconstructed from the intrinsic laws.
We do not replace either task by a parameter-dependent change of variables.

For the global experiment each channel detector records first and last
collision positions in one oriented Euclidean sensor frame shared by its
two endpoint types, together with the preparation-to-first-impact time and
the success or failure symbol.  Different channel sensor frames have no
supplied common registration.  The only design instructions are the marked
channel, endpoint type, even flight number and physical observation time.
Every preparation, including every failure, is retained and charged.
This endpoint-position acquisition is specified on common Borel spaces in
\eqref{eq:v25-design-record-spaces}.  The inherited local information
experiment using an anchored scalar laboratory coordinate is a distinct,
explicitly coarser setting; it is not silently substituted for this global
acquisition.

Let \(\mathfrak K\) be a compact marked analytic class with the preceding
exact uniqueness structure, uniform positive geometry and holomorphic
curvature collars, persistent channels and nondegenerate rank-two
holonomies, as specified in Section~\ref{sec:v25-signature-stability}.
Write \(d_q\) for the labelled \(C^q\) distance in a root-frame gauge.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform physical reconstruction using only long even bridges]
\label{thm:v25-intro-physical}
There exist finite, deterministically capped policies \(\pi_s\) in the
common physical endpoint-position experiment and measurable table estimators
\(\widehat T_s\) such that every used flight number is even and at least
\(s\), and for every fixed \(q\ge0\) and \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \sup_{T\in\mathfrak K}\Pr_T^{\pi_s}
                  \{d_q(\widehat T_s,T)>\epsilon\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Neither gaps, contact centers, tangents, inter-channel placements nor a
lattice metric are supplied to these policies.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-observable-calibration} constructs a charged onset and
contact-frame pilot at the already chosen final even flight number.  A
successful near-onset record localizes its endpoints to \(O(\sqrt h)\),
while the first successful observation time estimates the gap to \(h/j\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-fixed-order-physical} combines this pilot with mixed
onset--law separators and a finite-template minimum-distance estimator.
The compact inverse modulus and the diagonal construction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-global-physical-reconstruction} give the conclusion.
\end{proof}

At each fixed accuracy the preparation cap is finite and displayed, rather
than expressed only in uncharged successful records.  The finite separator
library and compact inverse modulus need not have an effective rate as
functions of the class bounds.  The result is uniform consistency, not a
claim of polynomial-time design generation, uniform-in-order conditioning
or an analytic minimax rate.  Separately,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-finite-signature-embedding} selects a finite signature
with a uniform local embedding and global exclusion margin, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:v25-noisy-signature-match} proves unique stable matching under
the stated \(C^2\) perturbations.  The compact inverse modulus does not
serve as a substitute for that local matching proof.

\subsection{Information under coarsening}

In the anchored finite-dimensional local family, retaining residual time
leaves positive density at a moving ceiling.  At contact-shape scale
\(k_n^{-1}\), the successful endpoint--time experiment is equivalent to
a generally non-dominated Poisson boundary-shift experiment.  The gap scale
in the fixed physical window is \((j_nk_n)^{-1}\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:v24-two-sided-deficiency} supplies explicit kernels in
both directions, including the layer count, its locations, the reconstructed
bulk and the intersection with the fixed residual-time face.  Its bulk
hypothesis is a bounded \(O(k_n^{-1})\) relative density perturbation,
verified from the smooth fixed-window model.

Removing residual time makes the endpoint density vanish linearly at its
support boundary.  The endpoint scale is then determined by
\(k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1\).  The original family need not be
dominated at finite sample size; its common-collar representative has the
uniform LAN expansion and its finite local subexperiments have the stated
Gaussian limit.  Waiting counts distinguish the normal quotient to a level
set of the hyperbolic exponent at scale \((j_n\sqrt{k_n})^{-1}\), while
endpoint information acts on the tangent directions at their slower scale.
The count quotient and the endpoint tangent space are formulated
intrinsically.  Deleting waiting counts is a further observation coarsening,
not an identification of these different experiments.

\subsection{Comparison with related inverse problems}

Marked-length results use observations different from the channel-law
family considered here.  De Simoi, Kaloshin and Leguil~\cite{DSKL} determine
analytic open dispersing billiards from marked lengths under the symmetry
and genericity assumptions of their theorem.  Finamore and
Leguil~\cite{FinamoreLeguil} treat finite-horizon Sinai billiards using an
enriched marked length spectrum and a CAT(0) construction.  Our local
forward law requires neither their spectral observation nor their global
symmetry hypotheses.  Conversely, our global inverse assumes signed channel
laws, onsets, marked deck labels and signature-rigid incidence structure;
these are not asserted to follow from an ordinary or enriched marked length
spectrum.  Without a proved relation between those data maps, neither
subsumption nor a direct generalization is claimed.

The observation restriction also matters within the present inverse
problem.  A mixed-type one-flight signed support function determines both
graph germs by the elementary square-root formulas of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v25-one-flight-inverse}.  An unrestricted finite-flight
consistency assertion is therefore not the principal reason for introducing
long bridges.  Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-intro-physical} permits the smallest
admissible flight number to diverge and uses only same-type even records;
its proof cannot invoke that short-flight inverse.  The relative law on a
fixed collar, the inversion of its two half-line actions and the information
hierarchy concern this restricted asymptotic observation family.

Nonregular support-dependent estimation and exceptional normal rates have a
substantial history; see Smith~\cite{Smith1985} and the references in Part II.
Meister and Reiss~\cite{MeisterReiss} prove Le Cam equivalence between a
nonregular regression experiment and Poisson experiments whose intensity
support carries the target function.  Thus a Gaussian/Poisson distinction or
Poissonization by itself is not claimed as a new statistical principle.
The results here identify the billiard-specific boundary density, its
unsymmetrized contact inverse, and the kernels connecting the retained
physical record levels, with their rare-event acquisition cost.

\subsection{Organization}

Part I proves the relative boundary law, signed contact inversion, intrinsic
analytic gluing and finite-signature stability, and records both short-flight
benchmarks.  Part II studies the moving-support local experiments and the
retained-time and waiting-count coarsenings.  The final part constructs the
common-observable calibration and global estimator, and separately gives a
compatible analytic variation bundle for uniform local designs.  The
appendices retain the complete auxiliary derivations and applications.
