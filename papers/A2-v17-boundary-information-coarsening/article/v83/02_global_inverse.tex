\section{Observable components and the exact global inverse}
\label{sec:v83-components}

Fix an endpoint. The positivity of the weights and the full column
ranks give $B=\operatorname{rank}P_1$. Choose sets $I,J$ of $B$ rows
and columns with $(P_1)_{IJ}$ invertible, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-operators}
 K_j=(P_j)_{IJ}(P_1)_{IJ}^{-1}
     =U_I\diag(q_{j,b})U_I^{-1},\qquad
 q_{j,b}=\pi_{j,b}/\pi_{1,b}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Joint separation and component recovery]
\label{lem:v83-components}
The vectors $(q_{2,b},\ldots,q_{q+2,b})$ are pairwise distinct.
Their common rank-one projectors determine
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-component}
 E_{j,b}=(P_1)_{:J}(P_1)_{IJ}^{-1}R_b(P_j)_{I:}
         =\pi_{j,b}U_{:b}V_{:b}^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-channel}
 \pi_{j,b}=\one^{\mathsf T}E_{j,b}\one,\quad
 U_{:b}=E_{1,b}\one/\pi_{1,b},\quad
 V_{:b}=E_{1,b}^{\mathsf T}\one/\pi_{1,b}.
\end{equation}
These objects are independent of the chosen minor and spectral
combination, up to one simultaneous permutation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If two joint vectors were equal, the ratio of the corresponding
unnormalized weights would take one constant value at
$T_1,\ldots,T_{q+2}$. Its logarithm is a polynomial of degree at most
$q$ plus $\log(T-W_b)-\log(T-W_c)$. When $W_b\ne W_c$, its
$(q+1)$st derivative has one strict sign on $T>\max(W_b,W_c)$:
it is a nonzero constant multiple of
$(T-W_b)^{-q-1}-(T-W_c)^{-q-1}$. Repeated Rolle's theorem excludes
$q+2$ equal values. When $W_b=W_c$, equality at those nodes forces
the difference polynomial to be constant, contrary to distinctness
of the action--detector tuples.

A real linear combination of the $K_j$ avoiding the finitely many
pairwise collision hyperplanes has simple spectrum and has the desired
common projectors. In their common basis
$R_b=U_I e_be_b^{\mathsf T}U_I^{-1}$ and
$(P_1)_{:J}(P_1)_{IJ}^{-1}=UU_I^{-1}$. Substitution proves
\eqref{eq:v83-component}. Summation and column normalization give
\eqref{eq:v83-channel}. These formulas identify the actual component
matrices, so different choices give the same unordered objects.
\end{proof}

There is a finite deterministic spectral choice. Put $L=B(B-1)/2$.
For $q\ge1$ use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-finite-combinations}
 A_t=\sum_{\ell=0}^{q}t^\ell K_{\ell+2},\qquad
                    t=0,1,\ldots,qL.
\end{equation}
Each pair excludes at most $q$ values, since its eigenvalue difference
is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $q$ in $t$. One of the
$qL+1$ choices therefore has simple spectrum. For $q=0$, $K_2$
already has simple spectrum.

For recovered components the scalar data are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-logratios}
 \ell_j^{bc}=\log(\pi_{j,b}/\pi_{j,c})
 =\log(a_b/a_c)+\sum_{\nu=1}^q
   (\theta_{b,\nu}-\theta_{c,\nu})T_j^\nu
       +\log(T_j-W_b)-\log(T_j-W_c).
\end{equation}
An unequal-action pair is detected by the nonzero first contrast in
\eqref{eq:v83-contrasts}. Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-scalar} recovers that
pair absolutely. The one-anchor inverse following
\eqref{eq:v83-monotone-solve} gives the other actions and all relative
coefficients. Centering yields \eqref{eq:v83-center} without choosing
one privileged reference branch globally.

\begin{proof}[Proof of exact identification and sharpness in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-main}]
Rank gives $B$, Lemma~\ref{lem:v83-components} gives the channels and
probabilities, and \eqref{eq:v83-logratios} with the scalar theorem
gives all centered parameters. This also proves uniqueness against
an alternative full-rank representation with unknown component count.
Conversely \eqref{eq:v83-gauge} multiplies every unnormalized component
at a given clock by the same positive factor. It leaves every $P_j$
unchanged and is the entire continuous ambiguity.

For the topological assertion, define the fibre over $z$ to be the
unordered set of recovered columns $U_{:b}(z)$. The columns are distinct
because they are linearly independent. Each spectral neighborhood
therefore gives $B$ disjoint local continuous sections of this set.
The resulting space $\cC\to Q$ is a $B$-sheeted covering, and
\eqref{eq:v83-channel} and the scalar inverse define $\Xi$ on it.
On a connected overlap, two enumerations differ by a locally constant
permutation, hence by a constant one. This alone does not establish
the existence of a global enumeration. If $Q$ is simply connected,
path lifting from one fibre is independent of the path and provides
such an enumeration. Two global enumerations, once they exist, differ
by a constant permutation on connected $Q$.

Finally fix two strictly positive full-rank channels. The level set
constructed at $q+2$ nodes in Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-scalar} keeps the
log ratio of the two component probabilities fixed. It therefore
keeps every normalized matrix fixed while an action changes. Choose
the starting parameters in the interior of all probability constraints;
a sufficiently small part of this level set preserves them. Baseline
retention can be decreased before the construction to ensure a strict
upper probability bound on the finite clock interval. This proves the
worst-case lower bound within the same observation class.
\end{proof}

\section{A global noisy inverse on the component covering}
\label{sec:v83-global}

We now give the quantitative construction. The exact covering and the
off-model extension are distinct objects. Local extensions do not in
general agree on overlaps. Their labels and gauges will first be
synchronized; only then will their values be averaged.

\subsection{Quantitative local charts}
Fix $q,m,u,v,B$, the clocks and a compact endpoint domain. Norms on
matrix spaces are fixed Euclidean norms; $C^m$ norms use the fixed
finite coordinate atlas. On a reference exact field suppose the
centered parameters and observations have $C^m$ norms at most $L_0$,
and suppose positive constants $r,R,p_*,\sigma_*,\delta,\gamma$
satisfy
\begin{gather}
 r\le T_j-W_b\le R,\qquad \pi_{j,b}\ge p_*,\qquad
               \sigma_B(P_1)\ge\sigma_*,\label{eq:v83-margins}\\
 \min_{b\ne c}\max_{2\le j\le q+2}|q_{j,b}-q_{j,c}|\ge\delta,
 \qquad \max_b W_b-\min_b W_b\ge\gamma.\nonumber
\end{gather}
On chosen minor charts also impose
$\sigma_{\min}((P_1)_{IJ})\ge\mu>0$. A finite refinement and all
subordinate partition functions are fixed before considering a noise
perturbation. Constants may depend on their $C^m$ norms and on the
listed margins. Only centered detector coefficients are bounded; an
unobservable common detector polynomial is never bounded as if it
were a recovered parameter.

These charts can be selected from exact observations, rather than
supplied latent labels. Rank determines the minor size, minor singular
values select admissible submatrices, and the candidate spectra in
\eqref{eq:v83-finite-combinations} select separating combinations.
After component recovery, the divided difference selects an anchor.
Continuity keeps the selected choices valid on a neighborhood in $Q$,
and compactness gives a finite subcover. This is an existence and
local reconstruction statement for continuous data; a finite noisy
mesh requires sampling bounds to certify the margins between mesh
points.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform local extension]\label{lem:v83-local}
On a fixed chart with \eqref{eq:v83-margins} and the chosen minor,
separating spectrum and unequal anchor, there is a pointwise smooth
extension $F_a$ from matrix data to locally enumerated centered
parameters. On a sufficiently small neighborhood it satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-local-estimate}
 \|F_a(\widehat P)-F_a(P)\|_{C^m}
       \le C_a\max_j\|\widehat P_j-P_j\|_{C^m}.
\end{equation}
No spatial derivative above order $m$ is used in this estimate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first justify a quantitative separating choice. For $q\ge1$ let
$C_V$ be the largest infinity norm of the inverse Vandermonde matrix
on any $q+1$ distinct integers in $\{0,\ldots,qL\}$. If a pair's
eigenvalue polynomial is smaller than $\delta/(2C_V)$ at $q+1$
of these integers, all its coefficients are smaller than $\delta$,
a contradiction. Each pair can therefore have a gap below this
threshold at at most $q$ choices. Counting the $L$ pairs gives a
choice with every gap at least $\delta/(2C_V)$. The case $q=0$
uses its gap $\delta$. Shrinking a chart preserves half this margin.

Matrix inversion is smooth on the selected minor neighborhood. For a
simple eigenvalue, the derivative of the characteristic polynomial at
that root is the product of its separations from the other roots.
The implicit function theorem gives smooth eigenvalue branches. Small
real perturbations retain real eigenvalues: conjugation and uniqueness
in each disjoint root disk force reality. The spectral projector is
\[
 R_b=\prod_{c\ne b}\frac{A_t-\lambda_c\id}{\lambda_b-\lambda_c}.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:v83-component}--\eqref{eq:v83-channel}, followed by
positive divisions and logarithms, extend smoothly. The extensions
need not jointly diagonalize all perturbed $K_j$; that is not required.

Here is an explicit anchor threshold. Set $a_0=\gamma/R^{q+2}$.
Some exact pair has first contrast of magnitude at least $a_0$,
because that contrast is an integral over an interval of length at
least $\gamma$ with integrand at least $R^{-q-2}$. If all contrast
errors are below $a_0/4$, the maximum observed absolute contrast
exceeds $3a_0/4$. Accept pairs above $a_0/2$ and freeze one on a
chart. Every accepted pair then has true contrast at least $a_0/4$,
hence true action separation at least $a_0r^{q+2}/4$.
The contrast error bound follows from the finite divided-difference
coefficients, the probability floor and the local spectral estimate.
This gives a threshold tied to the stated action margin, not an
assumption that a noisy nonzero contrast is reliable.

For this anchor, the inverse in Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-scalar} is
smooth with uniform derivative bounds on the compact parameter class.
For every other component the fixed-anchor contrast has derivative
$w_0(W_b)>0$, including when its action equals the anchor action.
Polynomial interpolation and fibrewise centering finish the local
extension. Repeated product and chain rules through order $m$ yield
\eqref{eq:v83-local-estimate}. Derivatives of the finite-dimensional
smooth formulas of order up to $m+1$ are controlled by the margins;
these are not unknown spatial derivatives of order $m+1$.
\end{proof}

The extension permits signed channel entries when the reference
channels have zeros. The column sums are still one. A smooth
open-neighborhood extension taking values in the probability simplex
at its boundary is not asserted. With a strict entrywise probability
margin, sufficiently small perturbations retain positivity. In either
case the observation identities hold on exact data and the stated
error estimate holds off the model.

\subsection{Quantitative overlap matching}
Let $Q_a$ be a finite good cover subordinate to the local charts,
with a fixed partition of unity $\chi_a$. Local chart values have
already been centered. Full rank and compactness give a constant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-signature-gap}
 s_*\le\inf_{z\in Q}\min_{b\ne c}
                    \|U_{:b}(z)-U_{:c}(z)\|,
                 \qquad s_*>0.
\end{equation}
For example, if $\sigma_B(U)\ge s_U$, one may take
$s_*=\sqrt2s_U$. All comparisons use the columns in the original
readout space, not eigenvectors in unrelated minor coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[Synchronization under off-model perturbations]
\label{lem:v83-match}
Suppose each extended local channel column is within $e<s_*/4$ of
its exact counterpart. On every overlap, nearest-column matching
between two local reconstructions is a unique bijection. It is
constant on each connected overlap, satisfies the exact permutation
cocycle on triple overlaps, and agrees with the reference covering.
The matching is unchanged in a sufficiently small neighborhood of the
reference data.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The two approximations to the same true column are at distance at
most $2e$. Approximations to different true columns are at distance
at least $s_*-2e>2e$. Thus each nearest neighbor is uniquely the
approximation to the same reference column, which proves bijectivity.
The matching cannot change continuously while this strict gap holds,
so it is constant on connected overlaps. On a triple overlap every
matching identifies the same reference column; their composition is
therefore exactly the direct matching. This proves the cocycle, not
an approximate cocycle. The strict inequalities persist under further
small perturbations. Reference labels are used in this proof only
to verify the rule; the rule itself compares observable local outputs.
\end{proof}

Write $g_{ab}$ for these transition permutations, acting on the local
centered vectors. They define a covering $\widehat\cC\to Q$
isomorphic to $\cC$. They need not give a global enumeration on a
nonsimply connected domain. Define, in chart $a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-gluing}
 \widehat\Xi_a=\sum_b\chi_b\,g_{ab}F_b(\widehat P).
\end{equation}
Only terms supported on $Q_a\cap Q_b$ occur; extend the products
by zero outside their supports. On a triple overlap the cocycle gives
$g_{ab}g_{bc}=g_{ac}$. Consequently \eqref{eq:v83-gluing}
transforms by $g_{aa'}$ when passing from chart $a'$ to chart $a$.
It is a well-defined field on the covering. No average of unmatched
vectors or of incompatible reference-branch gauges has been taken.

\subsection{The quotient metric and the global estimate}
For two fields on isomorphic component coverings define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-quotient-metric}
 d_m([\Xi],[\Xi'])=
       \min_{\iota:\cC\simeq\cC'}\|\Xi-\iota^*\Xi'\|_{C^m}.
\end{equation}
Isomorphisms are over the identity of $Q$; the finite-atlas norm uses
the local lifts of the base coordinates. There are finitely many such
isomorphisms, since each is determined by its action on one fibre of
connected $Q$. The continuous gauge has already been removed by
\eqref{eq:v83-center}. On a simply connected gate the expression is
just the minimum over one constant permutation of the global centered
vectors. We compare only the local stratum of isomorphic coverings;
no identification with a different monodromy type is implicit.

\begin{theorem}[Global quotient inverse and stability]
\label{thm:v83-global}
Fix an exact reference field satisfying
\eqref{eq:v83-margins}--\eqref{eq:v83-signature-gap}, its finite
quantitative chart cover, and $C^m$ bounds as above. There is an open
$C^m$ neighborhood $\mathcal N$ of its normalized matrix field and
a reconstruction $\mathcal R$ on $\mathcal N$, valued in centered
fields on its component covering modulo covering isomorphisms, such
that the following hold.

On exact $B$-component model data in $\mathcal N$ in the prescribed
quantitative class, $\mathcal R$ is the exact inverse of Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-main}. On arbitrary, not necessarily
model, real matrix fields $P',P''\in\mathcal N$ it satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-global-estimate}
 d_m\bigl(\mathcal R(P'),\mathcal R(P'')\bigr)
          \le C\max_j\|P'_j-P''_j\|_{C^m}.
\end{equation}
After choosing one representative covering, the map is locally
Lipschitz between $C^m$ spaces. It uses no derivatives above order
$m$, and the covering's monodromy is stable on this neighborhood.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Shrink the data neighborhood so that all frozen minors, separating
combinations, probability divisions and anchor inverses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v83-local} remain valid. Shrink it further so that
all column errors are less than $s_*/8$. Lemma~\ref{lem:v83-match}
then supplies locally constant transition permutations which remain
fixed throughout this neighborhood and agree with the reference
covering. Use these permutations in \eqref{eq:v83-gluing}.
For exact model data, all local inverses recover its same centered
field after these transitions. Since $\sum\chi_b=1$, gluing returns
that field exactly.

For arbitrary $P',P''$, compare them on the common reference covering.
The transitions are the same fixed matrices. Differentiate
\eqref{eq:v83-gluing} through order $m$ and use the product rule,
the fixed partition bounds and \eqref{eq:v83-local-estimate}.
A finite sum gives \eqref{eq:v83-global-estimate} for that particular
covering isomorphism, hence also for the minimum in
\eqref{eq:v83-quotient-metric}. The same construction shows that
every nearby field has the same transition cocycle, so its monodromy
is unchanged. This proves the off-model assertion as well as stability
on the exact image.
\end{proof}

Exactness here refers to the fixed visible-rank stratum. The ambient
neighborhood also contains models with additional arbitrarily weak
components; the fixed-rank extension is not their exact model-order
inverse. The rank-threshold statement below specifies the signal margin.

Thus the distinction between a local labelled inverse and a global
quotient inverse is substantive: the latter requires the synchronization
lemma. On a rectangle one may label a base fibre and transport the
labels through a spanning tree of the cover. The cocycle and simple
connectedness make the result independent of the remaining paths.
This is the global representative used by the usual labelled $C^m$
estimate; ``after a local permutation'' is not a substitute for it.

\begin{proposition}[A genuine monodromy example]\label{prop:v83-monodromy}
There are smooth exact observations on a circle satisfying the
log-affine model, all rank and separation margins and the unequal-action
condition, whose component covering has a nontrivial three-cycle.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $t\in[0,2\pi]$, $b=0,1,2$, and
$\phi_b=t/3+2\pi b/3$. Set
$W_b=-C+\cos\phi_b$, $\theta_{b,1}=\sin\phi_b$ and $a_b=1$,
where $C$ is large enough that all actions precede $T_1$.
Choose orthonormal $e,f$ in the plane of vectors of coordinate sum zero
in $\R^3$ and put
\[
 U_{:b}=V_{:b}=\tfrac13\one+
                \epsilon(e\cos\phi_b+f\sin\phi_b).
\]
For small positive $\epsilon$ every entry is positive. These three
columns are affinely independent in their probability plane, hence
linearly independent in $\R^3$. The action--rate pairs form three
distinct points on a circle, and their action coordinates are never
all equal. All strict margins are uniform by compactness.
At $t=2\pi$ the entire component tuple is the cyclic permutation of
its value at $t=0$. Therefore the unordered matrices are smooth on
the endpoint circle, whereas following one recovered column once
around it ends at the next column. This is a three-cycle monodromy
and admits no global enumeration. Conditional independence is realized
by two independent draws from the specified, here equal, channel laws.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Visible rank and error transfer]\label{cor:v83-error}
If $\|\widehat P_1-P_1\|$ in operator norm is below $\sigma_*/4$,
thresholding singular values at $\sigma_*/2$ recovers $B$. If
$H_j$ are joint endpoint/readout densities with
$f_j=\one^{\mathsf T}H_j\one\ge f_*>0$, bounded $C^m$ norms and
$\max_j\|\widehat H_j-H_j\|_{C^m}\le\varepsilon$, normalization
followed by $\mathcal R$ has quotient error at most $C\varepsilon$.
An additional $C^m$ model error $\eta$ changes this bound to
$C(\varepsilon+\eta)$ while the margins hold.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Singular values change by at most the operator-norm error. The zero
singular values remain below the threshold and all nonzero ones remain
above it. Summation and the floor $f_*$ keep the perturbed marginal
above $f_*/2$ for small error. The product and reciprocal estimates
in $C^m$ bound the normalization error by $C\varepsilon$.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-global} and the triangle inequality.
\end{proof}

The corollary is an error-transfer theorem, not a finite-sample rate
or a claim that exact latent model order is testable under unrestricted
misspecification. In particular the known rank margin is part of its
threshold guarantee.
