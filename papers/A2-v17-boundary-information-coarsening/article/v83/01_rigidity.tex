\section{Introduction and the sharp observation theorem}
\label{sec:v83-main}

An action can occur in a clocked observation as the zero of an affine
factor $T-W$. A selective detector multiplies this factor by an unknown
function of the deadline. The inverse problem is to separate the zero
from that multiplier after total mass and branch identity have both been
lost. The distinction between a finite-dimensional detector and a freely
variable detector is essential; the distinction between four and five
observations is not merely a question of numerical conditioning.

The main result gives the sharp number of deadlines for polynomial
log-detectors. Its scalar mechanism is an interval-moment inverse, rather
than a count of zeros of a rational derivative. Two consequences are a
criterion for general detector spaces and a global stability theorem on
the actual component covering. Thus the exact inverse, its clock
complexity, and its topological ambiguity are treated separately.

\subsection{Observation model}
Let $T_1<\cdots<T_J$ be fixed real deadlines. Let $Q$ be a compact
connected smooth domain, possibly with boundary or corners, equipped with
a finite coordinate atlas. A rectangle is the principal example.
Fix a nonnegative integer $q$. At each $z\in Q$ the observations are
positive projective classes of $u\times v$ matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-model}
 M_j=U\diag\left\{a_b\exp\left(\sum_{\nu=1}^q
             \theta_{b,\nu}T_j^\nu\right)(T_j-W_b)\right\}_{b=1}^B
 V^{\mathsf T},\qquad a_b>0,\quad W_b<T_1.
\end{equation}
The $B$ columns of each of $U$ and $V$ are probability vectors and are
linearly independent. Both channels may depend on $z$ but are independent
of $j$. The two readings are independent conditional on the component
and endpoint. The positive integer $B\le\min(u,v)$ is not given.
Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-probability}
 P_j=\frac{M_j}{\one^{\mathsf T}M_j\one}
     =U\diag(\pi_{j,b})V^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
The whole joint matrix is normalized, not its individual rows or columns.
Arbitrary positive factors depending on $j,z$ have consequently disappeared.
All coefficients are locally $C^m$ functions of $z$, where $m\ge0$ is an
integer; local enumerations are allowed to differ by permutations.

For clarity, a local enumeration specifies the tuple
\[
 (W_b,U_{:b},V_{:b},\log a_b,\theta_{b,1},\ldots,\theta_{b,q}).
\]
Distinct components form a finite covering of the endpoint domain. No
trivialization of that covering is included in the observations.
Center the continuous gauge fibrewise by putting
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-center}
 \alpha_b=\log a_b-B^{-1}\sum_c\log a_c,\qquad
 \vartheta_{b,\nu}=\theta_{b,\nu}-B^{-1}\sum_c\theta_{c,\nu}.
\end{equation}
These definitions are independent of an enumeration. We write
$\Xi=(W,U,V,\alpha,\vartheta)$ for the centered parameters.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp selective-detector rigidity]\label{thm:v83-main}
Suppose \eqref{eq:v83-model} holds and, at every endpoint, the tuples
$(W_b,\theta_{b,1},\ldots,\theta_{b,q})$ are pairwise distinct and the
actions are not all equal. Then any $q+3$ distinct projective deadlines
determine the visible count $B$, the component covering, and $\Xi$ on
that covering, up to a covering isomorphism.

The complete continuous ambiguity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-gauge}
 a_b\mapsto h a_b,\qquad
 \theta_{b,\nu}\mapsto\theta_{b,\nu}+\rho_\nu,
 \quad h>0,
\end{equation}
with the same $h,\rho_\nu$ for every component over a given endpoint.
No action is changed by this gauge. On a simply connected gate the
covering is trivial, and two global enumerations differ by one constant
permutation.

On the quantitative classes of Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-global}, the inverse
has a locally Lipschitz extension in $C^m$ to an open neighborhood of
the exact normalized data, with no loss of spatial derivatives. The
extension is defined modulo the same permutation and gauge.

The count $q+3$ is optimal for this observation class: with $q+2$
deadlines, two-component models satisfying strict positivity, rank,
and unequal-action margins have an action-changing continuous ambiguity.
In particular four deadlines are globally sufficient and necessary in
the worst case for log-affine selective detectors.
\end{theorem}

The optimality statement is uniform over the class, not a claim that
every larger model needs that many clocks. The word ``visible'' refers
to the full-column-rank model. An invisible duplicate or a component
with arbitrarily small mass cannot be counted stably without a signal
margin. Equal actions are allowed when other detector coefficients
distinguish the components and an unequal-action pair remains available.

\subsection{The conceptual reduction and its predecessors}
The spectral recovery of components is a classical mechanism. The
identifiability results of Allman--Matias--Rhodes~\cite{V83AMR},
observable-operator methods~\cite{V83AHK}, and the joint-diagonalization
construction of Bonhomme--Jochmans--Robin~\cite{V83BJR} are close
predecessors. We include the matrix calculation because the observable
normalizations and the spatial label transitions matter here. It is not
presented as a new theorem about general tensor decompositions.

The remaining data are scalar ratios of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-scalar}
 f(T)=P(T)+\log(T-x)-\log(T-y),\qquad P\in\cP_q,
                 \quad x,y<T_1.
\end{equation}
Divided differences are classical; we use the convention and elementary
identities surveyed by de Boor~\cite{V83deBoor}. The determinant condition
in Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-classification} is exactly the two-function
Chebyshev condition, in the terminology of Karlin--Studden~\cite{V83KS}.
The argument below is therefore not a claim of new general Chebyshev
or moment theory. Its use here identifies the \emph{moving endpoints}
of a signed interval after quotienting an unknown detector, and yields
both a sharp clock theorem and a necessary-and-sufficient criterion
within the stated positive-kernel class.

The geometric conversion uses the usual generating-function calculus
\cite{V83MW} and return-time primitive for contact suspensions
\cite{V83Geiges}. It is the recovery of the absolute sheets from the
weaker observation that precedes this conversion. The global geometry
requires phase-domain information; Section~\ref{sec:v83-geometry}
gives a positive covered-domain theorem and an exact obstruction to
recovering an unsampled open phase region in the smooth category.

\section{Oriented intervals and detector quotients}
\label{sec:v83-interval}

\begin{lemma}[Two moments of an interval]\label{lem:v83-interval}
Let $I$ be an open interval, let $w:I\to(0,\infty)$ be continuous, and
let $r:I\to\R$ be continuous. For $x,y\in I$, $x\ne y$, set
\[
 \mathcal M(x,y)=\left(\int_y^x w(s)\dd s,
                       \int_y^x w(s)r(s)\dd s\right).
\]
The map $\mathcal M$ is one-to-one on all oriented nondegenerate
intervals in $I$ if and only if $r$ is strictly monotone.
If $w,r$ are smooth and $r$ is strictly monotone, $\mathcal M$ is a
local smooth diffeomorphism everywhere on this domain, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-moment-jacobian}
 \det D\mathcal M(x,y)=w(x)w(y)\bigl(r(x)-r(y)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a primitive $F$ with $F'=w$. It maps $I$ increasingly onto an
open interval $I'$. Write $u=F(y)$, $d=F(x)-F(y)$, and
$R=r\circ F^{-1}$. The first moment is $d$. For fixed $d>0$ the
second is
\[
 H_d(u)=\int_u^{u+d}R(t)\dd t.
\]
If $R$ is strictly increasing, then
$H_d'(u)=R(u+d)-R(u)>0$; strict decrease gives the opposite sign.
Thus the two moments determine $d,u$, and then $x,y$. Negative
orientation is handled by reversing the endpoints. The sign of the
first moment distinguishes the two orientations.

Conversely, injectivity of $\mathcal M$ implies that $H_d$ is injective
on its interval of definition for every sufficiently small $d>0$.
A continuous injective real function on an interval is strictly
monotone. If $R$ is constant on a nonempty open interval, $H_d$ is
constant on a smaller interval for small $d$, a contradiction.
Otherwise choose $u_0<u_1$ with unequal values of $R$. For all small
$d$, the sign of $H_d(u_1)-H_d(u_0)$ equals that of
$R(u_1)-R(u_0)$, since $H_d/d\to R$ uniformly on compact intervals.
Hence all these $H_d$ have the same monotonicity orientation. Applying
this observation on a compact interval containing $u_0,u_1$ and any
other two points, and passing to the limit, shows that $R$ is
nondecreasing everywhere or nonincreasing everywhere. A monotone
function with equal values at distinct points is constant between
them. The already excluded constant intervals therefore make $R$
strictly monotone. Finally, differentiation of the two integrals gives
\eqref{eq:v83-moment-jacobian}, which is nonzero when $x\ne y$.
\end{proof}

This lemma concerns intervals with a known density, not arbitrary
measures. Two moments would plainly not determine an arbitrary measure.
It is also a global result: a nonsingular Jacobian alone would not
exclude two disjoint intervals with the same moments.

\subsection{An invariant criterion for a sampled detector space}
Fix $J=d+2$ deadlines and a $d$-dimensional subspace
$\cD\subset\R^J$ containing the constant vector. This is the space
of allowed sampled log-detectors, including log-weights. Let
$c^0,c^1$ be a basis of $\cD^\perp$, and define its Cauchy kernels by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-kernels}
 k_a(s)=\sum_{j=1}^J\frac{c_j^a}{T_j-s},\qquad a=0,1.
\end{equation}
All actions lie in an open interval $I\subset(-\infty,T_1)$.
Assume that the annihilator contains a kernel positive on $I$, and
choose $c^0$ so that $k_0>0$ there.

\begin{theorem}[Positive-kernel detector classification]
\label{thm:v83-classification}
For sampled scalar data
\[
 \ell_j=e_j+\log(T_j-x)-\log(T_j-y),\qquad
 e\in\cD,\quad x,y\in I,\quad x\ne y,
\]
the ordered actions and the sampled nuisance vector are globally
identifiable if and only if $k_1/k_0$ is strictly monotone on $I$.
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-chebyshev}
 k_0(s)k_1(t)-k_1(s)k_0(t)
\end{equation}
has one strict sign for all $s<t$ in $I$.
This criterion is independent of the chosen annihilator basis.
If it fails, two distinct nondegenerate action intervals and nuisance
vectors give exactly the same scalar observations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 \log(T_j-x)-\log(T_j-y)=-\int_y^x\frac{\dd s}{T_j-s}.
\]
Consequently $(-c^0\cdot\ell,-c^1\cdot\ell)$ is exactly the pair
of interval moments with $w=k_0$ and $r=k_1/k_0$. Since the two
contrasts have joint kernel $\cD$, equal contrasts are also sufficient
for two logarithmic action vectors to differ by an allowed nuisance
vector. Lemma~\ref{lem:v83-interval} proves both directions. After
the actions are known, subtraction gives $e$.
A change of annihilator basis multiplies \eqref{eq:v83-chebyshev} by
its nonzero determinant. The existence of a positive member belongs
to the kernel space, not to a particular basis. This proves invariance.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a classification on the declared positive-kernel class,
not a claim that every finite-dimensional detector space has such a
kernel. It characterizes the scalar stage at the chosen deadlines.
For an arbitrary detector space, spectral separation of latent
components is a separate condition. Both conditions are automatic in
the polynomial application below. Coefficients of an underlying
function space are recovered from its sampled vector only when its
evaluation map has full rank.

\subsection{Sharp polynomial clock rigidity}
For distinct nodes $t_0,\ldots,t_n$ use the divided difference
\[
 [t_0,\ldots,t_n]f=\sum_{i=0}^n
             \frac{f(t_i)}{\prod_{h\ne i}(t_i-t_h)}.
\]
It annihilates polynomials of degree less than $n$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-cauchy-dd}
 [t_0,\ldots,t_n]\frac1{T-s}
       =\frac{(-1)^n}{\prod_{i=0}^n(t_i-s)}.
\end{equation}
Here the divided difference is taken in $T$. The identity follows
inductively from the first-order difference; no limiting nodes are used.

\begin{theorem}[The sharp scalar inverse]\label{thm:v83-scalar}
Let $q\ge0$, $J=q+3$, and $x,y<T_1$ with $x\ne y$. The $J$ values
of \eqref{eq:v83-scalar} determine $x,y$ and $P$ globally. Its inverse
is locally smooth throughout this parameter domain. With only $q+2$
values, there are action-changing one-parameter families with exactly
the same observations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the two consecutive divided differences of order $q+1$ and set
\begin{align}
 d_0&=(-1)^{q+2}[T_1,\ldots,T_{q+2}]f,
 &w_0(s)&=\prod_{i=1}^{q+2}(T_i-s)^{-1},\label{eq:v83-contrasts}\\
 d_1&=(-1)^{q+2}[T_2,\ldots,T_{q+3}]f,
 &w_1(s)&=\prod_{i=2}^{q+3}(T_i-s)^{-1}.\nonumber
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:v83-cauchy-dd} and integration give
$d_a=\int_y^x w_a(s)\dd s$. The two contrasts are independent
and span the annihilator of the sampled polynomial space: the first
has a nonzero coefficient at $T_1$, the second at $T_{q+3}$, and
that annihilator has dimension two. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-ratio}
 \frac{w_1(s)}{w_0(s)}=\frac{T_1-s}{T_{q+3}-s},\qquad
 \left(\frac{w_1}{w_0}\right)'(s)
       =\frac{T_1-T_{q+3}}{(T_{q+3}-s)^2}<0.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:v83-interval} therefore recovers $x,y$ globally.
Subtraction and polynomial interpolation recover $P$. The same lemma
and polynomial evaluation give a local smooth inverse.

For completeness the full scalar Jacobian has columns
\[
 1,T,\ldots,T^q,-(T-x)^{-1},(T-y)^{-1}.
\]
Multiplying each row by $(T_j-x)(T_j-y)$ turns a linear relation
into a polynomial of degree at most $q+2$. Its vanishing at $q+3$
nodes forces it to be zero. Evaluation of that polynomial identity at
$x,y$ kills the two pole coefficients because $x\ne y$; the
polynomial coefficients then vanish as well.

At $q+2$ nodes the columns $1,T,\ldots,T^q,-(T-x)^{-1}$ alone are
independent, by multiplying by $T-x$ and using the same polynomial
argument. The implicit function theorem solves for these $q+2$
parameters as $y$ varies near any unequal-action reference. Thus $y$
actually changes along a local one-parameter level set. Strict action,
weight and detector margins persist on a small portion of it.
\end{proof}

The proof gives a monotone reconstruction, not merely uniqueness. For
$d_0>0$, let $F'=w_0$, write $u=F(y)$ and solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v83-monotone-solve}
 \int_u^{u+d_0}\frac{w_1(F^{-1}(t))}{w_0(F^{-1}(t))}\dd t=d_1.
\end{equation}
Its left side is strictly decreasing. Then
$y=F^{-1}(u)$ and $x=F^{-1}(u+d_0)$. Negative $d_0$ is treated
by reversing orientation. This formula also specifies the admissible
interval for a bracketed solve. It never sorts components by action.

If the anchor action $y$ is already known, a single contrast of order
$q+1$ recovers any $x$, including $x=y$, since
$x\mapsto\int_y^x w_0$ is strictly increasing. The remaining nuisance
polynomial follows by interpolation. Thus $q+2$ nodes suffice for the
remaining components once an unequal-action anchor has been found.
The contrast $d_0$ is nonzero exactly when the two actions are unequal.
