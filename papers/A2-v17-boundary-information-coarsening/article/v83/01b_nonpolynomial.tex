\subsection{A nonpolynomial detector family}

The positive-kernel criterion is not confined to polynomial interpolation.
The following application also separates the criterion from a degree
count for rational derivatives.

\begin{proposition}[Exponential log-detectors]\label{prop:v83-exponential}
Fix $\lambda>0$ and deadlines $T_j=j$, $1\le j\le4$.
Replace the detector polynomial in \eqref{eq:v83-model} by
$\beta_b e^{\lambda T_j}$, where the $\beta_b$ are unknown.
If the pairs $(W_b,\beta_b)$ are distinct, $W_b<1$, and the
actions are not all equal, four projective matrices determine the
absolute actions, both channels, the relative weights and the relative
coefficients $\beta_b$. Four are necessary in the worst case.
The local enumerations define the same intrinsic component covering
as in Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-main}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $a=e^\lambda>1$ and $B_0=3+2/(a-1)>3$. For the sampled
nuisance space $\cD=\operatorname{span}\{\one,(a,a^2,a^3,a^4)\}$
choose annihilator vectors
\[
 c^0=(a,-a-1,1,0),\qquad c^1=(0,a,-a-1,1).
\]
Their kernels satisfy, for $s<1$,
\[
 k_0(s)=\frac{(a-1)(B_0-s)}{(1-s)(2-s)(3-s)}>0,\qquad
 \frac{k_1(s)}{k_0(s)}
   =\frac{(1-s)(B_0+1-s)}{(4-s)(B_0-s)}.
\]
The logarithmic derivative of this positive ratio is
\[
 -\frac{3}{(1-s)(4-s)}+
       \frac1{(B_0-s)(B_0+1-s)}<0.
\]
Indeed the second term is smaller than
$1/((3-s)(4-s))$, which is smaller than the magnitude of the first.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v83-classification} therefore identifies an ordered
unequal-action pair and its two nuisance coefficients globally.
Once one action is known, the single positive-kernel contrast identifies
any other action, even when it coincides with the anchor.

It remains to justify the latent spectral step rather than assume it.
For a ratio $f(T)=\alpha+\beta e^{\lambda T}
+\log(T-x)-\log(T-y)$ with $x\ne y$, one has
\[
 e^{-\lambda T}f'(T)=\lambda\beta+
          (x-y)\frac{e^{-\lambda T}}{(T-x)(T-y)}.
\]
The nonconstant term is strictly monotone on $T>\max(x,y)$, so
$f'$ has at most one zero. Three equal values of $f$ are impossible
by Rolle's theorem. For $x=y$ the same conclusion follows from
$\beta\ne0$. Thus the two reduced operators at clocks two and three
have distinct joint component vectors. The explicit matrix argument
of Lemma~\ref{lem:v83-components} applies unchanged. Scalar inversion,
centering, and the column-signature construction then prove the stated
exact recovery and covering assertion.

For the lower bound use three clocks. The columns
$1,e^{\lambda T},-(T-x)^{-1}$ are independent at any three distinct
nodes above $x$: the derivative of a linear combination, after
multiplication by $e^{-\lambda T}$, is a constant plus a multiple of
$e^{-\lambda T}(T-x)^{-2}$, a strictly decreasing positive function.
Rolle's theorem forces a combination with three zeros to be identically
zero. The implicit function theorem therefore solves for
$(\alpha,\beta,x)$ while a nearby $y$ varies, keeping all three ratios
fixed. Fixed positive two-component channels convert this scalar level
set to identical projective matrices, as in the polynomial lower bound.
\end{proof}

Known positive affine changes of the deadline coordinate give the same
example on any four equally spaced clocks, with the actions transformed
back to their original absolute units. As throughout the finite-clock
model, a small positive baseline makes these detector functions valid
retention probabilities on the observation interval.
