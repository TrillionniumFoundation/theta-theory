\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

This paper studies one boundary structure from three points of view.  A long
alternating collision bridge in a planar dispersing billiard has an
exponentially small endpoint twist.  After that twist is normalized, the
finite bridge converges on a fixed offset collar to a nonlinear boundary law.
The support of the same law, when signed endpoint coordinates are retained,
determines the two participating contact germs.  Finally the moving support
of the endpoint law generates a logarithmic nonregular statistical
experiment which can be realized on fixed physical time windows in a common
laboratory coordinate system.  The theorem chain is
\[
 \boxed{\text{relative boundary law}}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\text{signed labelled contact rigidity}}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\text{physical endpoint-output Gaussian experiment}}.
\]
The final arrow is an experiment theorem.  It does not identify a
parameter-dependent coordinate chart with a physical observation rule, and it
does not write a finite-sample likelihood ratio where moving supports destroy
absolute continuity.

\subsection{Long bridges and a nonlinear boundary law}

Fix a labelled closest-pair channel between two strictly convex contacts in a
smooth periodic dispersing billiard.  In local transverse coordinates write
the facing boundaries as
\[
 (-\psi_0(u),u),\qquad (g+\psi_1(v),v),
\]
with gap $g>0$.  No reflection symmetry or equality of the two contacts is
assumed.  If $W_j(u,v)$ is the stationary length of the selected alternating
$j$-flight bridge, put
\[
 E_j(u,v)=W_j(u,v)-jg,
 \qquad
 t_j=-\partial_{uv}W_j(0,0)>0.
\]
The quadratic Jacobi recurrence has an exponent $\gamma>0$ and
$t_j\asymp e^{-j\gamma}$.  The first principal theorem is a relative, rather
than absolute, long-bridge limit.  On an endpoint box and a positive offset
collar independent of $j$, there are strictly convex half-line actions
$S_0,S_1$ and positive half-line amplitudes $B_0,B_1$ such that, after the
terminal parity is fixed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v21-relative}
 E_j(u,v)=S_0(u)+S_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),
 \qquad
 \frac{-W_{j,uv}(u,v)}{-W_{j,uv}(0,0)}
 =B_0(u)B_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),
\end{equation}
for every fixed derivative order and some $0<\tau<1$.  The determinant
comparison is localized at the two boundary layers through trace-class
perturbations.  In particular, the proof never obtains a relative statement
by dividing an extensive absolute error by the exponentially small reference
twist.

The amplitudes $B_b$ are also the normalized scalar linearizing densities of
the physical stable return maps.  This identifies the determinant boundary
factor with the intrinsic one-dimensional transport along stable branches,
while the nonlinear bridge theorem remains an independent stationary-action
statement.

\subsection{Signed support and arbitrary labelled contact jets}

Integrate out residual time but retain the two signed endpoint positions.
For a same-type endpoint experiment the limiting density is positive exactly
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v21-support}
                         S_b(u)+S_b(v)<d.
\end{equation}
Thus the support threshold satisfies
\[
 T_b(u,v)=S_b(u)+S_b(v),\qquad S_b(u)=T_b(u,0),
\]
and the two unsymmetrized actions are recovered directly from the physical
support.  The odd contact information which disappears from the scalar
profile is therefore lost by observation coarsening, not by a symmetry of the
billiard table.

Let $a_b=S_b''(0)$.  The physical onset determines $g$, and the exact
half-line Hessian identities give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v21-leading}
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}),
 \quad
 c_0=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_0/a_1},
 \quad
 c_1=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_1/a_0},
 \quad
 \kappa_b=\frac{c_b-1}{g}.
\end{equation}
Hence neither contact curvature is supplied.  Writing
\[
 \psi_b(y)=\frac{\kappa_b}{2}y^2+
 \sum_{n\ge3}\frac{q_{b,n}}{n!}y^n,
 \qquad
 s_n=(S_0^{(n)}(0),S_1^{(n)}(0))^t,
\]
the new pair $q_n=(q_{0,n},q_{1,n})^t$ enters the degree-$n$ action jet,
after all lower jets have been fixed, through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v21-jet-block}
 D_{q_n}s_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1=1.
\end{equation}
The determinant is one at every order.

Section~\ref{sec:v21-signed-rigidity} gives the all-order proof rather than
using this matrix as a formal reconstruction rule.  The half-line stationary
equations are solved in a weighted sequence space; the positive Jacobi
operator has the explicit half-line Green inverse; implicit differentiation
leaves the same invertible operator on the highest orbit derivative; the
envelope identity removes stationary-orbit variations from the first
variation of the action; and the two entries in each row of
\eqref{eq:intro-v21-jet-block} are the two absolutely convergent geometric
sums with the boundary and interior multiplicities displayed.  The finite
jet forward map has block lower-triangular differential with invertible
leading block and determinant-one higher diagonal blocks.  Thus its
differential is an isomorphism, not merely its nonlinear map an injection.

Consequently arbitrary smooth labelled contact jets, odd and even, are
recursively determined without reflection symmetry, equal contacts or
supplied curvature.  Real-analytic signed endpoint-law germs determine the
participating analytic contact germs, and connected analytic boundary images
then follow by analytic continuation.  For actual finite long bridges the
recovered finite jets differ from their limiting values by
$O_M(\tau^j)$ through every fixed order $M$.

This inverse theorem is local and labelled.  It does not claim that one
selected channel determines every obstacle.  Its data are also different
from a marked length spectrum.  Modern marked/enriched-length results use
global periodic-orbit information and obtain global conclusions under their
own analytic, symmetry or genericity assumptions
\cite{BDKL,DSKL,Osterman,FinamoreLeguil}.  The signed endpoint law is globally
narrower but locally richer: one channel supplies a continuum of signed
collision-coordinate support data and determines arbitrary smooth jets of
the two participating contacts.  Neither data set is asserted to be a
coarsening of the other.

\subsection{A non-dominated boundary experiment}

The density in \eqref{eq:intro-v21-support} vanishes linearly at a moving
regular boundary.  For a general vector family
\[
 f_\theta(x)=a_\theta(x)(w_\theta(x))_+,
 \qquad
 V=D_\theta w_\theta|_0,
\]
define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v21-information}
 \mathcal J_\Sigma=
 \int_{\Sigma}\frac{a_0VV^t}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma,
 \qquad \Sigma=\{w_0=0\}.
\end{equation}
The singular score is $V/w_0$, while the base density contributes a factor
$w_0$; the second score moment therefore diverges logarithmically.

The local family is genuinely non-dominated.  If the boundary moves outward,
the alternative assigns positive mass to a set on which the null has zero
mass.  We consequently do not write
$dP_{n,h}/dP_{n,0}$ for the original experiment.  Instead Section
\ref{sec:v21-vector-information} first proves compact-uniform bounds of order
$O(p_n\delta_n^2)$ for both types of support-exclusive mass and then censors
a collar of width
\[
 q_n=\delta_n(\log(1/\delta_n))^{1/4}.
\]
The resulting common-collar family is dominated and is uniformly LAN at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v21-critical}
 np_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1.
\end{equation}
The original and censored product experiments have Le Cam distance
$O((\log(1/\delta_n))^{-1/2})$.  Thus the original non-dominated family is
asymptotically equivalent to a genuinely dominated LAN representative and
converges to the same Gaussian shift with information
\eqref{eq:intro-v21-information}.  Compact-uniform contiguity, the Gaussian
testing profile, the local central-sequence estimator, the local asymptotic
minimax bound and uniform integrability for quadratic risk are proved only
after this finite-sample domination issue has been resolved.

The one-dimensional endpoint rate and parameter-dependent-support asymptotics
have classical antecedents
\cite{Akahira1975a,Akahira1975b,Smith1985,IbragimovHasminskii,HiranoPorter}.
The statistical contribution used here is the intrinsic hypersurface matrix,
its compact-uniform vector experiment formulation, and its coupling to the
billiard boundary law and signed inverse.  The paper does not claim generic
nonregular likelihood theory as new.

\subsection{Fixed laboratory windows and anchored unknown geometry}

A physical local experiment cannot center its observation rule at an unknown
gap or silently re-center endpoint coordinates at parameter-dependent
contacts.  We therefore fix one Euclidean laboratory chart and impose an
anchored registered submodel for the statistical theorem:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v21-anchored}
 \Gamma_{0,\vartheta}=(-\psi_{0,\vartheta}(u),u),
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{1,g,\vartheta}=(g+\psi_{1,\vartheta}(v),v),
 \qquad
 \psi_{b,\vartheta}(0)=\psi'_{b,\vartheta}(0)=0.
\end{equation}
The obstacle labels, signed transverse origin and tangent direction are fixed
for the entire family.  The observed endpoint coordinates are the literal
laboratory $y$ coordinates of the raw collision points.  Thus the observation
map is parameter independent.  Unknown contact/frame motion is not hidden in
the word ``registered''; Section~\ref{sec:v21-raw-physical} writes the
additional velocity term which would appear for a generic moving frame.

Fix a reference gap $g_0$ and positive offsets $d_\ell$.  At flight number
$j$ use the programmed physical times
\[
                         t_{j,\ell}=jg_0+d_\ell.
\]
For the local alternative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v21-multirate}
 g=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a,
 \qquad
 \vartheta=\delta_nh,
\end{equation}
the actual excess is exactly
$d_\ell-\delta_na$.  Hence gap and shape enter the same first-order support
velocity
\[
 U_b(u,v)(a,h)
 =a+D_\vartheta S_b(u)[h]+D_\vartheta S_b(v)[h].
\]
Its information matrix at a same-type window is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v21-physical-information}
 \mathcal K_{b,d}
 =\int_{\{S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d\}}
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)U_b(u,v)^tU_b(u,v)}
 {|\nabla(S_b\oplus S_b)(u,v)|}\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}

The exact fixed-window billiard endpoint density is not merely declared to be
``uniformly close'' to this ideal model.  Section
\ref{sec:v21-raw-physical} proves a quantitative expansion with support
remainder
\[
 r_n=O(\delta_n^2+\delta_n/j_n)
\]
and regular amplitude remainder $O(\delta_n)$.  A general stability lemma for
linearly vanishing hypersurface densities gives
\[
 H^2(P,Q)\le C\{r_n^2\log(e/r_n)+\delta_n^2\}.
\]
At $k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1$ this is $o(k_n^{-1})$, so the exact
and ideal endpoint experiments are at vanishing Le Cam distance.  This is the
abstract-to-billiard transfer at the same nonregular scale as the Gaussian
limit.

For every fixed finite labelled contact-jet order, finitely many positive
same-type windows make the weighted sum of
\eqref{eq:intro-v21-physical-information} positive definite.  The proof is
tangent-level.  A vector in the common kernel first has zero gap component,
then has zero action variation for both labels, and the differential
isomorphism of the signed jet map forces every finite jet component to
vanish.  The finite design may depend on the chosen jet order; no universal
finite design for the full infinite jet is asserted.

The resulting endpoint-output experiment is generally non-dominated, exactly
as in the abstract theorem.  After common-collar censoring it has a uniformly
LAN representative with positive-definite information matrix
$\mathcal K_{\rm design}$; before censoring it is asymptotically equivalent
to that representative and therefore has the same Gaussian limit experiment.
The gap is visible at rate $\delta_n/j_n$ and the anchored contact geometry at
rate $\delta_n$.

\subsection{Actual finite bridges, preparation cost and observation scope}

The boundary endpoint experiment is transferred to actual long finite
bridges before any statistical coarsening.  Under deterministic preparation
caps, the complete stopped transcript retains failures, successes, designs,
raw endpoints and stopping times.  The historical adaptive coupling, applied
on the common laboratory record space above, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-v21-stopped-transfer}
 \sup_{z\in K}
 \|\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}-\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}.
\end{equation}
Thus $k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0$ transfers the endpoint-output Gaussian experiment to
actual finite bridges.  Every failed preparation remains charged; the
expected raw cost is of order
$k_{n,\ell}d_\ell^{-2}e^{j_n\gamma_0}$ for the $\ell$th batch, uniformly on
compact local sets.

The local asymptotic minimax statement is deliberately attached to the
\emph{endpoint-output} experiment.  It is not an efficiency bound for all
procedures using the complete stopped transcript.  Waiting counts can contain
additional information through the long-bridge success probability and its
$\sinh(j\gamma)^{-1}$ factor.  Charging their cost is not the same as proving
them ancillary.  The complete transcript is used in the finite-to-boundary
coupling; the Gaussian information matrix describes the stated endpoint
coarsening.

A separate charged pilot justifies centered windows when desired.  The
comparison is made on the post-pilot endpoint-output sigma-field, while the
pilot transcript and realized acquisition times are used operationally and
their costs are charged.  If one retains those additional random coordinates,
the paper makes no claim that they are statistically equivalent to an oracle
which observes different design-time values.

\subsection{Scope and organization}

The deterministic theorem concerns a selected labelled channel and requires
no contact symmetry.  The principal statistical theorem concerns a local
anchored registered family in one fixed laboratory chart.  This is a genuine
physical submodel, not a parameter-dependent coordinate convention.  For a
generic nearby-table family, translations and rotations of the contact frames
must be included as nuisance directions in the same laboratory record space;
the abstract non-dominated boundary theorem then applies with their full
support velocity.  Alternatively a charged registration pilot is negligible
for endpoint output at the Hellinger scale
$k_n\varepsilon_n^2\log(1/\varepsilon_n)\to0$.

Part I proves the nonlinear relative law and the signed all-order inverse.
Part II proves the scalar and vector boundary-information theorems, the
fixed-window anchored endpoint experiment, the quantitative
abstract-to-billiard transfer, the actual finite-bridge stopped transfer, and
the fixed-table observation-coarsening hierarchy.  Earlier derivation modules
remain in the repository for provenance; the active source graph below uses
only the theorem versions needed by this revision.
