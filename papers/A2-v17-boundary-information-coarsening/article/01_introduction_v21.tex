\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

This paper studies one boundary mechanism from three directions.  A long
alternating collision bridge in a planar dispersing billiard has an
exponentially small endpoint twist.  After normalization by that twist, the
bridge has a nonlinear boundary law on an offset collar whose size does not
shrink with the number of flights.  The same law determines the labelled local
contact geometry from signed endpoint support.  Finally, on fixed physical
windows, its moving support generates a nonregular statistical experiment at a
logarithmic information scale.  The main chain is
\[
 \boxed{\text{relative boundary law}}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\text{signed local rigidity}}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\text{registered physical endpoint experiment}}.
\]
The finite-bridge comparison is proved on the richer complete stopped
transcript, including failures and stopping times.  The Gaussian
LAN/minimax statement is then made for an explicit endpoint-output coarsening;
we do not identify the richer waiting transcript with that coarsening.

Two interfaces require particular care.  A moving support is generally
non-dominated at every finite sample size, so a likelihood ratio against the
reference law need not exist.  We resolve this by a parameter-independent
collapse of a vanishing reference collar: the resulting experiment is
finite-sample dominated, is at $o(1)$ Le Cam distance from the original local
experiment, and is uniformly LAN.  Second, the physical statistical family is
defined in one fixed laboratory chart.  The contact origins and tangent axes
are anchored throughout the local family, the endpoint coordinates are
therefore genuine common observables, and the physical acquisition times are
programmed from the reference experiment rather than from the unknown table.

\subsection{The nonlinear relative boundary law}

Fix a labelled closest pair of strictly convex contacts in a smooth periodic
dispersing billiard.  In contact coordinates write the facing boundaries as
\[
 (-\psi_0(u),u),\qquad (g+\psi_1(v),v),
\]
with gap $g>0$.  No reflection symmetry or equality of the two contacts is
assumed.  Let $W_j(u,v)$ be the stationary length of the selected alternating
$j$-flight bridge and put
\[
 E_j(u,v)=W_j(u,v)-jg,
 \qquad t_j=-\partial_{uv}W_j(0,0)>0.
\]
The quadratic Jacobi recurrence has exponent $\gamma>0$ and
$t_j\asymp e^{-j\gamma}$.  The principal forward theorem proves, on a fixed
endpoint box and a fixed positive offset collar, a relative factorization of
the exact mixed endpoint derivative and of the associated physical flux.
There are half-line actions $S_0,S_1$ and positive half-line amplitudes
$B_0,B_1$ such that, after fixing terminal parity,
\[
 E_j(u,v)=S_0(u)+S_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),
 \qquad
 \frac{-\partial_{uv}W_j(u,v)}{-\partial_{uv}W_j(0,0)}
 =B_0(u)B_p(v)+O_{C^k}(\tau^j),
 \qquad 0<\tau<1,
\]
for every fixed derivative order.  The convergence includes every fixed mixed
geometric and offset derivative in the centered forward-law convention.  The
proof is nonlinear: the exponentially small twist is normalized before the
long-bridge limit, and the determinant comparison is localized at the two
ends through trace-class perturbations rather than estimated by an accumulated
per-site error.

The same half-line amplitudes are the normalized scalar linearizing densities
of the physical stable return maps.  This identifies the determinant law with
intrinsic transport along the two stable branches, but the scalar
linearization is not used as a substitute for the nonlinear bridge proof.

\subsection{Signed endpoint support and the determinant-one inverse}

After residual time is integrated out while the two signed endpoint positions
are retained, the limiting same-type endpoint density is positive precisely
on
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-support}
                   S_b(u)+S_b(v)<d.
\end{equation}
Consequently its support threshold satisfies
\[
 T_b(u,v)=S_b(u)+S_b(v),\qquad S_b(u)=T_b(u,0).
\]
The signed endpoint record therefore recovers the \emph{unsymmetrized}
half-line actions.  Odd information is lost only after a further
symmetrizing coarsening; it is not removed by a geometric parity assumption.

The all-order inverse is proved directly in the weighted half-line sequence
space.  The quadratic Jacobi operator has an explicitly decaying Green kernel
and a uniformly bounded inverse on a weighted $\ell^\infty$ space.  At every
endpoint differentiation order the highest orbit derivative is multiplied by
the same invertible nonlinear Jacobi operator, so the action jet of degree
$n$ depends on no graph derivative above degree $n$.  Varying only the new
graph jet and using the stationary envelope identity shows that the nonlinear
orbit correction cannot affect the first new homogeneous term.

Let $a_b=S_b''(0)$.  Physical onset gives $g$, and the exact half-line Hessian
identities yield
\[
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}),
 \quad c_0=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_0/a_1},
 \quad c_1=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_1/a_0},
 \quad \kappa_b=\frac{c_b-1}{g}.
\]
Thus neither contact curvature is supplied.  If
\[
 \psi_b(y)=\frac{\kappa_b}{2}y^2+
 \sum_{n\ge3}\frac{q_{b,n}}{n!}y^n,
 \qquad s_n=(S_0^{(n)}(0),S_1^{(n)}(0))^t,
\]
then, after the lower jets are fixed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-jet-block}
 D_{q_n}s_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1=1.
\end{equation}
Its determinant is exactly one for every $n\ge3$.  Hence arbitrary labelled
smooth contact jets, odd and even, are recovered recursively.  The same block
triangular argument is differentiated in the paper: on every fixed finite
labelled jet chart the differential of the action-jet map is injective and has
a uniformly bounded left inverse on compact positive sets.  This tangent
statement is later used to prove positive definiteness of a finite physical
information design; nonlinear injectivity alone is not used for that step.

For real-analytic contacts, the signed endpoint-law germs determine the two
participating analytic contact germs.  A finite registered network of such
channels determines every incident analytic contact germ; if every obstacle
under consideration is met by the network, analytic continuation determines
the corresponding labelled boundary images in the common registered frame.
No conclusion is asserted for obstacles not incident to the selected channel
network.

The data are different from marked or enriched length spectra.  Global
marked-length rigidity for dispersing billiards uses periodic-orbit data over
a symbolic family and obtains global conclusions under different hypotheses
\cite{BDKL,DSKL,Osterman}; enriched marked-length results use a still larger
global periodic-data object \cite{FinamoreLeguil}.  The signed endpoint law is
globally more localized but locally contains a continuum of signed collision
coordinates and recovers arbitrary labelled smooth contact jets without
reflection symmetry or supplied curvature.  Neither data set is asserted to
be a formal coarsening of the other.

\subsection{A non-dominated vector boundary experiment}

Consider on a fixed observation space
\[
 f_\theta(x)=a_\theta(x)(w_\theta(x))_+,
 \qquad \theta\in\mathbb R^r,
\]
with regular reference boundary $\Sigma=\{w_0=0\}$, and put
\[
 V=D_\theta w_\theta|_0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal J_\Sigma=
 \int_\Sigma\frac{a_0VV^t}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma.
\]
The singular score component is $V/w_0$.  Since the reference density itself
contains a factor $w_0$, the truncated score covariance grows only like
$\log(1/q)$.  This gives the critical scale
\[
 np_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1.
\]

For outward local directions, however, $f_{\delta_nh}$ assigns positive mass
to a region where $f_0$ is zero.  We therefore do not write a global
finite-$n$ Radon--Nikodym derivative of the original experiment against the
reference law.  Instead set
\[
 L_n=(\log(1/\delta_n))^{1/4},
 \qquad q_n=\delta_nL_n,
\]
and apply a parameter-independent Markov map which retains every observation
with $w_0\ge q_n$ and sends every other successful observation to one collar
symbol.  Uniformly over bounded local parameters, the product probability of
touching that collapsed collar is
\[
 O(np_nq_n^2)=o(1).
\]
Hence the original non-dominated experiment and the collapsed experiment are
at $o(1)$ Le Cam distance.  The latter is genuinely dominated, because the
retained core lies inside every bounded local support and the collapsed atom
has positive reference mass.

On this dominated companion experiment the paper proves a uniform likelihood
expansion on compact local sets,
\[
 \log\frac{d\widetilde P_{n,h}^{\otimes n}}
          {d\widetilde P_{n,0}^{\otimes n}}
 =h^t\Delta_n-\frac12h^t\mathcal J_\Sigma h+o_P(1),
 \qquad
 \Delta_n\Rightarrow N(0,\mathcal J_\Sigma).
\]
The collar, third-moment and fourth-moment estimates are explicit.  Le Cam
comparison then transfers the Gaussian shift, mutual contiguity of bounded
local alternatives, the Gaussian testing profile, and the finite-dimensional
local asymptotic minimax bound to the original moving-support family.  Thus the
non-dominated support is not suppressed by convention; it is separated and
shown quantitatively negligible at the logarithmic critical scale.

A second stability lemma will be used below.  If two linearly vanishing
boundary densities have defining functions differing by $\varepsilon$ in
$C^1$ and amplitudes differing by $\eta$, then uniformly on compact regular
families
\[
 H^2\le C\left\{\varepsilon^2\log(e/\varepsilon)+\eta^2\right\}.
\]
This is the scale at which the exact billiard endpoint family is compared to
its ideal first-order moving-boundary model.

The existence of nonregular endpoint asymptotics and general methods for
parameter-dependent support is classical
\cite{Smith1985,IbragimovHasminskii,HiranoPorter}.  We do not claim otherwise.
The contribution needed here is the regular-hypersurface coefficient, its
vector form, a proof that respects finite-sample non-domination, and its
coupling to the billiard inverse and physical transfer.

\subsection{Fixed laboratory windows and anchored endpoint coordinates}

The physical theorem is stated on an explicit registered local family.  In
one fixed Euclidean laboratory chart, for every local parameter the two
participating patches are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-anchored-graphs}
 (-\psi_{0,\vartheta}(u),u),
 \qquad
 (g+\psi_{1,\vartheta}(v),v),
 \qquad
 \psi_{b,\vartheta}(0)=\psi'_{b,\vartheta}(0)=0.
\end{equation}
Thus the two contact origins and tangent axes are anchored throughout the
statistical family, while the gap and graph shapes are unknown.  The measured
signed coordinates $(u,v)$ are literal common laboratory coordinates.  This
is a theorem hypothesis, not a parameter-dependent recentering rule.  Absolute
pose or an unknown construction of the endpoint frames can instead be added as
nuisance variables on the common record space or supplied by a charged pilot;
they are not treated as free observations.

Fix the reference geometry $(g_0,0)$ and program
\[
 t_{j,\ell}=jg_0+d_\ell,
 \qquad 0<d_\ell<d_*.
\]
These physical times do not change under the local alternative.  The natural
multirate coordinates are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-multirate}
 g=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a,
 \qquad \vartheta=\delta_nh,
\end{equation}
for which
\[
 t_{j_n,\ell}-j_ng=d_\ell-\delta_na.
\]
Hence the gap direction moves the endpoint support by order $\delta_n$ even
though the gap itself changes at the faster scale $\delta_n/j_n$.

For a same-type design $(b,d)$ the support velocity in direction
$z=(a,h)$ is
\[
 U_b(u,v)z=a+D_\vartheta S_b(u)[h]+D_\vartheta S_b(v)[h],
\]
with information matrix
\[
 \mathcal K_{b,d}
 =\int_{\{S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d\}}
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)U_b(u,v)^tU_b(u,v)}
 {|\nabla(S_b\oplus S_b)(u,v)|}\,d\sigma.
\]
If a vector belongs to the kernel for both labels and every sufficiently small
positive offset, the slice $v=0$ first forces its gap component to vanish.
Both action derivatives then vanish, and the differential signed-jet inverse
forces every finite graph-jet tangent component to vanish.  Compactness of the
unit sphere therefore selects finitely many positive offsets with a
positive-definite weighted information matrix on every \emph{fixed} finite
labelled jet chart.  The finite design may depend on the chosen jet order; no
universal finite design for the infinite jet is claimed.

The exact fixed-window billiard boundary density is not simply declared equal
to this ideal model.  Its defining function has the form
\[
 w^{\rm ex}_{n,z}=w_{b,d}-\delta_nU_bz+R_{n,z},
 \qquad \|R_{n,z}\|_{C^2}=r_n=o(\delta_n),
\]
and its smooth amplitude differs by $O(\delta_n)$.  The Hellinger stability
estimate gives one-record error
\[
 C\{r_n^2\log(e/r_n)+\delta_n^2\}.
\]
If $k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1$, tensorization makes the exact and ideal
endpoint experiments asymptotically equivalent.  The ideal family is then
passed through the parameter-independent collar collapse above.  Thus the
exact endpoint-output experiment, although itself potentially non-dominated,
converges to the Gaussian shift with the positive-definite finite-design
information matrix.  The gap is visible at rate $\delta_n/j_n$ and the
anchored graph geometry at rate $\delta_n$.

\subsection{Actual finite bridges, cost, and the richer transcript}

The physical preparations are rare and are not treated as free.  Each design
uses a deterministic preparation cap and stops when its prescribed number of
successful endpoint records is reached or the cap is hit.  The cap is chosen
from a compact-neighborhood lower success-probability bound, and its premature
activation has exponentially small probability.

The historical adaptive transfer theorem operates on the complete stopped
record.  Specialized to the fixed windows and anchored family above, it gives
\[
 \sup_{z\in K}
 \|\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin}-\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}.
\]
Consequently $k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0$ transfers the boundary experiment to actual
long finite bridges.  The full transcript in this comparison retains failures,
designs and stopping times.  The endpoint-output Gaussian experiment is a
common Markov coarsening of that transcript, so it inherits the transfer.

The inference theorem nevertheless does not call waiting counts ancillary.
Their success probability contains the long-bridge factor
$\sinh(j\gamma)^{-1}$ and may carry additional, faster information in
geometric directions changing $\gamma$.  The local asymptotic minimax bound
therefore characterizes the endpoint-output experiment, with the complete
preparation cost charged, rather than all procedures on the richer stopped
transcript.

A charged pilot also gives an exact route to centered windows.  The compared
sample space is stated explicitly: both experiments retain the same pilot
transcript, while conditionally on that transcript the post-pilot
endpoint-output kernel is either data-centered or oracle-centered.  If the
pilot coordinate error is $\varepsilon_n$ and
\[
 k_n\varepsilon_n^2\log(1/\varepsilon_n)\to0,
\]
the post-pilot endpoint experiment is asymptotically equivalent to the oracle
one.  The pilot preparation cost remains explicit.

\subsection{Observation coarsening and organization}

For a fixed table the same boundary structure also produces distinct
finite-versus-boundary comparison scales.  Complete endpoint--residual records
distinguish at the $e^{-j\gamma}$ scale.  After residual time is integrated out
but endpoints are retained, linear vanishing at the endpoint support produces
the $e^{-2j\gamma}j$ logarithmic scale.  If only a success indicator is
retained, the leading tangent shape information disappears.  These statements
concern different coarsenings of one physical record and are separate from the
unknown-geometry local experiment.

Part I proves the nonlinear relative law, half-line transport, and the
standalone signed all-order inverse.  Part II proves scalar boundary
information, the non-dominated vector Gaussian limit, the anchored fixed-window
physical endpoint experiment, its quantitative exact-to-ideal comparison, and
the complete stopped finite-bridge transfer.  Historical acquisition,
calibration, comparison and inverse modules remain in the auxiliary compendium
for provenance.  They are not used as substitutes for the theorem-level
arguments promoted in the main text.
