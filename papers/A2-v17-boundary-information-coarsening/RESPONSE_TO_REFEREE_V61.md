## 2. The positive case for the relative/action mechanism (R60-E1)

The question is whether a long hyperbolic bridge retains nonlinear boundary geometry at fixed positive excess time. The middle of the bridge approaches the two-periodic orbit, and its unconditioned mass becomes exponentially small; neither fact alone determines what survives in its conditional endpoint interaction. The introduction now separates the flight-number limit from a quadratic small-offset truncation before stating any inverse theorem.

The forward result identifies what survives on one flight-independent contact collar. Its distinctive analytical requirement is a bound for the *relative mixed derivative*, not merely convergence of stationary actions. The exact finite cofactor identity precedes the limiting argument. The two endpoint perturbations are localized in trace norm, the compressed finite Green operators are compared with the two half-line operators, and telescoping trace powers control the logarithmic determinant. This supplies a nonlinear amplitude without losing the exponentially small scale. Only then is the physical residual-time integration normalized. These facts, rather than the subsequent cancellation of a scalar normalizer, justify the conditional law.

The revised introduction prints the finite-flight normalized density and points to the existing fixed-order finite-flight inverse, Corollary `cor:v26-finite-flight-inverse`. Its error has the form `C_M(tau^j + epsilon + epsilon_g)` on the specified positive interior domain. The connection is therefore not solely between formal infinite-flight objects. It does not, however, make the real derivative order uniform or provide a sampling budget under arbitrary recording efficiencies.

The inverse has a separate analytical obstacle. A formal coefficient computation cannot establish that the action jet is independent of the choice of actual smooth remainder. The proof first interpolates actual graphs, differentiates finite stationary sums, and retains the terminal orbit term until its decay is established. This gives the finite smooth factorization before the determinant-one signed blocks are calculated. Odd orders and unequal curvatures are included. The analytic inverse then controls the complete lower-degree coupling through the same stationary envelope, not through the diagonal determinants alone.

The realized comparison makes the extra information concrete. The support family in Theorem `thm:v4-jet-fiber` keeps the gap, free area, both curvatures, and every leading endpoint covariance and count amplitude fixed, while its fourth contact derivative is `3 - 24s`. Its normalized onset coefficient has derivative `sqrt(3)/2`. The signed inverse also separates the fixed-positive-offset endpoint laws. This is an actual family of periodic tables, not a formal perturbation or an arbitrary-density example. It demonstrates a nonlinear geometric record beyond the entire leading record of this experiment. It does not establish a comparison of information with a marked length spectrum.

This is the affirmative significance case submitted for reconsideration: a physical rare-event limit produces a nonlinear boundary coordinate with an actual-smooth inverse, and this coordinate is sufficiently geometric to reconstruct unregistered analytic periodic tables and their unknown marked lattices. The rich, selected and marked observation model is stated at the outset; its usefulness is not defended by pretending it contains only finitely many scalar measurements. The claimed depth is concentrated in the forward-relative and smooth-inverse mechanism. Analytic continuation, finite matching and a displacement cochain are not counted as three independent breakthroughs.

We agree with the report that the v60 continuation and smoothing estimates should not be counted as a second unrelated innovation. Their role is to make the complete local inverse meaningful in a real observation topology under explicit priors. That is a useful consequence at its actual strength. Whether the integrated mechanism has the reach required by a highest general journal remains a substantive judgment for an independent referee. We request reconsideration of that mechanism; we do not report this placement judgment as a mathematically closed gap.
