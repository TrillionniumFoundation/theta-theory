\section{Statistical determination of an analytic contact}
\label{sec:v5-jets}
The scalar fourth-jet calculation extends to a triangular inverse at every
finite order. In this section the two facing graphs are identical and even.
This hypothesis is additional to those of the general threshold theorem;
it is satisfied by the analytic obstacles constructed below. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-graph-series}
 \psi(y)=\frac\kappa2y^2+\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{q_{2m}}{(2m)!}y^{2m},
 \qquad a=\frac{\sinh\gamma}{g},\quad
 \gamma=\arcosh(1+g\kappa),\quad \lambda=e^{-\gamma}.
\end{equation}
For smooth graphs this notation denotes their Taylor coefficients, not an
assertion that the series converges. Both limiting parities coincide. Put
\[
 f_k=\frac1{k!}\partial_d^k\mathcal F(0),\qquad
 f_{j,k}=\frac1{k!}\partial_d^kF_j(0).
\]
The functions $F_j$ and $\mathcal F$ are the normalized physical
probabilities of Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-law}.

\begin{theorem}[Triangular contact-jet recovery]\label{thm:v5-jets}
Fix $g,\kappa>0$. For every $m\ge2$, the coefficient $f_{m-1}$ depends
only on $g,\kappa,q_4,\ldots,q_{2m}$, is affine in its last argument,
and has derivative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-diagonal}
 \frac{\partial f_{m-1}}{\partial q_{2m}}
 =-K_m\{\coth(m\gamma)+2mD_m\}<0,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-constants}
 K_m=\frac{4}{2^m(m+1)(m!)^2a^m},\qquad
 D_m=\frac1{e^{2(m-1)\gamma}-1}
             -\frac1{e^{2m\gamma}-1}>0.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every fixed $M$, the map
\[
 (q_4,\ldots,q_{2M})\longmapsto(f_1,\ldots,f_{M-1})
\]
has a triangular smooth inverse, locally Lipschitz on compact finite-jet
sets. It is analytic in finite-jet coordinates. There is no separation
assumption on the higher jets. If two real-analytic even facing graphs
have the same $g,\kappa$ and the same germ of $\mathcal F$, they coincide
as graph germs.

For each fixed $M$, inserting the finite-bridge coefficients
$(f_{j,1},\ldots,f_{j,M-1})$ in the limiting inverse recovers the actual
jet vector with error at most $C_M\tau^j$, uniformly on a sufficiently
small compact parameter family. The constants may depend on $M$ and on
higher boundary norms; no inverse bound uniform in $M$ is asserted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first justify the finite-jet dependence. Assign degree one to each
endpoint coordinate. The coefficient of degree $n$ of a stationary
segment is obtained by differentiating its equation $n$ times at the
zero segment. The highest segment derivative is multiplied by the same
quadratic interior Hessian at every step. Its inverse is the Green
operator in \eqref{eq:v4-half-green}; all other terms involve already
determined lower derivatives and derivatives of the local length.
The weighted bounds in Lemma~\ref{lem:v4-halfline} justify this
induction also on the half-line. The action and the logarithmic edge and
determinant sums can be differentiated term by term, using their
summable entrywise bounds. Thus their coefficients through any given
degree depend on only finitely many graph derivatives. In particular,
the normalized coefficient of $d^{m-1}$ uses the action through degree
$2m$ and the amplitude through degree $2m-2$. This last assertion follows
also directly from the smooth Morse construction: after the change
$(u,v)=\sqrt d\,(U,V)$, an action term of degree $n$ carries degree
$d^{n/2-1}$ and an amplitude term degree $d^{n/2}$. Odd terms vanish
under simultaneous sign reversal. Taylor remainders in the common
Morse coordinates justify this bookkeeping with differentiated bounds.

Keep the jets below $2m$ fixed and vary $q_{2m}$. In the local length,
the first varying homogeneous term is
\[
 \frac{u^{2m}+v^{2m}}{(2m)!}.
\]
It has no mixed derivative. The linear half-line segment is
$x_i=\lambda^i u$. By the envelope identity for a stationary action,
the varying homogeneous term of its stationary value is obtained by
substituting this linear segment. This can first be done on finite
segments; the weighted derivative estimates pass its absolutely
convergent sum to the half-line. If $s_{2m}=S^{(2m)}(0)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-action-diagonal}
 \partial_{q_{2m}}s_{2m}=1+2\sum_{i\ge1}\lambda^{2mi}
                            =\coth(m\gamma).
\end{equation}
The boundary site is counted once, each interior site twice. Lower
nonlinear corrections to $x_i$ have degree at least three, and cannot
alter the displayed degree-$2m$ term.

Write $\beta_{2m-2}=[u^{2m-2}]B(u)$, with ordinary rather than factorial
coefficients. At degree $2m-2$ the logarithmic edge product has zero
variation. The varying interior Hessian is diagonal at this degree, with
entries
\[
 \frac{2\lambda^{(2m-2)i}u^{2m-2}}{(2m-2)!},\qquad i\ge1.
\]
The change of the stationary segment itself has degree at least $2m-1$.
Since the Hessian perturbation for even graphs starts in degree two,
that change cannot enter degree $2m-2$. For the same reason, only the
linear term of the logarithmic determinant contributes at this degree;
every product with an unvaried Hessian perturbation has at least two
additional degrees. Using $G_{ii}=(1-\lambda^{2i})/(2a)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-amplitude-diagonal}
 \partial_{q_{2m}}\beta_{2m-2}
 =-\frac1{a(2m-2)!}
    \sum_{i\ge1}(1-\lambda^{2i})\lambda^{(2m-2)i}
 =-\frac{D_m}{a(2m-2)!}.
\end{equation}
Passing from $\log B$ to $B$ does not change this first varying
coefficient. All products with a lower positive-degree term again lie
above the degree under consideration.

It remains to compute how these two variations enter the probability.
Set $k=m-1$. Under the quadratic residual-time density proportional to
$(d-a(u^2+v^2)/2)_+$, polar integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-moments}
 \E(u^{2k})=
 \frac{2\binom{2k}{k}}{2^k(k+1)(k+2)a^k}d^k,
 \qquad
 \int(d-a(u^2+v^2)/2)_+\dd u\dd v=\frac{\pi d^2}{a}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the angular average of $\cos^{2k}\theta$ is
$\binom{2k}{k}/4^k$, while the ratio of the radial integrals is
$2(2d/a)^k/((k+1)(k+2))$.
First variation with respect to the action gives the negative integral
of $(u^{2m}+v^{2m})/(2m)!$ over the quadratic sublevel. There is no
boundary contribution because the residual-time weight vanishes there.
Dividing by $\pi d^2/a$, its coefficient of $d^{m-1}$ is $-K_m$.
The amplitude variation contributes
\[
 \frac{4\binom{2m-2}{m-1}}{2^{m-1}m(m+1)a^{m-1}}
                  \partial_{q_{2m}}\beta_{2m-2}=-2mK_mD_m.
\]
Lower-order terms in the action and amplitude multiply either variation
only in higher degrees, so the calculation at the quadratic part is
valid for arbitrary fixed lower jets. Equations
\eqref{eq:v5-action-diagonal}--\eqref{eq:v5-amplitude-diagonal} prove
\eqref{eq:v5-diagonal}. They also show its independence of all higher
jets and of the value of $q_{2m}$ itself. The finite-jet dependence then
proves affinity in that argument.

Inductively write
$f_{m-1}=d_m q_{2m}+P_m(g,\kappa,q_4,\ldots,q_{2m-2})$, with $d_m$
given by \eqref{eq:v5-diagonal}, and solve this identity for $q_{2m}$.
Each $d_m$ is nonzero; for a fixed finite set of orders it is bounded
away from zero on compact positive gap--curvature boxes. The recursion
and its derivatives give the asserted local Lipschitz bounds. The
finite-jet operations are analytic: they consist of the local analytic
length expansion, inversion of its positive quadratic operator, and
absolutely convergent geometric sums. Equality of every recovered jet
implies equality of analytic graph germs by convergence of their
Taylor series.

Finally Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-law} gives
$|f_{j,k}-f_k|\le C_M\tau^j$ for $k\le M-1$. Apply the finite-dimensional
local Lipschitz inverse just constructed. For all sufficiently large
$j$ the argument is in its neighborhood. The recursion defines an
inverse for arbitrary finite coefficient vectors at fixed $g,\kappa$;
the finitely many remaining $j$ are covered by enlarging the constant
on the compact parameter set. This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Analytic obstacle determination from a selected onset]
\label{cor:v5-analytic-rigidity}
Within the identical-even contact class, the gap, the leading selected
probabilities, and the germ of their normalized limiting law determine
the analytic contact graph. If the participating obstacles have connected
real-analytic closed boundaries, this determines each such boundary in
its contact frame. It does not determine the lattice or other obstacles
which do not participate in this channel.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $L_j=\lim_{d\downarrow0}d^{-2}\Pr(E_{j,e}(d))$.
The ratio $L_1/(2L_2)=\cosh\gamma$ determines
$\kappa=(\cosh\gamma-1)/g$. Also
$F_j(d)=\Pr(E_{j,e}(d))/(d^2L_j)$, so the normalized datum does not
require a supplied area. The theorem determines the contact germ.
For the global assertion, parametrize two candidate boundaries by
oriented arclength from the common contact. Their signed curvature
functions are real analytic on the real line, by continuation around
each closed regular curve. They agree on an interval, hence everywhere
by the real-analytic identity theorem. Integrating the planar Frenet
equations with their common initial frame makes the two boundary images
identical. This is exact analytic continuation, not a stability estimate
for continuation from noisy local observations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-order geometric realization}
\begin{theorem}[Independent statistical jet coordinates at fixed leading data]
\label{thm:v5-realization}
For every fixed $M\ge2$ there is an $(M-1)$-parameter real-analytic
family near the unit disk on $3\Z\times4\Z$ which preserves
$g=1$, $A=12-\pi$, both horizontal contact curvatures, and all collision-order leading
count and endpoint-metric coefficients of the selected horizontal ground
channel, with its reverse orientation, exactly. Its coordinates can be
taken to be $(q_4,\ldots,q_{2M})$, or equivalently
$(f_1,\ldots,f_{M-1})$. Both coordinate changes have bounded inverse
derivatives on a sufficiently small compact parameter neighborhood.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the support functions
\[
 h_{s,z}(\theta)=1+\sum_{m=2}^M s_m\sin^{2m}\theta
                         +z\sin^{2M+2}\theta.
\]
At the disk the derivative of the obstacle area with respect to $z$ is
$\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^{2M+2}\theta\dd\theta>0$. The analytic implicit-function
theorem supplies $z=z(s)$ keeping that area equal to $\pi$. All the
perturbations vanish to order at least four at $0,\pi$. Thus the two
horizontal support points and their curvature radii remain fixed, and
so do the gap and free area. Positive curvature, clearance, and the
strict gap to nonhorizontal translates persist on a small neighborhood,
as in Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-jet-fiber}.

The compensator vanishes to order $2M+2$ and cannot affect graph jets
through order $2M$. More generally $q_{2m}$ depends only on
$s_2,\ldots,s_m$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-support-diagonal}
                 \partial_{s_m}q_{2m}=-(2m)!.
\end{equation}
To see the sign and coefficient without fixing the normal parameter,
write the right boundary as $x=x(y)$. At its supporting normal
$\theta(y)$, stationarity of
$(h(\theta)-y\sin\theta)/\cos\theta$ gives, at fixed $y$,
$\partial_{s_m}x=\sin^{2m}\theta(y)/\cos\theta(y)$.
The contact radius is one, so $\theta(y)=y+O(y^3)$. Since
$\psi(y)=1-x(y)$, the first varying coefficient is $-y^{2m}$,
proving \eqref{eq:v5-support-diagonal}. Lower graph coefficients cannot
vary because the perturbation starts in that order. The compensator
term has still higher order even after its implicit differentiation.
The triangular Jacobian is invertible. Combine it with
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-jets}. The constancy of this channel's full leading hierarchy follows
from Proposition~\ref{prop:v4-saturation} and the unchanged contact
Hessians, including both parities.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The one-flight comparison}
The preceding inverse information is nonlinear, but is not exclusive to
long records. This distinction can be made at every order, not just at
the fourth jet.
\begin{proposition}[One-flight triangular diagonal]\label{prop:v5-one-flight}
Let $\nu_1=gc/(c^2-1)$, where $c=1+g\kappa$. For $m\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v5-one-flight}
 \partial_{q_{2m}}f_{1,m-1}
       =-\frac{4\nu_1^m}{2^m(m+1)(m!)^2}<0.
\end{equation}
Thus the normalized one-flight nonlinear germ also determines the
analytic even contact graph at known $g,\kappa$.
For the family of Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-jet-fiber},
\[
 \left.\partial_s f_{1,1}^{(s)}\right|_0=\frac89,
 \qquad \left.\partial_s f_1^{(s)}\right|_0=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
There are no interior variables or interior determinant at one flight.
The inverse quadratic endpoint Hessian has both diagonal entries
$\nu_1$. The first varying action term is
$(u^{2m}+v^{2m})/(2m)!$ and its mixed derivative is zero. Hence the
amplitude does not vary in degree $2m-2$. Transform the quadratic
ellipse to a disk. The even moment of either endpoint linear functional
is the disk moment with its squared length $\nu_1$ in place of $1/a$.
The unweighted sublevel integral used in the proof of
\eqref{eq:v5-diagonal} gives exactly \eqref{eq:v5-one-flight}.
The same finite-jet recursion proves germ determination. For $m=2$,
$g=1,c=2$, this derivative is $-1/27$; multiplication by
$q_s'=-24$ gives $8/9$. The other identity is
\eqref{eq:v4-detected-jet}.
\end{proof}

The uniform long-bridge theorem establishes a limiting physical law and
relative two-boundary factorization, with uniform convergence of every
fixed statistical jet. It is not an assertion that increasing flight
number creates contact parameters absent from one-flight nonlinear
data. The distinction from the entire leading hierarchy is genuine at
arbitrarily high finite jet order, as the realized families show.
