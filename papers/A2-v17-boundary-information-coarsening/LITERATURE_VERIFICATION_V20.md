# Literature verification and information-set audit — A2 v20

## Purpose

The v19 referee did not identify a published theorem that directly duplicates the determinant-one signed-action block plus the moving-hypersurface information matrix and finite-bridge transfer. It nevertheless asked for a more exact comparison with modern billiard rigidity and with the nonregular statistics literature. This note records that comparison. It is not an exhaustive priority certificate.

Checked/rechecked on September 11, 2026 against publisher/author/arXiv records where available.

## 1. Marked-length rigidity for open dispersing billiards

### Bálint--De Simoi--Kaloshin--Leguil

The marked-length/homoclinic program extracts geometric information from labelled periodic-orbit lengths and their asymptotics. The data are global periodic data on the trapped symbolic dynamics, not physical endpoint-support germs in a single selected channel.

### De Simoi--Kaloshin--Leguil, Inventiones Mathematicae 233 (2023), 829--901

*Marked Length Spectral determination of analytic chaotic billiards with axial symmetries* proves global marked-length determination in the analytic setting under the paper's symmetry/genericity hypotheses. The exact comparison relevant to A2 is:

- DSKL data: a marked family of periodic-orbit lengths across the symbolic dynamics;
- A2 data: physical onset plus signed endpoint-support laws for one labelled closest-pair alternating channel;
- DSKL conclusion: global analytic geometric recovery in its hypothesis class;
- A2 conclusion: arbitrary smooth local labelled contact jets for the participating contacts, with analytic continuation only after analyticity is imposed.

A2 is therefore **globally narrower but locally richer in collision-coordinate information**. Neither information set is shown in A2 to be a measurable coarsening of the other.

## 2. Osterman, Journal of Modern Dynamics 19 (2023), 847--878

*On length spectrum rigidity of dispersing billiard systems* studies three convex analytic scatterers under the non-eclipse condition. It relates equality of marked length spectra to analytic conjugacy near a homoclinic orbit and proves a recovery result in which two scatterers plus the marked length spectrum determine the third.

Again the mechanism is periodic-length/homoclinic rather than signed endpoint support. This is an appropriate benchmark for the strength of a billiard inverse theorem, but it is not an endpoint-law predecessor.

## 3. Finamore--Leguil, arXiv:2510.18983 (2025)

*A CAT(0)-approach to the marked length spectral rigidity of Sinai billiards* introduces an enriched marked length spectrum for finite-horizon Sinai billiards and proves a global isometry conclusion when the enriched data agree. The enrichment is constructed through smooth geodesic approximations and CAT(0) limits; it is not the physical endpoint record used here.

This comparison is especially useful because it prevents an imprecise claim that the A2 local theorem is globally stronger than modern marked-length rigidity. A2's distinctive point is different: a selected physical endpoint law has enough signed local support information to remove reflection symmetry and supplied-curvature assumptions from the contact-jet inverse.

## 4. Classical nonregular endpoint asymptotics

### Akahira; Ibragimov--Has'minskii

These are retained as classical background for nonregular rates, limit experiments and local asymptotic minimax theory.

### Richard L. Smith, Biometrika 72 (1985), 67--90

Smith studies parameter-dependent endpoint models with densities behaving like a power at the endpoint and explicitly includes the borderline linearly vanishing case with asymptotically normal behavior at a nonstandard rate.

**V20 nonclaim:** logarithmically modified normal behavior for a one-dimensional linearly vanishing endpoint is not claimed to originate in A2.

## 5. Parameter-dependent support and limit experiments

### Hirano--Porter, Econometrica 71 (2003), 1307--1338

This work develops asymptotic efficiency for parametric structural models with parameter-dependent support using limits of experiments and local asymptotic minimax reasoning.

**V20 nonclaim:** parameter-dependent support, Le Cam limits and local asymptotic minimax methodology are not claimed as new.

### Shimizu--Otsu, arXiv:2403.16413

The current author pages describe *Optimal testing in a class of nonregular models* as revise-and-resubmit/revision requested at *Econometric Theory*. The arXiv record has been revised since the original 2024 posting. The paper develops optimal tests for a class of nonregular parameter-dependent-support models through a limit experiment.

**V20 nonclaim:** generic optimal testing for nonregular parameter-dependent-support models is not a new subject introduced by A2.

## 6. What the vector theorem adds

The abstract v20 theorem isolates a regular-hypersurface linearly vanishing model

`f_theta = a_theta (w_theta)_+`

and proves the intrinsic matrix coefficient

`J_Sigma = integral_Sigma a_0 V V^T / |grad w_0| d sigma`

with a self-contained compact-uniform LAN proof, testing profile and local minimax statement. The novelty claim is not that vector LAN exists in general. It is that this particular boundary coefficient is the correct intrinsic object for the moving hypersurface produced by the billiard endpoint law and that it couples to the deterministic signed inverse.

## 7. The v20 physical experiment is materially different from v19

The v19 objection was not literature priority but experimental formulation. V20 uses parameter-independent physical times

`t_{j,l}=j g_0+d_l`

and the multirate local parameter

`g=g_0+delta a/j`, `vartheta=delta h`.

The gap therefore contributes a constant normal support velocity and belongs to the same finite-design information matrix as the signed-action directions. A stopped physical policy with deterministic caps is compared to the boundary experiment in uniform total variation, failures and stopping times included.

This fixed-window multirate construction is the principal new statistical coupling in v20. The literature audit performed for v18/v19 did not identify an existing billiard theorem giving this combination of:

1. long-bridge relative boundary law;
2. signed endpoint support rigidity for arbitrary labelled contact jets;
3. positive-definite finite endpoint design including the gap at its physical `1/j` faster scale;
4. experiment-level stopped finite-bridge transfer.

This statement is deliberately narrower than an exhaustive priority assertion.

## 8. Information-set hierarchy used in the introduction

The v20 introduction adopts the following comparison, which is stronger and more precise than simply saying that the data are "different":

- **Marked/enriched length data:** globally distributed periodic information; strongest conclusions are global but typically analytic and/or use additional hypotheses/enrichments.
- **Signed endpoint data:** one selected physical channel; globally local, but contains continuous signed collision-coordinate support information and yields arbitrary smooth local contact jets.
- **Scalar onset/profile data:** a coarsening of the signed endpoint law and therefore loses odd local information.
- **Endpoint-only statistical records:** after residual-time integration, retain a logarithmic moving-support information channel.
- **Success indicators:** erase the leading tangent endpoint-shape information in the fixed-table tangent comparison.

This hierarchy is an information statement internal to A2. It is not a theorem that marked-length and endpoint data are mutually reducible.
