**Boundary laws and rigidity of periodic dispersing billiards**  
Qian Qi — September 16, 2026

Dear Referee,

We submit the revised principal article together with its complete technical manuscript and companion. The preceding author-requested report found no new mandatory core repair in its scoped examination. It asked instead for a focused account of why the relative boundary/action mechanism itself merits consideration at the highest general-journal level. This revision responds to that request without deleting results, altering their hypotheses, or presenting standard continuation estimates as an unrelated second breakthrough.

The question organizing the paper is whether an arbitrarily long alternating billiard bridge retains nonlinear boundary geometry. At fixed positive excess time, the unconditioned event becomes exponentially rare but the conditional law is not reduced to the contact Hessians. The forward theorem establishes a nonlinear limit on a flight-independent collar by exact cofactor normalization and a two-ended trace-class comparison. Its relative mixed-derivative estimate is the step that an absolute stationary-action estimate does not provide.

The inverse then identifies the resulting actions as geometric boundary coordinates. A finite stationary-envelope argument establishes factorization on actual smooth jets, before the signed determinant-one recursion is used. In the analytic category the same envelope controls the complete action map, including its lower-degree couplings. Under a local analytic prior, the complete inverse has a conditional real-observation consequence on a smaller disc. These are parts of one mechanism, not a catalogue of independent innovations.

The mechanism has a realized information separation and a global geometric output. An actual analytic periodic family keeps all leading metrics and count amplitudes fixed but has distinct nonlinear laws. On noncircular analytic tables, selected marked channels determine finitely many complete realizations, including the unknown marked lattice; proper asymmetry gives uniqueness. Local contact images are supplied neither in a common pose nor with an observed lattice metric. Their subsequent analytic continuation, finite incidence matching and cycle reconstruction are stated as consequences of the local inverse, with their proofs retained.

The observation model remains explicit: the selected marked laws are function-valued. The finite scalar-coordinate result is local to immersed finite-dimensional models. The local conditional stability estimate is not a uniform acquisition theorem, and the physical acquisition application continues to import its full additional sensor, calibration and charged-preparation hypotheses. These distinctions describe the theorems being submitted, rather than a reduction of the program's conclusions.

The revised introduction concentrates the mathematical argument and separates it from review history. A point-by-point response and a source-specific historical audit accompany the manuscripts outside the article. We ask that the paper be assessed on this integrated relative/action mechanism, its realized nonlinear information and its geometric consequences. We recognize that the judgment of exceptional significance remains independent of successful typesetting, source preservation, or the absence of a newly identified error.
