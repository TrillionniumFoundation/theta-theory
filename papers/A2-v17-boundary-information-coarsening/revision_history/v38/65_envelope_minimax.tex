\section{The sharp cost of unknown smooth remainders}
\label{sec:v9-envelope-minimax}
We now complement the preceding uniform upper bound by a matching
lower bound. The nuisance splice and the resulting comparison were
supplied in the referee memorandum~\cite{A2ReviewV8}. We give the
complete argument, including its additional interior assumption.
This experiment retains only three binary reports and the physical
leading-amplitude map; its nuisance functions are not claimed to be
exact finite-offset probabilities of billiard tables.

Use the sum convention
$\|H\|_{C^m}=\sum_{r=0}^m\|H^{(r)}\|_\infty$ in
\eqref{eq:v8-envelope}. Fix all its constants, a sufficiently small
compact physical neighborhood $K$ of
$\theta_c=(g_c,e)=(1/2,0)$, and the interval
$[L_0,U_0]$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-nuisance}. The set $K$ is to
contain the two-sided physical splitting paths and the circular gap
variations used below. In addition suppose that some $\rho>0$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-interior-slack}
       c_*+4\rho<C_j(\theta_c)<C_*-4\rho,
                                      \qquad j=1,2,3.
\end{equation}
This condition gives strict positivity and a norm budget around the
constant reference nuisances. It can be arranged when the envelope
is chosen, but is not a consequence of its non-strict bounds alone.

An admissible policy uses parameter-independent randomization and,
after each history, either stops or chooses $j\in\{1,2,3\}$ and
a time $t\ge0$. Require $t-jg(\theta)\le d_*$ for every
$\theta\in K$ on every history, including histories at which a
previous calibration failed. Each fresh preparation returns the bit
with probability \eqref{eq:v8-envelope}. The observation is the
stopped transcript of these queries and bits, together with the
recorded common randomization. For $0<\eta<1/4$, let
$\mathcal N_m^{\rm env}(\varepsilon,\delta,\eta)$ be the infimum
of the worst-case expected number of preparations among such policies
and Borel estimators satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-joint-loss}
 \Pr_{\theta,H}\{
 \operatorname{dist}_{\rm match}(\widehat\kappa,\kappa(\theta))
     \le\varepsilon,\quad |\widehat g-g|\le\delta\}\ge1-\eta
\end{equation}
uniformly over the envelope. The two outputs are target estimates;
they are not required to be coordinates of one exactly fitted table.

\begin{theorem}[Joint minimax preparation cost over the smooth envelope]
\label{thm:v9-envelope-minimax}
Under these fixed choices and \eqref{eq:v9-interior-slack}, there
are $c,C,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-envelope-rate}
 \begin{split}
 c(\varepsilon^{-(6+6/m)}+\delta^{-2})\log(1/\eta)
 &\le \mathcal N_m^{\rm env}(\varepsilon,\delta,\eta)\\
 &\le C(\varepsilon^{-(6+6/m)}+\delta^{-2})\log(1/\eta)
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
for $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$, $0<\delta<\delta_0$ and
$0<\eta<1/4$. The upper bound has a deterministic, all-history
preparation budget. The lower bound permits adaptive queries and
random stopping. For curvature loss alone the order is
$\varepsilon^{-(6+6/m)}\log(1/\eta)$, even when the common gap in
the lower-bound construction is supplied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first construct the curvature alternatives within one fixed
envelope, then charge the stopped experiment, and finally treat the
timing loss and the upper bound.

\emph{Physical leading parameters.}
In the support family of Section~\ref{sec:g-inverse}, set
$R=1/4$, $\alpha=\zeta=0$ and $\beta=\pm s$.
The two radius multisets are
\[
 (R+36s,R-18s,R-18s),\qquad (R-36s,R+18s,R+18s).
\]
Their gap is $g_c=1/2$ and their elementary coefficient triples are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-splitting-coefficients}
                  e^\pm=(0,-972s^2,\ \pm11664s^3).
\end{equation}
For small $s>0$, sorting the positive reciprocals gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-splitting-distance}
 \operatorname{dist}_{\rm match}(\kappa^+,\kappa^-)
                   =\frac{36s}{R^2-324s^2}\asymp s.
\end{equation}
For example the middle sorted coordinates differ by the displayed
quantity; the two outer differences are smaller when $s$ is small.
The leading amplitudes are analytic in a full coefficient
neighborhood by the leading inverse proved above. The two points
in \eqref{eq:v9-splitting-coefficients} differ by $O(s^3)$ there,
so, with constants independent of $s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-cubic-leading}
              |C_j^+(s)-C_j^-(s)|\le C s^3,
                                      \qquad j=1,2,3.
\end{equation}
This uses physical leading parameters, but not a free variation of
area or a separated-root inverse.

\emph{Splicing the nuisance functions.}
Set $\psi(u)=(1-u)_+^{m+1}$ on $[0,\infty)$. It is $C^m$,
equals one at zero, and it and its first $m$ derivatives vanish at
one. Write
$\overline C_j=(C_j^++C_j^-)/2$ and
$D_j=(C_j^+-C_j^-)/2$. Choose a fixed sufficiently large $L$ and
put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-nuisance-splice}
 h=L s^{3/m},\qquad
 H_j^\pm(d)=\overline C_j\pm D_j\psi(d/h).
\end{equation}
These functions have exactly the prescribed leading values at zero.
For $0\le r\le m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-splice-norms}
 \|(H_j^\pm-\overline C_j)^{(r)}\|_\infty
            \le C_mL^{-r}s^{3(1-r/m)}.
\end{equation}
Choose $L$ first so that the order-$m$ bound is smaller than a
specified part of $\rho$. Then make $s$ small so that the sum of
all lower-order bounds, and
$|\overline C_j-C_j(\theta_c)|$, use less than the remaining
margin. Condition~\eqref{eq:v9-interior-slack} proves all the
positivity and sum-norm bounds in the same fixed envelope. Also
$h<d_*$ for sufficiently small $s$. Only the alternatives depend on
accuracy; the parameter set and the nuisance norm ball do not.

\emph{Information at every allowed query.}
A common query has the same offset $d=t-jg_c$ under the two
alternatives. At $d\le0$ both probabilities are zero, and at
$d\ge h$ they agree exactly. For $0<d<h$,
\[
 |p_j^+-p_j^-|\le C d^2s^3,\qquad
 p_j^-\ge c_*d^2,\qquad 1-p_j^-\ge1-C_*d_*^2>0.
\]
The inequality $\log x\le x-1$ gives the Bernoulli entropy bound
$D_{\rm KL}(\operatorname{Ber}(p)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(q))
\le(p-q)^2/[q(1-q)]$ for $0<q<1$. Consequently every query has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-splice-entropy}
 D_{\rm KL}(\operatorname{Ber}(p_j^+)
                    \Vert\operatorname{Ber}(p_j^-))
       \le C d^2s^6\one_{\{0<d<h\}}
       \le C s^{6+6/m}.
\end{equation}
Queries are not restricted to shrinking offsets. It is the nuisance
pair itself that makes larger offsets uninformative.

\emph{Stopping and confidence.}
Condition on the common randomization and on the same realized finite
history. The policy's next query or stop decision is then the same
under the two hypotheses. Pad the transcript by a fixed stop symbol
after stopping. The entropy chain rule for its first $M$ positions
and \eqref{eq:v9-splice-entropy} give
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(\mathsf P_+^{[M]}\Vert\mathsf P_-^{[M]})
             \le Cs^{6+6/m}\E_+(T\wedge M).
\]
Entropy on the increasing finite-transcript sigma-fields converges
to the entropy on the full transcript: one may use its variational
formula and conditional approximations to each bounded test
function. Monotone convergence on the right therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-stopped-entropy}
 D_{\rm KL}(\mathsf P_+\Vert\mathsf P_-)
                              \le Cs^{6+6/m}\E_+T.
\end{equation}
An infinite worst-case expected cost already satisfies the lower
bound. No deterministic stopping assumption has been imposed.

Choose $s$ a fixed sufficiently large multiple of $\varepsilon$
so that \eqref{eq:v9-splitting-distance} exceeds $2\varepsilon$.
Classifying the curvature estimate by its nearer alternative gives a
test with both errors at most $\eta$. Binary data processing implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v9-confidence-entropy}
 D_{\rm KL}(\mathsf P_+\Vert\mathsf P_-)
 \ge(1-2\eta)\log\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}
 \ge\frac14\log(1/\eta),\qquad 0<\eta<1/4.
\end{equation}
The first inequality follows by minimizing the binary entropy over
the two error constraints. For the second, $1-2\eta\ge1/2$ and
$(1-\eta)/\eta\ge\eta^{-1/2}$ on this interval.
Together with \eqref{eq:v9-stopped-entropy}, this proves the
curvature lower bound, also for curvature-only loss with supplied gap.

\emph{Timing.}
Use the circular leading parameters $(g_c,0)$ and
$(g_c+\Delta,0)$, and constant nuisance functions
$H_{j,0}=C_j(g_c,0)$ and
$H_{j,\Delta}=C_j(g_c+\Delta,0)$.
They are in the same envelope by strict slack and continuity.
At a common query, put $d=t-jg_c$ and $z=j\Delta$. Then
\[
 p_0=d_+^2C_j(g_c,0),\qquad
 p_\Delta=(d-z)_+^2C_j(g_c+\Delta,0).
\]
Both vanish for $d\le0$. For $d>0$, splitting the cases $d\le z$
and $d>z$, expanding the squares and using the bounded derivative of
$C_j(g,0)$ gives $|p_\Delta-p_0|\le C d\Delta$.
Since $p_0\ge cd^2$ and $1-p_0$ is bounded away from zero, the
entropy in the support-compatible direction satisfies
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(\operatorname{Ber}(p_\Delta)
                  \Vert\operatorname{Ber}(p_0))\le C\Delta^2.
\]
The argument includes $p_\Delta=0$ and does not reverse this
entropy direction. Set $\Delta=3\delta$ and repeat the stopped
chain rule and the classification argument using the gap estimate.
It gives the lower cost $c\delta^{-2}\log(1/\eta)$.
The curvature and timing pairs need not be the same pair. Their two
worst-case bounds combine by $\max(a,b)\ge(a+b)/2$, proving the
lower half of \eqref{eq:v9-envelope-rate}.

\emph{The upper bound.}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-nuisance}, choose
\[
 h=c_m\min\{\varepsilon^{3/m},\delta^{1/(m+1)}\}
\]
with fixed $c_m>0$ sufficiently small. Both target errors then meet
\eqref{eq:v9-joint-loss}. The all-history preparation bound is at
most a constant times
\[
 \max\{\varepsilon^{-(6+6/m)},\delta^{-2}\}\log(1/\eta),
\]
which is bounded by the required sum. The known fixed gap interval
and all-history admissibility were part of that theorem. Its pilot
gap estimate is retained separately from the fitted coefficients,
as required for the finer timing loss. Omitting the timing loss and
using $h=c_m\varepsilon^{3/m}$ proves the curvature-only upper
bound and completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Information in a finite positive-window design]
\label{cor:v9-fixed-window-envelope}
For any prescribed finite set of query windows whose offsets at
$g_c$ are all strictly positive, there are two different curvature
targets in the fixed smooth envelope which give exactly the same law
for every stopped experiment using only those windows, including
adaptive choices and arbitrary repetitions among them.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $d_{\min}>0$ be the smallest such offset. Choose $s>0$ in
\eqref{eq:v9-nuisance-splice} so small that $h<d_{\min}$.
The two nuisance functions, and hence their probabilities, agree at
every permitted query. Induction on transcript length proves equality
on every finite history, and thus on the stopped observation. The
curvature targets differ by \eqref{eq:v9-splitting-distance}.
\end{proof}

This corollary identifies the extra information used in the exact
four-window inverse; it does not contradict that inverse. Under the
exact analytic physical family, Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-joint-minimax}
gives order $(\varepsilon^{-6}+\delta^{-2})\log(1/\eta)$.
For unknown $C^m$ remainders with strict slack,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v9-envelope-minimax} gives order
$(\varepsilon^{-(6+6/m)}+\delta^{-2})\log(1/\eta)$.
At the finer timing accuracy $\delta=\varepsilon^{3+3/m}$ these
orders coincide. The envelope alternatives establish an information
comparison, not a new geometrically realized lower bound for exact
billiard probabilities or a restriction on the general forward law.
