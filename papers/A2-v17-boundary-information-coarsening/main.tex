\begin{abstract}
We study long alternating collision bridges in smooth periodic dispersing
billiards and the geometry encoded by their physical boundary records.  On an
offset collar independent of the flight number, the normalized mixed endpoint
derivative has a uniform nonlinear relative limit with every fixed mixed
geometric and offset derivative.  Signed endpoint support recovers the two
unsymmetrized half-line actions.  Their quadratic jets recover the gap and
both contact curvatures, and at every order \(n\ge3\) the next labelled graph
jets enter the next action jets through an explicit \(2\times2\) block of
determinant one.  This yields an all-order two-sided contact inverse without
reflection symmetry, equal contacts or supplied curvatures.

For connected real-analytic obstacle boundaries we solve the global periodic
inverse problem without common registration of the measured channels and
without a prescribed Euclidean metric on the marked deck group.  Complete
analytic obstacle copies are recovered in their channel frames.  A
signature-rigid cycle has a data-determined Euclidean translation holonomy;
two cycles with linearly independent marked deck displacements determine the
entire Euclidean realization of the period lattice, including its Gram form,
shape and absolute scale.  A rooted signature-rigid spanning tree then places
all remaining obstacle orbits.  Thus the intrinsic signed channel laws
determine the complete marked periodic table up to one simultaneous global
element of \(\mathrm{SE}(2)\).  On compact analytic classes with explicit
channel, signature and holonomy margins we also obtain a uniform finite-data
inverse modulus.

The physical information depends on the retained sigma-field.  Conditional
successful endpoint--residual-time records have positive density at a moving
ceiling and, at shape scale \(k_n^{-1}\), are asymptotically equivalent in Le
Cam distance to a generally non-dominated Poisson boundary-shift experiment.
We give parameter-independent Markov kernels in both directions, controlling
the boundary-layer count and locations, the reconstructed bulk and the
moving-support remainder.  After residual time is removed, waiting counts
detect the normal quotient to the level set of the hyperbolic exponent at
scale \((j_n\sqrt{k_n})^{-1}\), while endpoints detect the tangent directions
at the slower moving-boundary scale.  The resulting Gaussian product
experiment is formulated invariantly on the normal quotient and tangent
space.  Removing waiting counts gives the endpoint-only Gaussian coarsening.

Finally, on every uniformly nondegenerate compact analytic class we construct,
rather than assume, uniform finite-order estimators from the actual
finite-bridge transcript.  Compactness produces a common finite positive
design and finitely many statistical separators at each fixed jet order; a
charged onset pilot makes their centered windows implementable without
supplying the unknown gaps; deterministic caps and the stopped
physical-to-boundary comparison control the complete adaptive transcript.
Choosing the growing jet order, design size, success allocation and flight
number diagonally gives a single sequence of implementable finite-bridge
policies whose table estimators are uniformly consistent in every fixed
labelled \(C^q\) loss.  No uniform-in-order condition number or sharp analytic
minimax rate is assumed.
\end{abstract}
