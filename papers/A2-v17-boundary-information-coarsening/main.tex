\begin{abstract}
We study the nonlinear law of long alternating collision bridges in smooth
periodic dispersing billiards, the local geometry encoded by their physical
endpoint records, and the information loss caused by coarsening those
records.  On a collar whose size is independent of the number of flights,
the normalized mixed endpoint derivative admits a uniform relative limit,
including every fixed mixed geometric and offset derivative.  The limiting
flux factors into two half-line determinant amplitudes, identified with the
normalized scalar linearizing densities of the physical stable returns.

Retaining the signed endpoint positions gives a stronger inverse than the
scalar onset law.  The support threshold of the two same-type endpoint laws
recovers the two unsymmetrized half-line actions.  Their quadratic terms,
together with the observed onset, recover the gap and both contact
curvatures.  At every order $n\ge3$ the new pair of labelled graph jets
enters the action jets through an explicit $2\times2$ block of determinant
one.  Hence arbitrary smooth labelled contact jets, including all odd jets,
are recursively determined without reflection symmetry, without equality
of the two contacts, and without supplied contact curvatures.  Real-analytic
endpoint-law germs determine the two participating analytic contact germs.

After residual time is integrated out, the endpoint density vanishes
linearly on a moving support boundary.  For a general regular hypersurface
model we prove
\[
 H^2(f_t,f_0)=\frac{\mathcal I_\Sigma}{4}t^2\log(1/|t|)+O(t^2)
\]
and extend this to vector parameters with the intrinsic matrix
\[
 \mathcal J_\Sigma=
 \int_\Sigma\frac{a_0VV^t}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma.
\]
At the logarithmic boundary rate the thinned experiment, with failures
retained, is locally asymptotically normal with information
$\mathcal J_\Sigma$.  In a finite-dimensional family of unknown billiard
geometries, signed-endpoint rigidity implies that finitely many positive
endpoint windows have a nonsingular summed information matrix, and the
relative law transfers this experiment to actual long finite bridges.
For the fixed-table finite-versus-boundary comparison, determinant-one
whitening gives $\mathcal I_\Sigma=1$ and the sharp endpoint scale
$q_j^2\log(1/q_j)$, whereas complete records distinguish already at scale
$q_j=e^{-j\gamma}$ and success bits lose the tangent shape information.
Thus geometry, statistical information and observation coarsening are all
controlled by the same relative boundary structure.
\end{abstract}
