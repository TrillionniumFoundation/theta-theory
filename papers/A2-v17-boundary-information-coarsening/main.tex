\begin{abstract}
We study long alternating collision bridges in smooth periodic dispersing
billiards, the labelled contact geometry encoded by their signed endpoint
records, and the nonregular information carried by the resulting moving
support.  On an offset collar whose size is independent of the number of
flights, the normalized mixed endpoint derivative has a uniform nonlinear
relative limit, including every fixed mixed geometric and offset derivative.
The limiting physical flux factors into two half-line determinant amplitudes,
which are also the normalized scalar linearizing densities of the physical
stable returns.

Retaining the signed endpoint positions recovers the two unsymmetrized
half-line actions from support thresholds.  Their quadratic terms together
with physical onset recover the gap and both contact curvatures.  At every
order $n\ge3$ the next pair of labelled graph jets enters the action jets
through an explicit $2\times2$ block of determinant one.  We derive this
block from the weighted half-line stationary equations and their Jacobi
inverse and prove that the finite signed-jet map has an invertible
differential.  Hence arbitrary smooth labelled contact jets, odd and even,
are recursively determined without reflection symmetry, equality of the two
contacts, or supplied contact curvatures.  Real-analytic endpoint-law germs
determine the two participating analytic contact germs.

For a linearly vanishing density on a moving regular hypersurface, the local
family is generally non-dominated.  We separate its absolutely continuous
and support-exclusive parts and show that, at
$np_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1$, it is uniformly asymptotically
equivalent on compact local sets to a dominated common-collar LAN experiment
with intrinsic information matrix
\[
 \mathcal J_\Sigma=
 \int_\Sigma\frac{a_0VV^t}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma.
\]
This yields the Gaussian testing profile and the finite-dimensional local
asymptotic minimax bound without asserting a nonexistent finite-sample
Radon--Nikodym derivative for the original moving-support family.

We then formulate the billiard experiment on parameter-independent physical
time windows in one fixed laboratory chart.  In the anchored registered
family
\[
 (-\psi_{0,\vartheta}(u),u),\qquad
 (g+\psi_{1,\vartheta}(v),v),
\]
the labels, transverse origin and tangent direction are fixed and the
observed endpoints are literal laboratory coordinates.  Around a reference
gap $g_0$, the local scaling
\[
 g=g_0+\delta_n a/j_n,\qquad \vartheta=\delta_n h
\]
makes the gap and the contact geometry enter the same first-order support
velocity.  The exact fixed-window billiard endpoint family is compared with
its ideal moving-boundary family by an explicit Hellinger/Le Cam estimate at
the critical logarithmic scale.  The tangent inverse implies that finitely
many positive same-type windows have a positive-definite summed information
matrix on every fixed finite labelled contact-jet model.

If $k_n$ successful endpoint records are collected by a capped stopping rule
and $k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0$, the complete actual finite-bridge stopped
transcript---with failures, designs and stopping times retained---is uniformly
asymptotically equivalent in total variation to its boundary transcript.
Every failed preparation remains charged.  The endpoint-output coarsening
therefore inherits the Gaussian local experiment and its minimax bound.  We
do not declare waiting counts ancillary: the complete stopped transcript may
contain additional information through the long-bridge success probability.
A separate charged onset pilot is shown to justify centered endpoint-output
windows when desired.

Thus a single nonlinear boundary structure controls the long-bridge relative
law, the signed all-order local inverse, and a physically realized
non-dominated endpoint experiment on fixed observation coordinates.
\end{abstract}
