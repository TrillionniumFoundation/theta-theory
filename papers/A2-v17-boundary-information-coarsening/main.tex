\begin{abstract}
We study the nonlinear law of long alternating collision bridges in smooth
periodic dispersing billiards and the loss of information caused by
coarsening their physical records.  On a collar whose size is independent
of the number of flights, the normalized mixed endpoint derivative admits
a uniform relative limit, including every fixed mixed geometric and offset
derivative.  The limiting flux factors into two half-line determinant
amplitudes; these are identified with the normalized scalar linearizing
densities of the physical stable returns.  Residual-time integration then
gives complete symmetrized boundary-energy profiles and a Volterra
compatibility law, while two independently even contacts admit explicit
finite-jet inverses from both limiting and two-flight germs.

The same geometric normalization yields a second phenomenon.  Before
residual time is removed, the tangent finite-versus-boundary experiment has
an erasure transition at the hyperbolic scale $q_j=e^{-j\gamma}$.  After
residual time is integrated out, the endpoint density vanishes linearly on
a moving support boundary.  For a general smooth hypersurface model we
prove
\[
 H^2(f_t,f_0)=\frac{\mathcal I_\Sigma}{4}t^2\log(1/|t|)+O(t^2)
\]
and a finite-critical Gaussian likelihood limit, with an intrinsic
boundary coefficient $\mathcal I_\Sigma$.  In the billiard cap the exact
determinant-one whitening gives $\mathcal I_\Sigma=1$ for unequal contact
curvatures and either flight parity.  Hence endpoint records have the sharp
scale $q_j^2\log(1/q_j)$, whereas success indicators lose the tangent shape
information completely.  We transfer these limits to positive physical
offsets and obtain a strict hierarchy, for the same table and preparations,
between complete records, endpoint records, and success bits.  The
supercritical conclusion is retained without any support-exclusivity
assumption by a Hellinger-affinity argument.  Classical one-dimensional
moving-endpoint normality is not claimed as new; the new content is the
intrinsic hypersurface coefficient, thinning law, billiard whitening, and
the resulting observation-dependent physical transition.
\end{abstract}
