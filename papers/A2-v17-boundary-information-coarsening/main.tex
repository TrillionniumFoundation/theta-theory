\begin{abstract}
We study long alternating collision bridges in smooth periodic dispersing
billiards, the labelled geometry encoded by their signed boundary records, and
the multiscale information carried by the associated physical acquisition
process.  On an offset collar independent of the flight number, the normalized
mixed endpoint derivative has a uniform nonlinear relative limit, including
every fixed mixed geometric and offset derivative.  The limiting flux factors
into two half-line determinant amplitudes, which are also the normalized
scalar linearizing densities of the physical stable returns.

The signed endpoint support recovers the two unsymmetrized half-line actions.
Physical onset and their quadratic jets recover the gap and both contact
curvatures.  At every order \(n\ge3\), the next pair of labelled graph jets
enters the two action jets through an explicit \(2\times2\) block of
determinant one.  A weighted inverse for the nonlinear half-line stationarity
operator, a finite-truncation envelope identity and a homogeneous action-jet
filtration give the all-order proof.  Thus arbitrary smooth labelled contact
jets, odd and even, are recursively determined without reflection symmetry,
equal contacts or supplied curvatures.

For connected real-analytic obstacle boundaries we formulate the global inverse
problem without a common laboratory placement of the measured channels.  A
connected graph of intrinsic signed channel data recovers complete analytic
copies of every incident obstacle in the individual channel frames.  Their
compatible Euclidean gluings form a data-determined symmetry-holonomy space,
and realizations of the observations modulo one global Euclidean motion are
in bijection with that space.  If the graph contains a signature-rigid spanning
tree, the gluing is unique: the complete labelled periodic table is determined
up to one simultaneous global Euclidean motion, with no absolute contact
points, tangent frames or inter-channel placements supplied in advance.

The physical information depends sharply on the retained record.  Conditional
on success, the endpoint--residual-time boundary law has positive density at a
moving ceiling.  With \(k_n\) successful records and local shape scale
\(k_n^{-1}\), the extreme ceiling slacks converge to an explicit generally
non-dominated Poisson boundary-shift experiment.  A finite positive design
identifies every fixed labelled contact-jet coordinate; gap is visible at
scale \((j_nk_n)^{-1}\).  Under \(j_n=o(\sqrt{k_n})\) the waiting counts are
ancillary at this fastest scale, and the complete stopped endpoint--time
transcript transfers to actual finite bridges whenever
\(k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0\).

After residual time is integrated out, waiting counts and endpoints carry two
distinct slower channels.  At
\(k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1\), the hyperbolic-exponent direction is
visible from the negative-binomial waiting counts at shape scale
\((j_n\sqrt{k_n})^{-1}\), while the iso-hyperbolic contact directions remain
at the moving-endpoint scale \(\delta_n\) and the gap at
\(\delta_n/j_n\).  The joint limit is the independent product of a Gaussian
count shift and the boundary endpoint Gaussian shift.  Removing the waiting
counts gives the endpoint-output experiment: a parameter-independent common
collar yields a dominated LAN representative with intrinsic information
matrix
\[
 \mathcal J_\Sigma=
 \int_\Sigma\frac{a_0VV^t}{|\nabla w_0|}\,d\sigma.
\]
Thus residual-time, count and endpoint coarsenings have different local
experiments rather than different descriptions of one experiment.

Finally, on a compact analytic class with unique intrinsic gluing, finitely
many channel action jets resolve any prescribed global \(C^q\) accuracy.
Consequently uniformly consistent fixed-order physical estimators admit a
diagonal choice \(M_n\to\infty\) and a globally consistent table estimator.
This gives a growing-order bridge from finite-jet statistical information to
global analytic recovery without assuming a uniform-in-order condition number.
\end{abstract}
