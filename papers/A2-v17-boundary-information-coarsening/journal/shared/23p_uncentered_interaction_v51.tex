\section{Interaction data with unknown transverse origins}
\label{sec:v51-interaction}

The geometric inverse uses the interaction of the two endpoint coordinates,
not the individual flux amplitudes.  This distinction also permits the
transverse contact origins to be recovered from the law.  We prove this for
an observation in which the two numerical origins are unknown and successful
records are subject to unknown, separate endpoint efficiencies.  The signed
units and directions of the transverse coordinates, the gap and the excess
time remain specified.  In particular, an unknown origin is not an unknown
nonlinear change of the physical coordinate.

\subsection{The recorded law and its interaction}

Let $I$ be a bounded interval containing zero.  Let $S$ be smooth on a
neighborhood of $\overline I$, with
\[
 S(0)=S'(0)=0,\qquad S''>0.
\]
Fix $d>0$ so small that $\{u\in I:S(u)\le d\}$ is compactly contained
in $I$.  For unknown $\xi,\eta\in\mathbb R$ and positive smooth
functions $A$ and $C$ on neighborhoods of $\xi+\overline I$ and
$\eta+\overline I$, respectively, consider the probability density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v51-recorded-law}
 \widetilde f(x,y)=\frac{A(x)C(y)}{Z}
       [d-S(x-\xi)-S(y-\eta)]_+,
 \qquad (x,y)\in(\xi+I)\times(\eta+I),
\end{equation}
extended by zero.  Here $Z$ is the positive integral of the numerator.
The positive set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v51-positive-set}
 D=\{(x,y):S(x-\xi)+S(y-\eta)<d\}
\end{equation}
is understood in the displayed coordinate box.  It is bounded, connected,
and has interval fibers.  Its closure is inside that box.  Strict positivity
of $A,C$ is important: no part of the cap is hidden by a zero recording
efficiency.  The law determines $D$ and its smooth interior density; a
continuous density is determined everywhere in its open positive set by
its almost-everywhere class.

For a same-type bridge, the physical limiting density of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-law} has this form with
$A(\xi+u)=B(u)$ and $C(\eta+v)=B(v)$.  Unknown recording probabilities
$e_1(x),e_2(y)>0$ multiply these factors before normalization.  Conversely,
since $B$ is positive on the compact collar, the positive smooth ratios
$A(\xi+u)/B(u)$ and $C(\eta+v)/B(v)$ can each be rescaled to recording
probabilities at most one without changing the normalized law.  Thus every
pair of effective factors in this class is allowed, and $A,C$ need not agree.
For fixed smooth efficiencies, the corresponding reweighted finite-flight
laws have this limit: bounded continuous reweighting preserves weak
convergence and the limiting normalizing mass is positive.  No uniform
bound over efficiencies tending to zero is asserted.

On $D$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v51-interaction}
 \mathcal K_{\widetilde f}(x,y)
   =\partial_x\partial_y\log\widetilde f(x,y)
   =\frac{\widetilde f\,\widetilde f_{xy}
                       -\widetilde f_x\widetilde f_y}{\widetilde f^2}.
\end{equation}
This mixed logarithmic derivative is the classical local dependence
function of Holland and Wang~\cite{HollandWang1987}; the related
odds-ratio description of association is developed in~\cite{Osius2009}.
We use those invariants in physical transverse coordinates, not after a
transformation to marginal quantiles.  The facts needed below are proved
directly.  No sampling-equivalence or efficiency theorem from association
models is imported into the billiard experiment.

\begin{lemma}[Recovery of the two transverse origins]
\label{lem:v51-origins}
The law \eqref{eq:v51-recorded-law} determines $\xi$ and $\eta$, independently
of $A,C,Z$.  More precisely, with $H=d-S(x-\xi)-S(y-\eta)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v51-factorized-interaction}
 \mathcal K_{\widetilde f}(x,y)
       =-\frac{S'(x-\xi)S'(y-\eta)}{H^2}.
\end{equation}
Among vertical fibers of $D$, exactly the fiber at $x=\xi$ has identically
zero interaction.  Among horizontal fibers, exactly the one at $y=\eta$
has that property.  If $(\xi,y_*)\in D$ with $y_*\ne\eta$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v51-simple-root}
 \partial_x\mathcal K_{\widetilde f}(\xi,y_*)
 =-\frac{S''(0)S'(y_*-\eta)}{[d-S(y_*-\eta)]^2}\ne0.
\end{equation}
The analogous horizontal root is simple as well.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $D$, $\log\widetilde f=\log A+\log C+\log H-\log Z$.
The mixed derivative of the first, second and fourth terms vanishes.
Since $H_{xy}=0$, differentiating $\log H$ gives
\eqref{eq:v51-factorized-interaction}.  Strict convexity implies that
$S'$ vanishes exactly at zero.  Every nonempty vertical fiber is an open
interval of positive length, and so contains a point different from
$\eta$.  If $x\ne\xi$, the interaction is nonzero at such a point.
For $x=\xi$ it vanishes on the entire fiber.  This proves the vertical
characterization; interchanging the coordinates proves the horizontal
one.  Differentiating \eqref{eq:v51-factorized-interaction} at $x=\xi$
eliminates the term containing $S'(0)$ and yields
\eqref{eq:v51-simple-root}.  All derivatives are taken in the positive
interior, not across the boundary of the cap.
\end{proof}

For the complete positive cap, there is also a support-only centering
method: its fiber widths attain their unique maxima at the two origins.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v52-support-centering} gives that comparison.  The
interaction proof above is useful because its simple roots, and the
finite-smooth estimate below, are confined to a positive interior rectangle.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v52-window-extraction} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:v52-window-geometry} state what survives when only
such an interior window is recorded.

\subsection{Recovery of the action, with separate endpoint weights}

After determining the two origins, set
$f^{\rm c}(u,v)=\widetilde f(\xi+u,\eta+v)$.  Choose $r>0$ with
$[-r,r]\subset I$ and $\max_{[-r,r]}S<d/4$, and let
$a\in(-r,r)\setminus\{0\}$.  Define
\begin{align}
 R(u,v)&=\frac{f^{\rm c}(u,v)f^{\rm c}(0,0)}
                   {f^{\rm c}(u,0)f^{\rm c}(0,v)},
                      \label{eq:v51-centered-ratio}\\
 q&=\sqrt{1-R(a,a)},\qquad
 T(u)=\frac{1-R(u,a)}{q},\qquad
 \mathcal S(\widetilde f)(u)=\frac{dT(u)}{1+T(u)}.
                      \label{eq:v51-centered-inverse}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[The action as an invariant of the recorded interaction]
\label{thm:v51-action}
For every law \eqref{eq:v51-recorded-law}, the construction above gives
$q>0$ and $\mathcal S(\widetilde f)=S$ on $[-r,r]$.  Hence the law
recovers the signed action germ without supplied transverse origins or
endpoint weights.  Once $S$ is known, the effective factors have the
locally determined ratios
\begin{align}
 \frac{A(\xi+u)}{A(\xi)}
   &=\frac{f^{\rm c}(u,0)}{f^{\rm c}(0,0)}(1+T(u)),
 \qquad
 \frac{C(\eta+v)}{C(\eta)}
   =\frac{f^{\rm c}(0,v)}{f^{\rm c}(0,0)}(1+T(v)).
                         \label{eq:v51-effective-weights}
\end{align}
For fixed $d$, two centered positive densities in this class have the same
action germ if and only if their ratio is a product of a function of $u$
and a function of $v$ on some common square about zero.  Thus the germ is
a complete local invariant for separate endpoint reweighting after the
origins are recovered.  This does not separate a physical flux factor
from an unknown recording factor multiplying it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $A_0(u)=A(\xi+u)$ and $C_0(v)=C(\eta+v)$.  Direct substitution,
without assuming $A_0=C_0$, gives
\[
 R(u,v)=\frac{d[d-S(u)-S(v)]}{[d-S(u)][d-S(v)]},\qquad
 1-R(u,v)=\frac{S(u)}{d-S(u)}\frac{S(v)}{d-S(v)}.
\]
Since $a\ne0$ and $S''>0$, the positive scalar root in
\eqref{eq:v51-centered-inverse} equals $S(a)/(d-S(a))$.  It follows
that $T(u)=S(u)/(d-S(u))$ for both signs of $u$.  Solving this identity
proves the action formula.  In particular, no pointwise square root at
the minimum is involved.  Substitution in the two axis densities proves
\eqref{eq:v51-effective-weights}.

Equal actions imply separate factorization of the ratio of the densities
by their defining formulas.  Conversely, multiplying by a positive
function of $u$ and a positive function of $v$ leaves $R$ unchanged.
The same nonzero anchor and \eqref{eq:v51-centered-inverse} then recover
the same action.  Equivalently, equality of the two ratios $R_f,R_h$
on a centered square gives the explicit factorization
\[
 \frac{f(u,v)}{h(u,v)}
 =\frac{f(u,0)}{h(u,0)}\frac{f(0,v)}{h(0,v)}
                          \frac{h(0,0)}{f(0,0)}.
\]
This assertion is local in the common positive square.  It does not claim
that arbitrary prescribed marginal laws are compatible with a given
nonrectangular cap, nor that a joint recording factor depending on both
coordinates would cancel.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-order regularity of the extraction}

The identification of a zero fiber is an exact operation on a law.  For a
local error estimate it is preferable to use simple roots on two fixed
slices.  This also makes the derivative with respect to the observation
explicit and avoids differentiating a set-valued zero-fiber operation.

\begin{proposition}[A local differentiable extraction with finite loss]
\label{prop:v51-stability}
Fix a law $\widetilde f_0$ of the preceding class.  For every integer
$m\ge0$, there are a closed rectangle $Q$ compactly contained in its
positive set, a number $r>0$, and an open neighborhood
$\mathcal V$ of $\widetilde f_0|_Q$ in $C^{m+3}(Q)$ on which maps
\[
 f\longmapsto\bigl(\Xi(f),\Upsilon(f),\mathcal S_*(f)\bigr)
       \in\mathbb R^2\times C^m([-r,r])
\]
are $C^1$.  They equal $(\xi,\eta,S)$ for laws of the recorded class
whose origins and action data are in the indicated local branch.  On a
sufficiently small convex ball in $\mathcal V$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v51-local-stability}
 |\Xi(f)-\Xi(h)|+|\Upsilon(f)-\Upsilon(h)|
  +\|\mathcal S_*(f)-\mathcal S_*(h)\|_{C^m([-r,r])}
       \le C_m\|f-h\|_{C^{m+3}(Q)}.
\end{equation}
The constants are local and depend on positive density, simple-root and
anchor margins.  For a differentiable recorded family, at its base law,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v51-center-variation}
 \dot\xi=-\frac{\dot{\mathcal K}(\xi,y_*)}
                     {\partial_x\mathcal K(\xi,y_*)},\qquad
 \dot\eta=-\frac{\dot{\mathcal K}(x_*,\eta)}
                     {\partial_y\mathcal K(x_*,\eta)}.
\end{equation}
In particular a stationary interior density has stationary origins and
stationary action at every separately fixed smooth order, even when its
separate endpoint weights vary.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose noncentral $x_*,y_*$ close enough to $\xi_0,\eta_0$ that a
closed rectangle $Q\Subset D_0$ contains $(\xi_0,\eta_0)$, both slice
anchors, and a small centered square, with positive room to its boundary.
Its density has a positive lower bound.  The map
\[
 f\longmapsto\mathcal K_f=f_{xy}/f-f_xf_y/f^2
\]
is $C^1$ from the open positive part of $C^{m+3}(Q)$ into
$C^{m+1}(Q)$.  Evaluating at $(x,y_*)$ or $(x_*,y)$ gives a $C^1$
map in $f$ and the remaining scalar variable.  The nonzero derivatives
in \eqref{eq:v51-simple-root} permit the scalar implicit function theorem.
It produces unique roots $\Xi(f)$ near $\xi_0$ and $\Upsilon(f)$
near $\eta_0$, and both root maps are $C^1$.  On the model these roots
are the actual origins whenever the latter are in those neighborhoods.
The exact global identification remains Lemma~\ref{lem:v51-origins};
no global root selection for arbitrary nonmodel perturbations is needed.

Shrink the neighborhood and choose $r$ so that
$(\Xi(f)+[-r,r])\times(\Upsilon(f)+[-r,r])$ is inside $Q$ for every
$f$ under consideration.  Translation followed by restriction defines
\[
 f\longmapsto f(\Xi(f)+u,\Upsilon(f)+v)
\]
as a $C^1$ map into $C^{m+1}([-r,r]^2)$, with the surplus derivatives
in the hypothesis.  Its differential in a direction $h$ is the translated
$h$ plus the translated $f_x D\Xi[h]+f_y D\Upsilon[h]$.
Choose a fixed $a\ne0$ in this square.  The base value
$1-R_0(a,a)$ is strictly positive.  On a smaller neighborhood every
axis denominator, the scalar anchor root, and $1+T$ retain positive
lower bounds.  Products, quotients, slice evaluations and a scalar square
root are $C^1$ in these finite smooth norms.  Composing them gives
$\mathcal S_*$ by \eqref{eq:v51-centered-inverse}.

Continuity of the derivative at $f_0$ bounds its operator norm on a
sufficiently small ball.  Integration of that derivative on the segment
from $f$ to $h$ proves \eqref{eq:v51-local-stability}.  This uses a
finite smooth observation norm; it is not a total-variation estimate.
Differentiating the two root equations on their fixed slices proves
\eqref{eq:v51-center-variation}.  If $\dot f=0$ in these smooth norms,
then $\dot{\mathcal K}=0$ and both root derivatives vanish.  The
translated density is then stationary, and differentiation of the scalar
anchor and action formulas makes $\dot S=0$.  Unknown derivatives of
$A,C,Z$ have disappeared through the interaction and ratio identities,
not through an assumption that they were known or constant.
\end{proof}

For a small nonmodel perturbation the extracted action need not have zero
first derivative.  Subtracting its constant and linear terms fixes that
anchoring and changes no coefficient of degree at least two.  With a pair
of recorded laws and the gap, the finite smooth inverse of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v26-density-stability} therefore gives, for each
fixed contact order $M$, a local bound for the recovered graph coefficients
by the gap error plus the two $C^{M+3}$ density errors in
\eqref{eq:v51-local-stability}.  Its constants also depend on the inherited
positive geometric bounds.  No infinite-order condition bound or uniform
estimate near a vanishing recording efficiency follows from this statement.

\subsection{The complete geometric inverse with these nuisances}

For a selected channel $e$ and each same-type law $b=0,1$, let
$\widetilde P_{e,b}$ be its complete translated and separately weighted
law as in \eqref{eq:v51-recorded-law}.  The gaps $g_e$, positive offsets
$d_e$, obstacle and deck marks, and signed transverse length conventions
are retained.  The two origins and both recording profiles in each law
are unknown, and may differ between endpoint types and channels.

\begin{theorem}[Geometric determination from uncentered weighted laws]
\label{thm:v51-geometric}
These recorded laws and the gaps determine every finite labelled contact
jet of smooth strictly convex channels.  For analytic noncircular tables
with a selected skeleton as in Theorem~\ref{thm:v48-main}, the set of
compatible tables, after allowing the stated nuisance origins and positive
separate recording profiles, is exactly the finite table fiber of the
original ideal-law datum.  In particular the complete marked lattice is
recovered on every branch, and the fiber is a singleton on $\mathscr A_N$.

For a $C^1$ common-strip analytic-support table family with a persistent
selected design and fixed positive offsets, allow also $C^1$ origins and
positive recording profiles with $C^1$ dependence in each finite smooth
norm on the common recorded collars.  If the gaps and all recorded interior
densities are stationary, the table variation is a common infinitesimal
proper Euclidean motion.  Conversely, every such table variation admits
stationary recorded laws by keeping its intrinsic origins and recording
profiles fixed.  Thus the projection of the joint data-derivative kernel
onto table variations is exactly the common Euclidean tangent space.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:v51-origins} identifies the origins in each record, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v51-action} recovers both signed action germs.  The gap
and their quadratic coefficients give
\[
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}),\quad
 c_b=\cosh\gamma\sqrt{a_b/a_{1-b}},\quad
 \kappa_b=(c_b-1)/g,\qquad a_b=S_b''(0).
\]
The finite-order identities of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity} then give
$q_n=M_n^{-1}(s_n-R_n)$ successively.  The smooth remainder result is
still necessary; cancellation of recording factors does not replace it.
Every finite signed jet, including its odd terms, is recovered.

For analytic boundaries the resulting contact germs determine the complete
ordered channel-frame images.  Finite matching and the displacement cochain
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v49-finite-fiber} enumerate the compatible tables and
recover $L=(v_1\ v_2)M^{-1}$ on each accepted branch.  Every realization
of the recorded laws yields these same jets and images, and so belongs
to that list.  Conversely, each accepted branch places every recovered
ordered channel pair by a proper motion.  Its local half-line actions and
normalized physical amplitudes therefore agree with the original ones;
any global phase-volume constant cancels in the conditional law.  Reusing
the original numerical origins and endpoint recording profiles realizes
the complete recorded laws on that branch.  This proves both inclusions
of the table fibers, not merely finiteness of a list containing them.
Asymmetric uniqueness follows from the same matching theorem.

For the differential assertion, work on common positive rectangles about
the base contacts.  The inherited forward differentiability and the assumed
regularity of the profiles give the finite smooth dependence required in
Proposition~\ref{prop:v51-stability}.  The normalizer is differentiable by
the positive-part calculation in Lemma~\ref{lem:v48-forward-C1}; translations
can first be removed in that integral, so the same regular level set applies.
Stationarity of the recorded densities gives $\dot\xi=\dot\eta=0$ and
$\dot S_b=0$.  Differentiate the displayed leading recovery and then
\[
 \dot q_n=M_n^{-1}(\dot s_n-\dot R_n-\dot M_nq_n).
\]
Finite induction makes every graph-jet variation zero.  Analyticity of the
support variation on a smaller common strip and the identity theorem give
stationary complete channel-frame images, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:v48-analytic-variation}.  The actual local congruence branch
of Lemma~\ref{lem:v49-local-alignment} makes their relative angular and
translation velocities zero.  The same finite cochain then makes the
lattice derivative zero in one common gauge.  Undoing that gauge yields
exactly the common infinitesimal Euclidean motion.  This argument never
infers an injective derivative from exact injectivity.

For the converse choose a common Euclidean motion of the table and keep
all nuisance profiles fixed as functions of their intrinsic numerical
coordinates.  Gaps and the ideal laws are invariant, hence the recorded
laws are stationary.  This is an existence assertion about a joint kernel
vector.  Arbitrary changes of the recording profiles need not preserve
a law even when the table itself is stationary.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Relation to the other observations}

The extraction recovers numerical origins, not an unknown clock, channel
direction, transverse scale or deck marking.  The axes remain the specified
oriented transverse axes of each channel; this is not an arbitrary fixed
laboratory projection while the channel direction varies.  It requires the positive cap
not to be cropped before either origin is visible.  An inseparable joint
recording factor $w(x,y)$ adds $\partial_x\partial_y\log w$ to
\eqref{eq:v51-interaction} and is a different model.  Similarly, an arbitrary
nonlinear coordinate change alters the physical action; it is not an
unobserved translation.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v51-geometric} concerns exact laws, with the local finite
smooth bound \eqref{eq:v51-local-stability}.  It is not a parameter-independent
Markov kernel transforming individual uncentered samples into ideal samples,
a uniform finite-histogram theorem, or a semiparametric efficiency assertion.
The unknown recording factors may drastically reduce the chance of accepting
a successful bridge.  The preparation budgets and density margins in
Part III of the complete technical manuscript cannot be transferred uniformly without additional quantitative
bounds on those factors.  The physical pilot there also determines quantities
other than transverse origins, and its proved sensor and clock assumptions
remain in force.  In this way the same boundary law has distinct roles:
its interaction determines geometry, while its amplitude and retained
auxiliary records govern acquisition and local statistical information.
