\subsection{What the complete support already determines}
\label{subsec:v52-support-benchmark}

There are two different reasons for using an interior interaction.  It
eliminates separate recording factors, and its local regularity does not
require control of a support boundary.  The second point should not be
confused with nonidentifiability of the origins from a complete cap.
The following observation, communicated in~\cite{A2ReviewV51}, identifies
exactly what that support supplies.

\begin{lemma}[Centering from complete fiber widths]
\label{lem:v52-support-centering}
In the setting of \eqref{eq:v51-recorded-law}, the positive set $D$ alone
determines both origins.  Its vertical fiber length has a unique maximum
at $x=\xi$, and its horizontal fiber length has a unique maximum at
$y=\eta$.  Both maxima are nondegenerate.  No symmetry of $S$ is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $0<q\le d$, strict convexity and the compact-sublevel assumption give
exactly two inverse branches $v_-(q)<0<v_+(q)$ of $S(v)=q$, lying in
$I$.  The inverse function theorem applies to both branches, including
in a neighborhood of $q=d$, since their derivatives $S'(v_\pm(q))$
are nonzero and the sublevel has positive room inside $I$.  Put
\[
 W(q)=v_+(q)-v_-(q),\qquad
 W'(q)=\frac1{S'(v_+(q))}-\frac1{S'(v_-(q))}>0.
\]
For every $x$ with a nonempty vertical fiber, its length is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v52-width}
 w(x)=W\bigl(d-S(x-\xi)\bigr).
\end{equation}
This expression has its unique maximum at $\xi$, since $S$ has its
unique minimum at zero.  Differentiation in a neighborhood of that
maximum gives
\[
 w'(\xi)=0,\qquad w''(\xi)=-W'(d)S''(0)<0.
\]
The horizontal calculation is identical, with $\eta$ in place of $\xi$.
Strict positivity of the recording factors makes $D$ the interior of
the support of the recorded law, so these are observable fiber lengths.
The width need not be symmetric about its maximum.
\end{proof}

This comparison concerns centering, not recovery of the entire action
from one support: Proposition~\ref{prop:v27-support-gauge} preserves a
whole cap while changing its action germ.  It is also not a replacement
for Proposition~\ref{prop:v51-stability}, whose observation norm controls
only a fixed rectangle strictly inside the positive set.  The support
boundary is outside that norm.  The four-density cancellation, including
unequal separate factors, is already the algebra of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-density-inverse}; it is not an additional independent
high-order inverse.  The geometric work is done by the relative law and
the actual-smooth contact recursion to which the invariant is applied.

\subsection{Recording only a positive interior window}
\label{subsec:v52-window}

The extraction can be formulated without an observed cap boundary.
Let $J_x,J_y$ be bounded open intervals with known endpoints and let
$\mathcal W=J_x\times J_y$ be a known recording window.  The numerical
origins $\xi\in J_x$ and $\eta\in J_y$ are unknown.  On neighborhoods
of the translated closed intervals let $S$ be smooth with
$S(0)=S'(0)=0$ and $S''>0$, as above.  Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v52-window-margin}
 H(x,y)=d-S(x-\xi)-S(y-\eta)>0
             \quad\hbox{on }\overline{\mathcal W}.
\end{equation}
Only records in $\mathcal W$ are retained.  Their law has density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v52-window-law}
 p_{\mathcal W}(x,y)=\frac{A(x)C(y)}{Z_{\mathcal W}}H(x,y)
                           \mathbf1_{\mathcal W}(x,y),\qquad
 Z_{\mathcal W}=\int_{\mathcal W}A(x)C(y)H(x,y)\,\dd x\,\dd y,
\end{equation}
where $A,C$ are positive and smooth near the respective closed intervals.
They need not agree.  Neither the density nor its support outside
$\mathcal W$, nor the discarded mass, is part of this datum.  In
particular its positive support is just the fixed recording window.

The visibility assumption $\xi\in J_x$, $\eta\in J_y$ retains both
critical coordinate lines.  It specifies a class of observations, not
an algorithm for locating a suitable window without prior information.
The known signed transverse units, directions and excess time remain
those of Section~\ref{sec:v51-interaction}.  We do not replace them by
unknown scales or arbitrary coordinate transformations.

\begin{theorem}[Interior-window extraction]
\label{thm:v52-window-extraction}
The law \eqref{eq:v52-window-law} determines $\xi$, $\eta$ and the
signed germ of $S$.  A complete cap and an observation of its boundary
are not needed.  The two effective factor ratios in
\eqref{eq:v51-effective-weights} are determined on a small interval
about the recovered origins.

For each fixed integer $m\ge0$, the extraction has a local $C^1$
extension from an open neighborhood in $C^{m+3}(Q)$ to
$\mathbb R^2\times C^m([-r,r])$, where
$Q\Subset\mathcal W$ contains the two slice anchors and the translated
square.  On a sufficiently small convex neighborhood it satisfies
\eqref{eq:v51-local-stability}.  The constants depend on the chosen
positive density, root, anchor and window margins.  They do not require
bounds on a density outside $Q$ or on a discarded cap boundary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the open window the mixed logarithmic derivative is
\[
 \mathcal K_p(x,y)=-\frac{S'(x-\xi)S'(y-\eta)}{H(x,y)^2}.
\]
Every vertical fiber is the nonempty interval $J_y$.  Since $S'$ has
only one zero and $J_y$ has positive length, exactly the vertical line
$x=\xi$ has identically zero interaction; similarly the horizontal
line is $y=\eta$.  This recovers the origins using no boundary value.
The smooth positive representative of a density on the window is
uniquely determined by its almost-everywhere class.

Choose a small $r>0$ so that
$(\xi+[-r,r])\times(\eta+[-r,r])\Subset\mathcal W$ and
$\max_{[-r,r]}S<d/4$.  On that square put
$p^{\rm c}(u,v)=p_{\mathcal W}(\xi+u,\eta+v)$ and choose one fixed
$a\in(-r,r)\setminus\{0\}$.  The window normalization is a positive
scalar, and hence cancels together with both separate factors:
\[
 R_p(u,v)=\frac{p^{\rm c}(u,v)p^{\rm c}(0,0)}
                   {p^{\rm c}(u,0)p^{\rm c}(0,v)},\qquad
 1-R_p(u,v)=\frac{S(u)}{d-S(u)}\frac{S(v)}{d-S(v)}.
\]
The scalar $q=\sqrt{1-R_p(a,a)}$ is positive and equals
$S(a)/(d-S(a))$.  Thus $T(u)=(1-R_p(u,a))/q$ and
$S(u)=dT(u)/(1+T(u))$, for both signs of $u$.  Substitution on the
axes gives the asserted effective-factor ratios.  Nothing in these
identities involves $Z_{\mathcal W}$ or the unobserved mass separately.

For the local regularity assertion choose noncentral slices $x_*,y_*$
inside the window and then a closed $Q\Subset\mathcal W$ containing
these slices near their roots and a small centered square.  Compactness
and \eqref{eq:v52-window-margin} give a positive density minimum there.
The two root slopes are exactly \eqref{eq:v51-simple-root} and its
horizontal counterpart, so they are nonzero.  The proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v51-stability} is local to this $Q$: the map
$f\mapsto f_{xy}/f-f_xf_y/f^2$ is $C^1$ from positive
$C^{m+3}(Q)$ to $C^{m+1}(Q)$; two scalar implicit function theorems
give the origin maps; and restriction after their translations, followed
by the slice products and scalar anchor inverse, gives a $C^1$ action
map into $C^m([-r,r])$.  The derivative of the translation contains
both $f_xD\Xi$ and $f_yD\Upsilon$, and the scalar anchor is also
differentiated.  None of these operations evaluates the edge of
$\mathcal W$.  A smaller convex ball has bounded derivative, which
gives the stated estimate by integration on line segments.  This
argument applies to arbitrary nearby positive nonmodel inputs as a
local extension; exact identification is claimed on the model class.
\end{proof}

The fixed support of \eqref{eq:v52-window-law} cannot perform the
centering in Lemma~\ref{lem:v52-support-centering}.  For example, begin
with an actual channel law and a window strictly inside its cap, with
both origins strictly inside the coordinate intervals.  Small changes
of the numerical origins preserve these strict conditions.  The cropped
laws then have the same positive support $\mathcal W$ and different
origins, while their interactions locate those origins by the theorem.
This is a change of the recorded numerical origins of an existing
channel, not a claim that an arbitrary functional action is realized
by a billiard.  The window theorem records the boundary-independent
content of the existing interior proof; it does not introduce a new
association invariant.

\subsection{The geometric fiber after interior cropping}
\label{subsec:v52-window-geometry}

\begin{corollary}[The same table fiber from interior-window laws]
\label{cor:v52-window-geometry}
For every selected channel and both same-type laws, retain a known
window satisfying \eqref{eq:v52-window-margin} and containing the
respective unknown origins, together with the gaps, marks, signed units,
directions and fixed positive offsets.  Allow independent positive
smooth separate recording factors for every law.  Then these window
laws determine every finite labelled contact jet of smooth strictly
convex channels.  On the analytic noncircular class of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v48-main}, their compatible table fiber is exactly
the ideal-law fiber, including its unknown marked lattices.  It is
therefore finite, and is a singleton on $\mathscr A_N$.

In the differentiable-family setting of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v51-geometric}, keep the numerical windows fixed and
the visibility and positivity conditions locally strict.  The projection
of the joint window-data derivative kernel onto table variations is
exactly the common infinitesimal proper Euclidean tangent space.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:v52-window-extraction} to each record.  The gap
and the two recovered quadratic action coefficients give the leading
geometry by the formulas in Theorem~\ref{thm:v51-geometric}.  The
finite-smooth factorization of Lemma~\ref{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization}
then makes each higher coefficient an actual graph-jet invariant before
the triangular inverse is applied.  Thus all finite signed contact jets
are fixed.  For analytic boundaries these jets give complete ordered
channel-frame images by analytic continuation.  Every table realizing
the window laws therefore gives one of the compatible incidence
assignments in Theorem~\ref{thm:v49-finite-fiber}, with its lattice
$L=(v_1\ v_2)M^{-1}$.  This is one inclusion in the claimed fiber.

Conversely, each accepted ideal branch places every recovered ordered
channel pair by a proper Euclidean motion.  Its local physical actions
and amplitudes agree with those of the original pair, in the same
intrinsic transverse coordinates.  Reuse the original numerical origins,
windows and recording factors on each such pair.  The resulting
normalized window density is exactly \eqref{eq:v52-window-law}.
A global phase-volume factor cancels under conditioning.  This gives
the reverse inclusion; no unobserved portion of a recorded density is
being assumed as extra data.  The uniqueness conclusion follows on
the original properly asymmetric subclass, without altering that class.

For a family, the normalizing integral on the fixed window is
particularly simple.  In the notation of \eqref{eq:v52-window-law},
with $w_t=A_tC_t$,
\[
 \dot Z_{\mathcal W}
       =\int_{\mathcal W}(\dot w H+w\dot H),\qquad
 \dot H=-\dot S(x-\xi)-\dot S(y-\eta)
           +S'(x-\xi)\dot\xi+S'(y-\eta)\dot\eta.
\]
Local smooth bounds on the compact window justify differentiation under
the integral and give continuity of the derivative.  There is no moving
integration boundary.  Together with the inherited forward estimates
this gives the finite smooth density dependence needed above.
Stationary window densities have stationary origins and stationary
action jets.  Differentiating the leading recovery and then
$q_n=M_n^{-1}(s_n-R_n)$ at each finite order gives zero graph-jet
variation, retaining the derivatives of both $R_n$ and $M_n$.
The variation of a common-strip analytic-support family is analytic on
a smaller strip, so it vanishes on each complete channel-frame image.
The actual continuous alignment branches and the displacement cochain
then make the table variation zero in one common Euclidean gauge,
exactly as in Theorem~\ref{thm:v51-geometric}.  Undoing the gauge gives
the asserted tangent space.  Conversely, a common proper Euclidean
motion with fixed intrinsic numerical origins and recording profiles
leaves every window law stationary.  This is a statement about the
projection of a joint kernel, not identification of all profile
variations.
\end{proof}

The effective factors in this window model are compatible with separate
recording probabilities.  On the two compact coordinate intervals the
physical amplitudes are positive.  Extend the logarithms of the smooth
positive ratios $A/B$ and $C/B$ to the physical collars by smooth cutoffs,
and exponentiate.  The extensions stay positive and bounded; multiplying
each by a sufficiently small positive constant makes it at most one.
The two constants cancel in the normalized window law.  The additional
known crop is itself a product of the two interval indicators.

For fixed recording factors these window laws are also limits of the
corresponding recorded finite-flight laws.  The ideal endpoint laws
converge weakly by Theorem~\ref{thm:v4-law}.  Multiplication by the
bounded recording weight and $\mathbf1_{\mathcal W}$ preserves the
limit of integrals against bounded continuous tests: its discontinuities
are confined to the four edges, which have zero mass for the absolutely
continuous limiting law.  The limiting denominator is positive, so
normalizing gives \eqref{eq:v52-window-law}.  This is a fixed-observation
limit, not a uniform bound over efficiencies or windows with vanishing
accepted mass.

In particular, neither interior-window rigidity nor complete-cap rigidity
transfers the preparation budgets of Part III of the complete technical manuscript to an unconstrained
recording class.  Multiplying both recording probabilities by
$\varepsilon$ leaves the normalized law unchanged and multiplies the
recorded-success probability by $\varepsilon^2$.  A charged uniform
budget therefore still requires a separate positive success bound.
The interior smooth estimate, the exact finite-dimensional coordinates,
and the finite-histogram theorem retain their different observation
norms and hypotheses.
