\section{Graph and support coordinates}
\label{sec:v56-graph-support}

The differential image argument uses a local geometric coordinate change,
not the quantitative statistical hypotheses of the full technical
manuscript.  We record it here so that its proof is part of the principal
article.

\begin{lemma}[Finite graph-to-support conversion]
\label{lem:v56-graph-support}
In an outward-normal contact frame let the boundary be $(-\psi(y),y)$,
where $\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0$ and $\psi''(0)=\kappa>0$.
On a small normal-angle interval there is a unique solution
$\psi'(y(\theta))=\tan\theta$.  Its support function is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v46-graph-support}
 h(\theta)=-\psi(y(\theta))\cos\theta+y(\theta)\sin\theta.
\end{equation}
For each $m\ge2$, the support jet through degree $m$ is a smooth function
of the graph jet through degree $m$, on positive curvature domains.
The same assertion holds for parameter derivatives in the corresponding
finite smooth norms.  In particular, zero variations of all anchored
graph coefficients imply zero variations of all anchored support
coefficients.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The scalar implicit function theorem applies to $\psi'(y)=p$ at
$(y,p)=(0,0)$ since its derivative in $y$ is $\kappa$.  Strict convexity
identifies this point with the unique local support point for normal
$(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$.  Put
$H(p)=-\psi(y(p))+py(p)$.  Stationarity gives $H'(p)=y(p)$, so
$h(\theta)=\cos\theta H(\tan\theta)$.
The coefficient of $p^k$ in $y(p)$, for $k\le m-1$, is obtained by
successively solving the scalar equation $\psi'(y(p))=p$; it depends
only on graph coefficients through order $k+1$ and divides by the
positive number $\kappa$.  Since $H(0)=0$, integration of $H'=y$
shows that the jet of $H$ through order $m$ needs no higher graph jet.
The compositions by $\tan\theta$ and $\cos\theta$ preserve this
finite-order property.  The implicit function theorem and the finite
chain rule justify this computation for actual smooth functions, and
also justify parameter differentiation.  For example $h(0)=h'(0)=0$
and $h''(0)=\kappa^{-1}$.

When the second contact's outward normal is reversed, its positively
oriented tangent must also be reversed.  This known proper frame change
multiplies its degree-$n$ graph coefficient by $(-1)^n$ before the same
formula is used.  It introduces no unknown relative pose.  Thus vanishing
of all graph-coefficient variations in the common signed channel frame
implies vanishing of all support-coefficient variations on both sides.
\end{proof}
