# Cover letter for renewed consideration

Dear Editors and Referee,

Please consider revision 67 of *Boundary laws and rigidity of periodic dispersing billiards*. The paper studies a geometric inverse problem for nonlinear endpoint laws of long billiard bridges. Its main point is that conditioning an exponentially rare event preserves nonlinear boundary geometry on a fixed contact collar, and that the resulting law admits an inverse in the class of actual reflecting contacts.

The two parts are essential to the same result. The forward proof normalizes an exact corner cofactor before using a two-ended trace-class comparison. This controls the relative twist at a scale where an absolute length expansion is insufficient. The inverse proof differentiates the actual stationary boundary envelope, with the endpoint and interior multiplicities and the terminal limit controlled. The resulting signed response, together with the global positive-curvature inverse, determines finite contact jets and analytic germs without candidate closeness. Whole analytic obstacle images are determined when placement, lattice and full visitation are supplied. The complete local analytic norm theorem is separately proved and retains its conditional real-law stability hypotheses.

The significance claim is not a count of a contraction lemma, a jet induction and analytic continuation as separate advances. It is the combined relative-law/geometric-response theorem. Its reach is concrete: the retained area-preserving analytic family has identical gap, area, contact curvatures and every leading endpoint/count datum but different nonlinear laws; the recovered amplitudes also determine nonlinear stable return coordinates. These statements concern the physical observations of the paper, not a claimed solution of marked-length rigidity from different data.

The latest report credits the exact global theorem and requests one quantitative correction. We have corrected it in the existing finite-flight proposition and supplied the complete proof. A residual-certified curvature error is propagated through the signed finite-jet map with a recursively specified Lipschitz constant. The manuscript distinguishes a priori first-increment bounds from a posteriori residual bounds and specifies how an approximate curvature enters the later reconstruction. The exact theorem, density-error topology and statistical exponents are unchanged.

The revised abstract and principal theorem put the relative physical law and geometric inverse together at the beginning of the paper. The full technical manuscript, principal article and companion retain every inherited proof input. No auxiliary headline theorem has been added to substitute a repair for a significance argument. The point-by-point response addresses every item of the report and distinguishes the corrected estimate from the still evaluative placement decision.

We respectfully request renewed consideration of this combined mechanism at the proposed general mathematical level. We do not regard source retention, numerical diagnostics or successful compilation as evidence of exceptional significance or as proof certificates. The versioned delivery identifies the actual compiled source and provides the complete manuscripts and reproducibility evidence for further independent scrutiny.
