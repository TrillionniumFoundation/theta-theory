\section{Signed endpoint rigidity and the all-order determinant-one inverse}
\label{sec:v27-signed-rigidity}
\label{sec:v22-signed-rigidity}

The scalar onset laws integrate out the signs of the endpoint coordinates and
therefore retain only a symmetrized profile.  The physical endpoint record is
strictly richer.  If the signed endpoint positions are retained, the support
recovers the two half-line actions before symmetrization.  We prove here that
those two actions determine the complete labelled contact jets.  The proof is
written at the level needed by the inverse theorem: the nonlinear half-line
stationarity operator is inverted quantitatively in a weighted Banach space,
the infinite action is differentiated through finite truncations, and the
first occurrence of each new graph jet is isolated by homogeneous degree.

Fix oriented transverse coordinates at the two labelled contacts.  For
$b\in\{0,1\}$ use an even number of flights and the orientation for which the
first and last impacts have type $b$.  Let
$\Pi^{\mathrm{end},\infty}_{bb,d}$ be the limiting conditional endpoint law
after residual time has been integrated out.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-main-relative}, on a common endpoint box its interior
density is proportional to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-signed-endpoint-density}
       (d-S_b(u)-S_b(v))_+B_b(u)B_b(v),
\end{equation}
where $B_b>0$.

\begin{theorem}[All-order signed-endpoint rigidity]
\label{thm:v22-signed-rigidity}
Let the two labelled facing contacts be $C^\infty$ and strictly convex.  No
reflection symmetry, equality of the two contacts, evenness, or supplied
contact curvature is assumed.  Suppose the selected channel and its signed
endpoint coordinates are retained.

There is $d_*>0$ such that the two families
\[
 \{\Pi^{\mathrm{end},\infty}_{00,d}:0<d<d_*\},\qquad
 \{\Pi^{\mathrm{end},\infty}_{11,d}:0<d<d_*\}
\]
determine the half-line actions $S_0,S_1$ on a fixed contact neighborhood.
If physical time is retained, the onset determines the gap $g$.  Hence the
gap, both contact curvatures, and every finite labelled graph jet are
determined by the signed endpoint laws.

In the fixed oriented contact frames set $q_{b,n}=\psi_b^{(n)}(0)$,
$q_{b,2}=\kappa_b$, and $q_n=(q_{0,n},q_{1,n})^t$.  For each finite
$M\ge2$ Taylor's formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-general-contact-jets}
 \psi_b(y)=\sum_{n=2}^{M}\frac{q_{b,n}}{n!}y^n+R_{b,M}(y),
 \qquad
 |\partial_y^rR_{b,M}(y)|\le C_M|y|^{M+1-r},\quad 0\le r\le M+1.
\end{equation}
Here the bound uses a $C^{M+1}$ bound on a common contact interval.
For smooth contacts no convergence of an infinite Taylor series is
asserted.  Equal finite graph jets, including arbitrary smooth remainders,
are treated in Lemma~\ref{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization} below.  Put
\[
 a_b=S_b''(0),\qquad
 s_n=(S_0^{(n)}(0),S_1^{(n)}(0))^t.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-leading-recovery}
 \gamma=\operatorname{arsinh}\!\bigl(g\sqrt{a_0a_1}\bigr),\qquad
 c=\cosh\gamma,
 \qquad
 c_0=c\sqrt{a_0/a_1},\quad c_1=c\sqrt{a_1/a_0},
 \qquad
 \kappa_b=\frac{c_b-1}{g}.
\end{equation}
For every $n\ge3$, $s_n$ depends only on the leading geometry and
$q_3,\ldots,q_n$, is affine in $q_n$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-general-jet-block}
 D_{q_n}s_n=M_n:=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak r_b=\sqrt{c_b/c_{1-b}}.
\end{equation}
The determinant of $M_n$ is exactly one.  For every fixed $M$, the finite
signed-jet forward map through order $M$ is $C^1$ with an everywhere
invertible differential; on compact positive geometric and finite-jet sets
its least singular value is bounded away from zero.  Its inverse is locally
Lipschitz there.

For real-analytic contacts, equality of the two signed endpoint-law germs as
$d\downarrow0$ and of the physical onset determines the two labelled analytic
contact germs.  If the participating obstacle boundaries are connected and
real analytic, their boundary images are determined in the fixed contact
frames.  For a smooth compact family and every fixed $M$, the corresponding
finite even-flight support families recover both graph jets through order
$M$ with error $O_M(\tau^j)$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Support thresholds and the leading geometry}

\begin{proof}[Recovery of the actions and the quadratic geometry]
Positivity of $B_b$ makes the interior support in
\eqref{eq:v22-signed-endpoint-density} exactly
\[
                  \{(u,v):S_b(u)+S_b(v)<d\}.
\]
Define
\[
 T_b(u,v)=\inf\{d>0:(u,v)\in
       \operatorname{int}\operatorname{supp}
       \Pi^{\mathrm{end},\infty}_{bb,d}\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-support-threshold}
             T_b(u,v)=S_b(u)+S_b(v),\qquad S_b(u)=T_b(u,0),
\end{equation}
because $S_b(0)=0$.  Thus the signed support recovers the unsymmetrized
action.

For a selected $j$-flight itinerary the infimum of physical times at which
the near-onset event has positive probability is $jg$, hence the time axis
gives $g$.  The half-line Hessian identity
\[
 a_b=\frac{c\sinh\gamma}{gc_{1-b}},
 \qquad c=\sqrt{c_0c_1}=\cosh\gamma
\]
gives
\[
 a_0a_1=\frac{\sinh^2\gamma}{g^2},
 \qquad \frac{a_0}{a_1}=\frac{c_0}{c_1},
\]
which is equivalent to \eqref{eq:v22-leading-recovery}.  No curvature is
supplied as an external parameter.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A quantitative weighted inverse for the nonlinear half-line}

Let $\ell_b(y,z)$ be the local flight length from a point of type $b$ with
transverse coordinate $y$ to the facing point of type $1-b$ with coordinate
$z$.  For a half-line beginning at type $b$, set $x_0=u$ and, for $i\ge1$,
write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-stationary-equations}
 \mathscr F_{b,i}(x,u;q)
 :=\partial_2\ell_{b+i-1}(x_{i-1},x_i)
  +\partial_1\ell_{b+i}(x_i,x_{i+1})=0,
\end{equation}
where contact types are read modulo two.  Fix a compact positive geometric
set.  Its hyperbolic exponent has a positive minimum $\gamma_->0$.  Choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-rho-choice}
                     e^{-\gamma_-}<\rho<1,
\end{equation}
and define
\[
 X_\rho=\left\{x=(x_i)_{i\ge1}:
       \|x\|_\rho:=\sup_{i\ge1}\rho^{-i}|x_i|<\infty\right\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Weighted Green bound and nonlinear inverse]
\label{lem:v22-weighted-inverse}
Let $H_b^0(q)=D_x\mathscr F_b(0,0;q)$ be the quadratic half-line Jacobi
operator.  Its inverse has kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-half-green}
 G^{(b)}_{ik}=\frac{g\sigma_i^{(b)}\sigma_k^{(b)}}
 {2c\sinh\gamma}
 \left(e^{-\gamma|i-k|}-e^{-\gamma(i+k)}\right),
 \qquad i,k\ge1.
\end{equation}
There is $C_0<\infty$, uniform on the compact geometric set, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-green-weighted-bound}
                  \|G^{(b)}y\|_\rho\le C_0\|y\|_\rho.
\end{equation}
After shrinking a common endpoint box, the unique stationary half-line
$x^{(b)}(u;q)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-orbit-weight}
                  \|x^{(b)}(u;q)\|_\rho\le C_1|u|,
\end{equation}
and, with
$H_b(u,q)=D_x\mathscr F_b(x^{(b)}(u;q),u;q)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-hessian-perturbation}
 \|H_b(u,q)-H_b^0(q)\|_{X_\rho\to X_\rho}\le C_2|u|.
\end{equation}
Consequently, after choosing $u_*$ so that $C_0C_2u_*\le1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-uniform-inverse}
          \|H_b(u,q)^{-1}\|_{X_\rho\to X_\rho}\le2C_0,
          \qquad |u|\le u_*.
\end{equation}
For every fixed finite collection of endpoint and geometric derivatives, the
corresponding derivatives of $x^{(b)}$ are uniformly bounded in $X_\rho$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The factors $\sigma_i^{(b)}$ take only two positive values on the compact
geometric set, so \eqref{eq:v22-half-green} gives
$|G^{(b)}_{ik}|\le C e^{-\gamma_-|i-k|}$.  If
$|y_k|\le\|y\|_\rho\rho^k$, then for $k\le i$
\[
 e^{-\gamma_-(i-k)}\rho^k
 =\rho^i(e^{-\gamma_-}/\rho)^{i-k},
\]
while for $k\ge i$
\[
 e^{-\gamma_-(k-i)}\rho^k
 =\rho^i(\rho e^{-\gamma_-})^{k-i}.
\]
Both geometric series are uniformly summable because of
\eqref{eq:v22-rho-choice}; the reflected term in
\eqref{eq:v22-half-green} is smaller.  This proves
\eqref{eq:v22-green-weighted-bound}.

The nonlinear part of \eqref{eq:v22-stationary-equations} is local and
vanishes quadratically at the zero orbit.  On a weighted ball
$\|x\|_\rho\le R|u|$, its derivative differs from $H_b^0$ by at most
$C(|u|+\|x\|_\rho)$ in $X_\rho$ operator norm.  The Green bound therefore
makes the standard fixed-point map a contraction on a common ball and gives
\eqref{eq:v22-orbit-weight}.  Substitution of that bound gives
\eqref{eq:v22-hessian-perturbation}.  Factor
\[
 H_b(u,q)=H_b^0(q)\bigl[I+G^{(b)}(H_b(u,q)-H_b^0(q))\bigr].
\]
The bracket differs from the identity by an operator of norm at most $1/2$,
so its Neumann series converges and proves \eqref{eq:v22-uniform-inverse}.

Differentiate the fixed-point or stationarity equation.  At every order the
highest orbit derivative is multiplied by the same operator $H_b(u,q)$;
all remaining terms are finite sums of local coefficient derivatives and
lower orbit derivatives.  The uniform inverse bound, the weighted product
estimate, and induction give the last assertion.  This is the quantitative
version of the half-line construction in Lemma~\ref{lem:v4-halfline}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Derivative bookkeeping, the infinite action, and the envelope limit}

The stationarity equations contain first derivatives of the local flight
lengths.  Hence differentiating them $k$ times can expose graph derivatives
of order $k+1$.  We do not infer the jet order of the action from that
stationarity count.  Instead the action filtration is proved directly from
its first variation.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-infinite-action}
 \mathscr A_b(u,q)=\sum_{i\ge0}
 \bigl[\ell_{b+i}(x_i^{(b)}(u;q),x_{i+1}^{(b)}(u;q))-g\bigr].
\end{equation}
The weighted orbit bounds imply absolute and locally uniform convergence of
this series and of every fixed finite collection of its mixed derivatives.
Its value is $S_b(u)$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-truncation envelope identity]
\label{lem:v22-envelope}
Fix a finite jet coordinate $q_{r,m}$, realized by a smooth local graph
variation which equals $y^m/m!$ in a neighborhood containing the statistical
contact patch.  Then, uniformly for $|u|\le u_*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-envelope}
 \partial_{q_{r,m}}S_b(u)
 =\sum_{i\ge0}\partial_{q_{r,m}}\ell_{b+i}
       (x_i^{(b)}(u),x_{i+1}^{(b)}(u)).
\end{equation}
The series and every fixed finite number of $u$-derivatives converge
absolutely.  In particular no derivative of the stationary orbit appears in
the right-hand side.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 A_{b,N}(u,q)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}
 \bigl[\ell_{b+i}(x_i^{(b)},x_{i+1}^{(b)})-g\bigr].
\]
Differentiate with respect to $q_{r,m}$.  The coefficients of
$\partial_{q_{r,m}}x_i$ telescope into the stationarity equations for
$1\le i\le N-1$.  They vanish.  The only orbit-variation term left is the
right boundary term
\[
 R_N=\partial_2\ell_{b+N-1}(x_{N-1}^{(b)},x_N^{(b)})
             \,\partial_{q_{r,m}}x_N^{(b)}.
\]
The first factor is $O(|u|\rho^N)$ by stationarity and the local Taylor
expansion at the zero orbit; the second is $O(\rho^N)$ after any prescribed
finite collection of $u$-derivatives, by Lemma
\ref{lem:v22-weighted-inverse}.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-tail-bound}
                |\partial_u^kR_N|\le C_k\rho^{2N}
\end{equation}
for every fixed $k$ (after harmless adjustment at $u=0$).  Thus the boundary
term tends to zero uniformly with the derivatives used below.

The direct parameter derivatives of the $i$th flight are bounded by a fixed
multiple of a summable power of $\rho^i$, because both endpoints of that
flight are $O(|u|\rho^i)$ up to a shift of one index.  The Weierstrass test
therefore permits passage to the limit $N\to\infty$ and termwise finite-order
differentiation.  This proves \eqref{eq:v22-envelope} and the asserted tail
control.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Finite smooth remainders and factorization through jets}

The coordinate variation in Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-envelope} alone does not
justify independence from all smooth remainders.  The next lemma treats
the remainder as a function, before choosing any finite jet coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[Smooth finite-jet factorization]
\label{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization}
Fix $M\ge2$.  Let $\psi^{[0]}_0,\psi^{[0]}_1$ and
$\psi^{[1]}_0,\psi^{[1]}_1$ be two smooth strictly convex anchored graph
pairs with the same gap and the same graph jets through order $M$.
On a common sufficiently small interval their half-line actions satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v27-remainder-action-bound}
 |S_b^{[1]}(u)-S_b^{[0]}(u)|
 \le C_M|u|^{M+1}\sum_{i\ge0}\rho^{(M+1)i}
 =\frac{C_M}{1-\rho^{M+1}}|u|^{M+1},\qquad b\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $j_0^MS_b^{[1]}=j_0^MS_b^{[0]}$.  The constants and contact
interval can be chosen uniformly with positive gap and curvature margins
and bounded $C^{M+2}$ graph norms on a common initial interval.  For
additional differentiated estimates, the corresponding higher smooth
bounds are required.  Bounds on a finite list of jet coefficients alone
do not supply these functional remainder bounds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $\Delta\psi_r=\psi_r^{[1]}-\psi_r^{[0]}$ and
$\psi_{r,t}=\psi_r^{[0]}+t\Delta\psi_r$, $0\le t\le1$.
Strict convexity and the anchor conditions persist along this interpolation
on a common contact interval.  Equal quadratic jets and the common gap
fix the quadratic half-line operator.  The contraction proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-weighted-inverse}, uniformly in $t$, gives stationary
orbits $x^{(b,t)}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v27-interpolated-orbit}
 |x_i^{(b,t)}(u)|\le C|u|\rho^i,\quad i\ge0,
 \qquad x_0^{(b,t)}=u.
\end{equation}
The same implicit-function argument gives $C^M$ dependence on $u$ and
smooth dependence on $t$ at the finite orders used here.  In particular
$\|\partial_t x^{(b,t)}\|_\rho$ is uniformly bounded.  These assertions
are local statements about graph pairs; no globally realized interpolation
of periodic tables is required.

For a flight of starting type $r$, write
\[
 h_{r,t}(y,z)=g+\psi_{r,t}(y)+\psi_{1-r,t}(z),\qquad
 \ell_{r,t}(y,z)=\sqrt{h_{r,t}(y,z)^2+(z-y)^2}.
\]
Taylor's remainder bound for the equal $M$-jets gives
$|\Delta\psi_r(y)|\le C_M|y|^{M+1}$.  At fixed endpoints,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v27-direct-remainder-variation}
 \partial_t\ell_{r,t}(y,z)
 =\frac{h_{r,t}(y,z)}{\ell_{r,t}(y,z)}
                 \{\Delta\psi_r(y)+\Delta\psi_{1-r}(z)\},
 \qquad
 |\partial_t\ell_{r,t}(y,z)|
 \le C_M(|y|^{M+1}+|z|^{M+1}).
\end{equation}
The ratio is between zero and one since the separation is positive.

Apply finite-truncation differentiation to
\[
 A_{b,N,t}(u)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}
       [\ell_{b+i,t}(x_i^{(b,t)},x_{i+1}^{(b,t)})-g].
\]
Interior orbit derivatives cancel by stationarity, and $\partial_t x_0=0$.
Thus its derivative is the sum of the direct variations in
\eqref{eq:v27-direct-remainder-variation}, plus exactly the boundary term
\[
 B_{N,t}=\partial_2\ell_{b+N-1,t}(x_{N-1}^{(b,t)},x_N^{(b,t)})
                                         \partial_t x_N^{(b,t)}.
\]
The first factor is $O(|u|\rho^N)$ and the second is $O(\rho^N)$;
hence $B_{N,t}\to0$ uniformly in $t$ and on the endpoint interval.
By \eqref{eq:v27-interpolated-orbit}, the direct variations are bounded
by $C_M|u|^{M+1}\rho^{(M+1)i}$, a summable majorant.  Instead of
assuming an interchange of infinite differentiation, integrate the exact
finite identity in $t$ and then let $N\to\infty$.  Uniform action
convergence, the vanishing boundary term and dominated convergence give
\[
 S_b^{[1]}(u)-S_b^{[0]}(u)
 =\int_0^1\sum_{i\ge0}
    \partial_t\ell_{b+i,t}(x_i^{(b,t)},x_{i+1}^{(b,t)})\,dt.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:v27-remainder-action-bound}.  The actions are $C^M$
by the weighted construction.  Taylor's formula then implies equality
of their derivatives at zero through order $M$: a nonzero Taylor
coefficient of degree at most $M$ would contradict the displayed
$O(|u|^{M+1})$ bound.  Every constant used is uniform under the stated
functional bounds and positive margins.
\end{proof}

In particular, the finite map from graph jets to action jets is independent
of the chosen smooth representatives.  One may compute it on polynomial
graph representatives with smooth cutoffs on a common small interval.
On a compact positive finite-jet set these representatives and intervals
can be chosen with uniform finite smooth bounds.  This is the
finite-dimensional map used below, not an identification of a smooth
function with its formal Taylor series.

A flat variation illustrates the distinction.  For sufficiently small
$\epsilon$ and a smooth cutoff equal to one near zero, the two graphs
\[
 \psi^{[0]}(y)=\frac{\kappa}{2}y^2,\qquad
 \psi^{[1]}(y)=\frac{\kappa}{2}y^2+
 \begin{cases}\epsilon e^{-1/y^2},&y\ne0,\\0,&y=0\end{cases}
\]
are strictly convex near zero and have identical jets of every finite
order, without being equal as functions.  Applied for every $M$, the
lemma shows equality of every finite action jet.  It does not assert
equality of the action functions or of the smooth boundary images.
Analytic boundary determination below uses analyticity only after the
finite-jet inverse has been established.

\begin{lemma}[Jet filtration and homogeneous isolation]
\label{lem:v22-homogeneous-isolation}
Let $m\ge3$.  Holding the lower graph jets fixed, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-variation-order}
              \partial_{q_{r,m}}S_b(u)=O(u^m).
\end{equation}
Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization}, this shows
that $S_b^{(n)}(0)$ depends only on the graph jets through order $n$,
including independence from smooth remainders and flat variations.
For $m=n$, the coefficient of $u^n$ in
$\partial_{q_{r,n}}S_b(u)$ is obtained by substituting only the linear
half-line orbit into the pure endpoint monomials.  The error is
$O(u^{n+1})$, uniformly on compact smooth classes with the required
finite derivative bounds.  The resulting finite-jet coefficient is
uniformly controlled on compact positive finite-jet sets.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a flight joining transverse coordinates $y,z$, let $h(y,z)$ denote its
positive horizontal separation.  In the anchored local graph representation
its length is
\[
             \ell(y,z)=\sqrt{h(y,z)^2+(z-y)^2}.
\]
If the varied $m$th graph jet occurs at the $y$ endpoint, then, on the region
where the cutoff equals one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-flight-jet-expansion}
 \partial_{q_{r,m}}\ell(y,z)
 =\frac{h(y,z)}{\ell(y,z)}\frac{y^m}{m!}
 =\frac{y^m}{m!}+O((|y|+|z|)^{m+2});
\end{equation}
for a variation at the $z$ endpoint the same formula holds with $z^m$.
Indeed $h/\ell=1+O((|y|+|z|)^2)$ at the normal contact.  The signs are
positive for both facing graphs because increasing either inward graph height
increases the horizontal separation.

Lemma~\ref{lem:v4-halfline} and Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-weighted-inverse} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-linear-orbit-precise}
 x_i^{(b)}(u)=t_i^{(b)}u+R_i^{(b)}(u),
 \qquad |R_i^{(b)}(u)|\le C|u|^2\rho^i,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-linear-coefficients}
 t_i^{(b)}=
 \begin{cases}
 e^{-i\gamma},&i\text{ even},\\
 \mathfrak r_b e^{-i\gamma},&i\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Hence
$(x_i^{(b)}(u))^m=(t_i^{(b)})^mu^m+O(u^{m+1}\rho^{mi})$.
Insert this and \eqref{eq:v22-flight-jet-expansion} into the absolutely
convergent envelope sum \eqref{eq:v22-envelope}.  Every summand is
$O(u^m\rho^{mi})$, proving \eqref{eq:v22-variation-order}.  At degree $m$
only the first term of \eqref{eq:v22-flight-jet-expansion} and the linear
orbit in \eqref{eq:v22-linear-coefficients} survive; every nonlinear orbit
correction raises the endpoint degree by at least one.  Summability is
uniform, so the total remainder is $O(u^{m+1})$.
\end{proof}

This lemma is the derivative-order bookkeeping needed by the arbitrary-order
inverse.  The orbit derivative of order $k$ may depend on a $(k+1)$st graph
derivative, but the degree-$n$ action jet cannot: a graph variation of order
$m>n$ starts at endpoint degree $m$ in the exact envelope formula.

\subsection{The determinant-one block at every degree}

\begin{proposition}[Last-jet block]
\label{prop:v22-last-jet-block}
For every $n\ge3$, after the lower graph jets are fixed, $s_n$ is affine in
$q_n$ and its derivative is the matrix $M_n$ in
\eqref{eq:v22-general-jet-block}.  In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-last-jet-det}
                              \det M_n=1.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-homogeneous-isolation}, the degree-$n$ first variation
is the pure endpoint-power sum on the linear orbit.  A boundary site is
counted once and every interior site twice.  For a half-line starting at type
$b$, varying the $n$th jet of the starting contact gives
\begin{align}
 \partial_{q_{b,n}}S_b^{(n)}(0)
 &=1+2\sum_{k\ge1}e^{-2n\gamma k}
   =\frac{1+e^{-2n\gamma}}{1-e^{-2n\gamma}}
   =\coth(n\gamma),                         \label{eq:v22-own-sum}
\end{align}
whereas varying the opposite contact gives
\begin{align}
 \partial_{q_{1-b,n}}S_b^{(n)}(0)
 &=2\mathfrak r_b^n\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-n\gamma(2k+1)}
   =\mathfrak r_b^n\csch(n\gamma).          \label{eq:v22-cross-sum}
\end{align}
All multiplicities are explicit and the sums converge absolutely.
Interchanging the labels gives the second row of
\eqref{eq:v22-general-jet-block}.  Since
$\mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1=1$,
\[
 \det M_n
 =\coth^2(n\gamma)
  -(\mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1)^n\csch^2(n\gamma)
 =1.
\]
The derivative of $s_n$ with respect to $q_n$ is independent of $q_n$:
the linear orbit depends only on the quadratic geometry and the degree-$n$
homogeneous coefficient above contains no higher occurrence of $q_n$.
Therefore the dependence is affine.
\end{proof}

The inverse block is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-general-recursion}
 M_n^{-1}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&-\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 -\mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
If $R_n$ is the contribution of the already reconstructed lower jets, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-recursion}
                         q_n=M_n^{-1}(s_n-R_n).
\end{equation}
For fixed maximal order $M$, all coefficients and derivatives in this finite
recursion are uniformly bounded on compact positive geometric and finite-jet
sets.

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative tangent isomorphism]
\label{prop:v22-tangent-jet-isomorphism}
Fix $g>0$ and $M\ge2$, and define
\[
 \mathscr A_M:
 (\kappa_0,\kappa_1,q_3,\ldots,q_M)
 \longmapsto(s_2,s_3,\ldots,s_M),
 \qquad s_2=(a_0,a_1)^t.
\]
Then $\mathscr A_M$ is $C^1$ and $D\mathscr A_M$ is an isomorphism at every
strictly convex labelled contact jet.  On every compact positive geometric
and finite-jet set there is $c_M>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-tangent-lower-bound}
                    \|D\mathscr A_M[h]\|\ge c_M\|h\|.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization} makes $\mathscr A_M$
well defined independently of smooth representatives.  Choose the local
polynomial representatives just described.  The weighted implicit-function
construction and differentiated action bounds give $C^1$ dependence on
their finite jet coordinates; the result is independent of this choice.  Formula \eqref{eq:v22-leading-recovery} is a smooth inverse of
the quadratic map
$(\kappa_0,\kappa_1)\mapsto(a_0,a_1)$ at fixed $g$.  This gives an invertible
leading $2\times2$ diagonal block.  For $n\ge3$ the new pair $q_n$ enters
$s_n$ through the diagonal block $M_n$, while all dependence on lower jets is
strictly below the block diagonal.  Hence $D\mathscr A_M$ is block lower
triangular with invertible diagonal blocks.  The least singular value is a
positive continuous function on a compact set, which proves
\eqref{eq:v22-tangent-lower-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Completion of Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity}]
The support threshold and quadratic recovery were proved first.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v27-smooth-jet-factorization} proves independence from
arbitrary smooth remainders at each finite order.  Lemmas
\ref{lem:v22-weighted-inverse}--\ref{lem:v22-homogeneous-isolation} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:v22-last-jet-block} give the all-order recursion
\eqref{eq:v22-recursion}; Proposition
\ref{prop:v22-tangent-jet-isomorphism} gives the quantitative tangent inverse.

For analytic contacts, equality of all recovered labelled jets gives equality
of the convergent graph germs.  Parametrize two candidate connected analytic
boundaries by oriented arclength from the recovered contact frame.  Their
analytic curvature functions agree on an interval and hence everywhere by
the identity theorem; uniqueness for the planar Frenet equations gives equal
boundary images.  This is exact analytic continuation, not a stability claim
for distant boundary points.

For the actual finite even-flight law, positivity of the physical flux makes
its endpoint support threshold the exact stationary excess action
$E_{j,b}(u,v)$.  The differentiated relative estimate gives
\[
 \|E_{j,b}-(S_b\oplus S_b)\|_{C^M}\le C_M\tau^j.
\]
Taking the slice $v=0$ and applying the finite-order locally Lipschitz inverse
proves the $O_M(\tau^j)$ reconstruction statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Oriented intrinsic meaning]
Odd graph jets change sign when the chosen transverse orientation is
reversed.  Equivalently, the recovered data determine the full oriented
curvature jet with respect to arclength at each labelled contact.  Thus the
inverse is intrinsic after the labelled contact point, tangent line, and an
orientation of that line have been fixed; no reflection convention is hidden
in the theorem.
\end{remark}
