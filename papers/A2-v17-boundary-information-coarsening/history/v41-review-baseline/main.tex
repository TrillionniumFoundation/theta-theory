\begin{abstract}
We determine the two labelled contact jets of a smooth dispersing billiard
channel from its gap and the signed endpoint law of each contact type at
one fixed positive offset.  The laws arise from long alternating collision
bridges.  On a collar independent of the flight number, the normalized
mixed endpoint derivative has a nonlinear relative limit with every fixed
mixed geometric and offset derivative.  A four-density identity cancels
the unknown flux amplitudes and recovers the unsymmetrized half-line
actions.  A weighted nonlinear half-line inverse, finite-truncation
envelope differentiation with smooth finite remainders, and homogeneous
degree isolation then recover the contact jets through explicit
two-by-two blocks of determinant one.  No reflection symmetry, equal
contacts, or supplied curvatures are assumed.

For connected real-analytic boundaries, intrinsic channel data determine
complete obstacle images in their separate frames.  Signature-rigid incidence
matching and a rank-two anchoring pair based at one channel frame recover
the Euclidean period lattice, including its Gram form and scale.  A rooted
signature-rigid spanning tree places every remaining obstacle orbit.  On compact analytic
classes we obtain stable unique finite-signature matching and a uniform
finite-data inverse modulus.  The common-orientation orbit of the entire
marked signed datum yields the corresponding classification up to one global
Euclidean isometry; this orbit is distinct from pointwise sign erasure.

The local information depends on the retained record.  Endpoint--residual-time
observations are equivalent to a moving-ceiling Poisson experiment, with
explicit kernels controlling layer, bulk, and corner errors.  Removing
residual time gives a linearly vanishing endpoint density and a nonregular
Gaussian limit; waiting counts supply a separate faster normal information
channel.  In the anchored finite-dimensional local models, the endpoint and
count--endpoint Gaussian approximations hold in Le Cam distance on every
fixed compact local parameter set.  These scalar and finite-strip Poisson
families have common dominating measures, although a designated reference
member need not dominate them.  For anchored homothetic channels, the
information loss from deleting normal endpoint coordinates is maximal when
the observed contact varies, whereas observing only the fixed contact gives
equivalent matched endpoint experiments.

Finally, a charged near-onset pilot and single-offset transverse-law tests
give uniform global reconstruction from common physical position records,
using only even bridges whose minimum flight number diverges.  We also prove
a direct graph-interpolation benchmark in the same position experiment.
This separates its noiseless geometric sampling from the intrinsic inverse
based only on transverse laws.  All preparation failures are charged, and
no finite-dimensional parametrization of the compact analytic class is used.
\end{abstract}
