\section{A physical endpoint experiment on fixed laboratory windows}
\label{sec:v22-raw-physical}

We now realize the boundary-information theorem on a parameter-independent
physical observation space.  Three points are fixed at the outset.  First,
all acquisition times are programmed from the reference channel and are
unchanged under local alternatives.  Second, the statistical theorem is
formulated for the anchored registered family of
Section~\ref{sec:v22-anchored-realization}, in one laboratory chart.  Third,
we distinguish the sigma-fields used at the successive transfer steps.  The
finite-to-boundary theorem does not compare the complete growing collision
array.

\subsection{The declared physical record hierarchy}
\label{subsec:v22-record-hierarchy}

A physical preparation has a full raw collision history, denoted
$\mathcal R^{\rm full}$, consisting of all collision points, obstacle labels,
flight times and collision count observed during that preparation.  We use
three successively coarser records.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The \emph{per-preparation endpoint--time record} is the measurable
coarsening
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-per-prep-coarsening}
 \mathcal C_{j,t}(\mathcal R^{\rm full})=
 \begin{cases}
 (y_{\rm first},y_{\rm last},r),&\text{if the prescribed bridge succeeds},\\
 \dagger,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $r$ is the residual preparation-to-first-impact time used in
Section~\ref{sec:v6-transfer}.  This is the record for which the historical
finite-to-boundary total-variation theorem is proved.

\item A policy produces the \emph{stopped endpoint--time acquisition
transcript}: every chosen design, every record
\eqref{eq:v22-per-prep-coarsening}, the success/failure sequence and the
stopping time are retained.  The adaptive coupling theorem of
Section~\ref{sec:v16-adaptive} compares the finite and boundary laws of this
entire stopped transcript at the declared record level.

\item The \emph{endpoint-output experiment} is a further common Markov
coarsening which retains the selected endpoint pairs needed for inference and
the cap cemetery symbol.  The Gaussian information theorem below is a theorem
for this endpoint-output sigma-field.
\end{enumerate}

The complete raw collision history $\mathcal R^{\rm full}$ may contain more
information than item 1, and the stopped endpoint--time transcript may contain
more information than item 3 through the waiting counts.  No theorem below
identifies these different sigma-fields.

\subsection{An anchored registered channel in one laboratory chart}

Fix once and for all a Euclidean chart $(x,y)$, two obstacle labels, and two
facing patches.  In this chart the admissible local family has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-anchored-graphs}
 \Gamma_{0,\vartheta}=\{(-\psi_{0,\vartheta}(u),u):|u|<u_*\},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{1,g,\vartheta}=\{(g+\psi_{1,\vartheta}(v),v):|v|<u_*\},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-anchor-normalization}
 \psi_{b,\vartheta}(0)=\psi'_{b,\vartheta}(0)=0,
 \qquad \psi''_{b,\vartheta}(0)>0.
\end{equation}
The labels, transverse origin and tangent direction are fixed throughout the
family.  Varying $g$ translates the second graph in the normal $x$ direction;
it does not re-center the observed transverse coordinate.  The local
parameter $\vartheta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb R^r$ changes only the anchored
graph geometry.  For the finite-dimensional theorem below, $\vartheta$ is a
finite labelled contact-jet coordinate realized by
Proposition~\ref{prop:v22-anchored-realization}.

The endpoint observation used after the residual-time coarsening is the same
measurable map at every parameter:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-laboratory-endpoint-map}
 \mathcal O_{bb}(\mathcal R^{\rm full})=
 \begin{cases}
 (y_{\rm first},y_{\rm last}),&\text{if the prescribed $bb$ bridge succeeds},\\
 \dagger,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus endpoint positions are literal laboratory $y$ coordinates rather than
parameter-dependent contact coordinates.

Let $S_{b,g,\vartheta}$ and $B_{b,g,\vartheta}$ denote the half-line actions
and positive amplitudes constructed in the same fixed transverse coordinate.
At $(g_0,0)$ write
\[
 S_b=S_{b,g_0,0},\qquad B_b=B_{b,g_0,0},\qquad
 \dot S_b(u)h=D_\vartheta S_{b,g_0,\vartheta}(u)|_0[h].
\]
The anchoring gives
$S_{b,g,\vartheta}(0)=S'_{b,g,\vartheta}(0)=0$ throughout the family.

Fix finitely many same-type designs $(b_\ell,d_\ell)$ with
$b_\ell\in\{0,1\}$ and $0<d_\ell<d_*$, $1\le\ell\le L$.  A same-type design
is always implemented with an \emph{even} number of alternating flights.
For even $j$ its programmed acquisition time is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-fixed-physical-window}
                         t_{j,\ell}=jg_0+d_\ell.
\end{equation}
The parity convention is part of the physical design: an odd flight number
would terminate at the opposite contact and would instead involve the mixed
action $S_b\oplus S_{1-b}$.

\subsection{The multirate local parameter and fixed-time expansion}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-even-flight-sequence}
                 j_n\in2\mathbb N,\qquad j_n\longrightarrow\infty,
\end{equation}
and let $\delta_n\downarrow0$.  For bounded
$z=(a,h)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^r$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-multirate-parameter}
 g_n(a)=g_0+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n}a,
 \qquad
 \vartheta_n(h)=\delta_nh.
\end{equation}
The fixed laboratory time has exact excess
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-raw-excess}
       t_{j_n,\ell}-j_ng_n(a)=d_\ell-\delta_na.
\end{equation}
Thus a gap displacement of order $\delta_n/j_n$ and a shape displacement of
order $\delta_n$ enter the moving endpoint support at the same first order.

For a same-type design $(b,d)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-base-defining-function}
                    w_{b,d}(u,v)=d-S_b(u)-S_b(v).
\end{equation}
Let $A_{b,d}>0$ be the normalized smooth amplitude, so the base conditional
endpoint density is
$f_{b,d}=A_{b,d}(w_{b,d})_+$.  Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-support-velocity}
 U_b(u,v)z=a+\dot S_b(u)h+\dot S_b(v)h
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-information-matrix}
 \mathcal K_{b,d}
 =\int_{\{S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d\}}
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)\,U_b(u,v)^tU_b(u,v)}
 {|\nabla(S_b\oplus S_b)(u,v)|}\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}

The derivatives $\dot S_b$ in this display are the historical half-line
derivatives in the fixed transverse coordinate at fixed reference gap.  The
physical acquisition time itself is held fixed.  Its gap variation is the
explicit term $-\delta_na$ in \eqref{eq:v22-raw-excess}; the remaining smooth
gap dependence of the half-line action is only
$(\delta_na/j_n)\partial_gS_b$ and belongs to the quantitative remainder
below.  Thus channel-centered action differentiation is not confused with
differentiation of the laboratory time.

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative fixed-window reduction]
\label{prop:v22-fixed-window-reduction}
For every compact local set $K\subset\mathbb R^{1+r}$, the exact limiting
boundary endpoint density at \eqref{eq:v22-fixed-physical-window} can be
written
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-exact-endpoint-density}
 f^{\rm ex}_{n,z}(u,v)
 =A^{\rm ex}_{n,z}(u,v)(w^{\rm ex}_{n,z}(u,v))_+,
 \qquad z\in K,
\end{equation}
where, uniformly on the common endpoint box,
\begin{align}
 \bigl\|w^{\rm ex}_{n,z}
 -(w_{b,d}-\delta_nU_bz)\bigr\|_{C^2}
 &\le C_Kr_n,
 &r_n&:=\delta_n^2+\frac{\delta_n}{j_n},
                                      \label{eq:v22-support-remainder}\\
 \|A^{\rm ex}_{n,z}-A_{b,d}\|_{C^1}
 &\le C_K\delta_n.                  \label{eq:v22-amplitude-remainder}
\end{align}
Let $\widetilde f_{n,z}$ be the normalized ideal density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-ideal-endpoint-density}
 \widetilde f_{n,z}(u,v)=
 \frac{A_{b,d}(u,v)(w_{b,d}(u,v)-\delta_nU_b(u,v)z)_+}
 {\displaystyle\int A_{b,d}
  (w_{b,d}-\delta_nU_bz)_+\,du\,dv}.
\end{equation}
Then, with the Hellinger convention
\eqref{eq:v22-hellinger-convention},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-exact-ideal-hellinger}
 \sup_{z\in K}H^2(f^{\rm ex}_{n,z},\widetilde f_{n,z})
 \le C_K\left\{r_n^2\log\frac e{r_n}+\delta_n^2\right\}.
\end{equation}
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-critical-success-scale}
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1,
\end{equation}
then the exact and ideal $k_n$-record endpoint experiments have Le Cam
distance $o(1)$, uniformly on $K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:v22-raw-excess} supplies the exact fixed-time contribution
$-\delta_na$.  Smooth dependence of the half-line action on
$(g,\vartheta)$ gives, with two endpoint derivatives and uniformly on $K$,
\[
 S_{b,g_n(a),\vartheta_n(h)}
 =S_b+\delta_n\dot S_bh
 +\frac{\delta_na}{j_n}\partial_gS_b
 +O_K(\delta_n^2+\delta_n^2/j_n^2).
\]
Substitution at both endpoints proves
\eqref{eq:v22-support-remainder}; smooth dependence of the flux amplitude and
its normalizing constant gives \eqref{eq:v22-amplitude-remainder}.

The ideal normalizing denominator is $1+O_K(\delta_n)$.  Lemma
\ref{lem:v22-hypersurface-stability} with support error $C_Kr_n$ and amplitude
error $C_K\delta_n$ proves \eqref{eq:v22-exact-ideal-hellinger}.  The product
error is negligible at \eqref{eq:v22-critical-success-scale}: if
$j_n^{-1}\le\delta_n$, then $r_n\le2\delta_n^2$ and
$k_nr_n^2\log(e/r_n)=O(\delta_n^2)$; if
$j_n^{-1}>\delta_n$, then $r_n\le2\delta_n/j_n$ and the same quantity is
$O(j_n^{-2})$.  The amplitude term is
$k_n\delta_n^2=O(1/\log(1/\delta_n))$.  Product Hellinger subadditivity and
$\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}\le H(P,Q)$ up to the fixed convention give the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite positive designs from the tangent inverse}

\begin{lemma}[Finite positive-offset design]
\label{lem:v22-finite-positive-design}
Fix a finite labelled contact-jet order $M$.  Let $\vartheta$ consist of the
two labelled contact jets through order $M$ at fixed gap in the anchored
chart.  There exist finitely many same-type designs $(b_\ell,d_\ell)$ and
positive weights $\alpha_\ell$, $\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell=1$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-design-information}
 \mathcal K_{\rm design}
 =\sum_{\ell=1}^L\alpha_\ell\mathcal K_{b_\ell,d_\ell}
\end{equation}
is positive definite on the full local coordinate $(a,h)$.  The finite design
may depend on $M$ and on the reference geometry; no universal finite design
for the infinite jet or for all reference tables is asserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $z=(a,h)$ lies in the kernel of $\mathcal K_{b,d}$ for both labels and
all sufficiently small $d>0$.  Positivity of the weight in
\eqref{eq:v22-information-matrix} and continuity imply
\[
 a+\dot S_b(u)h+\dot S_b(v)h=0
 \quad\text{whenever}\quad S_b(u)+S_b(v)=d.
\]
Take $v=0$ and $d=S_b(u)$.  Anchoring gives
$\dot S_b(0)h=0$, so $a+\dot S_b(u)h=0$.  Letting $u\to0$ gives $a=0$;
hence $\dot S_b(u)h=0$ for both labels near zero.  All action-jet variations
through order $M$ vanish, and Proposition
\ref{prop:v22-tangent-jet-isomorphism} gives $h=0$.  Thus the common kernel is
trivial.  Every unit vector is detected by some positive design; compactness
of the unit sphere selects finitely many.  Any positive convex combination of
these designs is positive definite.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A single deterministic cap sequence for all compact local sets}

Let $p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)$ be the one-preparation success probability in
the boundary model at design $\ell$, with the fixed even flight number $j_n$.
We use one reference-based cap sequence rather than a cap chosen after a
compact local set has been specified.

\begin{lemma}[Reference-based cap]
\label{lem:v22-reference-cap}
Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-slow-hyperbolic-variation}
                         j_n\delta_n\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Then, for every fixed compact local set $K$ and each design $\ell$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-success-ratio}
 \sup_{z\in K}
 \left|\frac{p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)}
              {p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)}-1\right|\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Choose, before quantifying over $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-cap-choice}
 N_{n,\ell}=
 \left\lceil\frac{4k_{n,\ell}}{p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Then for every fixed compact $K$ there is $n_K$ such that, for
$n\ge n_K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-cap-uniform}
 \inf_{z\in K}N_{n,\ell}p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)
 \ge2k_{n,\ell}.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-cap-tail}
 \sup_{z\in K}\Pr_z\{\text{cap reached before }k_{n,\ell}
 \text{ successes}\}\le e^{-c k_{n,\ell}}
\end{equation}
for all large $n$, with the same cap sequence for every compact $K$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The boundary success law has the form, uniformly on compact local parameter
sets,
\[
 p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)
 =C_\ell(z,n)\,[d_\ell+O_K(\delta_n)]^2
   \exp\{-j_n\gamma(z,n)\},
\]
where $C_\ell(z,n)/C_\ell(0,n)=1+O_K(\delta_n)$ and
$\gamma(z,n)-\gamma(0,n)=O_K(\delta_n)$.  This is the uniform threshold law
and smooth parameter dependence of its prefactor.  Therefore
\[
 \log\frac{p^\partial_{n,\ell}(z)}
              {p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)}
 =O_K(\delta_n)+O_K(j_n\delta_n)=o(1),
\]
uniformly on $K$, proving \eqref{eq:v22-success-ratio}.  In particular the
ratio is at least $1/2$ for all $z\in K$ and all $n\ge n_K$.  Substituting
\eqref{eq:v22-cap-choice} proves \eqref{eq:v22-cap-uniform}.  A standard
binomial Chernoff bound then gives \eqref{eq:v22-cap-tail}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The boundary endpoint-output Gaussian experiment}

Choose a finite design from Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-finite-positive-design}.  Let
$k_{n,\ell}$ be target success counts and put
$k_n=\sum_\ell k_{n,\ell}$.  Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-batch-rate}
 \frac{k_{n,\ell}}{k_n}\to\alpha_\ell,
 \qquad
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1,
 \qquad
 j_n\delta_n\to0,
 \qquad j_n\in2\mathbb N.
\end{equation}
Use the fixed cap sequence \eqref{eq:v22-cap-choice}.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-window multirate endpoint Gaussian experiment]
\label{thm:v22-fixed-window-gaussian}
The capped boundary endpoint-output experiment for $z=(a,h)$ is, uniformly on
every fixed compact local parameter set, at $o(1)$ Le Cam distance from a
dominated uniformly LAN representative with information matrix
$\mathcal K_{\rm design}$.  Equivalently, its finite local subexperiments have
the Gaussian limit with that positive-definite information matrix.

After applying to each design the common-collar censoring of
Section~\ref{sec:v22-vector-information}, the representative has central
sequence $\Delta_n\Rightarrow
N_{1+r}(0,\mathcal K_{\rm design})$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-physical-lan-representative}
 \log\frac{d\overline{\mathsf Q}_{n,z}^{\rm end}}
          {d\overline{\mathsf Q}_{n,0}^{\rm end}}
 =z^t\Delta_n-\frac12z^t\mathcal K_{\rm design}z+o_P(1)
\end{equation}
uniformly on compact $z$-sets.  No finite-sample likelihood ratio is asserted
for the original endpoint-output family when its support extends beyond the
null support.

The gap is visible at rate $\delta_n/j_n$ and the anchored registered contact
geometry at rate $\delta_n$.  The Gaussian testing profile, reference-model
central-sequence estimator and local asymptotic minimax conclusions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-vector-boundary-gaussian} hold with
$\mathcal J_\Sigma$ replaced by $\mathcal K_{\rm design}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Before applying the cap, Proposition~\ref{prop:v22-fixed-window-reduction}
puts each exact boundary endpoint experiment at $o(1)$ Le Cam distance from
the ideal linearly moving-support experiment.  Theorem
\ref{thm:v22-vector-boundary-gaussian} applies to the latter, with boundary
velocity $U_b$ and information matrix $\mathcal K_{b,d}$.  Independent design
batches add their central sequences and information matrices; the proportions
$k_{n,\ell}/k_n\to\alpha_\ell$ give
$\mathcal K_{\rm design}$, which is positive definite by Lemma
\ref{lem:v22-finite-positive-design}.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-reference-cap}
changes the experiment by $o(1)$ in total variation on every fixed compact
set, using the same deterministic cap sequence independently of that set.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transfer to actual long finite bridges at the declared record level}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform stopped finite-to-boundary transfer]
\label{thm:v22-stopped-local-transfer}
Let $\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin,ET}$ be the law of the stopped
endpoint--time acquisition transcript of item 2 in
Section~\ref{subsec:v22-record-hierarchy}, under the deterministic caps,
fixed physical times and target success counts above.  Thus it retains every
design, every success or failure record
\eqref{eq:v22-per-prep-coarsening}, and the stopping time.  Let
$\mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial,ET}$ be the boundary transcript generated by the
same policy on the same laboratory record space.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-transfer-condition}
                         k_n\tau^{j_n}\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
then, for every fixed compact local set $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-lecam-transfer}
 \sup_{z\in K}
 \|\mathsf P_{n,z}^{\rm fin,ET}-
   \mathsf Q_{n,z}^{\partial,ET}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}=o(1).
\end{equation}
The bound survives every common Markov coarsening.  In particular the actual
finite-bridge endpoint-output experiment inherits
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-fixed-window-gaussian}.

This theorem makes no total-variation comparison of the full raw collision
histories $\mathcal R^{\rm full}$.  No failed preparation is free.  If
$T_{n,\ell}$ is the number of raw preparations in batch $\ell$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-physical-cost}
 \mathbb E_zT_{n,\ell}
 \le \frac{k_{n,\ell}}{p_{n,\ell}(z)}
 \le C_Kk_{n,\ell}d_\ell^{-2}e^{j_n\gamma_0}
\end{equation}
uniformly on $K$ for all large $n$; the deterministic cap gives an absolute
upper bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For bounded local $z$, the excess \eqref{eq:v22-raw-excess} stays in a common
compact subcollar and the anchored graphs remain in a compact smooth family.
The policy satisfies the deterministic bounded-stopping hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer}.  At each preparation, that theorem
uses exactly the per-preparation endpoint--time coarsening of
Section~\ref{sec:v6-transfer}; it does not compare the full collision array.
Its success-weighted coupling gives
$C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}$ for the entire stopped transcript at this declared record
level.  Total variation contracts under a common Markov kernel, proving the
endpoint-output statement.  The cost estimate follows from domination by the
uncapped negative-binomial stopping time and the uniform success law.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Endpoint information is not complete physical information]
\label{rem:v22-endpoint-coarsening}
The matrix $\mathcal K_{\rm design}$ characterizes the endpoint-output
experiment of item 3.  It is not an efficiency bound for every procedure
based on the stopped endpoint--time transcript of item 2, and still less for
the full raw collision history.  The success probability contains the factor
$\sinh(j\gamma)^{-1}$, so waiting counts can carry a different and possibly
faster information channel in directions changing the hyperbolic exponent.
Charging preparation cost is not the same as proving those counts ancillary.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A charged pilot for centered windows}

\begin{corollary}[Pilot-centered endpoint-output equivalence]
\label{cor:v22-pilot-centered}
Assume the coarse-bracket hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-self-calibration}.  Let $r_n\downarrow0$ and let an
independent pilot produce $\widehat g_n$ with
\[
 j_n|\widehat g_n-g|\le Cr_n^{m+1}
\]
with probability $1-\eta_n$, where $\eta_n\to0$.  Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-pilot-smallness}
 k_nr_n^{2m+2}\log(1/r_n)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
After the pilot use the common data-dependent rule
$t_{n,\ell}=j_n\widehat g_n+d_\ell$ and the same reference-based capped
success targets.  On the post-pilot endpoint-output sigma-field alone, this
experiment is at vanishing Le Cam distance from the oracle centered endpoint
experiment using $t_{n,\ell}=j_ng+d_\ell$.  Including the finite-bridge
replacement adds at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-pilot-total-error}
                    \eta_n+C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}+o(1).
\end{equation}
The pilot transcript and programmed times are used operationally and their
costs are charged, but no equivalence is claimed after those richer random
coordinates are added to the statistical output.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On the good pilot event the post-pilot excess differs from its oracle value by
at most $Cr_n^{m+1}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-hypersurface-stability} gives
one-record squared Hellinger distance at most
$Cr_n^{2m+2}\log(1/r_n)$, uniformly over the finite design.  Product
Hellinger bounds and \eqref{eq:v22-pilot-smallness} give $o(1)$ for endpoint
output.  Add $\eta_n$ for the bad pilot event and use
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-stopped-local-transfer} for the finite-bridge
replacement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Generic nearby tables}

The anchored model is a physical submodel, not a parameter-dependent
coordinate convention.  A generic nearby-table family can still be described
on one laboratory record space, but translations and rotations of the contact
frames become nuisance directions.  If $\chi_{b,\eta}$ maps a fixed
laboratory transverse coordinate $y$ to the contact-centered coordinate for
table parameter $\eta$, then
\[
 \mathscr S_{b,\eta}(y)=S_{b,\eta}(\chi_{b,\eta}(y))
\]
and, at a reference with $\chi_{b,0}(y)=y$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v22-frame-motion-term}
 D_\eta\mathscr S_b(y)[h]
 =D_\eta S_b(y)[h]+S_b'(y)D_\eta\chi_b(y)[h].
\end{equation}
The abstract non-dominated theorem of
Section~\ref{sec:v22-vector-information} applies once these nuisance
coordinates and their full laboratory support velocity are included.  We do
not identify the anchored information matrix with the generic-table one after
silently removing those directions.  A charged registration pilot with
coordinate error $\varepsilon_n$ is negligible for endpoint output whenever
$k_n\varepsilon_n^2\log(1/\varepsilon_n)\to0$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-hypersurface-stability}.
