\section{Waiting counts and endpoint information after residual-time coarsening}
\label{sec:v23-count-endpoint}

Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-complete-et} identifies the fastest local experiment
when residual time is retained.  We now remove residual time but retain the
waiting counts.  The positive-density ceiling is thereby integrated out.
Two information mechanisms remain: the rare-event probability detects the
hyperbolic exponent, while the endpoint mark detects motion of its linearly
vanishing support.  We prove that their product is a Gaussian experiment in
\emph{two-sided Le Cam distance on every fixed compact local parameter set}.

Fix one anchored labelled channel and a finite contact-jet model of order
\(M\ge2\).  Let \(\vartheta\in\mathbb R^{r_M}\) be its shape coordinate at
fixed gap, and let \(\gamma(g,\vartheta)\) be the channel hyperbolic exponent.
At the reference point \((g_0,0)\), put
\[
 \lambda=D_\vartheta\gamma(g_0,0),\qquad H_\gamma=\ker\lambda.
\]
The coordinate contains the two contact curvatures, on which \(\gamma\)
depends nontrivially, so \(\lambda\ne0\).  Choose
\(v_\gamma\in\mathbb R^{r_M}\) with \(\lambda(v_\gamma)=1\).
This choice gives a fast hyperbolic direction and an iso-hyperbolic tangent
space; changing the complement merely changes the local coordinates.

Choose the finite positive design of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-finite-positive-design}, with target counts
\(k_{n,\ell}\), total \(k_n=\sum_\ell k_{n,\ell}\), and proportions
\(k_{n,\ell}/k_n\to\alpha_\ell>0\).  Let \(\delta_n\downarrow0\) satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-count-endpoint-critical}
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\longrightarrow1.
\end{equation}
For bounded \((b,a,h)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\times H_\gamma\), set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-count-endpoint-parameter}
 \eta_n=(j_n\sqrt{k_n})^{-1},\qquad
 g_n(a)=g_0+\delta_na/j_n,\qquad
 \vartheta_n(b,h)=\eta_nbv_\gamma+\delta_nh.
\end{equation}
All physical windows remain \(t_{j_n,\ell}=j_ng_0+d_\ell\), and the caps
are the parameter-independent reference caps
\eqref{eq:v22-cap-choice}.  In batch \(\ell\), retain the waiting count
\(T_{n,\ell}\) and the successful endpoint pairs, with the cap cemetery
convention.  This is a common coarsening of the stopped endpoint--time
transcript, not of an experiment in which preparation failures are free.

\begin{lemma}[Negative-binomial hyperbolic score]
\label{lem:v23-negative-binomial-score}
Suppose \(j_n\to\infty\) and \(j_n\delta_n\to0\).  Uniformly on compact
\((b,a,h)\)-sets,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-success-log-expansion}
 \log\frac{p^\partial_{n,\ell}(b,a,h)}
          {p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0,0,0)}
 =-b/\sqrt{k_n}+o(k_n^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
For the uncapped boundary waiting experiment, define under its reference law
\[
 S_{n,\ell}=
 \frac{k_{n,\ell}-p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)T_{n,\ell}}
      {1-p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)},
 \qquad Z_n=-k_n^{-1/2}\sum_{\ell=1}^L S_{n,\ell}.
\]
Then \(Z_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-count-lan}
 \log\frac{d\mathsf W_{n,b,a,h}}{d\mathsf W_{n,0,0,0}}
 =bZ_n-\tfrac12b^2+o_P(1),
\end{equation}
uniformly on compact sets.  The slow coordinates contribute no limiting
count score.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the fixed positive offset collar the success probability has a smooth
positive prefactor times \(\exp\{-j_n\gamma(g,\vartheta)\}\); the
logarithmic derivatives of the prefactor are uniformly bounded.  Taylor
expansion of \(\gamma\), using \(\lambda(h)=0\), gives
\[
 \gamma(g_n(a),\vartheta_n(b,h))-
 \gamma(g_0,0)
 =\eta_nb+O_K(\delta_n/j_n)
       +O_K(\delta_n^2+\delta_n\eta_n+\eta_n^2).
\]
Consequently, the remainder in the success log ratio after the term
\(-b/\sqrt{k_n}\) is bounded by
\[
 C_K(\delta_n+\eta_n+j_n\delta_n^2
                    +j_n\delta_n\eta_n+j_n\eta_n^2).
\]
After multiplication by \(\sqrt{k_n}\), these terms tend to zero:
\(\sqrt{k_n}\delta_n\to0\),
\(\sqrt{k_n}\eta_n=1/j_n\to0\),
\(\sqrt{k_n}j_n\delta_n^2
=(j_n\delta_n)(\sqrt{k_n}\delta_n)\to0\),
\(\sqrt{k_n}j_n\delta_n\eta_n=\delta_n\to0\), and
\(\sqrt{k_n}j_n\eta_n^2=\eta_n\to0\).
This proves \eqref{eq:v23-success-log-expansion} without discarding the mixed
fast--slow Taylor terms.

For a negative-binomial waiting time stopped at \(k\) successes, the score
for \(\phi=\log p\) is \((k-pT)/(1-p)\), with variance \(k/(1-p)\).
Represent each waiting time as a sum of independent geometric waiting times.
For \(p\) small, the centered one-success log-probability scores have
uniformly bounded third absolute moments: the variables \(pT\) have a
uniform exponential tail.  The Lyapunov bound for the normalized sum is
\(O(k_n^{-1/2})\).  Since \(p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)\to0\), this proves
\(Z_n\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).

The second and third derivatives of the batch log likelihood are
\[
 -\frac{(T-k)p}{(1-p)^2},\qquad
 -\frac{(T-k)p(1+p)}{(1-p)^3},
\]
respectively.  On the compact local neighborhoods, their orders are
\(O_P(k)\), and the normalized negative second derivative converges to its
information.  Taylor expansion with
\eqref{eq:v23-success-log-expansion} gives \eqref{eq:v23-count-lan}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform comparison of waiting experiments]
\label{lem:v32-waiting-Hellinger}
For geometric laws \(G_p\) on \(\{1,2,\ldots\}\), if \(p,q\le p_*<1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-geometric-Hellinger}
 H^2(G_p,G_q)\le \frac{(\log p-\log q)^2}{4(1-p_*)}.
\end{equation}
For the sum of \(k\) independent such waiting times, the bound is multiplied
by at most \(k\).  Define ideal batch probabilities
\[
 p^*_{n,\ell}(b)=p^\partial_{n,\ell}(0)\exp\{-b/\sqrt{k_n}\}.
\]
The uncapped joint waiting experiment is uniformly at \(o(1)\) total
variation distance, on compact \((b,a,h)\)-sets, from the product of the
negative-binomial laws with probabilities \(p^*_{n,\ell}(b)\).
This ideal experiment converges in Le Cam distance on every compact
\(b\)-set to \(N(b,1)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
As a curve in \(\ell^2\), the square-root mass function of \(G_{e^\phi}\)
has derivative with squared norm \(1/[4(1-e^\phi)]\), one quarter of its
log-probability information.  Integrating this derivative on the segment
between \(\log p\) and \(\log q\) proves
\eqref{eq:v32-geometric-Hellinger}.  Product subadditivity and contraction
under summation give the waiting-time bound.  In particular, this proof uses
the exact discrete waiting law, not a Poisson replacement.

By \eqref{eq:v23-success-log-expansion}, the maximum difference between the
true and ideal log probabilities is \(o(k_n^{-1/2})\), uniformly on the
compact set.  All probabilities are at most \(1/2\) for large \(n\).
Summing \eqref{eq:v32-geometric-Hellinger} over all \(k_n\) required
successes gives squared Hellinger distance \(o(1)\), hence the stated total
variation comparison.

The ideal family has the finite-subexperiment limit \(N(b,1)\), by the same
count LAN calculation and the finite-parameter likelihood convergence
theorem used above.  Its pairwise squared Hellinger distance is bounded by
\(C|b-b'|^2\), uniformly in \(n\), because the log-probability difference
is exactly \(-(b-b')/\sqrt{k_n}\).  The Gaussian family has a continuous
Hellinger modulus as well.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v32-finite-net} proves compact
Le Cam convergence.
\end{proof}

Let \(\mathcal K_H\) be the restriction of
\(\mathcal K_{\rm design}\), defined in
\eqref{eq:v22-design-information}, to \(\mathbb R\times H_\gamma\).
It is positive definite on that space.

\begin{theorem}[Two-speed count--endpoint local experiment]
\label{thm:v23-count-endpoint-joint}
Assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-count-endpoint-conditions}
 \begin{gathered}
 k_n\delta_n^2\log(1/\delta_n)\to1,\qquad j_n\delta_n\to0,\\
 \log(j_n\sqrt{k_n})/j_n^2\to0,\qquad
 k_n\tau^{j_n}\to0,\qquad j_n\in2\mathbb N,\quad j_n\to\infty.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Use \eqref{eq:v23-count-endpoint-parameter}, the fixed laboratory windows and
the reference caps.  For each fixed compact
\(K\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\times H_\gamma\), let
\(\mathsf E^{\partial,CE}_{n,K}\) be the capped boundary experiment retaining
waiting counts and successful endpoint pairs, and let
\(\mathsf E^{\mathrm{fin},CE}_{n,K}\) be its actual finite-bridge counterpart.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v23-count-endpoint-limit}
 \Delta(\mathsf E^{\partial,CE}_{n,K},\mathsf G_K)\to0,
 \qquad
 \Delta(\mathsf E^{\mathrm{fin},CE}_{n,K},\mathsf G_K)\to0,
\end{equation}
where \(\mathsf G_K\) observes, independently,
\[
 X\sim N(b,1),\qquad
 Y\sim N(\mathcal K_H(a,h),\mathcal K_H),
 \qquad (b,a,h)\in K.
\]
Thus the limiting information is \(1\oplus\mathcal K_H\).  The hyperbolic
shape direction is visible at scale \((j_n\sqrt{k_n})^{-1}\), the
iso-hyperbolic shape directions at scale \(\delta_n\), and the gap at
scale \(\delta_n/j_n\).  Both comparison kernels are independent of the
unknown \((b,a,h)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First remove the caps.  In an iid Bernoulli-mark experiment the successful
marks are independent of the waiting times and have their conditional
success law.  This factorization is exact for the uncapped experiment; the
cap error will be added only after the product comparison.

Lemma~\ref{lem:v32-waiting-Hellinger} replaces the waiting factor by its
ideal \(b\)-only experiment with a uniform \(o(1)\) total variation error.
For the endpoint factor, compare the parameter
\((g_n(a),\eta_nbv_\gamma+\delta_nh)\) with
\((g_n(a),\delta_nh)\) at the same physical time.  Smooth dependence of the
actions and amplitudes changes the defining function in \(C^1\) and the
amplitude in \(C^0\) by at most \(C_K\eta_n\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-hypersurface-stability}, the total squared Hellinger
error over the successful records is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v32-fast-mark-error}
 C_Kk_n\eta_n^2\log(e/\eta_n)
 =C_K\frac{1+\log(j_n\sqrt{k_n})}{j_n^2}=o(1).
\end{equation}
The smooth amplitude term is bounded by \(C_Kk_n\eta_n^2\) and is included
in this estimate.  The remaining endpoint experiment depends only on
\((a,h)\), and converges on compact sets to its Gaussian shift by
Corollary~\ref{cor:v32-compact-fixed-window}, restricted to
\(\mathbb R\times H_\gamma\).

Take the compact projections of \(K\) onto its count and endpoint
coordinates.  The product of the two comparison kernels on those projection
sets has error at most the sum of the component errors.  Restriction from
their Cartesian product to \(K\) cannot increase either deficiency.  This
proves compact two-sided convergence for the uncapped boundary experiment,
with independent Gaussian factors; it is stronger than convergence of a
fixed finite vector of likelihood ratios.

By \eqref{eq:v23-success-log-expansion}, the true success probabilities are
uniformly asymptotic to their reference values on \(K\).  The reference cap
therefore has failure probability at most
\(\sum_\ell e^{-c k_{n,\ell}}=o(1)\), as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v22-reference-cap}.  Coupling to the uncapped acquisition
sequence changes the experiment by at most this probability; a reverse
kernel may assign any fixed completion on the cap event.  This proves the
first limit in \eqref{eq:v23-count-endpoint-limit}.

The common count--endpoint coarsening of the stopped transcript preserves
the total variation bound \(C_Kk_n\tau^{j_n}\) from
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-stopped-local-transfer}.  Its hypotheses hold here: the
local graphs stay in the same compact collar, the fast perturbation tends
to zero, and the policy and caps are parameter independent.  The same
success-weighted adaptive comparison consequently applies, without a free
failure convention.  The triangle inequality for Le Cam distance proves the
second limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Likelihoods and the retained record]
Count likelihoods are ordinary negative-binomial likelihoods.  For endpoint
marks, likelihood expansions relative to a reference member are taken only
after common-collar censoring.  Formula
\eqref{eq:v23-count-endpoint-limit} instead concerns the original capped
experiments and therefore does not assert reference domination where the
support moves.  Nor does it compare complete raw collision histories.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Three levels of information]
\label{rem:v23-three-level-information}
Under the respective rate hypotheses, retaining residual time gives the
non-Gaussian Poisson boundary experiment of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-complete-et}, with shape scale \(k_n^{-1}\) and gap
scale \((j_nk_n)^{-1}\).  Removing residual time but keeping waiting counts
gives Theorem~\ref{thm:v23-count-endpoint-joint}.  Removing both gives the
endpoint-output Gaussian experiment of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-fixed-window-gaussian} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:v32-compact-fixed-window}.  These are different experiments
related by common Markov coarsenings, not alternative names for one record.
\end{remark}
