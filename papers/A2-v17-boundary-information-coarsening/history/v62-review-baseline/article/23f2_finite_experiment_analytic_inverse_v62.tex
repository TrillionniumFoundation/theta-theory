\subsection{Finite preparations and complete analytic contact recovery}
\label{subsec:v62-finite-experiment}

We now pass from limiting laws to finite experiments while keeping the
complete analytic contact pair as the target.  The order of this passage
matters.  A finite-bridge density does not in general have the separated
form \eqref{eq:v60-recorded-density}, and an empirical histogram has still
less reason to have that form.  We compare realizable limiting laws before
applying their inverse.  This avoids extending a factorization identity to
an arbitrary observed density.

Fix the local analytic neighborhood, radii, squares and anchor of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v60-real-observation-stability}.  Let $\mathcal K$ be a
nonempty class of periodic realizations whose anchored contact pairs and
gaps belong to this neighborhood.  Require uniform positive curvature,
separation, clear-channel and roof margins on the selected itinerary, and
$0<A_-\le A(T)\le A_+<\infty$.  We use ideal endpoint recording in this
subsection.  The signed frames and physical units are the observation
conventions already specified for the law inverse.  The sampling theorem
below first treats an exactly calibrated channel; estimated calibration
is treated afterwards.  The class need not be finite dimensional.

All contact graphs have a common bound on $D_R$.  Cauchy estimates on a
strictly smaller physical collar give the finitely many real derivative
bounds needed in the relative construction.  By decreasing the analytic
neighborhood and the collar, the weighted contractions, diagonal dominance
and trace-norm estimates have uniform margins.  Thus their endpoint
estimates are uniform on $\mathcal K$; no differentiation with respect to
an infinite-dimensional parameter is being asserted.  Fix a compact
positive-offset interval $I_d$ about $d$, contained in the common onset
collar.  Decrease $Q_+$ once so that it lies strictly inside the positive
region for every offset in $I_d$ and every member of the class.

\begin{lemma}[Interior density and acceptance bounds]
\label{lem:v62-finite-densities}
There are $j_0\ge2$, $C,H,F,c>0$, $\omega>0$, and
$\Gamma\ge\sup_{T\in\mathcal K}\gamma(T)>0$ such that, for every even
$j\ge j_0$, both starting types $b$, and $d'\in I_d$, the actual
successful endpoint density $f_{j,b,d'}^T$ and its limiting density
$f_{b,d'}^T$ satisfy
\begin{align}
 \|f_{j,b,d'}^T-f_{b,d'}^T\|_{C^0(Q_+)}
       &\le C e^{-\omega j},
       \label{eq:v62-interior-approx}\\
 \|f_{j,b,d'}^T\|_{\operatorname{Lip}(Q_+)}
       +\|f_{b,d'}^T\|_{\operatorname{Lip}(Q_+)}&\le H.
       \label{eq:v62-lipschitz}
\end{align}
Their zero extensions are bounded by $F$ on a common endpoint box, and
are zero outside it.  Their densities are uniformly Lipschitz in the
offset in the uniform norm.  The probability $p_{j,b,d'}^T$ of this
selected success in one normalized full-phase preparation obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-acceptance}
                 p_{j,b,d'}^T\ge c e^{-\Gamma j}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppress the indices $T,b$ and put
\[
 F_{j,d'}=b_j(d'-E_j)_+,
 \qquad F_{\infty,d'}=B_b(u)B_b(v)(d'-S_b(u)-S_b(v))_+.
\]
Integrate over the fixed box containing their supports, and write the
normalizers as $Z_{j,d'}$ and $Z_{\infty,d'}$.  The relative estimate
\eqref{eq:v8-relative} and the Lipschitz property of positive part give
$\|F_{j,d'}-F_{\infty,d'}\|_\infty\le C e^{-\omega j}$ and the same
bound for the normalizer difference.  A fixed small square at the
origin gives $Z_{j,d'},Z_{\infty,d'}\ge z_->0$, after enlarging $j_0$.
Division proves \eqref{eq:v62-interior-approx}, in fact in the uniform
norm on the full box.  On $Q_+$ the cutoff is inactive, so the first
endpoint-derivative estimate gives \eqref{eq:v62-lipschitz}.  Bounded
numerators and the lower normalizer bound give $F$.  Also
\[
 \|F_{j,d'}-F_{j,d''}\|_\infty\le C|d'-d''|,
 \qquad |Z_{j,d'}-Z_{j,d''}|\le C|d'-d''|.
\]
These estimates, and their limiting versions, prove offset continuity
without differentiating a moving boundary.

The exact full-phase formula gives
\[
 p_{j,b,d'}^T=\frac{d_{j,b}^0}{2\pi A(T)}Z_{j,b,d'},
 \qquad d_{j,b}^0=\frac{a_b}{\sinh(j\gamma(T))}.
\]
The positive lower bound for $a_b$, $A(T)\le A_+$, and
$1/\sinh(j\gamma)\ge2e^{-j\gamma}$ give
\eqref{eq:v62-acceptance}.  The itinerary is selected throughout; no
nonminimal channel is identified with the complete maximal-count event.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete contact stability from finite bridges]
\label{thm:v62-finite-contact}
For two members $T,\widetilde T\in\mathcal K$, let $j,k\ge j_0$ be
even, let their physical offsets both equal $d$, and put
\[
 e_{j,k}=\max_b\|f_{j,b,d}^{T}-f_{k,b,d}^{\widetilde T}\|_{C^0(Q)}.
\]
For sufficiently small errors,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-finite-contact}
 \|\psi^T-\psi^{\widetilde T}\|_r
 \le C\left\{|g_T-g_{\widetilde T}|+
       (e_{j,k}+e^{-\omega j}+e^{-\omega k})^\vartheta\right\}.
\end{equation}
Every $0<t<r$ gives the simultaneous coefficient bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-coefficient-bound}
 \sum_{n\ge2}\frac{t^n}{n!}
       \max_b| (\psi_b^T)^{(n)}(0)
                       -(\psi_b^{\widetilde T})^{(n)}(0)|
 \le \frac{(t/r)^2}{1-t/r}\,
                          \|\psi^T-\psi^{\widetilde T}\|_r.
\end{equation}
The constants and exponent do not depend on a jet truncation order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:v62-interior-approx} at the two ends of the triangle
inequality comparing the limiting densities.  Their $C^0(Q)$ distance
is at most $e_{j,k}+C(e^{-\omega j}+e^{-\omega k})$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v60-real-observation-stability} applies to these two
limiting laws and gives \eqref{eq:v62-finite-contact}.  Cauchy's
coefficient estimate on $D_r$ gives
\eqref{eq:v62-coefficient-bound}.  We have not applied the separated
law inverse to either finite density.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{A fixed preparation budget}
For the next theorem assume that $g$ and the two contact frames are
exactly calibrated and fixed throughout $\mathcal K$.  All window times
and endpoint categories are then programmed without the unknown table.
Choose an even $j$ and perform $N$ independent normalized full-phase
preparations for each starting-type experiment, each of duration $jg+d$.
A preparation returns failure, or the endpoint category on the prescribed
selected itinerary.  Partition $Q_+$ into $J$ equal squares of side $h$
and retain its complement as an additional category.  In particular,
endpoints outside $Q_+$ are not discarded from the successful-sample
denominator.  Let $M_b$ be the number of successes in group $b$ and
$C_{b,i}$ the number in cell $i$.  For $M_b>0$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-observed-cell-density}
                 \widehat f_{b,i}=\frac{C_{b,i}}{M_bh^2}.
\end{equation}
The counters $M_b$ are used for normalization and may subsequently be
forgotten.  No ancillary interpretation of the waiting counts is needed.

\begin{theorem}[Analytic recovery with every preparation charged]
\label{thm:v62-budget}
Under the preceding hypotheses let $\alpha\in(0,1/2)$, set
\[
 x=\log\frac{16(J+1)}\alpha,
 \qquad m_0=\frac c2N e^{-\Gamma j},
\]
and assume $cNe^{-\Gamma j}\ge8\log(8/\alpha)$.
For every $\xi>0$ there is a measurable estimator with values in
$\mathcal K$ such that, with probability at least $1-\alpha$,
\begin{align}
 \|\widehat\psi-\psi\|_r
   &\le C\left(e^{-\omega j}+h+
          \sqrt{\frac{x}{m_0h^2}}+
                    \frac{x}{m_0h^2}+\xi\right)^\vartheta,
                    \label{eq:v62-budget-bound}
\end{align}
whenever the quantity in parentheses is sufficiently small.
The experiment uses exactly $2N$ preparations and evolution time
$2N(jg+d)$, including all failures.  No finite-dimensional model for the
contact functions is imposed.

More precisely, for $L_N=\log(C_*N/\alpha)$ and all sufficiently large
$N/L_N$, an even choice
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-optimal-schedule}
 j_N=2\left\lfloor
       \frac{\log(N/L_N)}{2(4\omega+\Gamma)}\right\rfloor\ge j_0,
 \qquad h_N\asymp
        \left(\frac{L_N e^{\Gamma j_N}}{N}\right)^{1/4},
 \qquad 0<\xi\le N^{-1},
\end{equation}
with $h_N$ rounded to an equal-square grid, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-budget-rate}
 \sup_{T\in\mathcal K}\Pr_T\left\{
  \|\widehat\psi-\psi^T\|_r>
        C\left(\frac{L_N}{N}\right)^{
                     \vartheta\omega/(4\omega+\Gamma)}\right\}
 \le\alpha.
\end{equation}
The same event controls all coefficients by
\eqref{eq:v62-coefficient-bound}.  The rate is an upper bound, not an
optimality assertion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The binomial lower-tail estimate and \eqref{eq:v62-acceptance} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-count-tail}
 \Pr\{\min_b M_b<m_0\}\le
                 2\exp(-cNe^{-\Gamma j}/8)\le\alpha/4.
\end{equation}
For example the usual exponential Markov argument at parameter
$-\log2$ bounds $\Pr\{M\le Np/2\}$ by $e^{-Np/8}$; the displayed
bound also holds when the threshold is decreased to $m_0$.
Conditional on $M_b=m$, the successful marks have their actual iid
conditional law.  This assertion follows directly by factoring the
joint density at each fixed set of $m$ successful indices and summing
over those sets.  We use a fixed number of attempts, not a stopped
experiment conditioned on completing its cap.

For a cell probability $p_i\le Fh^2$, the Bernoulli exponential bound
gives, simultaneously in both types and all $J$ cells, except on an
event of probability at most $4Je^{-x}\le\alpha/4$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-bernstein-cells}
 \left|\widehat f_{b,i}-h^{-2}\int_{C_i}f_{j,b,d}\right|
  \le \sqrt{\frac{2Fx}{mh^2}}+\frac{2x}{3mh^2}.
\end{equation}
For completeness, for a Bernoulli variable $X$ of mean $p$ and
$0<\lambda<3$,
\[
 \log\mathbb E e^{\lambda(X-p)}
 \le p(e^\lambda-1-\lambda)
 \le \frac{p\lambda^2}{2(1-\lambda/3)}.
\]
The same upper bound with $|\lambda|$ holds for the lower tail.
Applying exponential Markov and optimizing gives
$|\bar X-p|\le\sqrt{2px/m}+2x/(3m)$ with failure probability at most
$2e^{-x}$.  This proves \eqref{eq:v62-bernstein-cells}.
The bound is uniform for every $m\ge m_0$; integrating over these
counts and adding \eqref{eq:v62-count-tail} yields a simultaneous
probability at least $1-\alpha$.

Here is an explicit measurable selection construction.  The image
\[
 \{(f_{0,d}^T|_{Q_+},f_{1,d}^T|_{Q_+}):T\in\mathcal K\}
                    \subset C(Q_+)^2
\]
is a separable metric space.  Choose once a countable dense subset of
this image and a realizing table $T_l$ for each of its members.  Define
\[
 D_l=\max_{b,i}\left|
             h^{-2}\int_{C_i}f_{b,d}^{T_l}-\widehat f_{b,i}\right|.
\]
On nonempty success groups take the first $l$ with
$D_l<\inf_{q\ge1}D_q+\xi$, and otherwise take a fixed member of the
class.  The countable infimum and this first-index rule are measurable.
The output contact pair is measurable in the $D_r$ norm as well, since
its range is countable.  Existence of this estimator is not an assertion
of a finite computational search bound.

Put
$\varepsilon_N=\sqrt{2Fx/(m_0h^2)}+2x/(3m_0h^2)$.
Density of the selected image, the approximation lemma and
\eqref{eq:v62-bernstein-cells} give
$\inf_lD_l\le Ce^{-\omega j}+\varepsilon_N$.
Two triangle inequalities therefore bound the cell-average difference
between the fitted and true limiting densities by
$2Ce^{-\omega j}+2\varepsilon_N+\xi$.  On each cell, their common
Lipschitz bound supplies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-cells-to-uniform}
 \|f^{\widehat T}_{b,d}-f^T_{b,d}\|_{C^0(Q_+)}
       \le2Ce^{-\omega j}+2\varepsilon_N+\xi+2\sqrt2 Hh.
\end{equation}
This is a comparison of two realizable limiting laws, not a regularity
claim about the empirical histogram.  Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:v60-real-observation-stability} to obtain
\eqref{eq:v62-budget-bound}.

To prove the rate, put $a_N=L_N/N$ and
$\zeta=\omega/(4\omega+\Gamma)$.  Rounding $j_N$ down by less than
two gives
\[
 e^{-\omega j_N}\le e^{2\omega}a_N^\zeta,
 \qquad \left(\frac{L_Ne^{\Gamma j_N}}N\right)^{1/4}
                                      \le a_N^\zeta.
\]
The rounded grid satisfies $h_N\asymp
(L_Ne^{\Gamma j_N}/N)^{1/4}$ and
$J_N\le C(N/L_N)^{2\zeta}$.  Since $2\zeta<1/2$, enlarging the
fixed $C_*$ ensures $x\le C L_N$.  Also
$Ne^{-\Gamma j_N}/L_N\ge (N/L_N)^{4\zeta}\to\infty$,
so the success-count condition holds.  The square-root term in
\eqref{eq:v62-budget-bound} is $O(a_N^\zeta)$ and the linear term
is $O(a_N^{2\zeta})$.  The selection error $N^{-1}$ is smaller.
This proves \eqref{eq:v62-budget-rate} and the asserted charges.
\end{proof}

The exponent records three different operations: relative finite-flight
error, rare-event thinning, and estimation of a two-dimensional density
with a first-order real bound.  The outer analytic prior and the inner
radius determine $\vartheta$.  The experiment is a sequence of independent
full-phase preparations, not a mixing assertion for one continuously
observed orbit.  The theorem does not assert a rate for global analytic
continuation, lattice registration, unknown channel discovery, or
arbitrarily inefficient recording.  Those operations retain their own
hypotheses.  In particular the exactly calibrated budget in
\eqref{eq:v62-budget-rate} must not be called a calibration-inclusive rate.

\subsubsection*{Estimated offset and endpoint coordinates}
The finite-budget estimate also has a calibration-aware form.  Consider
now variable gaps in $\mathcal K$.  Suppose an independent preliminary
experiment has deterministic cap $B_{\rm pil}$ and, except with probability
$\beta$, supplies correct signed channel labels, a gap estimate
$|\widehat g-g|\le v_g$, and observable endpoint projections inducing a
Borel map $\Phi$ with $\|\Phi-\mathrm{id}\|_\infty\le r_c$ on the
actual endpoint collar.  Program the subsequent windows at
$j\widehat g+d+\epsilon_t$, with $|\epsilon_t|\le v_t$.
Require $\Delta=jv_g+v_t$ small enough that all actual offsets belong
to $I_d$, and $0\le r_c\le h/2$.  The observed variables after this
preliminary experiment are fixed maps of the raw record; no unknown true
chart is applied by the observer.

\begin{corollary}[A charged bound with calibration errors]
\label{cor:v62-calibration}
Under these hypotheses perform the two groups of $N$ fresh attempts as
above.  With $m_0,x$ and the count condition of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v62-budget}, there is a measurable estimator satisfying,
with probability at least $1-\alpha-\beta$,
\begin{align}
 \|\widehat\psi-\psi\|_r
 \le C\biggl[ v_g+\xi+
 \biggl(e^{-\omega j}+\Delta+h+\frac{r_c}{h}
       +\sqrt{\frac{x}{m_0h^2}}+
                \frac{x}{m_0h^2}+\xi\biggr)^\vartheta\biggr].
 \label{eq:v62-calibration-bound}
\end{align}
The complete deterministic preparation charge is $B_{\rm pil}+2N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix a true table and a good preliminary history.  For a square cell $C_i$
of side $h$, a change of its category under $\Phi$ can occur only in the
$r_c$-neighborhood of its boundary.  For $r_c\le h/2$ that neighborhood
has area at most
\[
                  (h+2r_c)^2-(h-2r_c)^2=8hr_c.
\]
The global density bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:v62-finite-densities} therefore
gives a cell-mass error at most $8Fhr_c$ and a cell-average error at most
$8Fr_c/h$.  This includes the outer edges of $Q_+$; the outside category
is still included in $M_b$.  No density or Jacobian for $\Phi_\#P$ is
assumed.  Its cell masses are at most $Fh^2+8Fhr_c\le5Fh^2$, which
suffices for \eqref{eq:v62-bernstein-cells} with $F$ replaced by $5F$.

Uniform offset continuity adds $C\Delta$ and the finite-flight lemma
adds $Ce^{-\omega j}$.  The success bound is uniform on $I_d$, hence
the count and cell concentration estimates hold conditionally on every
good preliminary history.  Choose countably many representatives dense
in the joint image $(g,f_0|_{Q_+},f_1|_{Q_+})$.  In the previous
first-index selection allow only candidates with
$|g_l-\widehat g|<v_g+\xi$.
On a good history this set is nonempty and approximates the true density
image arbitrarily well: the strict slack $\xi>0$ keeps the true image
inside the permitted gap strip.  The selected gap differs from the true
one by at most $2v_g+\xi$.  Off that event use any fixed default when
necessary.  The allowed indices and their residual infimum are measurable.

Repeat \eqref{eq:v62-cells-to-uniform} with the two additional biases
$C\Delta+8Fr_c/h$, and apply the two-point analytic inverse with the
selected gap error.  This proves \eqref{eq:v62-calibration-bound}
conditionally.  Integrate over good histories and add $\beta$.
The two subsequent groups always use exactly $2N$ attempts; adding the
preliminary cap proves the charge without conditioning on cap completion.
\end{proof}

For the persistent position-sensor design, the existing calibration theorem
\ref{thm:v25-observable-calibration} supplies
$jv_g\le\varepsilon_{\rm pil}$ and
$r_c\le C_\chi\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\rm pil}}$ under its stated compact
marked-class hypotheses, with the explicit cap
\eqref{eq:v25-pilot-cap}.  It is a substantive input to this application.
With an exact clock, choosing
$\varepsilon_{\rm pil}\le c_1h^4$ makes both $\Delta$ and $r_c/h$
$O(h)$, and $r_c\le h/2$ for small $h$.  The charge is then
$B_{\rm pil}(j,\varepsilon_{\rm pil},\beta)+2N$, not $2N$.
The pilot cost can also be estimated explicitly.  This yields a slower
rate in the complete preparation budget rather than treating calibration
as a free oracle.

\begin{corollary}[Complete preparation budget with the position pilot]
\label{cor:v62-total-budget}
Assume in addition the compact marked-class hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-observable-calibration}, persistent position recording,
and an exact clock.  The number of marked channels is fixed, and the
channel and starting-type labels used by that theorem are retained.
There are $C_0,C_1>0$ such that, for $0<\alpha<1/2$ and all sufficiently
large $B/[\log(C_0B/\alpha)]^2$, a parameter-independent experiment with
at most $B$ preparations, counting the pilot and every rejected attempt,
has a measurable estimator satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-total-budget-rate}
 \sup_{T\in\mathcal K}\Pr_T\left\{
  \|\widehat\psi-\psi^T\|_r>
 C_1\left(\frac{[\log(C_0B/\alpha)]^2}{B}\right)^{
                  \vartheta\omega/(12\omega+\Gamma)}\right\}
 \le\alpha.
\end{equation}
Here $\Gamma$ is enlarged, if necessary, to bound also the hyperbolicity
exponent in the pilot mass estimate.  The total evolution time is at
most $CB\log(C_0B/\alpha)$.  All coefficient bounds
\eqref{eq:v62-coefficient-bound} hold on the same event.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Clip the preliminary gap estimate to the known interval $[g_-,g_+]$
(and use a fixed value there on a failed pilot).  This cannot enlarge
the good-history error and bounds every programmed time on all histories.
Use preliminary failure probability $\alpha/2$, and apply
Corollary~\ref{cor:v62-calibration} with subsequent failure probability
$\alpha/2$.  The pilot has success lower bound
$p_*(j,\varepsilon)=c_*\varepsilon^2e^{-\Gamma j}$ at a grid point
within $[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]$ of onset.  Its grid contains at
most $C(j+1)/\varepsilon$ points.  Thus the exact integer cap
\eqref{eq:v25-pilot-cap} satisfies, for $j\ge j_0$ and
$0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v62-pilot-cost}
 B_{\rm pil}(j,\varepsilon,\alpha/2)
 \le C(j+1)e^{\Gamma j}\varepsilon^{-3}\log(C/\alpha).
\end{equation}
The ceiling terms are absorbed since the logarithm is bounded below
and $\varepsilon$ is small.

Choose an equal-square grid with
$h\asymp e^{-\omega j}$, put $\varepsilon=c_1h^4$ with fixed
sufficiently small $c_1>0$, and take
\[
 N=\left\lceil C_2 e^{\Gamma j}h^{-4}
                         \log\frac{C_2}{\alpha h}\right\rceil,
 \qquad 0<\xi\le h^2.
\]
For sufficiently large fixed $C_2$, the count condition holds and
both concentration terms are $O(h)$ (the linear term is $O(h^2)$).
The biases $e^{-\omega j}$, $\Delta$, and $r_c/h$ are $O(h)$;
the additive gap error is $O(h^4/j)$.  Hence
\eqref{eq:v62-calibration-bound} gives error $Ch^\vartheta$.
The complete cap satisfies
\begin{align*}
 B_{\rm pil}+2N
 &\le C(j+1)e^{\Gamma j}h^{-12}
                         \log\frac C{\alpha h} \\
 &\le C(j+1)e^{(12\omega+\Gamma)j}
                              \bigl(\log(C/\alpha)+\omega j\bigr).
\end{align*}
The grid-rounding constants are fixed.

Let $L=\log(C_0B/\alpha)$ and choose the even integer
\[
 j=2\left\lfloor
       \frac{\log\{B/(C_3L^2)\}}{2(12\omega+\Gamma)}
       \right\rfloor\ge j_0.
\]
Then $j+1\le CL$ and $\log(C/\alpha)+\omega j\le CL$.
Taking the fixed $C_3$ sufficiently large makes the last cap at most
$B$, including all ceilings.  Rounding down by less than two gives
$h\le C(L^2/B)^{\omega/(12\omega+\Gamma)}$, which proves
\eqref{eq:v62-total-budget-rate}.  Every pilot time is at most
$jg_++2\varepsilon$, and the programmed later times are bounded by
$C(j+1)$.  Multiplying by the deterministic cap gives the stated
evolution-time bound.  The labels, grid, tolerance, stopping caps and
subsequent window times are either fixed in advance or measurable
functions of the preliminary record, as required.
\end{proof}

The power $12$ in this bound comes from the concrete pilot rather than
from an asserted intrinsic information barrier: the onset scan costs
$\varepsilon^{-3}$, while displacement control for histogram cells uses
$\varepsilon\asymp h^4$.  Neither preparation-budget exponent is claimed
optimal.  This result complements the retained full-table acquisition
result \ref{cor:v47-physical-histograms}.  It recovers complete local
analytic germs at a quantitative rate; global continuation, matching and
lattice recovery retain their separate hypotheses and stability moduli.
