\section{Contact reconstruction along a marked periodic orbit}
\label{sec:v65-periodic-inverse}

The future and past actions of a periodic orbit need not coincide.
Nevertheless their separation in the physical window law permits an
inverse which does not use time-reversal symmetry.  Two positive offsets
separate the actions without a flux model.  The derivative of the future
action at all collision phases is then a sum of successive boundary
visits.  Its homogeneous blocks are Cayley transforms of a signed cyclic
shift.  We prove below that this calculation applies to actual smooth
boundaries and gives an inverse of the complete analytic action map.

\subsection{Fixed geometric marks and the observation coordinates}

Fix a clear nongrazing periodic orbit with $r\ge2$ collision phases.
The collision points are distinct modulo the prescribed lattice, and
phase $i$ has a fixed point $q_i$, unit outward obstacle normal $n_i$
and signed unit tangent $t_i$.  These data, including the lifted closing
translation, will be called its \emph{marked polygon}.  Indices are read
modulo $r$, with that translation understood.  Set
\[
 e_i^0=\frac{q_{i+1}-q_i}{L_i},\qquad
 L_i=|q_{i+1}-q_i|,\qquad
 \nu_i=e_i^0\cdot n_i>0,\qquad p_i=e_i^0\cdot t_i/\nu_i.
\]
The reflection condition is part of the marked polygon.  In particular
$e_{i-1}^0\cdot n_i=-\nu_i$ and
$e_{i-1}^0\cdot t_i=e_i^0\cdot t_i$.
No curvature or higher boundary derivative is included in these marks.

Write the unknown arcs as normalized normal graphs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-normal-graphs}
 R_i(x)=q_i+\nu_i^{-1}\{t_i x-n_i F_i(x)\},\qquad
 F_i(0)=F_i'(0)=0,\qquad c_i:=F_i''(0)>0.
\end{equation}
The actual curvature is $\kappa_i=\nu_i c_i$.
The recorded coordinate is the scaled tangent projection
$x=\nu_i t_i\cdot(R_i-q_i)$, not the unknown normal displacement.
Thus recording $x$ does not supply $F_i(x)$.  The marks specify the
coordinate, and its use in the inverse does not require converting
measured arclength by means of an unknown boundary.
Put $k_i=L_i^{-1}$ and
\[
 \ell_i(x,y)=|R_{i+1}(y)-R_i(x)|,\qquad
 h_i(x,y)=\ell_i(x,y)-L_i+p_i x-p_{i+1}y.
\]
These gauges have zero value and differential at the origin.
The marked polygon remains a stationary orbit for every anchored graph
in \eqref{eq:v65-normal-graphs}.  On a sufficiently small common real
collar, small perturbations preserve positive curvature, clearance and
nongrazing incidence.

\begin{lemma}[Signed Jacobi coordinates]\label{lem:v65-signed-jacobi}
There are signs $\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$, determined by the marked
polygon and tangent choices, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-graph-hessian}
 D^2h_i(0,0)=
 \begin{pmatrix}k_i+c_i&-\epsilon_i k_i\\
                 -\epsilon_i k_i&k_i+c_{i+1}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
On the infinite itinerary a diagonal change of signs makes all mixed
entries negative.  The resulting Jacobi operator has positive masses
$2c_i$.  The future half-line with endpoint $x_0=u$ has linearization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-linear-visits}
 x_{b,j}'(0)=\sigma_b\sigma_{b+1}\cdots\sigma_{b+j-1},\qquad
 \sigma_i=\epsilon_i a_i,\qquad 0<a_i<1.
\end{equation}
The factors $a_i$ are periodic.  They depend smoothly on the positive
curvatures, and are uniformly bounded away from one on a compact marked
class.  In particular $\Lambda:=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\sigma_i$ satisfies
$|\Lambda|<1$.

The relative and physical laws of Section~\ref{sec:v64-periodic-itinerary}
hold in the graph coordinates, for a returning oriented period $P$
(which may be taken to be $2r$).  In these coordinates write their limits
as $S_b^\pm,B_b^\pm$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-physical-actions}
 A_b(u)=S_b^-(u)-p_bu,\qquad C_b(v)=S_b^+(v)+p_bv.
\end{equation}
Then the physical residual in the limiting law is
$d-A_b(u)-C_b(v)$.  The action and amplitude estimates remain estimates
at each separately fixed differentiation order.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At the origin $R_i'=t_i/\nu_i$ and $R_i''=-n_i c_i/\nu_i$.
Differentiating a chord twice gives the same-end entry $k_i+c_i$.
Its mixed entry is
\[
 -\frac{t_i\cdot t_{i+1}-(e_i^0\cdot t_i)
                (e_i^0\cdot t_{i+1})}{L_i\nu_i\nu_{i+1}}.
\]
The two transverse projections have lengths $\nu_i,\nu_{i+1}$;
their relative sign defines $\epsilon_i$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:v65-graph-hessian}.  If $s_0=1$ and
$s_{j+1}=\epsilon_{b+j}s_j$, the substitution $x_j=s_j y_j$
gives the positive-mass operator
\[
 (Hy)_j=(k_{b+j-1}+k_{b+j}+2c_{b+j})y_j
              -k_{b+j-1}y_{j-1}-k_{b+j}y_{j+1}.
\]
Its Green kernel and fixed differentiated bounds follow from the walk
expansion in the preceding section; the diagonal change of signs
preserves their absolute bounds.

For endpoint $y_0=1$, finite Dirichlet solutions are strictly positive
in their interiors by the discrete maximum principle.  Their half-line
limit is positive and decays exponentially.  Define
$z_{j+1}=k_{b+j}(y_{j+1}-y_j)$.  The recurrence gives
$z_{j+1}-z_j=2c_{b+j}y_j$.  Since $z_j\to0$ at infinity,
$z_{j+1}=-\sum_{h\ge j+1}2c_{b+h}y_h<0$.
Consequently $0<y_{j+1}<y_j$.  Uniqueness of a decaying tail shows
that its ratio at a site depends only on that phase; call it $a_i$.
This proves \eqref{eq:v65-linear-visits}.  Smooth dependence follows
by differentiating the uniformly invertible weighted equations.
Compactness and finitely many phases give $\max_i a_i<1$ uniformly.

The signed charts return after $2r$ steps, even when they do not return
after $r$.  This does not create $2r$ independent unknown contact germs.
To compare with signed scaled arclength at the initial phase, let
$\phi_b(x)$ be its expression in the graph coordinate, with the same
initial orientation.  Directly from \eqref{eq:v65-normal-graphs},
\[
 \phi_b(x)=\int_0^x\sqrt{1+F_b'(s)^2}\,ds
                  =x+O(x^3).
\]
Use the positive branch on the real collar.  The endpoint twist is
multiplied by $\phi_b'(u)\phi_b'(v)$, whose value at zero is one.
The exact reference $D_{N,b}$ is therefore unchanged.  The graph future
action is the arclength action composed with $\phi_b$, plus
$p_b(x-\phi_b(x))$; the past action has the opposite gauge correction.
The amplitude acquires the ordinary endpoint Jacobian.  In particular
the graph actions are still anchored and have positive Hessians at zero.
Composition on smaller collars preserves the fixed-order relative bounds.
The physical actions in \eqref{eq:v65-physical-actions} are simply the
physical arclength actions composed with $\phi_b$; no physical time
has been changed.  This proves the last assertion, without making an
unknown coordinate conversion an input to the reconstruction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Separation of unequal endpoint actions}

Choose two fixed offsets $0<d_1<d_2$ in the physical positive window,
and a common square $I^2$ on which both residuals have a positive floor.
At phase $b$ the two limiting densities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-two-laws}
 f_{b,d}(u,v)=Z_{b,d}^{-1}B_b^-(u)B_b^+(v)
                       \{d-A_b(u)-C_b(v)\},\qquad d=d_1,d_2.
\end{equation}
Here $A_b(0)=C_b(0)=0$ and $B_b^\pm(0)=1$.
The two normalizing constants are unrelated unknown positive numbers.

\begin{proposition}[Two-offset action separation]\label{prop:v65-two-offset}
The two conditional laws in \eqref{eq:v65-two-laws} determine the
complete future and past actions and normalized amplitudes.  More
precisely, suppressing the phase index, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-offset-quotient}
 Q(u,v)=\frac{f_{d_1}(u,v)f_{d_2}(0,0)}
              {f_{d_2}(u,v)f_{d_1}(0,0)},\qquad
 H(u,v)=\frac{d_1d_2\{1-Q(u,v)\}}{d_2-d_1Q(u,v)}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-action-extraction}
 A(u)=H(u,0),\qquad C(v)=H(0,v),\qquad H(u,v)=A(u)+C(v).
\end{equation}
The gauged actions follow from \eqref{eq:v65-physical-actions}, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-amplitude-extraction}
 B^-(u)=\frac{f_d(u,0)}{f_d(0,0)}\frac{d}{d-A(u)},\qquad
 B^+(v)=\frac{f_d(0,v)}{f_d(0,0)}\frac{d}{d-C(v)}.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $M\ge0$ these formulas are locally Lipschitz in
$C^M(I^2)$, on compact positive-density classes with fixed separated
offsets and residual floors.  They do not require equality of the two
actions, a model for the amplitudes, or an acceptance count.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $h=A(u)+C(v)$.  Cancellation of the amplitudes and the two
normalizers gives
\[
 Q=\frac{1-h/d_1}{1-h/d_2},\qquad
 d_2-d_1Q=\frac{d_2(d_2-d_1)}{d_2-h}>0.
\]
Solving the first identity for $h$ proves
\eqref{eq:v65-offset-quotient}--\eqref{eq:v65-action-extraction}.
Since $h$ is bounded on the common square, the second identity gives a
positive denominator floor, uniformly on the stated compact class.
Taking a single density ratio on each coordinate axis proves
\eqref{eq:v65-amplitude-extraction}.  Continuous densities are fixed
everywhere on the positive square by their probability laws, even though
finite samples would require estimation.

For stability, slice evaluation is bounded in $C^M$.  Products and
reciprocals are locally Lipschitz in $C^M$ when the denominators have
positive floors.  Apply this first to $Q$, then to $H$, and finally to
the two axis formulas.  Small perturbations need not themselves satisfy
the separation identity.  The formulas remain defined on an open
neighborhood with the same, possibly halved, denominator floors.
The constants are not uniform as $d_2-d_1$ or a residual floor tends
to zero.  No assertion of a minimal number of offsets is made.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Actual boundary variations and cyclic blocks}

Let $\mathcal S(F)=(S_0^-,\ldots,S_{r-1}^-)$ be the future action
map in the fixed graph coordinates.  A reference half-line starting at
phase $b$ is denoted $x_{b,j}(u)$, and its $j$th unit chord direction
is $e_{b,j}(u)$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-visit-weights}
 \alpha_b(u)=\frac{e_{b,0}(u)\cdot n_b}{\nu_b},\qquad
 v_{b,j}(u)=\frac{(e_{b,j}(u)-e_{b,j-1}(u))\cdot n_{b+j}}
                        {\nu_{b+j}}\quad(j\ge1).
\end{equation}
At zero these weights are respectively $1$ and $2$.

\begin{lemma}[Stationary boundary visits]\label{lem:v65-envelope}
For an anchored smooth variation $\eta=(\eta_i)$, on a sufficiently
small common collar the derivative of the actual action is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-full-envelope}
 (D\mathcal S(F)\eta)_b(u)
   =\alpha_b(u)\eta_b(u)
        +\sum_{j\ge1}v_{b,j}(u)\eta_{b+j}(x_{b,j}(u)).
\end{equation}
The series and its stipulated fixed derivatives converge.  The action
jet through degree $M$ depends only on the graph jets through degree
$M$, for every $M\ge2$.  This is a statement about smooth representatives,
not only formal Taylor series.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The weighted Green construction of the preceding section applies to the
signed graph equations.  It gives the actual stationary half-line and
bounds $|x_{b,j}(u)|\le C|u|\rho^j$, with the corresponding bounds
for each fixed differentiated order.  Changing a graph by $\eta_i$
changes its position by $-n_i\eta_i/\nu_i$.  Direct differentiation
of one chord therefore contributes
\[
 \frac{e_{b,j}\cdot n_{b+j}}{\nu_{b+j}}
                  \eta_{b+j}(x_{b,j})
 -\frac{e_{b,j}\cdot n_{b+j+1}}{\nu_{b+j+1}}
                  \eta_{b+j+1}(x_{b,j+1}).
\]
The marked linear gauges have zero variation.  Differentiate the sum of
the first $J$ gauged chords along the stationary family.  All internal
orbit variations cancel.  The initial one is zero because its coordinate
is fixed.  Retain the terminal term
\[
 h_{b+J-1,2}(x_{b,J-1},x_{b,J})\,D_Fx_{b,J}[\eta].
\]
The first factor is $O(|u|\rho^{J-1})$, since $Dh(0,0)=0$;
the second decays exponentially by the differentiated weighted equations.
Thus this term, and each of its separately fixed derivatives, tends to
zero.  Direct graph variations are summable, because anchored variations
are $O(x^2)$.  Gathering the initial visit once and every internal visit
twice gives precisely \eqref{eq:v65-full-envelope}.

For completeness, suppose two smooth profiles have the same jets through
$M$.  Their difference obeys $\eta_i(x)=O(x^{M+1})$ on a smaller
collar.  Interpolate the profiles there.  Their positive quadratic jets
agree, and uniform small-collar estimates hold along the interpolation;
no global interpolation of obstacles is needed.  The envelope formula
gives, uniformly along this path,
\[
 |D\mathcal S(F)[\eta]_b(u)|
  \le C|u|^{M+1}\left(1+\sum_{j\ge1}\rho^{j(M+1)}\right).
\]
Integration along the path shows that the two actual actions differ by
$O(|u|^{M+1})$.  Their smoothness, already obtained from the weighted
equations, implies equality of the Taylor jets through $M$.
This also explains why equality of all smooth jets is not being identified
with equality of smooth germs.
\end{proof}

For a degree $n\ge2$ define the signed cyclic shift on $\mathbb R^r$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-shift}
 (T_n z)_b=\sigma_b^n z_{b+1},\qquad
 T_n^r=\Lambda^n I,
 \qquad \mathcal C_n=(I+T_n)(I-T_n)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The notation uses the physical $r$ contact germs, not the doubled list
of oriented charts.

\begin{theorem}[All-order periodic contact blocks]\label{thm:v65-cyclic-jets}
Let $q_n=(F_b^{(n)}(0))_b$ and
$s_n=((S_b^-)^{(n)}(0))_b$.  The map $q_2\mapsto s_2$ is locally
invertible throughout the positive-curvature class; its derivative is
$\mathcal C_2$.  For every $n\ge3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-jet-recursion}
 s_n=\mathcal C_n q_n+\mathcal R_n(q_2,\ldots,q_{n-1}),\qquad
 q_n=(I-T_n)(I+T_n)^{-1}(s_n-\mathcal R_n).
\end{equation}
The coefficients also depend on the fixed marked polygon.  The formulas
hold for actual smooth graphs.  They give a local inverse through every
finite order, without supplied curvatures.

In the maximum row-sum norm, with $a_*:=\max_b a_b<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-block-bounds}
 \max(\|\mathcal C_n\|_\infty,\|\mathcal C_n^{-1}\|_\infty)
          \le\frac{1+a_*^n}{1-a_*^n}\quad(n\ge2),\qquad
 \det\mathcal C_n=
       \frac{1-(-1)^r\Lambda^n}{1-\Lambda^n}>0.
\end{equation}
In particular the determinant is one for an even contact cycle.  For an
odd cycle it need not be one, but is never zero.  These bounds concern
the homogeneous blocks, not arbitrary lower-degree smooth couplings.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Vary only the new degree by $\eta_i(x)=z_i x^n/n!$, using a smooth
cutoff equal to one near zero when necessary.  In
\eqref{eq:v65-full-envelope} the coefficient of $u^n/n!$ uses only
$\alpha_b(0)=1$, $v_{b,j}(0)=2$, and
\eqref{eq:v65-linear-visits}.  It is therefore
\[
 z_b+2\sum_{j\ge1}
   (\sigma_b\cdots\sigma_{b+j-1})^n z_{b+j}
   =\left(I+2\sum_{j\ge1}T_n^j\right)z
   =(I+T_n)(I-T_n)^{-1}z.
\]
This proves the derivative formula in degree two.  For $n\ge3$, the
linearized orbit and the constant visit weights depend only on the
quadratic jets, not on the fresh degree.  Together with the smooth-jet
factorization of Lemma~\ref{lem:v65-envelope}, integration in that
fresh degree gives the affine identity
\eqref{eq:v65-jet-recursion}.

Since $\|T_n\|_\infty\le a_*^n<1$, the two geometric series for
$(I-T_n)^{-1}$ and $(I+T_n)^{-1}$ converge.  They give the inverse
and both estimates in \eqref{eq:v65-block-bounds}.  For a cyclic shift,
$\det(I-zT_n)=1-z^r\Lambda^n$.  Apply this at $z=1,-1$ to
obtain the displayed determinant; $|\Lambda|<1$ makes its numerator
and denominator positive.  The finite-dimensional inverse function
theorem now inverts the degree-two map locally.  Successive applications
of \eqref{eq:v65-jet-recursion} invert all later degrees.  At any fixed
order this is a smooth inverse on an open finite-jet neighborhood; its
constants can be chosen uniformly on compact subsets of that neighborhood.
No global uniqueness of the degree-two map is needed or asserted.
\end{proof}

For a normal two-contact channel, the positive signed factors are
$\sigma_b=\mathfrak r_b e^{-\gamma}$ and
$\Lambda=e^{-2\gamma}$.  Substitution in \eqref{eq:v65-shift} gives
\[
 \mathcal C_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \coth(n\gamma)&\mathfrak r_0^n\csch(n\gamma)\\
 \mathfrak r_1^n\csch(n\gamma)&\coth(n\gamma)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus the earlier determinant-one inverse is the two-site specialization.
For an odd cycle, replacing the signed $\sigma_i$ by their absolute
values would generally change the odd-degree reconstruction.  Doubling
the oriented forward period does not justify making that replacement.

\subsection{An inverse on a space of analytic germs}

Fix a real analytic graph tuple $F^*$, holomorphic near a closed disc
$\overline{D_{R_0}}$, with positive $c_i^*$.  For $R<R_0$ put
\[
 X_{R,q}=\{\eta\in H^\infty(D_R;\mathbb C^r):
                   \eta_i^{(j)}(0)=0\ (0\le j<q)\},\qquad
 \|\eta\|_R=\max_i\sup_{D_R}|\eta_i|.
\]
The marked polygon is fixed; curvature is allowed to vary in $X_{R,2}$.
The complex extension of the Euclidean chord uses the bilinear dot
product and the square-root branch near the positive reference length.

\begin{lemma}[Holomorphic graph half-lines]\label{lem:v65-holomorphic}
For all sufficiently small $R>0$ there is an open neighborhood
$\mathcal U\subset F^*+X_{R,2}$ on which the graph half-lines and
the actual future actions depend holomorphically on $F$.  Their endpoint
maps obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-protected-contraction}
 |x_{b,j}(u)|\le\rho^j|u|\quad(j\ge1,\ u\in D_R),\qquad \rho<1.
\end{equation}
The action map takes values in
$\mathcal S(F^*)+X_{R,2}$.  Its full Fr\'echet derivative is
\eqref{eq:v65-full-envelope}.  On a smaller neighborhood its coefficients
are bounded and $\inf_{b,u}|\alpha_b(u)|>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We give the function-space argument, since analyticity of finite
parameter families would not suffice.  Conjugate the reference Jacobi
operator by the diagonal signs and use its weighted Green bound.
Choose constants
\[
 \max(a_*,q)<\beta<\rho<\sigma<1,
\]
where $q<1$ is a walk-decay base of that operator.  For sequences of
holomorphic functions vanishing at zero use
$\|y\|_\beta=\sup_{j\ge1}\beta^{-j}\|y_j\|_R$.
The Green operator is bounded on this space.  For
$\|\eta\|_R<\delta R^2$, $\eta\in X_{R,2}$, the higher-order
Schwarz estimate and protected Cauchy estimates give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-protected-cauchy}
 |\eta_i(z)|\le\delta|z|^2\ (z\in D_R),\qquad
 |\eta_i^{(k)}(z)|\le C_{\sigma,k}\delta|z|^{2-k}
                  \ (z\in D_{\sigma R},\ 0\le k\le2).
\end{equation}
For the second estimate write $\eta_i=z^2v_i$ and use Cauchy's
bounds for $v_i$ on an intermediate inner disc.

An interior stationarity equation differentiates the two chords with
respect to their common interior argument.  It uses the initial graph
only through $F_b(u)$, never through $F_b'(u)$ at the unprotected
boundary argument.  Every differentiated graph is evaluated at an
interior site in $D_{\sigma R}$.  Thus neither graph differentiation
nor composition at the boundary of $D_R$ is being treated as a bounded
operator on $H^\infty(D_R)$.

Subtract the reference linear equation and apply the reference Green
operator.  On the ball
\[
 \|x-x^{\rm lin}\|_\beta\le C_0(\delta R+R^2),\qquad
 x^{\rm lin}_{b,j}(u)=(\sigma_b\cdots\sigma_{b+j-1})u,
\]
the residual is $O(\delta R+R^2)$ and its Lipschitz constant in the
interior sequence is $O(\delta+R)$.  Here the $O(\delta R)$ term
is the variation of the quadratic Jacobi coefficients.  These estimates
follow directly from the chord formulas: the square roots and reciprocal
lengths stay in fixed neighborhoods of $L_i$; first and second graph
derivatives occur only at protected interior arguments and satisfy
\eqref{eq:v65-protected-cauchy}; neighboring weights differ by at most
$\beta^{\pm1}$; products of two coordinates are $O(R^2)$ in the
weighted norm.  The fixed analytic reference has bounded higher
remainders on those discs.  Choose $C_0$ above twice the residual
constant times the Green norm, then $\delta$ and $R$ small.  The
resulting map preserves the ball and has contraction constant below
$1/2$.

The correction vanishes at $u=0$.  Schwarz's estimate gives
\[
 |x_{b,j}(u)|\le
       \{a_*^j+C_0(\delta+R)\beta^j\}|u|\le\rho^j|u|
\]
after a further reduction of $\delta,R$.  This verifies, rather than
assumes, the protected arguments used above.  On these protected sets
the Cauchy expansions of derivatives and compositions converge uniformly
as maps of the Banach variables.  All fixed-point iterates are therefore
holomorphic, and their locally uniform limit is holomorphic as well.
The gauged action summands are bounded by $CR^2\rho^{2j}$, so their
sum is a holomorphic $H^\infty(D_R)$-valued map.  Its constant and
linear jets are zero.

On a smaller parameter ball, Cauchy's estimate in the Banach parameter
gives $\|D_Fx_{b,j}[\eta]\|_R\le C_R\beta^j\|\eta\|_R$.
The retained terminal term in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:v65-envelope} consequently vanishes in the same function
norm.  Direct variations have the summable majorant
$C\rho^{2j}\|\eta\|_R$, proving the full derivative formula in
operator norm.  The chord directions are uniformly close to their
reference directions on a small disc, so the weights are bounded and
$\alpha_b$ is uniformly close to one.  Real members give strictly
convex physical arcs on a smaller real collar, by protected Cauchy
bounds and the uniqueness of the real weighted construction.
No extension of every resulting germ to a closed analytic obstacle is
asserted by this local lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete analytic periodic contact inverse]
\label{thm:v65-analytic-inverse}
For a fixed marked polygon and any positive-curvature real analytic
contact tuple, the map
\[
 \mathcal S:F^*+X_{R,2}\longrightarrow\mathcal S(F^*)+X_{R,2}
\]
is a biholomorphism between sufficiently small neighborhoods, for all
sufficiently small $R>0$.  On a smaller convex neighborhood there are
constants $0<c<C<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-bilip}
 c\|F-\widetilde F\|_R
 \le\|\mathcal S(F)-\mathcal S(\widetilde F)\|_R
 \le C\|F-\widetilde F\|_R.
\end{equation}
These bounds include curvature and all higher coefficients, together
with their lower-degree couplings.  The derivative and its inverse
induce mutually inverse operators on
$X_{R,2}/X_{R,N+1}$, bounded independently of $N$ in the induced
quotient norms.  For $0<s<R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-coefficient-bound}
 \sum_{n\ge2}\frac{s^n}{n!}
     \max_b|F_b^{(n)}(0)-\widetilde F_b^{(n)}(0)|
 \le\frac{(s/R)^2}{c(1-s/R)}
                \|\mathcal S(F)-\mathcal S(\widetilde F)\|_R.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the base derivative $L=D\mathcal S(F^*)$.  In
\eqref{eq:v65-full-envelope} write $L=A+K$, where $A$ is the
invertible diagonal multiplier $\alpha_b$ and $K$ is the sum of
contracted visits.  Let $\|A^{-1}\|\le a^{-1}$ and
$\sup_{b,j,u}|v_{b,j}(u)|\le W$.  On $X_{R,m}$ the Schwarz bound
gives
\[
 \|K|_{X_{R,m}}\|\le\frac{W\rho^m}{1-\rho^m}.
\]
Choose a single finite $m>2$ so that
$q_m=a^{-1}W\rho^m/(1-\rho^m)<1$.
The tail inverse is the norm-convergent operator series
\[
 (L|_{X_{R,m}})^{-1}
       =\sum_{j\ge0}(-A^{-1}K)^jA^{-1},\qquad
 \|(L|_{X_{R,m}})^{-1}\|\le\frac{a^{-1}}{1-q_m}.
\]
Let $P_m$ retain the Taylor degrees $2,\ldots,m-1$.
Cauchy's coefficient bounds make it a bounded finite-rank projection.
The finite quotient $P_mL|_{P_mX_{R,2}}$ is block lower triangular,
with diagonal blocks $\mathcal C_2,\ldots,\mathcal C_{m-1}$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v65-cyclic-jets}.  Every block is invertible, including
those at odd degree for an odd contact cycle.  Hence the quotient has
an inverse.  Given $y$, first solve that finite quotient for
$\eta_{\rm low}$ and then set
\[
 \eta_{\rm tail}=(L|_{X_{R,m}})^{-1}(y-L\eta_{\rm low}).
\]
The residual has zero jets below $m$, so this is defined and gives a
unique bounded full inverse.  This is the contracted-evaluation argument
of Proposition~\ref{prop:v60-contracted-criterion}, here with $q=2$,
$r$ components, and the actual geometric blocks just computed.  In
particular boundedness of the diagonal blocks alone has not been used
to assert boundedness of an infinite triangular inverse.

The action map is holomorphic by Lemma~\ref{lem:v65-holomorphic}.
Write its increment as $L\eta+\mathcal N(\eta)$, where
$D\mathcal N(0)=0$, and shrink the ball until
$\|L^{-1}D\mathcal N\|<1/2$.  The equation
$\eta=L^{-1}y-L^{-1}\mathcal N(\eta)$ is then a contraction,
with holomorphic locally uniform iterates.  It proves the asserted
biholomorphism.  On a smaller convex ball make
$\|D\mathcal S-L\|<(2\|L^{-1}\|)^{-1}$.
Integration along line segments proves \eqref{eq:v65-bilip}.
Conjugation and uniqueness give the real analytic inverse for real data.

Both $L$ and $L^{-1}$ preserve every higher vanishing tail: preservation
by $L$ follows from the envelope formula, and preservation by $L^{-1}$
follows inductively from the invertible homogeneous blocks.  Their
quotient operator norms are bounded by their full operator norms,
independently of $N$.  Continuity gives the same local assertion at
nearby base points.  Cauchy's coefficient inequality and
\eqref{eq:v65-bilip}, summed over $n\ge2$, give
\eqref{eq:v65-coefficient-bound}.  These are analytic norms on a fixed
disc, not maximum norms of arbitrarily many unweighted smooth derivatives.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rigidity from physical laws and its observation topology}

\begin{corollary}[Periodic contact rigidity and finite-flight inversion]
\label{cor:v65-law-rigidity}
Fix the marked polygon, the graph-coordinate sensors at every physical
phase, and two offsets as above.  Their limiting conditional laws locally
determine all finite jets of every contact in the smooth category.
In the analytic category they determine the complete local contact germs.
No curvature, normal graph value, or flux amplitude is supplied.

For a fixed $M\ge2$, densities of $N$-flight bridges, $N$ a multiple
of a returning oriented period, determine the jets through order $M$
on the chosen local inverse branch with error at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-finite-flight-error}
 C_M\{\tau^N+\epsilon\},
\end{equation}
when the recorded densities at both offsets and all phases have interior
$C^M$ error at most $\epsilon$.  The constants are local fixed-order
constants.  This statement is about error in densities, not acquisition
of those densities from a stipulated number of preparations.

If two actual periodic tables have the same fixed lattice and obstacle
labels, every obstacle is visited by the marked polygon, and their
boundaries are connected closed embedded real analytic curves, equality
of these laws implies equality of their full obstacle images, provided
the contact germs lie in the stated local analytic inverse neighborhood.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:v65-two-offset} recovers $A_b,C_b$ and hence
$S_b^\pm$ in the measured graph coordinates.  Theorem~\ref{thm:v65-cyclic-jets}
inverts their finite jets locally, beginning with degree two.
For analytic germs, equality on a real interval makes the action
extensions equal by the identity theorem.  Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:v65-analytic-inverse}.  In fact the future actions at
all phases suffice for the geometric inverse; the recovered past actions
provide additional data, not an assumed symmetry.

The graph-coordinate version of Theorem~\ref{thm:v64-physical-law}
gives the density error $C_M\tau^N$ on the same square at both offsets.
The rational extraction is locally Lipschitz there.  Before finite-jet
inversion subtract the constant and linear Taylor terms of an estimated
gauged action; this bounded projection fixes the exact action.  Apply the
local degree-two inverse and then the finitely many smooth operations in
\eqref{eq:v65-jet-recursion}.  They give
\eqref{eq:v65-finite-flight-error}.

Finally equality of contact germs gives an open common boundary arc on
each visited labelled obstacle.  Two connected embedded analytic closed
curves which agree on an open arc agree everywhere.  Indeed at an endpoint
of a continued common arc both curves are locally graphs over their
common tangent; the one-variable identity theorem continues equality
across that endpoint.  Compactness and connectedness then continue around
the whole curve.  Every obstacle is visited, so all labelled boundary
images coincide.  The lattice was fixed, and therefore so does the table.
This continuation argument gives exact rigidity, not a global quantitative
continuation estimate and not reconstruction of an unmarked lattice.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional stability from real laws]
\label{thm:v65-real-stability}
Fix a positive-curvature analytic base tuple and its marked polygon.
There are radii $0<s<r_0<\varrho<R$, with
$r_0+2s<\varrho$, a bounded neighborhood of that tuple in
$F^*+X_{R,2}$, and $C<\infty$, $0<\theta<1$, for which the
following holds.  Choose the two offsets and the square
$Q=[-s,s]^2$ inside their common positive-density region.
For two admissible tuples in this neighborhood set
\[
 \epsilon=\max_{b,d\in\{d_1,d_2\}}
       \|f_{b,d}-\widetilde f_{b,d}\|_{C^0(Q)}.
\]
For $0\le\epsilon\le\epsilon_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v65-real-holder}
 \|F-\widetilde F\|_{r_0}\le C\epsilon^\theta.
\end{equation}
The analytic prior, local inverse branch, fixed marked coordinates,
positive window and radius loss are part of the assertion.
For finite-flight densities the right side may instead use
$C(\epsilon+\tau^N)^\theta$ when the corresponding two finite
experiments are compared.  No total-preparation budget is asserted here.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose small nested radii on which the holomorphic construction holds,
and use Theorem~\ref{thm:v65-analytic-inverse} also on $D_{r_0}$.
Shrink the neighborhood in the larger-disc norm until all its restrictions
belong to that smaller-disc inverse neighborhood.  This is possible by
bounded restriction; for anchored increments the sharper bound is
$\|\eta\|_{r_0}\le(r_0/R)^2\|\eta\|_R$.
The larger-disc action norms have a uniform bound by
Lemma~\ref{lem:v65-holomorphic}.

The $C^0$ case of Proposition~\ref{prop:v65-two-offset} gives
$\max_b\|S_b^--\widetilde S_b^-\|_{C^0([-s,s])}\le C_0\epsilon$.
The momenta cancel because the marked polygon is fixed.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:v60-real-to-disc} to each holomorphic action difference,
using its common larger-disc bound.  Explicitly, its exponent can be
chosen as
\[
 A_0=\frac{2(r_0+s)}s,\qquad
 b_0=\frac{r_0+s}{\varrho-s}<1,\qquad
 \theta=\frac{\log(1/b_0)}{\log(A_0/b_0)}.
\]
That lemma yields an $O(\epsilon^\theta)$ bound for the action
differences on $D_{r_0}$.  The complete inverse bound on that disc
proves \eqref{eq:v65-real-holder}.  When $\epsilon=0$, use the
zero-error case of the same lemma or the identity theorem.
The triangle inequality and the uniform finite-flight relative law add
$O(\tau^N)$ before this argument is applied, proving the last assertion.
A bounded analytic prior is essential in this argument; unrestricted
analytic continuation or same-disc stability from a real norm is not
being inferred.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A nonnormal analytic realization}

\begin{proposition}[A three-contact family with varying curvature]
\label{prop:v65-nonnormal-rigidity}
There are noncircular analytic three-obstacle periodic tables with a
marked nongrazing three-contact polygon to which the preceding inverse
applies.  The marked polygon stays fixed in a family whose three contact
curvatures vary independently.  With the lattice and obstacle labels
fixed, the phase-resolved two-offset laws locally determine every full
obstacle image in that family.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the three-disk realization in Proposition~\ref{prop:v64-triangle},
or a sufficiently small scalene perturbation of its contact triangle.
Let $q_i$ be its vertices and $n_i$ the inward unit bisectors of the
triangle, which are the outward normals of the obstacles there.
For unit reference radii the centers are $q_i-n_i$.
Choose $t_i$ perpendicular to $n_i$.  In polar angle $\vartheta$
about each center, replace the radius by
\[
 R_i(\vartheta)=1+\delta_i(1-\cos\vartheta)
                                  (1+\eta_i\sin\vartheta),
\]
and use radial direction $n_i\cos\vartheta+t_i\sin\vartheta$.
The value and first derivative of the radius at zero are $1,0$.
Thus the contact point and tangent are unchanged.  Its second derivative
at zero is $\delta_i$, and the polar curvature formula gives contact
curvature $1-\delta_i$.  The three parameters $\delta_i$ therefore
vary the three curvatures independently.

For sufficiently small parameters these are analytic strictly convex
closed curves: the polar curvature numerator
$R_i^2+2(R_i')^2-R_iR_i''$ stays positive by continuity from one.
Their disjointness, lifted disjointness and selected-flight clearance
persist by the strict margins in Proposition~\ref{prop:v64-triangle}.
Reflection on the fixed polygon continues to hold because its normals
are fixed.  Nonzero generic parameters give noncircular obstacles;
unequal choices and a scalene triangle require no axial symmetry.
Every obstacle is visited.  The local analytic germs belong to the
inverse neighborhood after the parameters are made small, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:v65-law-rigidity} proves the final assertion.
The physical period is three; the returning oriented forward period may
be six.  Only three unknown contact germs are inverted.
\end{proof}

The new inverse and the alternating inverse have different data
requirements.  Here the full marked polygon and phase-resolved laws at
two offsets are supplied, while curvatures and complete contact germs
are recovered locally.  The alternating results below need only the gap
and a single law of each contact type, and retain their unknown-lattice,
finite-ambiguity, calibration and total-preparation conclusions.  Neither
observation model is substituted for the other.  In the general-period
experiment the exact success mass remains the one in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v64-physical-law}; conditional reconstruction does not
make rejected preparations free.
