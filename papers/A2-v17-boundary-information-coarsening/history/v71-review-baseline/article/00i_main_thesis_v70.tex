\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:v70-introduction}

Does a conditional law supported near one hyperbolic periodic orbit
retain the reflecting boundary as a smooth function, or only its Taylor
series at the orbit?  The distinction is substantial.  The mass of a long
bridge tends to zero exponentially, while its endpoints can remain in a
fixed neighborhood of the contacts.  Moreover, a smooth boundary can
change on every such neighborhood without changing any contact jet.
The main result of this paper identifies the complete smooth contact
germs from phase-resolved endpoint laws at two positive excess times.
It uses neither analyticity nor an initially chosen local inverse branch.
A finite-preparation consequence recovers the complete real profiles on
bounded geometric classes, with unsuccessful preparations included in
the observation budget.

The proof has two essential interfaces.  An exact mixed-twist
normalization passes from stationary lengths to physical conditional
laws without losing the exponentially small scale.  An envelope identity
for actual reflecting graphs then passes from the observed half-line
actions to smooth boundary functions, including their flat parts.
Neither passage follows from inversion of formal action coefficients.
We state this mechanism first; analytic continuation and unregistered
multichannel reconstruction are subsequent results with different data.
The local-completion comparison in Section~\ref{sec:v71-local-observation}
identifies exactly why these laws determine visited contact germs without
supplying a global exterior scattering observation.

\subsection{The marked experiment}

Fix a periodic polygon with $r$ distinct physical contacts in a planar
periodic dispersing billiard.  Its lifted vertices $q_i$, signed unit
tangents $t_i$, outward obstacle normals $n_i$, phase labels and closing
translation are supplied.  The polygon satisfies the specular reflection
condition, has positive flight lengths and nongrazing incidence, and its
open flights are clear of unintended obstacles.  These are geometric
marks, not measurements of curvature or boundary height.  Put
\[
 L_i=|q_{i+1}-q_i|,\qquad e_i=(q_{i+1}-q_i)/L_i,\qquad
 \nu_i=e_i\cdot n_i>0,\qquad p_i=e_i\cdot t_i/\nu_i.
\]
The unknown contacts have the graph representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v70-graphs}
 R_i(x)=q_i+\nu_i^{-1}\{t_i x-n_iF_i(x)\},\qquad
 F_i(0)=F_i'(0)=0,\qquad F_i''(0)>0.
\end{equation}
The recorded coordinate $x=\nu_i t_i\cdot(R_i-q_i)$ is a tangent
projection; recording it does not reveal $F_i(x)$.  In particular,
neither normal collision coordinates nor unknown arclength conversions
are supplied to the inverse.

Let $P$ be a returning oriented period, allowing the physical period to
be doubled so that signed frames return, and let $\mathcal L$ be its
length.  For $N=nP$ flights starting at phase $b$, write $W_{N,b}(u,v)$
for the stationary bridge length and set
\[
 E_{N,b}=W_{N,b}-n\mathcal L+p_bu-p_bv,
 \qquad D_{N,b}=-W_{N,b,uv}(0,0)>0.
\]
Two distinct fixed offsets $0<d_1<d_2$ are selected in the small positive
time window.  The observation at offset $d$ is the pair of tangent
endpoints conditional on the specified itinerary event.  It is restricted
to a common square on which the physical residual time is positive.
The excess $d$ is fixed as $N$ increases; it is not sent to zero in the
long-flight limit.  The exact event and full-phase normalization are
specified in Theorem~\ref{thm:v64-physical-law}.

\begin{theorem}[Boundary determination by periodic endpoint laws]
\label{thm:v70-main}
For every clear nongrazing marked polygon as above, with positive-curvature
$C^\infty$ reflecting contacts, the following statements hold on a
contact collar independent of $N$.

There are anchored smooth half-line actions $S_b^-,S_b^+$ and positive
amplitudes $B_b^-,B_b^+$, with $B_b^\pm(0)=1$, such that, for each
separately fixed finite order $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v70-relative}
 \|E_{N,b}-S_b^-\oplus S_b^+\|_{C^k}
 +\left\|\frac{-W_{N,b,uv}}{D_{N,b}}
                       -B_b^-\otimes B_b^+\right\|_{C^k}
 \le C_k\tau^N,\qquad 0<\tau<1.
\end{equation}
In returning signed Jacobi coordinates the exact reference is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v70-reference}
 D_{N,b}=\frac{\sinh\chi}
 { (\mathcal M_b)_{12}\sinh(n\chi)},\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}\mathcal M_b=2\cosh\chi.
\end{equation}
The transfer matrix $\mathcal M_b$ is determined by the actual geometric
Jacobi operator, not additional observation data.

The conditional endpoint densities converge in every fixed $C^k$ norm
on positive interior squares to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v70-laws}
 f_{b,d}(u,v)=Z_{b,d}^{-1}B_b^-(u)B_b^+(v)
                 \{d-A_b(u)-C_b(v)\},\qquad d=d_1,d_2,
\end{equation}
where $A_b(u)=S_b^-(u)-p_bu$, $C_b(v)=S_b^+(v)+p_bv$ and
the positive constants $Z_{b,d}$ are unknown and may differ.
Equality of the phase-resolved two-offset laws for two actual contact
tuples implies equality of their complete smooth contact germs in
\eqref{eq:v70-graphs}.  No curvature input, reflection symmetry,
analyticity or closeness between the two candidates is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The relative theorem and the physical measure formula are
Theorems~\ref{thm:v64-periodic-relative} and
\ref{thm:v64-physical-law}.  Lemma~\ref{lem:v65-signed-jacobi}
transfers them to the measured graph coordinates without treating the
unknown boundary as a coordinate measurement.
Proposition~\ref{prop:v65-two-offset} extracts the actual actions.
The global quadratic comparison in Theorem~\ref{thm:v66-curvature}
and the signed finite-jet inverse in Theorem~\ref{thm:v65-cyclic-jets}
identify the jets.  Theorem~\ref{thm:v68-smooth-rigidity}, using the
actual envelope once more, identifies the smooth functions.  Complete
proofs of these statements occupy
Sections~\ref{sec:v64-periodic-itinerary}--\ref{sec:v68-smooth-contact}.
The next subsection explains the two passages at which an absolute
asymptotic or a formal inverse would be insufficient.
\end{proof}

\subsection{From a rare physical law to a smooth boundary}

The mixed twist is the scale of the unconditioned endpoint measure.
Indeed, writing $\beta_{N,b}=-W_{N,b,uv}/D_{N,b}$, its density on a
positive-residual square is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v70-physical-interface}
 \frac{D_{N,b}}{2\pi\mathcal A}\,
 \beta_{N,b}(u,v)\{d-E_{N,b}(u,v)+p_bu-p_bv\},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal A$ is the free cell area.  The linear momenta in this
formula cannot be discarded by a change of action gauge.  Although
$D_{N,b}$ cancels upon conditioning, that cancellation supplies a limit
only after the normalized twist $\beta_{N,b}$ has been controlled.
An absolute estimate for $W_{N,b}$ alone supplies no such control.

The cofactor formula resolves this scale mismatch before a probability
quotient is formed.  In the returning signed coordinates, if $H_N$ is
the interior Hessian, $G_N=H_N(0)^{-1}$ and
$\Delta H_N=H_N-H_N(0)$, then
\[
 \log\beta_{N,b}
 =\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\log
   \frac{-\ell_{b+i,12}(y_i,y_{i+1})}{L_{b+i}^{-1}}
       -\log\det(I+G_N\Delta H_N).
\]
The geometric perturbation is localized near the two endpoints, so its
trace norm is bounded independently of the number of interior sites.
Weighted Green estimates separate the two end contributions.  The
result is a relative limit for the physical measure, not a quadratic
conditional model to which a nonlinear correction is later added.
The full construction and differentiated determinant comparison are in
Section~\ref{sec:v64-periodic-itinerary}.

The density-to-action passage is exact and does not require a known
flux amplitude.  Suppressing the phase, define
\[
 \mathfrak q(u,v)=
 \frac{f_{d_1}(u,v)f_{d_2}(0,0)}
      {f_{d_2}(u,v)f_{d_1}(0,0)},\qquad
 H(u,v)=\frac{d_1d_2(1-\mathfrak q(u,v))}
                   {d_2-d_1\mathfrak q(u,v)}.
\]
Cancellation in \eqref{eq:v70-laws} gives
$H(u,v)=A(u)+C(v)$, whence $A(u)=H(u,0)$ and $C(v)=H(0,v)$.
The denominator is positive on the stipulated square.  This operation
uses the two exact offsets and the fixed origin, but not acceptance
counts, curvature or normal height.  It is applied to realizable laws;
an arbitrary fitted density need not satisfy this identity.

The action-to-boundary passage is not merely a coefficient calculation.
The global Schur-complement comparison determines positive curvatures
without choosing a nearby inverse branch.  With these quadratic data
fixed, the signed response at order $j\ge3$ has the form
\[
 s_j=(I+T_j)(I-T_j)^{-1}q_j+\mathcal R_j,
 \qquad (T_jz)_i=\sigma_i^jz_{i+1},\qquad |\sigma_i|<1,
\]
where $\mathcal R_j$ depends only on lower graph jets.
The finite stationary envelope, with its terminal contribution estimated
before passage to a half-line, proves that these are derivatives of the
actual boundary map.

To pass beyond the jets, interpolate the actual graphs from $F$ to $G$.
The integrated future-action envelope is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v70-envelope-interface}
 \mathcal S(G)-\mathcal S(F)=\overline\alpha(G-F)+K(G-F),
 \qquad \overline\alpha\ge\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
Each term in $K$ evaluates a difference at a later visit
$x_{b,j}(u)$, with $|x_{b,j}(u)|\le a^j|u|$ for a single $a<1$
and, crucially, with multiplicative constant one.  On functions vanishing
through order $m-1$, put
\[
 \|h\|_{m,R}=\max_b\sup_{0<|u|\le R}\frac{|h_b(u)|}{|u|^m}.
\]
The actual chord weights give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v70-tail}
 \|\overline\alpha^{-1}K\|_{m,R}
        \le\frac{6a^m}{1-a^m}<1
\end{equation}
for a sufficiently large finite $m$.  Equal jets make the norm of $G-F$
finite; equality of the full actions in
\eqref{eq:v70-envelope-interface} then forces that difference to vanish.
The operator depends on the candidate pair, which is appropriate for a
separation proof and is not a directly computable observation-only
inverse.  For approximate data, low-degree polynomial alignment is made
\emph{before} the weighted estimate is used.  This yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v70-real-stability}
 \|F-G\|_{C^0([-R,R])}\le
 C\max_{b,d}\|f^F_{b,d}-f^G_{b,d}\|_{C^m(Q)}
\end{equation}
on the bounded positive $C^{m+3}$ classes of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v68-smooth-stability}, for sufficiently small realizable
law distance.  Thus smooth identity and finite-order stability use the
same geometric mechanism, with different uniformity hypotheses.

\subsection{Finite preparations and information beyond Taylor data}

The differentiated norm in \eqref{eq:v70-real-stability} need not be
provided as an observation.  Fix a nonempty bounded class $\mathscr C$
of actual marked tables as in Section~\ref{sec:v69-sampled-contact}.
In addition to the bounded positive $C^{m+3}$ contact class, impose the
finitely many higher graph bounds needed for the forward law through
order $m+s$, where $s\ge1$.  Clearance, incidence, flight-length and
residual margins, an upper free-area bound, the period and the two
separated offsets are fixed.  These priors give
\[
 \|f_N-f\|_{C^m(Q_+)}\le C_0e^{-\omega N},\qquad
 \|f_N\|_{C^{m+s}(Q_+)}+\|f\|_{C^{m+s}(Q_+)}\le H,
 \qquad p_N\ge c_0e^{-\Gamma N}.
\]
Here $Q\Subset Q_+$, acceptance is gated on $Q_+$, and densities
normalized on $Q_+$ are merely restricted, not renormalized, on $Q$.
Each independent full-phase preparation returns an exact success/failure
tag and, on success, an endpoint pair with recording error at most
$\delta$.  Tags, gates and offsets remain exact.  There are $J=2r$
phase--offset groups.

\begin{theorem}[Complete-profile recovery with a charged budget]
\label{thm:v70-finite}
In the preceding experiment there is a measurable estimator taking values
in actual tables of $\mathscr C$.  For $B$ preparations in each group,
set
\[
 \alpha=\frac{s}{2(m+s)+2},\qquad
 \beta=\frac{\alpha\omega}{\omega+\alpha\Gamma},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{s}{m+s+3},\qquad L_B=\log(C_*JB/\zeta).
\]
Choose the returning flight number
\[
 N_B=P\left\lfloor
 \frac{\alpha\log(B/L_B)}{P(\omega+\alpha\Gamma)}
 \right\rfloor.
\]
For $0<\zeta<1/2$, under the sufficient-success, bandwidth and
small-error conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:v69-charged-recovery},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v70-finite-rate}
 \sup_{T\in\mathscr C}\mathbb P_T\!\left(
 \|F^{\widehat T}-F^T\|_{C^0([-R,R])}>
 C\{(L_B/B)^\beta+\delta^\gamma\}\right)\le2\zeta.
\end{equation}
All $JB$ preparations, including failures, are charged.  At fixed
confidence the conditions hold for sufficiently large $B$ and
sufficiently small recording error.  The target is the complete real
profile on a class-dependent collar, not a finite jet.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:v69-density-estimation} gives an interior kernel estimate
in the required differentiated norm.  The estimator is first compared
with a countable dense subset of the image of actual tables in the
product $C^m$ space.  This measurable minimum-distance selection,
followed by the triangle inequality, permits
\eqref{eq:v70-real-stability} to be used only between realizable
limiting laws.  Theorem~\ref{thm:v69-successful-samples} supplies the
accepted-sample bound.  The binomial success estimate and logarithmic
choice of $N_B$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:v69-charged-recovery} give
\eqref{eq:v70-finite-rate}.
\end{proof}

For varying confidence, in particular, the requirements
$N_B\ge N_0$, $n_B=\lfloor Bc_0e^{-\Gamma N_B}/2\rfloor\ge2$
and $Bc_0e^{-\Gamma N_B}\ge8\log(J/\zeta)$ are retained; the upper
bandwidth and local comparison restrictions are retained as well.
The estimator is an existence construction, not an efficient search
algorithm.  The rate is a sufficient upper bound, not a minimax
threshold.  The constants and the order $m$ are not uniform in a
vanishing contraction margin or a varying period.  Independence is a
property of the preparations, not a conclusion about a single unreset
billiard trajectory.  Bounded endpoint errors after acceptance do not
cover uncertain gate membership, phase labels or offsets.

The geometric content of the recovered function can be tested within
the physical class.  Proposition~\ref{prop:v68-flat-family} constructs
actual closed obstacles with fixed free area and marked polygon for which
all contact and action Taylor jets agree, but the two-offset laws differ
on every sufficiently small contact square.  The perturbation at one
contact is $\varepsilon\chi(u)e^{-1/u^2}$, extended smoothly by zero
at $u=0$; a remote area correction leaves the local actions unchanged.
Its real action response is
\[
 S_b^{-,\varepsilon}(u)-S_b^{-,0}(u)
  =\varepsilon e^{-1/u^2}\left\{1+O(|u|)
       +O\bigl(e^{-(a^{-2}-1)/u^2}\bigr)\right\}.
\]
At any fixed nonzero $u_*$ where $\chi=1$, the profile separation is
at least $\Delta=\varepsilon e^{-1/u_*^2}$.  When the error radius in
\eqref{eq:v70-finite-rate} is less than $\Delta/3$, nearest-candidate
comparison gives a finite charged test with error at either table at
most $2\zeta$.  This separates complete-law information from the
formal all-order contact record.  It makes no claim about equality of
normalized density jets or of full marked length spectra, and it does
not give a uniform inexpensive resolution as $u_*\to0$.

\subsection{Relation to lens and spectral rigidity}

\input{article/00k_lens_comparison_v71}

The comparison with marked length rigidity is a comparison of inverse
problems, not an inclusion of data sets.  B\'alint--De Simoi--Kaloshin--Leguil
\cite{BDKL} recover two-periodic contact curvature and marked Lyapunov
data from the marked length spectrum of open dispersing billiards.
Their Theorem D and Corollary E treat arbitrary periods; Remark 2.3
identifies a limitation of the particular asymptotic relations used
there in separating higher-period contact parameters.  That remark is
not an obstruction theorem for every use of the full spectrum.  Here
the phase-resolved endpoint experiment supplies different information,
and its polygon and frames are fixed marks.

De Simoi--Kaloshin--Leguil \cite{DSKL} determine analytic open billiards
from marked lengths under their non-eclipse, symmetry and genericity
assumptions.  Finamore--Leguil \cite{FinamoreLeguil} use an enriched
marked length spectrum in the finite-horizon Sinai setting.  Smooth
contact determination from \eqref{eq:v70-laws} removes analyticity
in the present observation problem; it does not remove hypotheses
from those theorems or assert same-data dominance.  The corrected
version of Florio--Leguil \cite{FlorioLeguil} retains dynamical
conjugacy conclusions, not the removed geometric spectral-rigidity
assertion.  These comparisons do not establish an exhaustive priority
claim.

The statistical ingredients in Theorem~\ref{thm:v70-finite} are classical:
interior kernel approximation, countable minimum-distance selection and
concentration.  For uniform kernel estimates see, for example,
Gin\'e--Guillou \cite{GineGuillou2002}; the exact derivative, gate and
bounded-readout estimate needed here is proved in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v69-density-estimation}.  The acquisition theorem changes
the admissible input from an exact law to a finite charged record.  The
geometric identification it uses is the relative physical law together
with the actual contracted-visit envelope, rather than a new general
method of statistical regularization.

Several further conclusions are developed in the paper without being
premises of Theorem~\ref{thm:v70-main}.  The analytic Banach inverse
controls complete holomorphic germs on a small disc and requires its
analytic norm.  Whole-obstacle continuation requires connected closed
analytic boundaries, fixed placement and visitation of every obstacle.
The unregistered alternating-channel problem instead has weaker local
marks, a single-offset extraction and separate finite-channel matching
hypotheses; it yields the stated lattice reconstruction and finite
ambiguity.  The finite-jet stopping estimates retain the propagated
curvature error $C_M(\tau^N+\varepsilon)+L_ME_m$.  None of these
statements is combined with smooth contact recovery to assert an
assumption-free whole-table inverse.

\subsection{Organization of the proof}

The central route is printed first.  Section~\ref{sec:v64-periodic-itinerary}
constructs the periodic Jacobi problem, proves the relative law and derives
the exact physical measure.  Section~\ref{sec:v65-periodic-inverse}
identifies measured coordinates and proves two-offset separation, the
actual stationary envelope and signed finite-jet inversion; its analytic
norm theorem is a further branch of the argument.
Section~\ref{sec:v66-global-contact} supplies the global positive
quadratic comparison and the corrected stopping estimate.
Section~\ref{sec:v68-smooth-contact} proves constant-one visit contraction,
smooth separation, polynomially aligned stability and the actual flat
family.  Section~\ref{sec:v69-sampled-contact} then proves the finite
experiment.  Section~\ref{sec:v71-local-observation} proves exact
locality of the charged record and gives equal-area completions that
separate this observation from exterior scattering data.  In particular,
the global analytic and lattice results are
not hidden inputs to the smooth theorem.

The subsequent parts contain the finite-chain foundations, the more
specialized alternating formulas, and the analytic and multichannel
consequences with their complete proofs.  The appendices retain the
broader formulations, comparison arguments and coordinate conversions.
The complete technical manuscript additionally contains the calibration,
coarsening and acquisition catalogue.  These complementary inverse
problems are organized by their observations and targets, rather than
counted as separate proofs of the central contact theorem.
