\section{Finite channel skeletons and generic periodic rigidity}
\label{sec:v45-generic-rigidity}

The incidence hypotheses of the intrinsic inverse are not restricted to
specially arranged families.  We prove that a finite clear channel skeleton
exists in every positively separated periodic table, and that its
signature-rigidity requirements hold on an open dense set.  The resulting
inverse uses $N+1$ selected channels for $N$ labelled obstacle orbits.  The
selection is a geometric existence statement, not an algorithm for discovering
unmarked channels from a trajectory.

Write $K_a=\overline D_a$, $1\le a\le N$, and fix representatives in the
plane.  Let $L\in\mathrm{GL}^+(2,\mathbb R)$ realize the marked deck group
$\mathbb Z^2$.  Throughout this section distinct members of
\[
 \mathscr B=\{K_a+Lm:a\in\{1,\ldots,N\},\ m\in\mathbb Z^2\}
\]
are disjoint compact strictly convex bodies with smooth boundary and positive
curvature.  Analyticity will be imposed only when signatures and complete
image recovery are used.  An edge is counted without its reverse; loops and
multiple edges in the quotient graph are allowed.

\subsection{Clear closest segments and descent through an obstruction}

For two distinct bodies $A,B\in\mathscr B$, let $d(A,B)>0$ be their distance
and let $[p_{AB},q_{AB}]$ be their closest segment.  The closest points are
unique.  Indeed, strict convexity of the squared norm makes the minimizing
vector in $B-A$ unique, and the two supporting faces perpendicular to this
vector consist of single points.  We call the pair \emph{clear} if its closed
closest segment meets no member of $\mathscr B\setminus\{A,B\}$.  Thus a
third-body tangency is an obstruction, not a clear pair.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform separation and finite distance sublevels]
\label{lem:v45-finite-sublevels}
There is $\sigma>0$ such that $d(A,B)\ge\sigma$ for all distinct bodies in
$\mathscr B$.  For every $D<\infty$, only finitely many pairs at distance at
most $D$ occur modulo common deck translation.  A clear closest segment has
positive distance from the union of all third bodies.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $R$ with $K_a\subset\overline B(0,R)$ for every $a$, and write
$s=s_{\min}(L)>0$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v45-finite-label-bound}
 d(K_a,K_b+L\ell)\ge s|\ell|-2R.
\end{equation}
For any fixed finite upper distance, the possible $\ell$ therefore belong to
a finite set.  Select one pair and use its finite positive distance as an
upper bound when taking the minimum over all pairs.  The minimum is attained
among finitely many disjoint pairs and is positive.  This proves the first
two assertions.  For the last, bodies intersecting the closed unit
neighborhood of a fixed compact segment form a finite set, by the same
estimate.  The distance from the segment to each of these third bodies is
positive when the pair is clear; all remaining bodies have distance greater
than one.  Taking a finite minimum proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Obstruction descent]
\label{lem:v45-obstruction-descent}
For every pair $A\ne B$ there is a finite path of clear pairs
\[
 A=A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_k=B
\]
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v45-descent-budget}
 \sum_{i=1}^k d(A_{i-1},A_i)\le d(A,B),\qquad
 k\le\left\lfloor\frac{d(A,B)}{\sigma}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
In particular, the graph of clear pairs on all lifted obstacles is connected.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If the pair is not clear, choose a third body $C$ and
$z\in C\cap[p_{AB},q_{AB}]$.  Separation implies that $z$ is in the open
segment.  With $d=d(A,B)$, the two shorter distances obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v45-descent-step}
 \begin{split}
 d(A,C)&\le |p_{AB}-z|,\qquad d(C,B)\le|z-q_{AB}|,\\
 d(A,C)+d(C,B)&\le d,\qquad
 \max\{d(A,C),d(C,B)\}\le d-\sigma.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The last inequality uses $|p_{AB}-z|,|z-q_{AB}|\ge\sigma$.
Induct on $\lfloor d/\sigma\rfloor$.  A blocked pair cannot have
$d<2\sigma$; each pair on the right of \eqref{eq:v45-descent-step} has a
strictly smaller induction index.  Concatenate their clear paths.  Their
total edge distance is at most $d(A,C)+d(C,B)\le d$.  Every terminal edge
has distance at least $\sigma$, proving the bound on $k$.
Erasing any repeated-vertex subwalk gives a path with the same bounds.
This proof also treats tangential obstructions.  No length inside an
intermediate body is charged: the assertion concerns a graph path, not a
physical billiard itinerary or a bound on its travel time.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite skeleton with two independent deck cycles}

An oriented quotient edge is $e=(a,b,\ell)$, joining $K_a$ to $K_b+L\ell$.
A path lifts uniquely once its starting lift is fixed, and its deck gain is
the signed sum of its edge labels.  For a closed quotient walk this gain
belongs to $\mathbb Z^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite clear skeleton]
\label{thm:v45-clear-skeleton}
Every table in the setting above has a set $E$ of $N+1$ clear selected
channels whose quotient multigraph is connected and contains two closed
walks with linearly independent deck gains.  It is the union of a spanning
tree and two further edges.  All selected contacts have positive
third-obstacle clearance and admit fixed gates and alternating contact
collars.  The same finite labels, tree, gains and gates persist for
sufficiently small $C^2$ perturbations of the supports and small perturbations
of $L$ that preserve the stated setting.

More precisely, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v45-seed-distance}
 D_0=\max\bigl(\{d(K_1,K_a):2\le a\le N\}
          \cup\{d(K_1,K_1+L\mathsf e_i):i=1,2\}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The channels may be selected among the finite set of clear pairs with gap
at most $D_0$.  With $R,s$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:v45-finite-sublevels}, their
labels satisfy $|\ell|\le(D_0+2R)/s$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:v45-obstruction-descent} to the $N-1$ pairs
$(K_1,K_a)$ and to the two pairs $(K_1,K_1+L\mathsf e_i)$.
Every edge on the resulting finite paths has gap at most $D_0$.
Project their union to a finite quotient graph $\Gamma$.  It is connected:
the first group of paths reaches every obstacle type.  The last two paths
project to closed walks based at type $1$ whose deck gains are exactly
$\mathsf e_1$ and $\mathsf e_2$.  In particular the gains of all closed
walks in $\Gamma$ generate the whole deck group, not merely a real
one-dimensional subspace.  Finiteness and the label bound follow from
\eqref{eq:v45-finite-label-bound}.

Choose a spanning tree $\mathcal T$ of $\Gamma$, rooted at type $1$.
For its root-to-$a$ path let $m_a\in\mathbb Z^2$ be the signed deck sum,
with $m_1=0$.  For an oriented edge $e=(a,b,\ell_e)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v45-fundamental-gain}
                       \eta_e=m_a+\ell_e-m_b.
\end{equation}
This is zero for tree edges.  The gain of any closed walk is the signed
sum of its $\eta_e$, since the $m_a$ terms telescope.  Conversely, for a
non-tree edge, $\eta_e$ is the gain of the walk from the root to $a$ along
the tree, then across $e$, and back along the tree from $b$ to the root.
The non-tree gains consequently generate the deck-gain group of $\Gamma$.
Two of them, say $\eta_{f_1},\eta_{f_2}$, are linearly independent over
$\mathbb R$.  Take
\[
                       E=\mathcal T\cup\{f_1,f_2\}.
\]
It has $N-1+2=N+1$ edges, including when $N=1$ and the tree is empty.
The two gains need not form an integer basis.  Their real independence is
exactly the hypothesis needed to recover $L$ from $L\eta_{f_i}$; one must
not replace their displacement matrix by the identity.

We verify the geometric assertions rather than identifying a graph edge
with an observation without a collar.  At a closest pair, use transverse
coordinates whose positive directions agree in the plane.  The Hessian of
the flight length at its normal contact is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v45-contact-hessian}
 \begin{pmatrix}\kappa_a+g^{-1}&-g^{-1}\\-g^{-1}&\kappa_b+g^{-1}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det=\kappa_a\kappa_b+(\kappa_a+\kappa_b)/g>0.
\end{equation}
Thus the closest point is nondegenerate.  The local implicit construction
and strict convexity make the closest contacts continuous under $C^2$
perturbation and smooth, respectively analytic, along smooth, respectively
analytic, finite-dimensional families.  Their connecting segments inherit
the positive clearance of Lemma~\ref{lem:v45-finite-sublevels}.

Distinct clear incidences on a given lifted obstacle have distinct contact
points.  Otherwise their closest segments issue from the same point along
the same outward normal ray.  The nearer endpoint lies on the farther
segment and on a third body, contradicting clearness; equality of the two
endpoints contradicts disjointness.  Since $E$ is finite, choose disjoint
small planar gates around its incident contacts on each representative
obstacle, and choose fixed reference gates also at the finitely many lifted endpoints
used by $E$.  Periodic copies at the reference table are assigned their
marks before observation.  Shrink these gates so that their connecting
segments stay in the clear tubes.  During a perturbation the finitely many
laboratory gates stay fixed; the unknown lattice is not used to relocate
them.  They are not exact contact observations.
After decreasing the perturbation neighborhood they still contain the
corresponding contacts with positive interior margins.

There is no unexamined infinity of third obstacles in this persistence
argument.  On a small parameter neighborhood all bodies have a common
radius bound and $s_{\min}(L)$ has a positive lower bound.  Only finitely
many translates can approach any of the finitely many selected segments;
their positive margins persist.  The same argument preserves all-pair
separation.  Formula \eqref{eq:v45-contact-hessian}, positive curvature,
and the fixed gates give the alternating contact collars.  The labels and
the two integer gain vectors do not change under this perturbation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Locality of a selected clear channel]
\label{lem:v45-selected-locality}
The selected-channel conclusions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-main-relative}, the signed contact inverse, and the
single-offset density inverse apply to every channel of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v45-clear-skeleton}.  These channels need not attain the
minimum gap of the whole table.  Each fixed table has a common positive
offset collar for this finite channel set.  On compact smooth or analytic
subfamilies with the uniform higher-norm assumptions of the relative
theorem, the collar and every fixed-order bound may be chosen uniformly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The event under consideration specifies the same two facing patches and
alternation as the selected event of
Section~\ref{sec:v18-formal-setup}.  Every one of its flights has length
at least this pair's gap $g_e$.  In a window $jg_e+d$, the total excess
of the $j$ flights above $jg_e$, and both unused end intervals, is at most
$d$.  On fixed patches strict convexity and
\eqref{eq:v45-contact-hessian} give a positive quadratic lower bound on
one-flight excess near the closest points, and a positive lower bound
away from that neighborhood.  Thus a sufficiently small $d$ localizes
all the selected impacts in the contact collars.  Short unused end
intervals remain in their clear tubes after the offset collar is
shrunk once more.

The weighted Jacobi construction and its determinant comparison use the
local positive gap, positive curvatures and twist, not a comparison with
another pair's gap.  Their resulting bridges remain within the same
clear tubes.  The half-line construction and the fixed-domain sublevel
integration therefore give precisely the selected relative law.  Its
single-offset density and smooth contact inverse depend on that law and
the selected gap, so they apply unchanged.  Taking a minimum over finitely
many channels proves the fixed-table collar assertion; taking the uniform
constants in the stated compact-family hypotheses proves the last one.
A $C^2$ geometric neighborhood alone is not asserted to bound arbitrarily
high derivatives or to provide a compact analytic class.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The channel count for two-cycle reconstruction]
\label{prop:v45-channel-count}
A connected quotient multigraph on $N$ obstacle types that admits two
linearly independent deck-cycle gains has at least $N+1$ edges.
Consequently Theorem~\ref{thm:v45-clear-skeleton} attains the smallest
channel count for this connected two-cycle reconstruction mechanism.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If the graph has $m$ edges, a spanning tree leaves $m-N+1$ non-tree edges.
As in \eqref{eq:v45-fundamental-gain}, their gains generate every closed
walk gain.  Real rank two requires $m-N+1\ge2$.
\end{proof}

This is a graph-theoretic count of selected channels, not a lower bound on
the number of preparations, the dimension of a probability law, or the
information needed by every conceivable inverse observation map.

\subsection{Generic signature rigidity}

Let $\mathscr P_N^\omega$ denote the space of such labelled periodic tables
with real-analytic boundaries.  A parameter consists of $L$ and the support
functions $h_a$ of the representatives $K_a$; translations are retained in
the first harmonics.  Give this space the relative $C^2$ topology on supports
and the ordinary topology on $L$.  The assertions below hold also with any
fixed stronger $C^r$ support topology.  This definition of genericity makes
no compactness or common complex-extension assertion.

For a convex body $K$, put
\[
 s(K)=\frac1\pi\int_0^{2\pi}h_K(\theta)n(\theta)\,d\theta,
 \qquad
 z_k(K)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}h_K(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,d\theta.
\]
The vector $s(K)$ is translation and orthogonal covariant.  The coefficients
$z_k$ for $k\ge2$ are translation invariant; the centered body $K-s(K)$
has zero first harmonic.

\begin{proposition}[Open dense obstacle asymmetry]
\label{prop:v45-generic-asymmetry}
The subset $\mathscr A_N\subset\mathscr P_N^\omega$ for which every
obstacle has trivial orientation-preserving Euclidean symmetry group is
open and dense.  It contains the open dense subset $\mathscr H_N$ defined
by, for every $a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v45-harmonic-generic-set}
 z_2(K_a)z_3(K_a)\ne0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Im}\bigl(z_3(K_a)^2\overline{z_2(K_a)}^{\,3}\bigr)\ne0.
\end{equation}
Every individual obstacle of a table in $\mathscr H_N$ has no nonidentity
Euclidean symmetry, including reflections.  No restriction is placed on
its other analytic harmonics.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A symmetry of a bounded body fixes its covariant center.  For the centered
support, rotation by $\alpha$ is a symmetry exactly when
$e^{-ik\alpha}z_k=z_k$ for all $k\ge2$.  A body has trivial proper
symmetry exactly when the indices of its nonzero coefficients have greatest
common divisor one.  In that case finitely many such indices already have
greatest common divisor one: successive greatest common divisors are
positive integers and stabilize.  Those finitely many nonzero coefficients
remain nonzero after a sufficiently small support perturbation, proving
openness of $\mathscr A_N$.

Nonzero coefficients of orders two and three force a rotational symmetry
to be the identity.  A reflection across a line at angle $\alpha$ through
the center transforms $z_k$ to $e^{-2ik\alpha}\overline{z_k}$.  Invariance
of both coefficients would imply
\[
 2\alpha+\arg z_2\in\pi\mathbb Z,
 \qquad 3\alpha+\arg z_3\in\pi\mathbb Z.
\]
Eliminating $\alpha$ makes $z_3^2\overline{z_2}^{\,3}$ real.  Thus
\eqref{eq:v45-harmonic-generic-set} excludes reflections as well.
The conditions are open because Fourier coefficients are continuous in
the uniform norm.

To prove density, perturb each support by
\[
 u_{a,2}\cos2\theta+v_{a,2}\sin2\theta+
 u_{a,3}\cos3\theta+v_{a,3}\sin3\theta.
\]
These four real parameters vary its second and third complex coefficients
independently in a neighborhood.  Their zero sets and the zero set of the
nonzero real polynomial
$\operatorname{Im}(z_3^2\overline{z_2}^{\,3})$ have empty interior.
An arbitrarily small choice therefore satisfies
\eqref{eq:v45-harmonic-generic-set}.  Since $h_a+h_a''>0$ and separation
are open conditions, the perturbations preserve positive curvature and
all-pair disjointness.  They are trigonometric polynomials, so they preserve
analyticity and can be arbitrarily small in any fixed $C^r$ norm.  The
lattice and every other harmonic may be left unchanged.  This proves
density of $\mathscr H_N$, hence of $\mathscr A_N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Generic finite-channel determination]
\label{thm:v45-generic-determination}
For every $T\in\mathscr A_N$ there are $N+1$ marked selected channels and
a positive offset $d_0$ such that their $N+1$ onsets and their $2N+2$
same-type signed limiting endpoint laws at $d_0$ determine the complete
labelled periodic table and its Euclidean lattice up to one simultaneous
orientation-preserving Euclidean motion.  No inter-channel placements,
contact curvatures, support coefficients or lattice Gram form are given.
The competing realizations may be any tables in $\mathscr P_N^\omega$
realizing these selected data in the contact-law observation class of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-single-offset-global}, not only members of $\mathscr A_N$.

The channel labels, gates, spanning tree and two independent cycle gains
can be kept fixed in a geometric neighborhood of $T$.  In particular,
this finite-channel conclusion holds on an open dense subset of
$\mathscr P_N^\omega$.  For the common-orientation orbit of the entire
signed datum the conclusion is modulo one element of $\mathrm E(2)$,
not under independent sign erasures in different channels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Select $E$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:v45-clear-skeleton} and choose a common
$d_0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:v45-selected-locality}.  The four-density inverse
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-density-inverse} recovers both unsymmetrized
half-line actions at each channel from its two laws.  The smooth
finite-remainder inverse of Theorem~\ref{thm:v22-signed-rigidity} then
recovers both complete contact jets.  Lemma
\ref{lem:v23-analytic-germ-globalization} determines the two connected
analytic boundary images in that channel's own frame.

Every boundary of $T$ has trivial proper symmetry.  Lemma
\ref{lem:v23-signature-symmetry} implies that every one of its complete
oriented contact signatures occurs at exactly one framed point.  Hence
all incidence transitions in $E$ are signature-rigid.  Any competing
realization has the same complete incident images and inherits this
property; asymmetry is not imposed separately on the competitor.

The two fundamental closed walks may be based at the same root obstacle.
Choose one incident measured channel as the common starting frame.
Unique congruences between root-boundary copies transport each walk's
initial and final incidence to that frame.  Equivalently, prepend a
zero-net-gain backtrack $ee^{-1}$ when a common initial edge is
required.  These operations add neither a measured channel nor a deck
displacement.  They concern compositions of incidence congruences, not
realization of a multi-edge closed walk as a specular trajectory.
The resulting walks are a rank-two anchoring pair in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:v24-rank-two-anchoring}.  The selected tree is
signature-rigid and can be rooted at this same obstacle.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v26-single-offset-global}, or equivalently its
common-frame construction, now gives the conclusion.  Explicitly, align
the starting frames in two realizations.  Their unique incidence
congruences give the same cycle translations $v_1,v_2$.  With
$M=(\eta_{f_1}\ \eta_{f_2})$ and $V=(v_1\ v_2)$, they have the same
$L=VM^{-1}$, including its Gram form and scale.  Unique matching along
the tree then places every remaining obstacle image in that frame, with
deck corrections interpreted through this one $L$.  Realizability
supplies existence and admissibility; the argument proves uniqueness.
Persistence follows from the clear-skeleton theorem and openness of
$\mathscr A_N$.  The final assertion is the equivariance of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v28-unoriented-classification} applied to the entire
marked datum.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An explicit recovered-image reconstruction on the harmonic locus}

On $\mathscr H_N$ the incidence stage has a finite formula, independent of
how many higher harmonics the unknown obstacles possess.  This also gives
a quantitative statement in a clearly specified topology.

\begin{proposition}[Harmonic registration and the displacement cochain]
\label{prop:v45-explicit-image-inverse}
Suppose $E=\mathcal T\cup\{f_1,f_2\}$ is the skeleton above and every
obstacle has nonzero second and third support coefficients.  From the
complete boundary images already recovered in the separate channel frames,
all channel rotations, both lattice columns and all obstacle placements can
be computed by the following finite operations.

For two copies $C_s,C_t$ of the same labelled obstacle, the unique proper
congruence taking $C_s$ to $C_t$ has rotation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v45-general-registration}
 U_{s\to t}=\frac{z_3(C_s)z_2(C_t)}{z_3(C_t)z_2(C_s)},\qquad
 A_{s\to t}(y)=U_{s\to t}y+s(C_t)-U_{s\to t}s(C_s),
\end{equation}
where the plane is identified with $\mathbb C$ and $|U_{s\to t}|=1$ in
exact data.  Propagate these rotations along the tree, starting with one
root incidence in a fixed channel frame.  Let $R_e$ be the resulting
rotation of edge $e$ into that common frame, also obtained for the two
non-tree edges by matching their initial obstacle copies.  Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v45-displacement-cochain}
 d_e=R_e\{s(C_{e,+})-s(C_{e,-})\},\qquad
 p_1=0,\qquad
 p_a=\sum_{e\in[1,a]_{\mathcal T}}\epsilon_e d_e.
\end{equation}
For $f_i=(a_i,b_i,\ell_i)$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v45-cochain-recovery}
 v_i=p_{a_i}+d_{f_i}-p_{b_i},\quad
 L=(v_1\ v_2)M^{-1},\quad
 c_a=p_a-Lm_a,
 \qquad M=(\eta_{f_1}\ \eta_{f_2}).
\end{equation}
Then $c_a$ are the representative support centers, with $c_1=0$, and
centering and rotating one recovered copy of each type gives its complete
boundary at $c_a$.  Formula \eqref{eq:v45-cochain-recovery} also recovers
$G=L^tL$.

For fixed labels and tree, this inverse stage is locally Lipschitz in the
uniform norms of the support functions of the recovered complete images,
uniformly when their radii and centers are bounded, their second and third
coefficient moduli have positive lower bounds, and $\|M^{-1}\|$ is bounded.
It is not a Lipschitz assertion from endpoint laws or finite contact jets
to entire analytic boundaries.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a rotation $U=e^{i\alpha}$ and translation $t$,
\[
 z_k(UC+t)=e^{-ik\alpha}z_k(C)\quad(k\ge2),
 \qquad s(UC+t)=Us(C)+t.
\]
If $C_t=UC_s+t$, substituting the coefficient relation in
\eqref{eq:v45-general-registration} recovers $U$, with no choice of a
branch of an argument.  The center identity gives the translation.
Nonzero modes two and three exclude any competing proper rotation.
Starting with the root image, apply this rule to the parent copy in
each tree edge; rotating that edge also provides the child's image in
the common orientation.  Induction provides all obstacle orientations.
The parent type of either non-tree edge is now available, giving its
rotation as well.  All templates used in this induction are recovered
images, not supplied supports of the unknown table.

Let $c_a$ and $L$ temporarily denote the true representative centers and
lattice in the resulting common orientation, translating so that $c_1=0$.
The two endpoint centers of $e=(a,b,\ell_e)$ have displacement
\[
                         d_e=c_b+L\ell_e-c_a.
\]
Summing on the tree gives $p_a=c_a+Lm_a$.  Substitution in
\eqref{eq:v45-cochain-recovery} gives $v_i=L\eta_{f_i}$ and all claimed
identities.  Thus the formulas use the unknown realization only to prove
correctness; their inputs do not contain $c_a$, $L$, or an inter-channel
registration.

For stability, $h\mapsto z_k$ has uniform-norm operator bound one and
$h\mapsto s$ has bound two.  Quotients in
\eqref{eq:v45-general-registration} are locally Lipschitz away from their
stated zero denominators.  For perturbed noncongruent images, replace a
computed $U$ by $U/|U|$; this is smooth near the unit circle.  Finite tree
propagation preserves a local Lipschitz bound.  Support functions of
bodies in a common radius bound are uniformly Lipschitz in angle, so the
small rotation errors have the same bound in the uniform support norm.
The remaining operations are finite sums, multiplication by the fixed
$M^{-1}$ and translations by bounded marked deck vectors.  They preserve
the asserted local bound.  Finally
\[
 \|\widetilde L^t\widetilde L-L^tL\|
 \le2\|L\|\,\|\widetilde L-L\|+\|\widetilde L-L\|^2.
\]
This controls the recovered Gram form in exactly the stated image topology.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Persistent finite-design physical reconstruction]
\label{cor:v45-physical-chart}
Let $\mathfrak K$ be a compact analytic subfamily contained in one of the
persistent skeleton neighborhoods of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v45-generic-determination}.  Assume the uniform analytic,
chart and physical-record conventions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-global-physical-reconstruction}.  That theorem applies using
these $N+1$ selected channels, with a common positive offset and all
preparation failures charged.  The minimum even flight number can tend to
infinity as in the original protocol.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The finite skeleton supplies connected incidence, a signature-rigid tree
and two independent marked gains.  Its fixed gates, positive clearance,
and the stipulated compact-family regularity give the required uniform
local laws by Lemma~\ref{lem:v45-selected-locality}.  The signed inverse
is injective on this family by
Theorem~\ref{thm:v45-generic-determination}.  The finite-signature and
compact-inverse arguments, followed by the same charged pilot and capped
fresh-stage estimator, give precisely the cited physical theorem.  No new
sensor or uncharged design-selection stage is inserted.
\end{proof}

The existence of a skeleton at each table does not select one common design
throughout an arbitrary compact set crossing several such neighborhoods.
Nor do $N+1$ channels mean finitely many scalar observations: an exact
endpoint law is infinite-dimensional.  The generic theorem removes the
need to posit a special incidence architecture within this marked
observation model.  It does not replace that model by an ordinary marked
length spectrum, erase the endpoint signs, or equate it with the richer
position experiment.  The local nonlinear relative law and the smooth
finite-remainder inverse remain the analytic inputs to this broader
consequence.
