\begin{abstract}
We study the geometry encoded by long alternating collision bridges in
periodic dispersing billiards.  On an offset collar independent of the flight
number, the normalized mixed endpoint derivative has a nonlinear relative
limit with every fixed mixed geometric and offset derivative.  One signed
endpoint law at one positive offset for each contact type determines the two
unsymmetrized half-line actions: a four-density identity cancels the unknown
flux amplitudes.  Together with the onset gap, these actions recover every
labelled contact jet through explicit two-by-two blocks of determinant one.
The proof uses a weighted nonlinear half-line inverse, finite-truncation
envelope differentiation with finite smooth remainders, and homogeneous
degree isolation.  No reflection
symmetry, equal contacts, or supplied curvatures are assumed.

For connected real-analytic boundaries, intrinsic channel data determine
complete obstacle images in their separate frames.  Signature-rigid incidence
matching and two cycles with independent marked deck displacements recover
the Euclidean period lattice, including its Gram form and scale.  A rooted
spanning tree places the remaining obstacle orbits.  On compact analytic
classes we obtain stable unique finite-signature matching and a uniform
finite-data inverse modulus.

The local information depends on the retained record.  Endpoint--residual-time
observations are equivalent to a moving-ceiling Poisson experiment, with
explicit kernels controlling layer, bulk, and corner errors.  Removing
residual time gives a linearly vanishing endpoint density and a nonregular
Gaussian limit; waiting counts supply a separate faster normal information
channel.  These scalar and finite-strip Poisson families have common
dominating measures, although a designated reference member need not
dominate them.  For anchored homothetic channels, the information loss from
deleting normal endpoint coordinates is maximal when the observed contact
varies, whereas observing only the fixed contact gives equivalent matched
endpoint experiments.

Finally, a charged near-onset pilot and single-offset transverse-law tests
give uniform global reconstruction from common physical position records,
using only even bridges whose minimum flight number diverges.  We also prove
a direct graph-interpolation benchmark in the same position experiment.
This separates its noiseless geometric sampling from the intrinsic inverse
based only on transverse laws.  All preparation failures are charged, and
no finite-dimensional parametrization of the compact analytic class is used.
\end{abstract}
