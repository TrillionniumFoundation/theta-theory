# A2 v62 — targeted primary-record check

Checked September 16, 2026. This is a focused refresh of the comparisons used in the v61 report, not an exhaustive novelty/priority investigation and not a new audit of the cited papers' proofs.

* Finamore and Leguil, *A CAT(0)-approach to the marked length spectral rigidity of Sinai billiards*, arXiv:2510.18983. The current primary abstract/version record describes finite-horizon Sinai billiards and an **enriched** marked length datum. It does not identify this datum with ordinary periodic lengths or with the conditional endpoint laws used here. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18983
* De Simoi, Kaloshin and Leguil, *Marked Length Spectral determination of analytic chaotic billiards with axial symmetries*, arXiv:1905.00890v4, related DOI 10.1007/s00222-023-01191-8. The primary record specifies analytic open billiards, non-eclipse, symmetry and genericity hypotheses. No reduction to or from the present observation experiment is inferred. https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00890v4
* Florio and Leguil, *Smooth conjugacy classes of 3D Axiom A flows*, arXiv:2010.04120v5. The version notice states that the affected open-billiard geometric spectral-rigidity claim was removed; the corrected dynamical conclusions are a different assertion. The withdrawn claim is not used as a premise or as a priority objection. https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04120v5
* Trefethen, *Quantifying the ill-conditioning of analytic continuation*, arXiv:1908.11097. The primary record distinguishes bounded complex-domain conditional continuation from unrestricted continuation and discusses distance-dependent deterioration. The new v62 application retains the already proved v60 radius loss and prior; it does not claim a new general continuation principle. https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11097

The bibliography and all original literature discussions are retained. The new sampling lemma includes its elementary exponential-moment proof rather than relying on a secondary density-estimation summary. Its confidence rate is an upper bound; no minimax optimality, claim of first priority, or comparison theorem with marked-length observations is asserted. The v61 report's more detailed PDF-level primary-source examination is retained as prior provenance, not represented as newly repeated here.
