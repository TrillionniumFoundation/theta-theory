# Direct orbit input map

No new specialized dynamical theorem is imported by the averaged result.

`lem:explicit-frequency-error` and `eq:weighted-stopping-error` provide the pointwise lag estimate. Their initial density is `s_R`, so the pairing is the actual normalized section law. Uniform ellipticity is Theorem E. Summing the pointwise errors, not integrating them, gives the finite lag row budget.

`eq:actual-compressed-operator` already defines the uncompressed unitary `U_z`; it retains all actual return lengths. Unitarity makes the Gram entry depend only on the time difference. The analysis/synthesis estimate is proved by the elementary row-sum inequality and adjoints. The long averaging interval is split into exact orbit blocks, not Markov or projected blocks.

The unitary spectral theorem supplies the constant vector's probability measure. The arc estimate is a direct lower bound on a finite Dirichlet-square kernel; the exterior resolvent is a norm-convergent Neumann series. The Cauchy limit uses the uniform physical Gaussian theorem, the finite one-return second moment and the characteristic-function continuity theorem. It asserts no fixed-frequency spectral density.

For the finite-count convergence clarification, the existing Cluckers--Miller reference was checked at Definitions 2.2--2.3, Theorem 2.4 and Theorem 3.11, printed pages 7--8 and 15--16 of arXiv:0911.4373. The strong unit extends analytically across its compact monomial-image limit. This is the convergence input, not a uniform-in-time estimate of germ constants.
