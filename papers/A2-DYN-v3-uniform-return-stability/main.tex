\begin{abstract}
For triangular periodic billiards with disk radius in $[0.45,0.47]$, we
construct a uniformly regular family of true periodic collision orbits.
Their excess lengths are strictly increasing and bounded, as follows from
a convex optical action and a one-dimensional variational recursion. The
positive successive excess increments tend to zero. Together with explicit
winding cycles, this proves triviality of the complete joint periodic
annihilator for displacement, collision count, physical flight time and
induced period, for a specified first-return section. It also gives finite
uniform separation on compact frequency sets and a nonzero real period
determinant independent of the radius.

We retain the complete marked suspension law and the raw critical-edge
calculation, and prove a local inversion criterion allowing nonnegligible
edge mass away from the central window. The original measure is restored
by an exact decomposition; no smoothed observation model is substituted.
Exact induced means and stationary unfinished-flight distributions are
computed. For each fixed return count we prove continuity of the raw
mixed density in total variation and of the record in first moment as
the radius varies. Compactness and the exact return means give uniform
integrability, hence uniform stationary endpoint control. Weighted records
obey an explicit positive-denominator comparison. A full geometrically uniform raw density local limit still
requires a common operator realization, the measurable cohomology and
variance criteria, and the all-branch residual estimates specified in the
article. The periodic and mechanical conclusions do not assume those
remaining analytical inputs.
\end{abstract}
