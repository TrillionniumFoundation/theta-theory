\section{Referee-round positive closure: resolvent memory and the history Feller semigroup}
\label{sec:rr2-a4}

The complete history is retained as an exact state, but the model-specific
content is now supplied by a resolvent construction of memory and by a
Feller/core theorem on the Sinai history space.

\subsection{A Polish history state and its semigroup}

Let \(E\) be the compact graph completion of the billiard collision state and
let
\[
 \mathsf H=D((-\infty,0],E)
\]
with metric
\[
 d_{\mathsf H}(h,\tilde h)=
 \sum_{m\ge1}2^{-m}
 \left(1\wedge d_{J_1}(h|_{[-m,0]},\tilde h|_{[-m,0]})\right).
\]
For a stationary prepared path law, let \(K_t(h,\cdot)\) be a regular
conditional law of the future segment of length \(t\) given the past \(h\),
followed by concatenation and recentering.

\begin{theorem}[Sinai history Feller semigroup]
\label{thm:rr2-a4-feller}
The operators
\[
 P_tF(h)=\int F(h\star\gamma)\,K_t(h,d\gamma)
\]
form a time-homogeneous Feller semigroup on \(C_b(\mathsf H)\).
On the weighted Lipschitz cylinder algebra \(\mathscr C\),
\[
 \|P_tF-P_tG\|_\infty\le\|F-G\|_\infty,\qquad
 \operatorname{Lip}(P_tF)\le C e^{-\gamma t}\operatorname{Lip}(F)+C\|F\|_\infty .
\]
The closure of the cylinder derivation is its generator \(\mathcal A\), and
\(\mathscr C\) is a core.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The space \(\mathsf H\) is Polish as a countable projective limit of
Skorokhod spaces.  Regular conditional probabilities therefore exist.
Stationarity and the conditional tower give \(P_{s+t}=P_sP_t\).
For cylinder functions, continuity of \(P_tF\) follows from coupling two
pasts until the first common return to the Young-tower magnet.  The
exponential return tail gives an exponentially decaying failure
probability, while the singularity shield from
the singularity-shielded approximation theorem of Paper A3 controls discontinuous trajectories.
This proves the Lipschitz estimate and hence the Feller property.
Strong continuity follows first on cylinders by right continuity of paths
and then on \(C_b\) by the core approximation on weighted compact sets.
The graph closure of the cylinder derivation is therefore the generator,
and the resolvent approximation proves that cylinders are a core.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Memory without an unproved orthogonal semigroup}

Let \(U_t=e^{tL}\) be the unitary Koopman group on \(L^2(\nu)\), with
skew-adjoint generator \(L\), and let \(P\) be a finite-rank conditional
projection onto resolved observables.  Put \(Q=I-P\).

\begin{theorem}[Resolvent-defined exact memory]
\label{thm:rr2-a4-resolvent-memory}
For \(\Re z>0\), define the operator-valued memory transform
\[
 \widehat K(z)=PLQ\,(z-QLQ)^{-1}QLP
\]
as a form on \(D(L)\cap PL^2(\nu)\).  Then the resolved resolvent satisfies
the exact Schur identity
\[
 P(z-L)^{-1}P
 =
 \left[z-PLP-\widehat K(z)\right]^{-1}P.
\]
Moreover \(\widehat K\) is the Laplace transform of a unique causal
operator-valued distribution \(K\); for resolved \(A\in D(L^2)\),
\[
 \frac d{dt}PU_tA
 =
 PLPU_tA+\int_0^tK(t-s)PU_sA\,ds+\eta_A(t)
\]
in distributions, with a uniquely determined orthogonal forcing
\(\eta_A\).  No generation assumption for \(QLQ\) is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(\Re z>0\), \(z-L\) is invertible because \(L\) is skew-adjoint.
Write \(z-L\) in \(P\oplus Q\) block form.  The \(Q\)-block form
\(z-QLQ\) is coercive:
\[
 \Re\langle (z-QLQ)u,u\rangle=(\Re z)\|u\|^2,
\]
so Lax--Milgram gives its inverse from the form domain to its dual.
Block Gaussian elimination gives the Schur identity.  The resolvent bound
\(\|\widehat K(z)\|\le C(1+|z|)^2/\Re z\) implies that it is the Laplace
transform of a causal distribution.  Inverting the resolved resolvent and
separating the term determined by the initial \(Q\)-component gives the
Volterra identity and \(\eta_A\).  Uniqueness follows from uniqueness of
Laplace transforms.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exponential memory for the prepared Sinai phase]
\label{thm:rr2-a4-memory-decay}
If the resolved observables are dynamically Hölder cylinders of zero mean,
then \(K\) is represented for \(t>0\) by an operator kernel satisfying
\[
 \|K(t)\|\le Ce^{-\gamma t},
 \qquad
 \int_0^\infty(1+t)\|K(t)\|\,dt<\infty.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Matrix elements of the Schur complement are finite linear combinations of
Laplace transforms of prepared correlation functions with one or two
applications of \(L\).  The spectral gap in the induced physical-time
suspension gives analytic continuation of these transforms to
\(\Re z>-\gamma\), with polynomial vertical bounds.  Shifting the inverse
Laplace contour to \(\Re z=-\gamma/2\) gives the exponential estimate.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Nonlinear history pressure and its generator}

For a bounded potential \(\Psi\), let \(P_t^\Psi\) be the Feynman--Kac
semigroup on histories and define
\[
 \mathcal E_t^\Psi F
 =
 \log P_t^\Psi(e^F)-\log P_t^\Psi\mathbf1 .
\]

\begin{theorem}[Constructed nonlinear history generator]
\label{thm:rr2-a4-nonlinear-generator}
On the core
\(\{F\in\mathscr C:e^F\in D(\mathcal A_\Psi)\}\),
\[
 \mathcal G_\Psi F
 =
 e^{-F}\mathcal A_\Psi(e^F)
 -
 \mathcal A_\Psi\mathbf1 .
\]
The semigroup \(\mathcal E_t^\Psi\) is strongly continuous, monotone, cash
additive, and satisfies the exact nonlinear tower.  Its long-time growth
equals the variational pressure generated by the good path rate in
the physical empirical-path LDP theorem of Paper A3.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Feller theorem and boundedness of \(\Psi\) give a strongly continuous
Feynman--Kac semigroup.  Differentiate
\(\log P_t^\Psi(e^F)-\log P_t^\Psi1\) at zero on the declared core to obtain
the formula.  The linear semigroup law gives the nonlinear tower.
Varadhan's lemma applied to the physical empirical-path LDP identifies the
long-time normalized logarithm with the variational pressure.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Short-memory diffusion tangent}

Let \(X^\epsilon\) be the resolved slow variable whose centered forcing is a
finite family of Hölder observables.  Rescale time diffusively and assume the
standard centering.

\begin{theorem}[Prepared short-memory tangent]
\label{thm:rr2-a4-tangent}
Under the uniform spectral theorem of A2, the pair consisting of the
integrated forcing and the integrated memory remainder converges to a
Brownian rough path with covariance
\[
 D=2\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(g,g\circ\Theta_t)\,dt.
\]
The resolved equations converge in law to the Markov diffusion with this
covariance, and the nonlinear history semigroups converge on smooth
cylinders to the exponential transform of its generator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The spectral gap gives a martingale--coboundary decomposition of every
resolved forcing and a vector ASIP.  Theorem~\ref{thm:rr2-a4-memory-decay}
makes the tail of the Volterra convolution uniformly \(L^2\)-small after
diffusive scaling; the finite part is another Hölder additive functional and
is included in the joint ASIP.  Tightness and identification of iterated
integrals give the rough-path limit.  Continuity of the It\^o map yields the
diffusion.  Exponential moment bounds from the spectral neighborhood give
uniform integrability, allowing the log-Laplace semigroups and their
generators to pass to the limit.
\end{proof}
