## Overall assessment

The revision makes two substantial corrections. It replaces the state-dependent normalized Feynman--Kac expression which failed the tower law by a genuine Doob transform, and it constructs the finite-dimensional memory kernel directly from a Volterra equation rather than assuming a semigroup generated by `QLQ`. Those are mathematically appropriate repairs.

The resulting paper is still not closed. The general history-process theorem overstates what follows from a Ray realization, the exponential-memory theorem assumes the decisive meromorphic transfer decomposition rather than deriving it, and the rough diffusion limit omits the deterministic second-level area correction which is generally present in deterministic homogenization. The headline statement therefore remains conditional and, in one part, incorrectly formulated.

## Major objections

### 1. The general strict-Ray theorem is not proved at the stated strength

A right process on a standard Borel space can often be given a Ray compactification/realization. The manuscript asserts much more:

- a Polish Ray realization with the same completed measurable sets;
- a positive contraction semigroup on all `C_b` of that realization;
- strong continuity in the bounded-strict topology; and
- a strict graph core given by the Ray-resolvent algebra.

The proof consists of the resolvent identity, right-continuity, and completion of a countable evaluation metric. It does not verify the hypotheses needed for a Ray process theorem, quasi-regularity, separation modulo exceptional sets, or invariance of the completed `C_b` space under the transition operators. Nor does pointwise/right-continuous path behavior by itself give compact-uniform convergence for every Ray function.

The exposed spectral-phase result may be approachable through a concrete symbolic kernel, but the universal statement for every stationary prepared law is not established by the supplied argument.

### 2. The finite-dimensional Volterra construction is valid only under more regularity than acknowledged

The useful core observation is that, for a finite-dimensional resolved space contained in `D(L^2)`, the matrix correlation function

\[
C(t)=PU_tP
\]

is twice differentiable and one may solve a second-kind Volterra equation for a continuous kernel. This is a legitimate construction.

However, several subsequent statements need additional care:

- global `C^2` regularity and bounds of `C` follow from the finite-dimensional inclusion in `D(L^2)`, but the dependence on the chosen basis and graph norms must be recorded;
- the forcing `eta_B` is said to be continuous for every `B in D(L)`, although differentiating `x_B` and convolving with a merely continuous kernel requires explicit local bounds;
- compatibility under enlargement of the resolved subspace is not automatic; Schur complements of different projections need not form a projective family.

These are repairable, but the paper presents them as immediate universal facts.

### 3. Exponentially decaying memory is assumed, not proved

The manuscript begins the memory-decay subsection with:

> Assume the A2 suspension resolvent gives ... a rational plus regular decomposition on a left half-strip.

That decomposition is the entire nontrivial spectral input needed to obtain exponentially decaying memory. A2, even if its own spectral theorem were correct, concerns selected transfer operators and path observables; the paper does not show that every entry of the compressed Koopman resolvent for the chosen resolved space admits the stated proper rational decomposition with polynomial vertical bounds.

The final “Round-four A4 closure” then states unconditionally that prepared Sinai paths possess an exponentially controlled memory representation. This does not follow from a theorem introduced by an explicit assumption.

The claim that two integrations by parts produce an exponentially decaying inverse Laplace transform is also under-specified. “Polynomial vertical bounds” may grow; two derivatives are sufficient only with a quantified order and corresponding differentiability/decay of the transform. No such estimates are stated.

### 4. The Doob transform requires an eigenfunction theorem on the history state

The algebraic Doob formula

\[
\widetilde P_t^\Psi F
=e^{-\lambda_\Psi t}h_\Psi^{-1}P_t^\Psi(h_\Psi F)
\]

does yield a Markov semigroup if a strictly positive eigenfunction exists. The manuscript does not construct this eigenfunction on the history Feller space or show that it is bounded above and away from zero so that multiplication and division preserve `BUC_beta`.

A2--A3 spectral data live on billiard/induced transfer spaces, not automatically on the complete-history transition semigroup. An intertwining theorem is required. Without it, strong continuity of the Doob semigroup and the declared exponential core are unproved.

The statement that the long-time excess pressure equals the same `lambda_Psi` also needs this identification, not merely Varadhan's lemma.

### 5. The deterministic rough-path limit omits the area anomaly

For deterministic chaotic systems, enhanced functional central limit theorems generally converge to a Brownian rough path whose second level can contain a deterministic antisymmetric drift:

\[
\mathbb B_{s,t}
=\int_s^t(B_r-B_s)\otimes\circ dB_r
+(t-s)\Gamma.
\]

The matrix `Gamma` is generated by time-asymmetric correlations and need not vanish. The manuscript says the limit is “the Brownian rough path with covariance D” and then identifies the limiting slow equation as the corresponding Stratonovich system, without stating or proving a reversibility/symmetry condition forcing the area correction to zero.

Omitting this term changes the limiting drift of a nonlinear rough differential equation. Thus the advertised short-memory diffusion tangent is generally misidentified.

### 6. The memory scaling is not integrated into the slow system

The paper introduces a slow RDE `Y^epsilon`, a finite auxiliary state `z^epsilon`, and a rescaled memory kernel, but no complete prelimit coupled equation is written. It is therefore unclear:

- what variable the memory convolution acts on;
- how the auxiliary realization is driven by `Y^epsilon` or `X^epsilon`;
- what initial conditions are used for the unresolved memory state; and
- in which topology the full system converges.

The estimate showing a concentrating convolution tends to its mass is correct for sufficiently regular/tight input paths, but it does not by itself identify the dynamics being approximated.

### 7. Semigroup convergence needs conditional, not merely unconditional, convergence

The final step says that rough-path convergence plus a complex exponential-moment bound gives convergence of nonlinear Doob semigroups. A semigroup statement uniform over initial histories requires convergence of transition kernels or conditional laws on compact history sets, not only weak convergence under one stationary initial law. This interface is not proved.

### 8. The paper remains dependent on A2 and A3

The pressure identity, spectral phase, meromorphic resolvent, and physical empirical-path rate all rely on A2/A3. Those papers do not presently establish their headline results. A4 therefore cannot be accepted as an independent closure theorem even if its abstract Volterra construction is retained.

## Valuable part worth preserving

The finite-dimensional theorem deriving a continuous memory kernel from

\[
C'(t)=AC(t)+(K*C)(t)
\]

is a clean domain-safe alternative to formal orthogonal dynamics. With carefully stated assumptions it could form a useful abstract note. The exact Doob logarithmic tower is also standard and correct once the positive eigen-triple is constructed.

## Minimum viable reconstruction

A credible paper should separate three layers:

1. an abstract finite-dimensional compressed-correlation/Volterra theorem;
2. a model-specific theorem proving the required meromorphic continuation and eigenfunction bounds for one declared Sinai phase; and
3. a homogenization theorem which includes the possible antisymmetric area drift and writes the complete coupled slow-memory equation.

The universal Ray-process discussion should be limited to the precise topology guaranteed by a cited theorem with all hypotheses checked.

## Recommendation

**Reject.** The revision repairs the old tower and `QLQ` errors, but the model-specific memory decay, history Doob realization, and rough diffusion limit are not proved; the latter also omits the generally nonzero rough-area correction.
