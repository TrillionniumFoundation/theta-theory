\section{Round-twenty-three reconstruction: two-scale history control and the exact Schur--Feshbach memory kernel}
\label{sec:r23-a4}

Two algebraic contradictions in the preceding memory construction are removed
at the definition level.  We do not attach residues of the full resolvent at
points where that resolvent is holomorphic, and we do not claim that the
memory transform is generically $O(|z|^{-2})$.  The exact memory kernel is the
orthogonal-dynamics kernel
\[
 K(t)=PLQe^{tQLQ}QLP,
\]
whose transform has the correct leading term $z^{-1}PLQLP$.  Exponential
memory is proved in the time domain from decay of the $Q$-semigroup.  A
separate two-scale history estimate repairs the false assertion that a
Lyapunov sublevel is uniformly approximated by truncating its remote past.

\subsection{Two scales on the complete-history state}

Let $\mathsf H$, $K(h,dm)$, and the append map $U(h,m)$ be the exact objects of
A3.  Stable distortion at depth $j$ is bounded by
$C\vartheta^j\chi(m_{-j})$ for a hyperbolic constant $0<\vartheta<1$.
Choose
\[
 0<\vartheta<\rho<1,
 \qquad
 V(h)=1+\sum_{j\ge1}\rho^j\chi(m_{-j}),                  \tag{A4.1}
\]
and use the bounded history metric
\[
 d_\sigma(h,h')=\sum_{j\ge1}\sigma^j
 (1\wedge d_E(m_{-j},m'_{-j})),
 \qquad 0<\sigma<1.                                      \tag{A4.2}
\]
The three constants have different jobs: $\rho$ controls unbounded mark
size, $\vartheta/\rho<1$ controls the influence of a huge remote mark on the
future kernel, and $\sigma$ controls metric truncation independently of mark
size.

A3's branch estimate gives
\[
 PV\le\lambda V+C,\qquad 0<\lambda<1.                    \tag{A4.3}
\]
If $V(h)\le L$, then the remote distortion beyond depth $n$ is bounded by
\[
 \sum_{j>n}\vartheta^j\chi(m_{-j})
 \le L(\vartheta/\rho)^n,                                \tag{A4.4}
\]
whereas the metric tail is bounded by
$\sigma^n/(1-\sigma)$.  The counterexample with one mark of size
$L\rho^{-n}$ therefore has order-one $V$-tail, but its effect on the kernel is
at most $L(\vartheta/\rho)^n$ and its effect on the bounded metric is at most
$\sigma^n/(1-\sigma)$.  We never claim that the $V$-tail itself is small.

Let $\mathcal B_V$ be the completion of bounded Lipschitz functions under
\[
 \|f\|_V=\sup_h{|f(h)|\over V(h)}+
 \sup_{h\ne h'}{|f(h)-f(h')|\over
 (1+d_\sigma(h,h'))(V(h)+V(h'))}.                         \tag{A4.5}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Synchronized small set]
\label{lem:r23-a4-small}
For every $L<\infty$ there are $n_L$, $\epsilon_L>0$, and a probability
$\nu_L$ such that
\[
 P^{n_L}(h,\cdot)\ge\epsilon_L\nu_L(\cdot)
 \quad\text{for every }h\text{ with }V(h)\le L.          \tag{A4.6}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $n$ so that both bounds after (A4.4) are below a prescribed distortion
tolerance.  The truncated pasts then range over a compact set after excluding
a set of branch mass below the same tolerance by the exponential mark bound.
Cover this compact set by finitely many regular cylinders.  Finite-horizon
accessibility gives, for every cylinder in the cover, a regular connector to
one common target rectangle.  Pad shorter connectors by a fixed aperiodic
return loop to obtain one length $n_L$.  Inverse Jacobians and stable holonomy
densities are bounded above and below on the finite connector family; their
minimum gives $\epsilon_L$ and normalized Lebesgue measure on the common
target gives $\nu_L$.  Equations (A4.4) and (A4.2), not truncation of $V$,
transfer the bound from the finite pasts to every $h$ in the sublevel.
\end{proof}

Combining (A4.3), local contraction in $d_\sigma$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:r23-a4-small} gives a weak-Harris distance $d_\star$ and
constants $C,c>0$ such that
\[
 \|P^nf-\pi f\|_V\le Ce^{-cn}\|f\|_V.                  \tag{A4.7}
\]
The proof is the explicit coupling: use the drift to enter $\{V\le L\}$,
apply the synchronized connector, and contract uncoupled histories in
$d_\sigma$; iteration gives the geometric bound.  This establishes an
operator norm gap on the actual space (A4.5).

\subsection{Analytic sources and rough paths on the correct observable class}

For $g\in\mathcal B_V$ with sufficiently small norm, define
$P_gf=P(e^g f)$.  The product estimate following from (A4.5) and the power
drift show that $g\mapsto P_g$ is analytic as an operator
$\mathcal B_V\to\mathcal B_V$.  The isolated leading eigenvalue
$e^{\Lambda(g)}$, eigenfunction, and eigenmeasure are analytic by the Riesz
projection formula on a fixed contour.  This supplies the pressure and Doob
kernel on one fixed source space.

For a centered observable $A\in\mathcal B_V$ with
$|A|^{2+\epsilon}\le CV$, equation (A4.7) gives the Poisson solution
$\Phi=\sum_{n\ge0}P^nA\in\mathcal B_V$.  The increment
$D_{n+1}=\Phi(H_{n+1})-P\Phi(H_n)$ is a square-integrable martingale
difference and
\[
 A(H_n)=D_{n+1}+\Phi(H_n)-\Phi(H_{n+1}).                  \tag{A4.8}
\]
The weighted Lipschitz assumption is essential: a mere
$L^{2+\epsilon}$ moment is not promoted to membership in $\mathcal B_V$.
The same martingale calculation for the antisymmetric second level, together
with Burkholder bounds and (A4.8), proves convergence of the polygonal lift to
the Brownian rough path.  This is the standard martingale rough-path route
\cite{FrizHairer2014}, with its Poisson and moment hypotheses verified in the
history Banach space.

\subsection{Typed renewal factorization}

Let $\mathcal X$ be the anisotropic collision-section space of A2 and
$\mathcal Y=\mathcal B_V$ the history space.  Define bounded operators for
$\operatorname{Re}z$ large:
\begin{align*}
 E(z)&:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y,
 &E(z)f(h)&=\int_0^{\tau(m)}e^{-zs}f(\gamma_m(s))\,ds,\\
 X(z)&:\mathcal Y\to\mathcal X,
 &X(z)F(x)&=\int e^{-z\tau(m)}F(U(h_x,m))K(h_x,dm),\\
 R_0(z)&:\mathcal X\to\mathcal X
 &&\text{(the residual flight before the first complete return)},\\
 T(z)&:\mathcal Y\to\mathcal Y,
 &T(z)F(h)&=\int e^{-z\tau(m)}F(U(h,m))K(h,dm).
\end{align*}
Here $h_x$ is the stable-history disintegration at $x$.  A3's exponential
mark bound and A2's branch derivative estimate prove boundedness and analytic
continuation of these four maps to the common strip used below.  Their domains
and codomains are fixed and are not inferred from a scalar eigenvalue.

\begin{proposition}[First/last-return renewal identity]
\label{prop:r23-a4-renewal}
On the intersection of the resolvent sets,
\[
 (z-\mathcal L_{\rm flow})^{-1}
 =R_0(z)+E(z)(I-T(z))^{-1}X(z).                           \tag{A4.9}
\]
The identity continues meromorphically wherever the four typed factors do.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Partition every orbit segment according to the number $k$ of complete returns
between its entrance and exit.  The Laplace transform of the $k$-return piece
is exactly $E(z)T(z)^kX(z)$; the zero-return piece is $R_0(z)$.  Absolute
convergence for large $\operatorname{Re}z$ follows from the exponential mark
bound.  Summation gives (A4.9).  Both sides are analytic there and hence agree
on the meromorphic continuation by uniqueness.  This defines the entry,
exit, trace, and residual-flight maps at the operator level instead of using a
formal scalar factorization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact domain-safe memory equation}

Let $\mathcal H=L^2(\pi)$ and let $L$ be the closed generator of the prepared
continuous-time history semigroup.  Let $P$ be a finite-rank orthogonal
projection such that
\[
 P D(L)\subset D(L),\quad QD(L)\subset D(L),\quad Q=I-P,
\]
and assume the off-diagonal blocks $PLQ$ and $QLP$ extend continuously from
the graph norm to $\mathcal H$.  Put $L_Q=QLQ$ on $QD(L)$ and suppose the
prepared $Q$-dynamics satisfies
\[
 \|e^{tL_Q}Q\|\le Me^{-\gamma t}.                        \tag{A4.10}
\]
This follows for the resolved space consisting of constants and the finite
pressure tangent modes by applying (A4.7) to the orthogonal complement and
using the finite-rank equivalence of the Hilbert and Banach projections.

For $x\in D(L)$ write
$u(t)=Pe^{tL}x$ and $v(t)=Qe^{tL}x$.  The block equations are
\[
 u'=PLPu+PLQv,\qquad v'=QLPu+L_Qv.
\]
Variation of constants in the second equation gives the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Forced Mori--Zwanzig equation]
\label{thm:r23-a4-memory}
For every $x\in D(L)$,
\[
 u'(t)=PLPu(t)+\int_0^tK(t-s)u(s)\,ds+F_x(t),             \tag{A4.11}
\]
where
\[
 K(t)=PLQe^{tL_Q}QLP,
 \qquad F_x(t)=PLQe^{tL_Q}Qx.                            \tag{A4.12}
\]
Both satisfy $\|K(t)\|+\|F_x(t)\|\le
C e^{-\gamma t}(1+\|Qx\|)$.  For
$\operatorname{Re}z>-\gamma$,
\[
 \widehat K(z)=PLQ(z-L_Q)^{-1}QLP                         \tag{A4.13}
\]
and, whenever the displayed products through $L_Q$ are defined,
\[
 \widehat K(z)=z^{-1}PLQLP+z^{-2}PLQL_QQLP+O(|z|^{-3}).  \tag{A4.14}
\]
In particular the generic leading order is $|z|^{-1}$, not $|z|^{-2}$.
Moreover, whenever $z-L_Q$ and $z-L$ are invertible,
\[
 P(z-L)^{-1}P=
 \bigl[z-PLP-PLQ(z-L_Q)^{-1}QLP\bigr]^{-1}.              \tag{A4.15}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Variation of constants gives
$v(t)=e^{tL_Q}Qx+\int_0^te^{(t-s)L_Q}QLPu(s)\,ds$.
Substitution into the first block equation proves (A4.11)--(A4.12).  Estimate
(A4.10) gives time-domain exponential decay.  Taking Laplace transforms gives
(A4.13).  The graph-domain resolvent expansion
$(z-L_Q)^{-1}=z^{-1}+z^{-2}L_Q+O(z^{-3})$ gives (A4.14).
Finally solve the block system $(z-L)(u,v)=(f,0)$: the second row gives
$v=(z-L_Q)^{-1}QLPu$, and the first row gives the inverse in (A4.15).  This is
the Schur--Feshbach identity on the declared domains.
\end{proof}

No ``transmission-zero closure'' is needed.  In the region where
$z-L_Q$ is invertible, (A4.15) implies that a singularity of the reduced
resolvent is a zero of the Feshbach pencil and is simultaneously a pole of the
full resolved block.  At a regular point of the full resolvent, its residue is
zero and we never use it as a generator vector.  Actual spectral poles are
handled only by their genuine Riesz projections.

For an absolutely convergent vertical inversion, choose
$0<\omega<\gamma$ and put $A_0=PLQLP$.  Then
\[
 \widehat K_{\rm rem}(z)=\widehat K(z)-{A_0\over z+\omega}
 =O(|z|^{-2}),                                           \tag{A4.16}
\]
while the subtracted term has the explicit inverse $A_0e^{-\omega t}$.
Thus the remainder contour is absolutely integrable and the leading
$z^{-1}$ term is retained rather than cancelled incorrectly.  This
one-pole subtraction is a bookkeeping device, not an enlargement of the
resolved state.

\subsection{Pressure, Doob preparation, and regression}

The analytic Feynman--Kac eigenvalue constructed above defines the excess
pressure.  Differentiation uses the fixed source space $\mathcal B_V$ and the
Poisson solution from (A4.7).  The Doob transform preserves the history
Lyapunov estimate after shrinking the source ball, so all prepared kernels
share a common two-scale Harris construction.  Combining the local theorem of
A2 with the renewal identity (A4.9) gives source-uniform pole expansions; the
memory identity itself, however, rests only on the closed block generator and
(A4.10).

The Round-Twenty-Two counterexamples now become positive checks:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a mark $C\rho^{-n}$ at depth $n$ need not have a small $V$-tail, but its
future-kernel effect is $O((\vartheta/\rho)^n)$ by (A4.4);
\item rough paths are stated for $A\in\mathcal B_V\cap L^{2+\epsilon}$, not for
an arbitrary measurable moment observable;
\item renewal entry/exit maps have the explicit types preceding (A4.9);
\item the residue of a holomorphic full resolvent is never adjoined;
\item the exact transform has the algebraically correct expansion (A4.14),
and exponential decay is obtained from (A4.10), not from a false
$O(|b|^{-2})$ cancellation.
\end{enumerate}
The block identity is the rigorous form of the Mori--Zwanzig representation
\cite{Mori1965,Zwanzig1961,ChorinHaldKupferman2000}; the new point here is its
closed-domain implementation on the prepared Sinai history platform.
