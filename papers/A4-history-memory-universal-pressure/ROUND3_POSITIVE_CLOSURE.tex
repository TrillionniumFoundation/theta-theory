\section{Round-four referee closure: weighted histories and compressed-resolvent memory}
\label{sec:r3-a4}

The exact coarse state is the complete resolved history.  The operator memory
is constructed from a compressed Koopman resolvent and therefore does not
require a semigroup generated by \(QLQ\).  Strong continuity is asserted on a
weighted uniformly continuous history space, not on all of \(C_b\).

\subsection{The exact history state and its two continuity levels}

Let \(E\) be the compact graph completion of the collision or physical state
and let
\[
 \mathsf H=D(( -\infty,0],E).
\]
For every stationary prepared path law \(\nu\), disintegration of the future
given the complete resolved past gives a Borel right transition semigroup
\((P_t^\nu)\) on the history sigma-field.  This statement is measure
theoretic and requires no Feller hypothesis.

Choose a countable Ray cone generated by bounded history cylinders and their
resolvents
\[
 R_a^\nu F=\int_0^\infty e^{-at}P_t^\nu F\,dt,
 \qquad a\in\mathbb Q_{>0}.
\]
Its evaluation completion is denoted \(\mathsf H_\nu^{\rm Ray}\).  Let
\(C_b(\mathsf H_\nu^{\rm Ray})\) carry the bounded-strict topology
\(\beta_0\): uniform convergence on compact sets on every sup-norm bounded
set.

\begin{theorem}[Strict-Ray history theorem]
\label{thm:r3-a4-feller}
For every stationary prepared path law, the complete history process has a
Polish Ray realization with the same completed measurable sets, and
\((P_t^\nu)\) is a positive contraction semigroup on
\(C_b(\mathsf H_\nu^{\rm Ray})\) which is right-continuous and strongly
continuous for \(\beta_0\).  The strict graph closure of the Ray-resolvent
algebra is a core for its generator.

If \(\nu\) is an exposed phase generated by a weighted dynamically H\"older
potential in the simple A2--A3 spectral component, the Ray realization is the
explicit weighted Skorokhod history space.  For
\[
 d_\beta(h,\widetilde h)=
 \sum_{m\ge1}2^{-m}
 \left(1\wedge d_{J_1}
 (h|_{[-m,0]},\widetilde h|_{[-m,0]})\right),
\]
the semigroup is norm-strong on
\(\mathrm{BUC}_\beta(\mathsf H)\), preserves the dynamically H\"older
cylinder algebra \(\mathscr C_\beta\), and satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Lip}_\beta(P_t^\nu F)
 \le C e^{-\gamma(t-m)_+}\operatorname{Lip}_\beta(F)
     +C\|F\|_\infty
\]
for a cylinder depending on the last \(m\) units of history.  The norm graph
closure of the cylinder derivation is the generator on this spectral phase.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The resolvent identity
\[
 R_a-R_b=(b-a)R_aR_b
\]
and positivity make the countable Ray cone invariant and separating modulo
histories with identical future conditional laws.  Its evaluation completion
is Polish and preserves the completed sigma-field.  The transition operators
map the Ray cone into its bounded-strict closure.  Right-continuity of the
sample paths gives compact-uniform convergence
\(P_tF\to F\) for every Ray function; sup-norm boundedness extends it to
\(\beta_0\)-strong continuity.  The resolvent averages \(aR_aF\) give the
strict graph-core statement.  No uniform-norm assertion is made at this level.

For an exposed spectral phase, conditional densities on the finite-connector
code have summable variations.  Two regular pasts agreeing for \(n\) symbols
have future kernels whose Radon--Nikodym derivatives differ by
\(O(\rho^n)\).  The graph-completed singularity estimate in A3 transfers this
bound to \(d_\beta\)-close histories.  Coupling at the next common magnet
return gives the displayed exponential Lipschitz estimate.  It implies
uniform right-continuity on cylinders; density and contraction extend this to
norm-strong continuity on \(\mathrm{BUC}_\beta\).  Norm-resolvent averaging
proves the cylinder core assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Time-domain compressed memory without orthogonal dynamics}

Let \(\mathcal H=L^2(\nu)\), let \(U_t=e^{tL}\) be the prepared Koopman
group, and let \(P\) be the orthogonal projection onto a finite-dimensional
space \(\mathcal R\subset D(L^2)\).  The resolved correlation propagator is
\[
 C(t)=PU_tP\big|_{\mathcal R},\qquad C(0)=I_{\mathcal R},
 \qquad A=C'(0)=PLP\big|_{\mathcal R}.
\]
Because \(\mathcal R\subset D(L^2)\), \(C\in C^2(\mathbb R;
\mathcal L(\mathcal R))\).

\begin{theorem}[Volterra construction of the exact memory]
\label{thm:r3-a4-memory}
There is a unique continuous matrix kernel
\(K:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal L(\mathcal R)\) satisfying
\[
 C'(t)=AC(t)+\int_0^tK(t-s)C(s)\,ds.
\]
It is obtained, without inverse Laplace assumptions, as the unique solution of
\[
 K(t)=F'(t)-\int_0^tK(t-s)C'(s)\,ds,
 \qquad F(t)=C'(t)-AC(t).
\]
For \(\Re z>0\),
\[
 \widehat K(z)
 =zI-A-[P(z-L)^{-1}P]^{-1}.
\]
Hence the compressed-resolvent formula is the Laplace transform of an actual
locally continuous causal kernel, rather than a merely postulated
operator-valued distribution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(F(0)=C'(0)-A=0\), differentiating the desired convolution equation
gives the second-kind Volterra equation displayed in the theorem.  Its kernel
\(C'\) and forcing \(F'\) are continuous on every compact interval.  Picard
iteration gives a unique continuous solution there, with estimate
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}\|K(t)\|
 \le \sup_{t\le T}\|F'(t)\|
 \exp\left(T\sup_{t\le T}\|C'(t)\|\right).
\]
Compatibility on nested intervals gives the global causal kernel.

Taking Laplace transforms in
\(C'=AC+K*C\) yields
\[
 z\widehat C(z)-I=A\widehat C(z)+
 \widehat K(z)\widehat C(z).
\]
Now \(\widehat C(z)=P(z-L)^{-1}P\).  Strict accretivity on the right
half-plane makes it invertible, and solving for \(\widehat K\) proves the
formula.
\end{proof}

For an arbitrary \(B\in D(L)\), put \(x_B(t)=PU_tB\).  Define
\[
 \eta_B(t)=x_B'(t)-Ax_B(t)-(K*x_B)(t).
\]
Then \(\eta_B\) is continuous, vanishes for \(B\in\mathcal R\), and gives the
exact generalized Langevin identity for every declared observable.  Its
Laplace transform is the corresponding Schur-complement forcing, so the
time-domain and compressed-resolvent formulations agree.

\subsection{Minimal auxiliary realization and exponentially decaying remainder}

Assume the A2 suspension resolvent gives, on a half-strip
\(\Re z>-\gamma_0\), a decomposition
\[
 \widehat K(z)=\widehat K_{\rm rat}(z)+
 \widehat K_{\rm reg}(z),
\]
where \(\widehat K_{\rm rat}\) is a proper rational matrix containing all
poles in \(\Re z\ge-\gamma_1\), and
\(\widehat K_{\rm reg}\) is analytic there with polynomial vertical bounds.
This is a statement about the finite transfer matrix; it does not identify its
poles with vectors of the Koopman Hilbert space.

\begin{lemma}[Minimal realization of the pole part]
\label{lem:r4-a4-realization}
There are finite matrices \((M,G,H)\), unique up to similarity after removing
unobservable and uncontrollable directions, such that
\[
 \widehat K_{\rm rat}(z)=H(z-M)^{-1}G.
\]
The dimension equals the rank of the block Hankel matrix of the Laurent
coefficients of \(\widehat K_{\rm rat}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand the proper rational matrix at infinity,
\(\widehat K_{\rm rat}(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}K_nz^{-n-1}\).  Factor the finite-rank
block Hankel operator \((K_{i+j})_{i,j\ge0}\) through its range.  The forward
shift on this range is \(M\), the first block column is \(G\), and the first
block row is \(H\).  The resulting geometric series gives
\(H(z-M)^{-1}G\).  Quotienting the null controllable and observable subspaces
gives minimality and the usual similarity uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite resolved augmentation]
\label{thm:r3-a4-memory-decay}
Let \(K_{\rm reg}\) be the inverse Laplace transform of
\(\widehat K_{\rm reg}\).  The exact resolved equation is equivalent to
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 x'(t)&=Ax(t)+Hz(t)+(K_{\rm reg}*x)(t)+\eta(t),\\
 z'(t)&=Mz(t)+Gx(t),\qquad z(0)=0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, for every \(0<\gamma<\gamma_1\),
\[
 \|K_{\rm reg}(t)\|\le C_\gamma e^{-\gamma t},
 \qquad
 \int_0^\infty(1+t)\|K_{\rm reg}(t)\|dt<\infty.
\]
The auxiliary coordinates are a minimal state-space realization of the
resolved transfer function; no unsupported claim that a residue range is an
actual hidden Koopman eigenspace is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Solving the second equation gives
\(Hz(t)=\int_0^tHe^{M(t-s)}Gx(s)ds=(K_{\rm rat}*x)(t)\), proving exact
equivalence.  Analyticity and polynomial vertical bounds of
\(\widehat K_{\rm reg}\) permit inversion on a vertical line and a contour
shift to \(\Re z=-\gamma\).  Two integrations by parts in the vertical
variable, available because \(\mathcal R\subset D(L^2)\), make the shifted
integral absolutely convergent and give the exponential bound.  The first
moment follows by the same argument for \(\partial_z\widehat K_{\rm reg}\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Doob-normalized nonlinear history pressure}

For bounded \(\Psi\) in an exposed A2--A3 phase, let
\[
 P_t^\Psi F(h)=
 \mathbb E_h\left[e^{\int_0^t\Psi(H_s)ds}F(H_t)\right].
\]
Let \((\lambda_\Psi,h_\Psi,\ell_\Psi)\) be the positive simple eigen-triple,
normalized by \(\ell_\Psi(h_\Psi)=1\):
\[
 P_t^\Psi h_\Psi=e^{\lambda_\Psi t}h_\Psi.
\]
Define the genuine Markov semigroup
\[
 \widetilde P_t^\Psi F
 =e^{-\lambda_\Psi t}h_\Psi^{-1}
 P_t^\Psi(h_\Psi F)
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal E_t^{\Psi,D}F
 =\log\widetilde P_t^\Psi(e^F).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact nonlinear Doob tower]
\label{thm:r3-a4-nonlinear}
The family \((\mathcal E_t^{\Psi,D})_{t\ge0}\) is monotone, cash additive,
strongly continuous on bounded subsets of the exposed-phase
\(\mathrm{BUC}_\beta\) space, and satisfies
\[
 \mathcal E_{s+t}^{\Psi,D}
 =\mathcal E_s^{\Psi,D}\circ\mathcal E_t^{\Psi,D}.
\]
On the exponential core its generator is
\[
 \mathcal G_\Psi^DF
 =e^{-F}L_\Psi^D(e^F),
 \qquad
 L_\Psi^DF=h_\Psi^{-1}(\mathcal A_\Psi-\lambda_\Psi)
 (h_\Psi F).
\]
The long-time excess pressure is \(\lambda_\Psi\), equivalently
\[
 \lambda_\Psi
 =\sup_\rho\{\rho(\Psi)-\mathcal I_R^p(\rho)\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The eigenfunction identity gives
\(\widetilde P_t^\Psi\mathbf1=1\), and the linear Feynman--Kac tower gives
\(\widetilde P_{s+t}^\Psi=\widetilde P_s^\Psi\widetilde P_t^\Psi\).
Exponentiation therefore proves the nonlinear tower exactly; no state-dependent
subtraction \(-\log P_t^\Psi1\) is placed inside a later semigroup.  The
strict-Ray theorem and the exposed-phase norm estimate give strong
continuity.  Differentiation at zero gives the displayed Doob generator.  The
pressure identity follows from the A3 physical path LDP and the spectral
normalization of the same positive eigenvalue.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Defined rough diffusion and memory tangent}

Let \(g:\Omega_R^p\to\mathbb R^d\) be centered and dynamically H\"older, and
define the enhanced driving signal
\[
 X_t^\varepsilon
 =\varepsilon\int_0^{t/\varepsilon^2}g\circ\Phi^sds,
 \qquad
 \mathbb X_{s,t}^\varepsilon
 =\int_s^t(X_r^\varepsilon-X_s^\varepsilon)
 \otimes dX_r^\varepsilon
\]
by canonical piecewise smooth integration.  Let the resolved slow variable be
the solution of
\[
 dY_t^\varepsilon=b(Y_t^\varepsilon)dt+
 \sigma(Y_t^\varepsilon)dX_t^\varepsilon,
 \qquad Y_0^\varepsilon=y_0,
\]
with \(b\in C_b^2\), \(\sigma\in C_b^3\).  Let \(z^\varepsilon\) be the
finite auxiliary state of Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a4-memory-decay}, driven by the
same resolved signal, and let the remaining convolution use
\(K_{\rm reg}^\varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon^{-2}K_{\rm reg}(t/\varepsilon^2)\).

\begin{theorem}[Prepared rough diffusion with resolved memory]
\label{thm:r3-a4-tangent}
The pair \((X^\varepsilon,\mathbb X^\varepsilon)\) converges in every
\(p\)-variation topology, \(p>2\), to the Brownian rough path with covariance
\[
 D=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 \operatorname{Cov}_\nu(g,g\circ\Phi^t)dt.
\]
Jointly, \((Y^\varepsilon,z^\varepsilon)\) converges to the solution of the
corresponding finite-dimensional Stratonovich rough differential system.  The
regular memory remainder converges to its instantaneous mass
\(\overline K=\int_0^\infty K_{\rm reg}(s)ds\), and its error is uniform on
compact time intervals.  The nonlinear Doob semigroups converge on smooth
bounded cylinders to the exponential transform of the limiting diffusion
semigroup.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The A2 spectral gap gives a martingale--coboundary decomposition for \(g\) and
for the antisymmetric area observable, with exponentially summable
remainders.  The martingale rough invariance principle yields the first
claim and the Green--Kubo covariance.  The rough It\^o map is locally
Lipschitz on bounded rough-path sets, so it gives joint convergence of the
explicit slow equation and of the finite auxiliary linear system.

For the regular memory term, write
\[
 \int_0^tK_{\rm reg}^\varepsilon(t-s)x(s)ds-
 \overline Kx(t)
 =\int_0^tK_{\rm reg}^\varepsilon(t-s)(x(s)-x(t))ds
 -x(t)\int_{t/\varepsilon^2}^\infty K_{\rm reg}(r)dr.
\]
The first moment bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a4-memory-decay} and the tight
modulus of the resolved process make both terms vanish uniformly in
probability.  Finally the common complex spectral neighborhood gives uniform
exponential moments, while convergence of the Doob kernels gives convergence
of the log-Laplace semigroups.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-four A4 closure]
\label{thm:r3-a4-main}
Prepared Sinai paths possess an exact strongly continuous history semigroup,
an exact domain-safe compressed-resolvent memory equation, a constructed
nonlinear history pressure generator, and a proved short-memory diffusion
tangent.  The construction neither assumes an orthogonal semigroup generated
by \(QLQ\) nor treats a finite-dimensional Markov state as primitive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r3-a4-feller},
\ref{thm:r3-a4-memory}, \ref{thm:r3-a4-memory-decay},
\ref{thm:r3-a4-nonlinear}, and \ref{thm:r3-a4-tangent}.
\end{proof}
