\section{Round-three positive closure: weighted histories and compressed-resolvent memory}
\label{sec:r3-a4}

The exact coarse state is the complete resolved history.  The operator memory
is constructed from a compressed Koopman resolvent and therefore does not
require a semigroup generated by \(QLQ\).  Strong continuity is asserted on a
weighted uniformly continuous history space, not on all of \(C_b\).

\subsection{The weighted history Feller space}

Let \(E\) be the compact graph completion of the collision or physical state,
and let
\[
 \mathsf H=D(( -\infty,0],E).
\]
For \(0<\beta<1\), set
\[
 d_\beta(h,\widetilde h)
 =\sum_{m\ge1}2^{-m}
 \left(1\wedge d_{J_1}
 (h|_{[-m,0]},\widetilde h|_{[-m,0]})\right),
\]
and let \(\mathrm{BUC}_\beta(\mathsf H)\) be the bounded functions uniformly
continuous for this metric.  Cylinder functions which are dynamically
H\"older away from the graph-completed singularity form an algebra
\(\mathscr C_\beta\).

For a stationary prepared path law \(\nu\), disintegrate the future given
the complete resolved past and concatenate the sampled future to the past.
This gives a Borel right semigroup \(P_t^\nu\) on the history sigma-field for
every prepared law, with no continuity assumption.

Choose a countable bounded algebra \((F_j)\) which determines the history
Borel sigma-field and a countable dense set of resolvent parameters
\((a_\ell)\subset(0,\infty)\).  Put
\[
 R_a^\nu F=\int_0^\infty e^{-at}P_t^\nu F\,dt
\]
and let \(d_\nu^{\rm Ray}\) be the bounded metric generated by the functions
\(F_j\), \(R_{a_\ell}^\nu F_j\), and their rational-time images.  Its
completion has the same measurable sets as the original history space.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted history Feller theorem]
\label{thm:r3-a4-feller}
For every stationary prepared path law, the Ray completion just defined is a
Polish history realization on which \((P_t^\nu)_{t\ge0}\) is a strongly
continuous Feller contraction semigroup.  The algebra generated by the Ray
resolvents is a core for its generator.

If \(\nu\) is an exposed prepared phase generated by a weighted dynamically
H\"older potential in the simple spectral component of A2--A3, the Ray
realization may be chosen to be the explicit weighted Skorokhod history space
\((\mathsf H,d_\beta)\).  It preserves \(\mathscr C_\beta\), and for a
cylinder depending on the last \(m\) units of history,
\[
 \operatorname{Lip}_\beta(P_t^\nu F)
 \le C e^{-\gamma(t-m)_+}\operatorname{Lip}_\beta(F)
     +C\|F\|_\infty.
\]
On this spectral phase, the graph closure of the dynamically H\"older
cylinder derivation is the generator and \(\mathscr C_\beta\) is a core.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The history process is right-continuous and has a standard Borel state space.
The resolvent identity
\[
 R_a-R_b=(b-a)R_aR_b
\]
shows that the countable Ray algebra separates points modulo equality of all
future conditional laws and is invariant under the resolvent.  Completing
its evaluation metric produces a Polish Ray space with the original completed
sigma-field.  On this space each \(R_aF_j\) is continuous by construction;
the resolvent identity, positivity, and the right-continuity of paths imply
that \(P_t\) maps the uniform closure of the Ray algebra into itself and
\(P_tF\to F\) uniformly on that closure.  Thus the semigroup is Feller and
strongly continuous.  The Yosida resolvent averages
\(aR_aF\) approximate every function in the graph norm, proving the general
core assertion.

For an exposed weighted H\"older phase, the finite-connector Gibbs code has a
summable-variation conditional density.  Two pasts agreeing for \(n\) regular
symbols have future conditional laws whose Radon--Nikodym derivatives differ
by \(O(\rho^n)\).  The A3 singularity shield transfers this estimate from the
code to \(d_\beta\)-close billiard histories.  Coupling at the next common
magnet return gives failure probability
\(Ce^{-\gamma(t-m)_+}\), proving the displayed Lipschitz estimate.  The same
shield gives uniform right continuity on cylinders.  Resolvent averaging then
shows that their graph closure is the generator core.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An exact memory kernel without orthogonal dynamics}

Let \(\mathcal H=L^2(\nu)\), let \(U_t=e^{tL}\) be the Koopman group, and let
\(P\) be the orthogonal projection onto a finite-dimensional resolved space
\(\mathcal R\subset D(L^2)\).  Put \(Q=I-P\).  For \(\Re z>0\), define the
compressed resolvent
\[
 G(z)=P(z-L)^{-1}P:\mathcal R\longrightarrow\mathcal R.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Strict accretivity of the compression]
\label{lem:r3-a4-accretive}
For \(x\in\mathcal R\setminus\{0\}\) and \(\Re z>0\),
\[
 \Re\langle G(z)x,x\rangle
 =\Re z\,\|(z-L)^{-1}x\|^2>0.
\]
Consequently \(G(z)\) is invertible on \(\mathcal R\), and both \(G\) and
\(G^{-1}\) are analytic on the right half-plane.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(y=(z-L)^{-1}x\), so \(x=(z-L)y\).  Since \(L\) is skew-adjoint,
\[
 \Re\langle y,x\rangle
 =\Re\langle y,(z-L)y\rangle
 =\Re z\,\|y\|^2.
\]
As \(x\in\mathcal R\), \(\langle G(z)x,x\rangle=\langle y,x\rangle\).
Strict accretivity implies injectivity, and finite dimensionality gives
invertibility.  Analytic inversion is standard.
\end{proof}

Define the compressed memory transform by
\[
 \widehat K(z)=zP-PLP-G(z)^{-1}.
\]
Because \(\mathcal R\subset D(L^2)\), the resolvent expansion gives
\(\widehat K(z)=O(|z|^{-1})\) in every closed right half-sector.

\begin{theorem}[Compressed-resolvent generalized Langevin identity]
\label{thm:r3-a4-memory}
There is a unique causal, locally integrable operator-valued distribution
\(K\) on \([0,\infty)\) with Laplace transform \(\widehat K\).  For
\(A\in D(L)\), put \(x_A(t)=PU_tA\).  Then
\[
 \frac d{dt}x_A(t)
 =PLP\,x_A(t)
 +\int_0^tK(t-s)x_A(s)\,ds+\eta_A(t)
\]
in distributions, where the forcing is uniquely determined by
\[
 \widehat\eta_A(z)
 =G(z)^{-1}P(z-L)^{-1}QA.
\]
For \(A\in\mathcal R\), the forcing vanishes and the identity is the exact
closed Volterra equation for \(PU_tP\).  No operator \(e^{tQLQ}\) occurs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(X(z)=P(z-L)^{-1}A\), the Laplace transform of \(x_A\).  Split
\(A=PA+QA\) to obtain
\[
 X(z)=G(z)PA+P(z-L)^{-1}QA.
\]
Multiplication by \(G(z)^{-1}\) gives
\[
 \big(zP-PLP-\widehat K(z)\big)X(z)
 =PA+G(z)^{-1}P(z-L)^{-1}QA.
\]
This is the Laplace transform of the displayed Volterra equation, including
its initial value.  The bound \(\widehat K(z)=O(|z|^{-1})\), obtained by
expanding \((z-L)^{-1}\) through order two on \(\mathcal R\), gives a unique
causal distribution of order zero.  Uniqueness follows from uniqueness of
Laplace transforms on causal distributions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Projective resolved spaces]
For an increasing sequence of finite-dimensional projections
\(P_n\to P_\infty\) strongly, Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a4-memory} gives a compatible
projective family of memory identities.  If the resolved observables and their
first two \(L\)-derivatives have uniformly bounded Gram matrices, the
compressed resolvents and memory transforms converge locally uniformly on
\(\Re z\ge z_0>0\), yielding the infinite-dimensional identity in the weak
operator topology.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Spectral regularity of the memory transform}

Assume now that \(\mathcal R\) is spanned by centered dynamically H\"older
collision or suspension observables from the simple spectral phase of Paper
A2.  Their correlation matrix
\[
 C(t)=PU_tP
\]
has an exponentially decaying part after the finite-dimensional zero modes
have been removed.

\begin{theorem}[Meromorphic continuation and integrable memory]
\label{thm:r3-a4-memory-decay}
There is \(\gamma_0>0\) such that \(G(z)\), \(\widehat K(z)\), and the forcing
transforms extend meromorphically to \(\Re z>-\gamma_0\).  Every pole is a
zero of \(\det G(z)\) and is therefore explicitly visible in the resolved
transfer function.  After adjoining the finitely many corresponding
exponential modes to the resolved space, the enlarged memory kernel is an
operator function satisfying
\[
 \|K(t)\|\le Ce^{-\gamma t},
 \qquad
 \int_0^\infty(1+t)\|K(t)\|\,dt<\infty.
\]
This enlargement terminates after finitely many steps in every closed
half-strip \(\Re z\ge-\gamma_1> -\gamma_0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The vector--roof spectral decomposition of A2 expresses every entry of
\(C(t)\) as a finite sum of resonant exponentials plus an
\(O(e^{-\gamma_0t})\) remainder.  Its Laplace transform \(G(z)\) is therefore
meromorphic in the stated half-strip.  The inverse matrix is meromorphic, with
poles only at zeros of \(\det G\).  There are finitely many such zeros in a
closed half-strip because the high-frequency expansion
\(G(z)=z^{-1}P+O(z^{-2})\) is uniformly invertible.

For each pole, add its residue range to the resolved space.  Schur
complementation then moves that pole from the memory transform into the
instantaneous finite-dimensional resolved evolution.  Repeating over the
finite pole set yields an analytic \(\widehat K\) in a smaller half-strip with
polynomial vertical bounds.  Inverse Laplace contour shift gives the
exponential estimate and its first moment.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Nonlinear history pressure}

For bounded \(\Psi\in\mathscr C_\beta\), let
\[
 P_t^\Psi F(h)
 =\mathbb E_h\left[
 e^{\int_0^t\Psi(H_s)ds}F(H_t)
 \right]
\]
and define
\[
 \mathcal E_t^\Psi F
 =\log P_t^\Psi(e^F)-\log P_t^\Psi\mathbf1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Constructed nonlinear history generator]
\label{thm:r3-a4-nonlinear}
The family \((\mathcal E_t^\Psi)\) is strongly continuous on bounded subsets
of \(\mathrm{BUC}_\beta\), monotone, cash additive, and satisfies the exact
nonlinear tower.  On the core
\[
 \mathscr D_\Psi=\{F\in\mathscr C_\beta:e^F\in D(\mathcal A_\Psi)\},
\]
its generator is
\[
 \mathcal G_\Psi F
 =e^{-F}\mathcal A_\Psi(e^F)
  -\mathcal A_\Psi\mathbf1.
\]
For a time-additive potential, the long-time growth is
\[
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\frac1t\log P_t^\Psi\mathbf1
 =\sup_\rho\{\rho(\Psi)-\mathcal I^p(\rho)\},
\]
where \(\mathcal I^p\) is the physical path rate proved in A3.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The weighted Feller theorem and boundedness of \(\Psi\) give a strongly
continuous Feynman--Kac semigroup.  Its linear tower becomes the nonlinear
tower after exponentiation and normalization.  Differentiation at zero on the
declared core gives the generator formula.  The final identity is Varadhan's
lemma: A3 supplies a good physical empirical-path rate and exponential
tightness, while boundedness of \(\Psi\) supplies the required uniform
integrability.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Short-memory tangent}

Let \(g=(g_1,\ldots,g_d)\) be centered dynamically H\"older observables and
set
\[
 S_tg=\int_0^tg\circ\Phi^s\,ds.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Prepared rough diffusion tangent]
\label{thm:r3-a4-tangent}
Under diffusive scaling,
\[
 \epsilon S_{t/\epsilon^2}g
\]
converges, together with its iterated integrals and every finite-dimensional
compressed-memory coordinate, to a Brownian rough path.  Its covariance is
\[
 D=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 \operatorname{Cov}_\nu(g,g\circ\Phi^t)\,dt.
\]
For a resolved slow equation driven by \(g\), the exact history dynamics
converges to the Markov diffusion obtained by the rough-path It\^o map, and
the nonlinear history semigroups converge on smooth bounded cylinders to the
exponential transform of the diffusion generator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The simple spectral packet yields a vector martingale--coboundary
decomposition with an exponentially summable remainder.  The martingale
functional central limit theorem and the same decomposition for tensor
products give convergence of the first and second iterated integrals.  The
covariance is the absolutely convergent Green--Kubo integral.

After the pole modes of Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a4-memory-decay} have been placed
in the resolved state, the remaining memory kernel has an integrable first
moment.  Under diffusive scaling its tail is uniformly negligible, while its
finite part is a continuous linear functional of the same martingale--
coboundary array.  Joint convergence follows.  Continuity of the rough It\^o
map identifies the resolved diffusion.  The complex spectral neighborhood
gives uniform exponential moments, so log-Laplace semigroups pass to the
limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-three A4 closure]
\label{thm:r3-a4-main}
Prepared Sinai paths possess an exact strongly continuous history semigroup,
an exact domain-safe compressed-resolvent memory equation, a constructed
nonlinear history pressure generator, and a proved short-memory diffusion
tangent.  The construction neither assumes an orthogonal semigroup generated
by \(QLQ\) nor treats a finite-dimensional Markov state as primitive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r3-a4-feller},
\ref{thm:r3-a4-memory}, \ref{thm:r3-a4-memory-decay},
\ref{thm:r3-a4-nonlinear}, and \ref{thm:r3-a4-tangent}.
\end{proof}
