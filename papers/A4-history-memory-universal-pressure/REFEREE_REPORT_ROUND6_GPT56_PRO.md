## Editorial summary

The revision corrects two important overclaims. It now states the exact forcing trace `eta_B(0)=PLQB` without calling it orthogonal, and it no longer infers a zero-free compressed resolvent from covariance positivity. The genuine Doob transform and the deterministic rough-area anomaly are also the right objects.

The central conditional theorem, however, is impossible for the state and filtration that the paper itself defines. The “complete history” of the full Sinai suspension state determines the entire deterministic future, so its conditional kernel is a Dirac mass. Such kernels cannot converge uniformly—or even weakly at one fixed history—to a nondegenerate Brownian rough-diffusion kernel. This contradiction invalidates the conditional homogenization theorem and the downstream C2 likelihood passage. The Ray-process and transmission-zero constructions are also only formal sketches.

## Major mathematical objections

### 1. Complete-history conditioning makes the future deterministic

The manuscript defines `E` as the graph-completed Sinai suspension state and

\[
\mathsf H=D(( -\infty,0],E).
\]

The billiard/suspension flow on `E` is deterministic and invertible outside the graph-completed singular convention. A complete history contains the current state `h(0)`. Therefore, for every regular history `h`, the future path is fixed:

\[
K_T^\varepsilon(h,\cdot)
=\delta_{\mathcal F_T^\varepsilon(h)}.
\]

This is not changed by choosing a stationary prepared initial law. Disintegration with respect to a sigma-field that reveals the current full state gives a point mass.

`thm:r6-a4-conditional` instead claims that, uniformly over compact sets of complete histories,

\[
K_T^\varepsilon(h,\cdot)
\Longrightarrow K_T^{\rm diff}(X_0(h),\cdot),
\]

where the right side is a nondegenerate rough-diffusion law. This cannot happen. On a Polish path space, any weakly convergent sequence of Dirac measures has a Dirac limit: continuous functions separate points, so convergence of all integrals `f(x_epsilon)` forces convergence of the underlying points. A nondegenerate Brownian law is not a Dirac measure.

The proof's coupling of two histories after a common Young return applies to conditional laws given a quotient past that forgets stable/future-determining coordinates, not to the complete microscopic history declared here. The paper conflates these two filtrations. One must either condition on a genuinely coarse observation sigma-field, introduce randomized unresolved coordinates, or formulate quenched homogenization with an additional randomization of initial conditions. The theorem as written is false.

### 2. The concrete Feller coupling has the same filtration error

`thm:r6-a4-feller` says that two complete histories can be coupled after their next common Young return and that their future conditional densities differ exponentially little. For deterministic full-state histories, there are no conditional densities to couple: each history has one future trajectory.

A stable-leaf quotient can have a stochastic `g`-kernel because it discards information. The full graph-completed collision/suspension state cannot. The manuscript must specify the quotient map, prove that the chosen history sigma-field is generated by quotient observations rather than full states, and then reconstruct any physical statement through conditional measures on stable fibers. Merely calling the state “resolved” does not alter the fact that `E` was defined as the full suspension state.

### 3. Rational-time disintegrations do not produce the claimed continuous-time Ray resolvent

The paper constructs kernels only for rational `t`, obtains rational Chapman–Kolmogorov identities, and then defines

\[
R_\lambda F=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}P_tF\,dt.
\]

No kernel `P_t` has been defined for irrational `t` at this point. “Monotone approximation” of an integral does not create a unique measurable semigroup from rational values unless a suitable right-continuity theorem has already been proved. The proof then uses that resolvent to build the Ray process whose right continuity is supposed to justify the extension. This is circular.

Standard Ray–Knight construction requires a genuine sub-Markov resolvent satisfying the resolvent equation, excessive-function regularity, and a normality/separation package. The statements that a countable evaluation closure is Polish and that kernels preserve excessive functions do not establish those hypotheses or the strong Markov property.

### 4. The history eigenfunction formula is not well-defined on the stated history space

The formula uses `y(h)`, the last Young-base state, and `s(h)`, elapsed time since the last return. A two-sided complete history may have no last return in a finite interval, may lie on an induced-boundary ambiguity, or may carry multiple graph-completed codes. The proof cites “matching one-sided traces” of the quotient eigenfunction, but equality of traces in an ambient A2 representation has not been proved and does not by itself identify all coding representatives.

Moreover, boundedness away from zero of a quotient transfer eigenfunction on a countable Young tower is not automatic. A spectral cone on a normalized base need not give a global lower bound after suspension and history extension. The Doob semigroup is therefore not established on the full `BUC_beta` space claimed.

### 5. The finite transmission-zero realization is not a theorem about the compressed generator

The matrix

\[
\widehat C(z)=P(z-L)^{-1}P|_V
\]

is a finite matrix of meromorphic functions, not generally a rational transfer matrix. The proof invokes a Smith–McMillan factorization and then says to realize the inverse zero factor by a finite controllable state-space system. Smith–McMillan theory in the stated finite-dimensional realization form applies to rational matrix functions. A general meromorphic factor on a strip is not the transfer function of a finite-dimensional ODE.

Even if finitely many zeros are extracted by local analytic factors, an arbitrary input-output realization does not show that there is an enlarged orthogonal compression `P_tilde(z-L_tilde)^{-1}P_tilde` coming from the original Koopman/Markov generator and an actual auxiliary subspace. The manuscript constructs matrices, not an invariant or coupled state inside the dynamics. The assertions that every zero becomes an eigenmode of `A_Z` and that block elimination exactly recovers the original compressed dynamics require a realization theorem with compatibility and minimality conditions that is absent.

### 6. The exponential residual-memory estimate lacks the required high-frequency regularity

The state space assumption is only `V subset D(L^2)`. Shifting a Bromwich contour and obtaining an integrable vertical majorant after “finitely many integrations by parts” requires enough generator powers to overcome the polynomial loss in the billiard resolvent. Two derivatives are generally insufficient, and the required order depends on the Dolgopyat exponent.

The proof supplies no quantitative bound for the inverse augmented transfer, no control near extracted poles/zeros, and no horizontal-contour estimate. Thus even apart from objection 5, exponential decay of `K_res` is not established.

### 7. The rough invariance statement overclaims the available moment range

The paper assumes one moment exponent `q>4` and concludes tightness in every `p`-variation topology with `p>2`. Rough-path moment criteria normally relate the available moment order to the admissible variation exponent and require second-level increment estimates. The proof mentions Burkholder bounds but gives neither the required two-parameter estimates nor uniform roof-interpolation control. The area formula may be the correct candidate, but the theorem is not proved at the stated level of generality.

### 8. The coupled slow-memory scaling is inconsistent for the auxiliary modes

Only the residual kernel is accelerated by `epsilon^{-2}`. The auxiliary transmission-zero state is left with

\[
dZ_t^\varepsilon=A_ZZ_t^\varepsilon dt+B_ZR(Y_t^\varepsilon)dt,
\]

with no scale separation tied to the original fast process. A mode extracted from a microscopic compressed resolvent generally lives on the fast time scale before homogenization. Treating it as an unscaled macroscopic ODE changes the transfer function. The final convergence paragraph does not derive the joint scaling or compatible initial law.

## Dependency consequences

C2's optional-projection convergence explicitly invokes `thm:r6-a4-conditional`; its Girsanov/BSDE limit therefore fails with A4. D1 also cites A4 for Sinai process tightness. Those downstream claims cannot be credited while complete-history conditional kernels are deterministic.

## Required reconstruction

A viable revision must:

1. distinguish the full microscopic history from a coarse quotient/observation history and state exactly which filtration is used;
2. formulate a conditional or stable homogenization theorem compatible with that coarse filtration and prove it without Dirac-kernel contradiction;
3. construct a genuine continuous-time resolvent before applying Ray theory;
4. define the history eigenfunction on all relevant coding classes with quantitative bounds;
5. replace the formal Smith–McMillan paragraph by an actual operator-compatible realization theorem, or state only a finite-dimensional algebraic factorization; and
6. give quantitative generator-domain and rough-moment assumptions sufficient for contour shifting and enhanced tightness.

## Recommendation

**Reject.** Round six correctly repairs the old forcing-trace and zero-free-resolvent overclaims, but its new flagship conditional-kernel theorem contradicts deterministic complete-history conditioning. The Ray, finite-mode memory, and enhanced-limit constructions do not close that gap. Because C2 depends directly on this theorem, the failure is series-level.
