# Primary literature verification for A2 v14

Checked 10 September 2026. This is a targeted comparison, not an exhaustive priority search.

## Local analytic forward mechanism

J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin and M. Leguil, *Marked Length Spectral determination of analytic chaotic billiards with axial symmetries*, arXiv:1905.00890v4; published in Inventiones mathematicae 233 (2023), 829-901.

The versioned PDF was accessed through the public primary source. Printed pages 10 and 13 were inspected as page images. Page 10 recalls the local analytic hyperbolic normal form and its attribution. Page 13 distinguishes results not needing the later symmetry condition from geometric reconstruction under that condition. The v14 comparison therefore does not transfer global inverse symmetry hypotheses onto the mere existence of local normalizing charts.

Primary version: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00890v4

The physical endpoint determinant calculation is credited separately to the A2 v13 referee's companion `ANALYTIC_NORMAL_FORM_BENCHMARK.md`, commit `b3f0ad5843782c221651c9189fc156d20865cab1`. It is not represented as a probability theorem stated in the cited published paper. The current proof conditions its parameter-family version on supplied uniform charts. No original Moser or Sternberg article is claimed to have been independently checked in this revision; the local statement is used as recalled in the inspected primary billiard paper.

## Preserved inverse comparisons

D. Finamore and M. Leguil, arXiv:2510.18983v1, primary HTML introduction and Theorem A: the enriched marked length spectrum and finite-horizon hypotheses are retained in the manuscript comparison. https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18983v1

S. Zelditch, *Inverse spectral problem for analytic domains II*, arXiv:math/0111078: the prescribed symmetry/nondegeneracy classes and spectral observation are retained; no unrestricted determination theorem is attributed. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0111078

These comparisons concern different observation sets. The new forward subsection makes explicit that this difference alone is not a novelty argument for relative factorization. The smooth intrinsic density and width statements are proved from the manuscript's physical constructions; their proof does not require an assertion that a uniform smooth normal-form theorem is impossible or absent from all literature.
