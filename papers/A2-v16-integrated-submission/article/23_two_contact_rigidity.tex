\section{Determination of two independent even contacts}
\label{sec:v12-two-contact}
The boundary invariant has a geometric inverse on a class substantially
larger than the identical-contact class. In this section each facing
graph is even in the common transverse coordinate, but the two graphs
are otherwise unrelated. Their curvatures may be different or equal.
This is an additional hypothesis for geometric determination, not for
Theorems~\ref{thm:v8-main-relative} and~\ref{thm:v9-compatibility}.
The gap and the two labelled contact curvatures are fixed. They are not
inferred separately from the single multiplier.

Write, for $b=0,1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-two-jets}
 \psi_b(y)=\frac{\kappa_b}2y^2+
       \sum_{m\ge2}\frac{q_{b,2m}}{(2m)!}y^{2m},\qquad
 c_b=1+g\kappa_b,\quad c=\sqrt{c_0c_1},\quad
 \gamma=\arcosh c,\quad \varrho=e^{-\gamma}.
\end{equation}
For smooth graphs the series specifies jets only. Put
\[
 a_b=\frac{c\sinh\gamma}{g c_{1-b}},\qquad
 f_{b,k}=[d^k]\mathcal F_{bb}(d),\qquad
 q_m=(q_{0,2m},q_{1,2m})^t,\quad
 f_{m-1}=(f_{0,m-1},f_{1,m-1})^t.
\]
The two even laws are obtained by observing the two orientations of
one selected physical channel. In particular, their indices retain
contact labels even when the curvatures coincide.

\begin{theorem}[Block-triangular two-contact inverse]
\label{thm:v12-two-contact}
For every $m\ge2$, the vector $f_{m-1}$ depends only on
$g,\kappa_0,\kappa_1,q_2,\ldots,q_m$, and is affine in $q_m$.
Its derivative with respect to that last vector is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-block}
 \frac{\partial f_{m-1}}{\partial q_m}
 =-\begin{pmatrix}P_m&Q_m\\Q_m&P_m\end{pmatrix}
              \diag(K_{m,0},K_{m,1}),\qquad
 K_{m,b}=\frac{4}{2^m(m+1)(m!)^2a_b^m},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 E_m&=\frac1{e^{4(m-1)\gamma}-1}
                         -\frac1{e^{4m\gamma}-1},\notag\\
 O_m&=\frac1{2\sinh(2(m-1)\gamma)}
                         -\frac1{2\sinh(2m\gamma)},\notag\\
 P_m&=\coth(2m\gamma)+2mE_m,\qquad
 Q_m=\csch(2m\gamma)+2mO_m.\label{eq:v12-PQ}
\end{align}
Both eigenvalues of the symmetric factor are positive. More explicitly,
\begin{align}
 P_m+Q_m
 &=\coth(m\gamma)+2m\left\{
       \frac1{e^{2(m-1)\gamma}-1}-\frac1{e^{2m\gamma}-1}\right\},
                                      \label{eq:v12-plus}\\
 P_m-Q_m
 &=m\tanh((m-1)\gamma)-(m-1)\tanh(m\gamma)>0.
                                      \label{eq:v12-minus}
\end{align}
Consequently, for every finite $M\ge2$, the map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-jet-map}
 (q_2,\ldots,q_M)\longmapsto(f_1,\ldots,f_{M-1})
\end{equation}
has a block-triangular analytic inverse on its image. The inverse is
locally Lipschitz on compact finite-jet sets and positive compact
$g,\kappa_0,\kappa_1$ boxes. These bounds require no separation of
$\kappa_0$ and $\kappa_1$, and no separation of the higher jets.

Equality of the two normalized even-law germs, for two pairs of
real-analytic even graphs at the same labelled $g,\kappa_0,\kappa_1$,
implies equality of both graph germs. For smooth compact parameter
families, replacing the limiting coefficients by their even finite-flight
coefficients recovers the jet vector through order $2M$ with error
at most $C_M\tau^j$. No bound uniform in $M$ is asserted.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The two boundary contributions}
\begin{proof}
We first establish the last-jet derivative; the resulting block
invertibility will then give the inverse. Set
$\mathfrak r_b=\sqrt{c_b/c_{1-b}}$. The linear half-line orbit starting at type
$b$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-linear-orbit}
 x_i^{(b)}(u)=t_i^{(b)}u+O(u^3\rho^i),\qquad
 t_i^{(b)}=
 \begin{cases}\varrho^i,&i\text{ even},\\
                \mathfrak r_b\varrho^i,&i\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The cubic remainder follows from evenness and the half-line contraction
of Lemma~\ref{lem:v4-halfline}. Notice that
$a_b/a_{1-b}=\mathfrak r_b^2$.

For clarity, finite-jet dependence can be proved before making any
analyticity assumption on the graphs. Differentiate the stationary
half-line equations at zero in their weighted sequence space. At each
order, the highest orbit derivative is multiplied by the same positive
quadratic Jacobi operator. Its inverse is
\eqref{eq:v4-half-green}; all remaining terms contain lower orbit
jets and finite graph jets. This gives an induction in the endpoint
degree. The weighted derivative estimates justify the differentiated
action sums and the differentiated trace-class determinant series.
The coefficient of $d^{m-1}$ in the residual-time integral uses the
action through degree $2m$ and the amplitude through degree $2m-2$.
Indeed, scaling the two endpoints by $\sqrt d$ gives degrees
$d^{n/2-1}$ for an action term of endpoint degree $n$ and $d^{n/2}$
for an amplitude term. The common Morse coordinates justify this
calculation with smooth remainders down to $d=0$. Thus only graph
jets through order $2m$ can enter.

Keep all lower jets fixed and vary $q_{r,2m}$. The first varying
homogeneous term of a flight length is the sum of the corresponding
pure endpoint powers divided by $(2m)!$. Its mixed derivative is
zero. In the stationary action, a boundary site is counted once and
an interior site twice. The envelope identity therefore gives, with
$s_{b,2m}=S_b^{(2m)}(0)$,
\begin{align}
 \partial_{q_{b,2m}}s_{b,2m}
 &=1+2\sum_{k\ge1}\varrho^{4mk}=\coth(2m\gamma),\notag\\
 \partial_{q_{1-b,2m}}s_{b,2m}
 &=2\mathfrak r_b^{2m}\sum_{k\ge0}\varrho^{2m(2k+1)}
                   =\mathfrak r_b^{2m}\csch(2m\gamma).
                                      \label{eq:v12-action-block}
\end{align}
This can equally be computed on finite stationary segments and then
passed to the half-line. Every differentiated sum is absolutely
convergent. Orbit corrections of degree at least three cannot change
the degree-$2m$ terms displayed here.

Write $\beta_{b,2m-2}=[u^{2m-2}]B_b(u)$, using ordinary
coefficients. At this degree the edge-product variation vanishes,
since the varying length term is pure. The varying interior Hessian
is diagonal, with its $i$th diagonal entry, at a site of varied type,
equal to
\[
              \frac{2(t_i^{(b)})^{2m-2}u^{2m-2}}{(2m-2)!}.
\]
The variation of the stationary orbit starts in degree $2m-1$.
An unvaried Hessian perturbation starts in degree two. Hence orbit
variation does not affect the degree under consideration, and only
the linear term of the logarithmic determinant contributes there.
The diagonal half-line Green entry is
\[
 G^{(b)}_{ii}=\frac{1-\varrho^{2i}}{2a_{(b+i)\bmod2}}.
\]
It follows that
\begin{align}
 \partial_{q_{b,2m}}\beta_{b,2m-2}
 &=-\frac{E_m}{a_b(2m-2)!},\notag\\
 \partial_{q_{1-b,2m}}\beta_{b,2m-2}
 &=-\frac{\mathfrak r_b^{2m}O_m}{a_b(2m-2)!}.
                                      \label{eq:v12-amplitude-block}
\end{align}
For the first formula sum $(1-\varrho^{4k})\varrho^{4(m-1)k}$
over $k\ge1$. For the second sum
$(1-\varrho^{2(2k+1)})\varrho^{(2m-2)(2k+1)}$ over $k\ge0$
and use $a_{1-b}^{-1}=\mathfrak r_b^2a_b^{-1}$. The sums are respectively
$E_m$ and $O_m$. Passing from $\log B_b$ to $B_b$ does not change
the first varying coefficient: multiplication by a positive-degree
unvaried term would raise its degree.

\medskip\noindent\emph{Residual time and the invertible block.}
For the same-type law both endpoint quadratic actions have coefficient
$a_b$. Under the density proportional to
$(d-a_b(u^2+v^2)/2)_+$ one has
\begin{align}
 \int(d-a_b(u^2+v^2)/2)_+\,\dd u\dd v&=\frac{\pi d^2}{a_b},\notag\\
 \mathbb E(u^{2k})
 &=\frac{2\binom{2k}{k}}{2^k(k+1)(k+2)a_b^k}d^k.
                                      \label{eq:v12-moment}
\end{align}
These identities follow from the angular average
$\binom{2k}{k}/4^k$ and a one-dimensional radial integration.
First variation of the action has no moving-boundary term, because
the residual-time weight is zero at the boundary. Its contribution
from $s_{b,2m}$ to $f_{b,m-1}$ is $-K_{m,b}s_{b,2m}$.
The amplitude contribution from $\beta_{b,2m-2}$ has coefficient
\[
 \frac{4\binom{2m-2}{m-1}}
             {2^{m-1}m(m+1)a_b^{m-1}}.
\]
Combining these coefficients with
\eqref{eq:v12-action-block}--\eqref{eq:v12-amplitude-block} gives
\[
 \partial_{q_{b,2m}}f_{b,m-1}=-K_{m,b}P_m,
 \qquad
 \partial_{q_{1-b,2m}}f_{b,m-1}=-K_{m,b}\mathfrak r_b^{2m}Q_m.
\]
But $K_{m,b}\mathfrak r_b^{2m}=K_{m,1-b}$. This proves
\eqref{eq:v12-block}, including the unequal-curvature factors.
Lower nonlinear action and amplitude terms can multiply a first
variation only at higher endpoint degree. Thus the computation remains
valid at arbitrary fixed lower even jets. The last-jet derivatives
are constant in that jet, proving the asserted affinity.

Both $E_m$ and $O_m$ are positive. The identities
\[
 \coth(2x)+\csch(2x)=\coth x,\qquad
 \coth(2x)-\csch(2x)=\tanh x
\]
and the geometric sums give \eqref{eq:v12-plus}. For the difference,
\[
 E_m-O_m=\tfrac12\{\tanh((m-1)\gamma)-\tanh(m\gamma)\},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:v12-minus}. The function $\tanh x$ is
strictly concave for $x>0$ and has value zero at zero. Applying
strict concavity at $(m-1)\gamma=((m-1)/m)m\gamma$ proves the
strict inequality in \eqref{eq:v12-minus}. Thus
\[
 \det\frac{\partial f_{m-1}}{\partial q_m}
       =K_{m,0}K_{m,1}(P_m+Q_m)(P_m-Q_m)>0.
\]
There is no denominator involving $\kappa_0-\kappa_1$.

To write the inverse explicitly, suppose all lower graph jets are
already known and let $R_m$ denote their contribution to $f_{m-1}$.
For $z_m=R_m-f_{m-1}$, solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v12-recursion}
 q_m=\diag(K_{m,0}^{-1},K_{m,1}^{-1})
        \frac1{P_m^2-Q_m^2}
       \begin{pmatrix}P_m&-Q_m\\-Q_m&P_m\end{pmatrix}z_m.
\end{equation}
The recursion proves uniqueness at every finite order. Its coefficients
are analytic in the positive geometric parameters and in lower finite
jets: the operations are local length expansions, inversion of a
positive quadratic operator, and convergent geometric sums. On any
fixed compact set and finite collection of orders, the positive
denominators have positive minima. The recursion and its derivatives
therefore give the stated locally Lipschitz inverse.

For analytic graphs, equality of all recovered even jets implies equality
of the two convergent graph series; their odd jets vanish by assumption.
For smooth families, the differentiated relative theorem gives
$|f_{j,b,k}-f_{b,k}|\le C_M\tau^j$ through $k=M-1$.
Apply the finite-order Lipschitz inverse. For large $j$ its inputs stay
in the chosen compact neighborhood. The algebraic recursion is defined
for every finite coefficient vector at fixed positive geometric data;
the finitely many remaining even $j$ are covered by increasing the
constant. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Labelled analytic boundary determination]
\label{cor:v12-analytic-boundaries}
In the two-independent-even contact class, the gap, the two labelled
contact curvatures, and the two normalized even-law germs determine
both real-analytic contact graphs. If their participating obstacles
have connected real-analytic closed boundaries, each boundary is
determined in its contact frame. The conclusion allows equal contact
curvatures and does not require congruent obstacles or identical graphs.
It makes no determination of unobserved obstacles or of the lattice.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The theorem determines each analytic graph germ. To compare two
candidate closed boundaries at one contact, parametrize both by
oriented arclength from that contact and its fixed tangent. Their
curvatures are real-analytic functions on the real line, continued
around the closed curves, and agree on an interval. The identity
theorem makes them agree everywhere. Uniqueness for the planar Frenet
equations with the common initial frame makes the two boundary images
identical. Apply the argument at each participating contact. This is
exact analytic continuation; no stability of distant boundary points
from noisy local data is asserted.
\end{proof}

At equal curvatures, restricting \eqref{eq:v12-block} to the diagonal
$q_{0,2m}=q_{1,2m}$ recovers exactly
Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-jets}. The new direction is the antisymmetric
one, whose nonvanishing coefficient is \eqref{eq:v12-minus}.
A single identical-contact calculation does not see that coefficient.
Without the evenness assumption, odd graph jets also enter the
symmetrized energy datum. The theorem does not identify their fibres;
the general relative law and full energy-profile inverse remain valid
without that assumption.
