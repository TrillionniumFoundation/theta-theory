\subsection{An exact mixed-parameter benchmark}
\label{sec:v16-strict-margin}
The strict exponential margin in Lemma~\ref{lem:v15-scalar-product}
is part of its mixed-parameter assertion. The following exact family,
recorded in the independent memorandum~\cite{A2ReviewV15}, explains
why the multiplier itself cannot in general replace that margin.

\begin{proposition}[A strict-margin example]
\label{prop:v16-strict-margin}
On a common interval about zero put
\[
 R_{\lambda,a}(u)=\frac{\lambda u}{1+a(1-\lambda)u},\qquad
 z_a(u)=\frac{u}{1+au},\qquad B_a(u)=(1+au)^{-2}.
\]
Let $0<\lambda_-\le\lambda\le\lambda_+<1$ and let $a$ range over a
compact interval containing zero in its interior. For a sufficiently
small common endpoint interval these maps are smooth contractions and
\begin{align}
 R_{\lambda,a}^n(u)
 &=\frac{\lambda^n u}{1+a(1-\lambda^n)u},
                                      \label{eq:v16-rational-iterate}\\
 \lambda^{-n}(R_{\lambda,a}^n)'(u)
 &=[1+a(1-\lambda^n)u]^{-2},
                                      \label{eq:v16-rational-density}\\
 \lambda^{-n}R_{\lambda,a}^n(u)-z_a(u)
 &=\frac{a\lambda^nu^2}
       {[1+a(1-\lambda^n)u](1+au)}.
                                      \label{eq:v16-rational-error}
\end{align}
The normalized errors converge with every fixed mixed derivative at
rate $C_{k,\sigma}\sigma^n$ for each $\lambda_+<\sigma<1$.
There is no such bound with $C\lambda_+^n$ for the mixed derivative
$\partial_\lambda\partial_a$ of
\eqref{eq:v16-rational-error} on a nontrivial endpoint interval.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The denominators are uniformly positive after restricting the endpoint
interval. Also $R'_{\lambda,a}(u)=\lambda/[1+a(1-\lambda)u]^2$
is then positive and uniformly less than one. Direct substitution gives
$z_a\circ R_{\lambda,a}=\lambda z_a$ and
$z_a^{-1}(x)=x/(1-ax)$. Iteration and one endpoint differentiation prove
\eqref{eq:v16-rational-iterate}--\eqref{eq:v16-rational-density}.
Subtraction proves \eqref{eq:v16-rational-error}. At each fixed order,
differentiation gives a polynomial in $n$ times $\lambda_+^n$, multiplied
by uniformly bounded rational functions; the lower bound on $\lambda$
controls the finitely many inverse powers introduced. A strict margin
absorbs that polynomial. The density estimate follows either directly
or by one additional endpoint derivative.

At $a=0$ the mixed derivative of the error is exactly
\[
 \left.\partial_\lambda\partial_a
   (\lambda^{-n}R_{\lambda,a}^n(u)-z_a(u))\right|_{a=0}
                         =n\lambda^{n-1}u^2.
\]
At $\lambda=\lambda_+$ and any fixed $u\ne0$, its ratio to
$\lambda_+^n$ is $nu^2/\lambda_+$, which is unbounded. Finally the
exact identity
\[
 \lambda^{-n}(R_{\lambda,a}^n)'(u)
                  B_a(R_{\lambda,a}^n(u))=B_a(u)
\]
checks the scalar product remainder with no asymptotic expansion.
\end{proof}

This is a benchmark for mixed estimates, not a billiard realization
or an additional linearization theorem. The physical theorem retains
its already strict exponential margin and its independent relative
finite-bridge proof.
