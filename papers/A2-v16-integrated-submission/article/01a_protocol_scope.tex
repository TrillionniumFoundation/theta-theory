\subsection{From relative limits to observation protocols}
The uniformity of the forward theorem also has an operational consequence.
For a fixed list of observations, the relative comparison controls the
entire physical experiment, with the failure atom retained
(Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-transfer}). For adaptive designs and bounded
stopping rules, Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-transfer} gives instead
\[
 \|\mathsf P_\xi^\pi-\mathsf Q_\xi^\pi\|_{\rm TV}
 \le C\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_\xi^\pi}
                 \sum_{i=1}^{T}Y_i\tau^{j(a_i)}.
\]
The observations in this statement are common physical endpoint and
residual-time records, or any common coarsening. The policy selects physical
windows without observing an unknown gap. Pilot observations can be kept
exact, with their full cost charged and their exceptional probability
included. The probabilistic coupling argument is elementary; its physical
input is the uniform relative law, not scalar linearization alone.
The exact common-support calculation for the known binary tangent pair
also extends to stopped adaptive policies, through the overlap functional
of Theorem~\ref{thm:v16-adaptive-critical}. Both conclusions are additional
consequences of the relative theorem and leave the distinct finite-jet
and function-valued inverse statements unchanged.
