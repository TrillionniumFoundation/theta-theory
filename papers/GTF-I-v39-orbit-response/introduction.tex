\section{Introduction}
A low-dimensional linear statistic may have a small positive realization
at every selected time and still require a large hidden register to
follow a whole command sequence. The issue is compatibility: one
stochastic update must serve all pasts that share a label. We study
this constraint for numerical matrix coefficients of compact-group
representations. The input alphabet is finite and fixed; the time
horizon grows. Each numerical response is required on every input
word, with one selected coordinate query at the end.

Our main result identifies a representation invariant rather than an
analyst-chosen family of geometric witnesses. Let a compact connected
Lie group act orthogonally on $V$, and decompose $V$ as its invariant
subspace and real irreducible constituents $V_i$. Define
\[
 s_i=\min_{v\in\mathbb S(V_i)}\dim(Gv),\qquad
 \sigma(U)=\max_i s_i,
\]
with $\sigma(U)=0$ when there is no nontrivial constituent. Equivalent
constituents have the same $s_i$, so this definition is independent of
the orthogonal decomposition. It is the smallest orbit within each
constituent, followed by a maximum across constituents, not the
dimension of a generic orbit or of one chosen seed orbit.

\begin{theorem}[Minimum orbits and numerical width]\label{thm:main}
Let $G$ be a compact connected Lie group, $U:G\to O(\R^D)$ a fixed
representation, and $\mathcal A\subset G$ a fixed finite alphabet
supporting a probability law with full mean-zero $L^2(G)$ norm
strictly below one. Let the finite unit seeds span $V$, and let
$0<\rho\le\min\{1/10,1/(8D)\}$. For every fixed
$0\le\varepsilon<\rho a_*/(2\sqrt D)$, with the positive activation
constant $a_*$ in \eqref{eq:seed-activation} when a nontrivial
constituent is present,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:headline39}
 W_{N,\varepsilon}=\Theta_{G,U,\mathcal A,p,\mathcal X,\rho,\varepsilon}
                              (N^{\sigma(U)/2}).
\end{equation}
Here width means the maximum number of available labels in a
nonuniform clocked atomic-row machine of Definition~\ref{def:machine}.
A new machine may be chosen for every $N$. The lower bound allows
arbitrary hidden states and cut-dependent rows. The upper bound is
exact and uses common command rows with an $N$-dependent decoder.
For a purely invariant representation an exact constant-width machine
exists. In a nontrivial irreducible representation, any nonempty
unit seed set suffices; if its seeds lie on a minimum-dimensional
orbit, the amplitude range extends to $0<\rho\le1/10$.
\end{theorem}

For conjugation on complex traceless Hermitian matrices,
$s=2(q-1)$ and the theorem recovers $N^{q-1}$ labels while preserving
the earlier projective seed and signal conventions. It also gives
$N^{(q-1)/2}$ and $N^{2(q-1)}$ for the real and quaternionic
self-adjoint families under the corresponding full gap. On every
fixed nontrivial spherical-harmonic representation of $\SO(3)$ the
least orbit dimension is two, giving linear label growth regardless
of the harmonic degree. The representation dimension is held fixed
in each asymptotic statement. For a reducible action the exponent
can remain unchanged after adding a large invariant component or
many copies of an easier constituent.

\subsection{The proof mechanism}
The geometric input is now derived, rather than assumed separately
for each application. A uniform rank bound for the differential of
$(g,u)\mapsto U_gu$, followed by a finite chart covering, gives
\[
 \sup_{u,v}m_G\{g:\norm{U_gu-v}\le r\}\le A r^{s(U)}.
\]
This holds across all stabilizer types, including directions of mixed
hidden conditional means. The orbital entropy occupation theorem
then turns it into a cumulative bound on small-register epochs.
That entropy theorem, proved in the preceding revision and reproduced
here, is the dynamic converse input.

For the other direction choose a least-dimensional orbit, not a net
on the ambient sphere. Its convex hull contains a fixed ball by
irreducible orthogonality. At a support maximum the tangential first
derivative vanishes, so an $h$-net loses only $O(h^2)$ in support.
Taking $h\asymp N^{-1/2}$ gives exact compatible stochastic updates
with $O(N^{s/2})$ labels. A randomized branch construction, with the
branch identity charged inside the label, proves the maximum rule
for reducible representations. One cannot replace this construction
by a free selector or by a claim about joint-output products.

\subsection{The numerical objective}
Recent one-way quantum-automata simulation theorems use
strict-cutpoint language equivalence \cite{CWapr,CWmay,ChenSep}.
Their positive embedding may attenuate a signed matrix coefficient
exponentially with word length. Proposition~\ref{prop:sign-simulator}
gives an explicit constant-label sign simulator for our own task,
whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:main} requires growing width to preserve
its numerical probabilities at fixed accuracy. This comparison does
not claim that exact mixed-state linearization or classical
quantization geometry is new.

The new conclusions are the uniform minimum-orbit concentration
principle, the sharp representation width law and its reducible
maximum rule. The entropy occupation argument and the quantitative
projective and six-gate results are retained with their proofs.
Classical orbit geometry, covering dimensions, stochastic positive
embedding and external spectral gaps are explicitly attributed.
For symmetric basis seeds, every separate internal cut still has
positive minimum $D+1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:separate-width39}); the
growing width is therefore a simultaneous compatibility cost.
No program description, arithmetic cost or independent quantum query
product is included in the label count.
