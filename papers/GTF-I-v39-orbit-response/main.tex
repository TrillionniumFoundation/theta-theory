\begin{abstract}
For a compact connected Lie group acting orthogonally on a fixed
finite-dimensional space, we identify the hidden-label width exponent
of its numerical command experiment under a full group norm gap.
In an irreducible representation, let $s$ be the least dimension of a
nonzero unit-vector orbit. We prove a uniform orbital small-ball bound
of exponent $s$ across every stabilizer type. Combined with an orbital
entropy occupation budget and a compatible minimal-orbit construction,
this gives sharp width $\Theta(N^{s/2})$ at fixed positive signal and
fixed sufficiently small wordwise error. For reducible representations
and spanning seeds at small fixed signal, the exponent is one half
the maximum of the least orbit dimensions of the nontrivial irreducible
constituents. The upper machine charges its randomized branch label
and uses common command rows; the lower bound permits arbitrary
hidden directions and time-dependent rows. We distinguish this
numerical law from strict-cutpoint simulation by an explicit
constant-width sign simulator with attenuated probabilities. The
projective and arithmetic qubit results retain their original scope.
The counted resource is nonuniform clocked atomic-row label width;
tables, arithmetic and horizon-specific design are not charged.
\end{abstract}
