\section{Matrix and harmonic representations}\label{sec:families}
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-orbits} identifies the correct geometric
exponent without a separate estimate for every spectral type. We
record its principal matrix consequences. The orbit dimensions below
are classical; their role is to determine the dynamic width exponent.

\begin{proposition}[Three conjugation families]\label{prop:matrix-orbits}
On the real space of traceless self-adjoint $q$ by $q$ matrices over
$\mathbb F=\R,\C,\mathbb H$, with inner product $\operatorname{Re}\tr(AB)$, let
$\beta=\dim_\R\mathbb F$. The compact groups $\SO(q),\SU(q),\operatorname{Sp}(q)$,
respectively, act irreducibly for $q\ge2$. The least nonzero unit-orbit
dimension is $\beta(q-1)$. Accordingly, under a full group gap, the
width order in Corollary~\ref{cor:irreducible-width} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-laws}
 N^{(q-1)/2},\qquad N^{q-1},\qquad N^{2(q-1)},
\end{equation}
respectively, in its fixed signal and fixed error ranges. The
$\SO(2)$ case supplies no finitely supported full-gap example.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A self-adjoint matrix with distinct eigenvalue multiplicities
$m_1,\ldots,m_r$ has orbit dimension
\[
 \beta\sum_{i<j}m_i m_j=\frac\beta2
                         \left(q^2-\sum_i m_i^2\right).
\]
For real matrices subtract the dimensions of the orthogonal
stabilizer blocks from $q(q-1)/2$; their disconnected components do
not affect dimension. For complex matrices subtract
$\sum m_i^2-1$ from $q^2-1$. For quaternionic matrices subtract
$\sum m_i(2m_i+1)$ from $q(2q+1)$. These computations also identify
the least orbits as real, complex and quaternionic projective spaces.
A nonzero traceless matrix has at least two eigenvalues. The largest
possible $\sum m_i^2$ under that restriction is $(q-1)^2+1$,
attained by multiplicities $(1,q-1)$. This proves the minimum.

For completeness, the conjugation representation is irreducible over
$\R$. Any nonzero invariant real subspace contains, after diagonalizing
a nonzero member, a nonconstant traceless real diagonal matrix.
Differences of its conjugates under coordinate interchanges give all
traceless diagonal directions. Rotations in a pair of coordinate
axes then give every self-adjoint off-diagonal direction; in the
complex and quaternionic cases, diagonal unit phases give each real
component of those entries. Signed interchanges and plane rotations
can be chosen in the stated special groups. Thus the subspace is the
entire traceless self-adjoint space. The general width law applies.
Finally a finite measure on a torus has Fourier multipliers arbitrarily
close to one by simultaneous approximation, so it has no full norm gap
on its mean-zero $L^2$ space.
\end{proof}

For complex matrices the projective seeds in
\eqref{eq:pure-orbit} belong to a least-dimensional orbit. The signal
need not be reduced to $1/(8(q^2-1))$ in this specialization.
\begin{corollary}[The preserved projective width law]\label{cor:projective39}
Let $q\ge2$, $0<\rho\le1/10$, and
$0\le\varepsilon<\rho/(2\sqrt{q^2-1})$ be fixed. For the finite
projective seeds and coordinate queries of Section~\ref{sec:projective},
any finite command alphabet supporting a full group norm gap has
\[
                       W_{N,\varepsilon}=\Theta(N^{q-1}).
\]
It has the fixed $q$-dimensional quantum realization of
Proposition~\ref{prop:quantum}. The explicit quantitative all-spectra
estimate and the rational-projector net from the preceding revision
are retained in Appendices~\ref{sec:conjugation} and \ref{sec:projective}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Every fixed spherical harmonic degree]\label{prop:harmonic-family}
Let $V_\ell$ be the real irreducible $\SO(3)$ representation of
harmonic homogeneous polynomials of degree $\ell\ge1$, with
$\dim V_\ell=2\ell+1$. Then $s(V_\ell)=2$. Consequently every fixed
$\ell$ has width $\Theta(N)$ under a full-gap command law, with the
signal and query conventions of Corollary~\ref{cor:irreducible-width}.
The same six-command arithmetic alphabet in Appendix~\ref{sec:effective}
supplies such a gap for all these representations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A nonzero vector has no three-dimensional stabilizer, since the
representation is nontrivial irreducible. The Lie algebra
$\mathfrak{so}(3)$ has no two-dimensional subalgebra: identifying its
bracket with a cross product, two independent elements bracket to a
third independent direction. The stabilizer dimension is therefore
at most one, so every orbit has dimension at least two. A zonal
spherical harmonic is fixed by the circle of rotations about its
axis and has a two-dimensional orbit. This gives the minimum.
The final assertion follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:lps-transfer39}:
the spherical norm bound controls all nontrivial $\SO(3)$ irreducibles,
not just the defining three-dimensional action.
\end{proof}
The degree is fixed before $N\to\infty$; no uniform-in-$\ell$ constants
are claimed. Generic harmonic orbits can be three-dimensional, so their
dimension would give the wrong exponent. In the real degree-two
representation this is already the distinction between generic
three-dimensional matrix orbits and the two-dimensional projective
orbit. These examples use the same general minimum-orbit theorem,
not separate orbital cap computations.
