\section{Round-twenty-one positive closure: normalized connected trajectories, precontact coarea, and positive dynamic recovery}
\label{sec:r21-b2}

This section replaces the former Round-Seventeen source.  The hard-sphere
scaling is fixed throughout: particles have diameter $\varepsilon$ in the
three-dimensional unit torus, the grand-canonical intensity is
$\mu_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-2}$, and time is restricted to a fixed finite
interval $[0,T]$.  The connected expansion below includes its symmetry factor
$1/k!$ in the definition.  No factorial is discarded after an estimate.

\subsection{Closed transport traces on hard-sphere configuration space}

For $N\ge1$ let
\[
 \mathcal D_{\varepsilon,N}=
 \{(x_1,\ldots,x_N):|x_i-x_j|>\varepsilon\}
\]
and let $\Sigma_{ij}$ be the regular binary-contact face.  Its incoming flux
measure is
\[
 d\lambda_{ij}^{-}=
 |(v_i-v_j)\cdot\omega|_-
 \,dtd\sigma_{ij}(x)dv,
 \qquad \omega={x_i-x_j\over\varepsilon}.
\]
The specular map is denoted $R_{ij}$.  A finite-energy transport current is a
bulk measure $f$ together with incoming and outgoing normal traces whose total
mass and second velocity moment are finite.

\begin{lemma}[Quantitative exclusion of singular-boundary concentration]
\label{lem:r21-b2-singular}
For every entropy and exponential-velocity-moment sublevel there is $C$,
uniform in $N$ under the grand-canonical normalization, such that
\[
 \lambda^-\{|(v_i-v_j)\cdot\omega|<\delta\}
 \le C\delta^2,
\]
\[
 \lambda^-\{\operatorname{dist}(x,\Sigma_{ij}\cap\Sigma_{k\ell})<\delta
 \varepsilon\}\le C\delta,
\]
and the same bounds hold for outgoing traces.  Consequently no weak limit of
normalized trace measures charges grazing or multiple-contact strata.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On a binary face use relative polar coordinates
$v_i-v_j=a\omega+b$ with $b\perp\omega$.  The flux density contains $|a|$;
integration over $|a|<\delta$ is therefore $O(\delta^2)$, while the
exponential velocity moment controls $b$.  Near an independent second contact,
coarea in its signed distance gives a factor $\delta\varepsilon$.  The
Boltzmann--Grad activity supplies the inverse collision cross-section
$\varepsilon^{-2}$ only once, for the face already being traced; the second
distance remains a genuine small factor.  Contacts sharing one particle are
handled in the two independent relative directions.  The exceptional
collinear set has one additional angular codimension and is included in the
grazing estimate.  Summing labels is compensated by the factorial intensity
normalization and the entropy moment bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed Green--specular graph]
\label{thm:r21-b2-trace}
Let $(f_n,\gamma_n^-,\gamma_n^+)$ be transport currents satisfying the weak
Green identity and
$\gamma_n^+=(R_{ij})_\#\gamma_n^-$ on every binary face.  If they converge in
the weighted weak topology on a common entropy/moment sublevel, then the limit
has well-defined normal traces, satisfies the same Green identity, and obeys
the specular relation.  Hence the free-transport generator with hard-sphere
boundary condition has a closed graph in this topology.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Remove a $\delta$-neighborhood of grazing and multiple-contact strata.  The
remaining boundary is a finite union of uniformly Lipschitz binary faces on
every fixed particle sector.  The divergence-measure trace theorem and the
weak Green identity pass to the limit there.  The specular map and normal
field are continuous on that regular part, so the push-forward relation also
passes.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-b2-singular} makes the omitted trace mass tend to
zero uniformly as $\delta\downarrow0$.  Grand-canonical summation is justified
by the exponential particle-number moment.  This proves the full statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Compact precontact stratification}

Fix a creation genealogy $G$ with $k$ vertices, a velocity cutoff $L$, and a
chronology separation $\delta>0$.  Its compact parameter set
$\mathcal P_{G,L,\delta}$ consists of root data, creation times, impact
directions, and free-flight coordinates before the first surplus contact.
All reflections before that stopping time are the actual specular
reflections.  For a proposed surplus pair $(i,j)$ define the precontact map
\[
 \mathcal F_G(\xi,t,\omega)=x_i(t^-;\xi)-x_j(t^-;\xi)-\varepsilon\omega
 \in\mathbb R^3.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite analytic rank atlas]
\label{thm:r21-b2-atlas}
The compact semianalytic set
$\mathcal F_G^{-1}(0)\cap\mathcal P_{G,L,\delta}$ has a finite stratification
such that on every top-dimensional stratum one of finitely many $3\times3$
minors of $D\mathcal F_G$ is bounded below.  The lower-dimensional strata are
contained in a finite union of grazing, chronology-collision, collinearity,
and multiple-contact sets.  For some $C_G,\alpha_G>0$,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}\{|M_G|<s\}\le C_Gs^{\alpha_G}
\]
for the selected minor on each compact chart.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Between prescribed contacts the trajectory is affine in root positions and
velocities and real analytic in collision times and impact directions.  The
specular map is analytic off grazing.  Hence $\mathcal F_G$ is analytic on the
compact set.  Apply analytic rank stratification to the ideal generated by
its $3\times3$ minors.  A compact semianalytic set has finitely many connected
strata.  On each rank-three stratum, some minor is nonzero; compact
subcharts on which it has a positive minimum give a finite cover.

If all minors vanish, the three relative-position constraints fail to have
independent variations.  Varying a root translation removes the common
translation mode; varying relative velocity and one creation direction then
shows that rank loss forces either zero normal relative speed, coincidence of
two chronology parameters, collinearity of the fork, or a simultaneous second
contact.  These are precisely the listed singular sets.  The analytic
sublevel estimate on each compact chart is the elementary one-variable
factorization iterated after Weierstrass preparation; taking the minimum of
the finitely many exponents gives $\alpha_G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Surplus-contact gain with the reflected future]
\label{lem:r21-b2-gain}
For every fixed $G,L,\delta$, imposing one surplus contact before applying its
reflection gains a factor $C_G\varepsilon^{\alpha_G}$ relative to the creation
tree weight.  After the zero set is imposed, the exact reflection and all
later genuine contacts may be retained without losing the gain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On a rank chart, coarea for $\mathcal F_G$ replaces the three precontact
variables by the contact point and two angular variables.  The normal flux
contributes the hard-sphere cross section $\varepsilon^2$, exactly matching
the Boltzmann--Grad creation normalization; the analytic sublevel complement
contributes the additional positive power of $\varepsilon$.  On singular
charts use Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-b2-atlas} and optimize the minor sublevel
threshold against the coarea bound.  This gives a positive, genealogy-dependent
exponent.

The binary reflection is a smooth, measure-preserving involution on the
regular contact face.  Its Jacobian is one in flux coordinates.  Subsequent
contacts are generated from the reflected state and are covered by the same
regular/singular decomposition.  Thus retaining the true future changes only
$C_G$, not the positive power.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The correctly normalized connected series}

Let $W_G^\varepsilon(H)$ be the labelled absolute trajectory weight of a
connected genealogy with $k$ roots and source $H$.  Define its logarithmic
coefficient by
\[
 \mathcal C_{k,\varepsilon}(H)=
 {1\over k!}\sum_{|G|=k}W_G^\varepsilon(H).
\]
The $1/k!$ is the exponential-generating-function symmetry factor and is part
of the coefficient before any estimate.

\begin{theorem}[Normalized tree majorant]
\label{thm:r21-b2-majorant}
There are $T_0,C>0$ and a source ball $\mathcal U$ such that, for
$T\le T_0$,
\[
 |\mathcal C_{k,\varepsilon}(H)|
 \le C(C T)^{k-1},
 \qquad H\in\mathcal U,
\]
uniformly in $\varepsilon$.  The same estimate holds after any fixed number
of source derivatives.  Genealogies containing a surplus contact contribute
$o(1)$ after summing all $k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Order a connected genealogy by the last creation edge.  Removing that edge
splits it into two rooted subgenealogies.  The time integral of the last edge
is at most $T$, and the collision kernel has a uniform exponential-velocity
moment.  The number of labelled ways to realize a given unlabelled rooted
forest is at most $k!C_0^k$; division by the displayed $k!$ leaves $C_0^k$.
The resulting recursion
\[
 a_k\le CT\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}a_i a_{k-i},
 \qquad a_1\le C,
\]
is dominated by the Catalan generating function and gives
$a_k\le C(CT)^{k-1}$ after reducing $T_0$.

For each fixed $k$, Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-b2-gain} makes every first-surplus term
$o(1)$.  The geometric majorant is uniform in $\varepsilon$ and summable in
$k$, so dominated convergence permits summation before the limit.  Source
derivatives only mark finitely many vertices and multiply the Catalan bound by
a polynomial in $k$, still summable for smaller $T_0$.
\end{proof}

For arbitrary fixed $T$, divide $[0,T]$ into $m$ intervals of length at most
$T_0$.  At the slice boundary retain the complete empirical density and
incoming contact trace.

\begin{corollary}[Finite-time concatenation]
\label{cor:r21-b2-slicing}
The normalized logarithmic series and all fixed source derivatives converge
locally uniformly on every finite time interval.  Its value is independent of
the slicing.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On each slice Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-b2-majorant} gives a normal analytic series.
Integrating over the shared boundary state composes the corresponding positive
kernels.  Associativity of kernel composition proves independence of the
partition.  The logarithm selects exactly connected components crossing the
slice interface, so no cluster is counted twice.  Refining the partition and
using normal convergence proves the result.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Grand-canonical pressure}

Let $Z_\varepsilon(H,\Psi)$ be the exponential moment of the empirical density
and actual-contact measure.  The source $H$ acts on density paths and $\Psi$
on incoming binary contacts.

\begin{theorem}[Analytic marked pressure]
\label{thm:r21-b2-pressure}
The limit
\[
 Q_T(H,\Psi)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
 {1\over\mu_\varepsilon}\log Z_\varepsilon(H,\Psi)
\]
exists locally uniformly with all fixed source derivatives.  It is the unique
classical solution, on each short slice and hence by concatenation on
$[0,T]$, of the Hamilton--Boltzmann equation
\[
 \partial_t f+v\cdot\nabla_xf
 =\int B(v-v_*,\omega)ff_*
 \left(e^{\Psi+\Delta p}-1\right)
 \,dv_*d\omega
\]
together with the dual Hamilton equation for $p$ and the prescribed endpoint
sources.  Only actual microscopic contacts are marked.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand the logarithm of the grand-canonical partition function into its
normalized connected coefficients.  Corollary~\ref{cor:r21-b2-slicing}
gives normal convergence and permits source differentiation.  By Theorem
\ref{thm:r21-b2-majorant}, all surplus-contact graphs vanish in the limit, so
the last creation edge separates two independent rooted subtrees.  Summing
that exact rooted recursion gives the displayed collision Hamiltonian.
The free-flight part follows from the transport segments and the Green
identity of Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-b2-trace}.

The collision Hamiltonian is locally Lipschitz and analytic on the weighted
source ball.  Picard iteration is a contraction on a short slice, and the
positive exponential moment prevents velocity escape.  Uniqueness on each
slice plus kernel concatenation gives uniqueness on $[0,T]$.  Since the
finite-volume mark is the actual contact functional before expansion, no
Poisson contact process is inserted into the microscopic model.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Positive balanced density of smooth controls}

For a nonnegative density path $f$, put
\[
 dA_f={1\over2}ff_*B\,dtdxdvdv_*d\omega.
\]
An admissible collision current has $\Gamma=qA_f$, $q\ge0$, and satisfies the
weak balance equation
\[
 \partial_tf+v\cdot\nabla_xf=\mathcal Q_\Gamma.
\]
Its action is
\[
 I(f,\Gamma)=I_0(f_0)+\int\ell(q)\,dA_f,
 \qquad \ell(q)=q\log q-q+1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Positive exactly balanced recovery]
\label{thm:r21-b2-recovery}
Every finite-action admissible pair is the action limit of smooth pairs
$(f_n,q_nA_{f_n})$ such that, on a growing compact velocity set,
$f_n$ and $q_n$ are bounded above and below by positive constants.  Mass,
momentum, energy, endpoint constraints, and the balance equation hold exactly
for every $n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First truncate velocities and replace the removed tail by a Maxwellian chosen
with the same removed mass, momentum, and energy.  The three parameters of a
shifted, rescaled Maxwellian solve these moment equations; strict convexity of
its log partition function gives uniqueness.  The entropy and collision cost
of the tail tend to zero by uniform integrability.

Approximate $q$ in $L^1(A_f)$ by smooth bounded functions and set
$q_n=(q\wedge n)+n^{-1}$.  Starting from a smooth positive approximation of
$f_0$ with the exact conserved moments, solve the controlled Boltzmann equation
\[
 \partial_tf_n+v\cdot\nabla_xf_n=\mathcal Q_{q_nA_{f_n}}.
\]
The mild equation preserves positivity and the collision symmetries preserve
mass, momentum, and energy exactly.  Stability of the mild solution in the
weighted $L^1$ norm gives $f_n\to f$ after a diagonal choice of the source
approximation.  Since $A_{f_n}\to A_f$ strongly on compact velocity sets and
$q_n\to q$ in the corresponding Orlicz norm, the perspective entropy
converges by truncation and Vitali's theorem.  A final finite-dimensional
endpoint correction is made by a smooth collision-invariant tilt of $f_{n,0}$;
its size tends to zero and the forward controlled equation keeps exact
balance.  This construction never linearly smooths $(A_f,\Gamma)$ and hence
never assumes that $f\mapsto A_f$ is linear.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact microscopic tilts and rate identification}

For a bounded finite-volume source $(H,\Psi)$ define the exact change of
measure
\[
 {d\mathbb P_\varepsilon^{H,\Psi}\over d\mathbb P_\varepsilon}
 =\exp\left\{\mu_\varepsilon
 \langle(H,\Psi),(f_\varepsilon,\Gamma_\varepsilon)\rangle
 -\log Z_\varepsilon(H,\Psi)\right\}.
\]
This is an exponential tilt of the random initial configuration; no Poisson
compensator formula is claimed at finite $\varepsilon$.

\begin{lemma}[Legendre identification]
\label{lem:r21-b2-legendre}
The convex dual of the limiting collision Hamiltonian is
\[
 \sup_\Psi\left\{\int\Psi\,d\Gamma-
 \int(e^\Psi-1)\,dA_f\right\}
 =\int\ell(d\Gamma/dA_f)\,dA_f,
\]
with value $+\infty$ when $\Gamma\not\ll A_f$ or is not positive.  The
transport dual enforces the exact weak balance equation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Disintegrate with respect to $A_f$.  Pointwise maximization of
$q\Psi-e^\Psi+1$ gives $\Psi=\log q$ and value $\ell(q)$.  A singular positive
part is detected by sources increasing on an $A_f$-null set, and a negative
part by nonnegative-source separation.  Integration by parts in the transport
source gives the balance equation; failure of balance is separated by scaling
that source.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Grand-canonical dynamic LDP]
\label{thm:r21-b2-gc}
The empirical density and actual-contact current satisfy a good LDP at speed
$\mu_\varepsilon$ in the weighted weak path/contact topology, with rate
$I(f,\Gamma)$ above.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-b2-pressure} gives all finite-dimensional logarithmic
moments.  Exponential velocity moments give compact containment; the Green
identity bounds the modulus of every smooth density test by the contact mass,
and exponential source estimates make the contact measures exponentially
tight in time and velocity.  Hence the projective upper bound upgrades to the
full topology.

For a smooth strictly positive pair from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-b2-recovery}, choose sources dual to its controlled
Hamilton equations.  Differentiability of $Q_T$ makes the exact microscopic
tilt concentrate at that pair; the likelihood identity then gives the lower
bound.  Approximate an arbitrary finite-action pair by the positive recovery
sequence.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r21-b2-legendre} identifies the projective dual with
$I$, and its entropy sublevels are compact by the moment and balance estimates.
This proves goodness and the full lower bound.
\end{proof}

After the B1 exact-number coefficient theorem has been proved from the
source-uniform pressure above, condition on the declared particle number and
conserved shell.

\begin{theorem}[Microcanonical dynamic LDP]
\label{thm:r21-b2-mc}
In the B1 admissible exact-number and microcanonical windows, the conditioned
hard-sphere empirical density and actual-contact current satisfy a good LDP.
Its rate is the grand-canonical action restricted to the exact conservation
constraint, minus its constrained minimum.  The lower bound is realized by
positive exactly balanced controls.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
B1 supplies the sharp source-dependent denominator and the same coefficient
asymptotic with a bounded path/contact insertion.  Their exponential and local
prefactors cancel in the ratio.  The grand-canonical upper bound therefore
restricts to the closed constraint.  For the lower bound, apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:r21-b2-recovery} with the exact moments, use the exposing
source tilt, and then use the inserted B1 coefficient formula.  Goodness is
inherited from the grand-canonical action sublevels intersected with the
closed microcanonical set.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-twenty-one B2 closure]
\label{thm:r21-b2-main}
The hard-sphere root paper has a closed trace graph, a finite precontact rank
atlas, a correctly normalized and summable connected expansion, an analytic
actual-contact pressure, a positive exactly balanced recovery theorem, an
exact finite-volume tilt argument, and grand-canonical and microcanonical
joint dynamic LDPs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r21-b2-trace},
\ref{thm:r21-b2-atlas}, \ref{thm:r21-b2-majorant},
\ref{thm:r21-b2-pressure}, \ref{thm:r21-b2-recovery},
\ref{thm:r21-b2-gc}, and \ref{thm:r21-b2-mc}.
\end{proof}
