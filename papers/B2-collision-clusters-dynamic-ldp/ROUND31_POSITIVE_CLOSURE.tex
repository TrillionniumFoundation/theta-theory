\section{Round-thirty-one reconstruction: chronological tangent pivots and Ovsyannikov history evolution}
\label{sec:r31-b2}

The collision-history algebra is retained, but its evolution now spends
analytic radius linearly in time.  This is the correct consequence of a
Cauchy creation estimate.  The surplus-contact pivots are constructed on every
regular finite genealogy by solving the tree-contact equations in
chronological coordinates; their rank is no longer inserted as a certificate.
The regular and singular coarea estimates are then summed under one factorial
graph majorant.

\subsection{Disintegrated histories and analytic radii}

For each ordered list $o$ of open half-edges let $(S_o,\lambda_o)$ be the
standard cut-state space with Maxwellian--Lebesgue reference measure.  A
labelled decorated history $G$ has a kernel density
\[
 k_G(x_-,z,x_+)\quad\text{relative to}\quad
 \lambda_{o_-}(\dd x_-)m_G(\dd z)\lambda_{o_+}(\dd x_+).                 \tag{B2.1}
\]
For $a>0$ put
\[
 \norm K_a=
 \sum_G{a^{|V(G)|}\over |V(G)|!}
 \sup_{x_-}\int e^{\alpha E(z,x_+)}
 |k_G(x_-,z,x_+)|m_G(\dd z)\lambda_{o_+}(\dd x_+),         \tag{B2.2}
\]
with the analogous backward norm.  Fully labelled histories have trivial
automorphism; $1/|V|!$ is the exponential-generating coefficient.  Unlabelled
histories use a separate $1/|\operatorname{Aut}G|$ convention.

Matching cut kernels are composed by
\[
 (k^-\star k^+)(x_-,z^-,z^+,x_+)
 =\int_{S_o}k^-(x_-,z^-,y)k^+(y,z^+,x_+)\lambda_o(\dd y).                \tag{B2.3}
\]
Tonelli and the labelled binomial identity give, for $a'<a$,
\[
 \norm{K^-\star K^+}_{a'}
 \le C(a,a')\norm{K^-}_a\norm{K^+}_a.                   \tag{B2.4}
\]

\subsection{Construction of the tree-contact tangent bundle}

Fix a labelled genealogy with at most $K$ vertices and a chronological
spanning tree $T$.  Its ambient variables are root positions and velocities,
ordered contact times $t_e$, and impact directions $\omega_e$.  For a tree
edge $e=(i,j)$ set
\[
 F_e=x_i(t_e^-)-x_j(t_e^-)-\varepsilon\omega_e\in\R^3.   \tag{B2.5}
\]
On the regular set
\[
 |(v_i-v_j)(t_e^-)\cdot\omega_e|\ge\eta,\quad
 |t_e-t_{e'}|\ge\eta,\quad |v_i|\le L,                    \tag{B2.6}
\]
order variables by contact time.  Differentiating $F_T=(F_e)_{e\in T}$ with
respect to $(t_e,\omega_e)$ gives a block lower-triangular matrix.  Its
diagonal block is
\[
 \bigl((v_i-v_j)\cdot\omega_e,\,-\varepsilon
 D_{\theta_1}\omega_e,\,-\varepsilon D_{\theta_2}\omega_e\bigr),        \tag{B2.7}
\]
whose determinant has magnitude at least $c\varepsilon^2\eta$.

\begin{proposition}[Constructed causal right inverse]
\label{prop:r31-b2-tree-right-inverse}
Every regular tree history satisfying (B2.6) has a finite chronological chart
on which $DF_T$ has an explicitly constructed right inverse $R_T$ with
\[
 \norm{R_T}\le C_{K,L}\varepsilon^{-2(K-1)}\eta^{-K}.     \tag{B2.8}
\]
If a constraint is created at time $t_e$, the correction $R_T$ can be chosen
to change only $t_{e'}$, $\omega_{e'}$, and descendant root coordinates with
$t_{e'}\ge t_e$.  The finitely many charts cover the entire regular set.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Invert the diagonal blocks (B2.7) successively in chronological order.
Earlier collision maps depend only on earlier variables, so forward
substitution never changes an already solved row.  Elastic reflection is a
smooth involution on (B2.6); its derivative and inverse are bounded by a
polynomial in $\eta^{-1}$ and $L$.  Induction over the $K-1$ tree contacts
gives (B2.8) and the causal support.  A finite atlas of sphere coordinates and
contact-order types covers the compact cutoff set.  This constructs the right
inverse from the collision equations rather than assuming it as a field of
certificate data.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Surplus pivots on the constrained manifold}

Let $e\notin T$ be a surplus contact at time $t_e$.  Choose an ambient
relative-root variation $a_e$ supported on the later of the two ancestral
branches after its last tree contact.  Let
\[
 X_e=a_e-R_TDF_Ta_e.                                      \tag{B2.9}
\]
Then $DF_TX_e=0$, so $X_e$ is tangent to the exact tree-contact manifold.
Let
\[
 g_e=|x_{i_e}(t_e^-)-x_{j_e}(t_e^-)|-\varepsilon          \tag{B2.10}
\]
be the scalar closure equation.

\begin{lemma}[Chronological pivot induction]
\label{lem:r31-b2-pivot}
Order surplus edges increasingly by closing time.  Outside the explicit
singular set consisting of grazing, simultaneous contacts, collinear forks,
and vanishing relative velocity, one may choose the $a_e$ recursively so that
\[
 Dg_e(X_{e'})=0\quad(t_{e'}>t_e),\qquad
 |Dg_e(X_e)|\ge c_{K,L}\eta^{q_K}.                        \tag{B2.11}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \left|\det(Dg_e(X_{e'}))_{e,e'\in E\setminus T}\right|
 \ge c_{K,L}^{s(G)}\eta^{q_Ks(G)},\qquad
 s(G)=|E|-|V|+1.                                          \tag{B2.12}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the earliest surplus edge, vary the relative root position in its closing
normal direction.  Projecting with (B2.9) preserves every tree contact.  If
the resulting derivative vanished identically, the closing normal would lie
in the span of the tree-contact conormals.  Backward substitution through the
triangular tree Jacobian then forces either a zero normal relative speed, a
simultaneous contact, or a collinear fork.  These are exactly the declared
singular strata.  On the complement compactness gives the lower bound.

Assume pivots have been constructed through edge $e_j$.  For $e_{j+1}$ choose
an ambient variation supported after all earlier closing times and subtract
its components in the finite span of the earlier $Dg_e$ by Gram--Schmidt on
the tree tangent space.  Causality preserves the zero entries for later
pivots; failure of the new diagonal repeats the conormal argument above.
Since there are at most $K$ contacts, finitely many chart refinements complete
the induction.  The determinant is the product of the diagonal entries.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Regular and singular coarea}

A regular surplus binary contact uses time and direction variables:
\[
 x_i(t^-)-x_j(t^-)=\varepsilon\omega,\qquad
 (t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb S^2.                    \tag{B2.13}
\]
The flux coarea factor is
$\varepsilon^2/|(v_i-v_j)\cdot\omega|$.  A creation edge has a compensating
activity $\mu_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-2}$; a surplus edge creates no new
particle and retains its surface factor.

The singular neighbourhood of thickness $\delta$ has weight
\[
 C_{K,L}(\delta^{\kappa_K}+e^{-cL^2})                     \tag{B2.14}
\]
by relative-velocity anti-concentration, time coarea, and the Maxwellian tail.
On the complement, (B2.12) gives a coarea constant polynomial in
$\delta^{-1}$.

\begin{theorem}[All-graph multiplicative loop gain]
\label{thm:r31-b2-loop}
For every $K,T,L$ there are $C_K<\infty$ and $\alpha_K>0$ such that every
connected graph with at most $K$ vertices satisfies
\[
 |\cI_G^\varepsilon|
 \le C_K^{|E(G)|}\varepsilon^{\alpha_Ks(G)}
       |\cI_T^\varepsilon|.                              \tag{B2.15}
\]
The constants include all label sums, finite chart changes, and overlapping
surplus genealogies.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the simultaneous pivot coordinates from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r31-b2-pivot}.  Their flows remain on the tree-contact manifold,
and the chronological construction provides a common rectangular domain
after a finite partition.  Coarea imposes all surplus scalar closures at once;
then (B2.13) contributes one retained $\varepsilon^2$ surface factor per
surplus contact.  Chart transitions and label choices are bounded by
$C_K^{|E|}K!$, the latter cancelled by the exponential-generating coefficient.
On the singular part use (B2.14).  On its complement the loss is
$\delta^{-q_Ks(G)}$.  Choosing
$\delta=\varepsilon^{\theta_K}$ and then $L=|\log\varepsilon|^{1/2}$ gives a
positive exponent per surplus edge.  Because the determinant is simultaneous
and triangular, nested and overlapping loops acquire the product power.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Ovsyannikov evolution with linear radius consumption}

Let $\cF_t$ be free transport and $\cC_t$ the creation operator.  The exact
kernel estimates are
\[
 \norm{\cF_tK}_a\le\norm K_a,\qquad
 \norm{\cC_tK}_{a'}\le{C\over a-a'}\norm K_a.             \tag{B2.16}
\]
For a final time $T$, choose radii
\[
 a(t)=a_0-\Lambda t,\qquad a_*=a_0-\Lambda T>0.           \tag{B2.17}
\]
Define the time-dependent norm
\[
 \norm K_{\mathbb X_T}
 =\sup_{0\le t\le T}(a(t)-a_*)
   \norm{K_t}_{a(t)}.                                     \tag{B2.18}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Ovsyannikov collision-history evolution]
\label{thm:r31-b2-ovsyannikov}
If $\Lambda>4C$ and $K_0\in\mathfrak K_{a_0}$, the time-ordered Duhamel
equation
\[
 K_t=\cF_tK_0+
 \int_0^t\cF_{t-s}\cC_sK_s\dd s                         \tag{B2.19}
\]
has a unique solution with
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}\norm{K_t}_{a(t)}
 \le {C_T\over a(t)-a_*}\norm{K_0}_{a_0}.                \tag{B2.20}
\]
The $n$-creation term is bounded by
\[
 \norm{K_t^{(n)}}_{a(t)}
 \le C^n\norm{K_0}_{a_0}
 {t^n\over n!}
 \prod_{j=1}^n{1\over a(t_{j-1})-a(t_j)}                 \tag{B2.21}
\]
and the optimized simplex integral is summable precisely because radius is
spent at rate $\Lambda$.  No arbitrary-time bound at a fixed radius gap is
claimed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $0<s<t$, use the intermediate radius
$a_s(t)=a(t)+\frac12\Lambda(t-s)$.  Equation (B2.16) costs at most
$2C/[\Lambda(t-s)]$, while the factor in (B2.18) cancels the endpoint
singularity.  The Volterra map is a contraction for $\Lambda>4C$ in the
standard Ovsyannikov weighted norm.  Iteration gives (B2.21); ordering the
radius decrements proportionally to the time increments yields the convergent
majorant.  The analytic-translation counterexample is therefore respected:
existence to time $T$ requires the initial reserve $a_0-a_* =\Lambda T$.
For the Poisson/Maxwellian initial kernel, all finite radii are available, so
any fixed regular Boltzmann time can be covered by choosing $a_0$ accordingly.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Connected logarithm and finite-time tree limit}

The disintegrated kernel algebra is filtered by vertex number.  On a short
Ovsyannikov slice, the series
\[
 \log_\star K=
 \sum_{n\ge1}{(-1)^{n+1}\over n}(K-1)^{\star n}           \tag{B2.22}
\]
converges in the smaller radius; at each fixed degree it is finite and selects
connected labelled histories.  Initial cumulants satisfy
\[
 \sum_{k\ge2}{a_0^k\over k!}\norm{\kappa_k^\varepsilon}_{\rm root}
 \longrightarrow0.                                      \tag{B2.23}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite-time factorial tree propagation]
\label{thm:r31-b2-propagation}
For $T<T_*$ and $a_0>a_*+\Lambda T$,
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}\norm{K_t^\varepsilon-K_t^{\rm tree}}_{a_*}
 \longrightarrow0.                                      \tag{B2.24}
\]
The limit is independent of the slicing and is the unique tree-history
solution of (B2.19).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At fixed vertex cutoff, Theorem~\ref{thm:r31-b2-loop} removes every surplus
graph and (B2.23) removes nonchaotic root components.  The Ovsyannikov
majorant is summable uniformly in $\varepsilon$ and controls the vertex tail.
Kernel composition is associative, so adjacent slices agree on overlaps of
their radius intervals.  Uniqueness in the time-dependent analytic norm gives
partition independence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Pressure, recovery, and the joint rate}

The connected logarithm with finite projective sources defines
$\Lambda_T(H)$.  The last creation edge of a tree gives the
Hamilton--Boltzmann exponential.  Positive exact recovery is built on a
refining space-time-velocity mesh: each cell has a finite elastic collision
frame spanning its local conservation quotient, with right inverse $R_M$, and
the mesh/background are chosen so that
\[
 \norm{R_Mr_M}_\infty=o(\delta_M).                        \tag{B2.25}
\]
Thus balance defects are corrected locally without importing B3.

\begin{theorem}[Grand-canonical pressure and joint dynamic LDP]
\label{thm:r31-b2-ldp}
For $T<T_*$, the laws of
$(f^\varepsilon,\Gamma^\varepsilon,K^\varepsilon)$ satisfy a good projective
LDP at speed $\mu_\varepsilon$.  The rate is
\[
 I_{\rm GC}(f,\Gamma,K)=
 \begin{cases}
 H(f_0\mid f_0^{\rm ref})+
 \displaystyle\int\ell(q)\dd A_f,
 &\Gamma=qA_f,\quad K=\mathfrak L(f,\Gamma),\quad
   \text{exact balance},\\
 +\infty,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{B2.26}
\]
where $\mathfrak L$ is the unique causal tree lift.  B1 coefficient extraction
then gives exact-number and microcanonical contractions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:r31-b2-propagation} gives locally uniform pressure convergence
with fixed source derivatives.  Exponential particle, energy, contact, and
analytic-radius bounds give exponential tightness.  The local frames (B2.25)
produce smooth positive exactly balanced approximants with convergent
perspective entropy.  Their exact finite-volume exponential tilts have a
unique exposed minimizer; a finite family of projective separating sources
and exponential Chebyshev give concentration on the full complement.
The projective upper and lower bounds assemble by Dawson--Gartner.  The tree
history is retained through the equation $K=\mathfrak L(f,\Gamma)$ instead of
being omitted from the rate.
\end{proof}

The hard-sphere root now constructs its tangent pivots, pays analytic radius at
the correct linear rate, controls all loop charts under one factorial
majorant, and exports the finite-time pressure/LDP used by B1--B4.
