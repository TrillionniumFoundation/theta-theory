\section{Round-twenty-three reconstruction: loop-opening gains and collision-history hierarchy closure}
\label{sec:r23-b2}

This paper is the hard-sphere root of the Round-Twenty-Three dependency chain.
The former proof failed at two structural points: it did not obtain an extra
small factor for a surplus contact on regular charts, and it attempted to
concatenate short-time expansions while retaining only one-particle density
and contact trace.  We replace these steps by a transverse loop-opening
estimate and by an exact factorial hierarchy whose state retains complete
collision histories across every time slice.

\subsection{Microscopic model and the history-valued observable}

Let $N$ hard spheres of diameter $\varepsilon$ move on $\mathbb T^3$ by free
flight and elastic reflection.  Write $\Phi_t^{\varepsilon,N}$ for the
Liouville flow on
\[
 \mathcal D_{\varepsilon,N}
 =\{(x_i,v_i)_{i\le N}:|x_i-x_j|>\varepsilon\}.
\]
We use the Boltzmann--Grad scaling
$\mu_\varepsilon\varepsilon^2=1$ and begin with the grand-canonical law whose
$n$-particle Janossy density is a symmetric Maxwellian-weighted density
$f^{\varepsilon}_{n,0}/n!$.  The initial cumulants obey the factorial norm
bound displayed below and have a uniform $(2+\delta)$ velocity moment.

A collision mark is
\[
 c=(t,x,v,v_*,\omega),\qquad
 B(c)=[(v-v_*)\cdot\omega]_+,
\]
and the limiting reference collision measure generated by a positive density
$f$ is
\[
 A_f(dc)=f(t,x,v)f(t,x,v_*)B(c)
 \,dt\,dx\,dv\,dv_*\,d\omega.                            \tag{B2.1}
\]
The empirical state contains the density path, the actual incoming contact
current, and the connected collision-history hierarchy
\[
 \mathbf H^\varepsilon=(H_k^\varepsilon)_{k\ge1}.         \tag{B2.2}
\]
Here $H_k^\varepsilon$ is a signed measure on rooted, time-ordered collision
trees with $k$ labelled leaves; a point records every collision time, impact
direction, pre/post-collisional velocity, label identification, and cut edge.
Cutting a history at time $s$ leaves its open ancestral half-edges in the
boundary state.  Consequently the exact microscopic composition law is
\[
 \mathbf H^arepsilon_{[0,t]}
 =\mathbf H^arepsilon_{[0,s]}\star
  \mathbf H^arepsilon_{[s,t]},                          \tag{B2.3}
\]
where $\star$ glues matching open half-edges and sums over all retained
partitions.  Density and contact trace are projections of (B2.2), not the
whole interface state.

For $a>0$ define the factorial history norm
\[
 \|\mathbf H\|_a=
 \sum_{k\ge1}{a^k\over k!}
 \sum_{G\in\mathfrak G_k}
 \int e^{\alpha\sum_i|v_i|^{2+\delta}}\,d|H_{k,G}|.       \tag{B2.4}
\]
The factor $1/k!$ is part of the state norm and of every connected coefficient
before any graph estimate is taken.

\subsection{Closed contact trace and quantitative grazing control}

Let $\lambda^-$ denote the incoming Green trace on the hard-sphere boundary.
The trace topology pairs it with compactly supported $C^1$ functions of the
precontact variables and includes the flux weight $B$.  Green's identity for
the free transport field gives
\[
 \int_{\mathcal D_{\varepsilon,N}}
 (V\cdot\nabla_X\varphi)F
 +\varphi(V\cdot\nabla_XF)
 =\int_{\partial\mathcal D_{arepsilon,N}}
 \varphi F(V\cdot n)\,dS,                                \tag{B2.5}
\]
which fixes the incoming orientation and shows that the trace graph is closed
under transport convergence plus uniform flux mass.

Moment bounds alone do not exclude grazing concentration.  For $p>1$ define
the regular tilted class by
\[
 \Gamma=qA_f,\qquad
 \|q\|_{L^p(A_f)}\le L,\qquad
 \int (1+|v|^{2+\delta}+|v_*|^{2+\delta})\,d\Gamma\le L. \tag{B2.6}
\]
For the grazing set
$G_\eta=\{0<(v-v_*)\cdot\omega<\eta\}$, direct spherical integration gives
$A_f(G_\eta)\le C_L\eta^2$.  Hence
\[
 \Gamma(G_\eta)
 \le\|q\|_{L^p(A_f)}A_f(G_\eta)^{1-1/p}
 \le C_L\eta^{2(1-1/p)}.                                \tag{B2.7}
\]
A general finite-entropy control is approximated by
$q_{M,\eta}=(q\wedge M)1_{G_\eta^c}$ followed by a conservative correction;
data processing and monotone convergence recover its entropy.  Thus (B2.7)
is used only on a dense regular class and is not falsely deduced from moments.

\subsection{Precontact rank ideals and the loop-opening lemma}

Fix a connected ordered collision graph $G$.  Choose a spanning tree $T_G$.
Tree edges introduce new particle variables; an edge in $G\setminus T_G$
connects two trajectories already determined by the tree and is called a
surplus edge.  In precontact coordinates $y$ (initial positions and velocities,
ordered times, and impact directions), define the loop-closure map
\[
 \mathcal C_G(y)=
 \bigl(x_i(t_e^-)-x_j(t_e^-)-\varepsilon\omega_e
       \bigr)_{e\in G\setminus T_G}.                      \tag{B2.8}
\]
All reflections before $t_e$ are evaluated only after the preceding contact
constraints have been imposed.  The map is analytic on every regular
chronology chart.

Let $\mathfrak d_G(y)$ be the sum of squares of all maximal minors of
$D\mathcal C_G$ after quotienting translations and the tree variables.  We do
not assert that the zero set consists only of a short named list of geometric
configurations.  Instead, on every compact velocity/time cutoff, analytic
stratification and the Łojasiewicz inequality give
\[
 |\{\mathfrak d_G<\eta\}|
 \le C_G\eta^{\kappa_G}.                                 \tag{B2.9}
\]
This is the exhaustive singular estimate; grazing and simultaneous contacts
are separate boundary pieces controlled by (B2.7) and chronological
regularization.

\begin{lemma}[Transverse surplus-contact gain]
\label{lem:r23-b2-loop}
For every connected graph with cyclomatic number
$s(G)=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1\ge1$, there are $\alpha_G>0$ and $C_G(T,L)$ such that,
on the regular class (B2.6), its normalized precontact integral satisfies
\[
 |\mathcal I_G^{\varepsilon}|
 \le C_G(T,L)^{|E(G)|}\varepsilon^{\alpha_G s(G)}.        \tag{B2.10}
\]
The standard $\varepsilon^2$ cross-section of each collision has already been
cancelled by the Boltzmann--Grad activity; (B2.10) is an additional gain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After solving the spanning-tree contacts by coarea, every surplus edge requires
the loop map (B2.8) to enter a tube of radius $C\varepsilon$.  On
$\{\mathfrak d_G\ge\eta\}$, a maximal minor supplies at least one new
transverse coordinate for each independent loop.  Quantitative coarea gives
for that tube
\[
 C_G\varepsilon^{s(G)}\eta^{-m_G}.
\]
On $\{\mathfrak d_G<\eta\}$ use (B2.9), while boundary/grazing pieces are
bounded by (B2.7).  Thus one loop contributes at most
$C(\varepsilon\eta^{-m_G}+\eta^{\kappa_G}
+\eta^{2(1-1/p)})$.  Taking
$\eta=\varepsilon^{1/(m_G+\kappa'_G)}$, where
$\kappa'_G=\min\{\kappa_G,2(1-1/p)\}$, gives a positive power
$\alpha_G$.  Open the loops successively in an order compatible with the
chronology; the determinant ideal is recomputed after each tree reduction,
so the gains multiply.  This is the missing factor on regular charts; the
small-minor estimate is used only for their complement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Normalized connected expansion}

Let $C_k^\varepsilon(H)$ be the connected coefficient with $k$ collision
vertices for a bounded analytic path/contact source $H$.  It is defined by
\[
 C_k^\varepsilon(H)={1\over k!}
 \sum_{G\in\mathfrak G_k^{\rm conn}}
 \mathcal I_G^\varepsilon(H).                            \tag{B2.11}
\]
For trees, Cayley's bound and the time simplex give
\[
 {1\over k!}\sum_{G\text{ tree}}|\mathcal I_G^arepsilon|
 \le {k^{k-2}\over k!}(CT)^k
 \le Ck^{-5/2}(CeT)^k.                                  \tag{B2.12}
\]
Surplus graphs have the additional factor from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r23-b2-loop}.  Hence the expansion is absolutely convergent on
a source ball for a short slice.  The coefficient $1/k!$ has not been dropped
between definition and estimate.

\subsection{Finite-time propagation without reduced-state concatenation}

Let $f$ be the regular Boltzmann solution on $[0,T_*]$.  The limiting connected
history hierarchy is the fixed point of the tree Duhamel map in (B2.4).  On a
slice of length $\Delta$, (B2.12) gives
\[
 \|\mathcal T_\Delta(\mathbf H)-
       \mathcal T_\Delta(\widetilde{\mathbf H})\|_{a'}
 \le C\Delta\|\mathbf H-\widetilde{\mathbf H}\|_a,
 \qquad a'<a.                                            \tag{B2.13}
\]
At the interface we retain every open ancestral half-edge in (B2.2), so
composition is the exact operation (B2.3).  A cutting map separates histories
whose backward cluster exceeds $K$ collisions.  The factorial norm bounds the
cut tail by $C(a'/a)^K$, while loop-opening controls recollisions created by
the cut.  Choose $K$ after the number of slices but before
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$.  Iterating (B2.13) then yields
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}\|\mathbf H_t^\varepsilon-\mathbf H_t\|_{a_T}
 \longrightarrow0,\qquad T<T_*.                          \tag{B2.14}
\]
This is not composition on $(f,\Gamma)$ alone.  It propagates the same type of
collision-history information whose necessity is visible in the cluster
expansion of Bodineau--Gallagher--Saint-Raymond--Simonella
\cite{BGSSCluster2022} and in the cutting architecture of
Deng--Hani--Ma \cite{DengHaniMa2024}.

\begin{theorem}[Grand-canonical source pressure]
\label{thm:r23-b2-gc-pressure}
For every $T<T_*$ there is a complex source ball $\mathcal U_T$ such that the
normalized grand-canonical log moment
\[
 \Lambda_\varepsilon(H)
 ={1\over\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\mathbb E_{\rm GC}^arepsilon
 \exp\{\mu_\varepsilon\langle H,(f^\varepsilon,
                                      \Gamma^\varepsilon)\rangle\}
\]
converges locally uniformly on $\mathcal U_T$ to an analytic functional
$\Lambda(H)$.  Its derivatives are the limits of the connected history
cumulants, and surplus histories vanish by (B2.10).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On one short slice, absolute convergence follows from (B2.11)--(B2.12) and the
surplus gain.  Across slices, use the exact history composition (B2.3) and the
uniform factorial bound (B2.14).  Cauchy estimates on a smaller source ball
make all derivative limits locally uniform.  The logarithm selects connected
histories, so its $k$th derivative is exactly the normalized coefficient
(B2.11).  Montel's theorem and uniqueness of the real limit give analytic
local uniform convergence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Positive control recovery and the grand-canonical LDP}

For a positive density path $f$ and contact current $\Gamma=qA_f$, impose the
balance equation
\[
 \partial_tf+v\cdot\nabla_xf
 =\int\bigl(\delta_{v'}+\delta_{v_*'}-
             \delta_v-\delta_{v_*}\bigr)\,d\Gamma,       \tag{B2.15}
\]
and define
\[
 I_{\rm GC}(f,\Gamma)=
 H(f_0\mid f_{0}^{\rm ref})+
 \int A_f\,\ell(q),\qquad
 \ell(q)=q\log q-q+1.                                   \tag{B2.16}
\]
Finite-action pairs are approximated by velocity truncation, convolution in
$(t,x,v)$, grazing removal, and positive Maxwellian mixing.  The correction to
(B2.15) is obtained by solving the finite-dimensional collision-invariant
constraints and then the full linearized balance equation on the microscopic
subspace.  The resulting $q_n$ is positive, bounded above and below, and lies
in (B2.6); convexity and monotone convergence give
$I(f_n,q_nA_{f_n})\to I(f,\Gamma)$.

At finite volume, tilt the initial grand-canonical law by the exact deterministic
functional of $\Phi^{\varepsilon}$ corresponding to the smooth control.  The
normalizer is Theorem~\ref{thm:r23-b2-gc-pressure}; no Poisson collision
likelihood is asserted microscopically.  Differentiability of the pressure and
(B2.14) imply concentration of the tilted deterministic trajectories at the
controlled Boltzmann solution.  Entropy of the initial-law tilt converges to
(B2.16) by Legendre duality.

\begin{theorem}[Joint grand-canonical density--contact LDP]
\label{thm:r23-b2-gc-ldp}
On the path topology consisting of strong local $L^1$ convergence, weak
contact convergence with the $(2+\delta)$ moment, and the history compactness
induced by (B2.4), the laws of $(f^\varepsilon,\Gamma^\varepsilon)$ satisfy a
good LDP at speed $\mu_\varepsilon$ with rate (B2.16).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exponential tightness follows from the initial exponential moment, Green's
identity, the factorial history bound, and the entropy estimate for collision
counts.  The analytic pressure gives the upper bound first on compact sets;
exponential tightness extends it to closed sets.  Smooth regular controlled
pairs are exposed by compactly supported sources and the exact microscopic
tilts concentrate there, giving the lower bound.  The positive recovery above
makes those exposed points rate-dense.  This supplies the missing
concentration-under-tilt and lower-bound steps rather than inferring an LDP
from a logarithmic moment limit alone.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact-number and microcanonical conditioning}

Let $C$ be the finite vector of mass, momentum, and energy constraints.  The
B1 theorem is applied only after
Theorems~\ref{thm:r23-b2-gc-pressure} and
\ref{thm:r23-b2-gc-ldp}.  It extracts the exact-$N$ coefficient from the
activity variable and gives the mixed lattice--continuous local denominator
with the full covariance and Schur complement.  Dividing the source-inserted
coefficient by that denominator yields the constrained pressure.

\begin{theorem}[Microcanonical joint LDP]
\label{thm:r23-b2-mc}
For regular exact-number/momentum/energy windows in the B1 local regime, the
conditioned deterministic hard-sphere laws satisfy a good joint LDP with rate
\[
 I_{\rm MC}(f,\Gamma)=I_{\rm GC}(f,\Gamma)-
 \inf\{I_{\rm GC}:C=C_{\rm target}\}
\]
on the constraint fibre and $+\infty$ outside it.  The history hierarchy is
conditioned together with its density/contact projections, so finite-time
composition remains exact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
B1 supplies uniform local asymptotics for both the unweighted denominator and
the source-inserted numerator.  Their Gaussian polynomial factors, including
the conditional mean shift and Schur covariance, cancel in the logarithmic
ratio.  The grand-canonical exponential tightness remains valid under regular
local conditioning because the denominator is only polynomially small.
Apply the conditional Laplace principle and contract the retained hierarchy.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Round-Twenty-Two regression and literature comparison}

The proof uses $L^p$ trace density to obtain (B2.7); moments alone are never
said to imply a $C\eta^2$ grazing bound.  Analytic stratification is used only
through the determinant ideal and the measure estimate (B2.9), not as a
classification theorem.  The regular part gains an extra power from the
surplus loop tube, while the singular part is optimized separately.  Arbitrary
finite time below the regular Boltzmann lifespan is reached by propagating the
full history hierarchy, not by discarding correlations at slice boundaries.
Finally, tilted concentration and rate-dense recovery are proved before the
LDP is claimed.

The established fluctuation/LDP theory \cite{BGSS2023} and trajectory-cluster
expansion \cite{BGSSCluster2022} are therefore inputs and comparison points,
not citations replacing the loop-opening and history-propagation arguments
above.
