\section{Round-twenty-three reconstruction: transverse loop opening and an exact collision-history hierarchy}
\label{sec:r23-b2}

This paper is the hard-sphere root of the Round-Twenty-Three kinetic chain.
Two mechanisms are indispensable.  First, after the usual Boltzmann--Grad
cross-section has been cancelled, every independent surplus collision must
still yield a positive power of the sphere diameter.  Second, finite-time
iteration must retain the collision ancestry crossing a time cut.  We obtain
the first property from a loop-closure coarea estimate and the second from a
factorially weighted hierarchy of complete, time-ordered collision histories.

\subsection{Microscopic dynamics and the retained interface state}

Let $N$ hard spheres of diameter $\varepsilon$ move on $\mathbb T^3$ by free
flight and elastic reflection.  Their phase domain is
\[
 \mathcal D_{\varepsilon,N}
 =\{(x_i,v_i)_{i\le N}:|x_i-x_j|>\varepsilon
                         \text{ for }i\ne j\},
\]
and the deterministic Liouville flow is
$\Phi_t^{\varepsilon,N}$.  We use the Boltzmann--Grad scaling
$\mu_\varepsilon\varepsilon^2=1$ and a grand-canonical preparation whose
factorial cumulants have a uniform Maxwellian $(2+\delta)$-moment.

A collision mark is
\[
 c=(t,x,v,v_*,\omega),\qquad
 B(c)=[(v-v_*)\cdot\omega]_+ .                            \tag{B2.1}
\]
For a positive density $f$, the limiting reference contact measure is
\[
 A_f(dc)=f(t,x,v)f(t,x,v_*)B(c)
          \,dt\,dx\,dv\,dv_*\,d\omega .                 \tag{B2.2}
\]
The microscopic state retained at a cut time is not only the one-particle
density and incoming contact trace.  It is
\[
 \mathbf H^\varepsilon=(H_k^\varepsilon)_{k\ge1},        \tag{B2.3}
\]
where $H_k^\varepsilon$ is a signed measure on rooted, time-ordered collision
histories with $k$ labelled leaves.  A history records every collision time,
impact direction, pre- and post-collisional velocity, label identification,
and every half-edge cut by the observation time.  If $s\in(0,t)$, exact
microscopic concatenation is
\[
 \mathbf H^arepsilon_{[0,t]}
 =\mathbf H^arepsilon_{[0,s]}\star
  \mathbf H^arepsilon_{[s,t]},                         \tag{B2.4}
\]
where $\star$ glues matching open half-edges and sums over retained label
partitions.  Density and contact current are continuous projections of this
state, not a replacement for it.

For $a>0$ define the factorial history norm
\[
 \|\mathbf H\|_a
 =\sum_{k\ge1}{a^k\over k!}
   \sum_{G\in\mathfrak G_k}
   \int e^{\alpha\sum_i|v_i|^{2+\delta}}\,d|H_{k,G}| .   \tag{B2.5}
\]
The factor $1/k!$ is present before graph counting and remains present in all
subsequent estimates.

\subsection{Closed incoming trace and quantitative grazing removal}

Let $\lambda^-$ be the incoming Green trace on the hard-sphere boundary.
For smooth compactly supported test functions, transport integration by parts
gives
\[
 \int_{\mathcal D_{\varepsilon,N}}
 (V\cdot\nabla_X\varphi)F+arphi(V\cdot\nabla_XF)
 =\int_{\partial\mathcal D_{\varepsilon,N}}
   \varphi F(V\cdot n)\,dS .                             \tag{B2.6}
\]
This identity fixes the incoming orientation and shows that the trace graph is
closed under transport convergence plus a uniform flux-mass bound.

Moment bounds alone do not prevent concentration near grazing.  We therefore
first prove all geometric estimates on the regular tilted class
\[
 \Gamma=qA_f,\qquad
 \|q\|_{L^p(A_f)}\le L,\qquad p>1,                       \tag{B2.7}
\]
together with a uniform $(2+\delta)$ velocity moment.  If
$G_\eta=\{0<(v-v_*)\cdot\omega<\eta\}$, spherical integration in $\omega$
gives $A_f(G_\eta)\le C_L\eta^2$, and Holder's inequality gives
\[
 \Gamma(G_\eta)
 \le C_L\eta^{2(1-1/p)}.                                \tag{B2.8}
\]
A general finite-entropy current is approximated by
$(q\wedge M)1_{G_\eta^c}A_f$, followed by a conservative positive correction.
Data processing and monotone convergence recover its entropy.  Thus (B2.8) is
used on a dense regular class and is never inferred from moments alone.

\subsection{Loop coordinates and the determinant ideal}

Fix a connected ordered collision graph $G$ and choose a spanning tree $T_G$.
Tree edges introduce new particle variables.  Every edge of
$G\setminus T_G$ connects trajectories already determined by the tree and is
a surplus edge.  On a regular chronology chart with precontact coordinates
$y$, define the loop-closure map
\[
 \mathcal C_G(y)
 =\bigl(x_i(t_e^-)-x_j(t_e^-)-\varepsilon\omega_e
       \bigr)_{e\in G\setminus T_G}.                     \tag{B2.9}
\]
Reflections preceding $t_e$ are evaluated only after all earlier contact
constraints have been imposed.  On each chronology chart this map is real
analytic.

After quotienting global translations and the variables already solved by the
tree, let
\[
 \mathfrak d_G(y)=\sum_M|\det M(D\mathcal C_G(y))|^2,     \tag{B2.10}
\]
where the sum runs over maximal minors.  The zero set is not replaced by a
short list of named collision pictures.  Analytic stratification and the
Lojasiewicz inequality imply that, on every compact velocity/time cutoff,
\[
 |\{y:\mathfrak d_G(y)<\eta\}|
 \le C_G\eta^{\kappa_G}                                  \tag{B2.11}
\]
for some $\kappa_G>0$.  Grazing and simultaneous contacts are boundary
pieces and are controlled separately by (B2.8) and chronological
regularisation.

\begin{lemma}[Transverse surplus-contact gain]
\label{lem:r23-b2-loop}
Let $s(G)=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1$ be the cyclomatic number.  For every fixed history
cutoff $K$ there are constants $\alpha_K>0$ and $C_K(T,L)<\infty$ such that,
for every connected graph with at most $K$ vertices and every regular tilted
current satisfying (B2.7),
\[
 |\mathcal I_G^\varepsilon|
 \le C_K(T,L)^{|E(G)|}
       \varepsilon^{\alpha_K s(G)} .                     \tag{B2.12}
\]
The standard factor $\varepsilon^2$ for each collision has already been
cancelled by the activity $\mu_\varepsilon$; (B2.12) is additional smallness.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Solve all tree-contact equations by coarea.  A surplus edge then requires an
already determined pair of trajectories to enter a tube of radius
$C\varepsilon$ around one additional loop-closure equation.  On
$\{\mathfrak d_G\ge\eta\}$, a maximal minor provides one new transverse
coordinate for every independent loop.  Quantitative coarea therefore gives
\[
 C_K\varepsilon^{s(G)}\eta^{-m_K}                        \tag{B2.13}
\]
for the regular part.  On $\{\mathfrak d_G<\eta\}$ use (B2.11); boundary
pieces contribute at most the power in (B2.8).  One loop is consequently
bounded by
\[
 C_K\bigl(\varepsilon\eta^{-m_K}
          +\eta^{\kappa_K}
          +\eta^{2(1-1/p)}\bigr),                        \tag{B2.14}
\]
where $\kappa_K$ is the minimum of the finitely many exponents for graphs with
at most $K$ vertices.  Put
$\kappa'_K=\min\{\kappa_K,2(1-1/p)\}$ and choose
$\eta=\varepsilon^{1/(m_K+\kappa'_K)}$.  This gives the positive exponent
$\alpha_K=\kappa'_K/(m_K+\kappa'_K)$.  Open independent loops successively in
chronological order.  At every step the determinant ideal is recomputed after
the previous tree reduction, so the gains multiply and yield (B2.12).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Normalised connected coefficients}

For a bounded analytic path/contact source $H$, define the connected
coefficient with $k$ collision vertices by
\[
 C_k^\varepsilon(H)
 ={1\over k!}\sum_{G\in\mathfrak G_k^{\rm conn}}
   \mathcal I_G^\varepsilon(H).                          \tag{B2.15}
\]
For trees, Cayley's formula and the ordered time simplex give
\[
 {1\over k!}\sum_{G\ {m tree}}|\mathcal I_G^\varepsilon|
 \le {k^{k-2}\over k!}(CT)^k
 \le Ck^{-5/2}(CeT)^k.                                  \tag{B2.16}
\]
Surplus graphs carry the additional power from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r23-b2-loop}.  Hence on a short time slice the connected
series converges absolutely and locally uniformly on a complex source ball.

\subsection{Finite-time propagation of the complete hierarchy}

Let $f$ be the regular Boltzmann solution on $[0,T_*]$, and let $\mathbf H$
be the limiting tree-history hierarchy.  On a slice of length $\Delta$, the
Duhamel map satisfies, for $a'<a$,
\[
 \|\mathcal T_\Delta(\mathbf H)
       -\mathcal T_\Delta(\widetilde{\mathbf H})\|_{a'}
 \le C\Delta\|\mathbf H-\widetilde{\mathbf H}\|_a .     \tag{B2.17}
\]
Cut histories whose backward cluster exceeds $K$.  The factorial norm bounds
the cut tail by $C(a'/a)^K$.  For fixed $K$, all recollisions created by the
cut are controlled by Lemma~\ref{lem:r23-b2-loop}.  First choose $K$ large
relative to the number of slices and then let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.  Exact
composition (B2.4) and iteration of (B2.17) give
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}
 \|\mathbf H_t^\varepsilon-\mathbf H_t\|_{a_T}
 \longrightarrow0,\qquad T<T_* .                        \tag{B2.18}
\]
The diagonal choice $K=K(\varepsilon)\uparrow\infty$ is made only after the
finite family of determinant-ideal estimates at each fixed $K$; no uniform
Lojasiewicz exponent over all graph sizes is assumed.

\begin{theorem}[Grand-canonical source pressure]
\label{thm:r23-b2-gc-pressure}
For every $T<T_*$ there is a complex source neighbourhood $\mathcal U_T$ on
which
\[
 \Lambda_\varepsilon(H)
 ={1\over\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\mathbb E_{\rm GC}^\varepsilon
 \exp\{\mu_\varepsilon\langle H,
              (f^\varepsilon,\Gamma^\varepsilon)\rangle\}
\]
converges locally uniformly to an analytic functional $\Lambda(H)$.  Its
Fréchet derivatives are the limiting connected history cumulants, and every
surplus-history contribution vanishes by (B2.12).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On one slice, (B2.15)--(B2.16) give absolute convergence of the logarithmic
connected expansion, while (B2.12) eliminates surplus graphs.  Across slices,
compose the full state by (B2.4), not merely its density/contact projection,
and use the uniform factorial estimate (B2.18).  Cauchy estimates on a smaller
source ball make all derivatives locally uniform.  Montel compactness and
uniqueness of the real cumulant limit identify the analytic limit.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Positive control recovery and the dynamic action}

A candidate positive pair satisfies
\[
 \Gamma=qA_f,\qquad q\ge0,
\]
and the weak balance equation
\[
 \partial_tf+v\cdot\nabla_xf
 =\int(\delta_{v'}+\delta_{v_*'}-
        \delta_v-\delta_{v_*})\,d\Gamma .                \tag{B2.19}
\]
Its grand-canonical action is
\[
 I_{\rm GC}(f,\Gamma)
 =H(f_0\mid f_0^{\rm ref})+
   \int A_f\ell(q),\qquad
 \ell(q)=q\log q-q+1 .                                  \tag{B2.20}
\]
Finite-action pairs are approximated by velocity truncation, convolution,
grazing removal, and a small positive Maxwellian mixture.  The resulting
balance defect is corrected on the subspace orthogonal to mass, momentum, and
energy.  The corrected multipliers are positive and bounded, lie in the
regular class (B2.7), and converge in action by convexity and monotone
convergence.

At finite volume, tilt the exact deterministic initial law by the path
functional associated with a regular control.  The normalising constant is
provided by Theorem~\ref{thm:r23-b2-gc-pressure}; no independent Poisson
collision process is inserted into the microscopic dynamics.  The complete
history convergence (B2.18) and differentiability of the pressure imply
concentration of the tilted deterministic trajectories at the controlled
Boltzmann path.

\begin{theorem}[Joint grand-canonical density--contact LDP]
\label{thm:r23-b2-gc-ldp}
On the topology of strong local $L^1$ density convergence, weak contact
convergence with the $(2+\delta)$ moment, and compactness of the retained
history hierarchy in (B2.5), the laws of
$(f^\varepsilon,\Gamma^\varepsilon)$ satisfy a good LDP at speed
$\mu_\varepsilon$ with rate (B2.20).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The initial exponential moment, Green identity (B2.6), factorial history
bound, and entropy estimate for collision counts give exponential tightness.
The analytic pressure gives the compact upper bound, extended to closed sets
by tightness.  Regular controlled pairs are exposed by compactly supported
sources.  The exact microscopic tilts described above concentrate at those
pairs, and their relative entropy converges to (B2.20), giving the exposed
lower bound.  Positive recovery makes the exposed class rate dense, yielding
the full lower bound and goodness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact-number and microcanonical transfer}

The preceding two theorems are the grand-canonical root and do not import B1.
B1 is used only now: its source-uniform exact-number coefficient theorem gives
the mixed lattice--continuous local denominator, including the full covariance
and Schur complement.  Dividing the source-inserted coefficient by this
denominator yields the conditioned pressure.

\begin{theorem}[Microcanonical joint LDP]
\label{thm:r23-b2-mc}
For regular exact-number, momentum, and energy windows in the B1 local regime,
the conditioned deterministic hard-sphere laws satisfy a good joint LDP with
rate
\[
 I_{\rm MC}(f,\Gamma)
 =I_{\rm GC}(f,\Gamma)
  -\inf\{I_{\rm GC}:C=C_{\rm target}\}                  \tag{B2.21}
\]
on the constraint fibre and $+\infty$ outside it.  Conditioning is performed
on the complete history hierarchy together with its density/contact
projections, so finite-time composition remains exact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply B1's uniform local expansion to the unweighted denominator and to every
source-inserted numerator.  Their polynomial Gaussian factors, including the
conditional mean shift and Schur covariance, cancel in the logarithmic ratio.
The denominator is only polynomially small, so grand-canonical exponential
tightness survives the conditioning.  The conditional Laplace principle then
gives (B2.21), and contraction of the retained hierarchy gives the stated
observable-level LDP.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Regression against the Round-Twenty-Two objections}

The active proof uses an $L^p(A_f)$ trace density for quantitative grazing
control, not moments alone.  It uses the determinant ideal (B2.10) to cover all
rank-loss mechanisms on a fixed chart, rather than declaring a named list
exhaustive.  The regular surplus set obtains the extra factor in (B2.13), while
the small-minor and boundary sets are optimised separately.  The factor
$1/k!$ remains in the connected coefficient.  Arbitrary finite time below the
regular Boltzmann lifespan is reached by composing complete collision
histories with open half-edges.  Finally, tilted concentration and rate-dense
positive recovery are proved before either LDP is claimed.
