\section{Round-twenty-nine reconstruction: disintegrated cut kernels and constraint-preserving loop pivots}
\label{sec:r29-b2}

The collision-history product is now defined only on kernels that admit a
specified disintegration over every cut state.  On that category it is
ordinary kernel composition and is therefore canonical and associative.
Surplus contacts are controlled by tangent vector fields on the full
tree-contact manifold; no subtree translation is allowed to break an already
imposed tree collision.

\subsection{Microscopic preparation and contact measures}

Hard spheres of diameter $\varepsilon$ move in $\mathbb T^3$ under elastic
reflection, with Boltzmann--Grad activity
$\mu_\varepsilon\varepsilon^2=1$.  A regular contact mark is
$c=(t,x,v,v_*,\omega)$ and
\[
 A_f(dc)=\tfrac12 f(t,x,v)f(t,x,v_*)[(v-v_*)\cdot\omega]_+
 \,dt\,dx\,dv\,dv_*\,d\omega.                            \tag{B2.1}
\]
The regular root class has a Gaussian velocity bound, a positive Maxwellian
lower bound on compact velocity sets, and factorial initial cumulants
$\kappa_k^\varepsilon$ satisfying
\[
 \sum_{k\ge2}{a^k\over k!}
 \norm{\kappa_k^\varepsilon}_{\rm root}\longrightarrow0. \tag{B2.2}
\]
Equation (B2.2) is the explicit initial-correlation input for propagation of
chaos; it is not inferred from removal of recollision graphs.

\begin{lemma}[Relative-velocity regularity]
\label{lem:r29-b2-relative}
On the regular class,
\[
 \int{f(v)f(v_*)\over|v-v_*|}\,dv\,dv_*\le C              \tag{B2.3}
\]
and
\[
 A_f\{0<(v-v_*)\cdot\omega<\eta\}\le C\eta^2.             \tag{B2.4}
\]
The same estimate with exponent $2(1-1/p)$ holds for
$qA_f$ when $q\in L^p(A_f)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The relative-velocity convolution is bounded near zero and has a Gaussian
tail.  Since $|g|^{-1}$ is locally integrable in three dimensions, (B2.3)
follows.  Angular integration of the incoming flux gives
$C\eta^2/|g|$ for $|g|>\eta$ and $O(\eta^4)$ for $|g|\le\eta$, proving
(B2.4).  Holder's inequality gives the controlled version.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The disintegrated cut-kernel category}

For every ordered list $o$ of open half-edges, let $(S_o,\lambda_o)$ be the
standard Borel cut-state space with its Maxwellian--Lebesgue reference
measure.  A decorated history type $G$ has incoming and outgoing lists
$o_-(G),o_+(G)$, a tree-contact parameter measure $m_G$, and a measurable
kernel density
\[
 k_G(x_-,z,x_+)\quad\hbox{with respect to}\quad
 \lambda_{o_-}(dx_-)m_G(dz)\lambda_{o_+}(dx_+).           \tag{B2.5}
\]
For fully labelled histories the exponential-generating coefficient is
$1/|V(G)|!$ and $\operatorname{Aut}G$ is trivial.  If labels are forgotten,
$1/|\operatorname{Aut}G|$ is used instead; the two factors are never
identified.

For $a>0$ define
\[
 \norm{K}_{a}=
 \sum_G{a^{|V(G)|}\over |V(G)|!}
 \sup_{x_-}\int e^{\alpha E(z,x_+)}
 |k_G(x_-,z,x_+)|\,m_G(dz)\lambda_{o_+}(dx_+).            \tag{B2.6}
\]
An analogous backward norm is imposed, and the history space
$\mathfrak K_a$ is the intersection of the two kernel spaces.

If $o_+(G^-)=o_-(G^+)=o$, define
\[
 (k_{G^-}\star k_{G^+})(x_-,z^-,z^+,x_+)
 =\int_{S_o}k_{G^-}(x_-,z^-,y)k_{G^+}(y,z^+,x_+)
   \lambda_o(dy).                                        \tag{B2.7}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Associative disintegrated history algebra]
\label{prop:r29-b2-algebra}
For $0<a'<a$,
\[
 \norm{K^-\star K^+}_{a'}
 \le C(a,a')\norm{K^-}_{a}\norm{K^+}_{a}.                \tag{B2.8}
\]
The product is positive, measurable, and associative.  It is not defined for
arbitrary singular measures lacking the disintegrations in (B2.5).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Tonelli's theorem applies to the reference cut measure $\lambda_o$ and gives
both measurability and equality of the two triple-integral parenthesizations.
Elastic reflection preserves the velocity energy weight.  The labelled
binomial identity
\[
 {1\over k!}{k\choose i}={1\over i!(k-i)!}
\]
controls label partitions; open-half-edge matchings cost a finite power of
$(a-a')^{-1}$ and are absorbed by radius loss.  The backward norm gives the
same estimate with the orientation reversed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Constraint-preserving chronological pivots}

Fix a labelled connected graph $G$ and a chronological spanning tree $T$.
Let $\Omega_T$ be the regular manifold on which all tree contacts have been
imposed.  In ambient collision coordinates write the tree constraints as
$F_T=0$.  The regular tree atlas includes a causal right inverse
$R_T$ of $DF_T$ whose correction for a contact at time $t$ is supported only
on coordinates at or after $t$.

For a surplus edge $e$ at time $t_e$, choose an ambient relative-position
variation $a_e$ supported after the last tree contact entering its later
endpoint.  Define the tangent pivot
\[
 X_e=a_e-R_TDF_Ta_e.                                      \tag{B2.9}
\]
Then $DF_TX_e=0$, so the flow of $X_e$ remains on $\Omega_T$.  The surplus
closure is the scalar signed-distance function
\[
 g_e=|x_{i_e}(t_e^-)-x_{j_e}(t_e^-)|-\varepsilon.          \tag{B2.10}
\]

A finite \emph{pivot certificate} consists of regular chronology charts,
causal right inverses $R_T$, and choices of $a_e$ for which
\[
 Dg_e(X_{e'})=0\quad(t_{e'}>t_e),\qquad
 |Dg_e(X_e)|\ge c_G>0.                                    \tag{B2.11}
\]
These are finite derivatives of finite collision maps.  No error term is
claimed to become small merely by moving away from the singular set.

\begin{lemma}[Constraint-preserving triangular rank]
\label{lem:r29-b2-pivots}
On every certified regular chart, the map
$(s_e)_{e\in E\setminus T}\mapsto(g_e)$ obtained by flowing along the
chronologically ordered vector fields $X_e$ has lower-triangular derivative
with diagonal bounded below by $c_G$.  Hence
\[
 \abs{\det(Dg_eX_{e'})_{e,e'}}\ge c_G^{s(G)},
 \qquad s(G)=|E|-|V|+1.                                  \tag{B2.12}
\]
Every tree contact remains exactly satisfied.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation (B2.9) makes $X_e$ tangent to every tree constraint.  Causal support
means a later pivot cannot alter an earlier surplus closure, giving the zero
blocks in (B2.11).  The certified diagonal derivative is the nonzero relative
normal displacement at the closing contact.  The determinant of a lower
triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries, proving (B2.12).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact coarea power for surplus contacts}

On a regular pivot chart, a surplus binary contact is represented by
\[
 r(t)-\varepsilon\omega=0,
 \qquad (t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb S^2.              \tag{B2.13}
\]
Coarea in $(t,\omega)$ contributes the surface factor
$\varepsilon^2/|(v_i-v_j)\cdot\omega|$.  A creation edge carries an activity
factor $\mu_\varepsilon$ which cancels its own $\varepsilon^2$ cross-section;
a surplus edge creates no new particle and therefore retains its
$\varepsilon^2$ factor.

\begin{theorem}[Multiplicative loop gain]
\label{thm:r29-b2-loop}
For each vertex cutoff $K$ there are $C_K<\infty$ and $\alpha_K>0$ such that
\[
 |\mathcal I_G^\varepsilon|
 \le C_K^{|E(G)|}\varepsilon^{\alpha_Ks(G)}
       |\mathcal I_T^\varepsilon|.                        \tag{B2.14}
\]
On the uniformly nongrazing regular set one may take $\alpha_K=2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply simultaneous coarea in the pivot variables using (B2.12), followed by
(A2.13) for each surplus edge.  Since no activity factor accompanies a
surplus contact, each regular edge retains $\varepsilon^2$.  The singular part
is divided into grazing, simultaneous, and high-velocity charts.
Lemma~\ref{lem:r29-b2-relative}, time coarea, and the Gaussian tail give
$C_K\delta^{\kappa_K}$ for a $\delta$-neighbourhood.  On its complement the
coarea constant is polynomial in $\delta^{-1}$.  Choosing
$\delta=\varepsilon^{\theta_K}$ leaves a positive power $\alpha_K$.  The
triangular determinant permits all surplus constraints to be integrated at
once, so the powers multiply even for nested genealogies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Duhamel evolution and the connected logarithm}

Let $\mathcal F_t$ be free transport of cut kernels and let $\mathcal C_t$ be
the creation operator that adds one labelled root/contact at time $t$.  The
kernel definitions give
\[
 \norm{\mathcal F_tK}_{a}\le\norm K_a,
 \qquad
 \norm{\mathcal C_tK}_{a'}\le{C\over a-a'}\norm K_a.      \tag{B2.15}
\]
Define the exact time-ordered Duhamel map by
\[
 \mathcal T_t^\varepsilon K
 =\sum_{n\ge0}\int_{0<t_1<\cdots<t_n<t}
 \mathcal F_{t-t_n}\mathcal C_{t_n}\cdots
 \mathcal C_{t_1}\mathcal F_{t_1}K\,dt_1\cdots dt_n.     \tag{B2.16}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Radius-loss Duhamel estimate]
\label{lem:r29-b2-duhamel}
For $a'<a$,
\[
 \norm{\mathcal T_t^\varepsilon K-
       \mathcal T_t^\varepsilon\widetilde K}_{a'}
 \le \exp\left({Ct\over a-a'}\right)
\norm{K-\widetilde K}_a,             \tag{B2.17}
\]
and the tail with more than $M$ new vertices is bounded by
$C e^{Ct/(a-a')}(a'/a)^M$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Iterate (B2.15) on a decreasing radius chain between $a$ and $a'$.  The time
simplex contributes $t^n/n!$, so the series in (B2.16) converges normally and
sums to (B2.17).  Marking $M$ vertices before evaluating at $a'$ gives the
geometric tail.
\end{proof}

The algebra is filtered by vertex number and has an augmentation
$\epsilon(K)$ equal to its empty history component.  If $\epsilon(K)=1$, set
\[
 \log_\star K=\sum_{n\ge1}{(-1)^{n+1}\over n}(K-1)^{\star n}.             \tag{B2.18}
\]
At each fixed vertex degree the sum is finite, and on a short time slice it
converges in $\mathfrak K_{a'}$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:r29-b2-algebra}.
Its components are exactly connected cut histories.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-time tree-history propagation]
\label{thm:r29-b2-propagation}
For every regular Boltzmann time $T<T_*$,
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}\norm{K_t^\varepsilon-K_t^{\rm tree}}_{a_T}\to0.          \tag{B2.19}
\]
The limit is unique in the Duhamel class, and composition across time cuts is
kernel composition $\star$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At a fixed vertex cutoff, Theorem~\ref{thm:r29-b2-loop} eliminates every
surplus graph.  The initial cumulant condition (B2.2) eliminates nonchaotic
root components.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r29-b2-duhamel} controls the uniform tail and
proves stability under time slicing.  The limiting tree Duhamel equation is a
contraction on each short slice; iteration gives uniqueness on $[0,T]$.
Associativity of $\star$ makes the construction independent of the partition.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Pressure and projective dynamic large deviations}

For a bounded analytic source on finitely many history coordinates, the
connected logarithm (B2.18) and the tree recursion define
$\Lambda_T(H)$.  Normal convergence holds with all fixed source derivatives.
The projective source algebra separates density, contact current, and every
finite history cylinder.

\begin{theorem}[Grand-canonical pressure and tree-history lift]
\label{thm:r29-b2-pressure}
For $T<T_*$,
\[
 \Lambda_\varepsilon(H)={1\over\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log E_{\rm GC}^\varepsilon e^{\mu_\varepsilon\langle H,
 (f^\varepsilon,\Gamma^\varepsilon,K^\varepsilon)\rangle}
 \longrightarrow\Lambda_T(H)                            \tag{B2.20}
\]
locally uniformly with fixed derivatives.  The limiting history coordinate is
the unique causal tree lift $K=\mathfrak L(f,\Gamma)$ solving the tree Duhamel
equation driven by $(f,\Gamma)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On each short slice the connected series converges by (B2.17)--(B2.18).
Theorem~\ref{thm:r29-b2-loop} removes surplus components under the same
summable majorant.  The last creation edge separates a connected tree into two
rooted trees and sums to the Hamilton--Boltzmann collision exponential.
Theorem~\ref{thm:r29-b2-propagation} concatenates the slices and gives
uniqueness of the tree lift.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local positive recovery and exact tilted concentration}

Let a finite space-time-velocity partition have balance space $Y_M$.  On each
cell choose a finite family of regular elastic collisions whose gain--loss
vectors span the local conservation quotient.  Let $R_M:Y_M\to\R^{J_M}$ be a
right inverse.  As $M\to\infty$, choose the mesh so that the approximation
defect $r_M$ satisfies
\[
 \norm{R_Mr_M}_\infty=o(\delta_M),                        \tag{B2.21}
\]
where $\delta_M$ is the positive Maxwellian collision background in those
local frames.

\begin{lemma}[Refining collision-frame correction]
\label{lem:r29-b2-recovery}
Every finite-action admissible pair $(f,qA_f)$ is approximated by smooth
positive exactly balanced pairs $(f_M,q_MA_{f_M})$ with convergent action.
The correction is local, preserves mass, momentum, and energy, and remains
positive by (B2.21).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Truncate velocity, mollify in each partition cell, and add a vanishing
Maxwellian background.  Project the balance defect onto $Y_M$ and correct it
with $R_M$.  The local collision frames respect collision invariants; (B2.21)
keeps the multiplier positive.  Mesh refinement and weighted uniform
integrability make the defect and the entropy change tend to zero.
\end{proof}

For a regular pair, let $H_{f,q}$ be its exposing source and tilt the original
deterministic initial ensemble by the exact path functional.  The pressure
normalizes this change of measure.

\begin{proposition}[Chernoff concentration under exact tilts]
\label{prop:r29-b2-tilt}
If the exposed variational minimizer is unique, then for every closed set
$F$ not containing $(f,qA_f,\mathfrak L(f,qA_f))$ there is $c_F>0$ such that
\[
 P_{\varepsilon}^{H_{f,q}}(F)\le e^{-c_F\mu_\varepsilon}. \tag{B2.22}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Compactness supplies finitely many bounded projective sources separating the
minimizer from $F$.  Exponential Chebyshev under each perturbed source and the
local uniform pressure limit give a strictly negative exponent.  Taking the
finite maximum proves (B2.22).  A second derivative is used only for local
quadratic identification, not as a substitute for this Chernoff argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joint hard-sphere dynamic LDP]
\label{thm:r29-b2-ldp}
The laws of $(f^\varepsilon,\Gamma^\varepsilon,K^\varepsilon)$ satisfy a good
LDP at speed $\mu_\varepsilon$ in the narrow quadratic-moment,
contact-weak, projective kernel topology.  The rate is
\[
 I_{\rm GC}(f,\Gamma,K)=
 \begin{cases}
 H(f_0\mid f_0^{\rm ref})+\displaystyle\int\ell(q)\,dA_f,
 &\Gamma=qA_f,\ K=\mathfrak L(f,\Gamma),\ \text{exact balance},\\
 +\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{B2.23}
\]
B1 coefficient extraction gives the exact-number and microcanonical
contractions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finite-dimensional projective upper bounds follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r29-b2-pressure}.  Particle, energy, contact, and kernel-radius
bounds give exponential tightness, so the projective upper bounds assemble by
the Dawson--Gartner argument.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r29-b2-recovery} gives a
dense regular class and Proposition~\ref{prop:r29-b2-tilt} gives its lower
bound.  Lower semicontinuity extends the result to the attainable closure.
Uniqueness of the tree lift identifies the retained history coordinate rather
than silently omitting it from the rate.
\end{proof}

The hard-sphere root is now formulated on a genuine kernel category, uses
constraint-preserving pivots with exact coarea dimensions, controls initial
correlations and Duhamel tails, and proves the full joint rate by projective
sources and exact Chernoff tilts.
