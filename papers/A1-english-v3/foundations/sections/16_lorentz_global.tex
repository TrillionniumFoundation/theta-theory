\section{Whole-preparation response by a collision-tube identity}\label{sec:lorentz-global}
\subsection{The new scope and the short-horizon mechanism}
Retain the entire configuration domain $Q_R=\T_\Lambda^2\setminus\overline B(0,R)$ and all velocity directions. Fix
\begin{equation}
 0<T<d_*=3/50.
 \label{eq:short-T}
\end{equation}
Every regular trajectory has zero or one collision in this interval, even if its first collision is arbitrarily grazing. There is no restriction to a prepared directional tube.

More generally than equilibrium, let $\rho_R(q,\vartheta)\ge0$ be a density on the fixed ambient $\T_\Lambda^2\times\mathbb S^1$, extending $C^{r+1}$ in $(R,q,\vartheta)$ with bounded derivatives. Its restricted preparation is
\[
 d\mu_R=Z_R^{-1}\one_{Q_R}(q)\rho_R(q,\vartheta)\dd q\dd\vartheta,
 \qquad Z_R=\int_{Q_R\times\mathbb S^1}\rho_R\dd q\dd\vartheta\ge z_*>0.
\]
For equilibrium, $\rho_R=1$ and $Z_R=2\pi A_R$. The density's ambient extension is an explicit regularity assumption; arbitrary singular preparations are not covered by this theorem.

\subsection{Coordinates on all incoming collision tubes}
Write
\[
 n_\alpha=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha),\quad
 t_\alpha=(-\sin\alpha,\cos\alpha),
\]
\[
 v^-(\alpha,\phi)=-\cos\phi\,n_\alpha+\sin\phi\,t_\alpha,
 \qquad v^+(\alpha,\phi)=\cos\phi\,n_\alpha+\sin\phi\,t_\alpha.
\]
For an impact at time $s\in(0,T)$, the initial state is
\begin{equation}
 q_0=Rn_\alpha-sv^-,\qquad v_0=v^-.
 \label{eq:incoming-tube}
\end{equation}
Positions are taken modulo $\Lambda$. The trajectory before impact is the backward continuation from the circle; because its preceding collision is at least $d_*$ earlier, the segment of length $s<T$ contains no other collision. The forward reflected segment of length $T-s<d_*$ likewise has no further collision. This establishes the completeness of this parameterization up to the usual null grazing and endpoint sets.

For later use define the free and reflected trajectories
\[
 \gamma_R^0(t)=(Rn_\alpha+(t-s)v^-,v^-),
\]
\[
 \gamma_R^1(t)=
 \begin{cases}(Rn_\alpha+(t-s)v^-,v^-),&t<s,\\
 (Rn_\alpha+(t-s)v^+,v^+),&t\ge s.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:reflected-path}
\]
The superscript zero denotes a comparison trajectory which is allowed to cross a disk; it appears only in a subtract-and-replace identity, not as a physical continuation.

\begin{lemma}[Incoming-tube Jacobian]\label{lem:tube-jacobian}
The change of variables $(\alpha,\phi,s)\mapsto(q_0,\vartheta_0)$ in \eqref{eq:incoming-tube} has absolute Jacobian $R\cos\phi$. It is one-to-one on physical one-collision initial states modulo torus identifications and null boundaries.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The incoming velocity angle is $\vartheta_0=\alpha+\pi-\phi$. Use $(\alpha,\vartheta_0,s)$ as intermediate coordinates. At fixed $\vartheta_0$, differentiating $q_0=Rn_\alpha-sv(\vartheta_0)$ gives columns $Rt_\alpha$ and $-v(\vartheta_0)$ in the two position directions, with determinant of magnitude $R|n_\alpha\cdot v|=R\cos\phi$. The transformation from $(\alpha,\phi,s)$ to $(\alpha,\vartheta_0,s)$ has determinant of magnitude one. A physical path has a unique collision in the window, hence unique $(s,\alpha,\phi)$ except angular coordinate identifications. Conversely the preceding paragraph shows every such triple produces one physical collision and no other. Tangent endpoints have zero flux and phase volume.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact complete assembly}
Let $F_0$ be a test of an unmarked no-collision record, and $F_1$ a test of a marked one-collision record. They may report the initial state and, in the second case, its collision time; or any fixed finite collection of physical sample states and the collision mark. Denote the free path from an arbitrary initial state $(q,v)$ by $\gamma^0(q,v)$. Extend the no-collision test to all such comparison records; the subtraction below makes the final identity independent of that extension on colliding initial states.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-preparation collision-tube identity]\label{thm:global-tube}
For every bounded measurable physical-record test $F$,
\begin{align}
 Z_R E_{\mu_R}F
 &=\int_{Q_R\times\mathbb S^1}\rho_R(q,\vartheta)
 F_0(\gamma^0(q,v))\dd q\dd\vartheta\notag\\
 &\quad+R\int_0^T\int_0^{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}
 \cos\phi\,\rho_R(Rn_\alpha-sv^-,v^-)
 [F_1(\gamma_R^1,s)-F_0(\gamma_R^0)]\dd\phi\dd\alpha\dd s.
 \label{eq:global-tube}
\end{align}
The first integral plus the negative part removes all nonphysical free continuations of colliding initial states; the positive part inserts their actual reflected records. The resulting functional is a positive probability after division by $Z_R$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Split the initial preparation into no-collision states and states whose unique first collision occurs in $(0,T)$. On the first set the physical record equals the free record. On the second set, the integral of a physical test is obtained by replacing its free value by the reflected value. Lemma~\ref{lem:tube-jacobian} parameterizes precisely this second set and gives its weight. Substitution yields \eqref{eq:global-tube}. Positivity follows from its equality to the original path integral, not from positivity of each signed term separately. For $F\equiv1$ the bracket vanishes and the total is $Z_R$, proving normalization. No differentiation or exclusion of a positive-probability grazing neighborhood is used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Strong response of the complete one-collision record}
Use the disjoint tagged report spaces
\[
 E_0=\T_\Lambda^2\times\mathbb S^1,
 \qquad E_1=\T_\Lambda^2\times\mathbb S^1\times[0,T].
\]
A no-collision record contains its initial state; a one-collision record contains initial state and collision time. Given $R$, either record determines the full physical trajectory on $[0,T]$. Embed the time interval in a circle and use the direct sum
\[
 \cX_s=H^{-s}(E_0)\oplus H^{-s}(\T_\Lambda^2\times\mathbb S^1\times\mathbb S^1),
 \qquad s>2+r.
\]
The notation on $E_0$ is understood after a fixed smooth torus coordinate identification. The two tags remain distinct.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-preparation finite-order response]\label{thm:global-response}
Under the density regularity and normalization assumptions above, the law of this complete tagged record is strongly $C^r$ in $\cX_s$. Its derivatives are obtained by differentiating the three terms of \eqref{eq:global-tube}; no inverse $\cos\phi$ occurs. For $\rho_R$ smooth through every finite order, the conclusion holds at every fixed finite order in its corresponding reporting space.

For every fixed finite set of physical sample times, expectations of branchwise smooth cylinder tests of the sampled positions, velocities, and collision mark also have these derivatives. This latter assertion includes differentiation of the parameter-dependent decoding from the tagged record to physical states; it does not treat that decoding as parameter independent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First consider the no-event component. Its unnormalized ambient initial-state measure is
$\rho_R\dd q\dd\vartheta$ on the full torus minus its integral over $|q|\le R$. Parameterize that disk by $q=t n_\alpha$, $0\le t\le R$. For a fixed test, its disk contribution has the form
\[
 \int_0^R F(R,t)\dd t,
 \quad F(R,t)=t\int\rho_R(tn_\alpha,\vartheta)f(tn_\alpha,\vartheta)\dd\alpha\dd\vartheta.
\]
Repeated Leibniz differentiation gives bulk derivatives and boundary derivatives on the circle. The same operation in $H^{-s}$ is justified by Lemma~\ref{lem:dirac}: each boundary term is a Bochner integral of moving Dirac derivatives with uniformly bounded coefficients. The disk radius remains below $1/2$, so the local disk coordinates are embedded in the torus without geometric overlap.

For the tube correction, the integration domain in $(s,\alpha,\phi)$ is fixed. Initial position $Rn_\alpha-sv^-$ is affine in $R$, velocity and collision time are independent of $R$, and the weight
$R\cos\phi\rho_R(Rn_\alpha-sv^-,v^-)$ has bounded derivatives. The two tagged Dirac maps therefore have strong derivatives through order $r$, dominated by an integrable constant times $\cos\phi$. The endpoints $\phi=\pm\pi/2$ cause no denominator. Bochner differentiation proves the response of both tube terms. Normalize using $Z_R\ge z_*$ and the quotient recursion.

For physical sample tests, use \eqref{eq:reflected-path}. On each of the finitely many fixed $s$ intervals cut by the sample times, the report is smooth in $R$ with bounded derivatives: every sampled position has radius derivative $n_\alpha$ in the tube, and the velocities have zero radius derivative in these coordinates. The cuts in $s$ are independent of $R$. Differentiate on each interval and sum. The free bulk has its usual moving-domain terms. This yields the full decoder response without incorrectly assuming smoothness across a sample-time velocity jump in $s$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{An explicit derivative algorithm.}
For $I(R)=\int_0^R F(R,t)dt$,
\begin{equation}
 I^{(j)}(R)=\int_0^R\partial_R^jF(R,t)dt
 +\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\frac{d^{j-1-k}}{dR^{j-1-k}}
 [\partial_R^kF(R,t)|_{t=R}],\qquad j\ge1.
 \label{eq:diagonal-boundary}
\end{equation}
This follows by induction; the outer derivatives are total derivatives along the diagonal and hence include $\partial_t$. For a tube integral $R\int G_R$, its $j$th derivative is
$R\int G_R^{(j)}+j\int G_R^{(j-1)}$ for $j\ge1$. Inside $G_R$, use the finite chain and product rules from Appendix~\ref{app:jets}. Finally normalize by the quotient recursion. Equations~\eqref{eq:diagonal-boundary} and these fixed-domain operations specify every derivative rather than referring to an unnamed all-order calculus.

\subsection{Equilibrium probabilities and their derivatives}
At equilibrium $\rho_R=1$, the probability of a collision by $T$ is
\begin{equation}
 p_{\mathrm{eq}}(R)=\frac{2RT}{A_R}=\frac{T}{\bar\tau_R}.
 \label{eq:peq}
\end{equation}
Indeed the tube has unnormalized mass
$R\,T\,(2\pi)\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos\phi\dd\phi=4\pi RT$.
Conditioned on a collision, the joint law is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd s}{T}\frac{\dd\alpha}{2\pi}\frac{\cos\phi\dd\phi}{2}.
 \label{eq:conditional-hit}
\end{equation}
These conditional factorization identities are derived from the complete flux integral, not postulated as independent mechanical collisions.

\begin{corollary}[Strict global hit response and all-order recursion]\label{cor:global-hit}
On $I$ and $0<T<d_*$, $0<p_{\mathrm{eq}}(R)<1$, and
\[
 p_{\mathrm{eq}}'(R)=\frac{2T(A_\Lambda+\pi R^2)}{A_R^2}>0,\qquad
 p_{\mathrm{eq}}''(R)=\frac{4\pi RT(3A_\Lambda+\pi R^2)}{A_R^3}>0.
\]
For $j\ge2$,
\[
 p_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(j)}(R)
 =\frac{2j\pi R\,p_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(j-1)}(R)+j(j-1)\pi p_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(j-2)}(R)}{A_R}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The probability formula comes from the exact tube mass. The mean roof is at least the minimum free-flight length $d_*$, so $p_{\mathrm{eq}}=T/\bar\tau_R<1$ and is positive. Differentiate $A_Rp_{\mathrm{eq}}=2TR$ once and twice to obtain the first derivatives. At general order only $A_R'=-2\pi R$ and $A_R''=-2\pi$ are nonzero; the Leibniz rule yields the recursion.
\end{proof}

This full equilibrium result and the preceding fixed prepared-tube result answer different questions. The equilibrium preparation itself changes with $R$ through its allowed position domain and normalization. The earlier tube preparation is radius independent. Both responses are derived, but they must not be conflated.

The whole-preparation calculation is limited by \eqref{eq:short-T}, not by an artificial exclusion of grazing. Longer horizons require assemblies with multiple collisions, changing itineraries, and correlated event coordinates. The present finite identity does not prove those longer-time estimates.
