\begin{abstract}
We determine separated convex obstacle configurations from pooled
reciprocal collision responses under unknown stationary launch laws.
The occupation at each nominal point is the value of a stopping linear
program on a fixed finite stencil. Its inverse is Lipschitz for arbitrary
noisy response data and yields an occupation estimate with
$O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\delta))$ attempted bits at fixed sites.
The full translation period group of the scalar response is exactly
that of the obstacle configuration, without a periodicity prior.
This rigidity holds for every bounded centered displacement law with
positive second moment. For independently translated homothetic launch
supports, collision deficits identify the scale ratios and centered
support envelopes recover the footprint and obstacle configuration.
Uniform directions give an intrinsic perimeter normalization, without
a preferred axis. The complete geometric ambiguity consists of a common
translation and independent footprint translations by obstacle periods.
These exact results require neither boundary smoothness nor a finite
list of component species. For bounded $C^{6,\beta}$ periodic classes,
put $s=6+\beta$. With a fixed known uniform-disk launch law, we obtain
matching stationary minimax power $(3s/2+1)/(s-2)$ for $C^2$ boundary
accuracy, with one logarithmic factor between upper and lower bounds.
The lower bound permits adaptive directions and arbitrarily short
commands; the upper construction uses pooled compass bits.
Uniform finite period decisions retain a positive nonperiod-patch
margin. All solid starts and misses are counted, and control precision,
center batches, distinct sites, and numerical arithmetic are separated.
\end{abstract}
