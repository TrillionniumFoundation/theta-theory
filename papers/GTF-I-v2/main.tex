\begin{abstract}
We relate the geometry of acquired predictions to their causal retention
under a finite-label constraint. Exact suffix evolutions yield a
nonuniform stability bound, while concentration of the actual acquired
law gives a matching converse when the covering and stability hypotheses
are satisfied. A conditional block criterion gives infinite-horizon
expected bounds despite arbitrarily long noncontracting intervals.
For commanded finite-state hidden Markov chains we prove matching
$\epsilon^2M^{-2/d}$ prediction bounds at every integer budget and every
positive time, for every positive refresh rate, with explicit dimension
and mixing constants. A singular command measure gives a noninteger
resolution exponent. In a paired noisy contact experiment with unknown
additive calibration, we prove a finite-sample risk law combining contact
orders, calibration variance, and an anisotropic finite-label floor.
A finite reference decision process also yields an implementable policy
whose cost is compared with unrestricted optimal control. The foundational
results on predictive quotients, charged experiment reductions, information
loss, and exponential transforms are retained with complete proofs.
\end{abstract}
