\section{Introduction}\label{sec:v2-introduction}
A finite representation of an acquired prediction has two distinct costs.
Its range must resolve the distribution produced by the experiment, and its
successive values must be computable without recovering discarded data.
The first cost is geometric. The second depends on the stability of the
entire intervening evolution, not necessarily on contraction at each step.
A third issue arises when the target is an unknown physical parameter:
finite representation must then be separated from statistical uncertainty.

We treat these questions in one resource convention. After each acquisition
an observer retains one of $M$ labels. The current command and report can
be used during that update but are not subsequently available. A future
query is selected after the label has been formed. Known model constants
and a fixed program are read-only; an unknown calibration is not. Numerical
workspace, the description of the program, and acquisition costs are
specified separately. These distinctions are necessary even when all
state spaces are finite-dimensional.

Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-resolution} gives upper and lower resolution bounds
for a controlled predictive realization. Its upper bound uses the
Lipschitz constants of exact suffix evolutions along the actual visible
history. Its lower bound uses small-ball bounds for subprobability measures
of the actual acquired prediction. The two bounds agree when covering and
acquired concentration have the same scale and a normalized stability sum
is bounded. The theorem applies without a Euclidean state space, without a
density of acquired states, and without one-step uniform contraction.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-blocks} gives an infinite-horizon criterion under
intermittent contraction, with neither independence of the good blocks nor
a deterministic bound on the waiting time between them.

For the commanded hidden Markov experiment of
Section~\ref{sec:refreshing}, we remove the restriction
$\gamma\ge4/5$. For every $d\ge1$ and every $\gamma>0$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-refresh} proves the matching law
\[
 c_{d,\gamma}\epsilon^2M^{-2/d}
 \le R_{M,t}^{\rm av}\le\mathfrak R_{M,\infty}^{\rm max}
 \le C_{d,\gamma}\epsilon^2M^{-2/d},
 \qquad t\ge1,
\]
with explicit constants, for every integer $M$ and $0\le\epsilon\le1/4$.
The proof uses a projective metric in which positive likelihood
multiplication is an isometry. A second experiment permits arbitrarily
long noncontracting intervals and satisfies the corresponding uniform
\emph{expected} checkpoint law. A Cantor command law gives a noninteger
acquired dimension and a different exponent. These applications distinguish
mixing, the acquisition interface, and the prediction metric.

Our statistical result concerns a different loss, measured against the
unknown parameter rather than the full-history prediction oracle. In an
actual paired Gaussian experiment, an unknown additive calibration is
measured by a second noisy channel. For coordinate contact orders $k_j$,
parameter widths $a_j$, and effective noise levels $v_j$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-singular} proves
\[
 \inf_{\text{$M$-label procedures}}\sup_{\theta,\eta}
 \E_{\theta,\eta}\norm{\widehat\theta-\theta}_2^2
 \asymp_d
 \sum_{j=1}^d\min\{a_j^2,v_j^{2/k_j}\}
 +\max_{1\le\ell\le d}
       \left(\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{(i)}}{M}\right)^{2/\ell}.
\]
The constants do not depend on the contact orders. The calibration
measurements and their noise are part of the experiment. Both the
statistical and the representation obstructions are proved for the same
procedure class. This supplies a matched singular-statistical result,
rather than identifying a derivative rank with a statistical rate.

Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-control} constructs a policy from a finite
reference Markov decision process and compares its actual performance
with the unrestricted optimal policy. This is not inferred from a
same-controller simulation inequality. The exact optimal value is used
only in the proof, not as an oracle available to the finite controller.

\subsection{Antecedents and the relation to the preceding manuscripts}
Quantization of nonlinear filters, approximation of belief-state decision
processes, and finite-memory control have established theories
\cite{PagesPham2005,SaldiYukselLinder2017,KaraYuksel2022,SSSM2022}.
The projective contraction estimate used below is classical
\cite{Bushell1973}. The concentration argument and the statistical testing
arguments are likewise classical tools. The issue studied here is their
joint implication for \emph{one retained-label process}, including a
matching acquired-law converse, intermittent evolution, singular command
measures, and a separately charged calibration experiment.

The finite-horizon attainable-geometry theorem of \cite{QiA1} already
separates global geometry, acquired mass, and causal compatibility.
Corollary~\ref{cor:v2-a1} recovers precisely that transfer statement;
its model-specific collision geometry is not reproved by giving it a new
name. Our infinite-horizon and nonuniform results require different
stability hypotheses and include examples outside its bounded-format
finite-horizon setting. The contact experiments of \cite{QiA2} already
give local Gaussian comparisons and finite-precision score reductions.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-singular} concerns an actual noisy measurement experiment
with unrestricted unknown additive calibration; it does not identify
independent measurement noise with a correlated raw-data score.

Sections~\ref{sec:v2-resolution}--\ref{sec:v2-control} contain the new
quantitative development. Section~\ref{sec:v2-resources} specifies its
implementation costs, and Section~\ref{sec:v2-comparison} compares the
statements with the nearest literature. All the foundational propositions,
proofs, and boundary examples of the first edition are retained in the
appendices. They fix the measurable, decision-theoretic, and asymptotic
interfaces but are not additional claims of novelty.

\section{Acquired geometry and nonuniform causal resolution}
\label{sec:v2-resolution}
\subsection{The experiment, the metric, and the resource}
Let $D_t$ be separable metric spaces with metrics $d_t$ and their Borel
sigma fields. A prepared experiment has a known initial predictive state
$s_0$ and jointly Borel updates
\[
 s_{t+1}=F_t(s_t,a_t,y_{t+1}),\qquad
 F_t(D_t,a,y)\subset D_{t+1}.
\]
All actions admitted at a time have a common continuation interface on
$D_t$. The maps must be defined at every representative used below, not
only almost surely under the exact experiment. The current input may
include a publicly observed mode, but its past realizations are not
available to a decoder unless charged to the retained label.

The physical prediction is $z_t=\Pi_t(s_t)\in\R^{q_t}$, with
\[
 \norm{\Pi_t(s)-\Pi_t(s')}_2\le K_t d_t(s,s').
\]
For a finite query distribution, coordinates are multiplied by the square
roots of the query probabilities. The excess squared prediction loss is
then exactly $\norm{z_t-\widehat z_t}_2^2$. This follows by conditioning
on the full acquisition history; the cross term with the future outcome
has conditional mean zero. The query is drawn only after compression.

Fix an exploration law independent of coding randomness and denote the
resulting acquired law of $z_t$ by $\mu_t$. A lower bound always refers to
this law. An upper bound may hold for a larger family of feedback policies;
in that case its stability hypotheses must hold for that family. A
parameter-uniform statement requires the same implemented update for all
parameters, as in Section~\ref{sec:emulation}. Alternatively, a Bayesian
unknown parameter can be included in the predictive state.

For each $t$, choose a fixed Borel quantizer $q_t:D_t\to C_t\subset D_t$,
where $|C_t|\le M_t$ and
\[
 d_t(s,q_t(s))\le r_t\quad(s\in D_t).
\]
Finite covers with a first-minimum tie rule give such maps. Let the
implemented update $\widetilde F_t$ take values in $D_{t+1}$ and have
uniform error at most $\eta_{t+1}$ at representatives. The machine is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-machine}
 \widehat s_0=s_0,\qquad
 \widehat s_{t+1}=q_{t+1}\widetilde F_t(\widehat s_t,a_t,y_{t+1}).
\end{equation}
Only the index of $\widehat s_t$ persists. A prescribed sequence of
arbitrary codebooks is not automatically a finite uniform program. An
infinite-time finite-program assertion below uses a fixed codebook, a
periodic finite list, or a separately specified finite algorithm generating
and decoding the codebooks.

For a fixed visible input word, let $\Phi_{j,t}:D_j\to D_t$ be the exact
composition of updates from time $j$ to time $t$, and $\Phi_{t,t}=\id$.
Assume specified measurable bounds $\Lambda_{j,t}\ge0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-suffix}
 d_t(\Phi_{j,t}(x),\Phi_{j,t}(x'))
 \le\Lambda_{j,t}d_j(x,x'),\qquad \Lambda_{t,t}=1.
\end{equation}
The bounds can depend on that word. Taking an uncountable supremum and
silently declaring it measurable is unnecessary. The hypothesis is on
specified measurable bounds valid for every pair of states in the domain.

\subsection{A resolution theorem}
Write $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$. If $\nu\le\mu_t$ is a subprobability measure of
mass $b$, put
\[
 A_\nu(r)=\sup_{c\in\R^{q_t}}\nu(B(c,r)),\qquad
 \mathcal L_t(M)=\sup_{\nu\le\mu_t,\ r>0}
          r^2[b-MA_\nu(r)]_+.
\]
One may use any collection of specified submeasures in place of the
supremum over all of them; no existence of an optimizing submeasure is
asserted or required.

\begin{theorem}[Acquired-law resolution and exact-suffix stability]
\label{thm:v2-resolution}
Under the preceding hypotheses the following statements hold.

\textup{(i)} Every checkpoint encoder with at most $M$ labels, including
independent public and private coding randomization, has expected excess
at least $\mathcal L_t(M)$. Consequently every one-machine procedure with
stage budgets $M_t$ has maximum expected excess at least
$\sup_t\mathcal L_t(M_t)$.

\textup{(ii)} Along every admitted common input word the machine
\eqref{eq:v2-machine} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-hybrid}
 d_t(s_t,\widehat s_t)
 \le\sum_{j=1}^t\Lambda_{j,t}(r_j+\eta_j).
\end{equation}
In particular its expected checkpoint excess is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-mean-upper}
 K_t^2\left\|\sum_{j=1}^t\Lambda_{j,t}(r_j+\eta_j)\right\|_{L^2}^2
 \le K_t^2\left(\sum_{j=1}^t
               \|\Lambda_{j,t}\|_{L^2}(r_j+\eta_j)\right)^2.
\end{equation}
The probability and all the norms in this assertion are those of the
actual run of the implemented controller.

\textup{(iii)} Suppose the same integer budget $M$ is used at every time.
Let $\psi_t(M)>0$. If fixed constants $a,A,b>0$ give covers with
$r_t+\eta_t\le A\psi_t(M)$ and acquired submeasures $\nu_t$ of mass
at least $b$ such that
\[
 M A_{\nu_t}(a\psi_t(M))\le b/2,
 \qquad
 \sup_{t,M}\sum_{j=1}^t
 K_t\|\Lambda_{j,t}\|_{L^2}\frac{\psi_j(M)}{\psi_t(M)}\le G<\infty,
\]
then checkpoint expected risks are bounded below by
$ba^2\psi_t(M)^2/2$, and one causal machine has expected excess at most
$A^2G^2\psi_t(M)^2$ at each time. Thus the optimal maximum expected
checkpoint risk is comparable to $\sup_t\psi_t(M)^2$. Replacing the
$L^2$ bounds by bounds valid for all histories gives the analogous
worst-history upper assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Condition on an independent public seed. Averaging a randomized decoder
conditional on its label and that seed cannot increase squared loss.
There are at most $M$ prediction centres $c_1,\ldots,c_M$. Randomized
assignment cannot improve on the distance to the nearest centre. The
$\nu$-mass of their $r$-balls is at most $MA_\nu(r)$; all remaining mass
has squared error at least $r^2$. This proves (i), first for a fixed seed
and then after integration. The seed is independent of the acquired law.
Every causal label is a checkpoint encoding of the entire prefix, so the
same lower bounds hold before taking the infimum over causal machines.

For (ii), freeze the actual input word and set
$w_j=\Phi_{j,t}(\widehat s_j)$ for $0\le j\le t$. Then $w_0=s_t$,
$w_t=\widehat s_t$, and
\[
 w_{j-1}=\Phi_{j,t}
   \bigl(F_{j-1}(\widehat s_{j-1},a_{j-1},y_j)\bigr).
\]
The two arguments compared here belong to $D_j$. Their distance is at
most $r_j+\eta_j$. Applying \eqref{eq:v2-suffix} and summing the $t$
triangle inequalities proves \eqref{eq:v2-hybrid}. No hypothetical
controller is reevaluated at a different state: all suffixes use the same
actual word. The Lipschitz readout and the triangle inequality in $L^2$
give \eqref{eq:v2-mean-upper}. The finite-sum argument also applies at
each time of an infinite run. Substituting the hypotheses of (iii) into
(i) and (ii) proves (iii). In particular the conclusion is
$\sup_t\E L_t$, not $\E\sup_t L_t$.
\end{proof}

The theorem deliberately separates the upper and lower geometries.
A small-dimensional chart can supply a converse without covering the
whole domain; conversely, a small cover does not imply that the experiment
acquires mass at that scale. Certified perturbations of the model enter
$\eta_j$ only when measured on the stated domain in its stated metric.

\begin{corollary}[Finite-horizon anisotropic transfer]\label{cor:v2-a1}
Assume the global geometry, acquired rectangular marginals, and causal
compatibility hypotheses \textup{(G)}, \textup{(A)}, \textup{(C)} of the
attainable-geometry theorem in \cite{QiA1}. Its all-integer-budget
checkpoint profile and its finite-horizon causal conclusion follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-resolution}. Explicitly, with its notation,
\[
 Q_t(M)=\max_{1\le\ell\le p_t}
 \left(M^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{t,i}\right)^{2/\ell},
 \qquad
 d_t(s_t,\widehat s_t)\le C\sum_{j=1}^t
       \left(\prod_{i=j}^{t-1}L_i\right)Q_j(M_j)^{1/2}.
\]
The resulting comparison constants are those of the fixed horizon.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The bounded-format thin-rectangle covering lemma of \cite{QiA1}, whose
statement and proof are reproduced in the source supplement, gives
$r_t(M)\le C\sqrt{Q_t(M)}$ with representatives in the reachable set.
To recall the integer-budget step, its entropy bound is
$C\sum_{j=0}^{p_t}r^{-j}\prod_{i\le j}s_{t,i}$.
At $r=L\sqrt{Q_t(M)}$, all terms of positive degree sum to at most
$M/2$ for a sufficiently large fixed $L$. For $M\ge2C$ the constant
term fits the other half; the remaining finitely many budgets are
covered by one point and the bound $\sqrt{Q_t(M)}\ge s_{t,1}/M$.

For each acquired rectangular marginal in (A), the mass in a union of
$M$ balls of radius $r$ is at most
$C_\ell Mr^\ell/\prod_{i\le\ell}s_{t,i}$ of its normalized mass.
Choose $r$ a sufficiently small multiple of
$(\prod_{i\le\ell}s_{t,i}/M)^{1/\ell}$. A fixed fraction of the
actual subprobability remains. The bounded left inverse of the physical
query map and the bounded coordinate transformation transfer this
lower bound to prediction error. Maximizing over $\ell$ gives $cQ_t$.
Zero products need no converse; if every scale vanishes the domain is a
singleton. Causal compatibility gives
$\Lambda_{j,t}\le\prod_{i=j}^{t-1}L_i$.
Apply (ii), and use the finite number of times for the maximum-risk bound.
This reproduces the transfer conclusion; the experiment-specific proofs
of (G) and (A) remain those of \cite{QiA1}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Intermittent contraction}
Let $\mathcal H_t$ contain the visible past through time $t$. The next
criterion is useful when some steps are not contracting at all.

\begin{theorem}[A conditional block criterion]\label{thm:v2-blocks}
Assume each same-input map is nonexpansive. Fix an integer $b\ge1$ and
$0<p\le1$, $0\le\rho<1$. Starting at any deterministic time $j$, partition
the subsequent inputs into consecutive blocks of length $b$. For each
block starting at $k$, suppose there is an event $E_k\in\mathcal H_{k+b}$
with
\[
 \Pr(E_k\mid\mathcal H_k)\ge p,
 \qquad \Lip(\Phi_{k,k+b})\le\rho\ \text{on }E_k,
\]
and the block is nonexpansive otherwise. These hypotheses refer to the
actual policy or hold uniformly over the specified policy family.
Set $q=1-p(1-\rho^2)$. Then valid suffix bounds satisfy
\[
 \|\Lambda_{j,t}\|_{L^2}
 \le q^{\lfloor(t-j)/b\rfloor/2},\qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^t\|\Lambda_{j,t}\|_{L^2}
 \le\frac{b}{1-\sqrt q}.
\]
Thus a fixed radius-$r$ codebook, with numerical defect at most $\eta$,
gives
\[
 \sup_{t\ge1}\E d_t(s_t,\widehat s_t)^2
 \le\frac{b^2(r+\eta)^2}{(1-\sqrt q)^2}.
\]
No independence between blocks is required. If every block contracts by
$\rho$ on every history, the corresponding pathwise bound is
$b(r+\eta)/(1-\rho)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a complete block use the factor
$Z_k=\rho\1_{E_k}+\1_{E_k^c}$. Its conditional second moment is at
most $q$. For the first $m$ complete blocks after $j$,
\[
 \E\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}Z_{j+\ell b}^2\le q^m.
\]
Indeed the product through the penultimate block is measurable at the
start of the last, so conditional expectation removes the last factor
with multiplier at most $q$; induction completes the argument. The
unfinished final block is nonexpansive. Products of these factors
therefore give the required measurable suffix bounds. Each possible
value of the integer part occurs at most $b$ times in the sum over $j$.
Sum the geometric series and apply \eqref{eq:v2-mean-upper} with the
identity readout. The deterministic assertion is the same calculation
without expectations.
\end{proof}

Arbitrarily long strings of unsuccessful blocks have positive probability
in many examples satisfying this theorem. A uniform expected checkpoint
bound therefore cannot be reinterpreted as a pathwise bound by suppressing
the norm in the hypothesis. Conversely, expansion at individual steps is
allowed by Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-resolution} whenever the exact suffix bounds
are summable; the nonexpansive block criterion is a sufficient condition,
not the definition of the general class.

\section{Positive and intermittent hidden Markov experiments}
\label{sec:v2-hmm}
\subsection{Projective stability}
For positive probability vectors define
\[
 h(p,q)=\max_i\log(p_i/q_i)-\min_i\log(p_i/q_i).
\]
It is a metric on the interior of the simplex. For a positive likelihood
vector $r$, normalization of $r_ip_i$ does not change these log-ratios
apart from a common additive constant. Bayes multiplication is consequently
an isometry in $h$.

\begin{lemma}[Positive-matrix contraction]\label{lem:v2-projective}
For a positive matrix $A$, put
\[
 R(A)=\max_{i,j,k,l}\frac{A_{ik}A_{jl}}{A_{il}A_{jk}},
 \qquad \vartheta(A)=\frac{\sqrt{R(A)}-1}{\sqrt{R(A)}+1}.
\]
The normalized map $p\mapsto Ap/(\1^{\mathsf T}Ap)$ is
$\vartheta(A)$-Lipschitz in $h$. A nonnegative matrix mapping positive
vectors to positive vectors is nonexpansive. Also
$\norm{p-q}_1\le h(p,q)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We include the finite-dimensional argument for the classical projective
estimate \cite{Bushell1973}. Write $p_i=e^{x_i}/\sum e^{x_l}$.
The derivative of $\log\sum_k A_{ik}e^{x_k}$ is the probability row
$w_{ik}=A_{ik}e^{x_k}/\sum_l A_{il}e^{x_l}$. For two rows $i,j$, the
ratio $w_{ik}/w_{jk}$ has maximum divided by minimum at most $R(A)$.
If probability vectors $u,v$ have ratios in $[m,M]$, with $m\le1\le M$,
convexity of $|x-1|$ gives
\[
 \|u-v\|_{\TV}\le\frac{(M-1)(1-m)}{M-m}.
\]
Subject to $M/m\le R$, the right side is at most
$(\sqrt R-1)/(\sqrt R+1)$: increasing the endpoint ratio and then
putting $M=Rm$ reduces the maximization to $m=R^{-1/2}$.
Consequently the difference of two derivative rows applied to a vector
$v$ is at most $\vartheta(A)\operatorname{osc}(v)$ in absolute value.
Integrate along the segment joining two log-coordinate vectors and take
the maximum difference between rows. Normalization subtracts a common
constant and does not change oscillation. This proves the contraction.
For nonnegative matrices the same derivative argument uses only
$\|w_i-w_j\|_{\TV}\le1$.

For the last assertion join $q$ to $p$ by the normalized exponential
path with logarithmic increment $v_i=\log(p_i/q_i)$. Its derivative has
coordinates $p_i(s)(v_i-\sum_l p_l(s)v_l)$, whose $\ell^1$ norm is at
most $\operatorname{osc}(v)$. Integrating gives the assertion.
\end{proof}

Use the experiment and physical terminal probe in
Section~\ref{sec:refreshing}, now with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-range}
 d\ge1,\qquad 0<\gamma\le1,\qquad 0\le\epsilon\le1/4.
\end{equation}
All known constants and both binary report signs retain their original
meaning. Write $n=d+1$, $\tau=1/4$, and define
\begin{align}
 \rho_{d,\gamma}&=\frac{1-\gamma}{1-\gamma+2\gamma/n},&
 B_\epsilon&=\log\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon},&
 R_\epsilon&=\frac{B_\epsilon}{1-\rho_{d,\gamma}},\label{eq:v2-Hconst}\\
 D_{d,\gamma}&=2^{-d}(5/4)^{d+2}(n/\gamma)^{d+1}\cosh^{2d}(1).
 \label{eq:v2-Dconst}
\end{align}
Let $v_d$ denote the volume of the Euclidean unit ball in $\R^d$.

\begin{theorem}[The memory law for every positive refresh rate]
\label{thm:v2-refresh}
For \eqref{eq:v2-range}, every integer $M\ge1$, and every $t\ge1$,
\begin{align*}
 c_{d,\gamma}\epsilon^2M^{-2/d}
 &\le R_{M,t}^{\rm av}\le R_{M,t}^{\rm max}
       \le C_{d,\gamma}\epsilon^2M^{-2/d},\\
 c_{d,\gamma}\epsilon^2M^{-2/d}
 &\le\mathfrak R_{M,\infty}^{\rm av}
       \le\mathfrak R_{M,\infty}^{\rm max}
       \le C_{d,\gamma}\epsilon^2M^{-2/d}.
\end{align*}
One possible choice is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-explicit-constants}
 c_{d,\gamma}=\frac{\tau^2}{2d}(2v_dD_{d,\gamma})^{-2/d},\qquad
 C_{d,\gamma}=\frac{\tau^2}{d}\left(\frac{64}{3}\right)^2
                    (1-\rho_{d,\gamma})^{-4}.
\end{equation}
One deterministic fixed-codebook machine gives the upper bounds for all
histories and all times. The lower bounds use independent uniform
commands and allow independent coding randomization. At $\epsilon=0$,
one label is exact. If the terminal amplitude is multiplied by $\epsilon$,
all bounds acquire the additional factor $\epsilon^2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $a=1-\gamma$, $b=\gamma/n$. The transition matrix has diagonal
entries $a+b$ and off-diagonal entries $b$. Its maximum cross-ratio is
$((a+b)/b)^2$, attained by two distinct diagonal positions. Lemma
\ref{lem:v2-projective} gives coefficient $a/(a+2b)=\rho_{d,\gamma}$.
Multiplication by the likelihood does not alter $h$. Since $P_\gamma\nu=\nu$,
\[
 h(F(p,u,y),\nu)\le\rho_{d,\gamma}h(p,\nu)+B_\epsilon.
\]
Thus $D=\{p:h(p,\nu)\le R_\epsilon\}$ is an invariant compact subset
of the positive simplex. The initialization $\nu$ belongs to $D$.

In log-odds coordinates $x_i=\log(p_i/p_0)$, the domain is exactly
$\operatorname{osc}(0,x_1,\ldots,x_d)\le R_\epsilon$ and lies in
$[-R_\epsilon,R_\epsilon]^d$. Divide this cube into $k^d$ equal cells,
$k=\lfloor M^{1/d}\rfloor$. Select one point of $D$ in each occupied
cell. Two points in a cell have projective distance at most
$4R_\epsilon/k$, so the fixed codebook has radius
$r_M\le8R_\epsilon M^{-1/d}$. Its points are admissible normalized
beliefs, although they need not be reachable at a particular time.
Quantize after each exact update. The suffix bound is
$\rho_{d,\gamma}^{t-j}$, whence
\[
 \sup_t h(p_t,\widehat p_t)
 \le\frac{8B_\epsilon}{(1-\rho_{d,\gamma})^2}M^{-1/d}.
\]
For $0\le\epsilon\le1/4$, integration of $2/(1-\epsilon^2)$ gives
$B_\epsilon\le(8/3)\epsilon$. The physical loss is
$(\tau^2/d)\norm{p_{t,+}-\widehat p_{t,+}}_2^2$ and is at most
$(\tau^2/d)h(p_t,\widehat p_t)^2$. This proves the upper constant.

For the converse the pre-report coordinates satisfy $q_i\ge\gamma/n$.
The command-to-posterior Jacobian in
Lemma~\ref{lem:refresh-density}, with this floor in place of $m_*$,
gives a density bounded by $D_{d,\gamma}\epsilon^{-d}$ for $p_{t,+}$.
For clarity, the actual joint density of command and sign is
$2^{-(d+1)}D_y$ and
\[
 |\det D_uF(p,u,y)_+|
 =\frac{q_0\prod_{i=1}^dq_i}{D_y^{d+1}}
       \epsilon^d\prod_{i=1}^d\sech^2u_i.
\]
The odds $p_i'/p_0'=q_i(1+y\epsilon\tanh u_i)/q_0$ make each sign's
map injective. Changing variables, summing the two signs, and integrating
over every previous history gives exactly \eqref{eq:v2-Dconst}; no
conditioning cost is removed. The mass of $M$ Euclidean balls of radius
$r$ is at most $Mv_dD_{d,\gamma}\epsilon^{-d}r^d$. Take
$r=\epsilon(2Mv_dD_{d,\gamma})^{-1/d}$.
At least half the mass remains, and the physical factor is $\tau^2/d$.
This proves the lower constant and, by checkpoint relaxation, its causal
version. At zero signal the posterior stays $\nu$. Scaling the terminal
probe scales its squared loss, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The dependence on mixing and dimension in
\eqref{eq:v2-explicit-constants} is part of the statement. Since
$1-\rho_{d,\gamma}=2\gamma/[n(1-\gamma)+2\gamma]$, the upper constant
is explicit even for weak refresh. The theorem is uniform in $M,t$ and
$\epsilon$ for each positive $\gamma$; it does not assert matching powers
of $\gamma$ in a simultaneous $\gamma\downarrow0$ limit. This is a
strict enlargement of the refresh interval, not a relabeling of the old
$\ell^1$ estimate. At zero refresh the finite-horizon theorem remains
available whenever its separate geometry and stability hypotheses hold.

\subsection{An experiment with unbounded noncontracting intervals}
At each time first draw an observed mode $I_t$, independently of the
hidden state and of all past variables, with $\Pr(I_t=1)=p>0$.
For $I_t=1$ execute the preceding refresh and commanded observation.
For $I_t=0$ apply any prescribed permutation of the hidden states and
return an uninformative fair sign. Commands are independent uniform for
the converse. The upper construction accepts every admitted command word.
The mode is read in the current update, then discarded except for its
effect on the retained label. The terminal probe is unchanged.

\begin{corollary}[Intermittent-refresh expected resolution]
\label{cor:v2-intermittent}
In this experiment, for every integer $M\ge1$,
\[
 p c_{d,\gamma}\epsilon^2M^{-2/d}
 \le\mathfrak R_{M,\infty}^{\rm av}
 \le\frac{\tau^2}{d}
 \frac{(64/3)^2\epsilon^2M^{-2/d}}
 {(1-\rho_{d,\gamma})^2
  (1-\sqrt{1-p(1-\rho_{d,\gamma}^2)})^2}.
\]
The same lower bound holds at every positive checkpoint. The upper bound
uses one fixed codebook. For $0<p<1$, arbitrarily long noncontracting
intervals are admitted.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Permutations fix $\nu$ and preserve $h$; hence the invariant domain $D$
and its cover from the preceding proof work in both modes. A good step
contracts by $\rho_{d,\gamma}$, a bad step is an isometry.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-blocks} with $b=1$ gives the upper bound. Conditional
on the current good mode, the density calculation in the preceding proof
is uniform over the prior history. The good-mode submeasure has mass $p$
and density at most $pD_{d,\gamma}\epsilon^{-d}$. The same balls leave
at least $p/2$ of its mass, proving the lower bound. Independent Bernoulli
modes permit every finite string of bad steps.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Discrete and singular acquisition}
For a finite command alphabet of size $K$, a fixed time $t$ has at most
$(2K)^t$ command/sign histories in the ordinary refreshing experiment.
Its checkpoint prediction is therefore exactly encodable at that budget.
This elementary fact precludes a positive $M^{-2/d}$ lower bound for all
$M$ at fixed $t$ in that different experiment.

A singular command law has a different outcome. Let $d=1$, $\gamma=1$,
and let $u=2V-1$, where $V$ is the standard middle-thirds Cantor random
variable. Set $s=\log2/\log3$. The hidden state is freshly prepared at
each step, but the binary command observation and the terminal probe are
still physical observations as above.

\begin{proposition}[A singular acquired dimension]\label{prop:v2-cantor}
For this experiment there are constants $0<c<C<\infty$ such that
for every $t\ge1$, $M\ge1$, and $0\le\epsilon\le1/4$,
\[
 c\epsilon^2M^{-2/s}\le R_{M,t}^{\rm av}
 \le\mathfrak R_{M,\infty}^{\rm max}
 \le C\epsilon^2M^{-2/s}.
\]
Here worst histories use commands in the support of the prescribed Cantor
law. One fixed codebook attains the upper bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A level-$m$ Cantor cylinder has length $3^{-m}$ and mass $2^{-m}$.
At that scale an interval of length comparable to $3^{-m}$ meets only
a bounded number of cylinders. Thus the mass of an interval of radius
$r$ is at most $C r^s$, while the support has a cover by $2^m$ intervals
of length $3^{-m}$. Affine rescaling to $[-1,1]$ changes only constants.

For either sign the posterior coordinate is
$(1+y\epsilon\tanh u)/(2+y\epsilon\tanh u)$.
Its derivative in absolute value is
$\epsilon\sech^2u/(2+y\epsilon\tanh u)^2$, between two fixed positive
multiples of $\epsilon$ when $\epsilon>0$. Each sign therefore gives a
bi-Lipschitz image at scale $\epsilon$. The actual sign weight is between
$3/8$ and $5/8$. The sum of the two image measures has concentration at
most $C(r/\epsilon)^s$; each image has the corresponding covers. Allocate
half the available centres to each image, adjusting the fixed constant
for the finitely many budgets below four. The acquired-law lower bound
and this cover give the asserted rate. Since $\gamma=1$, the new
posterior does not depend on the old label. The same quantizer is an exact
one-step causal construction at every time. At $\epsilon=0$ it is constant.
\end{proof}

Neither the domain dimension of the command nor a Jacobian rank alone
determines these three rates. For a mixture with finitely many additional
atoms, assign one centre to each atom and use the remaining centres for
the singular component; the submeasure converse retains its actual
mixture weight. More generally Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-resolution} uses the
full concentration profile without assigning a single dimension.

\section{Singular statistical resolution with measured calibration}
\label{sec:v2-statistics}
We now change the target and the loss explicitly. Let
$\theta\in\Theta=\prod_{j=1}^d[0,a_j]$, $a_j>0$, and let
$\eta\in\R^d$ be an unrestricted unknown additive calibration. Fix
integers $k_j\ge1$. The experiment consists of the $2d$ observations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-paired}
 Y_j=\theta_j^{k_j}+\eta_j+\sigma_jZ_j,
 \qquad W_j=\eta_j+\tau_jZ'_j,
 \qquad 1\le j\le d,
\end{equation}
with independent standard normal variables and known
$\sigma_j,\tau_j\ge0$. A procedure observes the pair, retains one of
$M$ labels, and decodes an estimate of $\theta$. Only the label and
independent public coding randomness are available to the decoder.
Both $Y$ and $W$ count as acquisitions. In particular $W$ is not a free
read-only value of $\eta$.

Put $v_j=(\sigma_j^2+\tau_j^2)^{1/2}$, and let
$a_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge a_{(d)}$ be the ordered widths. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-statprofile}
 S=\sum_{j=1}^d\min\{a_j^2,v_j^{2/k_j}\},\qquad
 Q_M=\max_{1\le\ell\le d}
       \left(M^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{(i)}\right)^{2/\ell}.
\end{equation}
The risk here is squared parameter error, not the prediction excess used
in the preceding sections.

\begin{theorem}[A matched contact, calibration, and label law]
\label{thm:v2-singular}
For the actual experiment \eqref{eq:v2-paired}, write
\[
 \mathcal R_M=\inf_{\text{$M$-label procedures}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta,\eta\in\R^d}
      \E_{\theta,\eta}\norm{\widehat\theta-\theta}_2^2.
\]
For every integer $M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-statlaw}
 c_d(S+Q_M)\le\mathcal R_M\le2S+8dQ_M,
\end{equation}
where one may take
\[
 c_d=\tfrac12\min\left\{\frac3{128},
   \min_{1\le\ell\le d}\frac12(2\omega_\ell)^{-2/\ell}\right\},
\]
where $\omega_\ell$ denotes the volume of the Euclidean unit ball in $\R^\ell$.
The constants are independent of $a_j,k_j,\sigma_j,\tau_j,M$ and the
unknown calibration. The upper construction is deterministic. The lower
bound allows independent public and private randomization.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Estimation before compression.}
The actually observed differences $D_j=Y_j-W_j$ have laws
$N(\theta_j^{k_j},v_j^2)$ independent of $\eta$. Define
\[
 \widetilde\theta_j=
  \bigl(\operatorname{proj}_{[0,a_j^{k_j}]}D_j\bigr)^{1/k_j}.
\]
For nonnegative $x,y$, $|x^{1/k}-y^{1/k}|\le|x-y|^{1/k}$.
Projection cannot increase the distance to $\theta_j^{k_j}$.
Concavity of $x^{1/k_j}$ and $\E Z^2=1$ consequently give
\[
 \E|\widetilde\theta_j-\theta_j|^2
 \le\min\{a_j^2,v_j^{2/k_j}\E|Z|^{2/k_j}\}
 \le\min\{a_j^2,v_j^{2/k_j}\}.
\]
This is a finite-sample bound, including degenerate zero-noise coordinates.

\emph{An integer-budget cover.}
Set $\delta=2\sqrt{Q_M}$ and partition coordinate $j$ into
$n_j=\max\{1,\lfloor a_j/\delta\rfloor\}$ equal intervals.
Let $\ell$ be the number of widths greater than $\delta$.
If $\ell=0$ the product of the $n_j$ is one. Otherwise
\[
 \prod_jn_j\le\prod_{i\le\ell}a_{(i)}/\delta^\ell
 \le M/2^\ell\le M.
\]
Every interval has length at most $2\delta$, so its midpoint product
codebook has squared covering radius at most $d\delta^2=4dQ_M$.
Quantize $\widetilde\theta$ to this codebook. The squared triangle
inequality proves the upper bound in \eqref{eq:v2-statlaw}.

\emph{The statistical obstruction with unknown calibration.}
Write $r_j=\min\{a_j,v_j^{1/k_j}\}$. Coordinates with $r_j=0$ contribute
zero and can be omitted from this step. For $\omega\in\{0,1\}^d$, set
\[
 \theta_j(\omega)=\tfrac12r_j\omega_j,\qquad
 m_j=(r_j/2)^{k_j},\qquad
 \eta_j(\omega)=-\frac{\tau_j^2}{v_j^2}m_j\omega_j.
\]
These are admissible alternatives in the same unknown-calibration
experiment. Adjacent vertices differ in the mean pair at coordinate $j$
by $(m_j\sigma_j^2/v_j^2,-m_j\tau_j^2/v_j^2)$.
Their Gaussian divergence is
\[
 \frac12\left(\frac{m_j^2\sigma_j^2}{v_j^4}
             +\frac{m_j^2\tau_j^2}{v_j^4}\right)
 =\frac{m_j^2}{2v_j^2}\le\frac18.
\]
A zero-variance channel in this expression has identical means and is
simply omitted. Pinsker's inequality bounds the variation distance by
$1/4$. Any estimate determines a test of bit $j$ by thresholding at
$r_j/4$. An incorrect test has squared coordinate error at least
$r_j^2/16$. Pair adjacent vertices and average: their average error
probability is at least $(1-1/4)/2=3/8$. Summing over coordinates gives
average squared risk at least $3\sum_jr_j^2/128$. Compression and
independent randomization cannot improve the testing distance. Since
maximum risk dominates this finite average, $\mathcal R_M\ge3S/128$.

\emph{The representation obstruction.}
Fix $\eta=0$ and put uniform probability on the parameter box. At each
fixed public seed an averaged decoder has at most $M$ centres. Regardless
of what noisy observations led to the label, its error at $\theta$ is
at least the distance to the nearest centre. Project onto the $\ell$
widest coordinates. The fraction of this rectangular uniform law covered
by $M$ balls of radius $r$ is at most
$M\omega_\ell r^\ell/\prod_{i\le\ell}a_{(i)}$.
Choose $r=(\prod_{i\le\ell}a_{(i)}/(2M\omega_\ell))^{1/\ell}$.
At least half the mass has squared error at least $r^2$. Maximize over
$\ell$ and integrate over the seed. This proves
$\mathcal R_M\ge[\min_\ell\tfrac12(2\omega_\ell)^{-2/\ell}]Q_M$.
Combining the two lower bounds by $\max(x,y)\ge(x+y)/2$ proves the result.
\end{proof}

The testing alternatives explain why the calibration variance adds to the
signal variance. Holding the nuisance artificially fixed in that step
would prove only the weaker obstruction from $\sigma_j$, and would miss
the cost of measuring calibration. The encoder never reads the unknown
nuisance. Its read-only constants are the specified noise levels, contact
orders, and parameter box.

For $d=1$, $a_1=1$, and $v_1\asymp N^{-1/2}$, the theorem gives
$\mathcal R_M\asymp N^{-1/k_1}+M^{-2}$. Hence the statistical amplitude
scale is $N^{-1/(2k_1)}$, and order $N^{1/(2k_1)}$ labels suffice to
reach it. When $k_1=2$, this recovers the quadratic-contact amplitude
scale in the physical-ray result of \cite{QiA2}, now with an explicit
finite-label constraint and an actually measured unrestricted additive
calibration in \eqref{eq:v2-paired}. It is a comparison of rates in these
specified experiments, not an assertion of their Le Cam equivalence.
The nonnegative branch is part of the parameter space: an even-power
observation on a signed branch would have an additional identifiability
obstruction.

\section{Optimization under a retained-label constraint}
\label{sec:v2-control}
The following result supplies a policy comparison in addition to the
common-controller statements of Section~\ref{sec:emulation}. Let $D$ be
a compact metric predictive-state domain and $\mathcal A$ a finite action
set. The report kernel is $g(dy\mid s,a)$ and the state update is
$F(s,a,y)\in D$. Assume these are Borel, and, uniformly in $a,y$,
\[
 0\le c(s,a)\le C_0,\quad
 |c(s,a)-c(s',a)|\le L_c d(s,s'),\quad
 d(F(s,a,y),F(s',a,y))\le l d(s,s'),
\]
\[
 \|g(\cdot\mid s,a)-g(\cdot\mid s',a)\|_{\TV}\le\kappa d(s,s').
\]
The current conditional expected physical cost is $c(s,a)$. The predictive
state is sufficient for future costs and experiments under all admitted
policies. Thus the belief-state dynamic program represents the optimum
among all history-dependent policies. Fix discount $0<\beta<1$ with
$\beta l<1$, and define
\[
 L_* =\frac{L_c+\beta\kappa C_0/(1-\beta)}{1-\beta l}.
\]
Let $\mathcal C\subset D$ have at most $M$ points and a radius-$r$ Borel
quantizer $q$. Assume the initial state is in $\mathcal C$; it can be
reserved as a centre. The finite reference decision process uses cost
$c(c,a)$ and next state $qF(c,a,y)$ with report distribution $g(dy\mid c,a)$.
It has an optimal stationary policy $\widehat a(c)$ because its state and
action spaces are finite. Its transition probabilities are the specified
integrals of these kernels; numerical approximation is addressed below.

\begin{theorem}[A finite reference policy versus unrestricted control]
\label{thm:v2-control}
Implement $a_t=\widehat a(\widehat s_t)$ in the actual experiment and
update $\widehat s_{t+1}=qF(\widehat s_t,a_t,y_{t+1})$. Let $V^*$ be
the unrestricted optimal discounted value and $J(\widehat a)$ the actual
cost of this retained-label controller. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-control-bound}
 0\le J(\widehat a)-V^*(s_0)
 \le2L_*\sum_{t\ge0}\beta^t\E d(s_t,\widehat s_t)
           +\frac{2\beta L_*r}{(1-\beta)^2}.
\end{equation}
In particular, if the fixed-codebook construction has
$\sup_t\E d(s_t,\widehat s_t)\le Gr$, the upper bound is
$2L_*Gr/(1-\beta)+2\beta L_*r/(1-\beta)^2$.
Only one of $M$ labels persists; no exact value function or full history
is an input to the implemented policy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exact Bellman operator on bounded Borel functions is
\[
 TV(s)=\min_a\{c(s,a)+\beta\int V(F(s,a,y))g(dy\mid s,a)\}.
\]
It is a contraction in the uniform norm. Starting at zero, its iterates
have values in $[0,C_0/(1-\beta)]$. If an iterate has Lipschitz constant
$L$, the next has constant at most
$L_c+\beta lL+\beta\kappa C_0/(1-\beta)$: compare the same report
argument first and then the two report laws, using the range bound on
$V$. The minimum over the same finite action set preserves the bound.
It follows that $V^*$ is $L_*$-Lipschitz, and each optimal action-value
function $Q^*(\cdot,a)$ is also $L_*$-Lipschitz.

Let $\widehat V$ be the finite reference value. At a centre, replacing
$F(c,a,y)$ by $qF(c,a,y)$ in $V^*$ changes a Bellman expression by at
most $\beta L_*r$. The finite Bellman contraction therefore implies
\[
 \sup_{c\in\mathcal C}|\widehat V(c)-V^*(c)|
 \le\frac{\beta L_*r}{1-\beta}=:D_0.
\]
For every centre and action, the exact action value and the finite
reference action value differ by at most
$\beta(L_*r+D_0)=\beta L_*r/(1-\beta)=D_0$.
Minimizing the latter consequently yields
\[
 Q^*(c,\widehat a(c))-V^*(c)\le2D_0.
\]
For an actual state $s$ and retained centre $c$, Lipschitz continuity of
both $Q^*$ and $V^*$ gives
$Q^*(s,\widehat a(c))-V^*(s)\le2L_*d(s,c)+2D_0$.

Along the actual trajectory, the conditional definition of $Q^*$ makes
the discounted sum of these residuals equal to
$J(\widehat a)-V^*(s_0)$. More explicitly the finite sum telescopes,
and its final term has absolute value at most
$\beta^{T+1}C_0/(1-\beta)$, tending to zero. Sum the bound and let
$T$ increase. This proves \eqref{eq:v2-control-bound}.
The policy is a finite table indexed by the retained representative.
The exact value entered only this comparison proof.
\end{proof}

A stationary centre may be reserved at a cost of one label: for $M\ge2$
use the preceding covers with $M-1$ centres and add $s_0$. A power-law
covering rate is unchanged up to its stated fixed factor; for $M=1$ the
bounded-cost estimate is available. If an implemented finite table is
within $\xi$ in each action value of the exact reference table, choosing
its minimizing action adds at most $2\xi/(1-\beta)$ to the bound.
This numerical certificate is separate from computing that table.
Theorems~\ref{thm:v2-resolution} and \ref{thm:v2-blocks} provide $G$
when their assumptions hold under the lifted policy. A prediction lower
bound is not a matching lower bound for every control cost; no such
identification is used here.

\section{Finite descriptions and numerical implementation}
\label{sec:v2-resources}
We distinguish five resource coordinates: persistent labels, program
description, transient workspace, numerical error, and physical acquisitions.
The first coordinate is determined by the resolution laws; the others
are not silently identified with it. We give a finite-description
construction for the positive-refresh upper bound, so its codebook does
not require an independently stored list of $M$ arbitrary real vectors.

Suppose $\gamma,\epsilon$ are rational known inputs and $\epsilon>0$.
Choose a rational $R'$ with $R_\epsilon\le R'\le2R_\epsilon$.
Such a number is computable from the displayed elementary formula.
For an integer $k\ge1$, set $\delta=R'/k$ and use the log-odds grid
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-grid}
 \mathcal J_k=\{j\in\{-k,\ldots,k\}^d:
       \operatorname{osc}(0,j_1,\ldots,j_d)\le k\},\qquad
 p_i(j)=\frac{e^{\delta j_i}}{1+\sum_{l=1}^d e^{\delta j_l}},
\end{equation}
with $p_0$ the reciprocal denominator. Its domain is the invariant ball
of radius $R'$.

\begin{proposition}[An implicit codebook]\label{prop:v2-program}
There is a finite-description codebook in this ball with at most $M$
representatives and projective radius at most $12R'M^{-1/d}$. Its
persistent state uses at most $\lceil\log_2M\rceil$ bits. Its read-only
description consists of $d$, the rational calibration, $R'$, an integer
$k$, and fixed algorithms for enumeration and elementary functions; it
contains no list of $M$ arbitrary real numbers. Certified update error
$\eta$ and quantization error at most $r+\eta$ give projective filtering
error at most $(r+2\eta)/(1-\rho_{d,\gamma})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $M\ge3^d$ choose
$k=\lfloor(M^{1/d}-1)/2\rfloor$. Then $(2k+1)^d\le M$ and
$k\ge M^{1/d}/6$. Round each log-odds coordinate toward zero in
multiples of $\delta$. The result remains in the oscillation ball:
when both signs occur, both extreme absolute values decrease; when all
coordinates have one sign, their extreme absolute value decreases.
Each coordinate changes by at most $\delta$, so projective distance
changes by at most $2\delta\le12R'M^{-1/d}$. For $M<3^d$ use only
$\nu$; its radius $R'$ is at most $3R'M^{-1/d}$.
Lexicographic enumeration of the finite integer set labels the grid.
The model and this enumeration are finite descriptions of all its
representatives. The stated error is the geometric-series estimate,
with total one-step defect $r+2\eta$.
\end{proof}

All log-odds and softmax functions in this construction are computable
on their compact domains. With digitally represented commands, interval
arithmetic can evaluate the update and finitely many distances until a
representative within $r+\eta$ is certified, for any prescribed positive
rational $\eta$. The fixed positive slack ensures termination even at a
tie; exact comparison of two computable reals is not required. This is
an existence and accuracy construction, not a uniform polynomial-time
claim. The bound charges the counter needed to enumerate the grid and
the precision of the computed numbers as transient workspace. The
required precision can depend on $d,\gamma,\epsilon,M,\eta$.

A command rounded with coordinate error at most $h$ changes each log
likelihood by at most $\epsilon h/(1-\epsilon)$ and hence changes the
posterior projective distance by at most
$2\epsilon h/(1-\epsilon)$. This supplies an explicit additional
one-step defect. It does not replace the analog acquisition law in the
lower bound by a finite alphabet while keeping the same converse. The
sampling law and the numerical interface must be declared separately.
For a noncomputable real calibration, an exact read-only real convention
would be a different resource model. The finite-description assertion
above uses rational calibration and a numerical certificate instead.

The paired singular experiment uses $2d$ scalar measurements and one
terminal label. Its box codebook is generated by $d$ integer subdivision
counts rather than an arbitrary centre list. Computing the differences
and roots uses transient numerical workspace. A controller in
Theorem~\ref{thm:v2-control} additionally has a read-only policy table;
its construction and precision are not included in the label count.
