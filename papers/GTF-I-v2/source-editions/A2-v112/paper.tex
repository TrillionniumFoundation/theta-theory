\begin{abstract}
The second jet of a squared-distance function determines a normal compression of the inverse information matrix. For a calibrated Hankel family, its ambiguity is dual to failure of quadratic multiplication of normal polynomials. We determine the codimension of this failure locus for independently designed subspaces. In a stable dimension range we classify the entire determinantal scheme when it has expected codimension: it is integral and Cohen--Macaulay in one regime, and reduced with one nondegenerate component and $2^{L-1}$ signed secant components at the wall. In the excess regime the signed secant varieties are precisely the maximal-dimensional components; they are rational and generically smooth in the determinantal scheme. Removing the two secant points identifies the wall with saturation of a residual multiplication map. We obtain the fundamental cycle in expected codimension and local small-singular-value exponents at smooth real native strata. An exact positive realization criterion transfers these results to synchronized quadratic images and yields the sharp single-contact recovery threshold. The physical boundary experiment, quantitative Poisson comparison, and a deterministic finite-precision score reduction connect multiplication rank to local information loss. Finite-sample estimation and complementary contact results are retained in the appendices.
\end{abstract}
