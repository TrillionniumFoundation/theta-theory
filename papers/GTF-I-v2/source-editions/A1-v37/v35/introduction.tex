\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
An experiment does not produce every posterior law in its ambient
statistical model. It produces a family constrained by the likelihoods
that can actually be acquired. This distinction is decisive when the
future tests become nearly linearly dependent. The dimension at an
exact dependence and the dimension away from it do not determine how
much information can be retained at an intermediate memory budget.
A quantitative answer must identify the scales that are both visible
to future tests and attained by past observations.

We give such an answer for positive finite-horizon monomial experiments.
The principal result is a two-sided law for optimal prediction regret
at every integer label budget, uniform through all additive collisions
of the exponents in a compact chamber. Its invariant is a truncated
family of maximal Vandermonde volumes of the formal future exponents.
The truncation is imposed by acquisition, not by the ambient test space.
The same law holds for a single causal transducer charged after each
report, rather than only for separately chosen checkpoint encoders.
It determines persistent-bit requirements and resolution phases along
arbitrary orders of contact with collision loci.

The mathematical issue is attainment. Divided differences describe a
confluent test system, but do not show that acquired posterior laws
fill its initial flags. Quantization of a supplied rectangle would give
a rate, but does not supply that rectangle with positive probability
under the experiment. A local chart alone would not cover all histories,
and separate checkpoint covers need not be causally compatible. We prove
the requisite attainable tangent, uniform flag minorization under the
actual acquisition law, whole-image covering estimate, and reachable
raw-moment recursion. These are the four steps connecting the algebraic
collision data to the optimal causal memory law.

The nearby spectral theory describes all singular values of clustered
Fourier--Vandermonde matrices \cite{BDGGY2021}, and partial-clustering
estimates also yield superresolution minimax laws \cite{BDGY2020}.
The connection is exact at the level of comparison scales:
Proposition~\ref{prop:v35-exterior-spectrum} identifies our determinant
volumes with cumulative singular-value products of an auxiliary Fourier
matrix on the same exponent labels. Theorem~\ref{thm:resolution-main}
then identifies which of those products are attained with positive
probability and retained by one causal controller. Corollary~\ref{cor:v35-fixed-future}
exhibits three different acquired laws for the same future spectrum.
The comparison in Section~\ref{sec:v35-spectrum} keeps the observation
bandwidth, reconstruction noise and persistent-label budget distinct.

\subsection{The experiment and the retained resource}
Let $I=[l,u]\subset[0,\infty)$, $l<u$, and let $\mu$ be a probability
with support $I$. Write
\[
 A=\{0=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_{r-1}=D\},\quad
 W_A=\operatorname{span}\{t^{a_i}:0\le i<r\},\quad h_A(m)=|mA|,
\]
where $mA$ consists of sums of $m$ elements of $A$ and $0A=\{0\}$.
A finite detector has positive cells spanning $W_A$ and summing to one.
On each trial a command $g\in[\eta,1-\eta]^J$ accepts raw cell $k$
with probability $g_k$. Acceptance reports $k$; rejection gives one
common failure report. All trials have the same latent parameter, and
every report is counted. Exact information is studied on $I$; the
uniform collision theorem below uses $I=[0,1]$.

After each report a predictor retains one of $M$ labels. It reads only
its preceding label, the current command and the current report.
Fresh private coins may be used and discarded, but no past command
tape or persistent seed is available outside the label. The clock,
known calibration and fixed program are read-only. At a selected
checkpoint, an independent query is revealed and its remaining trials
are executed. Loss is squared prediction excess over the full-history
predictor. The mean criterion is the maximum of the unconditional
checkpoint risks of one common controller, not the expectation of a
pathwise maximum. Definition~\ref{def:monomial-risks-v24} also gives
the distinct worst-history criterion. One checkpoint and its future
query are evaluated in a run; those future trials are part of the horizon.

\subsection{The collision invariant and the main theorem}
Fix $N\ge2$ and a compact subset $K$ of the open chamber
$0<a_1<\cdots<a_{r-1}$. One fixed coefficient matrix of column rank
$r$ defines cells $k_j^a$ with a positive lower bound on
$K\times[0,1]$. Commands are independent and uniform on one fixed
cube, and the full-support prior is fixed; it may be singular. Choose
$H\ge\max\{1,N\max_{a\in K}a_{r-1}\}$ and put
\[
 \Lambda_m^+=\{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^r:|\alpha|=m\}\setminus\{me_0\},
 \quad x_\alpha=H^{-1}\alpha\cdot a,\quad q_m=|\Lambda_m^+|.
\]
Formal labels are retained when their values coincide. Let
$\mathcal V_{m,\ell}$ be the largest product of pairwise distances
among $\ell$ such nodes, with $\mathcal V_{m,1}=1$. Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 p_{n,m}&=\min\{n(r-1),q_m\},\\
 \Psi_{n,m}(M,a)&=\max_{1\le\ell\le p_{n,m}}
                   (\mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a)/M)^{2/\ell},\\
 \Xi_N(M,a)&=\max_{1\le n<N}\Psi_{n,N-n}(M,a).
 \end{split}
\]
Zero volumes contribute zero. The query menu consists of the ordered
products of one fixed attainable failure-factor basis. The definitions
and zero-pivot conventions are made explicit in
Section~\ref{sec:collision-geometry}.

The theorem is uniform jointly in the integer budget and the calibration.
It is not an interchange of limits at a collision. Its constants may
depend on the fixed prior, detector and horizon; no uniformity over all
full-support priors or growing horizons is asserted. The transducer
may use the known calibration. Fixed real program data are not counted
as history-dependent persistent labels.

The exact continuous-state dimension at checkpoint $(n,m)$ is
\[
                 d_A(n,m)=\min\{n(r-1),h_A(m)-1\}.
\]
The attainable binomial tangent has $n(r-1)+1$ distinct monomials.
Strict mixed-moment positivity pairs it with the future tests for every
fixed full-support prior; normalization loses exactly one direction.
At collisions, the analogous argument pairs this tangent with every
initial complete confluent Newton flag. The inverse-function argument
includes kernel coordinates and failure evidence, so gives lower mass
under the joint command-and-report law, not under a substituted law
conditioned on a chosen report word.

For the upper bound, bounded-format real geometry controls the entire
scaled attainable image inside its thin rectangle, with the covering
products stopping at the acquired dimension. The reachable representatives
are updated in raw moments. Positive report probabilities give a
Lipschitz bound with no inverse exponent gaps, and the finite-horizon
error recurrence realizes all checkpoints with one label process.
The interpolation, real entropy inequality and filtering error recurrence
are classical mechanisms; their role and the experiment-level inputs
are separated in Section~\ref{sec:v33-comparison}.

\subsection{Consequences for memory phases}
The bit law is the upper envelope
\[
 B_*(\varepsilon,a)=
 \max_{\substack{1\le n<N\\1\le\ell\le p_{n,N-n}}}
 \left[\frac\ell2\log_2\frac1\varepsilon+
             \log_2\mathcal V_{N-n,\ell}(a)\right]_++O(1),
\]
with zero volumes interpreted as in Corollary~\ref{cor:intrinsic-bits}.
Thus the rank gives the eventual slope at fixed calibration, whereas
the nonzero volume profile determines the intervening resolutions.
Theorem~\ref{thm:collision-tree} computes these phases from pairwise
orders of collision by a finite allocation rule on their nested clusters.
Its stated finite-power comparison hypothesis includes analytic paths
and arbitrarily high tangency orders. The determinant law itself requires
no regularity of a path; see Remark~\ref{rem:v35-flat-path}.

For example, take $A_{u,v}=\{0,1,2+u,3+v\}$, $N=5$, and the positive
detector of Corollary~\ref{cor:two-parameter}. Set
$\rho=\max(|u|,|v|)$ and
$\tau=\min(|u|,|v-u|,|v-2u|)$. Uniformly near the intersection,
\[
 \Xi_5(M,u,v)\asymp
 \max\{M^{-1/3},\rho^{1/2}M^{-1/4},
                      (\rho^2\tau)^{2/9}M^{-2/9}\}.
\]
The peak dimensions are six at the intersection, eight on its three
collision lines away from the intersection, and nine elsewhere.
Along $u=\theta$, $v=\theta+\theta^k$, $k\ge2$, the crossover
budgets have orders $\theta^{-6}$ and $\theta^{-(8k-2)}$.
These are consequences in the original per-report experiment, not
conclusions inferred from a choice of numerical precision.

\subsection{Realization theory and the general finite-cell algebra}
The controller representation is secondary to the attained classification,
but can be stated more generally. On an arbitrary standard Borel latent
space, let finitely many nonnegative Borel cells sum to one and keep
prescribed independent command laws. The acceptance/failure channel
defines an auxiliary experiment $\mathsf E$, indexed by the raw cell.
Its finite-output postprocessing region is
\[
              \mathscr A_M(\nu)=\{\mathsf E U:U:\Omega\to\Delta_M
                                      \text{ is Borel}\}.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:v34-blackwell} identifies this as a Blackwell
region and derives its Bayes support interpretation and common-submeasure
constraint. Raw cells that fail cannot be assigned independent failure
updates. This classical statistical interpretation is part of the
realization theory, not a new comparison-of-experiments principle.

For $A_n(i)\in\mathscr A_M(\nu_n)$ the conditional state laws satisfy
\[
 q_{n,j}(t)=\sum_{i,k}q_{n-1,i}(t)k_k^a(t)A_{n,kj}(i).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v33-moment-program} proves both necessity and
sufficiency and gives the attained formula
\[
 \mathfrak R_{M,N}^{a,\mathrm{av}}
 =\min_A\max_n\left\{B_n(a)-
     \sum_{i,q}\omega_{nq}
       \frac{\mu(q_{n,i}H_{nq}^a)^2}{\mu(q_{n,i})}\right\}.
\]
Zero occupancies and zero-evidence histories are included. All
prior-dependent coefficients use cell moments of degree at most $N$.
There are $TM^2J$ transition entries and at most
$M\binom{n+J-1}{J-1}$ formal occupancy coefficients at time $n$,
where $T=N-1$. The functions are an offline description of state laws,
not additional retained variables. The offset $B_n$ is independent of
the controller because acquisition is prescribed. Evaluating it and
solving the nonconvex minimum are separate computational questions.

The evaluation identity $\mathcal F_m(k^a)=W_{mA}$ in the monomial
case connects this finite-cell algebra to the future test space; it
does not prove attainment of that space. The positive two-cell example
in Proposition~\ref{prop:v34-two-cell} makes the distinction explicit.
Classical finite-action purification \cite{DWW1951} gives deterministic
realization of the entire mean-risk vector for atomless command laws.
For scalarized mean risks, optimal failure updates have affine comparison
regions, with ties specified also for atomic laws. Neither assertion
is a minimax exchange, a purification of worst-history risk, or a
removal of a persistent public seed. At fixed $M$, compact collision
transfer holds in the same coefficient domain. Its all-budget risk
scale is supplied by the separate attained classification above.

\subsection{Organization}
Sections~\ref{sec:experiment}--\ref{sec:collision-phases} give the
complete exact-information and collision proofs and their memory
consequences. Section~\ref{sec:v33-moments} develops finite-cell
realization. The finite compatibility, approximation and arbitrary

Section~\ref{sec:v33-instance} has matching global bounds over all
stochastic encoders. Its horizon is two, so it is an exact realization
example, not a claim about a multi-checkpoint causal trade-off.

the complete structural, exact-kernel, uncertainty, effective, graph,
occupation and regenerative developments, with their own hypotheses.
Its block-charged and public-descriptor protocols are not used to infer
the main per-report theorem.
