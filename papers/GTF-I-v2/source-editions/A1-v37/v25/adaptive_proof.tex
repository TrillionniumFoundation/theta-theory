\section{A converse under adaptive order selection}\label{sec:v25-adaptive-proof}
\subsection{Regular acquired boxes}
The inverse chart used to acquire a flag has two-sided Jacobian bounds.
Recording the upper bound as well as the earlier lower bound permits
arbitrary measurable restrictions of histories.

\begin{lemma}[One regular box for all prefixes]\label{lem:v25-regular-box}
At an edge's active checkpoint put $p=p_e$. For every calibration and
node ordering there is a measurable subset $\Omega_e$ of the first
$n_e$ command cube such that its uniform-command measure is bounded
below by a positive uniform constant. On the all-failure word, the
pushforward of this restricted command measure to
$z_e=(\E[g_i^{a^{(e)}}\mid h])_{i\le p}$ has a density bounded above
and below by positive uniform constants on a translated $p$-box of
fixed positive side length. For every $\ell\le p$ with
$d_{e,\ell}>0$, its pushforward to $(d_{e,i}z_{e,i})_{i\le\ell}$
has density at most $C/D_{e,\ell}$. The same $\Omega_e$ works for all
these prefixes. No density of the latent prior is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $u_0$ be the common interior binomial command tuple from
Lemma~\ref{lem:newton-attainment}, and let $b=n_eJ_e$ be the command
dimension. That lemma gives a positive uniform least row singular value
for $J=D(z_{e,1},\ldots,z_{e,p})(u_0)$, including exact collisions,
and uniform first- and second-derivative bounds near $u_0$.
Take a matrix $K$ whose rows are an orthonormal basis of $\ker J$.
The square map
\[
 F(u)=(z_{e,1}(u),\ldots,z_{e,p}(u),K(u-u_0))
\]
has an invertible derivative at $u_0$ with uniformly bounded norm and
inverse norm: its two row spaces are orthogonal. On a uniform interior
ball, its derivative differs from the central derivative by at most
half the latter's least singular value. The quantitative inverse
argument therefore supplies a translated product box $B\times C$,
of fixed positive side lengths, whose inverse lies inside that ball.
Here $\dim B=p$ and $\dim C=b-p$; a zero-dimensional factor has
volume one. On this inverse image both $\|DF\|$ and $\|(DF)^{-1}\|$
are uniformly bounded. Consequently
\[
 0<c_0\le |\det DF^{-1}(z,w)|\le C_0<\infty.
\]
Set $\Omega_e=F^{-1}(B\times C)$. Change variables in the constant
uniform-command density and integrate all the $w$ coordinates. This
gives both density bounds on $B$ and the lower bound on the command
mass of $\Omega_e$. Integrate the last $p-\ell$ coordinates of $B$
and scale the first $\ell$ by their positive $d_{e,i}$. The Jacobian
of this diagonal scaling is $D_{e,\ell}$, proving the last bound.
At zero scales no inverse is taken. Compactness and the finitely many
node permutations make all constants uniform; no measure on latent
parameters was differentiated.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A common event before the order is selected}
Use the complete edge tapes of \eqref{eq:v25-risk-definitions}.
For each edge choose the box in Lemma~\ref{lem:v25-regular-box} with
its Leja order and full cap $p_e$. Let $\mathcal A_a$ be the event
that the first command block of every edge belongs to $\Omega_e$ and
that \emph{all} $T$ reports on the complete tape are failures. The
completion commands remain unrestricted. Conditional on all commands,
the probability of this report word is
\[
 \prod_{e\in E}\mu_e\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N_e}
                                  L_{e,i,\mathrm{fail}}\right).
\]
Each factor is an actual evidence, and this product belongs to
$[\kappa^T,1]$ for a common positive report margin $\kappa$.
Independence of the command tapes and the regular-box mass bounds give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-common-mass}
 \Prob(\mathcal A_a)\ge\beta>0
\end{equation}
uniformly in calibration. This event and its mass are independent of
the scheduler and coding seed. The use of unrevealed reports in defining
a subevent for a lower bound does not reveal them to the controller.

For any edge set $F$ and positive allocation $(\ell_e)_{e\in F}$ of
total $k$, define the potential first-block coordinates
\[
 X_{F,\ell}=(d_{e,i}z_{e,i})_{e\in F,\ i\le\ell_e},\qquad
 D_{F,\ell}=\prod_{e\in F}D_{e,\ell_e}.
\]
They are defined by the first-block commands and all-failure formula
whether or not that block has yet been revealed. On $\mathcal A_a$,
if $F$ is an active cut, these are precisely the corresponding
coordinates of the actual posterior. From the upper density bound in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v25-regular-box}, the product input law, and the bound
of one on the full word evidence, we obtain for every Borel set
$U\subset\R^k$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-restricted-density}
 \Prob\{\mathcal A_a,\ X_{F,\ell}\in U\}
                  \le \frac{C}{D_{F,\ell}}\operatorname{vol}_k(U).
\end{equation}
Indeed, change variables in every first block, integrate all complementary
coordinates and all completion commands, and bound the report likelihood
by one. The constants are uniform over the finite list of edge sets and
allocations. This is an upper bound for a restricted \emph{subprobability}
measure, not an independence claim conditional on an adaptive event.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-adaptive}]
If $E=\varnothing$ every query has a known answer. Otherwise every
permutation has a nonempty cut, and $Q=Q_G(M,a)>0$, since
$V_{e,1}=1$. The deterministic construction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:v25-fixed-order}, applied to an order minimizing
\eqref{eq:v25-width-profile}, proves the upper bound, including the
maximum-tape criterion. The middle inequality follows directly from
\eqref{eq:v25-risk-definitions}.

For the converse fix all independent coding seeds. The controller and
decoder are now deterministic functions of their admitted inputs. We
may allow the scheduler its full revealed past and allow the decoder
the finite order prefix as extra information. There are at most $v!$
possible complete vertex traces. For each possible permutation $\pi$,
choose a boundary $j(\pi)$ maximizing its cut profile and a positive
allocation attaining that profile. Denote its total by $k_\pi$ and
its Newton product by $D_\pi$. The factorial comparisons imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v25-bottleneck-volume}
 (D_\pi/M)^{2/k_\pi}\ge c_1 Q,
\end{equation}
with a uniform $c_1>0$. These choices depend on $M,a,\pi$, not on
which tape produces that trace.

At this fixed trace and boundary the decoder has at most $M$ query
prediction vectors. Apply the tensor recovery map and the appropriate
leading nonzero Newton inverse, then project to the selected coordinates.
These maps are defined on arbitrary centres with a uniform norm bound.
On $\mathcal A_a$, if the loss at that boundary is at most $r^2$,
then $X_\pi$ lies in the union of the resulting $M$ balls of radius
$C_2r$. The order prefix and its menu are fixed when $\pi$ is fixed;
there is no extra continuously indexed family of centres.
By \eqref{eq:v25-restricted-density}, without conditioning on the
trace event,
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\Prob\{\mathcal A_a,\ \text{trace}=\pi,
                       \max_{1\le j<v}\mathcal L_j\le r^2\}\\
 &\hspace{15mm}\le C_3 M r^{k_\pi}/D_\pi
               \le C_4(r^2/Q)^{k_\pi/2}.
 \end{split}
\]
The trace classes may be arbitrary measurable sets. Summing over them,
and taking $r^2=c_2Q$ with $c_2>0$ sufficiently small, makes the sum
at most $\beta/2$, since $1\le k_\pi\le\sum_ep_e$ and there are
at most $v!$ terms. Equation~\eqref{eq:v25-common-mass} gives
\[
 \E_\tau\sum_{j=1}^{v-1}\mathcal L_j(\tau,\xi)
 \ge \E_\tau\max_j\mathcal L_j(\tau,\xi)
 \ge \tfrac12\beta c_2 Q.
\]
All constants are independent of the fixed coding seeds. Integrate them
and use that the maximum of $v-1$ expectations is at least their sum
divided by $v-1$. Thus every controller satisfies
$\max_j\E_{\tau,\xi}\mathcal L_j\ge cQ$. Taking its infimum proves
the converse. Fixing decoder randomness above is legitimate: a finite
menu permits pre-sampling a seed for each possible query. Alternatively,
conditional averaging of decoder predictions only improves squared loss.
The tape law is independent of every such seed.

The full trace was only an index in a finite union bound. No future
trace or report is supplied to the implemented predictor, and no
conditional-history law was substituted for the actual acquisition law.
\end{proof}

The lower-bound constant may depend on the number of traces and on all
fixed block horizons. Thus the theorem is uniform across calibration
collisions and memory budgets, not an assertion uniform in graph size.
It compares optimal orders and finite-state predictors; it does not
provide a polynomial-time algorithm for constructing either.
