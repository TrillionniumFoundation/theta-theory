\begin{abstract}
We formulate a common foundation for controlled statistical experiments and
attainable prediction without assuming a finite-dimensional state, a Fisher
metric, or a large-deviation principle. Future tests define a minimal
predictive quotient whenever the required measurable realization exists.
Experiment reductions are implemented by parameter-independent causal
machines; their errors compose in total variation and their internal
states are charged to the memory budget. We prove a finite-state emulation
theorem that combines state quantization, numerical perturbation, and
feedback-uniform path-law comparison, including stopped experiments. A
separate quantization identity characterizes checkpoint squared-prediction
regret under the actual preparation law.
For a refreshing hidden Markov chain with continuously commanded binary
observations, we derive matching bounds of order
$\epsilon^2M^{-2/d}$ for every integer memory budget $M$, uniformly over
all positive times and through vanishing observation strength
$0\le\epsilon\le1/4$. One finite-state filter attains the upper bound;
the lower bound follows from the exact Jacobian of the physical posterior
map, with failure reports retained. If the terminal query is attenuated as
well, the order becomes $\epsilon^4M^{-2/d}$. We also establish the
appropriate posterior, relative-entropy, score, and exponential-transform
identities, and give explicit examples separating sufficiency, information,
causal memory, and exponential asymptotics. The abstract identities are
attributed to their classical antecedents; the quantitative results have
explicit model, preparation, and resource assumptions.
\end{abstract}
