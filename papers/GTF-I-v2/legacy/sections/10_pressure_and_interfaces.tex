\section{Exponential transforms and conditional limit interfaces}\label{sec:pressure}
\subsection{Random channels have nonlinear exponential pullbacks}
For a Markov kernel $G:X\rightsquigarrow Y$, bounded measurable $f:Y\to\R$,
and a fixed $\beta>0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exponential-pullback}
 \mathcal T_{G,\beta}f(x)
 =\beta^{-1}\log\int e^{\beta f(y)}G(dy\mid x).
\end{equation}
For a probability $P$ on $X$ put
$\mathcal P_{P,\beta}(h)=\beta^{-1}\log P(e^{\beta h})$.
These expressions concern finite experiments and bounded sources; no
long-time limit is presumed.

\begin{theorem}[Exponential composition and entropy duality]
\label{thm:pressure}
For $H:Y\rightsquigarrow Z$ and bounded $f$,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal T_{GH,\beta}f
 &=\mathcal T_{G,\beta}(\mathcal T_{H,\beta}f),\label{eq:exp-compose}\\
 \mathcal P_{PG,\beta}(f)
 &=\mathcal P_{P,\beta}(\mathcal T_{G,\beta}f),\label{eq:exp-push}\\
 \mathcal P_{P,\beta}(h)
 &=\sup_{Q\ll P}\{Qh-\beta^{-1}D(Q\|P)\}.
 \label{eq:entropy-dual}
\end{align}
The supremum is attained at
$dP^h/dP=e^{\beta h}/P(e^{\beta h})$. The source derivatives are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pressure-derivative}
 D\mathcal P_{P,\beta}(h)[u]=P^hu,
 \qquad
 D^2\mathcal P_{P,\beta}(h)[u,v]
 =\beta\Cov_{P^h}(u,v).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Exponentiate \eqref{eq:exp-compose}. Its right side becomes
$G(H(e^{\beta f}))$, which is the definition of the composed kernel's
exponential integral. Integrating the same identity against $P$ proves
\eqref{eq:exp-push}. For any $Q\ll P$ of finite entropy,
\[
 D(Q\|P^h)=D(Q\|P)-\beta Qh+\log P(e^{\beta h})\ge0.
\]
Rearrangement gives the upper inequality in \eqref{eq:entropy-dual}, and
$Q=P^h$ attains equality. Measures of infinite entropy do not improve the
supremum for bounded $h$. Finally boundedness permits differentiation of
the exponential integral in the directions $u,v$; one quotient rule gives
the mean and the second gives the covariance with the factor $\beta$.
\end{proof}

If $G$ is the deterministic map $g$, the exponential pullback is exactly
$f\circ g$. If $G$ is random, it is generally not $Gf$. Jensen gives
$\mathcal T_{G,\beta}f\ge Gf$, with equality precisely when $f$ is
constant $G(\cdot\mid x)$-almost surely at each relevant $x$.
The entropy variational principle is classical
\cite{DupuisWang2007}; the proof fixes its conventions for this experiment
category rather than asserting a new variational principle.

\begin{proposition}[Second-order chain rule for a random channel]
\label{prop:pressure-chain}
Put $h=\mathcal T_{G,\beta}f$ and
\[
 G_f(dy\mid x)=\frac{e^{\beta f(y)}G(dy\mid x)}{G(e^{\beta f})(x)}.
\]
Then the tilted joint law is $P^h(dx)G_f(dy\mid x)$ and
\begin{align*}
 D\mathcal T_{G,\beta}(f)[u]&=G_fu,\\
 D^2\mathcal T_{G,\beta}(f)[u,v]&=\beta\Cov_{G_f}(u,v),\\
 D^2\mathcal P_{PG,\beta}(f)[u,v]
 &=\beta\Cov_{P^h}(G_fu,G_fv)
   +\beta\E_{P^h}\Cov_{G_f}(u,v).
\end{align*}
The last expression is $\beta\Cov_{P^hG_f}(u,v)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Multiplying the two tilted densities cancels $G(e^{\beta f})(x)$ and
produces the original joint law multiplied by $e^{\beta f(y)}$ and its
single total normalizer. Differentiation of the conditional normalizer
gives the first two formulas just as in
\eqref{eq:pressure-derivative}. Apply the ordinary second-order chain
rule to \eqref{eq:exp-push}. Its first term is the covariance of
conditional means and its second is the mean of conditional covariances.
The total covariance formula gives the final identity.
\end{proof}

The conditional covariance term is essential. Discarding it would treat a
random observation as a deterministic projection. This connects the
positive-kernel and source-response layers exactly, while keeping them
distinct from Fisher information for a model parameter.

\subsection{A finite risk-sensitive recursion}
Consider a known prepared predictive model with finite action sets, state
update $F_t(s,a,y)$, report kernel $g_t(dy\mid s,a)$, bounded observed
stage cost $c_t(s,a,y)$, and bounded terminal cost $v_N$. The cost here is
an optimization objective, not an undeclared memory resource. For
$\beta>0$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:risk-sensitive-dp}
 v_t(s)=\min_{a\in A_t}\frac1\beta\log\int
 \exp\{\beta[c_t(s,a,y)+v_{t+1}(F_t(s,a,y))]\}
 g_t(dy\mid s,a).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-horizon exponential dynamic programming]
\label{prop:exp-dp}
The value \eqref{eq:risk-sensitive-dp} is the least value of
$\beta^{-1}\log\E\exp(\beta[\sum_{j=t}^{N-1}c_j+v_N])$
over all causal strategies from $s$. A deterministic Borel minimizing
policy exists with a fixed tie rule.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $w_t=e^{\beta v_t}>0$. Conditional expectation over the next report
and then the future gives the multiplicative recursion
$w_t(s)=\min_a\int e^{\beta c_t(s,a,y)}w_{t+1}(F_t(s,a,y))g_t(dy\mid s,a)$.
At the last step this is the exact conditional objective. Induct backwards;
future randomized strategies cannot improve the already minimized
continuation, and mixing current actions produces a convex combination
of their positive objective values, no less than their minimum. Finite
Borel action comparisons give a Borel first minimizer. Taking logarithms
recovers \eqref{eq:risk-sensitive-dp}.
\end{proof}

This recursion needs a state sufficient for the future experiment and its
cost. It cannot be applied to an arbitrary lossy statistic without proving
closure. Compact infinite action sets would require appropriate existence
and selection hypotheses; they are not needed for this proposition.

\subsection{Deterministic LDP contractions}
We record a conditional interface for later work. Suppose a sequence
$P_n$ on a Polish space has a good large-deviation principle with speed
$a_n\to\infty$ and rate $I$. If $T$ is continuous into another Polish
space, its pushforward has rate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ldp-contraction}
 I_T(y)=\inf_{T(x)=y}I(x).
\end{equation}
For an open or closed target set its inverse image has the same property,
so the LDP lower and upper bounds give \eqref{eq:ldp-contraction}.
Goodness follows because each finite-rate infimum is attained on compact
sublevels and the sublevels of $I_T$ are continuous images of those of
$I$. This is the standard contraction principle
\cite{VaradhanNotes}. It is not a consequence of the raw experiment axioms.

For a random channel one needs the joint law, a conditional rate, or a
separate exponential approximation theorem. A small total-variation
simulation defect does not supply these ingredients; an explicit example
is given in Section~\ref{sec:boundaries}.

\subsection{Regular quadratic and labelled-phase interfaces}
\begin{proposition}[Quadratic constrained reduction]\label{prop:quadratic}
If $H>0$ and $A$ has full row rank, then
\[
 \inf_{Ah=z}\frac12h^{\mathsf T}Hh
 =\frac12z^{\mathsf T}(AH^{-1}A^{\mathsf T})^{-1}z.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Lagrange equations give
$h=H^{-1}A^{\mathsf T}(AH^{-1}A^{\mathsf T})^{-1}z$.
The constraint matrix in parentheses is positive definite. Substitution
gives the value, and strict convexity gives the unique minimizer.
\end{proof}

Only if a separate probabilistic theorem identifies $H^{-1}$ with the
appropriate Gaussian covariance does this become a covariance contraction
statement. Existence of an LDP alone is not that theorem.

For finitely many labelled components with common speed $a_n$, suppose
component rates are $I_j$ and their positive weights satisfy
$-a_n^{-1}\log w_{n,j}\to c_j$, with $\min_jc_j=0$. The joint
labelled rate is $c_j+I_j$ and the unlabelled rate is
$\min_j(c_j+I_j(x))$. To verify the bounds, bound a finite sum above by
the number of summands times its largest summand and below by each summand;
then apply the component LDPs. Compactness follows from the finite union
of component compact sublevels. Dynamic prediction may still require the
posterior over labels. A marginal log-sum is not automatically a Markov
semigroup.

\subsection{Closure and finite-dimensional memory}
For a Markov kernel $P$ and state map $q$, an exact reduced kernel $\bar P$
satisfies
\[
 P(f\circ q)=(\bar Pf)\circ q.
\]
Iterating this identity proves every finite-time identity, including the
corresponding exponential transforms. If it fails, the projected state need
not be Markov.
For a finite-dimensional linear evolution split as
\[
 \dot u=A_{PP}u+A_{PQ}v,\qquad
 \dot v=A_{QP}u+A_{QQ}v,
\]
variation of constants in the second equation gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:memory}
 \begin{split}
 \dot u(t)={}&A_{PP}u(t)+A_{PQ}e^{tA_{QQ}}v(0)\\
 &+\int_0^t A_{PQ}e^{(t-s)A_{QQ}}A_{QP}u(s)\,ds.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The unresolved initial-state term is not optional. Unbounded generators,
common form domains, and differentiable operator transports require
separate analytic results; the finite-dimensional identity does not prove
them. This is the precise boundary at which the history and memory
program of \cite{QiC2} can be connected to the present foundations.
