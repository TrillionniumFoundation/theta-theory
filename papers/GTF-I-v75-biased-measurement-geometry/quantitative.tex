\begin{abstract}
We determine the finite-use geometry of the full body of ordered binary
qubit measurements, including bias and singular effects. The adaptive
distance, with an arbitrary retained reference, is comparable to a sum
of two spectral endpoint scales and an angular scale. For eigenvalues
$0\le q\le p\le1$, the angular coefficient is
$(p-q)\sqrt{N/(p(1-p)+q(1-q)+N^{-1})}$. A scalar-overlap measurement
dilation gives a finite-distance upper bound for every common adaptive
tester. Extreme-eigenvalue tests, a bias-removing operation and binary
product estimates give a cancellation-free converse.
For every $N\ge1$ and every sufficiently small error $\delta>0$, the
covering number of the full effect body is comparable, with absolute
constants, to $N^2\log(N+2)\delta^{-4}$. The lower bound permits arbitrary
legal memoryless centres. Separated spectral regions near the projective
corner produce the logarithm; a rational spectral and stereographic code
attains the same order and charges bias, contrast and direction together.
Its exact encoder accepts rational Cartesian targets even when their
norm is irrational. The unbiased-slice weighted covering and boundary
contact theorems are retained, together with the preparation, coherent
and observable-readout description laws on which the development rests.
\end{abstract}
