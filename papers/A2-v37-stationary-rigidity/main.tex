\begin{abstract}
We determine convex obstacle configurations from pooled reciprocal
collision responses under unknown stationary launch laws. A global
stopped-Poisson inverse recovers the occupation field without knowing
its positive set. Consequently the scalar response has exactly the
translation periods of the underlying configuration, even when
periodicity is not assumed. For two or more independently translated
homothetic launch supports, collision-width deficits identify the
unknown scale ratios without matching components. Centered support
envelopes then recover the footprint and the obstacle configuration.
We characterize the full geometric ambiguity as a common translation
together with independent footprint translations by obstacle periods.
These exact results require convexity and separation, but neither
boundary smoothness nor a finite list of component species.
For bounded $C^{6,\beta}$ periodic classes, put $s=6+\beta$.
At $C^2$ accuracy $\nu$, a shrinking-layer construction with a fixed
uniform-disk launch law uses
$C\nu^{-(3s/2+1)/(s-2)}\log(C/(\nu\delta))$ attempted bits.
We prove the same polynomial lower bound for worst-case expected
attempts, including adaptive direction-controlled commands of
arbitrarily small length. The lower bound follows from cancellation
of the two curved boundaries of a collision sweep and a uniform
Hellinger estimate of order $\epsilon^{3/2}$.
Thus the stationary minimax power is sharp, with one logarithmic
factor between the bounds. Finite reconstruction also treats unknown
translated homothetic footprints and accounts separately for attempts,
center batches, distinct sites and command precision. Uniform finite
period decisions retain a positive nonperiod-patch margin; the exact
full-field period theorem does not require it. All solid starts and
misses are charged, and all earlier inverse and calibration results
are retained with complete proofs.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{Source history and assisted analysis}
The article contains all proofs required for its statements. Earlier
localized, finite-control and calibration results are retained in the
appendices; the reviewed source packages remain at their original
repository paths. They are not independently published articles.
AI-assisted analysis contributed to the collision-query, footprint and
global-response, registration and statistical lower-bound arguments,
as well as to the source checks. The named author is
responsible for the proofs and references. Finite diagnostics and a
successful build are reproducibility evidence and do not certify
continuum proofs or validate a physical apparatus.
