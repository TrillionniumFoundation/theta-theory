\section{Finite-flight contact recovery and coefficient sensitivity}
\label{sec:v6-finite-jets}
We continue in the identical-even contact class of
Section~\ref{sec:v5-jets}. The half-line inverse has a finite-flight
counterpart with constants uniform in the flight number at each fixed
jet order. Write $Y=(u,v)^{\mathsf T}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v6-finite-quadratic}
 H_j=a\begin{pmatrix}\coth(j\gamma)&-\csch(j\gamma)\\
              -\csch(j\gamma)&\coth(j\gamma)\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 L_i=\left(\frac{\sinh((j-i)\gamma)}{\sinh(j\gamma)},
           \frac{\sinh(i\gamma)}{\sinh(j\gamma)}\right).
\end{equation}
For $0\le i\le j$ put $\nu_i=L_iH_j^{-1}L_i^{\mathsf T}$.
For interior sites set
\[
 G_{ii}=\frac{\sinh(i\gamma)\sinh((j-i)\gamma)}{a\sinh(j\gamma)}.
\]
Let $w_0=w_j=1$ and $w_i=2$ for $1\le i<j$, and set
$\alpha_m=2/[2^m(m+1)(m!)^2]$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform finite-flight triangular inverse]
\label{thm:v6-finite-jets}
At each $j\ge1$ and $m\ge2$, the normalized coefficient
$f_{j,m-1}$ depends only on $g,\kappa,q_4,\ldots,q_{2m}$, is affine
in its last argument, and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v6-finite-diagonal}
 -\partial_{q_{2m}}f_{j,m-1}
 =\alpha_m\left\{\sum_{i=0}^j w_i\nu_i^m
            +4m\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}G_{ii}\nu_i^{m-1}\right\}
 \ge\frac{2\alpha_m}{a^m}>0.
\end{equation}
Fix $M$ and a compact finite-jet set with $g,\kappa$ in a compact
positive box. The triangular maps
$J_{j,M}:q\mapsto(f_{j,1},\ldots,f_{j,M-1})$ have locally
Lipschitz inverses on neighborhoods of their images whose radii and
Lipschitz constants can be chosen independently of $j$. In particular,
for coefficient errors at most $\delta$ in such a neighborhood,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v6-jet-error}
 \|J_{j,M}^{-1}(\widehat f)-q\|\le C_M\delta.
\end{equation}
Using the limiting inverse instead gives $C_M(\delta+\tau^j)$.
Every fixed finite-flight nonlinear germ also determines the analytic
even contact at known $g,\kappa$. No constant uniform in $M$ is claimed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite-degree argument in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-jets}
applies to every finite bridge. To calculate its diagonal, vary only
$q_{2m}$ while fixing lower jets. The first varying term in one flight
is $(u^{2m}+v^{2m})/(2m)!$. The linear stationary segment is $x_i=L_iY$,
so the envelope identity gives
\[
 \partial_{q_{2m}}W_j^{[2m]}(Y)
                   =\frac1{(2m)!}\sum_{i=0}^j w_i(L_iY)^{2m}.
\]
The interior sites occur in two lengths and the endpoints in one.
The first varying local length term has zero mixed derivative.
Therefore the edge product contributes nothing at degree $2m-2$.
The first varying diagonal of the interior Hessian is
$2(L_iY)^{2m-2}/(2m-2)!$. Differentiating its relative determinant gives
\[
 \partial_{q_{2m}}b_j^{[2m-2]}(Y)
            =-\frac2{(2m-2)!}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}G_{ii}(L_iY)^{2m-2}.
\]
The change of the stationary segment starts at degree $2m-1$.
The unvaried Hessian perturbation starts at degree two, since the graphs
are even. Thus that change, products of Hessian perturbations, and
products with lower nonconstant amplitude terms cannot enter the
displayed degree. The same observation justifies using the quadratic
sublevel to compute the coefficient under consideration.

The quadratic residual integral equals
$\pi d^2/\sqrt{\det H_j}$. Changing variables from the ellipse to a
disk, a linear functional $L_iY$ has squared metric length $\nu_i$.
The unweighted sublevel moment from action variation contributes
$-\alpha_m w_i\nu_i^m$ to the normalized coefficient. The
residual-weighted moment from amplitude variation contributes
$-4m\alpha_mG_{ii}\nu_i^{m-1}$. These constants follow from the
angular average $\binom{2k}{k}/4^k$ and radial integration, exactly as
in \eqref{eq:v5-moments}. The residual weight is zero on the sublevel
boundary, so its first variation has no boundary term. Addition proves
the identity in \eqref{eq:v6-finite-diagonal}.

All its summands are positive. Moreover
$\nu_0=\nu_j=a^{-1}\coth(j\gamma)\ge a^{-1}$, proving the lower bound.
This also proves that the coefficient is affine in the last jet:
finite-degree dependence excludes higher jets, and the displayed
derivative is independent of every higher and lower nonquadratic jet.
The coefficient maps and their fixed derivatives are uniformly bounded
on compact finite-jet sets by the differentiated threshold estimates.
One may apply those estimates to the local even polynomial graphs with
the specified finite jets on a sufficiently small common contact box;
no unprescribed higher jet can enter these coefficients.

Solve successively the equations
$f_{j,m-1}=d_{j,m}q_{2m}+P_{j,m}(q_4,\ldots,q_{2m-2})$.
The denominators $d_{j,m}$ are bounded away from zero uniformly in $j$
for $m\le M$. The derivatives of the numerators are uniformly bounded
on an enlarged compact finite-jet box. Induction gives bounded inverse
derivatives, and a common admissible coefficient perturbation radius:
at each step choose that radius small enough to keep the recovered
lower jets in the enlarged box. There are only $M-1$ steps. This proves
\eqref{eq:v6-jet-error}, without differentiating an infinite vector of
jets. The limiting bound follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-jets} and
the triangle inequality. Equality of all recovered Taylor coefficients
implies equality of analytic germs. It does not imply equality of
merely smooth graphs with identical Taylor jets.
\end{proof}

For $j=1$ the interior sum is empty and
$\nu_0=\nu_1=g\cosh\gamma/\sinh^2\gamma$, so
\eqref{eq:v6-finite-diagonal} reduces to \eqref{eq:v5-one-flight}.
At each fixed $m$, the two separated endpoint layers give
\[
 \sum_iw_i\nu_i^m\longrightarrow 2a^{-m}\coth(m\gamma),\qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^{j-1}G_{ii}\nu_i^{m-1}\longrightarrow a^{-m}D_m.
\]
Indeed the left layer has $\nu_i\to a^{-1}e^{-2i\gamma}$ and
$G_{ii}\to(1-e^{-2i\gamma})/(2a)$, and the right layer has its
reversed copy. The common geometric majorants supplied by endpoint
localization justify both sums. This recovers \eqref{eq:v5-diagonal}.

\begin{proposition}[Limiting versus one-flight diagonal sensitivity]
\label{prop:v6-sensitivity}
Let $D_m^\infty=|\partial_{q_{2m}}f_{m-1}|$ and
$D_m^1=|\partial_{q_{2m}}f_{1,m-1}|$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v6-sensitivity}
 \frac{D_m^\infty}{D_m^1}
  =(\tanh\gamma)^m\{\coth(m\gamma)+2mD_m\}
  =(\tanh\gamma)^m
       \{1+O_\gamma(m e^{-2(m-1)\gamma})\}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Divide \eqref{eq:v5-diagonal} by \eqref{eq:v5-one-flight} and use
$a\nu_1=\coth\gamma$. Expanding
$(e^{2k\gamma}-1)^{-1}=e^{-2k\gamma}/(1-e^{-2k\gamma})$
gives the stated remainder at fixed $\gamma>0$.
\end{proof}

At fixed lower jets and equal absolute error in the corresponding
coefficient, the inverse diagonal amplification is the reciprocal of
\eqref{eq:v6-sensitivity}. This comparison is unchanged by using ordinary
rather than factorial graph coefficients. It does not compare full
triangular condition numbers, the cost of extracting coefficients from
noisy probability values, or minimax risks of different experiments.
The fixed-$M$ uniformity in Theorem~\ref{thm:v6-finite-jets} is uniformity
in $j$, not in the number of recovered jets. Exact analytic continuation
in Corollary~\ref{cor:v5-analytic-rigidity} remains a uniqueness statement,
not a noise-stable continuation procedure.

\subsection{The channel index in geometric realization}
The unchanged leading data in Theorem~\ref{thm:v5-realization} are all
collision-order leading count and endpoint-metric coefficients of the
selected horizontal ground channel, with its reverse orientation.
The global free area is also fixed. Other selected channels need not
have the same leading data. This distinction can be checked within its
$M=2$ realization. Write
$h_s=1+s\sin^4\theta+z(s)\sin^6\theta$. The area constraint gives
\[
 z'(0)=-\frac{\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^4\theta\dd\theta}
              {\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^6\theta\dd\theta}=-\frac65.
\]
For the clear vertical channel on $3\Z\times4\Z$, put
$g_v=4-2h_s(\pi/2)$ and $r_v=(h_s+h_s'')(\pi/2)$.
Then
\[
 (g_v(0),g_v'(0))=(2,2/5),\quad
 (r_v(0),r_v'(0))=(1,3),
\]
\[
 (c_v(0),c_v'(0))=(3,-28/5),\qquad c_v=1+g_v/r_v.
\]
These equalities follow by differentiating the displayed trigonometric
polynomials. Positive curvature and clearance persist by openness.
Thus the oriented one-flight coefficient at its own onset satisfies
\[
 L_{1,v}=\frac1{2(12-\pi)\sqrt{c_v^2-1}},\qquad
 \left.\frac{\dd}{\dd s}\log L_{1,v}\right|_{s=0}=\frac{21}{10}.
\]
The horizontal realization is therefore a fibre of the complete leading
hierarchy of a specified channel, not a fibre of all channel-resolved
observations of the table. The general forward theorem and the full
ground-onset count law retain their original scope.
