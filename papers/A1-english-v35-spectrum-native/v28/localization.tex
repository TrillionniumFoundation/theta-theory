\section{Localization on independently acquired blocks}
\label{sec:v28-localization}
The common-event arguments above impose a regularity event on every
edge. For a sequence of graphs, its mass can decrease exponentially.
We instead require enough regular coordinates at one boundary which
every acquisition order must cross. The regular edges may differ from
one realized tape to another. The estimate is made before conditioning
on the visited set.

\subsection{A finite family of witnesses}
Retain the independent edge experiments and complete-tape construction
of Section~\ref{sec:v25-graph}. Fix a boundary $j$, $1\le j<v$, and
write $\mathscr S_j=\{S\subset V:|S|=j\}$. For each edge use the
first-block event $A_e=\mathcal A_e$ of
\eqref{eq:v26-first-block-mass}. More generally, the argument applies
to any first-block events with the following two properties. First,
for every positive acquired prefix of length $\ell$, its potential
scaled coordinate $X_{e,\ell}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v28-edge-density}
 \Prob\{A_e,X_{e,\ell}\in U\}
       \le \frac{h_{e,\ell}}{D_{e,\ell}}
                          \operatorname{vol}_{\ell}(U).
\end{equation}
Second, when $e$ is active, that potential coordinate is its actual
posterior coordinate on $A_e$, irrespective of the reports on other
edges. The first-block construction has both properties by
Lemma~\ref{lem:v26-first-block}. In particular, $h_{e,\ell}$ can be
chosen independently of exponent separation. Only positive
$D_{e,\ell}$ are used. No event is imposed on an unselected edge.

For $S\in\mathscr S_j$, let $\mathscr W_S$ be a nonempty finite
family of witnesses. A witness $w$ consists of a nonempty set
$F_w\subseteq\delta(S)$ and, for each $e\in F_w$, a positive
prefix length $\ell_{w,e}$ with $D_{e,\ell_{w,e}}>0$. Put
\[
 k_w=\sum_{e\in F_w}\ell_{w,e},\qquad
 d_w=\prod_{e\in F_w}D_{e,\ell_{w,e}},\qquad
 h_w=\prod_{e\in F_w}h_{e,\ell_{w,e}}.
\]
Let $q_S(h)$ be the finite query-probability vector, equipped with
its actual query-weighted Euclidean norm. Suppose that an affine map
$T_w$, defined on all prediction vectors, recovers
$(X_{e,\ell_{w,e}})_{e\in F_w}$ from $q_S(h)$ on
$\bigcap_{e\in F_w}A_e$ whenever $S$ is visited. Denote a bound on
the norm of its linear part by $L_w$. The tensor-probe recovery in
Section~\ref{sec:v25-graph} supplies such maps for the original
query menu. The next section supplies maps with controlled norms for
an enlarged menu containing those same probes.

The decoder receives its $M$-valued label and the visited-set query
descriptor. To record explicitly the cost of additional finite side
information, allow at most $J_S$ further descriptor values at $S$.
This is one side-information value determined at the boundary, before
the independent query is selected; it may depend on the history.
The original convention is
$J_S=1$. Independent coding seeds are not counted in $J_S$, since they
will be fixed before the tape is sampled. An unrecorded continuous
scheduler state is not among the admitted inputs.

Define the event
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v28-robust-event}
 E_j=\bigcap_{S\in\mathscr S_j}
          \ \bigcup_{w\in\mathscr W_S}\ \bigcap_{e\in F_w}A_e,
       \qquad \rho_j=\Prob(E_j).
\end{equation}
Thus every set at level $j$ has a witness consisting of regular edges.
The same tape need not supply the same witness at different sets.
Both $E_j$ and $\rho_j$ are defined without a scheduler or a coding
seed. Let $\omega_k$ denote the volume of the unit ball in $\R^k$.

\begin{theorem}[Localized acquisition bound]\label{thm:v28-localized}
Under the preceding hypotheses put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v28-witness-sum}
 Z_j(t)=M\sum_{S\in\mathscr S_j}J_S
              \sum_{w\in\mathscr W_S}
                   \frac{h_w\omega_{k_w}L_w^{k_w}}{d_w}
                                     t^{k_w/2}.
\end{equation}
Every admitted controller, including one whose scheduler reads its
entire revealed past, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v28-localized-risk}
 \max_i\E\mathcal L_i\ \ge\ \E\mathcal L_j
       \ \ge\ \int_0^\infty[\rho_j-Z_j(t)]_+\,dt.
\end{equation}
The assertion permits arbitrary measurable scheduling regions and
independent randomized encoders and decoders. The bound holds before
the infimum over a common controller is taken. All losses and event
masses refer to the unconditional acquisition law.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For one witness, independence of the complete edge tapes and
\eqref{eq:v28-edge-density} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v28-local-product}
 \Prob\{\bigcap_{e\in F_w}A_e,
                   (X_{e,\ell_{w,e}})_{e\in F_w}\in U\}
          \le (h_w/d_w)\operatorname{vol}_{k_w}(U).
\end{equation}
Indeed the selected subprobability measures have a product density
bounded by $h_w/d_w$. Every other edge, every completion command and
every completion report is integrated over its full law and contributes
one. This proves the assertion for Borel sets by domination of the
product measure, not by independence conditional on a visited set.

Fix all independent seeds. At $S$ the decoder has at most $MJ_S$
query-prediction vectors. Loss at most $t$ puts the true vector within
distance $\sqrt t$ of one of them. On the selected first-block events,
applying $T_w$ therefore puts the selected coordinates in at most
$MJ_S$ balls of radius $L_w\sqrt t$. Equation
\eqref{eq:v28-local-product} bounds the mass of these balls by
$MJ_Sh_w\omega_{k_w}L_w^{k_w}t^{k_w/2}/d_w$. Intersecting afterwards
with the event that $S$ is the visited set can only decrease this mass.
The side descriptor is dealt with by counting all its possible centres;
no independence of that descriptor is required.

On $E_j$, the actual visited set has at least one witness whose edges
all satisfy their first-block events. Taking a union over sets and
witnesses consequently gives
\[
        \Prob\{E_j,\mathcal L_j\le t\}\le Z_j(t).
\]
Thus $\Prob\{\mathcal L_j>t\}\ge[\rho_j-Z_j(t)]_+$. The bound
is uniform in the fixed seeds. Average them and apply Tonelli's theorem
to the nonnegative tail integral. This proves
\eqref{eq:v28-localized-risk}. A finite query menu permits all private
decoder seeds, for all labels and finite descriptors, to be fixed in
advance. Their law is independent of the tapes. The first inequality
concerns one fixed boundary and involves no exchange of expectation
and maximization.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Equal-dimensional witnesses]\label{cor:v28-power-tail}
If every witness has dimension $k>0$ and $Z_j(t)=AMt^{k/2}$, where
$A>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v28-power-tail}
       \E\mathcal L_j\ge
       \frac{k}{k+2}\rho_j^{1+2/k}(AM)^{-2/k}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $\rho_j=0$ the statement is immediate. Otherwise the positive
integrand in \eqref{eq:v28-localized-risk} is supported on
$[0,(\rho_j/(AM))^{2/k}]$. Its elementary integral is the right
side of \eqref{eq:v28-power-tail}.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:v28-localized} applies to the original tensor query
without changing that experiment. In that application its recovery
norms remain explicit; they are not asserted to be uniform in graph
size. The new feature is the event \eqref{eq:v28-robust-event}, not a
claim that finite-dimensional norm equivalence has size-independent
constants. Similarly, supplying an order prefix can give
$J_S\le j!$; its logarithmic cost then remains in the bound. Removing
this factor would silently change the decoder model.
