\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
A finite experiment produces an attainable family of posterior laws,
not an arbitrary subset of a statistical model. Two different questions
then arise. Which of these laws can be distinguished by the remaining
experiment? How much of the distinction can one causal finite-state
predictor retain? The second question cannot in general be answered
by choosing a separate quantizer at each time: all of those quantizers
must be realized by the same sequence of state transitions.

We study both questions for positive experiments whose likelihoods
belong to a finite span of monomials. Their future test spaces change
dimension when additive sums of exponents coincide. A dimension count
alone does not describe prediction near such a collision. We give an
all-budget resolution law in terms of the volumes of the formal future
exponents, and an exact finite-moment description of the common
controller in the same experiment. The former controls the attainable
geometry; the latter characterizes causal implementability without
introducing a variable for every history.

\subsection{The experiment and its retained resource}
Let $I=[l,u]\subset[0,\infty)$ with $l<u$, let $\mu$ be a probability
with support $I$, and write
\[
 A=\{0=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_{r-1}=D\},\quad
 W_A=\operatorname{span}\{t^{a_i}:0\le i<r\},\quad
 h_A(m)=|mA|.
\]
Here $mA$ is the set of sums of $m$ elements of $A$, and $0A=\{0\}$.
A finite detector has positive cells spanning $W_A$ and summing to one.
A command independently accepts each raw symbol with its specified
probability in $[\eta,1-\eta]$. Rejection is a single report. All reports
are counted, and every observation uses the same latent parameter.
The exact information results hold on $I$. For the uniform collision
results we use $I=[0,1]$ and the compact calibration family below.

After each report a predictor retains one of $M$ labels. It may read
only its preceding label, the current command and the current report.
Fresh private coins may be used and discarded; neither a historical
command tape nor a persistent seed is available outside the label.
The clock and known calibration are read-only. At a selected checkpoint
an independent query is revealed, and its remaining trials are executed.
The loss is squared prediction excess over the full-history predictor.
The mean criterion is the maximum of the unconditional checkpoint
risks of one common filter. Its precise quantifiers, and the distinct
worst-history criterion, are in
Definition~\ref{def:monomial-risks-v24}.

\subsection{Collision geometry}
Fix $N\ge2$ and a compact subset $K$ of the open chamber
$0<a_1<\cdots<a_{r-1}$. One fixed coefficient matrix of column rank
$r$ defines detector cells $k_j^a$ with a positive lower bound on
$K\times[0,1]$. Commands are independent and uniform on the fixed
cube. The prior is fixed and may be singular. Choose
$H\ge\max\{1,N\max_{a\in K}a_{r-1}\}$ and put
\[
 \Lambda_m^+=\{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^r:|\alpha|=m\}\setminus\{me_0\},
 \quad x_\alpha=H^{-1}\alpha\cdot a,\quad q_m=|\Lambda_m^+|.
\]
The formal labels are retained when their values coincide. Let
$\mathcal V_{m,\ell}$ be the largest product of pairwise distances
among $\ell$ such nodes, with $\mathcal V_{m,1}=1$. Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 p_{n,m}&=\min\{n(r-1),q_m\},\qquad
 \Psi_{n,m}(M,a)=\max_{1\le\ell\le p_{n,m}}
                  (\mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a)/M)^{2/\ell},\\
 \Xi_N(M,a)&=\max_{1\le n<N}\Psi_{n,N-n}(M,a).
 \end{split}
\]
Zero volumes contribute zero. The query menu consists of the ordered
products of one fixed attainable failure-factor basis. Definitions
and their zero-pivot conventions are given in
Section~\ref{sec:collision-geometry}.

The exact continuous-state dimension at a checkpoint is
$\min\{n(r-1),h_A(m)-1\}$, rather than the dimension of the whole
future test space. The proof identifies a monomial tangent inside the
attainable command cube and pairs it with every required future flag.
Strict mixed-moment positivity prevents a loss of rank for a fixed
full-support prior. A positive-mass acquired chart gives the converse;
a cover of the entire attainable image and reachable representative
updates give the common causal upper bound. The constants remain
uniform when formal future nodes merge. No division by a vanishing
exponent difference occurs in the state update.

As one illustration, for
$A=\{0,1,2+u,3+v\}$ and $N=5$, the complete calculation in
Section~\ref{sec:collision-phases} gives, for sufficiently small $u,v$,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \Xi_5(M,u,v)\asymp\max\{M^{-1/3},\rho^{1/2}M^{-1/4},
                       (\rho^2\tau)^{2/9}M^{-2/9}\},\\
 \rho=\max(|u|,|v|),\qquad
 \tau=\min(|u|,|v-u|,|v-2u|).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Thus distinct resolution regimes survive between the dimension at an
exact collision and the generic dimension. This is a law of the
experiment, not a consequence of a chosen numerical tolerance.

\subsection{Exact realization by a common controller}
For prescribed command laws $\nu_n$, let
$q_{n,i}(t)=\mathbb P(S_n=i\mid t)$. There is a compact convex
one-step body $\mathscr A_M(\nu_n)$, characterized explicitly in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v33-body}, for which the attainable functions satisfy
\[
 q_{n,j}(t)=\sum_{i,k}q_{n-1,i}(t)k_k^a(t)A_{n,kj}(i),
                       \qquad A_n(i)\in\mathscr A_M(\nu_n).
\]
The shared failure report couples the raw-cell rows of this body.
Replacing it by arbitrary stochastic matrices changes the experiment.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v33-moment-program} proves that this recursion is
both necessary and sufficient and gives the exact formula
\[
 \mathfrak R_{M,N}^{a,\mathrm{av}}
 =\min_A\max_n\left\{B_n(a)-
       \sum_{i,q}\omega_{nq}\frac{\mu(q_{n,i}H_{nq}^a)^2}
                                      {\mu(q_{n,i})}\right\}.
\]
The controller-independent term $B_n$ is the second moment of the
full-history prediction. The minimum is attained, zero occupancies
are included, and all prior-dependent quantities use moments of degree
at most $N$ in the detector cells. There are $TM^2J$ transition
entries and $M\binom{n+J-1}{J-1}$ formal occupancy coefficients at
time $n$, where $T=N-1$. These functions describe a controller's law
offline; only its label is retained during the experiment. Evaluation
of the fixed coefficients and global nonconvex optimization are
separate computational questions.

For atomless command laws, Theorem~\ref{thm:v33-pure} preserves the
entire vector of mean checkpoint risks by a deterministic replacement
of every private randomized controller. The finite-action purification
mechanism is classical \cite{DWW1951}; here it preserves the exact
implementability coefficients at every stage, including the coupled
failure coefficients. For a fixed weighted sum of checkpoint risks,
Theorem~\ref{thm:v33-polyhedral} further gives an optimal controller
whose failure updates are polyhedral decision regions, even for atomic
command laws. This scalarization result does not assert a minimax
exchange for the common checkpoint maximum. The coefficient domain
is unchanged at additive collisions, and optimal controller laws have
compact collision limits by Theorem~\ref{thm:v33-collision}.

\subsection{Finite certificates and the organization of the proofs}
The finite compatibility and command approximation results retain the
same per-report information restriction. Their command error bounds
hold for Borel transitions and do not require continuity of an optimal
policy. Theorem~\ref{thm:v33-precision} gives arbitrary certificate
precision together with the cardinality of the exhaustive search.
Section~\ref{sec:v33-instance} supplies an exact two-label experiment
with a continuous command cube, an explicit optimal controller and a
rational enclosure whose lower endpoint covers all stochastic encoders.
The certificate is an evaluation of a global theorem, not an estimate
from a sample of controllers.

Sections~\ref{sec:experiment}--\ref{sec:collision-phases} contain the
complete exact-information and collision proofs. Section~\ref{sec:v33-moments}
then characterizes the same common controller by finite moments.
Sections~\ref{sec:v32-compatibility}--\ref{sec:v32-certificates}
give finite compatibility, approximation and certificates. The
continuous-command example and the theorem-level comparison close the
main article. The companion volume retains the full graph, occupation,
regenerative, structural, exact-kernel and effective developments.
Its block-charged and public-descriptor protocols are stated separately;
none is used to infer the main per-report realization theorem.
