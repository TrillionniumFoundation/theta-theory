\section{Interpolation and the analytic inputs}\label{sec:classical}
The interpolation identities in this section are classical. They fix the
normalization used in the geometric argument; they do not assert
statistical attainment. The independent repository derivation of the
finite spectral comparison is acknowledged in \cite{A1v9note}.

\begin{lemma}[Arbitrary-prefix Hermite data]\label{lem:prefix-hermite}
Let $y_1,\ldots,y_p$ be an ordered multiset of real numbers, and let
$q_y$ be the multiplicity of $y$ in the full multiset. The functionals
\[
 f\longmapsto[y_1,\ldots,y_j]f,\qquad 1\le j\le p,
\]
form a basis of the space generated by
$f\mapsto f^{(k)}(y)$, $0\le k<q_y$. Repetitions need not be adjacent.
For positive nodes, application to $f(z)=t^{Hz}$ gives exactly the
complete blocks $t^{Hy}(\log t)^k$, $0\le k<q_y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A prefix divided difference depends only on the Hermite data at that
prefix, so belongs to the stated $p$-dimensional space. On polynomials
$1,z,\ldots,z^{p-1}$ its values vanish in degrees below $j-1$ and
equal one in degree $j-1$. The resulting matrix is triangular with
unit diagonal, so these $p$ functionals are independent. Substitution
of $t^{Hz}$ transforms differentiation at a node into multiplication
by the nonzero factor $H^k$ and the indicated logarithmic function.
This is the Newton--Hermite prefix principle; see
\cite[Proposition 7]{deBoor}. It does not assert attainable posterior rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exterior spectral form of determinant volumes]
\label{prop:exterior-profile}
Fix $q$ and nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_q\in[0,1]$, retaining repetitions.
Let $W=(x_i^{j-1})_{i,j=1}^q$ and let
$\sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_q\ge0$ be its singular values. Define
$\mathcal V_\ell$ as the maximal absolute Vandermonde product over
$\ell$ labels, with $\mathcal V_1=1$. Then
\[
 c_q\mathcal V_\ell\le\prod_{j=1}^\ell\sigma_j(W)
                         \le C_q\mathcal V_\ell,
 \qquad 1\le\ell\le q.
\]
The constants are independent of all gaps, and the assertion includes
coincident nodes. Equivalently the middle expression is the operator
norm of $\bigwedge^\ell W$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the finite Leja ordering of Lemma~\ref{lem:leja-scales}, and let
$d_j$ be its nonincreasing pivots. Put
$P_{j-1}(z)=\prod_{i<j}(z-x_i)$, and let $T$ have as its $j$th
column the coefficients of $P_{j-1}$ in the power basis. It is upper
triangular with diagonal one. Elementary symmetric polynomial bounds
on nodes in $[0,1]$ bound $T$ uniformly in $q$. Writing $T=I+U$
with $U^q=0$ gives $T^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}(-U)^k$, hence a uniform
bound on its inverse.

Evaluation gives $WT=LD$, $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_j)$. If $s$ is
the number of distinct sites, the active leading $s$-block of $L$ is
lower triangular with diagonal $\pm1$, bounded entries, and bounded
inverse. Replace each inactive column by the corresponding coordinate
vector. The resulting matrix $\widetilde L$ and its inverse are
uniformly bounded: it is block lower triangular, with the active block
and an identity block on its diagonal. Because inactive entries of $D$
are zero, $W=\widetilde LDT^{-1}$ still holds.
Multiplication by these uniformly bounded invertible matrices compares
the ordered singular values of $W$ with the ordered $d_j$, including
zero singular values. Finally Lemma~\ref{lem:leja-scales} gives
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^\ell d_j\le\mathcal V_\ell
                    \le\ell!\prod_{j=1}^\ell d_j.
\]
This proves the proposition without dividing by a vanishing pivot.
The Leja lemma is a finite interpolation calculation proved in the
main article before its use in the statistical theorem, so this
comparison introduces no circular dependence on attainment or memory.
\end{proof}
