\begin{abstract}
For positive sparse monomial experiments, we determine finite-memory
prediction uniformly through additive collisions of the exponents.
Maximal future Vandermonde products, truncated by the dimension
actually acquired, govern every integer label budget. The proof combines
attainable confluent flags, whole-image covering, unconditional acquired
mass, and one causal filter whose updates contain no reciprocal
collision gap. Intersecting collision loci yield distinct dimensional
regimes and separated memory crossovers.

For independent edge experiments on a fixed graph, a resolution-dependent
cut profile governs shared memory under history-dependent acquisition.
Quantitative separator bounds retain actual report evidence. A single
deterministic order dominates the entire prediction curve of any
resolution-blind acquisition law up to constant factors, and finite
policy menus admit a corresponding deterministic representation.
Analytic paths give order phases; an evaluated positive experiment
has a sharp average-distortion coefficient.

We further localize acquisition to subsets of active edges. A common
high-probability event requires regular acquired coordinates on every
cut of one compulsory level, rather than on every edge. This gives a
single-boundary converse with explicit coordinate-recovery costs. For
independent copies of a fixed scalar experiment on linear-size graphs
with sufficiently strong balanced cuts, an explicitly augmented query
menu retains the original tensor task and adds local tests from the
same future test space. Its optimal persistent bits are bounded above
and below by constant multiples of the number of vertices times the
scalar collision profile, uniformly in graph size and calibration.
The deterministic construction quantizes each active edge only once.
We prove that the required graph families exist and obtain simultaneous
bounds for graph growth and approach to a calibration collision.
\end{abstract}
