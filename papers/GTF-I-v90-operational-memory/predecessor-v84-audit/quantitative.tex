\begin{abstract}
We study ordered quantum measurements with classical output and no residual
device system.  A positive normalized-tuple covariance gives a global
complete-body upper bound for every finite-use adaptive experiment.
We describe its entire singular kernel in terms of the effect supports.
The resulting range criterion identifies the measurement-channel
Kraus span.  On every fixed unitary orbit, we prove matching finite-angle
trace-distance laws: the distance is zero on stationary orbits and has
order $\min\{1,\sqrt N|t|\}$ or $\min\{1,N|t|\}$ according to whether
the generator belongs to that span.  A corrected logical qubit gives
the latter lower bound with an explicit quadratic angle remainder.
This is a finite-pair formulation of the known metrological criterion,
not a new claim to that criterion.  A public-weight regularization also
obeys data processing when its weights are transported with the output.
On balanced interiors, exact description entropy and common learning
retain joint order $\Theta_k(N\delta^{-2}[d^2+d\log(1/\eta)])$ for fixed
outcome count; the displayed upper has a $k^3$ factor and does not settle
growing $k$.  Rational covariance evaluation, support decisions and
affine legalization are polynomial.  Global dictionary and readout
synthesis remain distinct resources.  Complete auxiliary binary proofs
are supplied in a separately reconstructible supplement.
\end{abstract}
