\begin{abstract}
We study which statistical comparisons are attainable when both the
choice of experiment and its retained continuation are resources.
Prepared positive kernels generate a future-test quotient; causal
simulators then transport prediction and decision guarantees with
explicit acquisition, quantization and memory costs. At a prescribed
finite register profile we give an occupation-state dynamic formulation
and an exact controlled family whose value is
$1/(2\lceil m/K\rceil)$, whereas a free mixture of complete controllers
has value $K/(2m)$. This strict discrepancy persists under positive-noise
perturbations. For a marked product experiment with two unknown
Bernoulli parameters, we determine the exact two-preparation minimax
value and its least-favorable two-point prior. The value is
$(-r^3+6r^2-3r+14)/32$, where $2r^3+25r^2-3=0$ and $1/3<r<7/20$.
An explicit interval of mark biases has a corresponding exact two-preparation
saddle family. A global polynomial certificate proves optimality over all responses
to the full marked training word. The optimal response uses five frozen
events and peak register width twelve, yielding an exact ideal physical
score and an enlarged preparation boundary for positive-noise collision
experiments. We also obtain arbitrary-integer revelation bounds, a
complete two-label law, and a matched online prediction-resolution law
under explicit dynamical and acquired-mass hypotheses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An experiment supplies observations, but a causal procedure must also
retain enough information to choose its next experiment and interpret
its eventual answer. These are different requirements. A posterior may
summarize all relevant observations without fitting into the available
register. Conversely, a small register sufficient for one terminal
decision may not support a subsequent informative action. The purpose
of this paper is to place these questions in one prepared causal model
and to determine several of its exact resource boundaries.

The underlying object consists of a preparation, positive normalized
experimental kernels, a causal action interface, executable future tests
and a resource profile. No information metric or geometric measure is
postulated independently of that object. The future-test quotient
identifies prepared histories with identical legal continuations.
Their actual probability law determines checkpoint resolution, whereas
closure of the quotient under observations determines whether a
quantization can be updated online. Sections~\ref{sec:foundations}
and \ref{sec:resolution} develop these distinctions. Under contractive
updates and an acquired small-ball condition, online squared prediction
loss is bounded above and below by constants times $M^{-2/d}$.
A separate transport theorem simultaneously accounts for acquired
calibration, state quantization, primitive model errors and the
register used by a downstream controller.

The finite-memory question is not solved by unrestricted belief-state
convexification. Section~\ref{sec:controlled-memory} retains the common
observable rows in a parameterwise occupation-state recursion. The
occupation state belongs to offline design, not to the executing
controller. Its exact supersolution formulation contains neither an
unobserved parameter oracle nor a free retained strategy tree.
The distinction is quantitative. In a routing-and-sensing family with
$m$ incomparable branch-specific experiments and $K$ labels at the
routing cut, we prove
\[
 V_{m,K}=\frac1{2\lceil m/K\rceil},\qquad
 \overline V_{m,K}=\frac K{2m}.
\]
The second value permits a persistent controller selector without
charging it. The first does not. The values differ whenever $K$ does
not divide $m$, including $1/4$ versus $1/3$ at $(m,K)=(3,2)$.
All other cuts, including the sensing-action buffer and the frozen
validation event, are specified. A small full-support perturbation
preserves the strict discrepancy.

Our principal explicit statistical calculation concerns
\[
 b(p,q)=\Ber(p)\otimes\Ber(q)\otimes\Ber(1/2),
                        \qquad(p,q)\in[0,1]^2.
\]
The last bit is the mark of the actual preparation. On atoms
$000,001,110,111$, the target $Q_*$ has masses $5/16,3/16,3/16,5/16$,
and it vanishes elsewhere. Training is followed by a frozen event
applied to independent candidate and target validations. If $U_N$
is the unrestricted-memory worst-case mean difference using $N$
candidate training preparations, the exact values at one and two are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-exact-values}
 U_1=\frac{85-7\sqrt{73}}{64},\qquad
 U_2=\frac{-r^3+6r^2-3r+14}{32},\quad 2r^3+25r^2-3=0,
\end{equation}
where $r\in(1/3,7/20)$. The first value belongs to the continuous
marked-target family in Theorem~\ref{thm:marked-exact}. The second is
proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:two-saddle}. Its optimal response depends on
the total of four training bits, with a nontrivial randomization at the
two extreme counts. A square factorization and strict transverse
monotonicity prove its lower bound on the full parameter square. The
upper bound uses a matching least-favorable prior and the signs of all
posterior-predictive coefficients, including the real training marks.
No restriction to symmetric responses or parameter grids is imposed on
the converse. Theorem~\ref{thm:two-family} extends this construction to
every target mark bias $6/25\le\gamma\le13/50$, with a cubic-defined
least-favorable prior and a matching exact $U_2(\gamma)$ formula.

The physical realization is part of the same argument. The five-event
residual machine has peak twelve even after the new randomized selection
step is charged. Its all-on law covers the original private candidate
class; a common-garbling subfamily suffices for the converse. Thus the
ideal two-preparation physical score is exactly $U_2$ at every
$W\ge12$. If $\TV(Q,Q_*)\le\beta$, then
\[
 N_c^{\rm phys}(W,Q)=2\quad\text{whenever}\quad
 W\ge12,\qquad U_1+\beta\le c<U_2-\beta.
\]
One candidate and one target validation are charged in addition to the
two training preparations. The local collision geometry and
positive-noise estimate are proved in Section~\ref{sec:physical-bridge}.
The exact atomic stochastic row and its finite fair-bit approximations
have distinct, explicit precision costs.

For comparison with the deterministic construction we retain the
constants
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constants}
 u_1=\frac{4035777}{10240000},\qquad
 m_2=\frac{12-3\sqrt3}{16},\qquad \beta_0=\frac1{300}.
\end{equation}
The rational $u_1$ is a valid one-preparation certificate and $m_2$ is
the exact minimum of the deterministic five-event selector. The exact
values in \eqref{eq:intro-exact-values} sharpen these two distinct
bounds without discarding their proofs or implementations.

A revelation family illustrates another integer obstruction. For
arbitrary alphabet size $m$, two decision labels have the complete law
\[
 V^{\rm er}_{N,m}(2)=\min\{2wb[1-(1-\alpha)^N],w\},\qquad
 w=\lfloor m/2\rfloor/m,\quad b=\lceil m/2\rceil/m.
\]
For odd $m$ there is a saturation transition before perfect revelation.
The corresponding balanced-partition Bayes formula is not the minimax
value at all reveal probabilities. General integer bounds and an exact
low-reveal range accompany this calculation. This example is a
controlled comparison of decision-cut resources; it is not a surrogate
for the collision model's bit-level width.

\subsection{Relation to comparison, control and finite memory}
Blackwell comparison and minimax theory provide the statistical
mechanisms \cite{Blackwell,Sion}. The finite-horizon belief-state
piecewise-linear representation is classical \cite{SmallwoodSondik}.
The unrestricted controlled dual in Section~\ref{sec:controlled}
specializes that machinery to a fixed adversarial parameter and a frozen
validation score. It is not a new principle of dynamic programming.
Its relation to the genuine constrained value is separated by
Theorem~\ref{thm:routing-gap}.

Controlled information acquisition has a substantial sequential-design
history, beginning with Chernoff \cite{Chernoff}. Naghshvar and Javidi
\cite{NJ}, Section 3 and equation (4), formulate a posterior-state
stopping problem minimizing expected observation count plus a wrong-
declaration penalty. Their continuation operator is the same Bayesian
mechanism that underlies our unrestricted recursion. Our terminal
functional instead compares fresh candidate and target draws, and our
finite-profile recursion optimizes common row prescriptions without
storing the full posterior. Their cost and asymptotic optimality results
are not replaced by a finite-horizon validation formula.

Nitinawarat, Atia and Veeravalli \cite{NAV}, Theorem 2 and its controlled
sensing example, already distinguish causal from open-loop experiments
in a multihypothesis error-exponent problem. Thus informative adaptation
or a strict adaptive advantage alone is not a novelty claim here.
The exact ceiling law and its charged-selector discrepancy concern a
different, fixed-horizon resource constraint; the marked saddle concerns
a continuous two-parameter composite family and an exact finite-$N$
validation value rather than an asymptotic error exponent.

Finite-memory testing and the role of randomization are treated in
\cite{HC,HCrandom,FH,CFH}. Our autonomous stationary comparison explicitly
retains the Hellman--Cover attribution. Managoli and Prabhakaran
\cite{MP} study a time-invariant finite-state tester against adaptive
sample-distribution choices. Here the unknown parameter is fixed
throughout an execution. The lack of mixed/behavioral equivalence with
imperfect recall is also established; Zheng, Sim and Varvitsiotis
\cite{ZSV} develop sum-of-squares methods over behavioral strategies.
We do not claim polynomial policy optimization, the mixed/behavioral
distinction or a general positivity hierarchy as new
\cite{MM,Putinar}. Our explicit finite-family values and full-parameter
saddle certificates are the mathematical claims requiring comparison.

Norberg's filtered comparison \cite{Norberg} remains relevant to the
general object-level setting. No non-overlap theorem is asserted without
a proof-level comparison of that original work. The foundational kernel,
barycenter and quantization arguments here are proved to fix their
resource and preparation meanings, not to establish priority for their
classical ingredients.

\subsection{Organization}
The causal object, acquired resolution and simultaneous simulation
budget come first. Compatible finite architectures and the
posterior-predictive dual then lead to controlled finite memory, exact
marked minimax laws and the physical realization. Integer revelation,
quantitative finite-architecture certification and the autonomous
comparison follow. A final consumer theorem transfers the sharpened
physical audit to repeated confidence tests and causally simulated
interfaces. The predecessor's complete mathematical and development
volumes are preserved separately. In particular, preservation of A2,
B4 and C2 derivations is not a claim that their geometric or operator
hypotheses follow from finite stochastic kernels.
