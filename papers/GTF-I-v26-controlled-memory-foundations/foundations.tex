\section{Causal experiments and their predictive quotients}
\label{sec:foundations}
We begin with the object and morphism structure. Its elementary
probability statements fix the meaning of later resource theorems;
classical kernel construction, conditional expectation and quantization
are not assigned a new priority. The substantive comparisons below
retain their preparation, memory and approximation coordinates
simultaneously.

\subsection{The experimental object}
An experiment has a fixed unknown parameter $\theta\in\Theta$, hidden
states $X_t$, visible actions $A_t$, reports $Y_{t+1}$, and preparation
laws $\mu_\theta$. We impose the following six conventions.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{A0 (types and visibility).}
These spaces are standard Borel. Visible histories include actions,
reports and publicly recorded resource increments. A legal-action set
and its remaining budget are part of the visible protocol type. Two
histories with different legal continuations are not identified.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{A1 (positive kernels).}
There are jointly measurable normalized nonnegative kernels
$K_t^\theta(dx',dy,dc\mid x,a)$, with $c$ a nonnegative resource
increment. An absorbing symbol represents termination or failure.
Dirac kernels are allowed. Positivity here does not require every
outcome to have strictly positive probability. A hidden state can
contain the necessary physical past; this notation does not assume a
finite-dimensional physical closure.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{A2 (causality).}
A policy is a kernel $\alpha_t(da\mid h_t)$ depending only on visible
past data and allowed independent randomization. It cannot read the
unknown parameter. Stopping is with respect to the visible filtration.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{A3 (executable tests).}
The specified bounded future tests include constants, required report
events and their legal one-step continuations. For discrete reports the
one-step instrument is
\[
 (\mathsf T_{t,a,y}f)(\theta,x)
       =\int f(\theta,x')K_t^\theta(dx',\{y\},dc\mid x,a).
\]
The test spaces are closed under the required instruments, finite
linear combinations and the bounded monotone limits needed to recover
the stated event laws. For general reports one uses event instruments
and regular conditional distributions, not division by the probability
of a continuous singleton.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{A4 (actual preparation).}
Average risks and acquired state laws refer to the specified preparation
and exploration protocol. Failed trials, calibration, resets and
conditioning events retain their probabilities and implementation costs.
A convenient measure on a reachable set does not replace its acquired
law.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{A5 (executable resources).}
Persistent labels, temporary buffers, program constants, calibration
precision, preparations and clocks are distinguished. A retained
selector is a state. A parameter-dependent table is not a known program
constant. A simulator with $R_t$ labels composed with a controller with
$K_t$ labels has the direct product bound $R_tK_t$, before any justified
quotient is taken. Atomic stochastic rows and fair-bit implementations
are separately specified.

\begin{proposition}[Prepared path laws]\label{prop:path-law}
For every fixed parameter and causal policy, the preparation and kernels
uniquely determine their finite-dimensional path laws. On a finite
horizon they are the iterated kernel product
\[
 \mu_\theta(dx_0)\prod_{t<T}
   \alpha_t(da_t\mid h_t)K_t^\theta(dx_{t+1},dy_{t+1},dc_t\mid x_t,a_t).
\]
These laws are consistent under marginalization. For a countable horizon
they determine the usual kernel extension. Deterministic dynamics give
their original trajectory laws when represented by Dirac kernels.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Successive integration constructs each finite law. Normalization of the
last two kernels gives its preceding marginal, and induction proves
consistency and uniqueness on cylinder events. The countable kernel
extension gives the full law; equivalently one applies the standard
extension theorem to these consistent iterated kernels. Integration
against a Dirac transition is evaluation along that transition, which
proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Prepared predictive equivalence}
For Bayesian predictions additionally fix a prior on the parameter, and
regard $(\theta,X_t)$ as the hidden variable. A prepared posterior $\pi$
at a given protocol type evaluates the future tests. Define
$\pi\sim_t\widetilde\pi$ if their evaluations agree on every specified
future test. This definition concerns one prepared experiment; it does
not identify a prior-dependent posterior with a statistic sufficient
simultaneously for all fixed parameters.

\begin{theorem}[Predictive quotient and causal closure]
\label{thm:predictive-quotient}
For finite report alphabets, predictive equivalence is preserved by every
legal positive-probability report update. Its class determines the laws
of all specified future experiments. Every deterministic history
statistic that determines those laws factors onto this quotient.
For a measurable realization it is sufficient that a countable
separating test family gives a measurable evaluation map with standard
Borel image, and that the chosen statistic admits a measurable factor.
In finite history spaces the latter conditions are automatic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a legal action and report, Bayes' rule gives
\[
 \pi'(f)=\frac{\pi(\mathsf T_{t,a,y}f)}
                    {\pi(\mathsf T_{t,a,y}1)}.
\]
Both numerator and denominator depend only on the old class by A3.
Equivalent posteriors assign the same probability to the report, so
updates agree on the next test space whenever that probability is
positive. Null reports do not affect the prepared law and may be
assigned a fixed admissible version. Induction determines every finite
future report word; the monotone-class extension gives the required
future events. Conversely those event laws determine the tests.
If two histories have the same sufficient statistic, they have the
same future evaluations; the quotient is therefore constant on its
fibers. This gives the factor set-theoretically. The stated measurable
factor assumption supplies its measurable version; no claim that every
abstract quotient of a standard Borel space is standard Borel is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Barycenters, entropy and normalized response]
\label{prop:prepared-geometry}
In a fixed preparation, suppose a hidden target $Z$, observation $Y$
and postprocessing $W$ form a Markov chain $Z\to Y\to W$.
For any bounded vector of tests $f$,
\[
 p_W=\E[p_Y\mid W],\qquad p_Y=\E[f(Z)\mid Y],\qquad
 \E\varphi(p_W)\le\E\varphi(p_Y)
\]
for every convex $\varphi$ for which these expectations exist.
For a parameter-independent kernel $G$, $P\ll Q$, and finite
$D(P\|Q)$, regular conditional laws give
\begin{align*}
 D(P\|Q)={}&D(PG\|QG)\\
 &+\int D(P(dx\mid w)\|Q(dx\mid w))\,(PG)(dw).
\end{align*}
Finally, if $f,g$ are bounded and
$\pi_u(dx)=e^{ug(x)}\pi(dx)/\pi(e^{ug})$, then
\[
               \left.\frac{d}{du}\pi_u(f)\right|_{u=0}
                          =\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(f,g).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Markov property and the tower rule give the conditional barycenter;
conditional Jensen gives convex order. The joint laws $P(dx)G(dw\mid x)$
and $Q(dx)G(dw\mid x)$ have relative entropy $D(P\|Q)$.
Disintegrating by $w$ and applying the entropy chain rule gives the
identity, and hence data processing. The last equality follows by
quotient differentiation; boundedness justifies differentiation under
the integral.
\end{proof}
The barycenter statement concerns a law of predictions, not a
pointwise map between reachable sets. Likewise the covariance in the
last formula is a normalized response, not an independently postulated
Fisher matrix or a global sufficiency theorem.

\section{Attainable resolution and resource-aware simulation}
\label{sec:resolution}
\subsection{Checkpoint and online prediction}
Fix an actual exploration protocol and its history law. Let $p(h)$ be a
bounded $d$-dimensional conditional prediction vector, with law $\nu$.
For instance, a future test is sampled independently after encoding,
and the coordinates include its sampling weights. Squared excess
prediction loss is then $\E\|p(h)-\widehat p\|^2$.

\begin{proposition}[Checkpoint resolution]\label{prop:checkpoint}
For at most $M$ retained labels the least checkpoint excess loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:checkpoint}
 \inf_{c_1,\ldots,c_M}\int\min_{1\le j\le M}\|p-c_j\|^2\,\nu(dp).
\end{equation}
Independent randomized encoders or decoders cannot improve it. This
formula concerns the stated preparation and does not provide a common
codebook by separately optimizing at each unknown true parameter.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional squared loss decomposes into its Bayes loss and squared
distance from the conditional mean. For each retained label replace a
random decoder by its mean. Random assignment to the resulting centers
cannot do better than a nearest center. Conversely a nearest-center
assignment attains the infimum over codebooks arbitrarily closely.
Conditioning on an independent shared random seed, if such a seed is
allowed, gives a codebook for each seed, each with risk at least
\eqref{eq:checkpoint}. Averaging cannot improve the bound. The formula
does not charge the computation of a checkpoint assignment as an online
algorithm; online compatibility is a separate condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A matched online resolution law]\label{thm:online-resolution}
Suppose a fixed prepared experiment has a predictive state $s_t$ in a
compact metric space $S$, a known initial state, and updates
$s_{t+1}=F_{t,a,y}(s_t)$. Assume every permitted update is
$\rho$-Lipschitz for one $0\le\rho<1$, and the prediction map
$p:S\to\mathbb R^d$ is $L$-Lipschitz. Suppose $S$ has a $\delta$-net
of at most $C\delta^{-d}$ points for all sufficiently small $\delta$.
At a checkpoint $T$, assume the actual law $\nu$ of $p(s_T)$ satisfies
the small-ball bound $\nu(B(z,a))\le A a^d$ for every center $z$ and
all sufficiently small $a>0$.
Then for all sufficiently large $M$, the minimum online excess
prediction loss with $M$ retained predictive labels satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:online-rate}
               c_0 M^{-2/d}\le R_M^{\rm on}\le C_0 M^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
Here one may take
\[
 c_0=\frac12(2A)^{-2/d},
 \qquad C_0=\frac{L^2C^{2/d}}{(1-\rho)^2},
\]
Any separate exploration
controller, report buffer or clock is charged in addition to these
$M$ labels. The exploration protocol and the preparation are fixed
before optimizing the encoder.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Any online $M$-label state is also a checkpoint encoder, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:checkpoint} applies. For any $M$ centers, the union
of balls of radius $a=(2AM)^{-1/d}$ has $\nu$-mass at most one half.
On its complement squared distance is at least $a^2$. Integration
gives $c_0M^{-2/d}$.
This argument also permits randomized online encoders.

For the upper bound take a $\delta$-net with
$\delta=(C/M)^{1/d}$, and a fixed measurable nearest-net assignment
(with a fixed tie rule). Quantize the known initial state and, after
each legal update, quantize again. The state error obeys
$e_{t+1}\le\rho e_t+\delta$, $e_0\le\delta$, and hence
$e_t\le\delta/(1-\rho)$. The prediction error is at most $Le_t$.
Squaring proves the upper bound, uniformly in time. Each update uses
only the current net label and the current permitted input, so the
construction is online. It does not grant the encoder free access to
the original discarded history. If the exploration policy needs another
register, that register is combined by a product construction.
\end{proof}

For example, a Lebesgue density bounded above by $B$ gives the
small-ball hypothesis with $A=Bv_d$, where $v_d$ is unit-ball volume.
The lower hypothesis is a condition on the \emph{acquired prediction
law}. A reachable set of dimension $d$ without a mass condition does
not imply the lower bound. Similarly, the contraction assumption is
additional dynamics, not a consequence of kernel positivity.

\subsection{Causal morphisms and simultaneous error budgets}
A causal simulator $\Gamma:\mathfrak E\to\mathfrak F$ is a
parameter-independent executable transducer that uses source
preparations to supply the target interface under every allowed target
policy. Its defect $\varepsilon$ is a uniform total-variation bound on
the resulting path laws, including the fields required by the task.
Its resource map $\rho_\Gamma$ bounds the source resources of the
transducer composed with a target controller.

\begin{proposition}[Composition and risk transfer]\label{prop:causal-morphism}
Causal simulators compose. Their uniform defects add, capped at one,
and their resource maps compose in the order
$\rho_{\Gamma_1}\circ\rho_{\Gamma_2}$. For a terminal loss in $[0,L]$
and a common target task,
\[
 \mathcal R_{\mathfrak E}(\rho_\Gamma(B))
           \le\mathcal R_{\mathfrak F}(B)+L\varepsilon.
\]
For reward in $[-1,1]$, the corresponding maximum worst-case rewards
satisfy the reverse inequality with an error $2\varepsilon$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Interleave the two transducers; their pair of internal states is a causal
implementation. Total variation contracts under a kernel and obeys the
triangle inequality, proving the defect sum. If their state counts are
$R_t$ and $M_t$, the pair has at most $R_tM_t$ states. Other declared
resource bounds compose by execution of the two protocols. A bounded
loss changes expectation by at most its range times total variation.
Apply this bound to every fixed parameter and target controller and
take the appropriate infimum or supremum. Uniformity in the controller
is essential to this step.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Acquisition, quantization and model-error transport]
\label{thm:joint-transport}
Suppose an exact source transducer has predictive state $s_t\in S_t$
and additional charged buffers of size $R_t$, supplies $T$ target
operations, and has uniform model defect at most $\varepsilon_{\rm mod}$.
Its initialization and update depend on a calibration quantity $\xi$.
From $N_{\rm cal}$ source preparations acquire an estimate $\widehat\xi$
with at most $C_{\rm cal}$ retained labels, such that
\[
 \sup_\theta\Pp_\theta(d(\widehat\xi,\xi)>a)\le\eta.
\]
Subsequent preparation noises are independent of calibration conditional
on the fixed parameter. Let finite nets of sizes $M_t$ have radii
$\delta_t$. Assume, on matching input/output histories and on the good
calibration event, that the quantized transducer's state error satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-error-recursion}
 e_0\le d_0+a_0a+\delta_0,\qquad
 e_{t+1}\le\rho_t e_t+d_{t+1}+a_{t+1}a+\delta_{t+1}.
\end{equation}
Assume its next output kernel and the exact transducer's output kernel
differ in total variation by at most
$\epsilon_t+L_t e_t+b_ta$, uniformly in the fixed parameter and legal
policy. All constants and nets are declared independently of that
unknown parameter. Then the quantized acquired transducer has defect
at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-defect}
 \Delta=\min\left\{1,\varepsilon_{\rm mod}+\eta+
                 \sum_{t<T}(\epsilon_t+L_te_t+b_ta)\right\}.
\end{equation}
Composing it with any target controller of cut widths $K_t$ requires at
most $C_{\rm cal}R_tM_tK_t$ labels at aligned execution cuts and transfers
its bounded reward with loss at most $2\Delta$. Its preparations are
$N_{\rm cal}$ plus all source preparations specified by the transducer;
no successful-trial conditioning removes the failures.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the good calibration event, iterate \eqref{eq:joint-error-recursion}.
More explicitly, with $D_0=d_0+a_0a+\delta_0$ and
$D_j=d_j+a_ja+\delta_j$,
\[
 e_t\le\sum_{j=0}^tD_j\prod_{k=j}^{t-1}\rho_k.
\]
Couple each pair of output kernels maximally while their external
histories agree. On agreement the target controller sees the same
inputs, so it uses the same legal action and may use the same independent
randomization. Its discontinuous action choices require no Lipschitz
assumption: before a disagreement their arguments are identical.
The chance of a first discrepancy is bounded by the sum in
\eqref{eq:joint-defect}. Add the probability of bad calibration and the
exact transducer's model defect. This proves the path bound, including
stopping after padding by absorbing states.

The executable approximate transducer stores its calibration label,
additional buffer, and net index. Combine these with the actual target
register, not its full history or an uncharged belief. The resulting
product gives the state count. If an operation contains further buffer
cuts, apply the same product at each such cut; $R_t$ was assumed to
include them. Preparation costs add by execution. Finally apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:causal-morphism}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A finite acquisition register]\label{cor:calibration-register}
Suppose $r_0$ calibration rows have at most $d$ outcomes, and take $n$
independent preparations from each row on a fixed schedule. Their
empirical rows satisfy, with probability at least $1-\eta$ uniformly in
the row probabilities,
\[
 \max\TV(\widehat P,P)\le\frac{dz}{2},\qquad
       \eta=\min\{1,2r_0d\exp(-2nz^2)\}.
\]
Their counts can be retained in at most
$(n+1)^{r_0(d-1)}$ labels. Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:joint-transport} can use
$N_{\rm cal}=r_0n$, $a=dz/2$ and this finite calibration register when
its calibration metric is the maximum row total variation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For each outcome the empirical frequency is the mean of independent
Bernoulli indicators. Hoeffding's bound and a union bound over at most
$r_0d$ coordinates give the event stated. The total-variation estimate
is half the sum of coordinate errors. Retain $d-1$ counts per row;
the last is reconstructed from the known number of draws at that stage
of the clocked schedule. Each retained count takes at most $n+1$ values.
An autonomous implementation must additionally charge its schedule.
\end{proof}

These results separate exact predictive equivalence, average checkpoint
resolution, online realizability, and uniform simulation risk. In
particular, neither an optimal checkpoint codebook nor the proof-state
bundle of Section~\ref{sec:controlled-memory} is automatically an
executable finite register.
