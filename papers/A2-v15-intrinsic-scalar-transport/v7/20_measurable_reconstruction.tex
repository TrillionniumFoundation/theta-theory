\section{Measurable reconstruction on compact parameter sets}
\label{sec:v7-selection}
All compact discrepancy minimizations in the sequel use the same
measurable convention. This removes a possible ambiguity at repeated
roots or at data having several best fits; uniqueness of a minimizing
parameter is unnecessary.

\begin{lemma}[A specified Borel minimizing selection]
\label{lem:v7-selection}
Let $K$ be a nonempty compact metric space, let $\mathcal Y$ be a
standard Borel data space, and suppose $G:\mathcal Y\times K\to\R$
is Borel measurable and continuous in its second argument. There is a
Borel map $\widehat\theta:\mathcal Y\to K$ such that
\[
 G(y,\widehat\theta(y))=\min_{\theta\in K}G(y,\theta).
\]
A single choice of finite closed-ball covers of $K$ specifies such a
map for every data value.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix, for each integer $r\ge1$, a finite cover of $K$ by closed balls
$B_{r,1},\ldots,B_{r,N_r}$ of radius $2^{-r}$, with centers in $K$,
and fix their indicated order. The minimum
$v(y)=\min_KG(y,\cdot)$ is Borel: it equals the infimum of $G(y,\cdot)$
over any fixed countable dense subset of $K$. For any nonempty fixed
compact subset $F\subset K$, the same argument shows that
$v_F(y)=\min_FG(y,\cdot)$ is Borel. For $F=\varnothing$, set
$v_F=+\infty$.

At the first stage choose the least index $i_1$ for which
$v_{K\cap B_{1,i_1}}(y)=v(y)$. At stage $r$, after the earlier choices,
choose the least $i_r$ for which
\[
 v_{K\cap B_{1,i_1}\cap\cdots\cap B_{r,i_r}}(y)=v(y).
\]
Such a choice always exists because the current nonempty compact set
of minimizers is covered by the balls at the next stage. For each
fixed finite prefix of indices the compact set in this expression is
fixed, so its equality event is Borel. Induction and the finite number
of indices at each stage show that every selected index is Borel.

Intersect the minimizer set with the selected balls. These are nested
nonempty compact sets with diameter at most $2^{1-r}$ at stage $r$.
Their intersection consists of a single point $\widehat\theta(y)$.
The centers of the selected balls converge to it, since their distances
from this point are at most $2^{-r}$. The centers form Borel maps into
$K$; their pointwise limit is Borel and is a minimizer by continuity.
\end{proof}

Apply the lemma with $G$ equal to the maximum discrepancy between the
measured finite vector and the continuous physical observation map.
The physical compact set, its covers, and their order are fixed before
observations. Every resulting estimator in
Theorems~\ref{thm:v5-pairwise}, \ref{thm:v6-three}, and
\ref{thm:v6-count-only} is then a Borel statistic. The proof is an
existence and specification result, not an assertion of computational
efficiency. It applies equally to compact constrained variance fits.

The polynomial root-or-zero convention in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v5-calibration} is Borel as well. One direct verification
is to minimize the squared polynomial on a fixed compact subinterval;
the minimum is a continuous function of its coefficients. Existence of
a zero on any closed rational interval is therefore a closed condition.
Existence of two distinct zeros in the prescribed interval is the
countable union of conditions asserting zeros in two disjoint closed
intervals with rational endpoints, also allowing the two fixed end
points. Existence of exactly one zero is consequently Borel. On that
set its location is recovered measurably from these interval tests;
on its complement the declared value is zero. This includes the zero
polynomial, for which uniqueness fails. Thus the pilot and the final
compact fit both define statistics on all data outcomes.
