\begin{abstract}
We relate minimax contact geometry to the memory of causal statistical
experiments. For a two-parameter family of full-support marked targets,
we determine the exact two-preparation saddle and prove that five states
are necessary and sufficient at the decision cut. The original
positive-noise hard-sphere sensor is shown, by its preparation and
reflection symmetries, to belong to this family throughout the explicit
range $0<\sigma\le1/24$. A facial scheduling criterion supplies converses
uniform over stochastic encoders and over the declared validation order.
Among all twenty clocked interleavings of the two three-slot validation
tapes, the exact minimum peak is twelve; precisely the two serial orders
attain it, and every nonserial order requires at least fourteen states.
At zero noise, reverse serial validation instead has exact peak ten.
Thus exact causal memory can jump at a support boundary while the
statistical value varies continuously. We also retain the causal
foundations, controlled minimax duality, exact ideal saddles,
all-preparation localization, and resource-accounted transport results
that enter this theorem chain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An optimal statistical decision and a minimum-memory realization of
that decision are different objects. A least-favorable prior exposes a
face of Bayes-optimal responses. Uniform minimax optimality imposes
additional contact conditions, including on coordinates to which that
prior assigns zero coefficient. Once those conditions have determined
an optimal response, its realizability depends on common positive
continuations across every charged cut. The order in which independent
validation preparations are read is then a mathematical part of the
memory problem, not a harmless implementation convention.

The present paper develops this connection from prepared causal
experiments to an exact positive-noise physical theorem. We use finite
registers, count all persistent selectors and report buffers, and freeze
one row--event continuation before independent candidate and target
validation. The unknown candidate parameter is fixed through the audit.
The target is a specified law; acquisition of unknown target calibration
has a separate resource charge. A clocked validation schedule is declared
before data are observed. Report-dependent scheduling is a distinct
controlled interface and is not treated as an uncharged clock.

\subsection{The principal theorem chain}
Let the private all-on experiment be
$b(p,q)=\Ber(p)\otimes\Ber(q)\otimes\Ber(1/2)$. On the four diagonal
marked atoms $000,001,110,111$, give the target masses
\[
 \frac{1+\gamma}{4}-\kappa,\quad
 \frac{1-\gamma}{4}-\kappa,\quad
 \frac{1-\gamma}{4}-\kappa,\quad
 \frac{1+\gamma}{4}-\kappa,
\]
and give each other atom mass $\kappa$. For
$(\gamma,\kappa)\in[6/25,13/50]\times[0,1/1000]$, the full-support
saddle theorem determines the exact value by the unique root
$r\in(33/100,7/20)$ of
\[
 r^3+(13-2\gamma+8\kappa)r^2
       +(3-12\gamma+48\kappa)r-1-2\gamma+8\kappa=0.
\]
A global polynomial factorization proves the lower bound. Every signed
posterior coefficient is checked for the matching two-point prior.
Support contact and stationarity determine the two Bayes-indifferent
response coordinates in an interval strictly inside $(0,1)$.
Consequently the minimum post-training decision width is exactly five,
not merely at the ideal target but throughout this explicit rectangle.
See Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-saddle}.

The physical identification is exact. In the original collision model,
the two Brownian report windows have one common random signal amplitude
and conditionally independent noises. If $e$ is their conditional sign
error probability, put $\mu_1=\mathbb E e$, $\mu_2=\mathbb E e^2$.
Preparation reflection and center-of-mass independence give
\[
 \gamma=\frac14-\frac{\mu_1}{2},\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\mu_1-\mu_2}{2}.
\]
The previously proved mechanical and Gaussian estimates place these
parameters in the saddle rectangle for every contact distance
$9/10\le d\le11/10$ and every $0<\sigma\le1/24$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:physical-noise-form} thus proves exact physical memory
on a quantified positive-noise range. It does not infer exact counts
from an unevaluated compactness gap.

Theorem~\ref{thm:facial-schedule} gives a general schedule certificate.
Contact-forced vertices and disjoint faces supply a capacity at each
consumed-prefix configuration. Compatible residual functions supply an
upper realization. A matching path in the finite schedule graph
identifies the exact minimum across schedules and stochastic
architectures. The distinction between expected responses and complete
output channels is essential: two independent gate coins can produce
a random output with mean zero. Our certificates do not replace such
a channel by a deterministic zero.

For the full-support marked family the certificates imply
\[
  W_{\rm clk}^{\min}=12,
  \qquad \operatorname{argmin}_{\rm clock\ orders}W
                       =\{XXXYYY,YYYXXX\}.
\]
Here each letter reads the next slot of its tape, preserving the order
of the two report bits and the mark. All other eighteen interleavings
need at least fourteen states. At the ideal target the exact reverse
serial peak is instead ten. Theorems~\ref{thm:all-clock-orders} and
\ref{thm:reverse-jump} therefore show how a support change can alter
exact memory even when the law and its minimax value change continuously.
They do not identify a feedback scheduler with the fixed-clock class.

\subsection{Foundations and retained results}
The object-level route is the prepared causal experiment, its
future-test quotient, an executable causal morphism, and the attainable
resource profile. The first sections give A0--A5, conditional-mean and
entropy identities, checkpoint resolution, online implementation, and
joint calibration/model-error transport. The classical mechanisms are
stated with their hypotheses rather than taken as a universal geometric
law. The unrestricted controlled dual uses a single adversarial prior;
the finite-memory occupation formulation does not convexify persistent
selectors for free. The informative routing example remains a sharp
multicut result.

The ideal marked values $U_1$ and $U_2$, their continuous target family,
and the self-contained microscopic bridge are included in full. For
arbitrary preparation number the closest-experiment endpoint and
least-favorable-prior localization remain as proved, including the
explicit contact geometry of the ideal marked family. The present
positive-noise and scheduling results do not assert a sharp large-$N$
expansion or a finite-$N$ support law. Revelation, finite-architecture
certification, autonomous testing and transported confidence results
are retained with their distinct resource conventions. No result is
removed to obtain the new memory theorem.
For reference, the deterministic precursor constants retain their notation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constants}
 u_1=\frac{4035777}{10240000},\qquad
 m_2=\frac{12-3\sqrt3}{16},\qquad\beta_0=\frac1{300}.
\end{equation}
The sensor moments $\mu_1,\mu_2$ used above are different quantities.


\subsection{Relation to earlier work}
Classical experiment comparison and minimax supply the convex
statistical ingredients \cite{Blackwell,Sion}. Controlled sensing,
sequential experimental design and finite-horizon belief control are
established subjects \cite{Chernoff,SmallwoodSondik,NJ,NAV}.
Positive stochastic realization, invariant generating cones and
nonnegative-rank geometry have a substantial literature
\cite{Heller,Vidyasagar,GG}. Partial-input state minimization requires
compatible coverings \cite{Reusch}. Finite-memory testing and the
role of stochastic policies are likewise classical
\cite{HC,HCrandom,FH,CFH}, and the recent settings of
\cite{MP,ZSV} have their own objectives and adversaries.

We do not claim the matrix-product normal form, the elementary loss
identity, residual minimization, or dynamic programming as new.
The loss identity is recorded separately as
Proposition~\ref{prop:loss-decomposition}. The realization theorem
retains its substantive contact and compatibility statements.
The new consequences under consideration are the full-support saddle
region containing the original Brownian model, the all-declared-order
physical memory theorem, and the exact support-boundary jump.
They use contact-forced channels common to every optimizer rather than
the state count of an arbitrarily selected Bayes tie rule.
The comparison with general filtered experiments \cite{Norberg} is
not represented as a completed proof-level priority audit.

The historical geometric and unbounded-operator pipelines retain their
own assumptions and derivations. In particular no finite-state argument
here proves the Fourier/LLT, stopped-LDP, nonlinear semigroup or
strict/form-response gates of A2, B4 or C2. The new physical memory
converse is a genuine proof consumer of the foundations layer, not a
replacement label for those independent analyses.
