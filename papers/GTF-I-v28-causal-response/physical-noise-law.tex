\section{Exact statistical form of the Brownian collision sensor}
\label{sec:physical-noise-law}
The full-support theorem applies to the original microscopic target,
not only to an independently constructed contamination model. The
preparation symmetries supply the required form exactly.

\begin{theorem}[Physical symmetry and a quantitative noise region]
\label{thm:physical-noise-form}
Use exactly the preparation boxes, elastic collision, detector and
Brownian windows of Section~\ref{sec:physical-bridge}. Conditional on
the preparation put
\[
 Z=|h(v_{1,y}^{+})|,\qquad
 e=\Phi\left(-\frac{Z}{\sqrt2\sigma}\right),\qquad
 \mu_1=\mathbb E e,\quad \mu_2=\mathbb E e^2.
\]
For every $9/10\le d\le11/10$ and $0<\sigma\le1/24$ the exact
all-on target law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-physical-target}
 Q_{d,\sigma}=Q_{\gamma_{d,\sigma},\kappa_{d,\sigma}},\qquad
 \gamma_{d,\sigma}=\frac14-\frac{\mu_1}{2},\quad
 \kappa_{d,\sigma}=\frac{\mu_1-\mu_2}{2}.
\end{equation}
In particular
\[
 \frac14-\frac1{1200}<\gamma_{d,\sigma}<\frac14,
 \qquad0<\kappa_{d,\sigma}<\frac1{1200},
\]
so the entire physical range lies inside $\mathcal R$, and every target
atom has strictly positive probability. The physical two-preparation
value in the unrestricted-memory original gate interface equals
\eqref{eq:leak-value} at these parameters. Its exact decision-memory
number is five.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The collision estimate gives $Z>1/4$ for every prepared point.
The conditional two window noises are independent, of equal variance,
and the deterministic signal is constant after the collision. Thus,
given $(B,W,Z)$, the two report bits are independent copies of $B$
flipped with probability $e$.

We justify the distribution of $Z$ rather than assume a noise symmetry.
Reflection in the spatial $y$ coordinate sends
$(r_y,g_y,V_y)$ to their negatives, interchanges the two $B$ boxes,
and preserves their uniform product laws and the elastic equations.
It negates $v_{1,y}^{+}$ and leaves $Z$ unchanged. The only box that
depends on $W$ is the one for $V_z$; changing this center-of-mass
velocity cannot change the relative collision or the outgoing
$y$ velocity. All other box coordinates are independent of $W$.
Consequently $Z$, and hence $e$, has the same conditional law for all
four $(B,W)$ labels. The Brownian increments are independent of these
labels and of the preparation.

The prepared masses of $(B,W)$ are $5/16$ for equality and $3/16$
otherwise. Direct averaging therefore gives
\begin{align*}
 Q(000)=Q(111)&=5/16-5\mu_1/8+\mu_2/2,\\
 Q(001)=Q(110)&=3/16-3\mu_1/8+\mu_2/2,\\
 Q(010)=Q(011)=Q(100)=Q(101)&=(\mu_1-\mu_2)/2.
\end{align*}
These are precisely \eqref{eq:leak-target} with
\eqref{eq:exact-physical-target}. This calculation uses both the common
signal and the actual mark; replacing the common signal by independent
unconditional report errors would in general replace $\mu_2$ incorrectly
by $\mu_1^2$.

The Gaussian bound already proved in the physical bridge yields
$0<e<1/600$ uniformly in this physical range. Thus
$0<\mu_2<\mu_1<1/600$, proving the parameter bounds. Positivity follows
also directly from $0<e<1$ and positive prepared label masses.
The all-on construction works against every private candidate in
Definition~\ref{def:physical-class}; its score is the product-family
score. Conversely the common-garbling private subfamily gives the
architecture-uniform upper bound. Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-saddle}
therefore supplies both exact value and exact decision count for the
original interface, not for an auxiliary sensor.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Known target and acquired calibration]
The exact value and exact atomic stochastic realization are statements
for a specified target law. Their event-selection probability depends
on that target, not on the unknown candidate. When $d,\sigma$ and the
preparation are known, the expectations in
\eqref{eq:exact-physical-target} are defined integrals of that known
physical law. They are not free information about the private parameter.
An auditor that must acquire the target instead uses finite calibration
and a precision allowance. The following explicit bound makes this
separation operational.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Acquisition and finite row precision]
\label{prop:physical-acquisition}
Let the fixed physical target be in the preceding theorem. From $n$
independent all-on target calibration words define
\[
 \widehat\kappa=\frac1{4n}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{\{X_{1i}\ne X_{2i}\}},
 \qquad
 \widehat\gamma=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \bigl(\mathbf1_{\{000,111\}}-\mathbf1_{\{001,110\}}\bigr)(X_i).
\]
Clip these separately to the two intervals of $\mathcal R$.
With probability at least $1-4\exp(-na^2/2)$, both coordinate errors
are at most $a$. Choose the optimal response for this clipped target
and replace its tie probability by a dyadic number with error at most
$2^{-b}$. Conditional on that calibration event, its worst-case score
against the true target is at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:acquired-leak-score}
 U_2(b,Q_{d,\sigma})-9a-2^{-b}.
\end{equation}
The calibration can be acquired with at most $(n+1)^3$ counter states and at most
$4(n+1)^3$ states including the current report buffer;
retaining the calibration output during the audit gives the conservative
product bound $12(n+1)^3$ at logical report cuts. A sequential fair-bit
realization of the tie coin, with its origin and bit phase retained,
has a finite explicit bound $12(n+1)^3+8(b+2)(n+1)^3$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The off-diagonal indicator has mean $4\kappa$ and range one. The signed
diagonal indicator has mean $2a-2b=\gamma$ and range two.
Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound give the displayed failure
probability; clipping to an interval containing the truth cannot
increase coordinate error. The two target laws have total variation
at most $|\Delta\gamma|/2+4|\Delta\kappa|\le9a/2$.
Every fixed trained response changes score by at most this amount when
only the target changes, and so does the unrestricted optimal value.
Using an optimizer for the estimated target therefore loses at most
$9a$ against the true one. Only the two extreme-count event choices
are rounded. Their score contribution changes by at most the coin
error, because their total probability is at most one and the change
in a single indicator expectation has magnitude at most one. This
proves \eqref{eq:acquired-leak-score}.

Three nonnegative category counts, each bounded by $n$, compute the
two statistics; they also fix the clipped estimates and the subsequent
program choice. Keeping this count triple with the twelve-state audit
costs at most the stated product. At an extreme count, sample the
dyadic coin using $b$ fair bits and comparison with its binary threshold,
retaining the two-valued origin, the comparison flag and the phase.
At most $8(b+2)$ additional states suffice per calibration choice.
All other counts select deterministic events. The bound includes the
coin workspace instead of identifying an algebraic row with a finite
random-bit primitive. The $n$ target calibrations and the four audit
preparations are all charged.
\end{proof}
