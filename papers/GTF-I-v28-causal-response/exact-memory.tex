\section{Exact memory from a marked saddle}
\label{sec:exact-memory}
The realization criterion is now applied to a saddle with genuine Bayes
indifference. The zero coefficients in the two-preparation problem are
not harmless: their values are fixed by contact with the parameter
family. This supplies a converse for all optimal audits, rather than
for a preselected response table.

Put $I=[6/25,13/50]$. Throughout this section $r=r_\gamma$,
$t=t_\gamma$, and $v=U_2(\gamma)$ are those of
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-family}. Denote the five deterministic events in
\eqref{eq:events} by $e_0,\ldots,e_4$. Responses displayed below are
ordered on $000,001,110,111$ and vanish at the other four atoms.

\begin{theorem}[Contact rigidity and exact decision memory]
\label{thm:five-memory}
For every $\gamma\in I$, every minimax optimal full-word response with
two training preparations has, after averaging the independent training
marks at the two extreme report counts, the five rows
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rigid-five}
 (t,0,1,1),\quad (1,0,1,1),\quad (1,1,1,1),\quad
 (1,1,0,1),\quad (1,1,0,t).
\end{equation}
At the three interior counts the displayed response is forced at each
individual training word, not merely after averaging.

In the original reset row--event interface, the least number of states
at the post-training cut that permits the exact ideal score $v$ is five.
This converse allows arbitrary upstream finite memory, randomized
encoding, adaptive training gates, stopping, independent target-only
calibration, and all legal post-cut randomizations. Other cuts are
unrestricted in this decision-memory assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First consider a response to the full two-preparation word. Under the
least-favorable prior of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-family}, every training
word has positive probability. The strict coefficient signs force
$e_1,e_2,e_3$ at report counts $1,2,3$. At count zero only the $000$
coordinate is free; at count four only the $111$ coordinate is free.
All off-diagonal validation coefficients are strictly negative: the
prior parameters are interior, each word likelihood is positive, and
the target assigns zero mass to those atoms. Thus those coordinates
are zero for every optimal response.

Let $a$ and $b$ be the averages of the two free coordinates over the
four equiprobable training mark words at their respective extreme
counts. On the diagonal parameter $(p,p)$ the score is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tie-score}
 H_\gamma((2p-1)^2,0)+aA_\gamma(p)+bB_\gamma(p),
\end{equation}
where
\[
 A_\gamma(p)=(1-p)^4\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{4}
                                  -\frac{(1-p)^2}{2}\right),\qquad
 B_\gamma(p)=p^4\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{4}-\frac{p^2}{2}\right).
\]
Here $H_\gamma$ is the deterministic five-event score in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-family}. At $p_+=(1+\sqrt r)/2$,
$A_\gamma(p_+)+B_\gamma(p_+)=J_\gamma(r,0)=0$ and
$A_\gamma(p_+)>0$. The latter follows from $p_+>1/2$ and
$(1+\gamma)/4>1/4$. Reflection exchanges $A$ and $B$ at
$p_-=1-p_+$. Also $H_\gamma(r,0)=v$. Optimality forces contact at
both prior points, by Theorem~\ref{thm:saddle-realization}. Subtracting
$v$ from \eqref{eq:tie-score} therefore gives
$(a-b)A_\gamma(p_+)=0$, hence $a=b$.

With this common value the diagonal score is $H_\gamma(u,0)+aJ_\gamma(u,0)$,
where
\[
 H_\gamma(u,0)=\frac{-u^3+(7-4\gamma)u^2-3u+13+4\gamma}{32},
 \qquad J_\gamma(u,0)=-\frac{f_\gamma(u)}{64}.
\]
The supporting value $r$ lies in $(0,1)$, so the derivative at $r$
vanishes. Since $f_\gamma'(r)=D_\gamma(r)>8$, the derivative equation
has the unique solution
\[
 a=b=\frac{64\,\partial_uH_\gamma(r,0)}{D_\gamma(r)}=t_\gamma.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:rigid-five}. Individual extreme mark words may
still use different tie values; no unnecessary uniqueness is asserted.

Now take an arbitrary audit with $K$ states at the decision cut.
Restrict the candidate to the common-garbling subfamily in
Definition~\ref{def:physical-class}. Generate two all-on words and
simulate the adaptive training and its stopping with independent coins.
The retained state is a parameter-independent stochastic function of
those two words. Conditional on that state, the frozen row--event
continuation pulls back to a response $D_s\in[0,1]^8$ on the all-on
alphabet. Any calibration is part of the same encoding and retained
state. Thus \eqref{eq:decision-factor} holds without increasing $K$.
If the audit attains $v$ against the full physical class, its pullback
attains $v$ against this subfamily, since the unrestricted upper bound
is $v$. Averaging selected rows preserves their factorization.

The three interior rows in \eqref{eq:rigid-five} are distinct vertices
of $[0,1]^8$, so the generators must include $e_1,e_2,e_3$ themselves.
The first row lies in the face
\[
 F_0=\{d:d(001)=0,\ d(110)=d(111)=1,\ d=0\text{ off the diagonal}\}.
\]
Of those three obligatory vertices only $e_1$ lies in $F_0$; since
$t<1$, this row is not $e_1$. It needs another generator in $F_0$.
Similarly the last row needs another generator in
\[
 F_4=\{d:d(000)=d(001)=1,\ d(110)=0,\ d=0\text{ off the diagonal}\}.
\]
Only $e_3$ among the obligatory vertices lies there. The two faces are
disjoint. Lemma~\ref{lem:face-packing} gives $K\ge3+2=5$.
Conversely the optimal response mixes $e_0,e_1$ at count zero and
$e_4,e_3$ at count four and otherwise selects the indicated event.
Only its five-valued event label survives the cut. The inherited
compiler realizes its subsequent validation with finite memory and
attains $v$. This proves exactness of the decision count.
\end{proof}

The contact argument is essential. Setting both free coordinates equal
to one gives a Bayes best response with only the three events
$e_1,e_2,e_3$, but its derivative at $r$ is nonzero because $t<1$.
It is not minimax. Counting the states of a Bayes best response alone
would give an incorrect resource conclusion.

\begin{corollary}[A strict gap and its physical persistence]
\label{cor:strict-four}
There is $\delta_*>0$, uniform for $\gamma\in I$, such that every
two-preparation audit with at most four states at the decision cut has
ideal worst-case score at most $U_2(\gamma)-\delta_*$. In particular,
this bound holds for every original-interface audit of whole-schedule
peak at most four. If only the target changes by total variation at
most $\beta<\delta_*/2$, the five-state decision construction still
strictly outperforms this class.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $W_4(\gamma)$ be the maximum worst-case score over the relaxed
factorizations \eqref{eq:decision-factor} with $K=4$ and two full
training words. Their encoder and decoder spaces are compact and fixed,
independently of $\gamma$. The score is jointly continuous in these
variables, the target bias, and $(p,q)$. Consequently $W_4$ is continuous.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:five-memory} it is strictly less than $U_2(\gamma)$
for each $\gamma\in I$. The minimum of this positive continuous gap on
compact $I$ is positive. This defines $\delta_*$ without pretending to
have evaluated it numerically. The domination argument in that theorem
makes it an upper bound for every original-interface audit in question.
Changing only the target changes any fixed audit's mean score by at
most $\beta$. The difference between the two classes therefore decreases
by at most $2\beta$.
\end{proof}

For the microscopic family at $\gamma=1/4$, the signal is larger than
$1/4$ and the standard deviation of each normalized sensor noise is
$\sqrt2\sigma$. The Gaussian tail bound used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-physical} gives the sharper, vanishing estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vanishing-noise}
 \TV(Q_{d,\sigma},Q_*)\le
 \frac{8\sigma}{\sqrt\pi}\exp\{-1/(64\sigma^2)\},
 \qquad 9/10\le d\le11/10.
\end{equation}
Thus Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-four} holds uniformly in $d$ for all
sufficiently small positive $\sigma$. This is an existence statement
with the displayed physical modulus. The present proof does not certify
that every $\sigma\le1/24$ lies below the unknown gap threshold.

\subsection{The sharp serial-validation peak}
A decision cut and the whole execution have different resource costs.
We now determine the latter in a declared validation schedule. In the
\emph{forward serial class}, both training preparations finish before
validation; the candidate validation's two report slots and mark are
read before any target validation report is read. The same frozen
row--event continuation is used on both preparations, as in
Definition~\ref{def:physical-class}. Training gates, stochastic state
updates and frozen gate policies are unrestricted within that interface.
No intermediate output can be reread as external memory.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp forward serial peak]
\label{thm:serial-twelve}
For every $\gamma\in I$, the least whole-schedule peak width attaining
$U_2(\gamma)$ in the forward serial class is twelve. In particular, a
peak of at most eleven in this class incurs a strictly positive loss,
which can be chosen uniformly for $\gamma\in I$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the common-garbling subfamily and the two raw all-on training words
as in Theorem~\ref{thm:five-memory}. Any optimal audit must finish both
training preparations almost surely under its least-favorable prior.
Indeed a stopped branch after a first preparation would have a response
independent of the unused second word. For each first report count
$0,1,2$, two choices of the unused word force two different interior
vertices from $e_1,e_2,e_3$. Each has positive conditional probability.
A positive stopped component cannot be a convex component of two distinct
vertices. The same argument excludes stopping before the first draw.
Null branches may be completed arbitrarily.

Consider the cut after the candidate's second report slot and before
its mark. On a canonical raw input tape its continuation is a channel
from $(w,y)\in\{0,1\}\times\{000,001,110,111\}$ to
$\{-1,0,1\}$, where $w$ is the unread candidate mark and $y$ the fresh
target word. All these target words have positive probability. Each
retained state supplies one such continuation channel, common to every
past leading to it. Hence the rows indexed by selected pasts factor
through at most the peak number of generators.

For the strict interior training counts, the following eight rows are
forced deterministic channels:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eight-residuals}
 e_1(y)-f(w),\quad f\in\{0,1-w,1\};\qquad
 e_2(y)-f(w),\quad f\in\{0,1\};\qquad
 e_3(y)-f(w),\quad f\in\{0,w,1\}.
\end{equation}
For example $e_1$ has candidate residual $0$ after an off-diagonal
report pair, $1-w$ after $00$, and $1$ after $11$.
The other cases follow by restriction of their masks. Each listed past
has positive probability under both supporting candidate parameters.
The eight functions are distinct: subtracting two with the same mask
compares their distinct $w$ functions; for different masks their
difference depends nontrivially on $y$ and cannot equal a function of
$w$ alone. They are eight distinct vertices of the product of output
simplices.

There are four additional required rows. Average the extreme training
marks as in Theorem~\ref{thm:five-memory}, and take the candidate raw
report pair to be, respectively, off diagonal and $11$ at count zero,
and off diagonal and $00$ at count four. Independence of a fresh raw
candidate preparation from the frozen continuation preserves the
averaged tie probability $t$. On the four target words these rows have
means
\begin{equation}\label{eq:four-mixed-residuals}
 (t,0,1,1),\quad(t-1,-1,0,0),\quad
 (1,1,0,t),\quad(0,0,-1,t-1),
\end{equation}
for both values of $w$. More precisely, each integer coordinate here
is a point mass at that integer, and the remaining coordinate is the
Bernoulli mixture between the two neighboring integers. This stronger
channel statement, not a statement about a zero mean alone, follows
because the candidate indicator is fixed at $0$ or $1$ on the selected
raw pair, while the target indicator is a bit. Fresh gate randomness
is already part of this channel; it supplies no additional past-dependent
argument to a retained state.

The four rows lie in four pairwise disjoint faces of the product of
output simplices. These faces fix, for both $w$, respectively,
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 &000&001&110&111\\\hline
 F_{0,+}&*&0&1&1\\
 F_{0,-}&*&-1&0&0\\
 F_{4,+}&1&1&0&*\\
 F_{4,-}&0&0&-1&*
\end{array}
\]
where a number specifies a point mass and $*$ is unrestricted.
Among the eight vertices \eqref{eq:eight-residuals}, each face contains
exactly one: $e_1$, $e_1-1$, $e_3$, and $e_3-1$, respectively.
Since $t<1$, none of the four required rows is that vertex. Face
packing forces four more generators, for a total of twelve.
This argument uses conditional future-output laws and not the continued
storage of an event name after a residual has replaced it.

The optimal five-event selection followed by the inherited forward
compiler has peak twelve, including its stochastic selection cut.
It proves the matching upper bound. To see strictness below twelve, use the finite-protocol normal form
of Proposition~\ref{prop:outer-normal-form} with all logical cuts
bounded by eleven, including selection and frozen-continuation buffers.
Unobserved internal random computations can be collapsed to common
transition kernels on the same register; an unretained random duration
cannot be supplied as a later input. The normalized row spaces and
closed frozen-interface restrictions are compact. If the constrained
value equalled $U_2$, the preceding face obstruction would apply.
Continuity and compactness in $\gamma$ give a uniform strictly positive
gap, just as in Corollary~\ref{cor:strict-four}.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:serial-twelve} is a minimum-width theorem for a
specified serial order, not a claim that an arbitrary validation order
has the same minimum. The ideal target has support restrictions that
can change future residuals when the target is read first. Reverse-order
and interleaved validations require their own continuation analysis.
The decision-memory converse in Theorem~\ref{thm:five-memory} does not
make this serial-order assumption. Neither statement replaces the
original physical interface by an uncharged atomic validation.
