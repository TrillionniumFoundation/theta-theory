\section{Facial certificates for schedule-optimized memory}
\label{sec:facial-scheduling}
A schedule is part of the resource problem. The following criterion
extracts a whole family of schedule converses from a saddle, rather than
minimizing the states of one selected program. Its hypotheses are finite
identities of continuation functions and faces. Its conclusion applies
to arbitrary stochastic state encoders.

Consider finitely many validation tapes, with a fixed internal order
on each tape and finitely many symbols per slot. A \emph{declared-clock
schedule} is a word specifying which tape supplies each successive
slot; it is fixed before training and is independent of all reports.
Let $\mathcal G$ be the finite acyclic graph of consumed-prefix counts.
Its directed paths from the initial vertex to the final vertex are the
legal schedules. This includes all interleavings, not only serial ones.
The lower bound below does not give an adaptive scheduler free storage:
a report-dependent choice of the next tape belongs to a different
controlled interface and requires its own charged rows.

At a vertex $v$ of $\mathcal G$, a retained state supplies a conditional
channel on the unread tape suffixes. Assume in this section that every
prefix and suffix combination used in a witness has positive probability
under a supporting experiment. One may instead restrict to a stated
common suffix subalphabet with this property. The channels form a
product of probability simplices, denoted by $\mathcal P_v$.

\begin{definition}[Contact-forced facial certificate]
\label{def:facial-certificate}
A certificate at $v$ consists of distinct deterministic channels
$d_1,\ldots,d_s$ and channels $r_1,\ldots,r_j$, such that every
minimax-optimal auditor has these rows among its conditional
continuations, allowing parameter-independent averaging of specified
past rows. Each $r_i$ lies in a face $F_i$ of $\mathcal P_v$, the faces
are pairwise disjoint, and
\[
 r_i\notin\operatorname{conv}\{d_a:d_a\in F_i\}.
\]
Its certified capacity is $s+j$. Define $c(v)$ to be the largest such
capacity within a declared finite witness family. Conditional averaging
must retain the same future-input coordinates and may not add the
unknown parameter as an input. A channel of mean zero is not thereby a
deterministic zero channel.
\end{definition}

For an upper certificate choose a minimax-optimal finite randomization
over frozen deterministic events, with the selected event retained at
the decision cut. Let $\mathcal D_v$ be its distinct future deterministic
output functions after all possible supported prefixes at $v$.
When the input support is a full product, equality of these functions
is closed under every next-symbol restriction. Put $r(v)=|\mathcal D_v|$.
More general supported experiments may use any explicitly supplied
shift-compatible deterministic completion; merely minimizing separate
partial-function covers at different cuts is insufficient.

\begin{theorem}[Facial capacity and schedule realization]
\label{thm:facial-schedule}
Suppose contact with the unrestricted saddle supplies the preceding
certificates. For each declared-clock path $\pi$ and every stochastic
auditor on that path attaining the unrestricted value,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-lower}
             W(\pi)\ge\max_{v\in\pi}c(v).
\end{equation}
A shift-compatible deterministic residual family realizes the selected
optimal event rule simultaneously with widths $r(v)$ along any such
path. If its training and selection peak is $W_0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-upper}
 \min_{\pi}\max_{v\in\pi}c(v)
 \le W_{\rm clk}^{\min}
 \le\min_{\pi}\max\{W_0,\max_{v\in\pi}r(v)\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, a certificate and a realizing path with the same peak
identify the exact minimum over all declared-clock schedules and all
stochastic architectures on those schedules. If $c(v)=r(v)$ at every
vertex and $W_0$ does not exceed the path peak, the entire minimum-profile
problem reduces to the intrinsic capacity field on $\mathcal G$.
The peak optimum of that field is computed by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bottleneck-recursion}
 b(v_0)=c(v_0),\qquad
 b(v)=\max\{c(v),\min_{u\to v}b(u)\}.
\end{equation}
The result is invariant under bijective, causal, slot-preserving
recodings of the tape alphabets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Condition on a charged retained state at $v$. Every past row is a
stochastic mixture of the same at most $W(\pi)$ future channels.
Parameter-independent averaging preserves this factorization.
The face-packing lemma therefore gives at least $s+j$ states for each
certificate, proving \eqref{eq:path-lower}. This step ranges over
all state wirings and all stochastic encoders, not a specified graph.

For the upper bound retain the current residual output function.
If two residuals agree on the future input product, their restrictions
after each legal next symbol agree as well. Therefore the next retained
label is determined by the old label and that symbol. At the last cut
it is the output itself. This constructs all cuts compatibly in one
machine. The initially sampled event and its training selector are
charged in $W_0$. Optimizing the two bounds over the finite path set
proves \eqref{eq:path-upper}. The recursion follows by classifying
paths according to their last edge and taking the maximum with the
cost of the final vertex. Induction proves it on the acyclic graph.
Finally, a bijective recoding induces an affine isomorphism of every
channel polytope, preserving vertices, faces, conditional averages and
restriction maps. It preserves both certificates and realizing paths.
\end{proof}

The general positive-realization normal form and residual quotient have
classical antecedents \cite{Heller,Vidyasagar,GG}. The minimization of
partially specified machines likewise requires compatible coverings,
not unrelated cutwise minima \cite{Reusch}. The contribution used here
is different: the witness rows are forced by \emph{every member of the
uniform saddle}, through contact on its Bayes-indifferent coordinates.
This makes the lower field independent of a chosen optimal program.
The criterion is a certificate theorem for this class of experiments,
not an assertion that arbitrary nonnegative-rank problems have become
easy or that facial packing is always exact.
