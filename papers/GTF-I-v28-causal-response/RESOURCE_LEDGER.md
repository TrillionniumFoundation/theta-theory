# Resource conventions — revision 28

The private simulator has width one between its two emissions; that is not a free restriction on the auditor. Two candidate training preparations, one candidate validation and one target validation make four preparations. The actual mark is never supplied to the simulator in advance.

Exact decision width five is a cut complexity with arbitrary finite upstream memory allowed in the lower bound. The upper construction has five trained count labels and five selected frozen events. It retains the event or a sufficient residual through both validations. Atomic stochastic rows may have real target-dependent probabilities; those probabilities never depend on the unknown candidate.

For every positive-support target in the proved rectangle, both serial validations have the profile `(5,10,12,10,10,7,3)` from event selection onwards. Their whole execution, including two-training bit/mark updates and the stochastic selection cut, has peak twelve. Both source and target tapes retain their internal report/report/mark order. A fixed external phase declares one of twenty schedules. Every nonserial fixed schedule has a lower peak bound fourteen. No post-report scheduling oracle is included.

For the ideal target, reverse serial validation has the compatible profile `(5,5,9,9,10,7,3)` and exact peak ten. Its use of target support zeros is explicit. This is not the ideal optimum over all twenty schedules. At positive physical noise the target has full support and that shortcut ceases to be exact.

The common-garbling reduction uses raw words for a lower-bound representation only; the runtime controller receives only its legal gated observations and register. Fresh independent gate coins are allowed, but a random output of mean zero is not counted as deterministic zero. All extra-face witnesses exclude simultaneous occurrence of the sole indifferent validation atom.

For n acquired target calibrations, three category counts have at most `(n+1)^3` labels; the current report buffer gives at most `4(n+1)^3` during calibration. Retaining the calibration program with the audit costs at most `12(n+1)^3` logical states. A b-bit tie-coin implementation has the conservative total bound `[12+8(b+2)](n+1)^3`, including origin, comparison flag and bit phase. The preparation count is n+4. This construction approximates the known-target optimum; it does not assert finite-bit exact sampling of a generic algebraic coin.

An autonomous realization must additionally store phase, as in the retained phase-tag construction. None of the declared-clock exact peaks is an autonomous stationary state count.
