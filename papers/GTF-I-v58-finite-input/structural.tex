\begin{abstract}
Under eventual approximate physical returns, the minimum width of a
horizon-specific clocked stochastic realization converges to the
unrestricted stationary minimum at the same closed numerical error.
Finite limits are eventually attained, and every smaller width has a
uniform occupation bound even with unrestricted intervening registers.
For compact physical groups, a stationary stochastic realization can be
replaced without width or error loss by a finite continuous physical
action with randomized initialization. The resulting finite-quotient
criterion includes equality at a positive critical radius and groups
with infinitely many connected components. Length phases have exact
limiting minima without a phase-register overhead. We give intrinsic
radius, algebraic-effectivity and explicitly tabulated finite-group
complexity consequences, separating existence, minimization and finite
precision. The invariant counts persistent stochastic labels, not an
uncharged claim about uniform computational memory.
\end{abstract}
