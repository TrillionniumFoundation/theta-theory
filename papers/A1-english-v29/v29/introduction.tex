\subsection{Occupation measures and quantitative acquisition constraints}
The local small-ball estimates have a further consequence which keeps
the distribution of the selected path throughout the proof. For a
finite layered acquisition graph, let $c_w(t)$ bound the normalized
mass of visiting $w$ with loss at most $t$, and put
$\phi_w(x)=\int_0^1[x-c_w(t)]_+\,dt$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:v29-occupation} bounds the maximum expected checkpoint
loss below by the minimum, over one feasible occupation flow, of
$\max_j\sum_{w\in\mathscr V_j}\phi_w(x_w)$, times the actual
common-event mass. Theorem~\ref{thm:v29-relaxation-exact} shows that
this is the sharp consequence of the specified local random-path data.
It is not asserted to be the exact risk of every statistical experiment.

The first choice in a graph experiment is made before any tape entry is
revealed. Theorem~\ref{thm:v29-pre} retains that fact seed by seed and
gives a stronger perspective-flow problem. Theorem~\ref{thm:v29-dual}
provides an exact dual in terms of supporting costs and capacitated
minimum-cost flows, so feasible dual values, rather than unverified
primal optima, certify lower bounds. All earlier collision-uniform
profile comparisons and causal constructions are retained. In the
positive two-trial experiment the new all-command certificate is
$M^{-2}/8306688$ at every integer budget. Its high-resolution gap from
the sharp average coefficient is less than $4.06$.

The information conventions can be summarized as follows. The original
decoder sees the visited-set query descriptor and one retained label.
The qualitative graph converse also allows a finite order prefix; its
constants are not automatically those of the original quantitative
certificate. Equation~\eqref{eq:v29-descriptor} now gives a quantitative
bound for any additional descriptor of specified finite cardinality.
For the whole-curve results an acquisition law is fixed across predictive
budgets, while the predictive encoders may be redesigned at every
budget. For deterministic-order regret, only the permutation is shared
across resolutions. None of these statements supplies uncharged
continuous history, a common nested codebook, or an accuracy-dependent
acquisition law under the name of a fixed one.

The graph-size-uniform bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:v28-size-uniform}
use an augmented local-and-tensor query menu. By contrast, the
occupation-flow corollary below uses the original tensor task with
explicit fixed-experiment constants. These statements retain their
different loss functions and neither transfers constants to the other.
