\subsection{Adaptive order and a resolution-dependent graph width}
The same acquired flags control a problem in which the observation order
is itself chosen from data. Put independent scalar experiments on the
edges of a fixed graph. Visiting the first endpoint of an edge acquires
a block of its reports; visiting the second completes it. Memory is
charged at vertex-batch boundaries, and the future-only query is the
full tensor product of the remaining probe menus. All raw trials are
counted. The scheduler and predictor share one finite label.

An active edge has a capped determinant profile. These profiles compose
by a maximum over allocations, with each edge's acquired cap retained.
At accuracy $\varepsilon$, logarithmic inversion gives the edge weight
\[
 b_e(\varepsilon)=\max_{0\le\ell\le p_e}
       \left\{\frac{\ell}{2}\log_2\frac1\varepsilon
                                  +\log_2V_{e,\ell}\right\}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v25-adaptive} and Corollary~\ref{cor:v25-graph-bits}
show that the optimal persistent-bit budget is, to a uniformly bounded
additive error,
\[
 \min_{\pi}\max_j\sum_{e\in\delta(S_j^\pi)}b_e(\varepsilon).
\]
Here $\pi$ runs over vertex orders and $\delta(S)$ is the edge cut.
The comparison holds even against measurable history-dependent
schedulers, not only against preselected orders. At fixed calibration
it specializes to a weighted-cutwidth error exponent. The deterministic
composition law is taken from the earlier shared-memory development
\cite{QiSharedMemory2026}; it alone does not give this adaptive converse.

The additional argument uses a regular inverse chart with two-sided
Jacobian bounds. A single positive-mass event in the complete command
and report experiment supports small-ball upper bounds for every cut
and every acquired prefix. A finite union over all possible vertex
traces then permits arbitrary measurable selection of the order, without
conditioning away report probabilities or assuming conditional
independence. Its proof is in Section~\ref{sec:v25-adaptive-proof}.

In the three-edge star of Corollary~\ref{cor:v25-star}, two noncolliding
experiments compete with a third whose rank changes from six to nine.
At accuracies $\theta$ and $\theta^4$, the optimal cut partitions differ.
Every single order incurs an excess of at least
$\tfrac12\log_2(1/\theta)-O(1)$ bits at one of those accuracies.
Thus collision resolution changes the optimal order, not merely the
error rate of an already prescribed experiment. The batch protocol and
its resource conventions are defined in Section~\ref{sec:v25-graph};
Section~\ref{sec:v25-graph-width} proves these width and ordering
consequences. The proof uses the scalar acquired flags, whole-image
covers and raw updates directly, not the separate companion results.
