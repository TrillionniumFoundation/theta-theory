\section{The observation algebra and asymmetric profiles}\label{sec:algebra}
For integral exponents define the standard graded algebra
\[
 \mathcal R_A=\R[z t^a:a\in A]\subset\R[z,t],\qquad \deg(zt^a)=1.
\]
Its degree-$m$ component has basis $\{z^m t^s:s\in mA\}$, so its
Hilbert function is exactly $h_A(m)$. The general sumset--Hilbert-function
connection is classical \cite{EliahouMazumdar,Elias}. Its role here is
operational: $h_A(m)-1$ is the number of independent future observable
tests, and Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} proves when the attainable past can
excite all of them. The algebra alone would not prove that transversality.

\begin{proposition}[Three-cell classification]\label{prop:three}
Let $A=\{0,a,b\}$ with integers $0<a<b$, put
$g=\gcd(a,b)$, $a'=a/g$ and $B=b/g$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hilbert-three}
 h_A(m)=\binom{m+2}{2}
       -\one_{\{m\ge B\}}\binom{m-B+2}{2}.
\end{equation}
The homogeneous relation is generated by
$x_1^B-x_0^{B-a'}x_2^{a'}$ under the map
$(x_0,x_1,x_2)\mapsto(z,zt^a,zt^b)$. In particular,
\[
 d_A(n,m)=\min\left\{2n,
 \binom{m+2}{2}-\one_{\{m\ge B\}}\binom{m-B+2}{2}-1\right\}.
\]
For nonnegative real exponents $0<a<b$ with $a/b$ irrational,
$h_A(m)=\binom{m+2}{2}$ for every $m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A monomial $x_0^kx_1^ix_2^j$ of degree $m$ maps to $z^m t^{ai+bj}$.
Two exponent triples of the same degree have the same image exactly when
their difference is an integer multiple of
$(-(B-a'),B,-a')$: this follows by dividing
$a(i-i')+b(j-j')=0$ by $g$ and using coprimality of $a',B$.
After cancelling a common monomial, each corresponding binomial is a
multiple of $x_1^B-x_0^{B-a'}x_2^{a'}$, by the difference-of-powers
identity. The kernel is spanned by these binomials, because monomials
with distinct images are linearly independent and each fiber's coefficient
sum must vanish. Thus this one relation generates the kernel.

The degree-$m$ polynomials in three variables have dimension
$\binom{m+2}{2}$. Multiplication by the displayed nonzero degree-$B$
relation is injective in the polynomial ring, so the degree-$m$ part of
the ideal has dimension zero for $m<B$ and
$\binom{m-B+2}{2}$ otherwise. Subtraction proves
\eqref{eq:hilbert-three}. If $a/b$ is irrational,
$a(i-i')+b(j-j')=0$ forces both differences to vanish; all
$\binom{m+2}{2}$ possible pairs are distinct. The operational formula
now follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The sparse space that separates rank from future dimension]\label{cor:sparse-example}
For $A=\{0,2,5\}$, the first values of $h_A(m)$, starting at $m=0$,
are $1,3,6,10,15,20,25$. At $n=3,m=2$ the exact information dimension
is five, for every full-support prior. For a total budget of six the
minimal causal dimensions, starting at $n=0$, are
\[
 0,\ 2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 5,\ 2,\ 0.
\]
Thus the peak need not be the peak of a rank-based symmetric tent.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:hilbert-three} with $B=5$ and then
\eqref{eq:main-dimension}. In particular
$2A=\{0,2,4,5,7,10\}$, while the binomial witness at $n=3$ has
$B_{A,3}=\{0,2,5,7,10,12,15\}$. The mixed matrix has rank six
and normalization leaves five. This proves the example uniformly over
full-support priors without relying on a single large rational determinant.
The uniform-prior three-cell example and its numerical witness first
appeared in the source-pinned report \cite{A1review}; the present
corollary is an application of the general theorem, not a new attribution
of that example.
\end{proof}

There is also an exact finite computation for any integral $A$: start
with $S_0=\{0\}$, form $S_{m+1}=\{s+a:s\in S_m,a\in A\}$, and
count distinct exponents. This handles all additive collisions, not just
a nominal degree range. For real exponents the formula is equally exact
as mathematics, but an effective implementation requires exact equality
information about the supplied exponents. Neither fixed rank nor arbitrarily
accurate floating-point exponents substitute for that information.
