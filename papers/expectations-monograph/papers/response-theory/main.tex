\begin{abstract}
We develop a collision-map conormal source framework for compact
families of finite-horizon dispersing billiards with moving singularities.
The conditional finite-order calculus uses a period-one response chart with a genuine
no-boundary-pair-birth collar, finite physical Ward data, and a separate
triangular strong-primitive certificate for assembled sources.  One-face
currents and clean two-face atoms are placed in a label-resolved quotient
Banach module rather than the ordinary Demers--Zhang weak completion.  Scalar
susceptibilities are asserted only after complete source recovery; periodic
point atoms, moving-face jumps, and a fourth-order grazing atom give explicit
obstructions to an eventwise all-seed theory.
All unconditional spectral gaps and density resolvents act on the transported
Demers--Zhang dynamical strong bundle.  The stronger positive flux-seed scale
is used for parameter traces only and is not asserted transfer invariant.

For a genuinely nonconjugate radial inflation of one circular scatterer, the
complete first- and second-order invariant assemblies recover to centered
strong densities, uniformly after summing all insertion positions at every
fixed positive waiting length.  Historical terminal labels need not close:
any surviving time-labelled terminal germ accumulates linearly.  An
assembly-first reset nevertheless yields finite and countable scalar
susceptibilities under summable primitive and reset bounds.  Within each
incident label, physical regrouping removes the artificial \(2^b\)
homogeneity-strip entropy.  A fixed-atlas block-flux graph seminorm records
the formal first-letter estimates \(2^{-(3-\epsilon_*)b}\) and
\(2^{-(1-\epsilon_*)b}\), but no chart-independent operator theorem is
claimed.  The calculation does not extend to
the isolated second letter: the circular impact angle has
\(\partial_s^2\alpha\asymp u^{-6}\), leaving the nonintegrable flux
coefficient \(u^{-3}\).  Higher radial response must therefore use specified
complete assemblies.  An explicit exact-current benchmark is supplied by every transported
collision observable \(a_s\): the exact current
\((I-\mathscr L_s)[(a_s-\langle\rho_s,a_s\rangle)\rho_s]\) passes all
represented Poisson recovery tests through order \(J\) and has a telescoping
susceptibility.  A common rigid translation supplies an independently
prescribed correlation-density consistency example: in the exact conjugacy
trivialization its Poisson sources and strong remainder vanish identically and
its susceptibility is \(C^J\).  This is not a moving-face cancellation.  A nonzero
differentiated-invariance radial benchmark satisfies
the total-current \(q_1,q_2\) identities and has a twice differentiable
infinite susceptibility.  These identities do not certify the label-resolved
terminal reset and strong remainder in the general Poisson criterion.
On the dynamically cut, \(L^\infty\)-anchored, branch-cocycle-weighted
primitive domain constructed below, every compact regular moving-face subarc
carries a split-surjective gluing map:
its kernel is complemented and has infinite codimension.  The same statement
holds first on a Poisson-independent weighted local-potential class and then on
its Poisson-pullback correlation graph class.  The complement of the passing
set is open and dense in each stated topology, and
the affine obstruction recurs at every finite source-jet order.  Under the
same weighted trace construction, the split surjection persists in every
finite-group isotypic space on a free orbit
of face subarcs, so parity does not supply the missing Ward identity.  A
separate geometric proposition proves that no nontrivial finite physical
isometry fixes an open regular grazing-face subarc pointwise; hence the only
abstract stabilizer exception is unavailable for dispersing-table symmetries.
A local Noether-type primitive faces a separate rigidity: its source must have
zero sum on every regular periodic orbit.  These orbit-sum functionals have
arbitrarily large finite rank, while an invariant local charge gives only the
zero source and a noninvariant increment is the exact-current benchmark.
At primitive level, face profiles and finitely many primitive-orbit values
are jointly split; source-orbit sums instead vanish whenever that primitive
is single-valued, as required by telescoping.  Finite-memory label rules passing
all coded periodic sums are cylinder coboundaries.

For continuous time we retain the corrected fixed-table BDL-v3 input and
three obstructions only.  Flow-box oscillations rule out transverse resolvent smoothing,
and generic deformation letters have a necessary \(u^{-2}\) grazing
multiplier.  Although \(|v\cdot n|=u^2\) is reflection-seam invariant, the
ratio between consecutive collision weights is unbounded near tangency; a
global theory therefore needs a dynamical branch/preimage weight, not scalar
pointwise conjugation.  No moving-family graph-domain or symbol theorem is
claimed; those constructions are left as future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

This paper develops conormal source modules and a conditional finite-order
response calculus for finite-horizon
dispersing billiards whose singularity sets move with a compact mechanical
parameter.  The central problem is to combine the singular collision geometry
with billiard-specific anisotropic transfer scales and a moving-boundary
conormal calculus.  The BDL suspension problem is treated separately because
a generic table deformation produces generator derivatives transverse to the
flow direction.
The resulting package can later be used in deterministic homogenization, but no
macroscopic equation or probabilistic representation is used as an input here.

\paragraph{Scope and separation of hypotheses.}
The primitive inputs are the fixed-energy dispersing billiard cell, smooth
scatterers with uniform curvature and separation bounds, uniform finite
horizon, specular reflection, and compact finite-response parameter data.  The
period-one simple stratification, the nonconjugate operator Ward certificate,
and the triangular strong-primitive certificate are additional analytic
hypotheses, stated explicitly in
\cref{ass:finite_order_clean_response_chart,ass:response_scale_dynamics}.  They
are not inferred from graph connectedness or compactness alone.  At no point
is a singular weak density declared strong merely because a later iterate is
regular.  Parameter-uniform corrected BDL-v3 graph and symbol estimates are
not part of the collision-map theorem; the retained continuous-time content
is the fixed-table input and obstruction in
\cref{prop:fixed_table_bdl_correspondence,prop:flow_obstruction_trichotomy}.

\paragraph{Main contribution and limitation.}
The central contribution is the collision-map moving-singularity source calculus:
physical trace currents, decorated clean two-wall atoms, repeated-wall normal
derivatives, and source-specific scalar insertions on top of uniform
finite-horizon billiard transfer estimates.  The abstract construction is
finite order, as every response theorem with finite primitive smoothness
must be, but its application beyond first order is source-specific.  The dynamical target is the
reduced collision-map susceptibility on a certified recovered response tree;
static invariant averages are differentiated directly from the explicit flux
measure.  Construction of that certificate for a genuinely nonconjugate
higher-order all-branch response tree remains open.  Radial inflation supplies
the source-specific invariant blocks and an exact observable-current benchmark,
but not the generic S1/S3 tree.  The continuous-time obstruction identifies,
rather than hides, the extra graph-domain and symbol estimates still needed.
The no-go mechanisms show that the missing theorem cannot be obtained by
extending the present certificates to all ambient sources: it must exploit the
special algebra of a complete physical response tree.
For an isolated genuinely moving face, the primitive gluing map on the
dynamically cut weighted trace domain is locally surjective onto smooth trace
profiles; its kernel therefore has
infinite codimension and is complemented on every fixed compact trace subarc.
This rules out repairing a generic correlation source
by tuning finitely many observable coefficients.  After a recovered
first source, the admissible first jets for the \(q_2\) face test form another
affine infinite-codimension slice.  The defect map is already split surjective
on the Poisson-independent weighted local-potential class.  The pullback
observable graph class gives the corresponding correlation statement; its
open-dense assertion is only in that explicit graph topology.  The same
complemented affine constraint recurs on each subsequent graph-domain
observable jet.
Finite geometric symmetry does not repair this: projecting the local right
inverse to any isotypic component leaves the defect map split surjective on every free face
orbit, while strict convexity rules out an open pointwise-fixed physical
grazing face.
Nor does a hidden local conservation law generically repair the problem:
every continuous local primitive forces all periodic-orbit sums of the source
to vanish, an independent infinite family of conditions.
At base response level we prove a useful converse: a centered ambient source
has a strong primitive exactly when its represented complete assembly belongs
to the centered strong density space.  Recovery is therefore reduced to
cancellation of every surviving face and point current, followed by the
ordinary reduced density resolvent; separate terminal bookkeeping still needs
either eventwise cancellation or assembly-first scalar reset.

\paragraph{Sharp boundary of the positive theorem.}
Flux conjugacy proves the finite operator Ward identities for a nonempty
symmetry class, including common rigid table translations, but
\cref{prop:flux_conjugate_symmetry_limitation} shows that it is not a
nonconjugate dynamical example.  For a general
independent scatterer deformation the Ward identities are a finite certificate,
not a consequence of primitive geometry.  Likewise, the ambient source scale
propagates decorated point currents for finite time but supplies no decay: a
regular periodic orbit gives a direct counterexample.  Infinite dynamical
words are therefore stated only for a certified recovered tree.  The generic
re-insertion theorem and the moving-flow symbol estimate remain separate new
problems rather than hidden consequences of density mixing or compactness.
In particular, the periodic-atom and fourth-order endpoint obstructions rule
out a uniform certificate on all point sources or all localized smooth seeds.
The recent small-hole response theorem
\cite{Canestrari2026SmallHoles} uses a different, genuinely positive coupling
mechanism: its vertical-line source is pushed to a regular unstable standard
family.  A physical moving-scatterer grazing trace instead has a one-sided
image on the grazing seam and is terminal in the present atlas.  Thus the
small-hole coupling theorem is an important model but does not verify (S1) or
(S3) for the sources considered here.
The abstract discontinuous-perturbation mechanism of
\cite{Canestrari2026Discontinuous} likewise requires a perturbation-uniform
mixing estimate for the limiting singular profile.  For correlation response,
one needs a two-time weighted version of that estimate.  Such estimates are
isolated as conditional-mixing hypotheses in
\cite{Wormell2024ConditionalMixing,Wormell2024PiecewiseResponse}; the
specialized form used for piecewise hyperbolic linear response remains
conjectural even in the Lozi model.  We record below the exact billiard
certificate rather than cite those works for a theorem they do not prove.
The radial-inflation example below is genuinely nonconjugate (a period-two
multiplier changes) and verifies this singular-sector cancellation for the
complete invariant source through order two.  Its fixed-length block recovery
allows arbitrary positive gaps after all insertion positions are summed; it
does not verify eventwise re-insertion for a general dynamical susceptibility.

\paragraph{Informal result map.}
For every compact finite-response family of finite-horizon dispersing billiard
tables satisfying the displayed geometric and analytic compatibility
assumptions below, the paper
constructs:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item an a.e. singular hyperbolic collision map with uniform
  homogeneity-strip, cone, distortion, and growth estimates;
  \item strong/weak anisotropic transfer scales, a spectral gap, and an ambient
  decorated source scale for one-face and point-current outputs;
  \item moving-singularity trace currents in transported Banach fibers, with
  finite-order observable-side response only along a certified recovered
  tree;
  \item a scalar calculus for certified recovered dynamical susceptibilities,
  together with the exact geometric reduction of static invariant averages and
  an obstruction to generic point-source decay.
\end{enumerate}
The source-specific billiard statement is \cref{thm:main_response_package};
the general certificate-based implication is
\cref{thm:certified_abstract_conormal_calculus}; the continuous-time boundary
is \cref{prop:flow_obstruction_trichotomy}.

\subsection{Relation to known billiard response theory}

The paper uses classical dispersing-billiard and anisotropic-transfer theory in
a layered way.  The hyperbolicity, homogeneity-strip, growth-lemma, and
standard-family mechanisms come from singular billiards
\cite{Sinai1970,KatokStrelcyn1986,chernov2006chaotic,ChernovZhang2009}; the
strong/weak transfer architecture for billiards follows
\cite{DemersZhang2011,DemersZhang2013}, with the general compactness mechanism
from \cite{Hennion1993,Baladi2000};
and the suspension estimates use the Dolgopyat finite-horizon billiard-flow
route \cite{Dolgopyat1998,BaladiDemersLiverani2018}.  What is local to this
paper is the finite-response stability, renormalized grazing-block scale, resolved
moving-boundary currents, and their observable-side scale pairing.
Detailed citation and dependency conventions are recorded in
\cref{app:citation_dependency_conventions}.

\subsection{Moving singularities and response obstructions}

For systems with moving discontinuities, differentiability of statistical
averages is not a formal consequence of smooth parameter dependence.  In
piecewise expanding unimodal maps, for example, linear response can fail unless
the perturbation is compatible with the relevant topological class
\cite{BaladiSmania2008}.  This paper avoids that obstruction by not
differentiating a pointwise invariant density across a moving partition.
Instead the first parameter derivative is taken in transported anisotropic
fibers, moving boundaries are represented by trace functionals, and a recovered
source is paired only through a source-specific scalar functional.  The theorem is scoped
to the finite conormal and decorated ambient source scales and their renormalized physical
grazing-block identity, not to arbitrary moving discontinuities.

Two nearby billiard perturbation settings should be distinguished.  The
moving-scatterer model of Stenlund--Young--Zhang
\cite{StenlundYoungZhang2013} allows the table to change between collisions and
proves uniform loss of memory for nonstationary compositions; here the table is
stationary for each parameter and the problem is differentiability of invariant
statistics inside one transported Banach bundle.  The small-force works of
Chernov and Chernov--Zhang--Zhang
\cite{Chernov2001,ChernovZhangZhang2014} perturb the flight dynamics and study
transport/current response.  In contrast, the present perturbation moves the
scatterer boundaries and therefore creates moving singular curves; the new
load-bearing object here is the first physical trace pairing.

\section{Uniform Collision and Spectral Input}

\begin{definition*}[Billiard cell, collision section, and flux]
For each parameter $\theta\in\Thetacal$, the billiard cell is
\begin{equation}
  Q_\theta=\T^2\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^N K_i(\theta),
  \qquad \Gamma_\theta=\partial Q_\theta .
\end{equation}
The velocity shell is the unit circle.  The collision section is the outgoing
boundary phase space
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_\theta
  =\{(q,v):q\in\Gamma_\theta,\ |v|=1,\ \langle v,n(q,\theta)\rangle\ge0\},
\end{equation}
with local coordinates $z=(i,s,\varphi)$, where $s$ is boundary arclength and
$\varphi\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ is the angle with the inward normal.  The reference
flux is
\begin{equation}
  d\nu_\theta=Z_\theta^{-1}\cos\varphi\,ds\,d\varphi ,
\end{equation}
where \(Z_\theta\) is the normalizing flux constant.  The symbol
\(\mathfrak c_\theta\) is reserved below for crossing speeds.  Throughout,
\(\nu_\theta\) denotes the collision-section flux reference measure, whereas
\(\mu_\theta\) denotes the invariant probability measure or its suspension lift
after the invariant density has been constructed.
The billiard map $\Pcal_\theta$ follows the free flight to the next regular
impact and then applies specular reflection; the roof function is the flight
time $\tau_\theta$.  Grazing, multiple-impact, and branch-change states form the
one-step singular set $\Ssing_\theta$.
\end{definition*}

\begin{remark*}[Invariant-density convention]
In the moving collision fiber \((\Sigma_\theta,\nu_\theta)\), the billiard map
preserves \(\nu_\theta\), so the collision invariant density is
\(\rho_\theta\equiv1\).  When all objects are pulled back to a fixed reference
fiber by \(\mathfrak T^{\rm den}_{\theta_0\leftarrow\theta}\), this constant
density becomes the transported density including the transport Jacobian.  Thus
symbols such as \(\nabla_s\rho_{\theta_s}|_0\) and
\(\nabla_s\Ltransfer_{\theta_s}|_0\rho_{\theta_0}\) are always interpreted in
the fixed transported fiber; the derivative of \(Z_\theta^{-1}\cos\varphi\) and
of the density-transport Jacobian is included in the transfer derivative
letters.
\end{remark*}

\begin{proposition}[Direct geometric derivative of static collision averages]
\label{prop:direct_geometric_invariant_average}
Let \(\Phi_\theta:\Sigma_{\theta_0}\to\Sigma_\theta\) be the chosen collision-
section transport and write
\[
  J_\theta
  =\frac{d(\Phi_\theta^*\nu_\theta)}{d\nu_{\theta_0}}.
\]
For a \(C^J\) transported observable \(f_\theta\), the static invariant
collision average is
\[
 I(\theta)=\int_{\Sigma_{\theta_0}}
   (f_\theta\circ\Phi_\theta)J_\theta\,d\nu_{\theta_0},
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:direct_geometric_static_average}
 \partial_\theta^j I(\theta_0)
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^j\binom j\ell
  \int
  \partial_\theta^\ell(f_\theta\circ\Phi_\theta)|_{\theta_0}
  \,\partial_\theta^{j-\ell}J_\theta|_{\theta_0}
  \,d\nu_{\theta_0}.
\end{equation}
In particular a static invariant average needs no dynamical resolvent.  Any
transfer/resolvent representation of its derivative must equal
\eqref{eq:direct_geometric_static_average}; this equality is used below as a
consistency identity, not as the source of static differentiability.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The invariant collision density is \(1\) relative to \(\nu_\theta\).  Pull the
integral back by \(\Phi_\theta\) and differentiate the product
\((f_\theta\circ\Phi_\theta)J_\theta\) \(j\) times.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Finite-response parameter data]
\label{def:finite_response_chart}
The admissible parameter set $\Thetacal$ is a compact $C^{r+3}$ manifold with
finite atlas, or a compact subset of such a manifold after restricting to one
atlas chart.  Locally, every scatterer boundary is moved by a compact normal
graph
\begin{equation}
  q_i(s,\theta)=q_i(s,\theta_0)+a_i(s,\theta)n_i(s,\theta_0),
  \qquad \|a_i\|_{C^{r+3}}\le C_{\rm par}.
\end{equation}
The map $\theta\mapsto a_i(\cdot,\theta)$ is $C^{r+3}$ into a bounded subset of
$C^{r+3}(\partial K_i)$.  Any microscopic drift, action density, or observable
read-out used by a response functional is specified as a transported section of
the branchwise observable space and is \(C^J\) in $\theta$ after the
homogeneous-branch trivialization.  Thus the admissible input data are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(P\arabic*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item the compact parameter set and finite atlas;
  \item the normal graph fields $a_i$ with uniform $C^{r+3}$ bounds;
  \item the induced table transports, roof/read-out sections, and branchwise
  parameter derivatives through order \(J\).
\end{enumerate}
No effective coefficient, homogenized equation, Green--Kubo tensor, or value
function is part of this finite-response datum.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Primitive finite-horizon dispersing billiard family]
\label{ass:primitive_family}
For every parameter $\theta\in\Thetacal$, the billiard cell is a fixed-energy
two-dimensional periodic dispersing billiard whose scatterers satisfy:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(G\arabic*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item each scatterer boundary is a closed $C^{r+3}$ curve depending $C^{r+3}$
  on $\theta$ in local normal coordinates;
  \item the dispersing curvature is uniformly bounded above and below by positive
  constants;
  \item physical scatterer boundaries are corner-free and remain uniformly
  separated;
  \item the family has uniform finite horizon: there are
  $0<\tau_-<\tau_+<\infty$ such that every regular free flight satisfies
  $\tau_-\le\tau_\theta(z)\le\tau_+$;
  \item the parameter family is a compact $C^{r+3}$ normal-graph family generated
  by the finite-response parameter data of
  \cref{def:finite_response_chart}, with uniform curvature, separation, and
  finite-horizon constants;
  \item the free region and the collision graph are connected.
\end{enumerate}
No spectral gap, trace summability, response formula, effective coefficient, or
homogenized equation is included in this primitive input layer.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark*}[Corners]
The physical scatterers in \cref{ass:primitive_family} have no boundary
corners.  Later references to ``partition corners'' or ``multiple-impact
corners'' refer only to artificial endpoints of refined homogeneous charts or
to lower-dimensional multiple-impact strata inserted into the singular
partition; they are not geometric corners of $\Gamma_\theta$.
\end{remark*}

\begin{assumption}[Finite response order and primitive reserve]
\label{ass:response_order_convention}
Fix an integer response order \(J\ge1\) and take \(r\ge J+4\).
Scatterer charts and normal-graph transports are \(C^{r+3}\), and the
finite-response observables are branchwise \(C^r\).  The proof constructs
parameter derivatives through order \(J\).  The map response uses the four
displayed reserve levels below.  No moving-family continuous-time response
order or derivative-loss budget is asserted in this paper.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Checkable flux-conjugate deformation class]
\label{def:flux_conjugate_deformation_class}
A \(C^J\) normal-graph family is \emph{flux-conjugate through order \(J\)} if
there is a \(C^J\) family of phase diffeomorphisms
\(\Psi_\theta:\Sigma_{\theta_0}\to\Sigma_\theta\) which preserves the grazing
boundary, the simple face labels, and the normalized collision flux, and for
which
\[
 \Pcal_\theta\circ\Psi_\theta
 =\Psi_\theta\circ\Pcal_{\theta_0},
 \qquad
 \Ltransfer_\theta
 =\mathcal C_\theta\Ltransfer_{\theta_0}\mathcal C_\theta^{-1}
\]
through parameter order \(J\), where \(\mathcal C_\theta\) is the induced
density transport.  These identities are checked before differentiating branch
indicators.  Common rigid translations of all scatterers on the flat torus,
with \(\Psi_\theta(q,v)=(q+\theta w,v)\), and tangential boundary
reparametrizations are examples.  A generic independent normal deformation of
one scatterer is not declared flux-conjugate.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Finite one-step transversality and physical Ward data]
\label{ass:finite_order_clean_response_chart}
Fix a base parameter \(\theta_0\).  The following conditions are imposed only
on the finitely many labelled one-step branch types and physical grazing germs,
represented on the countable homogeneous-strip atlas.  Long times will subsequently
be handled by operator iterates; no all-iterate singularity isotopy is assumed.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(R\arabic*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item The collision map is restricted to a fixed period-one mixing
  component.  Period one is not inferred from connectedness of the undirected
  scatterer graph.
  \item Each active one-step face has a \(C^J\) defining function
  \(g_{a,\theta}\) on a compact rescaled chart.  There are a parameter
  neighbourhood \(U_0\ni\theta_0\), a grazing collar \(0\le u<u_J\), and
  constants \(\delta_J,c_J>0\) with the following properties for every
  \(\theta\in U_0\).  The labelled stratification is \emph{simple}: every
  point belongs to at most two active physical faces and no triple-labelled
  vertex occurs.  Distinct physical faces have no common zero in the grazing
  collar,
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:no_boundary_pair_birth}
    \{g_{a,\theta}=g_{b,\theta}=0\}\cap\{0\le u<u_J\}
      =\varnothing\qquad(a\ne b).
  \end{equation}
  On the central region \(u\ge u_J\), every admitted meeting pair is rank two
  with
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:central_clean_determinant}
    |\det(Dg_{a,\theta},Dg_{b,\theta})|\ge2c_J,
  \end{equation}
  and every nonmeeting labelled pair has distance at least \(2\delta_J\).
  Repeated generators are recorded as normal derivatives of one face.  A
  boundary pair birth/death, a distinct-face tangency, or a triple meeting
  requires a separate unfolding model and lies outside this response chart.
  \item The operator Ward mechanism is supplied in one of two checkable ways.
  Either the family is flux-conjugate through order \(J\) in the sense of
  \cref{def:flux_conjugate_deformation_class}, or, after differentiating the complete branch identity, cancelling every
  artificial face, and integrating all normal delta derivatives by parts, let
  \(Q_j\) be the finite set of nonnegative orders of residual endpoint
  currents which actually occur, let \(W_{j,q}\) be the coefficient of
  \(\delta^{(q)}(u)\), and let \(F^{\rm reg}_j\) be the remaining regular
  coefficient.  The operator-level physical Ward conditions are
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:finite_physical_ward_conditions}
    W_{j,q}=0\quad(q\in Q_j),
    \qquad F^{\rm reg}_j(0,s;\rho,\psi)=0,
    \qquad 1\le j\le J,
  \end{equation}
  as identities on the smooth flux-seed/test core.  In the flux-conjugate case
  these identities follow from \cref{prop:flux_conjugacy_implies_operator_ward};
  in the general case they are an additional finite Ward certificate.  The first \(u\)-derivative
  of \(F_j^{\rm reg}\), with the required unstable-holonomy differences, is
  bounded by the order-\(j+2\) flux-seed and order-\(j+3\) trace-test norms.
\end{enumerate}
Conditions (R2)--(R3) are finite, base-table conditions.  They do not quantify
over singularity preimages of arbitrary length.  They are not consequences of
the unweighted growth lemma or of the fixed cubic flux zero.  The exact
invariant source satisfies a global Ward identity proved below; (R3) is the
strictly stronger operator identity needed when derivative letters act on an
arbitrary response seed.  When \(J\ge4\), the arbitrary-seed alternative is
not generic: \cref{prop:sharpness_clean_ward_data} shows that a physical
grazing face with nonzero first parameter velocity violates it already at
order four.  Thus a nonconjugate theorem must use cancellation of a specified
complete assembled source, rather than postulating (R3) on every seed.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Flux conjugacy implies the operator Ward identities]
\label{prop:flux_conjugacy_implies_operator_ward}
Every family in \cref{def:flux_conjugate_deformation_class} satisfies
\eqref{eq:finite_physical_ward_conditions} through order \(J\) for every smooth
flux seed.  Hence common rigid table translations and tangential
reparametrizations give a nonempty normal-graph class for which (R3) is a
theorem rather than an endpoint assumption.  This proposition does not prove
the separate all-branch response-scale bound (S1).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Transport by \(\Psi_\theta\) fixes the labelled branch partition, including
the grazing face, and preserves the complete normalized branch flux.  On the
fixed fiber the transfer family is therefore the conjugate family
\(\mathcal C_\theta\Ltransfer_0\mathcal C_\theta^{-1}\), with no moving
indicator.  Differentiating this equality \(j\le J\) times gives a finite sum
of commutators of the smooth transport generators with \(\Ltransfer_0\).
Writing the same equality in the resolved branch atlas and applying Stokes to
each branch, internal faces cancel in oppositely oriented pairs.  Because
\(\Psi_\theta\) preserves \(u=0\) and its flux, the sum has no distribution
supported only at the endpoint.  Thus every \(W_{j,q}\) and
\(F_j^{\rm reg}(0)\) in (R3) is zero.  The chain rule for the face-preserving
\(C^J\) diffeomorphism \(\Psi_\theta\) preserves the resulting finite
distributional identities.  Bounds on the completed countable branch scale are
not inferred from this finite Stokes calculation; they remain the distinct
analytic statement (S1).

A common torus translation is a phase
conjugacy preserving \(ds\,\cos\varphi\,d\varphi\); a tangential
reparametrization changes only the boundary chart.  Both examples satisfy the
definition directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Symmetry limitation of the flux-conjugate class]
\label{prop:flux_conjugate_symmetry_limitation}
For a flux-conjugate family, every finite-time collision correlation is the
corresponding reference correlation with transported observables.  Consequently
the class in \cref{def:flux_conjugate_deformation_class} verifies the Ward
mechanism but does not constitute a nonconjugate deformation of the billiard
dynamics.  In particular it cannot, by itself, verify (S2)--(S3) for the
conormal response tree of an independently moving scatterer.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Insert \(\Pcal_\theta\Psi_\theta=\Psi_\theta\Pcal_0\), transport the invariant
flux by \(\Psi_\theta\), and change variables in each finite-time correlation.
Only the transported observables remain parameter dependent.  The last
statement follows because an independent scatterer deformation is not covered
by the conjugacy identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local clean atlas and finite-order flux factorization]
\label{thm:local_clean_atlas_flux_factorization}
Under \cref{ass:primitive_family,ass:finite_order_clean_response_chart}, after
shrinking to a compact parameter neighbourhood \(U\ni\theta_0\):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item the labelled one-step branch domains form a parameter-uniform simple
  \(C^J\) stratified isotopy with constant face incidence and no triple
  vertices; distinct-face intersections occur only in the central region
  \(u\ge u_J\), while the grazing collar contains only one-face strata;
  \item every admitted distinct-face intersection satisfies
  \[
    |\det(Dg_{a,\theta},Dg_{b,\theta})|
      \ge c_J;
  \]
  \item the complete order-\(j\) grazing coefficient has the exact
  factorization
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:proved_flux_factorization}
    F_{j,m}(u,s;\rho,\psi)
       =u\,\widetilde F_{j,m}(u,s;\rho,\psi),
    \qquad 1\le j\le J,
  \end{equation}
  with the rescaled coefficient and holonomy differences bounded uniformly in
  the dyadic block.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the geometry needed by a derivative letter is constructed once
at one step.  The arbitrary times in a resolvent word are powers of the
undifferentiated transfer operator and do not require an isotopy of all
iterated singularity preimages.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
There are finitely many scatterer labels and, by finite horizon, finitely many
one-step lift labels in a fundamental cell.  In the compact blown-up atlas the
functions \(g_{a,\theta}\) are \(C^J\).  Condition
\eqref{eq:no_boundary_pair_birth} removes the boundary-fold mechanism before
the implicit-function theorem is used: no distinct pair can enter from or
leave through \(u=0\).  On the compact central region, the uniform rank-two
bound \eqref{eq:central_clean_determinant} gives \(C^J\) labelled
pair-intersection charts, while the uniform distance bound preserves every
nonincidence.  Simplicity and exclusion of triple-labelled vertices then give
constant incidence.  The one-face charts in the grazing collar are transported
separately and never promoted to two-face corners.  This proves (i)--(ii) with
one genuine determinant lower bound and without an infinite union of preimages
or determinant sublevel shells.

  Finally, the distribution identity
\(u^3\delta^{(n)}(u)=-n(n-1)(n-2)\delta^{(n-3)}(u)\) shows that the complete
  list of residual endpoint currents through order \(J\) is the finite set
  \(Q_j\) in \eqref{eq:finite_physical_ward_conditions}.  Those coefficients
vanish by (R3), so only \(F_j^{\rm reg}\) remains.  The fundamental theorem of
calculus gives
\[
 F_j^{\rm reg}(u,s)=u\int_0^1
   \partial_uF_j^{\rm reg}(tu,s)\,dt.
\]
The derivative bound in (R3), applied also to matched holonomy differences,
is exactly the asserted uniform bound for \(\widetilde F_{j,m}\).  Artificial
faces have already cancelled before this argument, so no boundary term is
discarded.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness of the clean and Ward data]
\label{prop:sharpness_clean_ward_data}
The primitive assumptions alone do not imply the conclusions of
\cref{thm:local_clean_atlas_flux_factorization}.  In local coordinates the
  boundary-fold family
\[
  g_{1,s}(x,y)=y,\qquad g_{2,s}(x,y)=y-x^2+s
\]
  has no intersection for one sign of \(s\), a tangency at \(s=0\), and two
  intersections for the other sign, with determinant \(2|x|=2\sqrt{|s|}\) at
  the intersections.  On the half-chart \(x=u\ge0\) it is exactly a pair born
  from the grazing boundary and therefore violates
  \eqref{eq:no_boundary_pair_birth}.  Likewise the local moving-cutoff model
\(u^3H(u-s)\) satisfies
\[
  \partial_s^4[u^3H(u-s)]|_{s=0}=u^3\delta^{(3)}(u)=-6\delta(u)
\]
up to the fixed orientation sign.  Thus neither stratified nonbifurcation nor
finite-order endpoint cancellation follows from smooth parameter dependence,
uniform curvature, separation, finite horizon, or the cubic flux zero.
More precisely, for
\[
 c_s(u,y)u^3H(u-\eta_s(y)),\qquad
 c_0(0,y_0)\ne0,\quad\dot\eta_0(y_0)\ne0,
\]
the coefficient of \(\delta(u)\) in the fourth derivative at \(y_0\) is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fourth_order_arbitrary_seed_ward_obstruction}
 -6\,\dot\eta_0(y_0)^4c_0(0,y_0).
\end{equation}
Consequently an operator Ward identity required for every localized smooth
seed/test cannot hold through order four on such a moving germ.  In
particular, a first-order nonconjugate deformation which moves a physical
grazing face cannot satisfy the arbitrary-seed version of (R3) for
\(J\ge4\) unless an additional exact identity removes that germ before the
localization is made.
Moreover, pairwise transversality alone does not imply constant incidence:
\[
 g_{1,s}(x,y)=x,\qquad g_{2,s}(x,y)=y,\qquad
 g_{3,s}(x,y)=x+y-s
\]
has three pairwise transverse faces with determinants \(\pm1\).  They meet at
one triple-labelled point when \(s=0\), but for \(s\ne0\) the three pairwise
intersections are distinct.  This is why simplicity/no-triple-unfolding is an
actual hypothesis rather than the vacuous statement that three independent
normals cannot occur in dimension two.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The fold and triple-incidence statements are the displayed direct
calculations.  For the Ward obstruction, differentiate
\(H(u-\eta_s)\) four times.  The unique highest normal derivative is
\(\dot\eta_0^4\delta^{(3)}(u)\); every term containing a parameter derivative
of \(c_s\) or a higher parameter jet of \(\eta_s\) carries at most
\(\delta^{(2)}(u)\).  Multiplication by \(u^3\) kills the latter terms, while
\(u^3\delta^{(3)}=-6\delta\), proving
\eqref{eq:fourth_order_arbitrary_seed_ward_obstruction}.  Localizing the seed
and test near one physical germ prevents cancellation by a disjoint branch.
These calculations
show that replacing (R2)--(R3) by compactness or by pairwise rank alone would
make the main theorem false rather than stronger.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Singular collision geometry}

\begin{definition}[Homogeneity strips, cones, and standard curves]
\label{def:strips_cones_standard_curves}
For $k\ge k_0$ define the grazing strips
\begin{equation}
  \Hstrip_k^\pm
  =\left\{(s,\varphi):
  \frac{1}{(k+1)^2}<
  \frac{\pi}{2}\mp\varphi<\frac{1}{k^2}\right\},
\end{equation}
and let $\Hstrip_0$ be the complement of the union of these boundary strips.
A homogeneous component is a connected component of the complement of
singularity curves and strip boundaries.  For a nonvertical tangent vector
$u=(ds,d\varphi)$ write
\begin{equation}
  |u|_p=\cos\varphi\,|ds|,
  \qquad
  \mathcal B(u)=\frac{d\varphi}{ds}+\kappa(s,\theta),
  \qquad
  \mathcal K(u)=\frac{\mathcal B(u)}{\cos\varphi}.
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal B\) is the collision-coordinate numerator, while
\(\mathcal K\) is the physical wavefront curvature measured in transverse
Jacobi arclength.  Only \(\mathcal K\) obeys the free-flight Riccati recurrence
used in the appendix.
The unstable cone is $\mathcal C^u_\theta(z)=\{u:ds\ne0,\mathcal B(u)\ge a_-\}$
with strip truncation
\(\mathcal B(u)\le a_+k(z)^2\); stable cones are defined by
time reversal.  A stable or unstable admissible curve is a connected $C^2$ curve
inside one homogeneous component, tangent to the corresponding cone, with
uniform length and curvature bounds in the rescaled strip coordinates.
These are the standard quadratic billiard strips used in the growth lemma and
the Demers--Zhang anisotropic norms.  No later proof is allowed to replace
their \(O(k^{-3})\) width by a freely chosen exponent.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Renormalized grazing blocks]
\label{def:renormalized_grazing_blocks}
Let
\[
  \mathcal G_m^\pm
  =
  \bigcup_{2^m\le k<2^{m+1}}\Hstrip_k^\pm .
\]
For an order-\(j\) physical response current, first sum all homogeneous pieces
inside \(\mathcal G_m^\pm\), cancel the two orientations of every artificial
strip face, and combine the interior, Jacobian, flux, and physical-face terms.
The resulting distribution is denoted
\(\mathcal T_{j,m}^{\rm phys}\).  It is defined from the resolved physical
branch current before any absolute stripwise estimate.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Flux-seed norms]
\label{def:flux_seed_norms}
Fix \(0<\epsilon_*<1/4\).  In a resolved grazing chart write
\(u=(\cos\varphi)^{1/2}\), and write the transported branch flux as
\(u^3a_{h,\theta}(u,s)\rho_{h,\theta}(u,s)\,du\,ds\), where \(a_{h,\theta}\)
is the smooth chart and branch-Jacobian factor.  For a smooth finite-strip
core density set
\begin{align}
 \mathcal N_q^{\rm flux}(\rho)
  :=\ &\sup_{\theta\in U}\|\rho_\theta\|_{\Bcal_\theta}\notag\\
 &+\sum_{\ell+|\beta|\le q}
   \sup_{\theta,m}2^{\epsilon_*m}
   \sum_{h:\,D_h\cap\mathcal G_m\ne\varnothing}
   \int_{D_h\cap\mathcal G_m}
   \left|u^\ell\partial_u^\ell\partial_s^\beta
   (u^3a_{h,\theta}\rho_{h,\theta})\right|\,du\,ds .
\end{align}
Add the analogous total-variation terms on the labelled one-face and clean
two-face restrictions of the resolved branch charts, with the physical coarea
Jacobian included.  These are coefficient norms for the undifferentiated
physical flux; they do not contain
\(\mathcal T_{j,m}^{\rm phys}\).  For a resolved trace test define
\[
  \|\psi\|_{\mathcal C_{\rm tr}^{q}}
  =\max_{\ell+|\beta|\le q}
   \sup_h\|u^\ell\partial_u^\ell\partial_s^\beta\psi_h\|_\infty,
\]
including the corresponding one-sided face and corner values.  Common
refinement merely subdivides the integrals and total variations, so these
norms are refinement invariant on the core.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Renormalized grazing-block summation]
\label{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum}
For each \(0\le j\le J\), there is \(C_j<\infty\) such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:renormalized_grazing_block_bound}
  |\langle\mathcal T_{j,m}^{\rm phys}\rho,\psi\rangle|
  \le
  C_j2^{-m}
  \mathcal N_{j+2}^{\rm flux}(\rho)
  \|\psi\|_{\mathcal C_{\rm tr}^{j+3}} .
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\sum_m\mathcal T_{j,m}^{\rm phys}\) converges in the adjoint
trace topology and extends to the corresponding flux-seed completions.  The
lemma does not assert the generally false absolute
moment \(\sum_k k^{M_j}m_W(E_{j,k})<\infty\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the physical grazing coordinate
\(u=(\cos\varphi)^{1/2}\).  The flux is
\(d\nu=2u^3a(u,s)\,du\,ds\), with \(a\) smooth and bounded above and below.
Differentiate the complete flux-preserving branch identity before separating
interior and face terms.  Artificial faces cancel between adjacent
homogeneity cells.  The cubic flux zero alone is insufficient at high order:
for \(n\ge3\),
\[
 u^3\delta^{(n)}(u)
 =-n(n-1)(n-2)\delta^{(n-3)}(u).
\]
Thus no endpoint is discarded merely because a factor \(u^3\) is present.

Apply \cref{thm:local_clean_atlas_flux_factorization} to the already assembled
physical branch identity.  It gives the proved exact factorization
\(F_{j,m}=u\widetilde F_{j,m}\), after all residual boundary
jets in the preceding distribution identity have cancelled.  On the dyadic
block \(u\asymp2^{-m}\), the rescaled coefficient bound in that theorem,
the flux-seed norm, and the trace-test norm therefore give
\eqref{eq:renormalized_grazing_block_bound}.  Matched unstable-holonomy
transport changes the bound by a fixed Jacobian; unmatched descendants remain
in the same dyadic block and are controlled by the weak terminal component.
The geometric series in \(2^{-m}\) proves convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma*}[Impact-function trace differentiation]
Fix a finite collision word and a homogeneous strip itinerary.  The system of
impact equations defining the successive flight times has, on each regular
homogeneous component, flight-time Jacobian
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{m=1}^{j}\langle v_m^-,n(q_m,\theta)\rangle .
\end{equation}
After the grazing change of variable $u=(\cos\varphi)^{1/2}$, all branch maps,
finite-word singular curves, and their parameter velocities satisfy polynomial
strip bounds.  Consequently every moving singular curve $\Gamma$ generated by a
finite word defines a trace functional on the smooth branchwise core, in the
stable-leaf pairing sense formalized in \cref{eq:core_trace_pairing},
\begin{equation}
  \rho\longmapsto (V_\Gamma\cdot n_\Gamma)\rho\,\delta_\Gamma
\end{equation}
and, for each finite word length $n$ and finite strip cutoff \(K\),
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\substack{\Gamma\in\Ssing_n\\k(\Gamma)\le K}}
  \|(V_\Gamma\cdot n_\Gamma)\rho\,\delta_\Gamma\|_{\Bweak_\theta}
  \le C_{n,K}\|\rho\|_{C^1_{\rm br}}.
\end{equation}
This finite-cutoff statement does not take point values of a general element of
\(\Bcal_\theta\).  The first-order completed trace is defined only on the graph
domain below, and its grazing contribution is completed by
\cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum}.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}
On a regular branch, the next impact with a scatterer is determined by an
equation $G(q+tv,\theta)=0$.  Differentiating in $t$ gives the normal velocity
factor $\langle v,n\rangle$.  Near grazing this factor is singular in
$\varphi$, but in the blown-up coordinate $u=(\cos\varphi)^{1/2}$ every
derivative loses only a polynomial power of the strip index.  Branch-change
curves are handled by the same computation applied to differences of impact
functions.  Transversality to admissible leaves is a consequence of the
dispersing curvature recurrence: failure of transversality is assigned to a
refined multiple-impact, branch-change, separation, infinite-flight, or unblown
grazing stratum.  Intersections with stable leaves are therefore polynomially
bounded in high strips.  The unweighted growth lemma does not sum the resulting
polynomial trace loss absolutely; the physical terms are instead assembled by
\cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Growth lemma]
\label{thm:growth_lemma}
Let $r_n(x)$ be the distance, measured along $\Pcal_\theta^n W$, from
$\Pcal_\theta^n x$ to the nearest endpoint of its homogeneous component.  There
exist $C>0$ and $\rho_g\in(0,1)$, uniform in the finite-response parameter,
such that for every admissible unstable curve $W$ and every $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}
  m_W\{x\in W:r_n(x)<\varepsilon\}
  \le C\varepsilon+C\rho_g^n m_W(W).
\end{equation}
For response currents, the terminal children are first grouped into the
dyadic physical blocks of \cref{def:renormalized_grazing_blocks}; their
combined pairings obey
\[
  \sum_{m\ge M}
  |\langle\mathcal T_{j,m}^{\rm phys}\rho,\psi\rangle|
  \le C_j2^{-M}
  \|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(j+2)}}
  \|\psi\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(j+3)}}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Construct the standard family tree for images of $W$.  At each adapted return
time, descendants above the reference length continue as long children, while
short descendants cut by singularities or high strips are recorded in the
boundary layer.  Cone expansion and the one-step expansion estimate make the
total inverse expansion of long children strictly smaller than one.  Bounded
distortion converts pointwise inverse expansion into componentwise length
control.  At the last generation, preimages of points within $\varepsilon$ of a
fresh endpoint have total length $O(\varepsilon)$.  For response currents,
sum all children in one dyadic grazing block before estimating them.
\Cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum} gives the factor
\(2^{-m}\); the adapted contraction propagates this terminal block
charge down the tree.  Iterating and absorbing the finitely many remainder
iterates gives both estimates.
This is the classical billiard growth-lemma mechanism of
\cite{chernov2006chaotic,ChernovZhang2009}; the present proof keeps all
constants uniform over the compact finite-response chart.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hyperbolic collision map with singularities]
\label{thm:singular_collision_map}
The collision map is defined globally modulo a zero-measure singular set, is
piecewise smooth on homogeneous components, preserves the billiard flux measure,
and satisfies uniform cone invariance, distortion, one-step expansion, and
growth-lemma estimates.  In homogeneous coordinates the map is $C^r$ on every
branch, singularities are countable unions of $C^r$ curves after the square-root
grazing blow-up, and stable/unstable cones are invariant with uniform expansion.
The constants are deterministic functions of \cref{ass:primitive_family}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Piecewise smoothness is obtained from the implicit impact equation on each
homogeneous component.  Cone invariance and expansion follow from the optical
curvature recurrence, distortion from the branch Jacobian formula, and
complexity from the one-step expansion plus \cref{thm:growth_lemma}.  The
zero-measure exceptional set is the countable union of preimages of grazing,
multiple-collision, and branch-change singularities.  Uniformity in $\theta$ is
inherited from the compact finite-response parameter chart.  The local
singular-hyperbolic mechanism follows the Sinai billiard framework
\cite{Sinai1970,KatokStrelcyn1986,chernov2006chaotic}; the uniform
finite-response dependence is the specialization needed here.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Anisotropic collision-map transfer background}

\begin{definition}[Notation for the billiard anisotropic spaces]
\label{def:anisotropic_spaces_resolvent}
For the collision map we fix a billiard-specific strong/weak pair from the
Demers--Zhang Lorentz-gas construction
\cite{DemersZhang2011,DemersZhang2013}, transported between nearby tables in
normal-graph coordinates.  The formulas below record only notation and the
types of seminorms; they are not a replacement definition of those spaces.
Let $\mathcal W_\theta^s$ and $\mathcal W_\theta^u$ be the stable and unstable
admissible curves from \cref{def:strips_cones_standard_curves}, cut so that
each curve lies in one homogeneous component, has length in a fixed interval
\([\ell_-/2,\ell_+]\) after standard subdivision, and has the curvature bounds
specified there.  Two stable curves \(W_1,W_2\in\mathcal W_\theta^s\) are
\emph{matched} if they lie in the same homogeneous chart and are connected by
the local unstable holonomy \(\mathfrak h^u:W_1\to W_2\) on all but terminal children whose
total stable mass is charged to the weak tail.  The distance \(d(W_1,W_2)\) is
the \(C^1\) graph distance between the matched stable curves in that chart plus
the stable mass of unmatched terminal pieces.  For a branchwise smooth density
\(h\), set
\begin{align}
  |h|_{w,\theta}
  &=\sup_{W\in\mathcal W_\theta^s}
    \sup_{\|\psi\|_{C^0(W)}\le1}
    \left|\int_W h\psi\,dm_W\right|,\\
  \|h\|_{s,\theta}
  &=\sup_{W\in\mathcal W_\theta^s}
    \sup_{\|\psi\|_{C^\alpha(W)}\le1}
    \left|\int_W h\psi\,dm_W\right|.
\end{align}
The unstable seminorm is
\begin{equation}
  \|h\|_{u,\theta}
  =
  \sup_{(W_1,W_2,\mathfrak h^u)}
  \sup_{\|\psi_i\|_{C^\alpha(W_i)}\le1}
  \frac{
  \left|
  \int_{W_1}h\psi_1\,dm_{W_1}
  -
  \int_{W_2}h(\psi_2\circ\mathfrak h^u)\,J_{\mathfrak h^u}\,dm_{W_2}
  \right|}
  {d(W_1,W_2)^\beta},
\end{equation}
where the first supremum ranges over matched pairs, \(J_{\mathfrak h^u}\) is the
unstable-holonomy Jacobian between nearby stable curves, and \(0<\beta<\alpha<1\).  Unmatched terminal pieces
are first cut and estimated by \(|\cdot|_{w,\theta}\).  With fixed
$b_0,c_0>0$,
\begin{equation}
  \|h\|_{\Bcal_\theta}
  =\|h\|_{s,\theta}+b_0\|h\|_{u,\theta}+c_0|h|_{w,\theta},
\end{equation}
The short-leaf weights, length scaling, admissible test exponents, and
unmatched-piece terms are those of the cited billiard space.  We denote its
completion by $\Bcal_\theta$.  The weak space \(\Bweak_\theta\) is the cited
weak completion, schematically represented by
\(|\cdot|_{w,\theta}+\|\cdot\|_{s,\theta}^{(\alpha_w)}\) with
\(\alpha_w<\alpha\) and without the unstable comparison seminorm.  The
completion elements are distributions defined by their stable-leaf pairings.
The
observable space $\Bcal_\theta^\#$ is the restricted dual generated by physical
branchwise \(C^r\) observables with the dual norm induced by stable-leaf
pairing; \(\Bcal_\theta^{\#,0}\) denotes the quotient by constants.  The map
transfer and observable operators are defined by
\begin{equation}
  \int f\,\Ltransfer_\theta h\,d\nu_\theta
  =\int f\circ\Pcal_\theta\,h\,d\nu_\theta,
  \qquad
  \mathscr U_\theta f=f\circ\Pcal_\theta .
\end{equation}
The density projector is
\(\Piden_\theta h=\langle h,1\rangle\rho_\theta\), and the dual observable
projector is
\(\Piobs_\theta f=\langle\rho_\theta,f\rangle1\).  Write
\(\Pidenperp_\theta=I-\Piden_\theta\) and
\(\Piobsperp_\theta=I-\Piobs_\theta\).
Uniform boundedness of the transports
\(\mathfrak T^{\rm obs}_{\theta\leftarrow\theta_0}\) and
\(\mathfrak T^{\rm den}_{\theta\leftarrow\theta_0}\) is included in
\cref{prop:response_scale_well_posed}; it is not deduced merely from
the schematic displays above.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition*}[Transported dynamical strong space]
For a compact normal-graph chart \(U\), write
\[
 \mathfrak B_U
 :=\{h=(h_\theta)_{\theta\in U}:h_\theta\in\Bcal_\theta\},
 \qquad
 \|h\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
 :=\sup_{\theta\in U}\|h_\theta\|_{\Bcal_\theta},
\]
with the fibers identified by the density transports, and let
\(\mathfrak B_U^0\) be its centered subbundle.  This is the dynamical strong
space on which \cref{thm:primitive_spectral_resolvent} supplies a uniform
spectral gap.  It is deliberately distinct from the stronger flux-seed
response domain \(\mathfrak D_U\) introduced below.  The latter controls
parameter traces and coefficient jets; no spectral gap or transfer invariance
in its stronger norm is inferred from the Demers--Zhang theorem.
\end{definition*}

\begin{definition*}[Pairing and resolvent notation]
Density-observable duality is written
\[
  \langle g,f\rangle_\theta=\int_{\Sigma_\theta} f\,g\,d\nu_\theta .
\]
Thus the transfer operator acts on densities and the Koopman operator acts on
observables.  For a centered source \(g\) and a target test \(f\), the notation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:early_scalar_observable_resolvent_notation}
  \langle g,R_\theta^{\rm obs}f\rangle_\theta
  :=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
    \langle g,\mathscr U_\theta^n\Piobsperp_\theta f\rangle_\theta
\end{equation}
is used only after scalar Cauchy convergence has been proved for that source in
\cref{thm:strong_primitive_conormal_coupling,thm:trace_insertion_recovery}.
It does not define an element \(R_\theta^{\rm obs}f\) in the positive trace
completion, nor a bounded Koopman resolvent on that completion.
The response formula uses the scalar functional
\(\langle g,R_\theta^{\rm obs}f\rangle_\theta\) from
\eqref{eq:early_scalar_observable_resolvent_notation}.  It never applies a
density-side map resolvent to a weak trace source.
\end{definition*}

\begin{definition*}[Reference convention for anisotropic norms]
The symbols in this paper use the following convention.  The pair
\((\Bcal_\theta,\Bweak_\theta)\) is the billiard strong/weak anisotropic pair:
the stable component is the stable-curve testing norm, the unstable component
compares nearby stable curves by the local unstable holonomy
\(\mathfrak h^u\) inside homogeneous charts, and the weak component is the
corresponding lower-regularity stable-curve test.  The holonomy here is the
local unstable holonomy of the collision map.
This is the billiard-specific strong/weak architecture of
\cite{DemersZhang2011,DemersZhang2013}, which modifies the bounded-second-
derivative setting of \cite{DemersLiverani2008} to handle grazing
singularities.  Compact embedding and quasi-compactness are separated through
\cite{Hennion1993,Baladi2000}.  The reference to
\cite{GouezelLiverani2006} is only for the general anisotropic-Banach-space
mechanism; all singular-cut, homogeneity-strip, and billiard trace estimates
are the local estimates of this paper.
\end{definition*}

\begin{theorem}[Billiard collision-map spectral gap]
\label{thm:primitive_spectral_resolvent}
Under
\cref{ass:primitive_family,ass:response_order_convention,ass:finite_order_clean_response_chart},
the singular collision maps admit the billiard-specific Demers--Zhang
anisotropic transfer spaces with uniform Lasota--Yorke estimates, compact
embedding, and a spectral gap on the zero-mean complement.  After quotienting
constants, there exist \(C>0\) and \(\vartheta\in(0,1)\), depending only on
the primitive and period-one chart constants, such that for every
allowed table parameter $\theta$,
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\Ltransfer_\theta^n h}_{\Bcal_\theta}
  \le C\vartheta^n\norm{h}_{\Bcal_\theta}
  \qquad (\langle 1,h\rangle=0).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:primitive_spectral_resolvent}]
We use the perturbative Lorentz-gas theorem of
\cite[Theorems~2.2, 2.3, 2.5, 2.6, and~2.11; Corollary~2.4]{DemersZhang2013}
with the following theorem-number-level
correspondence:

\section{Label-Resolved Sources, Recovery, and the Radial Model}

\begin{remark*}[Base-level source-module convention]
At the base level, \(\mathbb E_0\) denotes the weighted \(\ell^1\)-sum of
regular, one-sided face, point/normal-jet, and terminal coefficients;
\(\mathbf Q_0\) is their label-resolved realization and
\(\mathfrak X_U^{(0)}=\mathbb E_0/\ker\mathbf Q_0\).  The map
\(\mathsf S_0\) sums the realized nonterminal labels to the physical scalar
current, while \(\iota_{\rm reg}\) is the regular-density embedding.  The
weighted construction, its countable completion, and the higher-level
notation are given in
\cref{def:finite_order_response_scale,def:ambient_conormal_source_scale} in
Appendix~B.  Only these base-level quotient operations, not the conditional
all-branch certificates (S1)--(S3), are used in the radial reset theorem.
\end{remark*}

\begin{remark*}[Physical traces versus artificial cuts]
Only moving physical scatterer boundaries produce response trace terms in the
final formula.  Homogeneity-strip boundaries, partition cutoffs, and artificial
refinement edges are introduced to define the anisotropic norms and to localize
singularities.  Their central contributions cancel between adjacent partition
pieces because the partition of unity sums to one; the noncentral pieces are
recorded only as terminal weak-tail remainders.  In the trace theorems below,
``strip boundary'' and ``finite preimage'' terms therefore mean bookkeeping
terms for the partition calculus, not additional physical response forces.
\end{remark*}

\begin{definition}[Moving Banach bundle, transport maps, and response domain]
\label{def:moving_banach_bundle_response_domain}
The suspension phase-space bundle is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\mathfrak Z}
  =\{(\theta,z,u):\theta\in\Thetacal,\ z\in\Sigma_\theta\setminus
  \bigcup_{n\in\mathbb Z}\Pcal_\theta^{-n}\Ssing_\theta,\ 0\le u<\tau_\theta(z)\}/\sim,
\end{equation}
with $(\theta,z,\tau_\theta(z))\sim(\theta,\Pcal_\theta z,0)$.  Local
trivializations
\begin{equation}
  \mathfrak I_{\theta\leftarrow\theta_0}:
  \widehat\Sigma_{\theta_0}\to\widehat\Sigma_\theta
\end{equation}
move scatterer boundaries in normal coordinates, preserve the component label
and physical velocity, and use the normalized roof height
$u/\tau_{\theta_0}(z)$.  They carry strip boundaries and singular hypersurfaces
to corresponding finite unions, modulo zero flux sets.

The observable and density transition maps are
\begin{align}
  \mathfrak T^{\rm obs}_{\theta\leftarrow\theta_0}f
  &=f\circ\mathfrak I_{\theta_0\leftarrow\theta},\\
  \mathfrak T^{\rm den}_{\theta\leftarrow\theta_0}\rho
  &=(\mathfrak I_{\theta\leftarrow\theta_0})_*
    (J_{\theta,\theta_0}^{-1}\rho),
\end{align}
with the Jacobian chosen so that pairings are preserved.  A section is $C^k$ if
it becomes a $C^k$ map into one fixed Banach fiber after applying these
transport maps.

For a compact parameter ball $U$ about $\theta_0$, let
\(\mathcal C_{\rm hom}^r(U)\) be the smooth core of densities whose transported
representatives are branchwise \(C^r\) on every homogeneous component and have
finite strip support before completion.  On this core define
\(\mathcal T_{1,m,\theta}^{\rm phys,core}\) by differentiating
the homogeneous branch indicators, roof weights, inverse branches, and
Jacobian factors once, summing all strips in the dyadic block
\(\mathcal G_m\), cancelling artificial faces, and retaining the complete
physical current.  The finite-word refinement is fixed before completion; the
appendix proves that changing to a common refinement gives equivalent
seminorms.  A first moving singular curve $\Gamma$ with normal velocity
$V_\Gamma$ defines the core trace by stable-leaf testing:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:core_trace_pairing}
  \big\langle \mathcal B_\Gamma^{\rm core}\rho,\psi\big\rangle_W
  =
  \sum_{y\in W\cap\Gamma}
  (V_\Gamma\cdot n_\Gamma)(y)\,\rho|_W(y)\,[\psi J_h]_\Gamma(y).
\end{equation}
The notation \(\rho|_W(y)\) is used only for smooth core densities.  No
point-value estimate is asserted from the underlying anisotropic norm alone.
The response domain \(\mathfrak D_U\) is the completion of
\(\mathcal C_{\rm hom}^r(U)\) for the independent seed norm
\begin{equation}
  \|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U}
  =\mathcal N_3^{\rm flux}(\rho).
\end{equation}
It controls coefficients of the undifferentiated physical branch flux and its
resolved face/corner restrictions, not the target current itself.
\Cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum} therefore proves, rather than
defines, continuity of
\(\mathcal T_{1,m,\theta}^{\rm phys,core}\).  The weak
codomain $\mathfrak D_U^w$ is the corresponding weak completion.  After
\cref{thm:first_order_physical_trace} is proved, the combined physical current
has a unique continuous extension.  No isolated point trace or absolutely
summed strip trace of a general anisotropic distribution is assumed.

We also write \(\mathfrak D_U^{(1)}=\mathfrak D_U\).  This is the first level
of the finite scale in \cref{def:finite_order_response_scale}.  The observable
target class is its matching adjoint trace level.
All unconditional dynamical resolvents in the main text act instead on
\(\mathfrak B_U^0\).  Before the moving-face theorem we state the precise
interface that a separately constructed transfer-invariant trace domain would
have to satisfy; no such domain is constructed from \(\mathfrak B_U\) in this
paper.  A spectral gap in the positive flux-seed norm remains part of the
conditional Appendix~B certificate.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma*}[Flux-seed response completion is well posed]
The response domain in \cref{def:moving_banach_bundle_response_domain} is a
first-order flux-seed completion.  More precisely, the smooth-core seminorms
are finite, two finite homogeneous
refinements give equivalent seminorms after passing to a common refinement, and
the smooth core is dense by construction.  If \(\rho_n\) is Cauchy in
\(\mathfrak D_U\), then for every stable test \((W,\psi)\) the complete block
pairings
\(\langle \mathcal T_{1,m,\theta}^{\rm phys,core}\rho_n,\psi\rangle_W\)
have a limit independent of the approximating sequence.  The operators
\(\mathcal T_{1,m,\theta}^{\rm phys}\) are therefore defined on the completion
by this limiting pairing, and their boundedness
\(\mathfrak D_U\to\mathfrak D_U^w\) is the content of
\cref{thm:first_order_physical_trace}.  This construction does not imply
that the same point trace is bounded on the underlying \(\Bcal\), nor does it
by itself prove transfer invariance of the graph domain.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}
On the smooth core the weighted flux coefficients and resolved restrictions in
\cref{def:flux_seed_norms} have finite homogeneous support, so their integrals
and variations are finite.  A common refinement only subdivides those
quantities and preserves physical face labels; artificial internal faces have
opposite orientations.  Hence the seed norms are equivalent under finite
refinement.  If \(\rho_n\) is seed-Cauchy, the estimate in
\cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum} makes every complete physical block
pairing Cauchy uniformly in (m).  Its limit defines the current on the
completion independently of the approximating core sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[First-order physical response-current theorem]
\label{thm:first_order_physical_trace}
Let $D_h(\theta)$ be a homogeneous branch domain and let
$\Gamma_s\subset\partial D_h(\theta_s)$ be a moving singular curve with normal
velocity $V_\Gamma$ in the blown-up strip chart.  For a stable test leaf $W$, a
test function $\psi$, and a smooth core density $\rho$, cut \(W\) at
\(\Gamma_0\), at artificial strip boundaries, and at terminal near-grazing
cells.  On the remaining central transversal pieces, the derivative of the
branch characteristic function is the stable-leaf trace pairing
\begin{equation}
  \big\langle \mathcal B_\Gamma\rho,\psi\big\rangle_W
  =
  \sum_{y\in W\cap\Gamma_0}
  (V_\Gamma\cdot n_\Gamma)(y)\,\rho|_W(y)\,[\psi J_h]_\Gamma(y),
\end{equation}
where $[\psi J_h]_\Gamma$ is the jump of the pulled-back test-Jacobian factor
across adjacent homogeneous branches.  This formula is a local smooth-core
identity; no point trace of a general element of \(\Bcal\) is asserted.
After summing adjacent branches, cancelling artificial strip faces, and
combining the interior, Jacobian, flux, and physical-face terms in each
grazing block, the complete first response current extends by flux-seed
continuity and satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{m\ge0}
  |\langle\mathcal T_{1,m}^{\rm phys}\rho,\psi\rangle|
  \le
  C\|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(3)}}
  \|\psi\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(4)}} .
\end{equation}
An isolated stripwise \(\mathcal B_\Gamma\) need not have an absolutely
summable weak norm.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write the branch contribution in fixed coordinates as
$1_{D_h(\theta_s)}J_{h_s}^{-1}\rho\circ h_s$.  The ordinary derivative of the
smooth factor gives branch-interior and Jacobian terms.  The singular operation
is the derivative of the moving-domain indicator.  If \(\Gamma_s\) is written as
$g_s=0$ and if $\partial_s g_s|_0=-V_\Gamma\cdot n_\Gamma$, then
\begin{equation}
  \partial_s 1_{\{g_s>0\}}\big|_{s=0}
  =(V_\Gamma\cdot n_\Gamma)\delta_{\Gamma_0}.
\end{equation}
Pairing with a stable test curve gives the stated intersection sum.  Central
transversality gives a uniform intersection bound, while in \(\Hstrip_k\) the
impact equations and blown-up coordinate give polynomial bounds for
\(V_\Gamma\), crossing denominators, and branch Jacobians.  Thus each
stable-leaf
pairing is bounded by
\begin{equation}
  \left|
  \big\langle \mathcal B_\Gamma\rho,\psi\big\rangle_W
  \right|
  \le
  C k^{M_1}\|\rho\|_{C^1_{\rm br}}
  m_W\{y\in W:\hbox{the traced descendant of }y\hbox{ lies in }\Hstrip_k\}.
\end{equation}
This is a local smooth-core estimate and is not summed in absolute value over
\(k\).  After the physical terms and artificial-face cancellations are
assembled, the renormalized block estimate gives
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{m\ge0}
  |\langle\mathcal T_{1,m}^{\rm phys}\rho,\psi\rangle|
  \le C\|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(3)}}
         \|\psi\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(4)}},
\end{equation}
by \cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum}.  The flux-seed norm gives a
uniform Cauchy estimate for smooth-core approximants and hence the extension to
\(\mathfrak D_U\); the unstable comparison follows
by matching trace intersections under the local unstable holonomy between
nearby stable leaves, with unmatched pieces retained in the same dyadic
weak-terminal block.
\end{proof}


\begin{remark*}[Higher traces require resolved currents]
Formal differentiation produces same-wall terms such as
\(\delta'(g)\) and, for two transverse walls in the two-dimensional collision
section, a codimension-two coarea atom.  Three or more independent delta
constraints are not defined by declaring their intersection a finite vertex.
The positive construction in \cref{def:resolved_indicator_current,thm:second_multiple_trace}
therefore differentiates the resolved moving-domain current itself: repeated
walls give normal derivatives, two clean walls give coarea atoms, and resolved
vertices give parameter derivatives of an existing point current.  The
renormalized physical block identity then sums the resulting finite-order
losses without an absolute strip moment.
\end{remark*}

\begin{proposition}[First transfer derivative]
\label{prop:first_transfer_derivative}
Let \(\theta_s\) be an admissible \(C^1\) parameter curve.  On the smooth
finite-strip core, differentiation of the transported transfer operator gives
\[
  \nabla_s\Ltransfer_{\theta_s}|_0\rho
  =\sum_h\Ltransfer_{0,h}
    (\mathcal D_h^{\rm int}+\mathcal D_h^{\rm Jac})\rho
   +\sum_{\Gamma\subset\Ssing}\mathcal B_\Gamma\rho .
\]
The branch-interior and Jacobian series and the physical-boundary trace series
extend by continuity to a bounded map
\(\mathfrak D_U\to\mathfrak D_U^w\).  Artificial partition boundaries cancel
pairwise before the second sum is formed.  For the transported invariant
density, the difference quotient converges to this source in the target-class
dual topology of \cref{def:finite_order_response_scale}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate each homogeneous inverse-branch formula.  Differentiating its
smooth weight gives \(\mathcal D_h^{\rm int}+\mathcal D_h^{\rm Jac}\), with the
impact, reflection, flux, and transport derivatives included.  Differentiating
the branch indicator gives the physical trace of
\cref{thm:first_order_physical_trace}.  The branch estimates use the cited
billiard strong-to-weak bounds.  Adjacent artificial cutoffs form a partition
of unity, so their
indicator derivatives cancel.  Completion of the core gives the asserted
bounded complete physical current by
\cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum}, and the final convergence statement follows from
\cref{prop:response_scale_well_posed}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact invariant-source Ward identity and strong primitive]
\label{thm:invariant_source_ward_identity}
Work in one fixed transported collision fiber and write
\(\rho_s=d(\Phi_s^*\nu_s)/d\nu_0\).  Then
\(\Ltransfer_s\rho_s=\rho_s\).  If the transported family is \(C^J\) on the
smooth response core, then for every \(1\le j\le J\)
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:invariant_source_ward_identity}
 G_j:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\binom j\ell
   (\partial_s^\ell\Ltransfer_s|_0)
   (\partial_s^{j-\ell}\rho_s|_0)
   =(I-\Ltransfer_0)\,\partial_s^j\rho_s|_0
\end{equation}
as an identity in the distributional source/test pairing.  Consequently the
\emph{fully assembled invariant source} has the explicit strong primitive
\(\partial_s^j\rho_s|_0\).  All artificial-face currents and all residual
endpoint currents in that total source cancel exactly.

The theorem does not claim that an individual derivative letter
\((\partial_s^\ell\Ltransfer_s|_0)h\), with arbitrary \(h\), has a strong
primitive.  That stronger operator statement is precisely the additional Ward
or source-recovery condition needed for general dynamical susceptibilities.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The normalized collision flux is invariant for every table.  Pulling it to the
reference fiber gives \(\Ltransfer_s\rho_s=\rho_s\), with \(\rho_s\) the
explicit transport Jacobian.  Differentiate this identity \(j\) times and move
the \(\ell=0\) term to the right; this is
\eqref{eq:invariant_source_ward_identity}.  The equality holds first against
smooth finite-strip tests.  The branch/interior, Jacobian, flux, and physical
face terms are the complete distributional derivative of the left-hand side,
so no endpoint term can be discarded separately.  Adjacent artificial faces
have opposite orientations and cancel before the equality is completed.
Since the right-hand side is the difference of two strong transported
densities, completion gives the asserted source identity.  In particular every
coefficient of a residual distribution supported only at \(u=0\) in the total
invariant source is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dynamical-strong recovery]
\label{prop:strong_recovery_equivalence}
Assume the period-one Demers--Zhang spectral gap on the centered strong
density space.  Let \(g\) be a centered complete assembled source in
\(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\), and write
\(\bar g=\mathsf S_m\mathbf Q_m g\) for its total represented current.  At the
ordinary dynamical strong level the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item there is a centered \(h\in\mathfrak B_U\) such that
  \(\bar g=(I-\Ltransfer_0)h\) in the distributional pairing;
  \item the total represented current \(\bar g\) belongs
  to the centered dynamical strong space \(\mathfrak B_U\).
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions hold, the primitive is canonical and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:canonical_strong_recovery}
 h=(I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}\bar g
   =\sum_{n\ge0}\Ltransfer_0^n \bar g,
 \qquad
 \|h\|_{\mathfrak B_U}\le C_{\rm gap}\|\bar g\|_{\mathfrak B_U}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, in a clean local decomposition with separated one-face and
two-face point strata, a recovered represented current cannot retain a
nonzero isolated singular coordinate.  Those coordinates must cancel in the
complete physical assembly before the ordinary density resolvent is used.  If
the response state also carries a separate terminal symbol, vanishing or
closure of that component is an additional requirement not implied by the
distributional equality.
Intrinsic recovery as an element of the \emph{dynamical} labelled module
further requires
\[
 g=\iota_{\rm reg}\bar g\quad\hbox{in }\mathfrak X_U^{(m)},
\]
where \(\iota_{\rm reg}\) is the canonical regular embedding.  Thus
cross-label residues and \(\pi_{\rm term}g\) must vanish separately.
For a specified complete block, the weaker certified scalar reset of
\cref{prop:certified_scalar_assembly_reset} is an alternative: it proves that
the terminal truncation vanishes in every subsequent fixed scalar pairing and
then re-embeds \(\bar g\), without asserting the displayed equality in
\(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\).
At base level the regular embedding is understood through the continuous
inclusion
\(\mathfrak B_U\hookrightarrow\mathfrak D_U^{w,(0)}\).  The displayed
intrinsic equality is asserted at that level.  Its analogue at a higher
flux-seed response level is only conditional on a separately proved embedding
and centered spectral-gap estimate in that graph norm.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(\bar g=(I-\Ltransfer_0)h\) and
\(h\in\mathfrak B_U\), boundedness of \(\Ltransfer_0\) on the
Demers--Zhang strong space gives
\(\bar g\in\mathfrak B_U\).  Conversely, the period-one spectral gap makes the
Neumann series in \eqref{eq:canonical_strong_recovery} converge in the
centered \(\mathfrak B_U\)-norm, and telescoping proves
\((I-\Ltransfer_0)h=\bar g\).

A clean point atom is not even in the ordinary Demers--Zhang weak completion,
and a nonzero isolated transverse one-face current is, in general, weak but
not strong by \cref{prop:demers_zhang_trace_location}.  Local tests supported
near one separated stratum therefore detect any surviving coefficient.
Hence membership of the total represented assembly in \(\mathfrak B_U\)
forces every such singular coordinate to cancel (or to combine with another
term on the same total stratum into zero).  The total-current map deliberately
forgets cross-label and terminal information, so this argument makes no
assertion about those coordinates.  Equality with the regular embedding is
exactly the additional dynamical-module requirement.  The higher-level
statement is the same Neumann-series argument in the stipulated graph norm.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A genuinely nonconjugate second-order recovered assembly]
\label{thm:radial_inflation_second_order_recovery}
Consider a finite-horizon table containing circular scatterers \(i\) and
\(k\), with radii \(R_i,R_k\), and an isolated unobstructed normal period-two
orbit between them.  Keep all centers and all other scatterers fixed and set
\(R_i(s)=R_i+s\) for \(|s|\) small.  Assume the resulting family remains in
the period-one/no-boundary-pair-birth geometry (R1)--(R2) of
\cref{ass:finite_order_clean_response_chart}; no arbitrary-seed (R3) is used.
Let \(P_0\) be the total
boundary length and put \(a=2\pi/P_0\).  Under the transport which fixes the
boundary label, polar parameter, and collision angle, the pulled-back
normalized flux density is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:radial_inflation_flux_density}
 \rho_s(j,\sigma,\varphi)
 =\frac{P_0}{P_0+2\pi s}
 \begin{cases}
  1+s/R_i,&j=i,\\
  1,&j\ne i.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:radial_inflation_flux_jets}
 \dot\rho_0=
 \begin{cases}R_i^{-1}-a,&j=i,\\-a,&j\ne i,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \ddot\rho_0=
 \begin{cases}2a^2-2a/R_i,&j=i,\\2a^2,&j\ne i.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
These functions are centered strong densities.  If
\(B_1=\partial_s\Ltransfer_s|_0\) and
\(B_2=\partial_s^2\Ltransfer_s|_0\), then the complete first- and second-order
invariant assemblies satisfy
\begin{align}
 G_1&=B_1\rho_0=(I-\Ltransfer_0)\dot\rho_0,
 \label{eq:radial_inflation_first_recovery}\\
 G_2&=B_2\rho_0+2B_1\dot\rho_0
      =(I-\Ltransfer_0)\ddot\rho_0.
 \label{eq:radial_inflation_second_recovery}
\end{align}
Thus the total represented one-face and point current in each complete
assembly cancels to a strong density.  The family verifies the represented
base-level recovery criterion and the zero-waiting-time triangular step through order
two, with explicit primitives \eqref{eq:radial_inflation_flux_jets}.

This family is not a \(C^1\) conjugate deformation for a generic such
period-two orbit.  The next theorem upgrades the \emph{complete fixed-length
block} to arbitrary positive waiting lengths.  Neither result asserts
eventwise or all-seed (S3), label-resolved terminal closure, or the all-seed
operator bound (S1).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The normalized collision flux is
\(Z_s^{-1}\cos\varphi\,ds_s\,d\varphi\), with
\(Z_s=2P_s\) and \(P_s=P_0+2\pi s\).  On component \(i\), the arclength
Jacobian is \((R_i+s)/R_i\); it is one on every other component.  This proves
\eqref{eq:radial_inflation_flux_density}.  Two differentiations give
\eqref{eq:radial_inflation_flux_jets}; centeredness follows by differentiating
\(\int\rho_s\,d\nu_0=1\).  Since the collision components are disjoint, these
piecewise constant functions lie in every finite strong seed level used here.

Differentiate \(\Ltransfer_s\rho_s=\rho_s\) once and twice.  This gives
\eqref{eq:radial_inflation_first_recovery} and
\eqref{eq:radial_inflation_second_recovery}.  Their right-hand sides are
strong, so \cref{prop:strong_recovery_equivalence} forces the singular sector
of each complete left-hand assembly to vanish.  Notice that the individual
summands \(B_2\rho_0\) and \(B_1\dot\rho_0\) are not separately declared
strong.

For nonconjugacy, use paraxial Jacobi coordinates along the normal two-cycle.
With
\[
 \mathsf F_\tau=\begin{pmatrix}1&\tau\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \mathsf R_\kappa=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2\kappa&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
the return derivative is conjugate to
\(\mathsf R_{\kappa_i}\mathsf F_\tau
  \mathsf R_{\kappa_k}\mathsf F_\tau\), whose trace is
\[
 2+4\tau(\kappa_i+\kappa_k)+4\tau^2\kappa_i\kappa_k.
\]
Here \(\tau'(0)=-1\), \(\kappa_i'(0)=-\kappa_i^2\), and
\(\kappa_k'(0)=0\), so the derivative of this trace is
\[
 -4(\kappa_i+\kappa_k)-4\tau\kappa_i^2
 -8\tau\kappa_i\kappa_k-4\tau^2\kappa_i^2\kappa_k<0.
\]
The periodic multiplier therefore changes.  A \(C^1\) conjugacy would preserve
the multiplier spectrum of the isolated periodic orbit, which proves the
claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Positive-waiting block recovery through second order]
\label{thm:blockwise_second_order_invariant_recovery}
Let \(s\mapsto(\Ltransfer_s,\rho_s)\) be any \(C^2\) transported invariant
family for which the complete first- and second-order finite-time current
identities are defined, and write
\[
 B_1=\partial_s\Ltransfer_s|_0,\qquad
 B_2=\partial_s^2\Ltransfer_s|_0,\qquad
 \rho_j=\partial_s^j\rho_s|_0.
\]
For \(N\ge1\), set
\begin{align}
 \mathscr D_N
 &:=\partial_s(\Ltransfer_s^N)|_0
   =\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}
     \Ltransfer_0^{N-1-a}B_1\Ltransfer_0^a,
 \label{eq:first_block_derivative_expansion}\\
 \mathscr E_N
 &:=\partial_s^2(\Ltransfer_s^N)|_0 \notag\\
 &=\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}
     \Ltransfer_0^{N-1-a}B_2\Ltransfer_0^a
   +2\sum_{0\le a<b\le N-1}
     \Ltransfer_0^{N-1-b}B_1
     \Ltransfer_0^{b-a-1}B_1\Ltransfer_0^a.
 \label{eq:second_block_derivative_expansion}
\end{align}
Then the complete blocks containing all insertion positions and all positive
waiting gaps satisfy
\begin{align}
 \mathscr G_{1,N}
 &:=\mathscr D_N\rho_0
   =(I-\Ltransfer_0^N)\rho_1
   =(I-\Ltransfer_0)H_{1,N},
 \label{eq:first_positive_waiting_block_recovery}\\
 \mathscr G_{2,N}
 &:=\mathscr E_N\rho_0+2\mathscr D_N\rho_1
   =(I-\Ltransfer_0^N)\rho_2
   =(I-\Ltransfer_0)H_{2,N},
 \label{eq:second_positive_waiting_block_recovery}\\
 H_{j,N}&:=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\Ltransfer_0^r\rho_j.
 \label{eq:block_recovery_primitive}
\end{align}
The flux jets \(\rho_j\) are centered.  Hence the period-one spectral gap gives
the uniform bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uniform_block_recovery_primitive}
 \sup_{N\ge1}\|H_{j,N}\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
 \le C_{\rm gap}\|\rho_j\|_{\mathfrak B_U},
 \qquad j=1,2.
\end{equation}
Thus arbitrary positive waiting lengths are recovered after summing the
\emph{complete fixed-\(N\) block}; isolated insertion words are not asserted
to be recovered.  For the radial inflation of
\cref{thm:radial_inflation_second_order_recovery}, all primitives are explicit
through \eqref{eq:radial_inflation_flux_jets}.  The theorem proves total-current
recovery.  Label-resolved terminal closure requires, in addition, convergence
to the regular embedding in \(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The product rule for the \(N\)-fold product gives
\eqref{eq:first_block_derivative_expansion} and
\eqref{eq:second_block_derivative_expansion}, including the factor two for
two distinct insertion positions.  Differentiate
\(\Ltransfer_s^N\rho_s=\rho_s\) once and twice.  Moving the undifferentiated
\(\Ltransfer_0^N\rho_j\) term to the right gives the first equalities in
\eqref{eq:first_positive_waiting_block_recovery} and
\eqref{eq:second_positive_waiting_block_recovery}.  The factorization
\[
 I-\Ltransfer_0^N=(I-\Ltransfer_0)
   \sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\Ltransfer_0^r
\]
gives the displayed primitives.  Differentiation of
\(\int\rho_s\,d\nu_0=1\) centers \(\rho_1,\rho_2\), so the Demers--Zhang
spectral gap on \(\mathfrak B_U\) makes their geometric sums uniformly
bounded.  No invariance or gap on the stronger flux-seed completion
\(\mathfrak D_U\) is used.  Every equality is first a
finite complete source/test identity, hence it includes all branch, Jacobian,
flux, one-face, and clean point terms before the strong conclusion is taken.
The final sentence follows from the distinction between the total-current map
\(\mathsf S_m\) and the label-resolved realization \(\mathbf Q_m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[R3-certified fixed-length scalar grazing-terminal truncation]
\label{lem:fixed_length_scalar_terminal_truncation}
In the setting of
\cref{thm:blockwise_second_order_invariant_recovery}, fix \(N\ge1\) and
\(j\in\{1,2\}\), and assume that the complete block satisfies the
operator Ward/factorization hypothesis (R3) used in
\cref{thm:local_clean_atlas_flux_factorization}.  After forming the complete order-\(j\), length-\(N\)
physical block, cancelling artificial faces, and only then cutting the
physical grazing coordinate into dyadic blocks, let

\[
 \mathscr G_{j,N}^{[\ge M]}
\]

denote the sum of the blocks with index \(m\ge M\).  On the smooth
flux-seed/trace-test core, and hence in the scalar adjoint-trace completion,
whenever the displayed response-level density norms are finite, there is a
finite constant \(C_{N,j}\) such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fixed_length_scalar_terminal_tail}
 \bigl|\langle \mathscr G_{j,N}^{[\ge M]},f\rangle\bigr|
 \le C_{N,j}2^{-M}
 \max_{0\le \ell\le j}
 \|\rho_\ell\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(j+2)}}
 \|f\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(j+3)}}.
\end{equation}
Thus the grazing-terminal truncation of every such R3-certified fixed-length block
vanishes in the scalar current topology.  The assertion is deliberately not
a label-resolved terminal-closure theorem: it neither proves
\(\|\pi_{\rm term}\mathscr G_{j,N}\|_{\mathfrak T_U^{(m)}}=0\) nor supplies a
constant uniform in \(N\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(N\) the product-rule formulae
\eqref{eq:first_block_derivative_expansion}--
\eqref{eq:second_block_derivative_expansion} contain finitely many insertion
positions.  On each intervening finite-time regular branch, the ordinary
transfer and observable pullback have finite response-level bounds.  Form the
complete differentiated physical identity before estimating any one-sided
term.  The R3-dependent renormalized block estimate
\eqref{eq:renormalized_grazing_block_bound} then gives \(C2^{-m}\) for each
resulting physical block.  The finite number of insertion positions and
finite-time operator constants are absorbed into \(C_{N,j}\).  Summing the
geometric series for \(m\ge M\) proves
\eqref{eq:fixed_length_scalar_terminal_tail}.  Since this argument pairs the
already assembled total current with \(f\), it forgets branch labels and does
not estimate the independent terminal coordinate of the ambient module,
which proves exactly the stated limitation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Physical cutoffs descend to the label-resolved quotient]
\label{lem:physical_cutoff_descends_to_source_quotient}
Use the coefficient, realization, and terminal conventions of
\cref{def:ambient_conormal_source_scale}.  Let \(\chi\) be a bounded smooth
function of the physical grazing coordinate, pulled back identically to every
incident regular, face, point, and terminal label.  Assume its nonconstant
part meets only finitely many neighbouring dyadic blocks and that its
rescaled derivatives through the largest retained jet order are bounded.  The
cutoffs \(1-\zeta_M\) used below have these properties for every fixed \(M\),
with constants allowed to depend on \(M\).  On a regular coefficient multiply
by \(\chi\); on a face coefficient multiply by its one-sided trace; on a
point or normal-jet coefficient use the distributional Leibniz matrix for
multiplication by \(\chi\); and on a terminal coefficient use the pullback in
the preterminal physical chart retained by that label.  These rules define,
for each fixed \(\chi\), a bounded triangular multiplier
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:physical_cutoff_coefficient_multiplier}
 \mathsf M_\chi:\mathbb E_0\longrightarrow\mathbb E_0.
\end{equation}
If \(\widetilde{\mathsf M}_\chi\) denotes the corresponding multiplication on
the realized label space \(\mathbb Y_0\), including its terminal coordinate,
then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:physical_cutoff_realization_intertwining}
 \mathbf Q_0\mathsf M_\chi
 =\widetilde{\mathsf M}_\chi\mathbf Q_0,
 \qquad
 \mathsf M_\chi(\ker\mathbf Q_0)\subset\ker\mathbf Q_0.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\mathsf M_\chi\) descends to a bounded operator on
\(\mathfrak X_U^{(0)}\), and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:physical_cutoff_total_current_intertwining}
 \mathsf S_0\mathbf Q_0\mathsf M_\chi x
 =\chi\,\mathsf S_0\mathbf Q_0x
 \qquad(x\in\mathfrak X_U^{(0)}).
\end{equation}
The identity holds for the countable completed coefficient expansion, not only
for finite-support representatives.  The operator norm may depend on the
fixed cutoff \(\chi\); no bound uniform in a shrinking cutoff scale is claimed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On a finite coefficient tuple, multiplication of the realized distribution
by \(\chi\) is exactly the stated regular/trace/jet Leibniz operation.  The
same physical pullback is used on both sides of every artificial cut, so no
new cross-label jump is introduced.  On a jet of order \(q\), multiplication
is a finite lower-triangular matrix with entries among the derivatives of
\(\chi\) through order \(q\).  For fixed \(\chi\) these entries are bounded.
The cutoff meets only finitely many neighbouring dyadic blocks at each
transition, whose source weights are comparable by a fixed finite factor.
The weighted column sum defining \(\mathbb E_0\) therefore gives
\(
 \|\mathsf M_\chi e\|_{\mathbb E_0}
 \le C_\chi\|e\|_{\mathbb E_0}.
\)

The distributional product rule on each incident label proves the first
identity in
\eqref{eq:physical_cutoff_realization_intertwining}.  Kernel invariance follows
immediately, so the multiplier descends to the quotient.  The total-current
map sums the nonterminal realized labels and forgets only the terminal
coordinate; multiplication by the common physical \(\chi\) commutes with
that sum, proving
\eqref{eq:physical_cutoff_total_current_intertwining}.  Finally, finite-support
tuples are dense in the weighted \(\ell^1\) coefficient space.  Boundedness of
\(\mathsf M_\chi\), \(\mathbf Q_0\), and \(\mathsf S_0\) passes all three
identities to the countable completion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[R3-free radial truncation-to-assembly reset]
\label{thm:uniform_postassembly_radial_tail}
In the radial-inflation family of
\cref{thm:radial_inflation_second_order_recovery}, let
\(j\in\{1,2\}\), \(N\ge1\), and let
\(e_{j,N}^{\rm hist}\in\mathfrak X_U^{(0)}\) be the countable completed
label-resolved class of the complete length-\(N\) block before reset.  Denote
its assembled total
current by \(\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N}\).  Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:radial_postassembly_current}
 \mathsf S_0\mathbf Q_0e_{j,N}^{\rm hist}
 =\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N}
 =(I-\Ltransfer_0^N)\rho_j .
\end{equation}
Let \(\zeta_M\) be a uniformly bounded smooth cutoff supported in the physical
grazing collar \(u\le C2^{-M}\), equal to one on all dyadic blocks with index
at least \(M+O(1)\).  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uniform_postassembly_radial_tail}
 \sup_{N\ge1}
 \bigl|\langle\zeta_M\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N},f\rangle\bigr|
 \le C_j2^{-4M}\|f\|_{C^0},
\end{equation}
where \(C_j\) is independent of \(M\) and \(N\).  Moreover the canonical
block-boundary state
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uniform_radial_block_boundary_state}
 x_{j,N}^{\rm asm}
 :=\iota_{\rm reg}\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N}
 \in\mathfrak X_U^{(0)}
\end{equation}
is uniformly bounded in \(N\) and has zero terminal coordinate.
More precisely,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uniform_radial_postassembly_B_bound}
 \sup_{N\ge1}\|\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N}\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
 \le (1+C_{\rm gap})\|\rho_j\|_{\mathfrak B_U}.
\end{equation}

This theorem upgrades the \emph{post-assembly scalar} tail from fixed \(N\)
to uniform \(N\).  It does not say that the pre-assembly labelled history has
zero \(\pi_{\rm term}\)-coordinate: the passage from that history to
\eqref{eq:uniform_radial_block_boundary_state} is precisely the certified
assembly reset, not an identity in the historical module.

Put \(\chi_M=1-\zeta_M\).  Multiplication by \(\chi_M\) is made with the
same physical grazing coordinate in every incident label; write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:radial_historical_physical_truncation}
 e_{j,N}^{(<M),\rm hist}:=\mathsf M_{\chi_M}e_{j,N}^{\rm hist}.
\end{equation}
Then physical realization and assembly commute exactly with this truncation:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:radial_truncation_assembly_commutation}
 \mathsf S_0\mathbf Q_0e_{j,N}^{(<M),\rm hist}
 =\chi_M\mathsf S_0\mathbf Q_0e_{j,N}^{\rm hist}
 =\chi_M\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the canonical post-assembly truncation is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:radial_canonical_postassembly_truncation}
 g_{j,N}^{(<M),\rm asm}
 :=\iota_{\rm reg}(\chi_M\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N}).
\end{equation}
Then, uniformly in \(N\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:radial_direct_scalar_reset_tail}
 \left|\left\langle
 \mathsf S_0\mathbf Q_0e_{j,N}^{(<M),\rm hist}
 -\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N},f\right\rangle\right|
 \le C_j2^{-4M}\|f\|_{C^0}.
\end{equation}
This is the scalar reset tail of
\cref{prop:certified_scalar_assembly_reset}, obtained without (R3).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each fixed \(M\),
\cref{lem:physical_cutoff_descends_to_source_quotient} defines
\(\mathsf M_{\chi_M}\) on the countable completed quotient, independently of
the coefficient representative, and gives
\eqref{eq:radial_truncation_assembly_commutation}.  Its operator norm is not
required to be uniform in \(M\); the uniform scalar error comes instead from
the assembled radial density estimate below.

For the radial family the two flux jets in
\eqref{eq:radial_inflation_flux_jets} are bounded functions, constant on each
collision component.  Since the billiard map is invertible and preserves the
normalized collision flux,
\[
 \Ltransfer_0^Nh= h\circ\Pcal_0^{-N}\quad\text{a.e.},
 \qquad
 \|\Ltransfer_0^Nh\|_{L^\infty(\nu_0)}=\|h\|_{L^\infty(\nu_0)}.
\]
Hence
\[
 \sup_N\|\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N}\|_\infty
 \le2\|\rho_j\|_\infty.
\]
Independently, \(\rho_j\in\mathfrak B_U^0\) and the Demers--Zhang spectral
gap give
\[
 \|\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N}\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
 \le \|\rho_j\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
     +\|\Ltransfer_0^N\rho_j\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
 \le (1+C_{\rm gap})\|\rho_j\|_{\mathfrak B_U},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:uniform_radial_postassembly_B_bound}.  The continuous
embedding \(\mathfrak B_U\hookrightarrow\mathfrak D_U^{w,(0)}\) makes this a
regular base-level source; no \(\mathfrak D_U\)-invariance is asserted.
In the physical grazing coordinate the invariant flux is
\(d\nu_0=2u^3a(u,s)\,du\,ds\), with \(a\) uniformly bounded.  Therefore
\[
 \nu_0\{u\le C2^{-M}\}\le C'2^{-4M},
\]
and Hölder's elementary \(L^\infty\)-\(L^1\) estimate gives
\eqref{eq:uniform_postassembly_radial_tail}.  The regular embedding of the
just-proved \(\mathfrak B_U\) element into the base weak coordinate is bounded
by construction.  This proves the uniform
bound for \eqref{eq:uniform_radial_block_boundary_state}, whose terminal
coordinate is zero by definition.  Moreover,
\eqref{eq:radial_truncation_assembly_commutation} gives
\(\mathsf S_0\mathbf Q_0e_{j,N}^{(<M),\rm hist}
-\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N}=-\zeta_M\overline{\mathscr G}_{j,N}\),
so \eqref{eq:uniform_postassembly_radial_tail} proves
\eqref{eq:radial_direct_scalar_reset_tail}.  This argument uses only the
explicit bounded radial flux jets, flux preservation, and complete assembly;
it invokes neither (R3) nor
\cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum}.  The final qualification follows because
\(\mathsf S_m\) forgets labels and terminal data, whereas
\(\mathbf Q_m\) does not.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Certified scalar assembly reset]
\label{prop:certified_scalar_assembly_reset}
Let \(g^{(<M)}\) be the truncations of a complete fixed-length physical
source at a block boundary, and suppose that there is a centered
\(\bar g\in\mathfrak B_U\) at the base level \(m=0\), or a centered
\(\bar g\in\mathfrak D_U^{(m+1)}\) at a higher level where transfer
invariance has been separately certified, and numbers \(\eta_M\downarrow0\)
such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:certified_scalar_reset_tail}
 \bigl|\langle \mathsf S_m\mathbf Q_m g^{(<M)}-\bar g,f\rangle\bigr|
 \le \eta_M\|f\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(m+2)}}
\end{equation}
for every trace test \(f\).  Then the block has the canonical
\emph{assembly reset}
\[
 \mathfrak R_m^{\rm asm}g:=\iota_{\rm reg}\bar g.
\]
At base level this uses
\(\mathfrak B_U\hookrightarrow\mathfrak D_U^{w,(0)}\); it does not assert
\(\bar g\in\mathfrak D_U\).
For every fixed \(q\ge0\) for which
\(\mathscr U_0^q:\mathfrak A_U^{(m+2)}\to
\mathfrak A_U^{(m+2)}\) is bounded,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:certified_scalar_reset_future_pairing}
 \lim_{M\to\infty}
 \langle \mathsf S_m\mathbf Q_m g^{(<M)},\mathscr U_0^qf\rangle
 =
 \langle\bar g,\mathscr U_0^qf\rangle .
\end{equation}
Thus the labels required inside a singular event may be discarded after its
complete scalar assembly has been certified.  A finite succession of such
certified blocks can be reset inductively.

If in addition \(\bar g=(I-\Ltransfer_0)h\) with centered
\(h\in\mathfrak B_U\) (or in the separately certified higher graph level), the
post-reset scalar resolvent telescopes from \(h\).  This is a blockwise scalar
closure operation, not an identity
\(g=\iota_{\rm reg}\bar g\) in the label-resolved module and not a bounded
projection on all of \(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\).
For the radial-inflation blocks,
\cref{thm:blockwise_second_order_invariant_recovery,thm:uniform_postassembly_radial_tail}
verify the reset through order two for every fixed waiting length \(N\), with
strong primitives and post-assembly scalar grazing tails uniformly bounded in
\(N\), without using (R3).  The historical physical truncations are
\eqref{eq:radial_historical_physical_truncation}; their exact assembled images
are the canonical post-assembly cutoffs by
\eqref{eq:radial_truncation_assembly_commutation}.  No convergence of the raw
historical labels in their label-resolved norm is asserted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:certified_scalar_reset_tail} with
\(\mathscr U_0^qf\).  The fixed-\(q\) observable bound sends the right-hand
side to zero and proves
\eqref{eq:certified_scalar_reset_future_pairing}.  After the limit, the input
to the next block is the ordinary Demers--Zhang strong density \(\bar g\), so the same
argument applies inductively to any fixed finite chain of certified blocks.
When \(\bar g=(I-\Ltransfer_0)h\), duality gives
\[
 \langle\bar g,\mathscr U_0^nf\rangle
 =
 \langle\Ltransfer_0^nh,f\rangle
 -\langle\Ltransfer_0^{n+1}h,f\rangle,
\]
and the strong spectral gap controls the tail.  The radial conclusion uses
\eqref{eq:first_positive_waiting_block_recovery}--
\eqref{eq:uniform_block_recovery_primitive} for \(\bar g\), while
\eqref{eq:radial_direct_scalar_reset_tail} directly supplies
\eqref{eq:certified_scalar_reset_tail}.  None of these scalar identities
identifies the pre-reset labelled history with the regular embedding.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Historical terminal accumulation dichotomy]
\label{prop:historical_terminal_accumulation_dichotomy}
Let a complete first-order length-
\(N\) block be expanded before assembly reset, and retain the insertion time
as part of the terminal label.  Thus its historical terminal space contains
the isometric \(\ell^1\)-sum
\[
 \mathfrak T_{N}^{\rm hist}
 =\bigoplus_{a=0}^{N-1}{}^{\ell^1}\mathfrak T_a,
\]
where \(a\) is the position of the differentiated event.  Suppose the
one-event derivative of the stationary input has terminal coordinate
\(\tau\), and undifferentiated propagation merely transports it to the
corresponding time-labelled copy \(\tau_a\).  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:historical_terminal_linear_growth}
 \left\|\pi_{\rm term}^{\rm hist}
   \sum_{a=0}^{N-1}
   \Ltransfer_0^{N-1-a}B_1\Ltransfer_0^a\rho_0
 \right\|_{\mathfrak T_N^{\rm hist}}
 =\sum_{a=0}^{N-1}\|\tau_a\|
 =N\|\tau\|.
\end{equation}
The same conclusion holds for a high-grazing truncation
\(\tau^{[\ge M]}\).  Consequently
\[
 \sup_{N\ge1}
 \|\pi_{\rm term}^{\rm hist}\mathscr G_{1,N}^{[\ge M]}\|<\infty
\]
can hold only if the one-event terminal germ at that truncation vanishes (or
if the history quotient is changed so that different insertion times are
assembled before taking the norm).  In particular a uniform-in-\(N\)
intrinsic historical terminal-closure theorem is not a consequence of a
decaying one-event tail: for every nonzero tail,
\(\sup_N N\|\tau^{[\ge M]}\|=\infty\).

For the radial blocks this gives a precise dichotomy.  Either the actual
labelled coefficient calculation proves eventwise terminal cancellation, or
the uniform statement available after complete assembly is necessarily the
regular reset of
\cref{thm:uniform_postassembly_radial_tail}, not convergence of the raw
history in the label-resolved terminal norm.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The insertion positions are distinct coordinates of an \(\ell^1\)-sum.
The terminal convention assigns no fictitious later billiard image to a
nonextending germ, so undifferentiated letters do not identify two different
time labels.  The norm of the displayed sum is therefore the sum of the
coordinate norms, which proves
\eqref{eq:historical_terminal_linear_growth}.  The truncation statement is
the same calculation in the closed subspace of indices with block number at
least \(M\).  Taking the supremum over \(N\) proves the dichotomy.  The last
paragraph only applies this abstract fact to the pre-reset expansion in
\eqref{eq:first_block_derivative_expansion}; the post-assembly identity
\((I-\Ltransfer_0^N)\rho_1\) lives in the regular coordinate and is a
different object.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Countable assembly-first scalar susceptibility]
\label{thm:countable_assembly_first_susceptibility}
Let \(r\ge0\) index a countable family of complete physical blocks.  Suppose
the \(r\)th block has finite truncations \(g_r^{(<M)}\), a certified assembly
reset to a centered strong current
\[
 \bar g_r=(I-\Ltransfer_0)h_r,
 \qquad h_r\in\mathfrak B_U,
\]
and a scalar reset error \(\eta_{r,M}\) as in
\eqref{eq:certified_scalar_reset_tail}.  Let \(f_r\) be centered admissible
tests and assume
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:countable_reset_summability}
 \sum_{r\ge0}\|h_r\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
                    \|f_r\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(1)}}<\infty,
 \qquad
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\sum_{r\ge0}
        \eta_{r,M}\|f_r\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(1)}}=0.
\end{equation}
Then the assembly-first double susceptibility is absolutely convergent and
independent of the finite terminal truncations:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:countable_assembly_first_identity}
 \sum_{r\ge0}\sum_{n\ge0}
   \langle\bar g_r,\mathscr U_0^nf_r\rangle
 &=\sum_{r\ge0}\langle h_r,f_r\rangle,\\
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\sum_{r\ge0}
   \langle\mathsf S_m\mathbf Q_mg_r^{(<M)},f_r\rangle
 &=\sum_{r\ge0}\langle\bar g_r,f_r\rangle.
\end{align}
Thus an infinite scalar response does not require a uniform intrinsic norm on
the raw labelled terminal history.  It requires summable strong primitives
and summable scalar reset errors.  This theorem does not create either
estimate; it identifies the strictly weaker, physically sufficient target.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The strong spectral gap and the primitive identity give
\[
 |\langle\bar g_r,\mathscr U_0^nf_r\rangle|
 \le C\vartheta^n\|h_r\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
                         \|f_r\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(1)}}.
\]
The first condition in \eqref{eq:countable_reset_summability} and Tonelli's
theorem give absolute convergence; summing in \(n\) telescopes to
\(\langle h_r,f_r\rangle\), and then summing in \(r\) proves the first
identity.  Summing the reset inequalities over \(r\) and using the second
condition proves the second identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A non-invariant coboundary susceptibility class]
\label{thm:noninvariant_coboundary_susceptibility}
Let \(s\mapsto\Ltransfer_s\) be the nonconjugate radial-inflation family of
\cref{thm:radial_inflation_second_order_recovery}.  Let \(h_s\) be any
centered \(C^J\) transported section of the Demers--Zhang strong bundle
\(\mathfrak B_U\), uniformly
bounded on a compact parameter interval, and set
\[
 g_s=(I-\Ltransfer_s)h_s.
\]
For every centered \(C^J\) transported observable \(f_s\) in the corresponding
strong dual class, the dynamical scalar susceptibility
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:noninvariant_coboundary_susceptibility}
 \chi_s(g_s,f_s)
 :=
 \sum_{n\ge0}\langle g_s,\mathscr U_s^nf_s\rangle
 =
 \langle h_s,f_s\rangle
\end{equation}
exists, is \(C^J\), and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:noninvariant_coboundary_derivatives}
 \partial_s^j\chi_s(g_s,f_s)
 =
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{j}\binom j\ell
 \langle\partial_s^\ell h_s,
        \partial_s^{j-\ell}f_s\rangle ,
 \qquad 0\le j\le J,
\end{equation}
with the transported density--observable pairing understood.
Choosing a nonconstant \(h_s\) gives a non-invariant source on a genuinely
nonconjugate billiard family.

The theorem is an exact-coboundary dynamical benchmark.  It proves neither
termwise differentiation of the infinite insertion expansion nor all-seed
(S1)/(S3); those stronger conclusions require the vector-valued and
source-specific estimates isolated above.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every finite \(N\), duality gives
\[
 \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle g_s,\mathscr U_s^nf_s\rangle
 =
 \langle h_s,f_s\rangle
 -\langle\Ltransfer_s^Nh_s,f_s\rangle .
\]
The uniform centered strong spectral gap sends the last term to zero
geometrically, uniformly for \(s\) in the compact interval.  This proves
\eqref{eq:noninvariant_coboundary_susceptibility}.  Its right-hand side is a
\(C^J\) transported pairing, and the ordinary Leibniz rule gives
\eqref{eq:noninvariant_coboundary_derivatives}.  No parameter derivative of
\(\Ltransfer_s\) is used to obtain this identity, so no unproved all-branch
completion is hidden in the argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform correlation susceptibility on the dynamical strong space]
\label{thm:uniform_strong_correlation_susceptibility}
Let \(s\mapsto\Ltransfer_s\) be any transported billiard family on a compact
clean interval with the uniform period-one Demers--Zhang spectral gap.  Let
\(g_s\) be any centered strong source and \(f_s\) a centered admissible
observable such that
\[
 \sup_s\|g_s\|_{\mathfrak B_U}<\infty,
 \qquad
 \sup_s\|f_s\|_{\Bcal_{\theta_s}^\#}<\infty.
\]
Then the genuinely correlation-generated series
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uniform_strong_correlation_series}
 \Xi_s(g_s,f_s)
 :=\sum_{n\ge0}\langle g_s,\mathscr U_s^nf_s\rangle
\end{equation}
converges geometrically and uniformly in \(s\).  Its canonical primitive is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:canonical_correlation_primitive}
 h_s=(I-\Ltransfer_s)^{-1}g_s
     =\sum_{n\ge0}\Ltransfer_s^ng_s,
 \qquad
 \Xi_s(g_s,f_s)=\langle h_s,f_s\rangle .
\end{equation}
If the transported finite-time pairings are continuous in \(s\), then
\(\Xi_s\) is continuous.  In particular this applies to
\[
 g_s=a_s\rho_s-\langle\rho_s,a_s\rangle\rho_s
\]
for a uniformly strong transported correlation density \(a_s\rho_s\).

Thus on a period-one strong density space there is no separate
``non-coboundary strong sector'': every centered strong source has the
canonical primitive \eqref{eq:canonical_correlation_primitive}.  The new
moving-singularity problem is differentiability of \(s\mapsto h_s\), not
existence of the fixed-table susceptibility.  No parameter differentiability
is asserted by this theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The uniform centered spectral gap gives constants \(C<\infty\) and
\(\vartheta<1\) such that
\[
 |\langle\Ltransfer_s^ng_s,f_s\rangle|
 \le C\vartheta^n
 \|g_s\|_{\mathfrak B_U}\|f_s\|_{\Bcal_{\theta_s}^\#}
\]
uniformly in \(s\).  The Weierstrass test proves uniform convergence and the
strong Neumann series in \eqref{eq:canonical_correlation_primitive}; duality
gives the last equality there.  Continuity follows by taking a finite partial
sum, using continuity of each transported finite-time pairing, and then using
the uniform geometric tail.  Centering of the displayed correlation density
is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rigid-translation prescribed-source consistency]

 \Psi_s(q,v)=(q+sw,v)

 \widetilde\Ltransfer_s=\Ltransfer_0,\qquad

 h_0=(I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}g_0,
 \qquad h_s=\mathcal C_sh_0.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rigid_translation_poisson_sources}
 q_1=0,\qquad q_2=0,
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:independent_flux_conjugate_susceptibility}
 \sum_{n\ge0}\langle g_s,\mathscr U_s^nf_s\rangle
 =\langle h_0,\widetilde f_s\rangle
\end{equation}
converges uniformly and is \(C^J\).  This is an independently prescribed

formalism.  It is not a moving-face cancellation theorem: exact conjugacy

moving-face coefficient is absent rather than cancelled.

\begin{proof}
Common translation preserves normalized collision flux and conjugates the

\eqref{eq:rigid_translation_fixed_fiber_identities}.  Since \(a\) depends

the centered strong \(h_0\), while the chosen bundle trivialization makes
\(h_s=\mathcal C_sh_0\) constant in the fixed fiber.  The strong
\(C^J\) assertion, \eqref{eq:rigid_translation_poisson_sources}, zero

correlation.  Summing the centered fixed-table series gives
\((I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}g_0=h_0\), which proves
\eqref{eq:independent_flux_conjugate_susceptibility}.  Differentiability is
only that of the prescribed fixed-fiber observable section.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Second-order Poisson recovery criterion]
\label{prop:second_order_poisson_recovery_criterion}
In the setting of
\cref{thm:uniform_strong_correlation_susceptibility}, suppose \(g_s\) has two
transported strong jets \(g_1,g_2\) at \(s=0\), and write
\[
 B_1=\partial_s\Ltransfer_s|_0,
 \qquad B_2=\partial_s^2\Ltransfer_s|_0,
 \qquad h_0=(I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}g_0.
\]
Form the \emph{complete assembled} first Poisson source
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:first_poisson_recovery_source}
 q_1:=g_1+B_1h_0.
\end{equation}
If its represented current is centered strong and its terminal history has a
certified assembly reset, put
\(h_1=(I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}q_1\).  Next form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:second_poisson_recovery_source}
 q_2:=g_2+2B_1h_1+B_2h_0.
\end{equation}
If this second complete source has the same recovery property, put
\(h_2=(I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}q_2\).

Assume, finally, that the complete source expansion has the strong remainder
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:poisson_second_order_remainder}
 \bigl\|(I-\Ltransfer_s)
   (h_0+sh_1+\tfrac12s^2h_2)-g_s\bigr\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
 =o(s^2),
\end{equation}
after the two certified resets.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:poisson_primitive_second_order_expansion}
 h_s=h_0+sh_1+\tfrac12s^2h_2+o_{\mathfrak B_U}(s^2),
\end{equation}
and, for a \(C^2\) transported observable \(f_s\), the correlation
susceptibility \eqref{eq:uniform_strong_correlation_series} is twice
differentiable with
\begin{align*}
 \Xi'_0&=\langle h_1,f_0\rangle+\langle h_0,f_1\rangle,\\
 \Xi''_0&=\langle h_2,f_0\rangle
          +2\langle h_1,f_1\rangle+\langle h_0,f_2\rangle.
\end{align*}
Conversely, strong twice differentiability of the canonical primitive forces
\eqref{eq:first_poisson_recovery_source}--
\eqref{eq:second_poisson_recovery_source} to be centered strong identities.
Hence these two assembled sources are the exact second-order triangular test
for a non-invariant correlation density; they cannot be replaced by
eventwise all-seed recovery.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the Poisson equation
\((I-\Ltransfer_s)h_s=g_s\) once and twice.  The resulting identities are
\[
 (I-\Ltransfer_0)h_1=q_1,
 \qquad
 (I-\Ltransfer_0)h_2=q_2,
\]
which proves necessity and the formulae for the canonical jets.  For
sufficiency let \(\widetilde h_s=h_0+sh_1+s^2h_2/2\).  The uniform centered
strong resolvent bound and \eqref{eq:poisson_second_order_remainder} give
\[
 \|h_s-\widetilde h_s\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
 \le C_{\rm gap}
 \|(I-\Ltransfer_s)\widetilde h_s-g_s\|_{\mathfrak B_U}
 =o(s^2),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:poisson_primitive_second_order_expansion}.  Differentiate
the transported pairing \(\Xi_s=\langle h_s,f_s\rangle\).  The qualification
about complete assembly is essential: if the one-face or point sector of
\(q_1\) or \(q_2\) survives, it is not a strong source and the displayed
resolvent step is unavailable.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition*}[Canonical weighted branch-trace targets]
Fix a finite family \(\mathscr F\) of compact regular branch-face charts.  On
the square-root compactified blown-up phase surface fix a smooth background metric and
connection, uniform over the compact table chart.  Write
\(\|\cdot\|_{J^r(I)}\) for the maximum of the covariant derivatives through
order \(r\) on a branch interval.  For \(n\ge0\), cut each side
at the intersections with the singularity curves of the inverse branches in
\(\Ltransfer_0^n\), including every quadratic homogeneity boundary.  The
maximal connected pieces form the canonical countable label set
\(\Lambda_n(\mathscr F)\).  A label \(\lambda\) records the physical face,
incident side, length-\(n\) inverse word, homogeneity itinerary, and cut
interval \(I_\lambda\).  Genuine dynamical cuts always retain two one-sided
labels.

The high-strip loss is recorded rather than suppressed.  If \(e\) is one edge
of an inverse branch word, let \(\mathcal C_e\) be its intrinsic one-step
pullback on branch jets, including the invariant-flux Jacobian.  Set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:branch_cocycle_weight}
 b_r(e):=\max\{1,\|\mathcal C_e\|_{J^r\to J^r}\},
 \qquad
 W_r(\lambda):=\prod_{e\prec\lambda}b_r(e).
\end{equation}
The norm in \eqref{eq:branch_cocycle_weight} is intrinsic.  In a high
homogeneity strip \(b_r(e)\) is allowed to grow by the
polynomial strip factor supplied by the impact equations; no uniform
unweighted branch bound is assumed.

An \emph{admissible marked refinement} of \(\Lambda_n(\mathscr F)\)
subdivides the intervals \(I_\lambda\).  A cut inherited from an earlier
finite-depth singularity of the density is marked genuine and retains two
one-sided labels; a presentational cut is marked artificial and its two
\(r\)-jets are required to match.  Every child inherits the weight
\(W_r(\lambda)\).  On such a refinement \(\mathcal Q\), put
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dynamic_branch_trace_target}
 \|v\|_{\mathcal T_{n,\mathscr F}^r(\mathcal Q)}
 :=\sup_{J\subset I_\lambda,\ J\in\mathcal Q}
 W_r(\lambda)^{-1}\|v_J\|_{J^r(\overline J)}.
\end{equation}
Restriction to a further marked refinement is isometric: the supremum of each
derivative over a compact parent is the supremum over its children, and
matching across an artificial cut is retained.  The common refinement marks a
cut genuine if it is genuine in either presentation.  Define
\(\mathbf T_{n,\mathscr F}^{r,W}\) to be the Banach completion of the
algebraic direct limit over all admissible locally finite refinements.  Hence
two presentations are compared only after their canonical marked common
refinement; artificial cuts are forgotten by the direct-limit relation,
whereas genuine backward-singularity cuts are never regrouped.  This supplies one fixed target
for each \(n\), independent of an initial depth assigned to a representative.
Finite \(C^{r+1}\) charts merely represent the intrinsic jets.  Their
triangular chain-rule matrices and inverses are uniformly bounded on the
compact family, so any two finite presentations of this same intrinsic target
are canonically equivalent with constants independent of \(n\).

The dynamic trace core \(\mathscr C_{\rm dyn}^r\) consists of bounded
densities whose restriction and every finite transfer image are branchwise
\(C^r\) on an admissible refinement.  For \(h\) in this core,
\(\operatorname{Tr}^{(n)}_{\mathscr F}h\) is the direct-limit class of the
one-sided restrictions of \(\Ltransfer_0^nh\).  The finite-atlas norm
\(\|h\|_{C_{\rm br}^r}\) is the maximum of the branch-interior \(C^r\)-norm
and the two one-sided depth-zero trace norms.

For a general \(h\in\mathfrak B_U\cap L^\infty\), a weighted trace orbit is
said to exist if there are \(h_k\in\mathscr C_{\rm dyn}^r\) converging to
\(h\) in \(\mathfrak B_U\cap L^\infty\) whose trace-orbit vectors converge in
the weighted product norm used in \eqref{eq:actual_trace_orbit_norm}.  The
closability argument below proves that the resulting orbit is independent of
the approximating sequence; this is the meaning of every trace in
\eqref{eq:actual_trace_orbit_norm}.

For a compact subarc \(K\Subset I\) set
\[
 \mathcal T_K^r(I)
 :=\{\phi\in C^r(I):\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset K\},
 \qquad
 \|\phi\|_{\mathcal T_K^r(I)}
 :=\max_{0\le j\le r}\|\partial_t^j\phi\|_{C^0(I)}.
\]
The zero-extension into the appropriate component of
\(\mathbf T_{0,\mathscr F}^{r,W}\) is bounded.
\end{definition*}

\begin{proposition}[Weighted primitive trace domain and radial membership]
\label{prop:actual_trace_orbit_domain}
On \(\mathscr C_{\rm dyn}^r\), the branch-cocycle weight gives the one-step
estimate and its iterate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iterated_dynamic_trace_estimate}
 \|\operatorname{Tr}^{(n)}_{\mathscr F}h\|_{
       \mathbf T_{n,\mathscr F}^{r,W}}
 \le C_{\rm at}\|h\|_{C_{\rm br}^{r}},
 \qquad n\ge0.
\end{equation}
Fix \(A>1\), and define the weighted primitive trace domain by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:actual_trace_orbit_norm}
 \mathcal O_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}(h)
 :=\sup_{n\ge0}A^{-n}
     \|\operatorname{Tr}^{(n)}_{\mathscr F}h\|_{
       \mathbf T_{n,\mathscr F}^{r,W}}.
\end{equation}
Then set
\begin{equation*}
 \mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}
 :=\left\{h\in\mathfrak B_U\cap L^\infty(\nu_0):
 \substack{\operatorname{Tr}^{(n)}_{\mathscr F}h\in
      \mathbf T_{n,\mathscr F}^{r,W}\ \forall n,\\
   \mathcal O_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}(h)<\infty}\right\}.
\end{equation*}
with
\begin{equation*}
 \|h\|_{\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}}
 :=\|h\|_{\mathfrak B_U}+\|h\|_{L^\infty(\nu_0)}
   +\mathcal O_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}(h).
\end{equation*}
Then \(\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\) is an injective Banach subspace of
\(\mathfrak B_U\cap L^\infty(\nu_0)\), independent up to uniform equivalence
of admissible refinements and the finite atlas.  Every one-sided face trace is
bounded into the corresponding depth-zero component.  For every \(K\Subset I\)
there is a bounded tubular extension
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted_tubular_extension}
 E_+:\mathcal T_K^r(I)\longrightarrow\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F},
 \qquad
 \sup_{n\ge0}A^{-n}
 \|\operatorname{Tr}^{(n)}_{\mathscr F}E_+\phi\|_{
       \mathbf T_{n,\mathscr F}^{r,W}}
 \le C_E\|\phi\|_{\mathcal T_K^r(I)},
\end{equation}
supported near one preimage arc and having zero trace on the other.  No
transfer invariance of \(\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\) is asserted; the cocycle
weight is used precisely because deleting the first edge of a high-strip word
need not have a uniform unweighted norm.

For the radial inflation, all invariant density jets \(\rho_j\) appearing in
\eqref{eq:radial_inflation_flux_jets} belong to
\(\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\).  Hence
\[
 (I-\Ltransfer_0)\rho_j,\qquad
 (I-\Ltransfer_0^N)\rho_j,\qquad
 \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Ltransfer_0^k\rho_j
\]
belong to \(\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\) for every fixed \(N\).  With
\(R_0=(I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}\) on \(\mathfrak B_U^0\), the actual Poisson
graph space
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:actual_poisson_trace_graph_space}
 \mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}
 :=\{g\in\mathfrak B_U^0\cap L^\infty(\nu_0):R_0g\in
       \mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\},\qquad
 \|g\|_{\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}}
 :=\|g\|_{\mathfrak B_U}+\|g\|_{L^\infty}
   +\|R_0g\|_{\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}}
\end{equation}
is Banach.  In particular the radial invariant
sources, their displayed parameter jets, the fixed-\(N\) assembled currents,
and every exact current \((I-\Ltransfer_0)h\) with
\(h\in\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\) and
\((I-\Ltransfer_0)h\in L^\infty\) lie in
\(\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On one inverse edge, the chain rule and branch Jacobian formula give
\(\|\mathcal C_ev\|_{J^r}\le b_r(e)\|v\|_{J^r}\) by definition.  This
includes the polynomial homogeneity-strip loss; it is not replaced by a
uniform constant.  Multiplying along the word and dividing by
\(W_r(\lambda)\) proves \eqref{eq:iterated_dynamic_trace_estimate} on every
canonical piece.  Cutting before composition prevents derivatives from
crossing a singularity.  Taking a supremum introduces no branch multiplicity.
The direct-limit construction proves refinement independence, and the finite
maximum built into \(b_r(e)\) proves the atlas comparison.

We prove the point omitted by a bare anisotropic graph completion.  Suppose
\(h_k\to0\) in \(\mathfrak B_U\cap L^\infty\) and the weighted trace-orbit
coordinates are Cauchy in the last term of
\eqref{eq:actual_trace_orbit_norm}.  For every fixed \(n\), every label
\(\lambda\), and every interior point of its branch interval,
\(\Ltransfer_0^nh_k\to0\) in \(L^\infty\), because the fixed-table transfer is
composition with the inverse billiard map relative to invariant flux.  Each
one-sided representative is continuous up to the face from its incident open
branch, so its boundary value is bounded by the essential supremum on that
branch.  The \(C^r\)-limit of the labelled traces therefore vanishes pointwise and hence is
the zero \(C^r\) function.  Since the orbit coordinate is Cauchy in the
weighted \(\ell^\infty\) norm, its limit is zero there.  Thus no element
\((0,\tau\ne0)\) occurs in the graph closure.  This proves injectivity.
The same argument applied to a Cauchy sequence, together with completeness of
\(\mathfrak B_U\), \(L^\infty\), and every direct-limit target, proves that
\(\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\) is Banach.

Choose a fixed tubular extension in the finite atlas.  Its depth-zero
\(C^r\)-norm is bounded by \(C_E\|\phi\|_{\mathcal T_K^r(I)}\).
Applying the just-proved cocycle-normalized estimate to every future inverse
word gives \eqref{eq:weighted_tubular_extension}; this is a separate full-orbit
estimate, not an invocation of an unweighted high-strip bound.

Each \(\rho_j\) is componentwise constant in collision coordinates.  On every
dynamic cut, \(\Ltransfer_0^n\rho_j=\rho_j\circ\Pcal_0^{-n}\) is again
constant, so all positive trace derivatives vanish and the cocycle-weighted
supremum is bounded uniformly in \(n\).  The same direct calculation applies
to each fixed finite sum displayed above; no transfer invariance of
\(\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\) is used.  Finally, if \(g_k\to g\) in the first
two terms of \eqref{eq:actual_poisson_trace_graph_space} and
\(R_0g_k\to h\) in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\), boundedness of \(R_0\) on
\(\mathfrak B_U^0\) and injectivity of the primitive space give \(h=R_0g\).
Thus the graph is closed and \(\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\) is Banach.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition*}[Rooted weighted source presentations]
Let \(\kappa\ge1\) dominate the operator norm of
\(\Ltransfer_0:\mathfrak B_U\to\mathfrak B_U\); on \(L^\infty\) the
fixed-table transfer is an isometry.  A finite rooted presentation is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rooted_weighted_source_presentation}
 g=\sum_{d=0}^D\Ltransfer_0^df_d,
 \qquad f_d\in\mathscr C_{\rm dyn}^r,
\end{equation}
with cost
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rooted_weighted_source_cost}
 \|g\|_{\rm pres}
 :=\inf_{\eqref{eq:rooted_weighted_source_presentation}}
   \sum_{d=0}^D\kappa^d
   \left(
    \|f_d\|_{C_{\rm br}^r}
    +\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_d(\mathscr F)}
      W_r(\lambda)^{-1}
      \|\operatorname{Tr}_{\lambda}(\Ltransfer_0^df_d)\|_{J^r}
   \right).
\end{equation}
The depth-
\(d\) branch jets in \eqref{eq:rooted_weighted_source_presentation} are
measured after division by the intrinsic cocycle weight \(W_r\) of
\eqref{eq:branch_cocycle_weight}; thus the presentation remembers the smooth
root rather than imposing a false unweighted bound on its transfer image.
Let \(\mathfrak S_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}\) be the closed graph completion of the
realized density together with its cocycle-normalized rooted branch jets,
after quotienting finite presentations that give the same jets on their
marked common refinement, with norm
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted_source_presentation_norm}
 \|g\|_{\mathfrak S_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}}
 :=\|g\|_{\mathfrak B_U}+\|g\|_{L^\infty}+\|g\|_{\rm pres}.
\end{equation}
The \(L^\infty\) anchor makes the rooted jet graph closable: if realized
densities converge to zero in \(L^\infty\) and their normalized rooted jets
are Cauchy, then on each regular branch the limiting zero-order value is zero,
and the \(C^r\)-limit of the jets is therefore the zero jet.  Thus the
realization map is injective and continuous, and
\(\Ltransfer_0:\mathfrak S_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}\to
\mathfrak S_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}\) is bounded.  At every fixed regular point,
and hence on every fixed finite regular periodic orbit, evaluation on the
finite-presentation core extends continuously to
\(\mathfrak S_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}\).
\end{definition*}

Indeed, \(C_{\rm br}^r\) controls both the strong and supremum norms of a
root, while the factor \(\kappa^d\) controls its realization at depth \(d\).
The second term in \eqref{eq:rooted_weighted_source_cost} is bounded by the
first uniformly in \(d\), by
\eqref{eq:iterated_dynamic_trace_estimate}; it is displayed separately to
record the source-side branch topology rather than hiding it in the root.
The same anchored closability argument as in
\cref{prop:actual_trace_orbit_domain} shows that the graph completion is
Banach and injects into \(\mathfrak B_U\cap L^\infty\).  Shifting every root from depth \(d\) to
\(d+1\) multiplies the presentation cost by at most \(\kappa\).  Finally,
for a regular point \(z\),
\[
 |(\Ltransfer_0^df_d)(z)|
 =|f_d(T_0^{-d}z)|
 \le\|f_d\|_{C_{\rm br}^r}
 \le\kappa^d\|f_d\|_{C_{\rm br}^r}.
\]
Taking the infimum proves continuity of point and finite-orbit evaluation.
Most importantly, for the tubular extension in
\eqref{eq:weighted_tubular_extension},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted_source_two_root_bound}
 \|(I-\Ltransfer_0)E_+\phi\|_{
       \mathfrak S_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}}
 \le C_S(1+\kappa)\|\phi\|_{\mathcal T_K^r(I)},
\end{equation}
because the right-hand side has the explicit two-root presentation
\(f_0=E_+\phi\), \(f_1=-E_+\phi\).  No unweighted branch norm of
\(\Ltransfer_0E_+\phi\) is used.

\begin{theorem}[Split moving-face gluing cascade on the weighted pullback domain]
\label{prop:poisson_moving_face_gluing_obstruction}
Let \(s\mapsto g_s\) be a centered \(C^1\) source family in
\(\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\) and let
\[
 h_0=(I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}g_0.
\]
Fix an isolated moving physical face
\(\Gamma_s=\{G_s=0\}\) on which the two branch formulae are
\(\mathcal A_{+,s}\) and \(\mathcal A_{-,s}\).  Assume no other face current
has the same labelled support on the chosen open subarc.  The singular part
of the first Poisson source \(q_1=g_1+B_1h_0\) on that subarc is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:poisson_first_gluing_current}
 \dot G_0\,
 \bigl[\mathcal A_{+,0}h_0-\mathcal A_{-,0}h_0\bigr]_{\Gamma_0}
 \delta(G_0)
\end{equation}
up to the fixed smooth coarea factor and orientation.  Hence strong
differentiability of the canonical primitive
\(h_s=(I-\Ltransfer_s)^{-1}g_s\) forces the gluing condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:poisson_first_gluing_condition}
 \dot G_0\,
 \bigl[\mathcal A_{+,0}h_0-\mathcal A_{-,0}h_0\bigr]_{\Gamma_0}=0
\end{equation}
on every isolated moving face.  If it fails on one open subarc, then
\(q_1\) is not strong and the strong derivative \(\partial_sh_s|_0\) does not
exist.

Suppose on an open subarc \(I\Subset\Gamma_0\) that
\(\dot G_0\ne0\), that the two branch preimage arcs \(I_+,I_-\) are disjoint,
that the regular branch multipliers are nonvanishing, and that the bounded
tubular extension from \cref{prop:actual_trace_orbit_domain} is supported away
from the opposite preimage arc.  On the graph domain
\(\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\), the localized gluing map
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:localized_poisson_gluing_map}
 \mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}g
 :=\dot G_0[\mathcal A_{+,0}-\mathcal A_{-,0}]
 (I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}g\big|_I
\end{equation}
has range containing \(C_c^r(I)\).  In particular it has infinite rank.
For a compact \(K\Subset I\) with nonempty interior, the localized map onto
\(\mathcal T_K^r(I)\) has a bounded linear right
inverse.  Hence its kernel is complemented and the source
quotient by that kernel contains a Banach copy of \(\mathcal T_K^r(I)\).
Consequently the sources satisfying all conditions
\eqref{eq:poisson_first_gluing_condition} form a closed subspace of infinite
codimension, not merely a proper closed subspace.  A successful
finite-dimensional natural source family must therefore lie in that kernel by
a structural identity; parameter counting or generic coefficient tuning does
not provide one.

Assume additionally that the complete first source built from \(g_0\) and one
reference strong jet \(g_1^*\) is recovered, so that \(h_1^*\) is defined, that
the second-order one-sided traces exist on \(I\), and that \(g_2\) is strong.
Keep \(g_0\) fixed and vary the first source jet as
\(g_1=g_1^*+\delta g_1\) with
\(\delta g_1\in\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\).  After first gluing
removes the \(\delta'(G_0)\) coefficient, the localized \(\delta(G_0)\)
coefficient of \(q_2\) has the affine form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:second_poisson_local_affine_gluing}
 \mathcal F_{\Gamma,I}(g_0,g_1^*)
 +2\mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}\delta g_1,
\end{equation}
where the first term is independent of \(\delta g_1\).  Hence, for every
compact \(K\Subset I\) on which that fixed coefficient is smooth, the jets
\(g_1\) for which \(q_2\) has no face current on \(K\) form a nonempty affine
subspace modelled on the kernel of the restricted gluing map, and therefore
have infinite codimension.  Passing first-order gluing does not make the
second-order test a finite collection of scalar conditions.

More generally, suppose the complete Poisson sources have been recovered
through order \(m-1\), with reference jets fixed, and the required order-\(m\)
one-sided traces exist.  Vary only the source jet
\(g_{m-1}=g_{m-1}^*+\delta g_{m-1}\), with
\(\delta g_{m-1}\in\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\), keeping \(g_m\) strong and all lower
jets fixed.  The localized face coefficient of
\[
 q_m=g_m+\sum_{\ell=1}^m\binom m\ell B_\ell h_{m-\ell}
\]
has the affine form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finite_order_local_affine_gluing}
 \mathcal F_{\Gamma,I}^{(m)}
 +m\mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}\delta g_{m-1}.
\end{equation}
Thus at every finite order the next admissible source jet lies in a localized
affine infinite-codimension slice.  Finite-order recovery is a cascade of
functional gluing constraints, not a finite-dimensional transversality test.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The strong jet \(g_1\) has no transverse current.  Differentiating the
two-sided branch indicator gives exactly the jump term
\eqref{eq:first_event_jump_current} with \(h=h_0\), which is
\eqref{eq:poisson_first_gluing_current}.  An isolated nonzero transverse
current is weak but not strong by
\cref{prop:demers_zhang_trace_location}; it therefore cannot be cancelled by
the regular part of \(g_1\).  The necessity of
\eqref{eq:poisson_first_gluing_condition} follows also by differentiating the
Poisson equation, which would require
\((I-\Ltransfer_0)\partial_sh_s|_0=q_1\) in the strong space.

The map \eqref{eq:localized_poisson_gluing_map} is continuous and linear on the
weighted Poisson-pullback graph domain, so its kernel is closed.  To prove local surjectivity,
fix \(\phi\in C_c^r(I)\).  Since \(\dot G_0\) is bounded away from zero on the
compact support of \(\phi\), the trace datum \(\phi/\dot G_0\) is smooth.
Choose a smooth strong density \(h_\phi\), supported in a tubular
neighbourhood of \(I_+\) disjoint from \(I_-\), such that
\[
 [\mathcal A_{+,0}h_\phi]_I=\phi/\dot G_0,
 \qquad [\mathcal A_{-,0}h_\phi]_I=0.
\]
Put \(g_\phi=(I-\Ltransfer_0)h_\phi\).  It is a centered strong source.  Its
canonical centered primitive is
\[
 (I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}g_\phi
 =h_\phi-\langle h_\phi,1\rangle\rho_0.
\]
The invariant density has identical one-sided branch traces, so the subtracted
constant-density term has zero jump.  Therefore
\(\mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}g_\phi=\phi\).  Since
\(C_c^r(I)\) is infinite-dimensional, the localized gluing map has infinite
rank and its kernel has infinite codimension.  The joint kernel over all
moving faces is contained in this kernel, which proves the final assertion.

For the split statement, choose a fixed tubular extension operator
\[
 E_+:\mathcal T_K^r(I)\longrightarrow\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}
\]
supported near \(I_+\) and with zero trace on \(I_-\), including the inverse
of the nonvanishing branch multiplier.  Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:localized_gluing_right_inverse}
 \mathcal R_{\Gamma,K}\phi
 :=(I-\Ltransfer_0)E_+\!\left(\phi/\dot G_0\right).
\end{equation}
Boundedness of \(E_+\) and \(\Ltransfer_0\) makes
\(\mathcal R_{\Gamma,K}\) bounded into
\(\mathfrak B_U^0\cap L^\infty\).  Its \(\mathfrak B_U\)-resolvent is
\(E_+(\phi/\dot G_0)\) modulo the invariant-density centering term, hence lies
in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\).  Thus the right inverse is bounded into
\(\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\).  The
same calculation as above gives
\[
 \mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}\mathcal R_{\Gamma,K}\phi=\phi.
\]
Thus
\[
 \mathcal P_{\Gamma,K}
 :=I-\mathcal R_{\Gamma,K}\mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}
\]
is a bounded projection onto the localized gluing kernel on the corresponding
trace domain, proving the complemented-kernel assertion.

For the second-order statement, first gluing removes the coefficient of
\(\delta'(G_0)\) produced by twice differentiating the moving indicator.  A
graph-domain variation \(\delta g_1\) changes the recovered first source and its
primitive by
\[
 \delta q_1=\delta g_1,\qquad
 \delta h_1=(I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}\delta g_1.
\]
In \(q_2=g_2+2B_1h_1+B_2h_0\), the strong jet \(g_2\) has no face
coordinate, and the moving-face coefficient of the variable term
\(2B_1\delta h_1\) is precisely
\(2\mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}\delta g_1\).  All remaining terms form the fixed
coefficient \(\mathcal F_{\Gamma,I}(g_0,g_1^*)\), which proves
\eqref{eq:second_poisson_local_affine_gluing}.  Choose a cutoff
\(\eta\in C_c^r(I)\) equal to one on \(K\).  Local surjectivity supplies
\(\delta g_1\) with
\(2\mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}\delta g_1
=-\eta\mathcal F_{\Gamma,I}(g_0,g_1^*)\).
Thus the coefficient vanishes on \(K\), and the full localized solution set is
an affine translate of the kernel of the restricted gluing map.  Its
codimension is infinite by the first part.

For general \(m\), varying \(g_{m-1}\) changes the recovered primitive jet by
\[
 \delta h_{m-1}=(I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}\delta g_{m-1}.
\]
In \(q_m\), the only term containing this jet is
\(\binom m1B_1h_{m-1}=mB_1h_{m-1}\).  Its moving-face variation is therefore
\(m\mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}\delta g_{m-1}\); all other terms are fixed.  This
proves \eqref{eq:finite_order_local_affine_gluing}, and the same cutoff plus
local-surjectivity argument proves the affine infinite-codimension conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Split obstruction on weighted potential and Poisson-pullback classes]
\label{cor:prescribed_correlation_observable_split_obstruction}
For the radial-inflation family, in particular, let
\[
 g_s=a_s\rho_s-\langle\rho_s,a_s\rangle\rho_s
\]
with \(a_s\rho_s\) a transported strong correlation density, and let
\(h_0=(I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}g_0\).  Before any second-order calculation is
available, if
\(g_0\in\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\) so that the one-sided
Poisson traces are defined, strong differentiability requires
\eqref{eq:poisson_first_gluing_condition} on every radial moving face.  If
this test passes, the second-order problem is exactly the strong recovery of
\(q_2\) in \eqref{eq:second_poisson_recovery_source}, including its one-face
and point-current coefficients.  Therefore a natural radial correlation
density cannot be certified merely from the strong regularity of \(a_s\); its
Poisson primitive must satisfy the branch-gluing identities generated by the
moving singularities.

There is first a Poisson-independent formulation.  Fix \(K\Subset I\) as in
\cref{prop:poisson_moving_face_gluing_obstruction}.  At the base table
\(\rho_0\equiv1\) relative to \(\nu_0\), so the
observable-to-source map is the bounded centered projection
\[
 \mathcal M_0a=(a-\langle\rho_0,a\rangle)\rho_0.
\]
Define the weighted local-potential class
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted_potential_observable_domain}
 \mathfrak A_{\rm pot}(\mathscr F)
 :=\{a\in C_{\rm br}^r(\mathscr F):
       \mathcal M_0a\in\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}\},
 \qquad
 \|a\|_{\mathfrak A_{\rm pot}}
 :=\|a\|_{C_{\rm br}^r}+\|\mathcal M_0a\|_{\mathfrak P_{\mathscr F}}.
\end{equation}
This definition uses only the physical observable, invariant density, and
branch-derivative cocycle; it contains no Poisson inverse.  The local potential
class is Banach by the closed-graph argument in
\cref{prop:actual_trace_orbit_domain}.  The local potential
defect
\[
 \mathcal D_{\Gamma,K}a
 :=\dot G_0[\mathcal A_{+,0}(\mathcal M_0a)
             -\mathcal A_{-,0}(\mathcal M_0a)]\big|_K
\]
is a split surjection onto \(\mathcal T_K^r(I)\).  Apply
\eqref{eq:weighted_tubular_extension} to \(\phi/\dot G_0\), divide by
\(\rho_0\), and subtract the invariant mean.  The mean term has identical
one-sided traces and does not alter the defect.  Thus the zero-defect
potentials form a closed complemented infinite-codimension subspace, with open
dense complement in this explicit weighted branch-potential topology.  This
is the Poisson-independent split impossibility theorem.  It concerns
prescribed local potentials; it does not assert that every ordinary \(C^r\)
correlation observable has a trace-regular canonical Poisson primitive.

For the latter correlation problem, define the pullback graph class
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:correlation_observable_graph_domain}
\begin{split}
 \mathfrak A_{\rm corr}(\mathscr F)
 :=\{a\in L^\infty(\nu_0):
       \mathcal M_0a\in
       \mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\cap
       \mathfrak S_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}\},
 \\
 \|a\|_{\mathfrak A_{\rm corr}(\mathscr F)}
 :=\|a\|_{L^\infty}
   +\|\mathcal M_0a\|_{\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}}
   +\|\mathcal M_0a\|_{\mathfrak S_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
This is an actual Banach intersection-pullback domain over the weighted
primitive and rooted-source spaces.  It has continuous canonical regular
periodic evaluation: on the finite-presentation core use ordinary point
values and extend by
\eqref{eq:weighted_source_presentation_norm}; the invariant mean contributes
only the period times one bounded scalar.  It contains the
componentwise smooth radial invariant-density jets and those exact-current
observable classes in \cref{prop:actual_trace_orbit_domain} that admit a
rooted weighted presentation; it is not
asserted to contain every branchwise smooth correlation observable, since
membership requires both a trace-regular canonical Poisson primitive and a
rooted weighted source presentation.  In particular, this definition does
not reintroduce an unweighted \(C_{\rm br}^r\)-norm of the transferred
source.
The localized prescribed-observable defect map
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prescribed_observable_gluing_map}
 \mathcal H_{\Gamma,K}a
 :=\mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}\mathcal M_0a\big|_K
\end{equation}
is a split surjection onto \(\mathcal T_K^r(I)\).  Indeed, with
\(\mathcal R_{\Gamma,K}\) from
\eqref{eq:localized_gluing_right_inverse},
\[
 \mathcal S_{\Gamma,K}\phi
 :=\rho_0^{-1}\mathcal R_{\Gamma,K}\phi
\]
is centered.  Its \(\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\)-bound is the explicit
primitive estimate in
\eqref{eq:localized_gluing_right_inverse}, while its
\(\mathfrak S_{\mathscr F}^{r,W}\)-bound is the two-root estimate
\eqref{eq:weighted_source_two_root_bound}.  Hence it is bounded into
\(\mathfrak A_{\rm corr}(\mathscr F)\), without requiring
\(\Ltransfer_0E_+\phi\) to have a uniform unweighted branch norm, and it satisfies
\(\mathcal H_{\Gamma,K}\mathcal S_{\Gamma,K}=I\).
Consequently the observables in this pullback graph class that
pass first gluing on \(K\) form a closed complemented subspace of infinite
codimension; its complement is open and dense in the graph topology.  This
second statement is deliberately separated from the Poisson-free potential
theorem above: it is a graph-domain consequence, not a genericity claim about
all branchwise smooth billiard observables.


If the observable jets through order \(m-2\) are fixed and one varies only
\(a_{m-1}\) in the intersection-pullback topology, then
\(\delta g_{m-1}=\mathcal M_0\delta a_{m-1}\).  Hence the order-\(m\) face
coefficient varies by
\[
 m\mathcal H_{\Gamma,K}\delta a_{m-1}.
\]
The admissible next observable jets therefore form the same affine
complemented infinite-codimension slice at every finite order.  This is an
observable-level no-go statement, not merely a restriction on an abstract
source completion.

For clarity, continuous periodic evaluation does not imply periodic
surjectivity on the Ward subdomain.  If
\(R_0\mathcal M_0a\) has a single-valued continuous representative near a
regular period-\(p\) orbit, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:correlation_graph_periodic_telescoping}
 \sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(\mathcal M_0a)(T_0^kz)=0
\end{equation}
by telescoping.  Thus a joint right inverse realizing arbitrary nonzero
source-orbit sums cannot exist on that local-Ward subdomain.  The valid
primitive-level independence statement is given in Appendix~A.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Exact observable-current benchmark with complete finite-order recovery]
\label{thm:natural_observable_current_recovery}
Let \(a_s\) be a transported \(C^J\) family of physical collision
observables such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:natural_observable_current_primitive}
 h_s:=\bigl(a_s-\langle\rho_s,a_s\rangle\bigr)\rho_s
\end{equation}
is a centered \(C^J\) section of \(\mathfrak B_U\).  Define its one-collision
observable current by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:natural_observable_current_source}
 g_s:=(I-\Ltransfer_s)h_s.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, on the smooth invertible core, \(g_s\) is the invariant-flux
density of the discrete observable increment
\(a_s-a_s\circ T_s^{-1}\).  It is nonzero whenever this increment is nonzero.

Write \(B_\ell=\partial_s^\ell\Ltransfer_s|_0\),
\(g_j=\partial_s^jg_s|_0\), and \(h_j=\partial_s^jh_s|_0\).  At every
\(1\le j\le J\), the complete assembled Poisson source is the strong identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:natural_observable_current_all_order_recovery}
 q_j:=g_j+
 \sum_{\ell=1}^j\binom j\ell B_\ell h_{j-\ell}
 =(I-\Ltransfer_0)h_j.
\end{equation}
Consequently every represented one-face and clean point-current coefficient
in \(q_j\) cancels.  In particular the moving-face gluing test and the full
second-order source \(q_2\) are satisfied, with canonical jets \(h_1,h_2\).

For every transported centered test observable \(f_s\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:natural_observable_current_susceptibility}
 \sum_{n\ge0}\langle g_s,\mathscr U_s^nf_s\rangle
 =\langle h_s,f_s\rangle,
\end{equation}
and the left side therefore has the full \(C^J\) Leibniz response of the
right side.  Applied to the nonconjugate radial-inflation family, this is a
non-invariant exact correlation-current class which passes
all finite-order represented recovery tests without eventwise all-seed (S3).

The theorem is not a prescribed-source assertion: the source is an exact
one-collision current by construction.  It supplies an explicit consistency
class, while an independently specified correlation density remains subject
to the gluing obstruction of
\cref{prop:poisson_moving_face_gluing_obstruction}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \((I-\Ltransfer_s)h_s=g_s\) exactly \(j\) times.  Moving all
terms in which a derivative hits \(\Ltransfer_s\) to the right gives
\eqref{eq:natural_observable_current_all_order_recovery}.  Its right-hand side
is a centered strong density.  The strong-recovery criterion then forces all
represented conormal and point coefficients of the complete left-hand
assembly to cancel.  For \(j=1,2\) the identity is precisely
\[
 q_1=g_1+B_1h_0=(I-\Ltransfer_0)h_1,
 \qquad
 q_2=g_2+2B_1h_1+B_2h_0=(I-\Ltransfer_0)h_2.
\]

For finite \(N\), duality gives the telescoping identity
\[
 \sum_{n=0}^{N}\langle(I-\Ltransfer_s)h_s,
      \mathscr U_s^nf_s\rangle
 =\langle h_s,f_s\rangle
  -\langle h_s,\mathscr U_s^{N+1}f_s\rangle.
\]
The last term tends to zero geometrically because \(h_s\) and \(f_s\) are
centered and the period-one strong spectral gap is uniform.  This proves
\eqref{eq:natural_observable_current_susceptibility}; differentiating the
finite strong pairing gives the asserted \(C^J\) formula.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Prescribed-source boundary.}
On the centered strong space at a fixed table, the spectral gap makes

but whether the source section \(g_s\) is specified independently of that
primitive and whether the canonical primitive varies strongly with \(s\).
For such a prescribed section, the first two necessary and sufficient tests

\begin{proposition}[Eventwise all-seed recovery already fails at first order]
\label{prop:eventwise_all_seed_recovery_obstruction}
Let \(\Gamma_s=\{g_s=0\}\) be a moving physical face separating two one-sided
branch formulae \(\mathcal A_{+,s}\) and \(\mathcal A_{-,s}\).  On the smooth
core, the complete first derivative contains the transverse current
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:first_event_jump_current}
 \dot g_0\,\bigl[
   \mathcal A_{+,0}h-\mathcal A_{-,0}h
 \bigr]_{\Gamma_0}\,\delta(g_0),
\end{equation}
up to the fixed orientation and smooth coarea factor.  If this coefficient is
nonzero on an open subarc, then the total current represented by \(B_1h\) is
not in the strong Demers--Zhang space and has no strong primitive.

Consequently, whenever \(\dot g_0\not\equiv0\) and the two branch traces are
distinct, there are smooth strong seeds \(h\) for which eventwise recovery
fails.  Thus no all-seed version of (S3) can hold for a genuinely moving
branch face.  The positive result in
\cref{thm:blockwise_second_order_invariant_recovery} works because all
insertion positions and density-jet terms are assembled before recovery is
tested.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate
\[
 H(g_s)\mathcal A_{+,s}h+
 H(-g_s)\mathcal A_{-,s}h.
\]
The two indicator derivatives give
\eqref{eq:first_event_jump_current}; all smooth interior and Jacobian
derivatives remain regular on the two sides and cannot cancel a nonzero
isolated transverse current.  Such a current is weak but not strong by
\cref{prop:demers_zhang_trace_location}.  The represented recovery criterion
\cref{prop:strong_recovery_equivalence} therefore excludes a strong
primitive.  If the two branch trace operators differ, a smooth seed supported
where their difference is nonzero makes the coefficient nonzero.  This proves
the all-seed obstruction and the final comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition*}[Cubic-safe endpoint orders and the fourth-order obstruction]
Let a local moving cutoff contribution be
\(c_s(u,y)u^3H(u-\eta_s(y))\), with \(c_s\) smooth and \(\eta_0=0\).
For parameter orders \(j\le3\), every endpoint delta derivative produced by
the cutoff is annihilated by \(u^3\).  At order four the leading endpoint term
is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fourth_order_endpoint_obstruction}
 -6\,(\dot\eta_0)^4c_0(0,y)\,\delta(u)
\end{equation}
up to the fixed orientation convention; lower terms contain only derivatives
of \(c_s\) multiplying \(u^3\delta^{(q)}\), \(q\le2\), and vanish.  Thus the
cubic flux zero verifies the cutoff endpoint part through order three, whereas
order four and above require an actual Ward identity or an additional zero of
the numerator.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}
The highest distribution in \(\partial_s^jH(u-\eta_s)\) is
\((-\dot\eta_0)^j\delta^{(j-1)}(u)\); all other endpoint terms have lower delta
order.  The identities \(u^3\delta^{(q)}=0\) for \(q=0,1,2\) prove the first
claim.  At \(j=4\), the top term is
\((\dot\eta_0)^4u^3\delta^{(3)}(u)=-6(\dot\eta_0)^4\delta(u)\).
The product rule gives the stated lower terms.  This is a complete local cutoff
calculation and makes no claim about the separate regular coefficient
\(F_j^{\rm reg}(0)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[First-order moving-singularity response]
\label{thm:moving_singularity_response}
Let \(\theta_s\) be a \(C^1\) curve in the finite-response parameter data,
let \(\rho_0\) be the invariant collision density at \(s=0\), and let \(f_s\)
be a \(C^1\) transported observable section satisfying the adjoint trace
condition in \cref{def:finite_order_response_scale}.  Let the first transfer
derivative from \cref{prop:first_transfer_derivative} be
\[
  g=\nabla_s\Ltransfer_{\theta_s}|_0\rho_0
\]
with branch/interior plus physical-trace decomposition
\[
  g=\sum_h\Ltransfer_{0,h}
  (\mathcal D_h^{\rm int}+\mathcal D_h^{\rm Jac})\rho_0
  +\sum_{\Gamma\subset\Ssing}\mathcal B_\Gamma\rho_0
\]
in the weak trace range.  Then \(I(s)=\langle\rho_s,f_s\rangle\) is
differentiable at zero and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:first_order_observable_response}
  I'(0)=
  \langle\rho_0,\nabla_s f_s|_0\rangle
  +\langle g,R_0^{\rm obs}f_0\rangle .
\end{equation}
This value equals the first-order geometric flux derivative in
\eqref{eq:direct_geometric_static_average}; the transfer expression is a
consistency representation of a static average.
The second pairing is the trace pairing supplied by the target observable
class.  In particular, the theorem does not assert
\((I-\Ltransfer_0)^{-1}g\in\mathfrak D_U\).
Nevertheless this \emph{complete invariant source} is recovered in the precise
sense
\(g=(I-\Ltransfer_0)\partial_s\rho_s|_0\) by
\cref{thm:invariant_source_ward_identity}; no such statement is made for an
isolated trace summand.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct differentiability follows already from
\cref{prop:direct_geometric_invariant_average}.  Put
\(h=\partial_s\rho_s|_0\).  The exact invariant-source Ward identity gives
\(g=(I-\Ltransfer_0)h\), with (h) centered and strong.  Hence, for
\(f_0^\circ=\Piobsperp_0f_0\), duality gives the scalar identity
\[
 \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle g,\mathscr U_0^nf_0^\circ\rangle
 =\langle h,f_0^\circ\rangle-\langle\Ltransfer_0^Nh,f_0^\circ\rangle .
\]
The ordinary Demers--Zhang strong spectral gap sends the second term to zero
geometrically.  This proves existence of the scalar functional
\(\langle g,R_0^{\rm obs}f_0\rangle\), without convergence of the partial sums
in any observable jet norm, and identifies it with
\(\langle h,f_0^\circ\rangle\).  Adding the transported-observable derivative
gives \eqref{eq:first_order_observable_response}.  Total mass of \(g\) is zero because
\(\langle1,\Ltransfer_s\rho_0\rangle=\langle1,\rho_0\rangle\), so the centering
is compatible with the cohomological equation.  Both expressions were obtained
by differentiating the same finite-time invariance identity, so their equality
with \eqref{eq:direct_geometric_static_average} follows before the
\(N\to\infty\) limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[First-order weak derivative range]
\label{def:first_order_weak_derivative_range}
Let \(\mathfrak W_U^{\rm der}\subset\mathfrak D_U^w\) be the closure of finite
sums of weak inputs generated from strong response densities by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item branch-interior, Jacobian, and roof derivatives of the homogeneous

  \item first moving-boundary trace operators defined by
  \eqref{eq:core_trace_pairing};
  \item finite linear combinations of such terms after zero-mean projection and
  finite homogeneous refinement.
\end{enumerate}
Elements of \(\mathfrak W_U^{\rm der}\) carry their terminal-child
decomposition, dyadic physical-block data, and unstable-holonomy matching data as part of
the norm.  More precisely, for a smooth-core generator \(g\) write
\(g=g_{\rm cen}+g_{\rm term}\), where \(g_{\rm cen}\) is supported on central
homogeneous descendants and \(g_{\rm term}\) on high-strip, grazing, or
multiple-impact terminal descendants.  Set
\[
  \|g\|_{\mathfrak W_U^{\rm der}}
  =
  \|g_{\rm cen}\|_{\mathfrak D_U^w}
  +\|g_{\rm term}\|_{\mathfrak W_U^{\rm term}},
\]
where the terminal seminorm is the first-order physical-block stable-leaf
testing norm with the factor \(2^{\epsilon_1m}\).  For a finite sum, take the infimum of
the sum of these generator norms over all such decompositions, and complete.
The response calculus only uses weak inputs in \(\mathfrak W_U^{\rm der}\); no
weak-to-strong smoothing is asserted for an arbitrary element of
\(\mathfrak D_U^w\).
\end{definition}


\begin{proposition}[Observable-side trace insertion]

\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Three obstructions to a naive moving-flow extension]
\label{prop:flow_obstruction_trichotomy}

\subsection{A conditional standard-family route for a prescribed source}
\label{sec:conditional_standard_family_route}

There is a natural way to seek a genuinely positive result without forcing

what standard-family coupling actually proves from the additional two-time
estimate needed by a correlation susceptibility.

Choose a regular periodic orbit
\(\mathcal O_*=\{z_0,\ldots,z_{p-1}\}\), disjoint from the singular set, and
a compact regular collision rectangle containing \(z_0\) but no other point
of the orbit.  There is \(\chi\in C_c^r\), \(0\le\chi\le1\), with
\(\chi(z_0)=1\), \(\chi(z_j)=0\) for \(j\ne0\), and
\(p\langle\rho_0,\chi\rangle<1/2\).  Transport this already chosen physical
observable by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prescribed_local_bump_source}
 a_s=\chi\circ\Phi_s^{-1},\qquad
 g_s=(a_s-\langle\rho_s,a_s\rangle)\rho_s.
\end{equation}
This definition is made before a Poisson primitive is selected.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prescribed_bump_periodic_certificate}
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal O_*}
 \bigl(a_0(z)-\langle\rho_0,a_0\rangle\bigr)
 =1-p\langle\rho_0,\chi\rangle>\frac12.
\end{equation}
Thus the centered observable in \eqref{eq:prescribed_local_bump_source} is not
a continuous dynamical coboundary.  For the radial family, nonconjugacy is
independently certified by the varying period-two multiplier in
\cref{thm:radial_inflation_second_order_recovery}.

For a finite dynamic core element \(h\), write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:complete_first_defect_profile}
 \mathcal D_Vh
 :=\mathop{\mathrm{w}^*\!\!-\!\lim}_{s\to0}
 \frac{(\Ltransfer_s-\Ltransfer_0)h}{s},
\end{equation}
where the right-hand side is the \emph{complete} first current: regular
branch terms and all same-label physical faces are assembled before the
limit, and the grazing blocks are summed as in
\cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum}.  Set
\(h_0=R_0g_0=\sum_{m\ge0}\Ltransfer_0^mg_0\).  The exact missing estimate is
the following two-time profile certificate.  There must exist
\(C<\infty\) and \(\tau,\vartheta\in(0,1)\) such that, for small \(s\),
all \(m,n\ge0\), and every transported \(C^1\) test \(f_s\),
\begin{align}
\label{eq:two_time_conditional_mixing_certificate}
 \left|
 \left\langle
 \frac{(\Ltransfer_s-\Ltransfer_0)\Ltransfer_0^mg_0}{s},
 \mathscr U_s^nf_s
 \right\rangle\right|
 &\le C\tau^m\vartheta^n\|f_s\|_{C^1},\\
 \frac{(\Ltransfer_s-\Ltransfer_0)\Ltransfer_0^mg_0}{s}
 &\xrightarrow[s\to0]{\mathrm{w}^*}
 \mathcal D_V(\Ltransfer_0^mg_0).
\notag
\end{align}
The first line includes the nonlinear difference-quotient domination needed
to exchange the parameter limit with both time sums; a bound only for the
limiting current is not enough.

\begin{proposition*}[Conditional prescribed-source response and its exact open input]
Assume \eqref{eq:two_time_conditional_mixing_certificate}.  Assume also that
\((g_s-g_0)/s\to\dot g_0\) in the centered strong space with the corresponding
uniform geometric correlation bound, and that
\((f_s-f_0)/s\to\dot f_0\) in the observable test norm.  Then the independently
prescribed source \eqref{eq:prescribed_local_bump_source} has a differentiable
infinite susceptibility at \(s=0\), and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:conditional_prescribed_source_response_formula}
 \left.\frac d{ds}\right|_{0}\Xi_s(g_s,f_s)
 =\sum_{n\ge0}\langle\dot g_0,\mathscr U_0^nf_0\rangle
  +\sum_{m,n\ge0}
   \mathcal D_V(\Ltransfer_0^mg_0)(\mathscr U_0^nf_0)
  +\langle h_0,\dot f_0\rangle .
\end{equation}
Both series in \eqref{eq:conditional_prescribed_source_response_formula}
converge absolutely.  The source is nonzero, fixed before \(h_0\), and has the
non-coboundary certificate \eqref{eq:prescribed_bump_periodic_certificate}.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}
For a finite correlation sum use the exact telescoping identity
\[
 \Ltransfer_s^k-\Ltransfer_0^k
 =\sum_{m+n=k-1}
   \Ltransfer_s^n(\Ltransfer_s-\Ltransfer_0)
   \Ltransfer_0^m .
\]
After pairing with \(f_s\), the middle term is precisely the first line of
\eqref{eq:two_time_conditional_mixing_certificate}.  Its double geometric
majorant permits first passage to the infinite time sum and then dominated
convergence as \(s\to0\).  The strong spectral gap treats the source
difference quotient.  Uniform convergence of the fixed-table primitive gives
the final term \(\langle h_0,\dot f_0\rangle\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:conditional_prescribed_source_response_formula}.  The periodic
certificate was proved in \eqref{eq:prescribed_bump_periodic_certificate},
since every continuous coboundary has zero sum on a periodic orbit.
\end{proof}

The one-time part of this program is accessible to standard-family coupling.
If the positive and negative Jordan parts of one complete zero-mass profile,
after a fixed number of iterates, are regular measured unstable families with
finite boundary quantity \(Z\), then the coupling theorem of
\cite{StenlundYoungZhang2013} gives exponential decay in the future index
\(n\).  The small-hole construction of \cite{Canestrari2026SmallHoles}
verifies exactly this geometry for the image of a vertical line: its density
contains both the incoming and outgoing cosine factors, and these factors
make the double homogeneity-strip sum defining \(Z\) finite.  The same
argument applies to a signed zero-mass profile after normalizing its two
Jordan parts, provided both parts have the same uniform regular-family data.

This does \emph{not} prove
\eqref{eq:two_time_conditional_mixing_certificate}.  First, the radial
physical trace in the present paper is a terminal one-sided trace whose image
lies on the grazing seam; the proved dyadic estimate
\eqref{eq:renormalized_grazing_block_bound} is an adjoint trace estimate and
is not a representation by interior unstable curves with uniformly finite
\(Z\).  Second, even if that first geometric problem were solved, ordinary
standard-family memory loss supplies the factor \(\vartheta^n\), but no decay
in the past index \(m\).  The factor \(\tau^m\) asks for conditional mixing
of a singular slice weighted by a past observable.  This is the specialized
two-time hypothesis in
\cite{Wormell2024PiecewiseResponse}; it remains conjectural there even for
the Lozi singular line.  Neither the fixed-table Demers--Zhang spectral gap
nor the graph-domain definition implies it, because transverse trace is not
continuous in the bare strong norm.

Finally, the proposition is deliberately first order.  A second parameter
difference produces normal derivatives \(\delta'(G)\) and, at clean
two-face intersections, point atoms.  These are not finite signed measures on
unstable curves and hence are outside classical standard-family coupling.
Before a positive \(q_2\) theorem can be claimed, their coefficients must be
shown to cancel in the complete prescribed assembly, or a new mixing theory
for distributional curve derivatives and point profiles must be proved.
Thus twisting the operator or differentiating Green--Kubo does not bypass the
obstruction: the mixed shape--twist derivative contains the same double sum
\eqref{eq:two_time_conditional_mixing_certificate}, while its second shape
derivative contains the same \(q_2\) profiles.

\subsection{Exported interface with homogenization}
\label{sec:exported_interface}

The response package is meant to be used as a theorem interface rather than as
an informal regularity assumption.  A later argument may invoke
\cref{thm:main_response_package} only for the stated actual static,
source-specific radial, and exact-current conclusions.  The general
finite-order conormal implication, including differentiated map
susceptibilities for a certified recovered tree, is instead
\cref{thm:certified_abstract_conormal_calculus} and requires its displayed
R/S certificates through the chosen \(J\).  A later argument may
invoke a moving-family suspension response only after separately constructing
the common weighted graph domains and negative-order symbol estimates
identified after \cref{prop:flow_obstruction_trichotomy}; no such theorem is
exported here.  The
source-side scalar formulation is part
of the exported interface: downstream arguments may pair conormal sources with
the stated source/test levels, but may not replace them by an unproved
strong density resolvent.

In this separation, the present paper supplies a conormal source framework, a
source-specific nonconjugate block-recovery theorem through order two, and a
conditional scalar interface.  The fixed-atlas first radial calculation is
only a graph-norm remark; the paper does not supply a full
higher-order label-resolved all-branch
recovery theorem for a general dynamical response tree.  A downstream
macroscopic equation may use the certified interface, but
is not part of the present argument.

\section{Conclusion}

The actual collision-map results and the certified abstract calculus are now
separate.  The source module contains ordinary weak terms, one-sided face
currents, central point atoms, terminal symbols, and canonical same-label
dyadic block generators.  The latter make physical regrouping an identity in
the quotient norm, not merely after the scalar labels have been forgotten.
Under the finite Ward and triangular recovery certificates, this module gives
the finite-order scalar calculus of
\cref{thm:certified_abstract_conormal_calculus}; those certificates are not
claimed for a generic moving table.
The topology is likewise separated: the centered gap, Neumann primitive,
strong remainder, and base reset live on the Demers--Zhang dynamical strong
bundle, whereas the positive flux-seed response scale carries no asserted
transfer invariance.  Moving-face traces live on the separately constructed
\(L^\infty\)-anchored, dynamically cut, branch-cocycle-weighted primitive
domain; no transverse trace is inferred from the bare Demers--Zhang norm, and
no transfer invariance of the primitive domain is claimed.

For the nonconjugate radial inflation, the complete invariant assembly
recovers through order two, including arbitrary fixed positive waiting
lengths and an R3-free assembly-first reset obtained directly from the
post-assembly \(L^\infty\) tail.  A fixed-atlas block-flux graph seminorm
records the expected first-letter decay, but it is not a chart-independent
operator theorem and is not part of (S1).
This is sharp in
order: the isolated second letter contains the nonintegrable interior
coefficient \(u^{-3}\), so every higher radial theorem must concern a
specified complete assembly.  The exact observable-current class gives an
all-order telescoping benchmark.  A separately prescribed observable
correlation density under common rigid translation has a \(C^J\)
susceptibility in the exact conjugacy trivialization; its Poisson sources
vanish because the branch partition is stationary, so it is only a consistency
example and not a nonconjugate radial result.  The radial table-velocity current is a
nonzero differentiated-invariance benchmark and yields an infinite
susceptibility.  Its total-current \(q_1,q_2\) identities do not establish
label-resolved reset or the strong Taylor remainder; a generic local
correlation source therefore remains open.  On the weighted primitive domain,
the split-surjective moving-face
gluing map shows that passing sources occupy a complemented
infinite-codimension subspace.  The same conclusion first holds on the
Poisson-independent local-potential class.  The induced defect map on the
pullback correlation graph class has the same property, with open dense
complement only in that explicit graph topology; no genericity claim is made
on all branchwise smooth observables.  After
passing it, the localized \(q_2\)
condition imposes a second affine infinite-codimension constraint on the first
observable jet, and the cascade repeats at every finite order.
The obstruction survives restriction to every finite-group isotypic
space on a free orbit of moving faces; parity can force vanishing only through
an additional pointwise stabilizer identity.  No finite physical torus
isometry provides such an identity on an open regular grazing-face subarc.
A continuous local Ward primitive would additionally force zero source sum on
every regular periodic orbit.  These orbit functionals have unbounded finite
rank on the ambient observable class; an invariant local charge is constant,
while its noninvariant one-step increment is exactly the already identified
exact-current class.  At primitive level, the face profile and every finite
vector of primitive-orbit values are jointly split.  Source-orbit sums are not
an independent direction once a single-valued local primitive is imposed:
they vanish by telescoping.  On any finite strongly connected
regular itinerary subsystem, a finite-memory label rule satisfying all of its
periodic sums is already a cylinder coboundary and hence another exact-current
benchmark.

The remaining nonconjugate collision problem is therefore precise: prove that
an independently prescribed physical local source belongs to the
resolvent-trace graph domain and verify its strong recovery, with a pointwise
Ward identity
on every active face and at every source-jet order.  Neither finite symmetry
nor generic coefficient choice supplies that identity, and a hidden local
primitive must also satisfy every periodic-orbit Ward sum.  Uniform
intrinsic historical terminal closure is not a target;
a nonzero time-labelled terminal germ accumulates linearly.  Physical infinite
response instead uses the assembly-first scalar theorem with summable
primitives and reset errors.

Continuous time is retained only as fixed-table input plus obstruction, not as
a moving-family criterion or theorem.  A generic
parameter letter costs one transverse level and one \(u^{-2}\) grazing level,
while the pointwise weight \(u^2\) is not comparable at consecutive
collisions.  A future proof must construct a dynamical branch/preimage-weighted
corrected BDL completion, prove its weighted Lasota--Yorke and common-resolvent
bounds, cancel the zero-waiting endpoint Fourier polynomial, and establish the
regular \(W^{p,1}\) time-kernel estimates.  The present flow statement records
only the obstructions to the simpler shortcuts.

\begin{proposition*}[Finite-group isotypic extension]
Let a finite group \(H\) act boundedly on
\(\mathfrak G_{\mathscr F}\), commute with
\(\Ltransfer_0\), and carry the branch data covariantly.  If the face subarcs
\(\{\gamma I:\gamma\in H\}\) and their tubular extensions are pairwise
disjoint, then for every irreducible complex representation \(\pi\) of \(H\)
the localized gluing map restricted to the \(\pi\)-isotypic source space is
split surjective onto \(\mathcal T_K^r(I)\).  The corresponding statement
holds on each associated real isotypic component.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}
Denote the complexified action by \(\mathcal U_\gamma\).  For an irreducible
representation \(\pi\), of dimension \(d_\pi\) and character \(\chi_\pi\), set
\[
 \mathcal P_\pi
 :=\frac{d_\pi}{|H|}
   \sum_{\gamma\in H}\chi_\pi(\gamma^{-1})\mathcal U_\gamma
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:isotypic_gluing_right_inverse}
 \mathcal R_{\Gamma,K}^{\pi}\phi
 :=\frac{|H|}{d_\pi^2}\,
   \mathcal P_\pi\mathcal R_{\Gamma,K}\phi .
\end{equation}
The central-idempotent formula places the range in the \(\pi\)-isotypic
component.  When the defect is read on \(I\), covariance and disjointness make
every nonidentity summand vanish.  Since \(\chi_\pi(e)=d_\pi\), the displayed
normalization gives
\[
 \mathcal G_{\Gamma,I}\mathcal R_{\Gamma,K}^{\pi}\phi=\phi.
\]
The finite sum is bounded.  For a real irreducible type use its real central
idempotent.  For complex type combine the conjugate pair of complex
idempotents and normalize their common identity contribution; for quaternionic
type the complex central idempotent is conjugation invariant.  Taking the real
part of the resulting operator preserves the real identity-face profile and
gives the bounded right inverse on the associated real isotypic component.
\end{proof}

\section{Source-Module Construction and Conditional Abstract Calculus}

The base quotient construction in the first part of this appendix supplies
the notation used by the scalar reset theorem.  The later assumptions

\begin{definition}[Response Banach scale]
\label{def:finite_order_response_scale}
Let \(\mathcal C_{\rm hom}^r(U)\) be the transported branchwise smooth core.
For \(0\le m\le J+3\), define \(\mathfrak D_U^{(m)}\) as the completion of this
core for the independent flux-seed norm of order \(m+2\):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finite_order_density_scale_norm}
  \|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(m)}}
  =
  \mathcal N_{m+2}^{\rm flux}(\rho).
\end{equation}
Thus \(\mathfrak D_U^{(1)}=\mathfrak D_U\).  Conormal letters are defined on
the core by the resolved indicator calculus in
\cref{thm:second_multiple_trace}, combined into physical dyadic blocks, and
bounded on every finite decorated core by
\cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum}; their completed countable-atlas
extension is the independent mapping statement (S1), not a consequence of the
definition.  Their bounds are not inserted in
\eqref{eq:finite_order_density_scale_norm}.  The weak density scale
\(\mathfrak D_U^{w,(m)}\) uses the corresponding Demers--Zhang stable-leaf
tests and one fewer unstable comparison.  It contains the regular and
transverse one-face outputs for which those tests are defined; it is not
enlarged by fiat to contain codimension-two point masses.
At base source level only, define
\(\mathfrak D_U^{w,(0)}\) as the Banach sum, inside the common distribution
space, of the transported Demers--Zhang weak space and the base flux-response
weak completion, with the usual infimum norm.  Consequently
\(\mathfrak B_U\hookrightarrow\mathfrak D_U^{w,(0)}\) continuously.  This
base sum is used solely for the regular coordinate after physical assembly;
it supplies neither positive flux derivatives nor transfer invariance at any
higher response level.

For a branchwise smooth observable define the independent one-sided jet/
holonomy seminorm
\[
 \mathcal H_m(f)
 =\sup_{\Gamma}\max_{0\le\ell\le m}
   \|\partial_{n_\Gamma}^{\ell}f|_{\Gamma^\pm}\|_{C^\alpha}
 +\sup_{(W_1,W_2,\mathfrak h^u)}
   \frac{\|J_mf|_{W_1}-(J_mf|_{W_2})\circ\mathfrak h^u\|_{C^0}}
        {d(W_1,W_2)^\beta},
\]
where \(J_mf\) is the vector of one-sided normal/tangential jets of order at
most \(m\), \(\Gamma\) ranges over the labelled physical faces of the clean
stratification, and \(\mathfrak h^u\) is the local unstable holonomy between
matched stable test curves.  The adjoint trace scale
\(\mathfrak A_U^{(m)}\) is the completion of physical branchwise \(C^r\)
observables for the independent norm
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finite_order_observable_scale_norm}
  \|f\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(m)}}
  =
  \|f\|_{\Bcal^\#}
  +\|f\|_{\mathcal C_{\rm tr}^{m}}
  +\mathcal H_m(f).
\end{equation}
The norm contains no supremum over derivative-generated sources.  Pairing with
such sources is a theorem consequence of the physical block estimate.  The embeddings are
\[
  \mathfrak D_U^{(m+1)}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak D_U^{(m)}
  \hookrightarrow\mathfrak D_U^{w,(m)},\qquad
  \mathfrak A_U^{(m+1)}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak A_U^{(m)}.
\]
No embedding \(\mathfrak D_U^{w,(m)}\hookrightarrow
\mathfrak D_U^{(m)}\) is postulated.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ambient decorated conormal source scale]
\label{def:ambient_conormal_source_scale}
Let \(\mathscr G_m^{\rm at}\) be the countable set of \emph{atomic}
decorated supports in the fixed one-step response atlas.  An index
\(\gamma\in\mathscr G_m^{\rm at}\) records the
homogeneous chart, dyadic block, physical face or central clean face pair,
one-sided incident branch \(\omega\), and derivative order.  There are three
types:
\[
 \gamma={\rm reg},\qquad
 \gamma=(\Gamma,\omega,\ell),\qquad
 \gamma=(z,\omega,\alpha).
\]
Here \(\Gamma\) is a one-face germ; by
\eqref{eq:no_boundary_pair_birth}, every clean pair point \(z\) lies in the
central region \(u\ge u_J\).  A regular decorated point is retained only when
it is the one-sided image of such a central point.  Nonextending grazing and
multiple-impact germs carry a terminal symbol and no fictitious map value.

There is one additional physically canonical presentation.  For a physical
face family \(\Gamma\), an incident label \(\omega\), and a dyadic block
\(b\), put
\[
 \widehat\Gamma_{\Gamma,\omega,b}
 =\bigcup_{2^b\le k<2^{b+1}}\Gamma_{k,\omega},
\]
where the union is taken only across artificial homogeneity cuts, after the
two artificial-face orientations have cancelled.  Let
\(\widehat{\mathscr G}_0\) contain the grouped indices
\(\widehat\gamma=(\widehat\Gamma_{\Gamma,\omega,b},\omega,\ell)\) for which
the coefficient jets have matching one-sided traces at every artificial cut.
The coefficient space \(E_{\widehat\gamma,0}\) is the completion of those
matching tuples for the physical block-variation norm: the \(L^1\) norms and
total variations of the concatenated rescaled coefficient jets on
\(\widehat\Gamma_{\Gamma,\omega,b}\).  Its current map
\(q_{\widehat\gamma,0}\) integrates the concatenated coefficient against the
one-sided trace jet on the whole physical block.  A common refinement merely
subdivides the same variation measure, so this grouped coefficient space does
not depend on the artificial subdivision.  At level \(m\), the same notation
\(E_{\widehat\gamma,m}\) uses concatenated jets through order \(m-\ell\).

At a higher source level \(m>0\), a grouped index is admitted to
\(\widehat{\mathscr G}_m\) only when its actual one-sided chain-rule image has
a finite weighted column sum into level \(m-1\).  A block whose later image
undergoes an uncontrolled countable splitting is therefore available at base
level but is not declared to be a propagating higher-level generator.  Set
\[
 \mathscr G_m=\mathscr G_m^{\rm at}\sqcup
 \widehat{\mathscr G}_m.
\]
This makes the one-event regrouping below an element of the Banach module,
while leaving recursive regrouping as the separate content of (S3).

Let \(E_{{\rm reg},m}=\mathfrak D_U^{w,(m)}\), let
\(E_{\Gamma,\omega,\ell}=C^{m-\ell}(\Gamma,\omega)\), and let
\(E_{z,\omega,\alpha}=\mathbb C\).  The canonical current map
\(q_{\gamma,m}:E_{\gamma,m}\to(\mathfrak A_U^{(m+2)})^*\) is respectively the
ordinary weak pairing, the one-sided conormal derivative, or the decorated
point derivative; for \(\gamma=\widehat\gamma\) it is the grouped physical
block current just defined.  Write
\(A_{\eta\gamma}^{(m)}:E_{\gamma,m+1}\to E_{\eta,m}\) for the finite
one-sided chain-rule coefficient from \(\gamma\) to an ordinary or terminal
output index \(\eta\).  For \(\gamma={\rm reg}\), this notation means the
cited one-step Demers--Zhang weak operator; for a nonextending germ the only
nonzero coefficient is its terminal copy.  Each atomic nonregular index
already contains its one-sided target-strip refinement, so its local
chain-rule column has finitely many outputs.  For an admitted grouped index
the column is its actual, possibly countable, physical image column and is
retained at level (m+1) only when the weighted sum in
\eqref{eq:ambient_recursive_propagation_weight} is finite.  Summation over the
full countable family of input columns is not used to prove (S1); that
all-branch estimate remains the separate response-scale certificate.

Choose weights recursively.  At level zero, for both atomic and grouped
indices, put
\[
 w_{\gamma,0}
 =2^{\epsilon_*b(\gamma)}\max\{1,\|q_{\gamma,0}\|\},
\]
and give a terminal index the same base weight with \(q_{\gamma,0}=0\).
Terminal weights at higher levels are obtained by the same rule, with the
identity copy as their only coefficient.  After the ordinary and terminal
weights at level \(m\) have been fixed, put
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ambient_recursive_propagation_weight}
 w_{\gamma,m+1}
 =2^{\epsilon_*b(\gamma)}\max\{1,\|q_{\gamma,m+1}\|\}
  +\sum_{\eta}w_{\eta,m}\|A_{\eta\gamma}^{(m)}\|.
\end{equation}
Give \(E_{\gamma,m}\) the weighted norm
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ambient_local_coefficient_weight}
 \|e\|_{\gamma,m}^{\rm src}
 :=w_{\gamma,m}\|e\|_{E_{\gamma,m}},
\end{equation}
where \(b(\gamma)\) is the dyadic grazing-block index (zero for central point
indices).  The norm of \(q_{\gamma,m}\) includes the physical coarea Jacobian
and the one-sided coordinate-change matrix.  It is finite by the central
determinant bound and the trace-test norm.  By construction,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ambient_propagation_column_bound}
 \sum_\eta w_{\eta,m}\|A_{\eta\gamma}^{(m)}\|
 \le w_{\gamma,m+1}.
\end{equation}

Form the Banach \(\ell^1\)-sum over both presentations,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ambient_l1_source_sum}
 \mathbb E_m
 =E_{{\rm reg},m}\oplus_1
   \bigoplus_{\gamma\in\mathscr G_m\setminus\{{\rm reg}\}}^{\ell^1}
     E_{\gamma,m},
 \qquad e=(e_\gamma).
\end{equation}
Let \(\Omega_m\) be the countable set of one-sided branch labels, augmented by
the regular label, and let \(\mathscr G_m^{\rm term}\) be the terminal
indices.  Put
\[
 \mathfrak T_U^{(m)}
 =\bigoplus_{\tau\in\mathscr G_m^{\rm term}}^{\ell^1}E_{\tau,m}
\]
with the weights \eqref{eq:ambient_local_coefficient_weight}.  Define the label-resolved realization space
\[
 \mathbb Y_m
 =
 \bigoplus_{\omega\in\Omega_m}^{\ell^1}
   (\mathfrak A_U^{(m+2)})^*
 \oplus_1\mathfrak T_U^{(m)}
\]
and the joint realization map
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ambient_joint_realization_map}
 \mathbf Q_m e
 =
 \left(
  \left(\sum_{\substack{\gamma:\,\omega(\gamma)=\omega\\
                         \gamma\notin\mathscr G_m^{\rm term}}}
       q_{\gamma,m}e_\gamma\right)_{\omega\in\Omega_m},
  (e_\tau)_{\tau\in\mathscr G_m^{\rm term}}
 \right).
\end{equation}
Each series converges absolutely.  Retaining the branch label is essential:
two currents which cancel only after summing different one-sided branches can
have different future images and are not identified in the dynamical source
module.  Terminal coefficients are likewise not sent to zero.  Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ambient_conormal_source_norm}
 \mathfrak X_U^{(m)}=\mathbb E_m/\ker \mathbf Q_m,
 \qquad
 \|[e]\|_{\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}}
   =\inf_{k\in\ker \mathbf Q_m}\|e+k\|_{\mathbb E_m}.
\end{equation}
Since \(\mathbf Q_m\) is continuous into the Hausdorff Banach space
\(\mathbb Y_m\), its kernel is closed; hence \(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\) is a
Banach space.  The quotient removes representation ambiguity within each
one-sided label without discarding dynamically relevant cross-label or
terminal data.  In particular, if a matching atomic strip tuple and its
grouped block coefficient represent the same physical current, then
\[
 \big((c_{\Gamma_{k,\omega}})_k,
       -\widehat c_{\Gamma,\omega,b}\big)
 \in\ker\mathbf Q_m.
\]
Thus it is the within-label quotient, rather than the scalar total-current
map, which identifies the two presentations.  We use the same symbol
\(\mathbf Q_m\) for the induced
injective realization of the quotient in \(\mathbb Y_m\).  The bounded total-current map
\[
 \mathsf S_m:\mathbb Y_m\longrightarrow
   (\mathfrak A_U^{(m+2)})^*,\qquad
 \mathsf S_m((y_\omega)_\omega,t)=\sum_\omega y_\omega,
\]
forgets the terminal coordinate only at the final scalar pairing.
The canonical regular embedding is
\[
 \iota_{\rm reg}:\mathfrak D_U^{w,(m)}
 \longrightarrow\mathfrak X_U^{(m)},\qquad
 \iota_{\rm reg}g=[(g,0,0,\ldots)].
\]
Call a face-preserving chart change \emph{admissible} when its
triangular jet matrices and their inverses satisfy the two weighted column-sum
bounds obtained from \eqref{eq:ambient_propagation_column_bound}.  The
uniform \(C^{J+2}\) rescaled physical chart changes and their common
homogeneous refinements are admissible: their triangular jet matrices and
inverses are uniformly bounded, the central coarea determinants stay bounded
below, and the dyadic indices change by at most a fixed amount.  On grouped
indices a common refinement is an isometry of the physical block-variation
measure before the uniformly bounded triangular jet change.  The induced maps on the two coefficient
\(\ell^1\)-sums are then bounded inverses, preserve every labelled current and
terminal symbol, and descend to equivalent quotient norms.  Thus the construction is
independent, up to equivalent norm, of the admissible response atlas and is not a supremum over
derivative-generated sources.  Write \(\mathfrak X_{U,h}^{(m)}\) for the closed
quotient image of coefficients carrying the one-sided branch label \(h\), and
write
\(\pi_{\rm term}:\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\to\mathfrak T_U^{(m)}\)
for the bounded terminal-coordinate projection induced by \(\mathbf Q_m\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Point atoms require the ambient source scale]
\label{prop:point_atoms_ambient_not_dz_weak}
On the finite simple atlas:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item the pairing extends continuously as
  \[
    \mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\times\mathfrak A_U^{(m+2)}\longrightarrow\mathbb C;
  \]
  \item a nonzero codimension-two point atom, and a fortiori any of its
  derivatives, is not in the ordinary Demers--Zhang weak stable-leaf
  completion;
  \item each central decorated one-step branch on which the one-sided billiard
  map extends \(C^r\) has a bounded pushforward
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ambient_central_pushforward}
    \mathscr P_{h*}:\mathfrak X_{U,h}^{(m+1)}
       \longrightarrow\mathfrak X_U^{(m)},
  \end{equation}
  The direct sum over the one-step central branches is bounded by
  \eqref{eq:ambient_propagation_column_bound}.  A terminal germ is recorded in
  the component detected by \(\pi_{\rm term}\) and is not assigned a further
  image.  Iteration along a
  fixed regular decorated itinerary is therefore bounded with one source-level
  loss per step, but no decay or global \(\mathfrak X\)-semigroup is asserted;
  \item ordinary density mixing does not imply decay of centered point-source
  correlations.  If \(z\) is a regular periodic point and
  \(G_z=\delta_{z,\omega}-\rho_\theta\), then a smooth centered \(f\) can be
  chosen so that \(\langle G_z,\mathscr U_\theta^{nq}f\rangle\) stays nonzero
  along the period \(q\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently a scalar resolvent containing point atoms requires an independently
verified recovered/cancellation property; it cannot be deduced from the
Demers--Zhang strong spectral gap.  Moreover, no recovered class satisfying
(S2) can contain every centered point atom, and (S3) cannot hold uniformly on
the whole point-current sector of \(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The definition of the local weights makes every \(q_{\gamma,m}\) contractive
up to the fixed atlas constant.  Absolute convergence in the \(\ell^1\)-sum
and composition with the total-current map \(\mathsf S_m\) prove (i),
including independence of representative.  Terminal coordinates do not enter
this scalar pairing.  For (ii), let
\(\delta_{z,\varepsilon}\) be a two-dimensional mollification of a point mass.
Choose an admissible stable test leaf in one incident component crossing the
\(O(\varepsilon)\) ball about \(z\).  Then, for a test equal to one there,
\[
 \int_W\delta_{z,\varepsilon}\,dm_W\asymp\varepsilon^{-1}.
\]
Hence the defining weak stable-leaf norm diverges; derivatives diverge faster.
For (iii), a propagating decorated branch is a \(C^r\) diffeomorphism on its
one-sided germ.  The distributional chain rule sends face jets to face jets and
point jets to point jets, with a loss of one test-jet level and coefficient
matrices \(A_{\eta\gamma}^{(m)}\).  Therefore
\[
 \sum_\eta w_{\eta,m}
 \Big\|\sum_\gamma A_{\eta\gamma}^{(m)}e_\gamma\Big\|
 \le
 \sum_\gamma\sum_\eta
 w_{\eta,m}\|A_{\eta\gamma}^{(m)}\|\,\|e_\gamma\|
 \le\sum_\gamma w_{\gamma,m+1}\|e_\gamma\|.
\]
This proves the direct one-step bound before passing to the quotient.
Let \(\mathbb A_m\) denote this coefficient map.  On an extending labelled
germ, the distributional chain rule is an intertwining identity between
\(\mathbb A_m\) and pushforward of the labelled current; on a nonextending
germ it intertwines with the identity embedding into the corresponding
terminal coordinate.  Hence there is a bounded label-resolved map
\(\mathbf P_m\) on the realized range such that
\[
 \mathbf Q_m\mathbb A_m=\mathbf P_m\mathbf Q_{m+1}.
\]
In particular
\(\mathbb A_m(\ker\mathbf Q_{m+1})\subset\ker\mathbf Q_m\), which is the
kernel-invariance needed to descend to the quotient.  This proves
\eqref{eq:ambient_central_pushforward} without inventing a billiard value on
the singularity.  Finally choose a regular periodic orbit disjoint from
the singular set and a smooth \(f\) supported near one orbit point, then subtract
its invariant mean.  Evaluation on every \(q\)-th iterate proves (iv).
If \(G_z=(I-\Ltransfer_\theta)h\) for a centered
\(h\in\mathfrak D_U^{(m+1)}\), the ordinary strong spectral gap and duality
would force
\(\langle G_z,\mathscr U_\theta^nf\rangle\to0\) exponentially for every
centered smooth \(f\), contradicting (iv).  Hence \(G_z\) has no strong
primitive of the form required in (S2), which proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scalar physical tails cannot imply all-branch (S1)]
\label{prop:scalar_tail_not_all_branch_s1}
The total-current map
\(\mathsf S_m:\mathbb Y_m\to(\mathfrak A_U^{(m+2)})^*\) does not control the
label-resolved source norm.  More precisely, suppose a physical face has two
incident labels \(\omega_+\ne\omega_-\) and, on infinitely many disjoint
dyadic blocks \(b\), the two labelled realization ranges contain the same
nonzero current \(v_b\).  For every finite interval \(M\le b\le K\), the
labelled element
\[
 y_{M,K}
 =
 \sum_{b=M}^{K}
 \bigl(v_b\,\mathbf e_{\omega_+}
       -v_b\,\mathbf e_{\omega_-}\bigr)
\]
satisfies \(\mathsf S_my_{M,K}=0\), although after normalizing
\(\|v_b\|=1\),
\[
 \|y_{M,K}\|_{\mathbb Y_m}=2(K-M+1).
\]
Likewise, \(\mathsf S_m\) is identically zero on the retained terminal
coordinate.  Hence even a zero scalar physical tail gives no Cauchy estimate
in \(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\).

Consequently the scalar \(2^{-b}\) estimate can prove neither the
countable-atlas derivative map nor terminal-module closure.  A sufficient
all-branch input must instead be vector valued, for example a bound of the
form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:necessary_vector_valued_s1_target}
 \sum_{\gamma:\,b(\gamma)=b}
 w_{\gamma,m}
 \|c_\gamma(B_{j,\theta}\rho)\|_{E_{\gamma,m}}
 \le C2^{-\delta b}\|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(m+j+2)}}
\end{equation}
after the admissible within-label quotient minimization, with
\(\delta>0\).  Establishing such a bound for the actual derivative
coefficients is the unresolved analytic content of (S1).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two label components are distinct coordinates in the
\(\ell^1\)-sum \(\mathbb Y_m\), so their norms add even though their images
under \(\mathsf S_m\) cancel.  Finite partial sums therefore have the displayed
norm and are not Cauchy as \(K\to\infty\), whereas every total-current pairing
is zero.  The same conclusion for terminal data is immediate from the
definition of \(\mathsf S_m\).  Since the induced realization
\(\mathbf Q_m:\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\to\mathbb Y_m\) is continuous, an
\(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\)-Cauchy estimate would imply a
\(\mathbb Y_m\)-Cauchy estimate, contradicting this construction.  Summing
\eqref{eq:necessary_vector_valued_s1_target} over \(b\), on the other hand,
does give an absolutely convergent coefficient lift and hence the desired
source-module bound.  This proves both claims.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition*}[Dyadic entropy threshold for all-branch (S1)]
For one response level, let \(M_b\) be the number of nonzero labelled
coefficient columns in grazing block \(b\), let
\(W_b=\max_{\gamma:b(\gamma)=b}w_{\gamma,m}\), and suppose an actual
derivative source has the within-label minimized coefficient bound
\[
 \|c_\gamma(B_j\rho)\|_{E_{\gamma,m}}
 \le A_b\|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(m+j+2)}}.
\]
Then the block contribution to (S1) is at most \(M_bW_bA_b\).  In
particular, if
\[
 M_b\le C2^{\kappa b},\qquad
 W_b\le C2^{\mu b},\qquad
 A_b\le C2^{-\sigma b},
\]
the vector-valued completion follows whenever
\(\sigma>\kappa+\mu\), with
\(\delta=\sigma-\kappa-\mu\) in
\eqref{eq:necessary_vector_valued_s1_target}.

For the standard quadratic homogeneity strips, one dyadic block
\(2^b\le k<2^{b+1}\) contains \(\asymp2^b\) strip indices before any
within-label regrouping.  Thus a proof based on separate strip columns has
\(\kappa=1\).  The scalar assembled \(2^{-b}\) bound alone is at the
borderline before the positive source weight \(\mu\) is even paid and cannot
verify (S1).  A radial-inflation proof must therefore obtain either
additional coefficient decay, or an explicit within-label physical
regrouping that lowers the effective column entropy.  This is the exact
quantitative alternative left by (S1), rather than an unspecified
``all-branch constant.''
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the triangle inequality in the weighted
\(\ell^1\)-sum after minimizing within each label.  The three displayed
dyadic estimates give
\[
 \sum_{\gamma:b(\gamma)=b}w_{\gamma,m}
 \|c_\gamma(B_j\rho)\|
 \le C2^{-(\sigma-\kappa-\mu)b}\|\rho\|.
\]
The number of integers in
\([2^b,2^{b+1})\) is comparable with \(2^b\), which proves the quadratic-strip
statement.  No lower bound on an actual billiard coefficient is asserted;
the proposition identifies the decay or regrouping that a positive proof
must supply.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Within-label physical regrouping removes the artificial strip entropy]
\label{prop:physical_face_regrouping_removes_strip_entropy}
Fix one response event and one of the finitely many physical one-step face
families supplied by (R1)--(R2).  For a face \(\Gamma\), an incident label
\(\omega\), and a dyadic block \(b\), let
\(\{\Gamma_{k,\omega}:2^b\le k<2^{b+1}\}\) be the restrictions produced only
by the artificial homogeneity-strip subdivision.  Suppose that, after the
artificial-face partition-of-unity terms have been cancelled, the one-sided
physical coefficients have matching traces on every common artificial cut.
Then they concatenate, in the distributional sense, to one labelled block
current
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:blockwise_physical_face_regrouping}
 \sum_{2^b\le k<2^{b+1}}
 q_{\Gamma_{k,\omega},m}c_{\Gamma_{k,\omega}}
 =
 \widehat q_{\Gamma,\omega,b,m}
 \widehat c_{\Gamma,\omega,b}.
\end{equation}
At base level the right-hand side is the grouped generator
\(\widehat\gamma\in\widehat{\mathscr G}_0\) of
\cref{def:ambient_conormal_source_scale}.  The difference between the atomic
tuple and this grouped representative lies in \(\ker\mathbf Q_0\), and hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:grouped_quotient_norm_bound}
 \left\|\left[\sum_k c_{\Gamma_{k,\omega}}\right]\right\|_{
   \mathfrak X_U^{(0)}}
 \le
 \widehat w_{\Gamma,\omega,b,0}
 \|\widehat c_{\Gamma,\omega,b}\|_{E_{\widehat\gamma,0}}.
\end{equation}
At level \(m>0\) the same statement holds exactly when
\(\widehat\gamma\in\widehat{\mathscr G}_m\), namely when its actual recursive
image column has the finite weighted sum required in the definition.  This
regrouping never identifies different incident labels and never removes a
terminal coordinate.

Let \(F_*\) be the number of one-step physical face families, including their
two incident labels.  If the grouped currents in block \(b\) satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:grouped_radial_s1_target}
 \widehat w_{\Gamma,\omega,b,m}
 \|\widehat c_{\Gamma,\omega,b}\|
 \le C2^{-(\sigma-\mu)b}
 \|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(m+j+2)}}
\end{equation}
for all \((\Gamma,\omega)\), with \(\sigma>\mu\), then the one-event
source-side part of (S1) follows by summing over \(b\).  In particular the
effective column entropy of the grouped physical presentation is
\(\kappa_{\rm eff}=0\), rather than the artificial value \(\kappa=1\) from
the separate quadratic-strip presentation.

For radial inflation, therefore, the unresolved estimate is no longer required
to beat the \(2^b\) number of artificial strips.  It is enough to prove the
same-label gluing in \eqref{eq:blockwise_physical_face_regrouping} and a
strict grouped decay margin \(\sigma>\mu\).  Cross-label cancellation in the
total physical current cannot be used for this purpose.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The restrictions of a distribution to adjacent artificial cells add to its
restriction to their union.  The derivative of the artificial partition of
unity appears with opposite signs on the two cells, and the matching-trace
hypothesis removes the only boundary term created by concatenation.  This
proves \eqref{eq:blockwise_physical_face_regrouping} within the fixed label.
The matching atomic tuple and the grouped coefficient have the same component
of the joint realization map.  Their difference is therefore in
\(\ker\mathbf Q_0\), and choosing the grouped coefficient as the quotient
representative proves \eqref{eq:grouped_quotient_norm_bound}.  This is the
step which a scalar total-current regrouping cannot supply.
The no-boundary-birth atlas has finitely many one-step physical faces, so after
this regrouping there are at most \(F_*\) labelled columns in every dyadic
block.  Consequently \eqref{eq:grouped_radial_s1_target} gives
\[
 \sum_{\Gamma,\omega}
 \widehat w_{\Gamma,\omega,b,m}
 \|\widehat c_{\Gamma,\omega,b}\|
 \le C F_*2^{-(\sigma-\mu)b}\|\rho\|,
\]
and the geometric sum over \(b\) converges.  Since labels and terminal data
were retained, this is a legitimate source-module lift rather than a scalar
total-current cancellation.  The statement concerns the initial complete
event.  A later propagation which splits a grouped current must satisfy the
separate recursive column bound already built into (S3).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}[Fixed-atlas radial graph calculation]
For a resolved radial grazing block \(b\), let
\[
 F_{\theta,b}(\rho)
 =1_{\mathcal G_b}\,u^3a_{\theta}(u,s)\rho_\theta(u,s),
 \qquad R_{\ell,\beta}=(u\partial_u)^\ell\partial_s^\beta.
\]
Let \(\mathcal P_b\) be a homogeneous subdivision, let
\(\mathcal F_b^{\rm phys}\) be its labelled one-sided physical faces, and
let \(\mathcal P_b^{\rm cut}\) be its artificial internal cuts.  Define
\begin{align*}
 \|H\|_{\widehat{\mathcal V}_b^p}
 :=\sum_{\ell+|\beta|\le p}\Bigl\{&
 \sum_{P\in\mathcal P_b}
   \bigl(\|R_{\ell,\beta}H\|_{L^1(P)}
        +|R_{\ell,\beta}H|_{BV(P)}\bigr)\\
 &+\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal F_b^{\rm phys}}
   \bigl(\|J_\Gamma R_{\ell,\beta}H|_{\Gamma^\pm}\|_{L^1}
        +|J_\Gamma R_{\ell,\beta}H|_{BV}\bigr)\\
 &+\sum_{C\in\mathcal P_b^{\rm cut}}
   \|R_{\ell,\beta}H|_{C^+}-R_{\ell,\beta}H|_{C^-}\|_{L^1(C)}
 \Bigr\}.
\end{align*}
Here \(J_\Gamma\) is the physical coarea Jacobian and only same-label sides
are compared at an artificial cut.  The last line vanishes for the physically
concatenated representative.

Let \(\mathcal W_b^s\) be the standard stable test leaves in the block,
\(\mathcal M_b\) the matched triples
\((W_1,W_2,\mathfrak h^u)\), and \(\mathcal U_b\) the unmatched descendants
from the standard coupling cut.  With the same
\(b_0,c_0,\alpha,\beta\) and short-leaf scaling as in the Demers--Zhang norm,
put
\begin{align*}
 |H|_{w,b,p}
 &=\max_{\ell+|\beta|\le p}
   \sup_{W\in\mathcal W_b^s,\ \|\psi\|_{C^\alpha(W)}\le1}
   \left|\int_W R_{\ell,\beta}H\,\psi\,dm_W\right|,\\
 |H|_{hol,b,p}
 &=\max_{\ell+|\beta|\le p}
   \sup_{(W_1,W_2,\mathfrak h^u)\in\mathcal M_b}
   \sup_{\|\psi_i\|_{C^\alpha}\le1}
   \frac{\left|\int_{W_1}R_{\ell,\beta}H\psi_1\,dm
    -\int_{W_2}R_{\ell,\beta}H
      (\psi_2\circ\mathfrak h^u)J_{\mathfrak h^u}\,dm\right|}
        {d(W_1,W_2)^\beta},\\
 |H|_{unm,b,p}
 &=\max_{\ell+|\beta|\le p}
   \sum_{V\in\mathcal U_b}
   \sup_{\|\psi\|_{C^0(V)}\le1}
   \left|\int_VR_{\ell,\beta}H\psi\,dm_V\right|,\\
 \|H\|_{\mathcal W_b^p}&=|H|_{w,b,p}+b_0|H|_{hol,b,p}
   +c_0|H|_{unm,b,p}.
\end{align*}
These are norms of the undifferentiated flux coefficient; neither contains
\(B_1\rho\).

For \(0\le m\le J+3\), set \(p=m+2\) and define on the smooth finite-strip
core
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uniform_block_flux_jet_norm}
 \|\rho\|_{\mathrm{bfg}}^{(m)}
 :=\mathcal N_{m+2}^{\rm flux}(\rho)
 +\sup_{\theta,b}\left\{
 2^{3b}\|F_{\theta,b}(\rho)\|_{\widehat{\mathcal V}_b^p}
 +2^b\|u^{-2}F_{\theta,b}(\rho)\|_{\mathcal W_b^p}
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Fix one radial blown-up atlas, one homogeneous refinement convention, and one
coupling cut.  The resulting auxiliary completion is denoted
\(\mathfrak D_{U,\mathrm{bfg}}^{(m)}\).  No equivalence under a change of
atlas/refinement, no transfer invariance, and no recursive module structure
are asserted for this auxiliary completion.

Most importantly, the first term in
\eqref{eq:uniform_block_flux_jet_norm} gives the level-correct embedding
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:block_flux_correct_level_embedding}
 \mathfrak D_{U,\mathrm{bfg}}^{(m)}\hookrightarrow
 \mathfrak D_U^{(m)},\qquad
 \|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(m)}}\le
 \|\rho\|_{\mathrm{bfg}}^{(m)}.
\end{equation}
In the chosen atlas the graph seminorm is finite on the invariant flux jets
\eqref{eq:radial_inflation_flux_jets}.  Its two extra block terms are
essential: \(\mathcal N_{m+2}^{\rm flux}\) alone does not give either
\(2^{-3b}\) block-flux decay or \(2^{-b}\) decay after multiplication by
\(u^{-2}\).  The seminorm is used only to record these two missing estimates,
not to define a chart-independent response space.

Consider the radial-inflation family of
\cref{thm:radial_inflation_second_order_recovery}.  The base response letter
has on the smooth finite-strip core the fixed-atlas coefficient presentation
\[
 B_1:\mathfrak D_{U,\mathrm{bfg}}^{(3)}
 \dashrightarrow\mathfrak X_U^{(0)},
\]
where the dashed arrow is not a completed operator map.
After the same-label physical regrouping of
\cref{prop:physical_face_regrouping_removes_strip_entropy}, the singular

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:first_order_radial_grouped_coefficient}
 \widehat w_{\Gamma,\omega,b,0}
 \|\widehat c_{\Gamma,\omega,b}(B_1\rho)\|_{E_{\widehat\gamma,0}}
 \le C2^{-(3-\epsilon_*)b}
 \|\rho\|_{\mathrm{bfg}}^{(3)},
\end{equation}
and the regular interior/Jacobian block satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:first_order_radial_regular_block_coefficient}
 \|(B_1\rho)^{\rm reg}_b\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{w,(0)}}
 \le C2^{-(1-\epsilon_*)b}\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{bfg}}^{(3)}.
\end{equation}
The central clean point sector is finite, and the order-one grazing endpoint
is zero because \(u^3\delta(u)=0\).  Hence the complete base-level

with margin \(\delta=1-\epsilon_*>0\).

By \eqref{eq:block_flux_correct_level_embedding} the domain lies at the
correct formal input level
\(\mathfrak D_U^{(3)}=\{\mathcal N_5^{\rm flux}<\infty\}\).  Nevertheless

\begin{proposition}[The second radial letter is not an all-seed (S1) map]
\label{prop:second_radial_all_seed_s1_obstruction}
For the radial-inflation family, the isolated second transfer letter
\(B_2=\partial_s^2\Ltransfer_s|_0\) does not satisfy the base
label-resolved source estimate in (S1) on every strong seed.  More precisely,
on a circular target let \(r=R+s\), fix an incoming oriented line at
perpendicular distance \(h<r\) from the centre, and put
\[
 d(r)=\sqrt{1-h^2/r^2}=|v\cdot n|=u^2.
\]
In the transported target arclength chart the impact angle
\(\alpha(r)=\arcsin(h/r)\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:second_radial_impact_angle_singularity}
 \alpha''(r)
 =\frac{h(2r^2-h^2)}{r^5d(r)^3}
 \asymp d(r)^{-3}=u^{-6}
\end{equation}
as the outgoing collision becomes grazing.  Since the collision flux is
\(u^3a(u,y)\,du\,dy\), a locally affine target observable detects in the
interior second derivative the nonintegrable coefficient
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:second_radial_flux_obstruction}
 u^3\alpha''(r)\asymp u^{-3}.
\end{equation}
Consequently no within-label regrouping of artificial homogeneity strips can
give a decaying dyadic \(\ell^1\) source bound for \(B_2\rho\) uniformly over
strong \(\rho\).  The obstruction already occurs for a smooth seed which is
nonzero on the chosen branch.

This does not contradict the second-order invariant or exact-current
theorems.  Their complete sources contain the additional terms
\(2B_1\rho_1\), respectively
\(g_2+2B_1h_1\), and the exact differentiated invariance/Poisson identities
cancel \eqref{eq:second_radial_flux_obstruction} before the source is placed
in the strong regular embedding.  Thus second and higher radial response must
be formulated for specified complete assemblies; extending the first-order
all-seed theorem to \(B_2\) is impossible on the present source scale.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write the target intersection in coordinates in which the incoming line is
horizontal.  Its perpendicular coordinate is \(h\), while the longitudinal
distance from tangency is
\(a(r)=\sqrt{r^2-h^2}=rd(r)\).  Differentiating
\(\alpha(r)=\arcsin(h/r)\) twice gives
\eqref{eq:second_radial_impact_angle_singularity}.

Choose a sequence of smooth test functions supported in the interiors of
dyadic target blocks \(u\asymp2^{-b}\), away from the physical tangency face
and from artificial strip cuts, and make each test affine in \(\alpha\) on a
smaller block.  Their rescaled trace-test norms are uniform.  On those
supports the dual identity
\[
 \langle B_2\rho,f_b\rangle
 =\int \rho\,\partial_s^2(f_b\circ\Pcal_s)|_0\,d\nu_0
\]
has no face term.  The quadratic observable term vanishes on the affine
region, while the linear term is
\((\partial_\alpha f_b)\alpha''(r)\).  Smooth transport corrections have
strictly lower grazing order.  Taking \(\rho\) nonzero with fixed sign there,
flux invariance gives the fixed-sign block coefficient
\(u^3\alpha''(r)\asymp u^{-3}\).  It grows rather than decays after
multiplication by the positive source weight \(2^{\epsilon_*b}\).  Because
the tests avoid every block boundary, neither artificial-face cancellation
nor a conormal endpoint current can remove this interior term.  This violates
the vector-valued dyadic estimate required in (S1).
\end{proof}

The remainder of this appendix is an implication theorem under the following

\begin{assumption}[Collision response-scale and triangular recovery certificates]
\label{ass:response_scale_dynamics}
The independent completions in \cref{def:finite_order_response_scale} satisfy,
uniformly on the clean chart, the following structural estimates at every
level used up to order \(J\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(S\arabic*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item \(\Ltransfer_\theta:\mathfrak D_U^{(m)}\to
  \mathfrak D_U^{(m)}\) is bounded.  Let
  \(\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm der},(m)}\) be the closure in
  \(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\) of the complete order-\(m\) derivative sources.  Then
  \(B_{j,\theta}:\mathfrak D_U^{(m+j+2)}\to
  \mathfrak W_U^{{\rm der},(m)}\subset\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\).
  By \cref{prop:scalar_tail_not_all_branch_s1}, this clause requires a
  label-resolved vector-valued block estimate such as
  \eqref{eq:necessary_vector_valued_s1_target}; it is not implied by the
  scalar physical \(2^{-b}\) tail.
  \item There is a distinguished closed recovered source class
  \(\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm rec},(m)}\subset
    \mathfrak W_U^{{\rm der},(m)}\) and a bounded strong-primitive map
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:strong_primitive_recovery}
    \mathcal K_{\theta,m}:\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm rec},(m)}
       \longrightarrow \mathfrak D_U^{(m+1)},\qquad
    \bar g:=\mathsf S_m\mathbf Q_mg
      =(I-\Ltransfer_\theta)\mathcal K_{\theta,m}g,
  \end{equation}
  with centered primitive and
  \(\|\mathcal K_{\theta,m}g\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(m+1)}}
    \le C_m^{\rm prim}\|g\|_{\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm der},(m)}}\).
  At base dynamical level, \cref{prop:strong_recovery_equivalence} shows only
  that membership in \(\mathfrak B_U^0\) is equivalent to an ordinary
  Demers--Zhang strong primitive.  The present S2 clause is stronger: it
  additionally assumes that this primitive lies in the positive flux-seed
  level \(\mathfrak D_U^{(m+1)}\).  Thus the genuinely new estimate is
  cancellation of the
  represented conormal/point sector together with a strong norm bound, not a
  second resolvent construction.  To pass a block boundary, the certificate
  additionally supplies either intrinsic equality with the regular embedding
  or the scalar assembly-reset condition of
  \cref{prop:certified_scalar_assembly_reset}; raw terminal data are never
  discarded merely because \(\mathsf S_m\) forgets them.
  The fully assembled invariant sources in
  \cref{thm:invariant_source_ward_identity} belong to this class with the
  explicit primitive \(\partial_s^j\rho_s\).  The radial inflation in
  \cref{thm:radial_inflation_second_order_recovery} verifies this cancellation
  at zero waiting time, while
  \cref{thm:blockwise_second_order_invariant_recovery} verifies the complete
  fixed-length block uniformly for arbitrary positive gaps through order two,
  and \cref{thm:uniform_postassembly_radial_tail,prop:certified_scalar_assembly_reset}
  close every radial fixed-length block in the scalar reset sense without
  (R3).  The separate
  \cref{lem:fixed_length_scalar_terminal_truncation} concerns only blocks for
  which (R3) has been certified.
  \item A \emph{certified recovered response tree} starts from a specified
  centered source in \(\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm rec},(m)}\).  It is triangular in
  the following sense: if a
  recovered centered source is evolved for a finite time and the next
  branch/Jacobian/roof/conormal event is inserted, the \emph{complete assembled
  source at that event} again belongs to the next recovered level, either
  intrinsically or after a certified assembly reset.  Its strong
  primitive is bounded by the product of the intervening transfer-letter norms
  and the preceding primitive norm.  Difference quotients of these assembled
  primitives converge in the stated strong level.  This clause does not assert
  that \(B_{j,\theta}\rho\) is recovered for an arbitrary
  \(\rho\in\mathfrak D_U^{(m+j+2)}\); the initial recovery and every subsequent
  closure step are part of the certificate.
  By \cref{prop:eventwise_all_seed_recovery_obstruction}, this clause cannot
  hold for every strong seed at a genuinely moving face; it must be either
  source-specific or formulated for complete time blocks.
\end{enumerate}
These are genuine structural hypotheses on the \emph{assembled} physical
source tree.  They are not consequences of the ordinary density spectral gap
or of the definition of the norm.  Unlike the former (S2)--(S3), they do not
assume the desired correlation decay: once
\eqref{eq:strong_primitive_recovery} is available, that decay is a theorem
consequence of the ordinary Demers--Zhang strong spectral gap.  A raw isolated
trace need not be recovered and is not placed in
\(\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm rec},(m)}\).  The exact invariant sources in
  \cref{thm:invariant_source_ward_identity} are the only response tree verified
  to satisfy (S2) in full finite order in this paper.  The nonconjugate radial
  inflation verifies the complete invariant assembly through second order,
  but no nonconjugate moving-scatterer family is claimed here to satisfy the
  eventwise repeated closure (S3).  It does verify source-specific blockwise
  positive-waiting reset through order two for every fixed block length; the
  infinite dynamical tree remains open.  A uniform intrinsic historical
  terminal estimate is not an independent soft bound: by
  \cref{prop:historical_terminal_accumulation_dichotomy} it requires the
  one-event terminal germ to vanish.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[No adjoint Lasota--Yorke contraction in a positive jet norm]
\label{prop:no_adjoint_jet_contraction}
Let \(T\) be any \(C^2\) uniformly hyperbolic diffeomorphism on a regular
branch, with an expanding covector direction of factor \(\lambda>1\).  A norm
which dominates a nonzero first derivative of \(f\) in that direction cannot
satisfy
\[
 \|f\circ T^{N}\|_{\rm jet}
 \le a\|f\|_{\rm jet}+C\|f\|_{\rm lower},\qquad a<1,
\]
on all centered smooth \(f\), when the lower norm is compact with respect to
the jet norm.  In particular the former adjoint trace Lasota--Yorke assertion
for \(\mathfrak A_U^{(m)}\) cannot be used to prove source-series convergence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a regular chart and smooth functions
\(f_k(x)=k^{-1}\chi(x)e^{ik\xi(x)}\), subtracting their means, where
\(d\xi\) is the expanding covector and \(\chi\) is supported away from the
branch boundary.  Their first-jet norms stay bounded below and above, while
the compact lower norm tends to zero along a subsequence.  The chain rule gives
\(d(f_k\circ T^N)=D T^{N\,*}(df_k)\circ T^N\), so the controlled derivative is
multiplied by at least \(c\lambda^N\).  Taking \(N\) with
\(c\lambda^N>a\) and then \(k\to\infty\) contradicts the displayed
inequality.  Centering changes only a constant and hence no derivative.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact Demers--Zhang location of a transverse trace]
\label{prop:demers_zhang_trace_location}
In the notation of Demers--Zhang, a nonzero codimension-one current supported
on a \(C^2\) curve uniformly transverse to the stable test leaves is, in
general, an element of the weak completion \(\Bweak\) but not of the strong
stable space \(\Bcal\).  Therefore the Demers--Zhang strong spectral gap does
not by itself produce a strong primitive for an isolated trace source.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(W_\ell\) be a stable test segment of length \(\ell\) meeting the trace
curve once and transversely.  In the weak norm the test normalization is
\(\|\psi\|_{C^p(W_\ell)}\le1\), so the point trace is bounded by the inverse
crossing angle.  In the Demers--Zhang strong stable norm the allowed tests
satisfy \(|W_\ell|^\alpha\|\psi\|_{C^q(W_\ell)}\le1\).  Taking a test which is
constant near the crossing with value \(\ell^{-\alpha}\) makes the trace
pairing of size \(c\ell^{-\alpha}\), which diverges as \(\ell\downarrow0\).
Thus the current need not lie in the strong space.  The fixed-table
Lasota--Yorke theorem contracts the strong norm and only bounds the weak norm;
it supplies no exponential decay for an arbitrary weak trace source.  A
source-specific recovery/coupling estimate is genuinely additional.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Strong-primitive conormal coupling]
\label{thm:strong_primitive_conormal_coupling}
Assume the map spectral gap of
\cref{thm:primitive_spectral_resolvent}.  Let
\(g\in\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm rec},(m)}\) be centered and put
\(\bar g=\mathsf S_m\mathbf Q_mg\) and
\(h=\mathcal K_{\theta,m}g\), so that
\(\bar g=(I-\Ltransfer_\theta)h\).  All pairings with \(g\) below mean the
total-current pairing with \(\bar g\).  Then for every centered
\(f\in\mathfrak A_U^{(m+1)}\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proved_recovered_source_decay}
 |\langle g,\mathscr U_\theta^n f\rangle|
 \le C_m\vartheta^n
   \|h\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(m+1)}}
   \|f\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(m+1)}} ,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proved_recovered_source_telescoping}
 \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle g,\mathscr U_\theta^n f\rangle
 =\langle h,f\rangle-\langle\Ltransfer_\theta^Nh,f\rangle.
\end{equation}
In particular the series is Cauchy with an explicit \(O(\vartheta^N)\) tail.
For a certified finite response tree satisfying (S3), applying this theorem
after every complete assembled event gives the product decay and absolute
summability of the scalar words in that tree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Duality and \(\bar g=(I-\Ltransfer_\theta)h\) give
\[
 \langle g,\mathscr U_\theta^nf\rangle
 =\langle\Ltransfer_\theta^nh,f\rangle
  -\langle\Ltransfer_\theta^{n+1}h,f\rangle .
\]
The two terms are bounded by the ordinary zero-mass Demers--Zhang spectral gap
on the strong density space; the continuous embedding of the response density
level into that space and the restricted observable dual give
\eqref{eq:proved_recovered_source_decay}.  Summing the displayed difference
telescopes and proves
\eqref{eq:proved_recovered_source_telescoping}.  The triangular statement is
an induction: (S3) supplies the next strong primitive, and the same argument
supplies the next intervening geometric factor.  Thus correlation decay is
derived from the spectral gap and the primitive identity rather than assumed
as a Koopman jet contraction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Nontriviality and consequences of scale compatibility]
\label{prop:response_scale_well_posed}
For each \(m\le J+3\), the spaces in
\cref{def:finite_order_response_scale} are nonzero Banach spaces independent,
up to equivalent norm, of finite homogeneous refinement.  The transported
invariant density belongs to every required density level, and
\[
  \Ltransfer_\theta:
  \mathfrak D_U^{(m)}\longrightarrow\mathfrak D_U^{(m)}
\]
is bounded under (S1).  For \(1\le j\le J\), the \(j\)th transported transfer derivative
extends as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transfer_derivative_scale_map}
  B_{j,\theta}:=\partial_\theta^j\Ltransfer_\theta:
  \mathfrak D_U^{(m+j+2)}
  \longrightarrow
  \mathfrak W_U^{{\rm der},(m)}
  \subset\mathfrak X_U^{(m)} .
\end{equation}
Every physical branchwise \(C^r\) observable with finite displayed jets belongs
to \(\mathfrak A_U^{(m)}\).  If (S2) holds, then for a centered recovered source
\(g\in\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm rec},(m)}\), the scalar functional
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:source_scalar_resolvent_functional}
  \mathcal R_{\theta,g}^{\rm scal}(f)
  :=\sum_{n\ge0}\langle g,\mathscr U_\theta^nf\rangle
\end{equation}
is bounded on centered \(\mathfrak A_U^{(m+1)}\), with norm at most
\(C_m(1-\vartheta)^{-1}C_m^{\rm prim}
  \|g\|_{\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm der},(m)}}\).
Neither \(R_\theta^{\rm obs}\) nor \(B_{j,\theta}^*\) is asserted to be a
bounded operator on the positive jet completion \(\mathfrak A_U^{(m)}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Branchwise \(C^r\) functions with support compactly contained in one regular
homogeneous chart have finite flux-seed norms, so every level is nonzero.  The
collision invariant density is \(1\) in the moving flux fiber; after
normal-graph transport it is a \(C^r\) Jacobian and has the stated finite
norms.  Two finite refinements admit a common refinement.  Central pieces are
merely subdivided, artificial internal faces occur with opposite orientations,
and terminal pieces retain the same strip index; hence both seed norms
dominate the common-refinement norm by a finite-complexity constant.

The transfer and derivative maps are (S1), written in
\eqref{eq:transfer_derivative_scale_map}.  The strong primitive from (S2) and
\cref{thm:strong_primitive_conormal_coupling} prove
\eqref{eq:source_scalar_resolvent_functional}; (S3) supplies the corresponding
primitive after a complete re-insertion event.  This proposition deliberately gives a scalar resolvent functional and
not a Koopman resolvent on the positive jet completion; the latter is ruled out
by \cref{prop:no_adjoint_jet_contraction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Resolved indicator current]
\label{def:resolved_indicator_current}
Let
\[
  D_s=\{z:g_{1,s}(z)>0,\ldots,g_{\ell,s}(z)>0\}
\]
be a moving branch domain in a two-dimensional collision chart.  Resolve its
finite singular stratification into the parameter-uniform \(C^J\) manifold
with corners supplied by
\cref{ass:finite_order_clean_response_chart},
\(\widetilde D_s\) whose codimension-one faces are labelled by one generator
and whose codimension-two corners are labelled by an ordered pair of distinct
faces.  The order-\(m\) moving-boundary current is defined before any formal
delta multiplication by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:resolved_indicator_definition}
  \mathcal T_{D}^{(m)}
  :=
  \partial_s^m\big(\iota_{s*}[\widetilde D_s]\big)\big|_{s=0}
\end{equation}
as a distribution on compactly supported branch tests.  This definition is
intrinsic under changes of the resolving chart and fixes the meaning of every
higher trace used below.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Second and multiple moving traces]
\label{thm:second_multiple_trace}
For every \(1\le m\le J\), the current
\(\mathcal T_D^{(m)}\) in
\cref{def:resolved_indicator_current} is a finite sum of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item branch-interior derivatives;
  \item one-face currents
  \(a_{m,\ell}\delta^{(\ell)}(g_\Gamma)\), \(0\le\ell<m\), representing
  repeated derivatives of the same moving wall;
  \item derivatives of codimension-two coarea atoms at clean intersections
  \(g_\Gamma=g_\Lambda=0\), with coefficient
  \[
    \frac{a_{\Gamma\Lambda}}
    {|\det(Dg_\Gamma,Dg_\Lambda)|};
  \]
  \item parameter derivatives of the preceding point currents on the labelled
  clean pair-intersection vertices of the fixed simple stratified isotopy.
\end{enumerate}
All point-current terms lie in the central region \(u\ge u_J\); the grazing
collar contributes only one-face currents.  Thus a point atom is never born
from or lost into the grazing boundary inside the theorem.
No term is defined as
\(\delta(g_1)\delta(g_2)\delta(g_3)\) in the two-dimensional collision
section.  Triple-labelled meetings are excluded by the simple-incidence
hypothesis; they cannot be hidden inside a ``resolved vertex.''  For each
\(m\le J\) and each finite decorated core subfamily \(\mathscr F\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:higher_trace_scale_bound}
  \|\mathcal T_{D,\mathscr F}^{(m)}\rho\|_{\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}}
  \le C_{m,\mathscr F}\|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(m+2)}} .
\end{equation}
The completed countable physical branch sum belongs to
\(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\), with a uniform version of this estimate, only under
the all-branch mapping certificate (S1).  No isolated trace component is
asserted to have a uniform completion before the complete physical event is
assembled.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the pushforward current in
\eqref{eq:resolved_indicator_definition}.  On a smooth face, the
one-dimensional distributional chain rule gives
\[
  \partial_s^mH(g_s)\big|_0
  =
  \sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}
  P_{m,\ell}(\dot g,\ldots,\partial_s^{m-\ell}g)
  \delta^{(\ell)}(g_0).
\]
In normal coordinates \(y=g_0\), the action of
\(\delta^{(\ell)}(y)\) moves \(\ell\) normal derivatives onto the smooth
coefficient, density, test, and chart Jacobian.  This is a standard conormal
distribution and requires no self-product.

At two distinct clean faces, the coarea formula turns the intersection
into the displayed decorated point atom in
\(\mathfrak X_U^{(m)}\).  Repeated parameter differentiation moves the
atom and differentiates its coarea coefficient; it does not add a third
normal equation.  The clean lower bound in
\cref{ass:finite_order_clean_response_chart,lem:clean_two_face_determinant_bound}
controls its inverse determinant.  A tangency of two distinct faces is outside
the response chart; it is not reclassified as a vertex and no dyadic
\(1/\Delta\) summation is used.

For a finite decorated core, the coarea and trace-test bounds give
\eqref{eq:higher_trace_scale_bound} directly in the quotient coefficient
norm.  For the countable atlas, the local coefficient of an order-\(m\)
current can have polynomial strip and propagation growth.  The complete
physical event is assembled before summation, and
\cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum} supplies its basic
\(2^{-b}\) block tail.  Membership in the stronger recursively weighted
completion is exactly the separate all-branch statement (S1), not a
consequence of that scalar tail.  The ambient source norm and its corner
pairing are \cref{def:ambient_conormal_source_scale,prop:point_atoms_ambient_not_dz_weak}.
Internal artificial faces appear twice
with opposite induced orientations, so they cancel before completion.  The
local cases and determinant estimates are recorded in
\cref{lem:supp_second_order_single_wall_trace,prop:supp_two_wall_higher_order_response_closure,lem:supp_worked_double_trace_coarea,lem:supp_repeated_generator_trace_derivatives,prop:supp_normal_determinant_lower_bound_cases}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}[Wavefront interpretation]
For two distinct faces the wavefront conormals are linearly independent on a
clean cell, which is exactly the Hörmander condition for the coarea product.
For one repeated face the theorem uses a normal derivative of one conormal
current, not a product with itself.  Pair-current derivatives remain
derivatives of that same point current.  These cases exhaust the finite branch
derivative tree on the stipulated simple chart; a triple unfolding is a
different local problem and is not covered.
\end{remark*}

\begin{lemma*}[Chart independence of resolved conormal currents]
Let \(\beta_s:\widetilde D_s\to D_s\) and
\(\beta'_s:\widetilde D'_s\to D_s\) be two finite resolutions of the same
moving branch domain, compatible with the physical face labels.  Then for
every \(m\le J\),
\[
  \partial_s^m\beta_{s*}[\widetilde D_s]\big|_0
  =
  \partial_s^m\beta'_{s*}[\widetilde D'_s]\big|_0
\]
as distributions.  Hence
\(\mathcal T_D^{(m)}\) is independent of the order in which strip,
branch-change, and admitted multiple-impact cells are refined inside the
one-step clean stratified isotopy of
\cref{thm:local_clean_atlas_flux_factorization}.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}
Take a common resolution
\(\widehat D_s\) obtained by the fiber product of the two finite
stratifications followed by subdivision along every face preimage.  There are
proper face-preserving maps
\[
  p_s:\widehat D_s\to\widetilde D_s,\qquad
  p'_s:\widehat D_s\to\widetilde D'_s
\]
of degree one on the interior, and
\(\beta_s p_s=\beta'_sp'_s\).  Integration of a compactly supported test
function over the three interior currents therefore gives the same scalar
function of \(s\).  It is \(C^J\) because the resolved charts and their
Jacobians are \(C^J\) up to each face.  Differentiate this scalar identity
\(m\) times.  Oppositely oriented artificial faces cancel in the common
resolution, while physical faces and corners retain their labels.  The
resulting distributional derivatives agree.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Clean central two-face determinant bound]
\label{lem:clean_two_face_determinant_bound}
Let
\(\Delta(z)=|\det(Dg_\Gamma(z),Dg_\Lambda(z))|\) on an admitted two-face trace
cell.  Under \cref{ass:finite_order_clean_response_chart}, that cell lies in
\(u\ge u_J\) and \(\Delta\ge c_J\).  Hence for every stable test segment \(W\),
\[
  \int_{W\cap\{g_\Gamma=g_\Lambda=0\}}
  \Delta^{-1}\,d\mathcal H^0
  \le C_J
  \#(W\cap\{g_\Gamma=g_\Lambda=0\}).
\]
The intersection multiplicity is part of the finite-word clean-atlas bound.
Repeated faces carry one conormal support with normal derivatives and do not
use this estimate.  No claim is made that a sublevel estimate alone makes
\(1/\Delta\) integrable through a simple tangency.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The clean determinant lower bound gives \(\Delta^{-1}\le c_J^{-1}\)
pointwise at every distinct-face intersection.  Summing over the uniformly
finite intersection set gives the displayed inequality.  The counterexample
\(\Delta(t)=|t|\) shows why the excluded tangential case cannot be recovered
by summing dyadic determinant shells: every shell contributes a nonvanishing
constant.  The boundary-fold example in
\cref{prop:sharpness_clean_ward_data} shows separately why a determinant jet
vanishing at \(u=0\) cannot replace \eqref{eq:no_boundary_pair_birth}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition*}[Scalar limit interchange at finite order]
Let \(g_s^{(j)}\) be a recovered order-\(j\) assembled source produced by a
difference quotient of the transported transfer family, assume its strong
primitives converge in the level stated in (S3), and let
\[
  u_{f,N}=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathscr U_0^n\Piobsperp_0 f.
\]
For \(j\le J\), the two limits
\[
  \lim_{s\to0}\lim_{N\to\infty}
  \langle g_s^{(j)},u_{f,N}\rangle,
  \qquad
  \lim_{N\to\infty}\lim_{s\to0}
  \langle g_s^{(j)},u_{f,N}\rangle
\]
exist and agree as scalar source/test pairings.  The common value is the
observable-side word specified by
\cref{prop:multispace_resolvent_differentiation}.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}
Write
\(S_N(g,f)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle g,\mathscr U_0^nf\rangle\).
The strong-primitive identity gives
\[
 S_N(g,f)=\langle\mathcal K g,f\rangle
 -\langle\Ltransfer_0^N\mathcal K g,f\rangle .
\]
Hence
\[
 |S_N(g_s^{(j)},f)-S_\infty(g_0^{(j)},f)|
 \le C\|\mathcal K g_s^{(j)}-\mathcal K g_0^{(j)}\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(j+1)}}
      \|f\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(j+1)}}
 +C\vartheta^N\|\mathcal K g_0^{(j)}\|\|f\|.
\]
The first term tends to zero by (S3) and the second uniformly by the ordinary
strong spectral gap.  The same scalar estimate applies after each complete
earlier event in an order-\(j\) word.  No element
\(R_0^{\rm obs}f\) in the positive jet completion is introduced.  This proves
joint Cauchy convergence and the interchange.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Observable-side scalar insertion for recovered sources]
\label{thm:trace_insertion_recovery}
For \(0\le m\le J\), let
\[
  g\in\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm rec},(m)},\qquad
  \langle g,1\rangle=0,
\]
and let \(f\in\mathfrak A_U^{(m+1)}\) be centered.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:observable_trace_resolvent_pairing}
  \langle g,R_\theta^{\rm obs}f\rangle
  :=
  \lim_{N\to\infty}
  \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle g,\mathscr U_\theta^n f\rangle
\end{equation}
exists and
\[
  |\langle g,R_\theta^{\rm obs}f\rangle|
  \le C_m C_m^{\rm prim}\|g\|_{\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm der},(m)}}
          \|f\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(m+1)}}.
\]
More precisely, for \(M>N\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:observable_trace_cauchy_tail}
 \left|\sum_{n=N}^{M}
   \langle g,\mathscr U_\theta^nf\rangle\right|
 \le
 \frac{C_m C_m^{\rm prim}\vartheta^N}
      {1-\vartheta}
 \|g\|_{\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm der},(m)}}
 \|f\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(m+1)}}.
\end{equation}
More generally, every alternating scalar word in a certified recovered tree,
with total response order at most \(J\), defines a bounded pairing between the
corresponding ambient source and observable levels.  No statement is made for a
word whose first derivative source has not been certified recovered.

This theorem makes no assertion that
\[
  \sum_{n\ge0}\Ltransfer_\theta^ng
  \in\mathfrak D_U^{(m)}.
\]
In particular, no finite prefix
\(g,\Ltransfer g,\ldots,\Ltransfer^{N-1}g\) is assigned to the strong density
space merely because the complete assembled source has a separate strong
primitive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a smooth finite-strip source, duality gives
\[
  \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle g,\mathscr U_\theta^nf\rangle
  =
  \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle\Ltransfer_\theta^ng,f\rangle .
\]
The complete physical block estimate and the explicit one-sided jet/holonomy
norm give the time-zero pairing bound.  Applying
  \cref{thm:strong_primitive_conormal_coupling} to every summand and summing the
  geometric tail gives
\eqref{eq:observable_trace_cauchy_tail}.  This proves Cauchy convergence
uniformly on the smooth core.

Completion gives \eqref{eq:observable_trace_resolvent_pairing}.  Notice that
the finite prefix was estimated only as a dual pairing.  It was not inserted
  into a strong-density norm.  Longer words are the scalar re-insertion blocks
  of (S3): evolve a centered recovered block, assemble the next complete event,
  take its strong primitive, and apply
  \cref{thm:strong_primitive_conormal_coupling} again.  No transpose derivative operator on
\(\mathfrak A_U^{(m)}\) is introduced.  Each insertion consumes the finite
number of source/test levels recorded in its order, so total order at most
\(J\) closes before the primitive reserve is exhausted.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Certified scalar multi-space differentiation]
\label{prop:multispace_resolvent_differentiation}
Let a centered input source and all of its successive complete derivative
events form a certified recovered response tree in the sense of (S3).  After
pairing that tree at level \(m+j+3\) with an observable at level
\(m+j+3\), the \(j\)th parameter derivative of its centered scalar
susceptibility is a finite sum, over ordered compositions
\(\pi_1+\cdots+\pi_q=j\), of absolutely convergent scalar series
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scale_kato_word}
  \sum_{n_0,\ldots,n_q\ge0}
    \mathcal C_{\pi,\mathbf n,\theta}(\rho,f).
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal C_{\pi,\mathbf n,\theta}\) is the finite scalar correlation
block obtained by differentiating the corresponding certified finite-time
identity
identity, centering after each insertion, and retaining every branch,
Jacobian, flux, roof, and physical-face term.  It is not notation for a
composition of operators \(R^{\rm obs}B^*R^{\rm obs}\) on a jet space.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate a finite partial sum.  The ordinary product rule gives finitely
many branchwise correlation blocks indexed by an ordered composition and by
the intervening times \(\mathbf n\).  The initial source is recovered by the
certificate, and the assembled strong primitives converge by the last clause
of (S3).  Estimate every intervening block by
\cref{thm:strong_primitive_conormal_coupling}; the triangular primitive bound
in (S3) makes the product of geometric factors summable over
\(\mathbf n\).  Dominated convergence therefore passes the parameter limit
through the multiple series.  Induction gives the Faà di Bruno coefficients
and consumes exactly the displayed source/test levels.  At no stage is a
strong-to-weak derivative inserted into a one-space Kato identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform certified finite-order response words]
\label{prop:uniform_multiresponse_bounds}
For each \(0\le j\le J\), every ordered composition
\(\pi=(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_q)\) of \(j\), and every certified recovered response
tree whose initial primitive \(h_{\pi,\rho,\theta}\) satisfies
\[
 \|h_{\pi,\rho,\theta}\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(j+3)}}
 \le C_{\rm init}(\pi)\|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(j+3)}},
\]
there is a constant
\(C_{\rm word}(j,q)\), independent of \(\theta\), such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uniform_response_word_bound}
  \sum_{n_0,\ldots,n_q\ge0}
  |\mathcal C_{\pi,\mathbf n,\theta}(\rho,f)|
  \le
  C_{\rm word}(j,q)
  \|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(j+3)}}
  \|f\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(j+3)}} .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the ordinary strong decay to the certified initial primitive, and then
apply the triangular strong-primitive estimate (S3) after every subsequent
complete event.  Merely knowing
\eqref{eq:transfer_derivative_scale_map} does not certify the first derivative
source; that extra implication is not used here.  The
branch number, strip complexity, conormal multiplicity, and terminal pieces
are already included in the operator norm of \(B_{\pi_i,\theta}\); no
artificial factor \(\vartheta_0^n\) is inserted into a series.  The only
  geometric decay is the Demers--Zhang strong decay applied to the constructed
  primitive in \cref{thm:strong_primitive_conormal_coupling}.
\end{proof}

 \chi_\theta(g_\theta,f_\theta)

\end{equation}
is \(C^J\).  Its derivatives are the finite ordered observable-side words in

no density-side resolvent is applied to \(g_\theta\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The explicit Cauchy tail
\eqref{eq:observable_trace_cauchy_tail} is uniform on the clean compact chart.
Differentiate finite partial sums and use the assumed \(C^J\) strong primitive,
the certified triangular recovery bounds (S3), followed by
\cref{thm:strong_primitive_conormal_coupling}, to pass to the limit with the same uniform tail
at each consumed source/test level.  The product rule gives precisely

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-order moving-singularity response]
\label{thm:finite_order_moving_singularity_response}
Assume \cref{ass:response_scale_dynamics}.  Let \(\theta_s\) be a \(C^J\)
finite-response curve, let \(\rho_s\) be the
transported invariant collision density, and let \(f_s\) be a \(C^J\)
observable section in the required source/test levels.  Then
\[
  I(s)=\langle\rho_s,f_s\rangle
\]
is \(C^J\) by the direct flux formula
\eqref{eq:direct_geometric_static_average}.  The transfer expression
\eqref{eq:first_order_observable_response} equals that direct derivative.  For
\(2\le j\le J\), differentiating the invariance equation gives an equivalent
finite Faà di Bruno sum obtained by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},wide,labelindent=0pt]
  \item differentiating \(f_s\), the flux transport, and the invariant
  projection;
  \item replacing every transfer derivative by its branch/interior,
  Jacobian, and resolved conormal decomposition from
  \cref{thm:second_multiple_trace};
  \item placing the resulting conormal source in
  \(\mathfrak X_U^{(j)}\); if its complete tree is certified recovered, insert
  it through the scalar observable-side words of
  \cref{prop:multispace_resolvent_differentiation}.
\end{enumerate}
If \(\Phi_s\) is the fixed collision-section transport and
\(J_s=d(\Phi_s^*\nu_s)/d\nu_0\), then the non-circular bound is
\[
  |I^{(j)}(0)|
  \le C_j
  \max_{\ell\le j}\|\partial_s^\ell(f_s\circ\Phi_s)|_0\|_{C^0}
  \max_{\ell\le j}\|\partial_s^\ell J_s|_0\|_{C^0}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pulling the invariant flux integral back by \(\Phi_s\) proves differentiability
and the displayed bound directly, with no invariant-density derivative on its
right-hand side.  To obtain the transfer representation, differentiate the
finite-time invariance identity
\(\langle\rho_s,f_s\rangle
=\langle\rho_s,\mathscr U_s^Nf_s\rangle\), center after every derivative
letter, and move all transfer derivatives to the observable side.  At first
  order, the limit \(N\to\infty\) is
  \eqref{eq:first_order_observable_response}; its Cauchy remainder follows from
  the explicit invariant strong primitive.  At order \(j\), the product rule
produces the resolved conormal letters of \cref{thm:second_multiple_trace} and
the successive-scale words of
  \cref{prop:multispace_resolvent_differentiation}.  When the assembled tree is
  certified, their uniform Cauchy limits and bounds are
  \cref{prop:uniform_multiresponse_bounds}.  Without that certificate the
  finite-time conormal identity remains valid, but no infinite scalar word is
  asserted.  Since both sides are
derivatives of the same finite-time identity, the singular trace/resolvent
terms combine exactly to the geometric Leibniz formula
\eqref{eq:direct_geometric_static_average}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectral separation and infinite scalar words}

\begin{definition*}[Period-one response convention]
The entire response theorem is formulated on the fixed period-one mixing
component required in \cref{ass:finite_order_clean_response_chart}.  The
period is a dynamical hypothesis, not the period of an arbitrary finite
transition graph and not a consequence of connectedness of the undirected
scatterer graph.  A family with a genuine \(q>1\) cyclic decomposition
requires a separate formulation using \(\Ltransfer_\theta^q\), cyclic
projectors, and periodic centering; it is not reassembled in this paper.
\end{definition*}

\begin{proposition*}[Peripheral spectrum on the period-one component]
On the stipulated period-one mixing component, \(1\) is a simple eigenvalue of
\(\Ltransfer_\theta\) and there is no other unit-modulus eigenvalue.  This is
the peripheral-spectrum input used in
\cref{thm:primitive_spectral_resolvent}; no conclusion for a \(q>1\) cyclic
component is claimed.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}
Quasi-compactness is supplied by the billiard-specific Demers--Zhang
Lasota--Yorke and compact-embedding theorem.  Simplicity and exclusion of the
remaining peripheral roots use the stipulated period-one mixing property and
the classical ergodic/mixing theorem for the selected finite-horizon
dispersing component
\cite{Sinai1970,chernov2006chaotic,ChernovZhang2009}.  Connectedness alone is
not used as an aperiodicity proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma*}[Uniform period-one spectral separation]
On the compact clean response chart, the selected component remains period
one and the simple eigenvalue \(1\) stays uniformly separated from the rest of
the spectrum.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}
Period one is part of \cref{ass:finite_order_clean_response_chart}.  The
Demers--Zhang perturbation theorem gives continuous spectral projectors and
spectral stability in the strong-to-weak perturbation metric.  The clean chart
is compact and does not cross a cyclic bifurcation, so the pointwise spectral
separations have a positive common minimum.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition*}[Expanded observable resolvent word]
For an ordered derivative composition
\(\pi=(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_q)\), differentiate the corresponding finite centered
correlation block and retain all physical terms.  Denote the resulting scalar
at intervening times \(\mathbf n=(n_0,\ldots,n_q)\) by
\(\mathcal C_{\pi,\mathbf n,\theta}(\rho,f)\).  The expanded scalar word is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:expanded_observable_resolvent_word}
  \sum_{n_0,\ldots,n_q\ge0}
  \mathcal C_{\pi,\mathbf n,\theta}(\rho,f).
\end{equation}
Centering is inserted after every derivative event.  This definition uses the
finite branch calculus and scalar pairing only; it does not presuppose that a
formal transpose \(B_j^*\) maps a positive observable jet space into itself.
No extra numerical weight is attached to the summand.
\end{definition*}

\begin{lemma}[Actual decay of a recovered derivative block]
\label{lem:actual_derivative_block_decay}
Let \(g\in\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm rec},(m)}\) be centered and let
\(\psi\in\mathfrak A_U^{(m+1)}\) be centered.  There are \(C_m>0\) and
\(\vartheta\in(0,1)\) such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:actual_derivative_block_decay}
  |\langle g,\mathscr U_\theta^n\psi\rangle|
  \le
  C_m C_m^{\rm prim}\vartheta^n
  \|g\|_{\mathfrak W_U^{{\rm der},(m)}}
  \|\psi\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(m+1)}} .
\end{equation}
The constants are uniform on a compact response chart.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is \cref{thm:strong_primitive_conormal_coupling} together with the
primitive bound in \eqref{eq:strong_primitive_recovery}.  The decay is therefore
a consequence of the ordinary Demers--Zhang strong spectral gap for the
primitive, not an assumption about Koopman contraction on a positive jet norm.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition*}[Certified infinite scalar-word summability]
Every expanded word
\eqref{eq:expanded_observable_resolvent_word} of total derivative order at
most \(J\) that belongs to a certified recovered response tree and has the
initial primitive bound in \cref{prop:uniform_multiresponse_bounds} is
absolutely summable after grouping into adapted blocks, and
\[
  \sum_{n_0,\ldots,n_q\ge0}
  |\text{the }(n_0,\ldots,n_q)\text{ summand}|
  \le
  C_{\pi}
  \|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(J+3)}}
  \|f\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(J+3)}} .
\]
The constant \(C_\pi\) contains the real geometric factors
\((1-\vartheta)^{-(q+1)}\), the finite strong-primitive constants, finite branch multiplicities,
and the proved physical grazing-block sums.  It contains no postulated
\(\vartheta_0^{n_0+\cdots+n_q}\) multiplier.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}
Apply the actual decay from
\cref{lem:actual_derivative_block_decay} to the certified initial primitive.
The triangular estimate (S3) then gives a strong primitive at every subsequent
complete event, followed again by the actual decay from
\cref{lem:actual_derivative_block_decay}.  Repeat for
\(n_{q-1},\ldots,n_0\).  This bounds the absolute value of the summand by
\[
  C_\pi
  \prod_{a=0}^q
  \vartheta^{n_a}
  \|\rho\|_{\mathfrak D_U^{(J+3)}}
  \|f\|_{\mathfrak A_U^{(J+3)}}.
\]
Now the geometric factors are consequences of
\eqref{eq:actual_derivative_block_decay}.  Summing them proves the result.
Branch number and trace-intersection number are already included in the
finite derivative-letter norms and in the complete physical block estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition*}[Rigid translation through second order]
Translate one scatterer by \(q_i(\sigma,s)=q_i(\sigma,0)+sw\), and allow the
incoming section coordinates to be transported, \(x=x(s)\), \(v=v(s)\).  At a
regular impact
\(x(s)+\tau(s)v(s)=q_i(\sigma(s),0)+sw\), the first two impact-time derivatives
are
\[
  \dot\tau=
  \frac{n_i\cdot(w-\dot x-\tau\dot v)}{n_i\cdot v},\qquad
  \ddot\tau
  =
  \frac{
    \dot n_i\cdot(w-\dot x-\dot\tau v-\tau\dot v)
    -n_i\cdot(\ddot x+2\dot\tau\dot v+\tau\ddot v)
  }{n_i\cdot v},
\]
where
\(\dot n_i=-S_i(\dot x+\dot\tau v+\tau\dot v-w)^{\top}\).
For fixed incoming coordinates this reduces to
\(\dot\tau=(n_i\cdot w)/(n_i\cdot v)\) and the corresponding fixed-(x,v)
second derivative.  The order-two
transfer source is
\[
  B_{2,w}\rho
  =
  B_{2,w}^{\rm int}\rho+B_{2,w}^{\rm Jac}\rho
  +\sum_{\Gamma}
   \bigl(a_{\Gamma,w}\delta(g_\Gamma)
        +b_{\Gamma,w}\delta'(g_\Gamma)\bigr)\rho
  +\sum_{\Gamma\pitchfork\Lambda}
   c_{\Gamma\Lambda,w}\delta_{\Gamma\cap\Lambda}\rho .
\]
Its invariant response is the observable-side pairing prescribed by
\cref{prop:multispace_resolvent_differentiation}; no density-side resolvent is
applied to \(B_{2,w}\rho\).
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}
The first derivative of the impact equation is
\[
 \dot x+\dot\tau v+\tau\dot v-w=q_{i,\sigma}\dot\sigma .
\]
Its normal projection gives the displayed formula for \(\dot\tau\); its
tangential projection and the Weingarten identity give the formula for
\(\dot n_i\).  Differentiating the normal equation a second time produces the
terms \(2\dot\tau\dot v+\tau\ddot v\) and gives the displayed formula for
\(\ddot\tau\).  Differentiate the
reflection law and branch Jacobian to obtain the interior and Jacobian terms.
For a single moving branch face, the second derivative of \(H(g_s)\) is
\[
  \ddot g\,\delta(g)+\dot g^{\,2}\delta'(g).
\]
Two distinct clean faces give the coarea atom with determinant denominator.
These are exactly the three cases in
\cref{thm:second_multiple_trace}.  The renormalized physical block identity
sums their combined grazing contributions, and
\cref{thm:trace_insertion_recovery} supplies the final observable pairing.
\end{proof}

and standard-family growth enter through

\begin{lemma*}[First-order strip tail]
Let \(M_1\) be the strip power obtained by differentiating the impact,
Jacobian, flux, and crossing factors once.  On the smooth core and with a
finite strip cutoff,
\[
  |\langle\mathcal T_{1,k,\theta}^{\rm core}\rho,\psi\rangle_W|
  \le C k^{M_1}\|\rho\|_{C^1_{\rm br}}m_W(E_{1,k,\theta}(W)).
\]
This local inequality is not summed absolutely over \(k\).  Instead the
complete physical current is grouped into dyadic blocks and summed by
\cref{lem:renormalized_grazing_block_sum}.  Neither the quadratic-strip width
\(O(k^{-3})\) nor the unweighted growth lemma supplies an arbitrary moment.

\end{lemma*}

\begin{lemma*}[Resonant-window counting correction]
Suppose
\(\Phi_b(s)=b(\tau_\alpha^{(N)}(s)-\tau_\beta^{(N)}(s))\) and
\(|\Phi_b'(s)|\ge |b|\Delta\) on a fixed interval.  For fixed angular width
\(\omega\), each component of
\[
  \{s:\operatorname{dist}(\Phi_b(s),2\pi\mathbb Z)<\omega\}
\]
has length \(O(|b|^{-1})\), but there are \(O(|b|)\) relevant resonances.
Consequently the union has only an \(O(1)\) bound and generally occupies a
fixed positive proportion; no \(O(|b|^{-1})\) estimate for the whole union is
valid.  The high-frequency estimate used in this paper is therefore the BDL
generator-resolvent theorem from
\cref{prop:uniform_dolgopyat_nonintegrability,prop:imported_bdl_high_frequency},
whose gain comes from local pairing, amplitude cutoffs, and iteration.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{remark*}[No weak-to-strong prefix inference]

\(\sum_{n\ge0}\Ltransfer^ng\in\mathfrak D_U\) when the finite prefix contains
the original weak distribution \(g\notin\mathfrak D_U\).  Accordingly the
paper uses the observable-side pairing in

claim.
\end{remark*}
