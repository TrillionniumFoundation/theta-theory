# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** *A First-Principles Construction of theta-Expectations from Deterministic Billiards*  
**Source reviewed:** `main.tex`, `technical-appendix.tex`, theorem inventory, and risk memo  
**Repository state reviewed:** `main` at commit `08aac05e51577bcd2f2ec5a6aef2a960a8d3ef9d`  
**Standard applied:** Annals of Mathematics / Inventiones Mathematicae / Journal of the AMS / Acta Mathematica  
**Recommendation:** **Reject**

## 1. Summary

The manuscript claims to derive a nonconvex Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman limit and a nonlinear “theta-expectation” semigroup from deterministic finite-horizon dispersing billiards equipped with a finite-response mechanical port.

The argument has three layers:

1. import a strong moving-billiard response and suspension-resolvent package from the companion response paper;
2. define correctors and effective coefficients \(D(x,p)\) and \(H(x,p)\);
3. use contact-frozen perturbed tests and half-relaxed limits to prove convergence of prelimit “viscosity-duality solutions” to the HJB equation.

The proposed result would be important if proved. It is not proved. The manuscript assumes or sketches every nonstandard bridge: the upstream moving-flow response, the global prelimit equation, the singular contact selection, and the small residual. Moreover, the imported Paper 1 interface is materially stronger than the unconditional theorem actually proved in that paper.

## 2. Fatal dependency problem

### 2.1 The manuscript imports results that Paper 1 does not unconditionally prove

The assumption `ass:paper1_response_input` says that Paper 1 proves:

- moving-singularity response formulae in a Banach bundle;
- a suspension Laplace contour for the moving family;
- trace insertion into resolvent words;
- uniform multi-response bounds;
- a finite regularity-loss budget for all coefficient derivatives used here.

The response manuscript does contain a conditional finite-order calculus, but it assumes the response-scale and triangular recovery certificates (S1)–(S3). Its actual nonconjugate positive theorem is source-specific and stops at second order for a radial invariant assembly. It explicitly states that:

- all-seed (S1) is not proved;
- eventwise (S3) is not proved;
- raw terminal closure is not proved;
- no moving-family flow graph domain is proved;
- no moving-family symbol theorem is proved;
- continuous time is retained only as a fixed-table input plus obstructions.

Those qualifications exclude exactly the general response mechanism used in the HJB corrector hierarchy.

### 2.2 The cross-paper citation mechanism conceals nonmatching theorem labels

The macro
```tex
\newcommand{\PaperOne}[2]{Paper 1, \emph{#1}}
```
prints the theorem description and discards the label argument. Consequently the HJB paper can cite identifiers that do not match the current response manuscript without producing an undefined-reference warning.

Examples include the named “suspension Laplace contour,” “smooth deterministic response,” “trace insertion into resolvent words,” and “finite regularity-loss budget.” The current response manuscript uses different statements and, more importantly, attaches recovery certificates to the higher-order results.

This is not a cosmetic cross-reference defect. It obscures the logical status of the principal input.

### 2.3 The main HJB theorem is therefore conditional on an unstated stronger Paper 1

Once the missing Paper 1 results are restored as explicit hypotheses, the paper becomes an abstract conditional homogenization theorem. It is not a first-principles derivation from billiards.

## 3. The prelimit problem is not rigorously constructed

### 3.1 “Prelimit viscosity-duality solution” is not a defined standard solution concept

The main theorem begins with “Let \(u^\varepsilon\) be the prelimit viscosity-duality solution generated by the deterministic slow-fast billiard system.” The paper does not give a complete well-posedness theorem for this object.

The appendix defines a branchwise action graph and writes a branchwise Hamilton–Jacobi identity. This is not enough. One needs:

- a global state space over all billiard fibers;
- a domain for the fast generator;
- a precise treatment of reflection and moving singularity hypersurfaces;
- a definition of sub- and supersolution in the anisotropic duality setting;
- existence and comparison for the prelimit equation;
- stability under branch changes and collision refinements.

The proposition asserting that branch solutions can be concatenated while “ignoring only the canonical zero-flux singular set” does not establish a global viscosity solution.

### 3.2 The cotangent-dependent moving table creates a coupled singular PDE, not an ordinary frozen family

The billiard parameter is \(\Theta(x,D_xU^\varepsilon)\). Thus the fast vector field and its singularity set depend on the gradient of the unknown. When a perturbed test is inserted, the gradient also depends on the microscopic state through correctors.

The appendix says this is a single vector field on a universal bundle. That is a useful formal viewpoint, but the necessary regularity and well-posedness do not follow from saying so. Differentiating this composition generates precisely the moving-boundary and transverse terms that Paper 1 does not control unconditionally.

## 4. The residual lemma is a statement of the desired estimate, not a proof

The central lemma asserts
\[
\mathcal L^\varepsilon\varphi^\varepsilon
=
\partial_t\varphi+\mathcal H(x,D\varphi,D^2\varphi)
+R^\varepsilon_\varphi,
\qquad
|\langle R^\varepsilon_\varphi,\zeta\rangle|
\le \omega_M(\varepsilon)\|\zeta\|_{\mathcal B^\#}.
\]

The proof then says, in substance:

- the first cell equation cancels the \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) terms;
- the invariant projection gives \(D\) and \(H\);
- the second cell equation cancels the centered order-one part;
- every remaining term has a small factor;
- Paper 1 supplies the required bounds.

This is not an adequate proof of a singular homogenization theorem. The manuscript must display the full residual, with every term, source/target space, power of \(\varepsilon\), derivative loss, moving-face trace, and high-strip tail. It must then prove a uniform estimate for each term from actual Paper 1 theorems.

At present the lemma merely packages the missing analysis into the symbol \(\omega_M(\varepsilon)\).

## 5. The contact-selection lemma is invalid as written

The manuscript argues that prelimit contact points may be chosen outside the singular strata because the iterated grazing and multiple-collision sets have zero Liouville flux measure.

A pointwise maximum can lie on a measure-zero set. A sequence of maxima can be trapped in such a set. Measure-zero information does not imply the existence of nearby regular maximizing points at which the prelimit viscosity inequality is valid.

Subtracting the microscopic value of the corrector fixes a gauge; it does not solve this problem.

A valid proof would require a specific regularization/penalization theorem or a viscosity notion whose test inequality is formulated after averaging against admissible densities. The manuscript has neither. This is a central gap, not a technicality.

## 6. Pairing a residual is not enough for a pointwise viscosity limit

The subsolution proof obtains
\[
\partial_t\varphi+\mathcal H(\cdots)
\le |\langle R^\varepsilon_\varphi,\zeta_\varepsilon\rangle|.
\]

Where does the admissible density \(\zeta_\varepsilon\) come from at a pointwise contact, and why does the prelimit viscosity inequality reduce to this pairing? The manuscript moves between:

- a branchwise pointwise Hamilton–Jacobi equation;
- a distributional fast generator;
- anisotropic pairings;
- half-relaxed limits in macroscopic variables;
- Liouville-a.e. microscopic identification.

These are different notions. A rigorous theorem must define the topology and solution concept in which they are compatible. The current paper assumes that compatibility.

The final claim that paired convergence plus a countable rectangle-density basis yields Liouville-a.e. statewise convergence is also unsupported. Weak convergence against a countable family does not generally imply pointwise convergence without a proved oscillation/compactness theorem.

## 7. The effective coefficients are underdefined

In the main text,
\[
H(x,p)=\langle V(x,p,\cdot)\rangle+\text{“the finite-response work and corrector contributions.”}
\]
A theorem cannot use prose as a coefficient definition. Every contribution must be displayed.

Similarly, the second corrector is defined as the solution of the cell equation whose right-hand side is “the centered second-order transport/action residual.” That is circular until the residual is explicitly given.

The coefficient smoothness, symmetry, and comparison modulus are then asserted from imported response estimates. Because the coefficient formula is not fully specified, these assertions cannot be audited.

## 8. The comparison proof does not close the global assumptions

The comparison argument is standard once the operator is continuous and degenerate elliptic with the required global modulus. The paper proves only compact-jet estimates derived from the finite-response port. In the doubled-variable argument the common gradient
\[
p_\lambda=\lambda(x_\lambda-y_\lambda)
\]
need not remain in a fixed compact jet set.

A global saturation might repair this, but then the paper must state and prove the exact global modulus after saturation. It cannot invoke a compact-window estimate inside a global comparison proof.

The claimed Hilbert factorization estimate for the Green–Kubo tensor also needs a theorem. Positive semidefiniteness alone does not supply all the spatial regularity used in the Ishii trace bound.

## 9. The nonconvexity example is engineered, not derived

The port is permitted to include
\[
V(x,p,z)=V_0-\lambda(x)\eta(z)(p\cdot e)^2
+\kappa(x)\eta(z)(p\cdot e)^4+\cdots
\]
with \(\lambda,\kappa>0\). The direct negative quadratic term is then chosen to dominate all response corrections.

This shows that one can prescribe a nonconvex microscopic action read-out. It does not show that billiard dynamics generate nonconvexity. The distinction should be explicit in the title and theorem.

The statement that changing the sign of the microscopic response direction yields the opposite Hamiltonian defect is not generally justified: even-order terms do not change sign, and the full response corrections need not transform in the asserted way.

## 10. The “theta-expectation” conclusion adds little beyond the PDE semigroup

Once the HJB equation is well posed, defining
\[
\mathcal E_t^\theta[\varphi](x)=u(0,x)
\]
is standard. Monotonicity, constant preservation, and time consistency are ordinary properties of the solution semigroup. Non-subadditivity follows from a suitable local generator defect under sufficient regularity.

This terminology may be useful, but it is not a theorem commensurate with the advertised derivation. The mathematical burden lies entirely in the billiard-to-HJB limit, which remains unproved.

## 11. Relation to existing theory

Deterministic homogenization of chaotic fast–slow systems is well developed; for example, Kelly–Melbourne derive diffusion limits under precise weak-invariance-principle hypotheses. A new HJB result with feedback through the gradient and moving billiard singularities would be significant, but only if the new singular and nonlinear closure is actually proved.

The fixed-table spectral theory of Baladi–Demers–Liverani and perturbation theory of Demers–Zhang do not automatically supply the parameter-uniform moving-flow calculus used here. The paper must not combine them as if they did.

## 12. Minimum requirements for a new submission

A new paper would need all of the following:

1. a precisely defined global prelimit equation and solution concept;
2. existence/comparison for that prelimit;
3. a verified Paper 1 theorem with exactly the moving-flow and response estimates used;
4. a complete displayed residual identity;
5. term-by-term anisotropic estimates with explicit losses;
6. a rigorous regular-contact selection theorem;
7. compactness/tightness sufficient for the claimed topology;
8. an explicit formula for \(D\) and \(H\);
9. one concrete billiard/port family satisfying every hypothesis;
10. a title and abstract that distinguish prescribed port nonconvexity from emergent billiard effects.

## 13. Recommendation

The principal theorem rests on an unavailable upstream interface and on unproved prelimit, residual, and contact-selection statements. These are the core mathematical problems, not removable gaps.

**Recommendation: reject.**
