# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** *Representation Calculus for theta-Expectations*  
**Source reviewed:** `main.tex`, theorem inventory, and referee-risk memo  
**Repository state reviewed:** `main` at commit `08aac05e51577bcd2f2ec5a6aef2a960a8d3ef9d`  
**Standard applied:** Annals of Mathematics / Inventiones Mathematicae / Journal of the AMS / Acta Mathematica  
**Recommendation:** **Reject**

## 1. Summary

The paper takes as input a nonlinear HJB semigroup from the companion paper and, after fixing a terminal payoff, defines a payoff-dependent linearized operator. It then records:

- a linearity obstruction to one payoff-independent martingale law;
- a formal first-variation equation;
- a diffusion representation of the linearized equation;
- a decoupled BSDE obtained by Itô's formula;
- a Hamiltonian-shift identity called a Cameron–Martin–Girsanov formula;
- a robust-pricing stress envelope.

The smooth, uniformly elliptic part is standard. The only potentially nontrivial extension—the degenerate regularization theorem—is not proved and is false under the stated hypotheses. The paper also depends entirely on an HJB theorem that is not established in the companion manuscript.

There is no result here remotely at the level of the four journals named above.

## 2. Major mathematical objections

### 2.1 The linearization theorem assumes the conclusion

The theorem states:

> If \(u^\delta=u+\delta v+o(\delta)\) locally uniformly, then \(v\) solves the linearized PDE.

This is a formal differentiation result. It assumes the first-order expansion whose existence is the substantive theorem.

To prove differentiability of the nonlinear semigroup one would need conditions ensuring:

- differentiability of the solution map with respect to terminal data;
- control of gradients and Hessians;
- uniqueness in the linearized equation's class;
- a remainder estimate uniform in the relevant cylinder.

None of this is supplied. The theorem cannot be advertised as a representation of the derivative semigroup unless the derivative has first been proved to exist.

### 2.2 Locally uniform convergence does not imply convergence of linearized coefficients

The “degenerate representation by viscosity regularization” is the weakest point. It assumes only that smooth uniformly parabolic solutions \(u^\varepsilon\) converge locally uniformly to a viscosity solution \(u\), and then claims that the calibrated linear diffusion semigroups converge to the derivative representation of the degenerate nonlinear semigroup.

This does not follow.

The calibrated coefficients contain
\[
D(x,\nabla u^\varepsilon),\qquad
\partial_pH(x,\nabla u^\varepsilon),\qquad
\partial_pD(x,\nabla u^\varepsilon):\nabla^2u^\varepsilon.
\]
Local uniform convergence of \(u^\varepsilon\) gives no convergence of \(\nabla u^\varepsilon\) or \(\nabla^2u^\varepsilon\). The Hessian term can blow up or oscillate. Viscosity stability identifies the limit of the nonlinear equations; it does not identify limits of their derivatives or of payoff-linearized semigroups.

A valid theorem would require strong uniform regularity, compactness of the gradients/Hessians, differentiability of the limiting semigroup, and convergence of the associated martingale problems. None is assumed.

### 2.3 The smooth diffusion result is classical Feynman–Kac/martingale-problem theory

Once a smooth payoff \(u\) is fixed and
\[
\mathcal L_t^u f
=
\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(a^u\nabla^2f)+b^u\cdot\nabla f
\]
has regular uniformly elliptic coefficients, the representation of the linearized PDE by the diffusion generated by \(\mathcal L^u\) is standard.

The payoff dependence is not a new theorem; it is the usual dependence of a linearized operator on the base solution. The manuscript does not prove new well-posedness, regularity, or probabilistic structure for this diffusion.

### 2.4 The BSDE theorem is only Itô's formula along a preselected decoupling field

The forward diffusion is defined using \(u\), and then \(Y_t=u(t,X_t)\) and \(Z_t=\sigma^\top\nabla u\) are substituted into Itô's formula. This yields a decoupled BSDE.

This is not a probabilistic construction of \(u\), because \(u\) is already known and enters the coefficients of the forward equation and the driver. It is not a coupled FBSDE theorem and supplies no existence/uniqueness result independent of the PDE.

The inverse relation \(p=(\sigma^\top)^{-1}Z\) also requires nondegeneracy and regularity that exclude the degenerate regimes emphasized elsewhere.

### 2.5 The claimed Cameron–Martin–Girsanov formula is not a Girsanov theorem

The theorem defines
\[
D^h(x,p)=D(x,p+h),\qquad
H^h(x,p)=H(x,p+h)-H(x,h),
\]
then adds and subtracts the unshifted terms to obtain an algebraic defect
\[
\Lambda_h
=
\operatorname{Tr}((D(x,p+h)-D(x,p))X)
+H(x,p+h)-H(x,h)-H(x,p).
\]

This is an identity between two PDE operators. It does not provide:

- a change of probability measure;
- a Radon–Nikodym density;
- an exponential martingale;
- Novikov/Kazamaki conditions;
- equivalence or absolute continuity of path laws;
- a Cameron–Martin space;
- a transformed Brownian motion.

Calling it a “semigroup-level Cameron–Martin formula” and later a “Cameron–Martin–Girsanov formula” is materially misleading. In the linear-quadratic special case an algebraic cross term resembles a drift tilt; that does not turn the general identity into a Girsanov theorem.

### 2.6 The linearity obstruction is elementary and overstated

Subtracting the martingale identities for \(f\), \(g\), and \(f+g\) shows that a single linear expectation law has an additive generator on its test domain. This is immediate.

The proof as written also says that varying the initial point yields additivity on the test class. That requires the family of laws to have adequate support and to be specified for all initial points; the theorem's first sentence mentions only “a single probability law.” The statement should be corrected even as an elementary lemma.

### 2.7 The carre-du-champ is merely the standard one for the frozen linear operator

Once \(\mathcal L^u\) is fixed, its carré du champ is
\[
\Gamma^u(f,g)=\tfrac12\nabla f^\top a^u\nabla g.
\]
There is no new nonlinear carré-du-champ calculus here. The object is payoff-dependent because the coefficients are payoff-dependent.

### 2.8 The robust-pricing section is a definition, not a theorem

Taking a supremum over a compact family of shifted PDE solutions defines a stress envelope. The proposition then says each member has the same calibrated representation under the previous assumptions. This is an illustration, not research-level mathematics.

### 2.9 The entire paper depends on an unproved companion theorem

The only connection to billiards is the input assumption that Paper 2 has already constructed and justified the HJB semigroup. The HJB manuscript does not prove that result. Therefore this paper currently has no established deterministic-billiard application.

Even if Paper 2 were correct, the present paper would remain a collection of standard consequences in a very strong smooth representation window.

## 3. Novelty assessment

The paper cites the appropriate classical sources: Stroock–Varadhan/martingale problems, viscosity theory, Pardoux–Peng BSDEs, and standard stochastic calculus. The results in the manuscript are direct applications of those theories after a payoff is frozen.

For a top journal, one would expect a theorem such as:

- differentiability of a degenerate nonconvex HJB semigroup in a sharp function space;
- a new probabilistic representation valid without a smooth decoupling field;
- convergence of regularized derivative semigroups under minimal assumptions;
- a genuine nonlinear Cameron–Martin/Girsanov theorem;
- a representation tied nontrivially to the deterministic microscopic billiard.

No such theorem is present.

## 4. Internal overclaims and terminology

The paper should not say that “each sufficiently regular payoff determines” a regularized diffusion representation unless differentiability and the regularization limit have been proved.

The phrase “represents the already-derived semigroup” is also inaccurate. The diffusion represents the first variation at a fixed payoff, not the nonlinear semigroup value itself.

The main package lists a “semigroup-level Cameron–Martin–Girsanov formula,” but the corresponding theorem is only an operator identity.

## 5. What would be required for a serious new paper

A viable project could focus on one genuinely nontrivial problem:

1. prove Fréchet or Gâteaux differentiability of the terminal-data solution map for the relevant nonconvex, possibly degenerate quasilinear HJB equation;
2. identify the derivative as the unique solution of a linearized PDE;
3. prove convergence of uniformly parabolic regularized derivative semigroups;
4. establish tightness and convergence of their calibrated diffusions;
5. state a genuine change-of-measure theorem, with an explicit density and integrability conditions, if “Girsanov” remains in the title.

Without such work, the manuscript is at most an expository appendix to the HJB paper.

## 6. Recommendation

The manuscript's correct smooth statements are standard; its degenerate statement is unsupported; its Girsanov terminology is inaccurate; and its only application depends on an unproved companion theorem.

**Recommendation: reject.**
