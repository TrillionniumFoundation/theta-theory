# Executive verdict

Round Forty Three is a substantial improvement over the non-materialized Round-Forty-One object. There is now an actual TeX manuscript, with a readable theorem chain and several genuinely useful ideas. In particular:

- the finite-dimensional experiment now has a finite-prefix exploration condition rather than a merely asymptotic density condition;

- the compact response-class strong-law argument is much better than the former uncountable `L^2`-net step;

- the response-to-moment generating identity and finite Gram reconstruction are constructive;

Those improvements do not make the present frozen object acceptable.


# 3. Genuine improvements and mathematically promising ideas

A harsh report should distinguish defects from progress. The following changes are meaningful.

## 3.1 Finite-prefix calibration

The condition

```text
N_cal(m) >= rho m - C_cal,  1 <= m <= n,
```

and the exact count

```text
W_n = floor(N_cal(n)/6) >= rho n/6 - C_win
```

repair the delayed-calibration defect in the earlier triangular policy class. The duration is selected before the fresh sign, so the conditional cross term vanishes at the correct sigma-field. The radial compactification and fixed-net Azuma argument are plausible and materially better than the earlier proof.

## 3.2 Compact response-class strong law

The appendix now uses a finite net in `C([0,T])` and controls an entire net ball by

```text
||f-g||_infinity * n^{-1} sum |xi_i|.
```

This is the correct mechanism for passing from finitely many strong laws to a compact sup-norm response class. It avoids the former unjustified leap from a fixed-function maximal estimate to an uncountable `L^2` supremum.

## 3.3 Full compact-space posterior LDP

Once one has the uniform likelihood limit

```text
sup_beta | n^{-1} log(L_n(beta)/L_n(beta0)) + I_beta0(beta) | -> 0
```

and an `o(n)` exact-likelihood correction, the posterior LDP on a compact parameter space follows by elementary Laplace upper and lower bounds and full support of the prior. The proof in `thm:jacobi-rate` is structurally convincing for the undifferentiated logarithmic-washout experiment.

## 3.4 Constructive response-to-Jacobi map

The generating identity in `lem:response-moment-triangularity`, the finite Hankel systems, and the formulas for monic norms and Jacobi coefficients form a useful finite algorithm. This is substantially more informative than a bare compactness modulus.

## 3.5 Predictable-intercept Rademacher orthogonalization

The decomposition

```text
Delta_i(beta) = P_i(beta) + a S_i [h_beta(t_i)-h_beta0(t_i)]
```

with `P_i` measurable before `S_i` gives

```text
E[Delta_i(beta)^2 | past, design] = P_i(beta)^2 + a^2 response_difference^2.
```

This is a clean observation. It explains why arbitrary bounded baseline actions need not destroy an exploration information floor. With a fully detailed uniform empirical-process proof, this mechanism could support a worthwhile specialist contribution.

These positives are why I read the manuscript beyond the repository failure. They do not close the objections below.

# 4. Major mathematical objection M1: the effective inverse constant is wrong

## 4.1 The two source estimates

`prop:quantified-moment-map` states, with `M=2J+2`, a response-derivative-to-moment Lipschitz factor

```text
Q^{4(M+1)^2} = Q^{4(2J+3)^2}.
```

`prop:effective-gram-reconstruction` states a moment-to-coefficient factor

```text
Q^{8(J+1)^3}.
```

Their composition therefore yields the exponent

```text
E_J = 4(2J+3)^2 + 8(J+1)^3.
```

The manuscript nevertheless defines

```text
L_J = Q^{12(J+1)^3}
```

and asserts in `thm:effective-response-jet-audit` that

```text
4(2J+3)^2 + 8(J+1)^3 <= 12(J+1)^3,
```

apart from supposedly harmless cases `J=0,1`.

## 4.2 Direct arithmetic contradiction

The relevant values are:

| `J` | `4(2J+3)^2` | `8(J+1)^3` | actual summed exponent | claimed exponent `12(J+1)^3` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 36 | 8 | 44 | 12 |
| 1 | 100 | 64 | 164 | 96 |
| 2 | 196 | 216 | 412 | 324 |
| 3 | 324 | 512 | 836 | 768 |
| 4 | 484 | 1000 | 1484 | 1500 |

The inequality fails for `J=0,1,2,3`, not only `J=0,1`.

The phrase “after enlarging the harmless cases by the standing factor `Q>=16`” does not repair this. For `Q>1`, one cannot infer `Q^44 <= Q^12`, `Q^164 <= Q^96`, or the analogous inequalities by making `Q` larger. One would need an additional exponent or a separately declared multiplicative constant.

## 4.3 Internal inconsistency with the shorter proof

The earlier proof of `lem:effective-response-jet` in `quantitative_jacobi.tex` gives the moment step the much smaller exponent `Q^{4(J+1)^2}`. The later line-addressable section gives `Q^{4(2J+3)^2}`. These are not the same estimate. The second is the one obtained by substituting `M=2J+2` into the displayed quantified proposition.

Thus the article contains two incompatible condition propagations for the same map.

## 4.4 Downstream damage

The error is not confined to one decorative constant. The too-small `L_J` enters:

- `lem:effective-response-jet`;
- `thm:effective-response-jet-audit`;
- the scale choice `s_J(delta)`;

A larger valid `L_J` would make the inverse modulus smaller and alter all displayed finite-sample thresholds. The qualitative existence of some effective finite-depth modulus is plausible, but the stated quantitative theorem is not proved.

# 5. Major mathematical objection M2: the washout derivative estimate omits the Duhamel polynomial

The logarithmic-washout section states, for every fixed cylinder derivative,

```text
sup |partial^alpha eta_i^beta(z)|
    <= C_alpha (i+1)^{-1-epsilon_w}.
```

Repeated parameter differentiation of a semigroup generally gives an estimate of the form

```text
C_alpha (1+w_i)^{|alpha|} exp(-lambda_* w_i),
```

or the same expression with a carefully tracked derivative stability rate. Since

```text
w_i = ((1+epsilon_w)/lambda_*) log(i+1),
```

this produces a logarithmic polynomial factor. A valid convenient bound is of the form

```text
C_alpha (1+log(i+1))^{|alpha|} (i+1)^{-1-epsilon_w}
<= C_{alpha,bar_epsilon} (i+1)^{-1-bar_epsilon}
```

for `0<bar_epsilon<epsilon_w`, provided the same stability rate has been justified.

The omitted factor does not destroy the undifferentiated LDP: for `alpha=0`, the summable residual estimate is available. It does invalidate the displayed all-fixed-order derivative envelope and any ledger entry claiming that bookkeeping has been completed as written.

Again, the failed finalizer attempts exactly this correction. The current test suite also explicitly rejects the frozen formula. That is compelling evidence that the submitted source is an acknowledged pre-correction state.

A revised proof must additionally distinguish the base semigroup decay rate from the decay rate after parameter differentiation. If a smaller rate `lambda_alpha` is used, the washout coefficient must be chosen to preserve summability at the required derivative order.

# 6. Major mathematical objection M3: the confidence-cylinder grid condition is impossible as stated

The multiscale diagnostic cells are defined by

```text
q=(J,s,k),  1 <= k <= R_J.
```

Thus each scale has exactly `R_J` positive-time diagnostic cells. The finite-grid lemma uses the algebraic grid `k=0,...,R_J`, but explicitly observes that `g(0)=0` is known and only the positive grid points require measurement.

Both `thm:honest-jacobi-cylinders` and `thm:linear-time-honest-cylinders` then require the finite cell set `Q` to contain the `R_J+1` grid cells. Under the defined cell family, there is no `k=0` cell. Literally, no such `Q` exists.

The likely intended statement is:

> `Q` contains the `R_J` positive-time cells, while the origin is supplied deterministically by `g(0)=0`.

That is easy to repair, but until repaired the coefficient-diameter implication has an impossible antecedent and is vacuous as written. The coverage bound for the response set may still be valid; the advertised finite-sample coefficient diameter is not correctly stated.

# 8. Major mathematical objection M5: the growing-depth theorem lacks the required finite-n empirical-process estimates

`thm:growing-depth-recovery` is supported by a one-paragraph proof. The proof says that a `k_n/16` net, Gaussian concentration, and interpolation yield uniform likelihood error below `k_n/8`, and that a response ball supplies the denominator. At fixed separation this is standard. At shrinking `k_n` and growing depth it is the main theorem and requires actual estimates.

At minimum the article needs a proposition of the following type, with constants uniform over the declared experiment class:

```text
P{ sup_beta |empirical score/square error| > c k }
<= C exp{ H(k/16) - c' n k^2 },
```

plus a precise interpolation bound, a treatment of the empirical first moment of `|xi_i|`, and a denominator inequality showing how the product-prior lower bound enters. It must also state whether the probability estimate is uniform over horizon-dependent policies and how the triangular experiments are coupled when an almost-sure assertion is used.

The current proof does not derive such an inequality. It merely names the ingredients. This is insufficient for a theorem whose novelty is precisely the growing-depth quantitative regime.

There is also a sign error in the prose introducing `mathcal P`: a coordinate-box lower bound on prior mass gives an **upper** bound on minus the log prior mass, not a lower bound. The later denominator argument appears to use the intended upper bound, but the statement should be corrected.

The displayed concrete regime may well follow once the finite-n lemma is supplied. It is not currently established at top-journal proof standard.

# 9. Major mathematical objection M6: the linear-time uniform theorem is underproved

The predictable-intercept identity itself is sound and valuable. The passage from that identity to an almost-sure posterior exponent uniformly over arbitrary bounded adaptive baselines is not proved with comparable care.

The source asserts that compactness of two response images supplies finite deterministic nets for the class of policy-dependent fields `Delta_i(beta)`, uniformly in `i` and the policy. To make this rigorous, the paper should define the controlling pseudometric explicitly, for example

```text
d_*(beta,gamma)
 = U_* ||g_beta-g_gamma||_{L^1}
   + a ||h_beta-h_gamma||_infinity,
```

show that one parameter net controls every bounded history-dependent convolution, and then prove uniform concentration for:

- the centered conditional quadratic field;
- the Gaussian score field;
- their interpolation errors;
- the exact integrated initial-state correction;
- triangular or horizon-dependent policy sequences.

The manuscript instead says “Azuma--Hoeffding and Gaussian exponential bounds, followed by finite-net interpolation, give on one event” and writes the limits. That is an outline, not a proof of the uniform theorem as stated.

The denominator argument is plausible because `d_*`-continuity gives a policy-uniform neighbourhood of the truth. It too should be stated as a separate lemma with a uniform finite-n bound.

The theorem proves only a guaranteed closed-set posterior upper exponent. It does not prove a matching lower bound or identify the actual adaptive rate function, because the predictable baseline may add parameter-dependent information. The manuscript acknowledges this in a remark, but the distinction must remain prominent in the abstract, title-level claims, and significance discussion.

# 10. Assessment of the individual theorem packages

## 10.1 The five-parameter BvM theorem

The finite-prefix repair, common force/duration bounds, and duration-before-sign chronology address the most serious earlier objections. The abstract nonlinear Gaussian theorem, global contrast, local expansion, and relative `L^1` Laplace lemma form a plausible route to a random-information BvM without convergence of `I_n`.

I do not identify a comparably explicit fatal error in this finite-dimensional package. Some null-history and triangular-policy measurability details could be expanded, but the principal issue is significance rather than basic correctness.

At a four-journal level, however, this remains a regular five-dimensional Gaussian LAN/BvM result under deliberately engineered exploration and uniform exponential stability. It is a competent application, not a transformative theorem.

## 10.2 Strong filter jets and memory push-forward

The deterministic separable subspaces generated by jet evaluations are a responsible repair of the former strong-measurability ambiguity. The concentrated push-forward lemma is plausible, and the extra test derivative is used to obtain norm continuity in the support point.

This section is technically useful but subsidiary. It inherits the finite-dimensional BvM and does not create an infinite-dimensional Gaussian limit; the state image has rank at most five.

## 10.3 Full posterior LDP under logarithmic washout

This is the strongest clean statistical statement in the paper. Conditional on the undifferentiated summable correction, compactness plus uniform likelihood convergence does give a good LDP with the displayed response rate. The open-set lower bound is not merely asserted; the denominator normalization and positive prior mass are written down.

The theorem should be separated from the flawed high-order derivative claim. The base LDP does not need the false all-derivative envelope.

Conceptually, however, the LDP is a Laplace consequence of a uniform law of large numbers on a compact forward image. That is worthwhile, but the manuscript overstates its depth relative to general Bayesian inverse-problem and posterior-LDP methods.

## 10.4 Effective inverse stability

The response-moment and Gram algorithms are useful. The currently advertised constants are not valid because of M1, and the condition audit is incomplete because of M4. No claim of “no hidden modulus” is justified until every constant used downstream is recomputed and checked against the actual finite maps.

## 10.5 Weighted operator recovery

The result concerns

```text
|| W_tau (J_beta-J_beta0) W_tau ||_op,
```

not the ordinary operator norm. The exponential weights suppress all remote coefficients, so on the bounded tridiagonal coefficient product this topology is close in spirit to a metrization of coordinatewise convergence. The result should always be called **exponentially weighted operator-norm recovery**, not simply operator-norm recovery.

There is no unweighted operator-norm recovery of the complete bath and no recovery theorem for the full spectral measure in a strong metric.

## 10.6 Confidence cylinders

The response sets are frequentist confidence sets obtained from martingale/sub-Gaussian inequalities and deterministic inversion. They are not Bayesian credible sets, posterior coverage theorems, or Bernstein--von Mises confidence sets for a growing infinite-dimensional parameter.

The coverage mechanism is plausible. The coefficient-diameter implication has the grid-count defect M3 and uses the invalid `L_J` from M1. It therefore cannot presently support the abstract's advertised coefficient-cylinder conclusion.

# 11. Top-four significance assessment

Even assuming all correctable gaps are repaired, the package remains below the threshold of the four named journals.

## 11.1 Classical identification engine

The Weyl/Schur recovery of a Jacobi matrix from its boundary spectral data is classical, as the manuscript acknowledges. The new work is statistical wrapping, effective finite-depth bookkeeping, and adaptive experimental chronology.

## 11.2 The LDP is structurally elementary after uniform convergence

On a compact parameter space, a uniform log-likelihood limit and full-support prior yield the posterior LDP by a direct Laplace argument. The real work is establishing the uniform limit and transient correction. That work is useful, but the final theorem does not amount to a new general theory of infinite-dimensional posterior large deviations.

## 11.3 No sharp inverse theory or optimal rate

The effective modulus is deliberately enormous. The article gives no matching lower bound, minimax result, optimality statement, or demonstration that the depth dependence reflects the true instability of the Jacobi inverse problem. The concrete regime

```text
J_n = o((log n)^{1/5})
```

is sufficient, not shown necessary or close to optimal.

## 11.4 Weak global topology

The coefficient product topology learns every fixed finite block, but permits arbitrary disagreement in the remote tail. The weighted operator norm similarly suppresses the remote tail. The paper does not recover the full operator in ordinary operator norm, a strong resolvent metric with quantitative spectral consequences, or a nontrivial spectral-measure loss at an established rate.

## 11.5 The adaptive theorem is an information floor, not an optimal design theorem

The no-washout theorem allows reward-seeking or stabilizing baselines and proves that fresh-sign exploration preserves a universal information floor. It does not optimize the exploration fraction, characterize the additional information supplied by the baseline, prove a matching adaptive LDP, establish regret/inference tradeoffs, or solve an optimal experimental-design problem.

## 11.6 No infinite-dimensional BvM or Bayesian uncertainty theorem

The infinite model has posterior concentration/LDP and externally constructed confidence sets. It does not have an infinite-dimensional BvM theorem, posterior credible-set coverage, semiparametric efficiency for growing coordinates, or a Gaussian limit for a nontrivial infinite-dimensional functional.

## 11.7 The article remains overpacked

The manuscript combines:

- an abstract finite-dimensional adaptive BvM;
- a five-parameter physical application;
- filter derivatives;
- a memory-kernel push-forward;
- inverse Jacobi reconstruction;
- a posterior LDP;
- effective moment/Gram inversion;
- growing-depth contraction;
- two experimental clocks;
- confidence sets.

Several proofs are compressed to sketches because too many theorem packages are being carried in one short article. A specialist paper focused on one coherent contribution would be stronger and more credible.
