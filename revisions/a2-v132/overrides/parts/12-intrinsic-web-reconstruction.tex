\section{The deepest normal cone and generic reconstruction of the web}
\label{sec:intrinsic-web-reconstruction}

The polarized Jacobian retains only one of two irreducible components
of the Pluecker vector of a web.  The full failure scheme retains
both.  In this section we recover these two projective components
intrinsically and prove that they determine the web on a nonempty
invariant open.  Thus the deeper boundary separates the generic
finite ambiguity of the polarized-K3 invariant.  The normal cone
used here is along the deepest Schubert stratum, not along the
reduction of the failure scheme.

Throughout this section \(\dim V=4\), \(W=\Sym^2V\), and
\(E=\bigwedge^4W\).  There is a multiplicity-free decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-web-components}
 E=E_{175}\oplus E_{35},\qquad
 E_{175}=\mathbb S_{(4,3,1,0)}V,\qquad
 E_{35}=\mathbb S_{(5,1,1,1)}V
       \simeq\det V\otimes\Sym^4V.
\end{equation}
Write \(P\) and \(Q\) for its two equivariant projections, in
that order.  For a web \(R\), write \([w_R]\in\Pj(E)\) for
its Pluecker point.  On the locus where both projections are nonzero,
put
\[
 \mathscr L_R=\Pj\bigl(\C Pw_R\oplus\C Qw_R\bigr).
\]
The quadratic ideal of \(\Gr(4,W)\) is denoted by
\(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Pl},2}\).  Each of its quadrics satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plucker-linear-quotient}
 F(aPw_R+bQw_R)=(b-a)\ell_{F,R}(a,b),
 \qquad F\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Pl},2},
\end{equation}
for a linear form \(\ell_{F,R}\), since \(w_R\) is decomposable.
Define \(G_4^{\mathrm{rec}}\subset G_4^\circ\) by the conditions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reconstruction-open}
 Pw_R\ne0,\quad Qw_R\ne0,\quad
 \dim\langle\ell_{F,R}:F\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{Pl},2}\rangle=2.
\end{equation}
These conditions are independent of a representative of \([w_R]\)
and invariant under \(\PGL(V)\).

\begin{theorem}[Generic intrinsic reconstruction of the web]
\label{thm:generic-intrinsic-web}
The set \(G_4^{\mathrm{rec}}\) is a nonempty invariant open in
\(G_4^\circ\).  Let \(R\in G_4^{\mathrm{rec}}\) and
\(R'\in G_4^\circ\).  Every isomorphism of complex schemes
\[
 \widehat D_R\simeq\widehat D_{R'}
\]
implies \(R'=gR\) for some \(g\in\PGL(V)\).  In particular,
the abstract nonreduced failure scheme determines the cube-zero
algebra \(B_R\), up to isomorphism, on this open.

More precisely, the normal cone of \(\widehat D_R\) along its
intrinsically determined deepest Schubert stratum recovers
\(([Pw_R],[Qw_R])\), up to the same projective change of coordinates
in \(V\).  The webs with this pair of projective components are
exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-plucker-pencil}
 \bigl(\mathscr L_R\cap\Gr(4,W)\bigr)
 \setminus\{[Pw_R],[Qw_R]\}.
\end{equation}
On \(G_4^{\mathrm{rec}}\), this intersection is the single
reduced point \([w_R]\), before taking projective-coordinate
orbits.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The intrinsic deepest stratum and its oriented tensor cone}

For clarity, set
\[
 \Sigma_r(R)=\{K\in\Gr(4,V\oplus S_R):
                    \rank(K\to V)\le r\},\quad 0\le r\le3,
\]
with reduced structure.  Thus \(\Sigma_3(R)=\widehat\Delta_R\)
and \(\Sigma_0(R)=\Gr(4,S_R)\).

\begin{lemma}[The deepest normal cone]
\label{lem:deepest-normal-cone}
Starting with the reduction of \(\widehat D_R\), three iterations
of taking the reduced singular locus give \(\Sigma_0(R)\).
Consequently \(\Sigma_0(R)\) and
\(\mathscr C_R=C_{\Sigma_0(R)/\widehat D_R}\) are intrinsic.
Let \(\mathcal K_0\) denote the tautological rank-four bundle on
\(\Sigma_0(R)\).  The ambient vector bundle determined by the degree-one part is
\(\Hom(\mathcal K_0,V)\).  At a point \(K_0\subset S_R\),
choose bases and write \(T:K_0\to V\).  The fibre cone is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deepest-cone-equation}
 \Spec\C[t_{ij}]/\bigl(d(T)J_R(T)\bigr),\qquad
 d(T)=\det T,\qquad J_R(T)=I_6(\gamma_R\Sym^2T).
\end{equation}
Its ideal is generated in degree sixteen and its reduction is the
determinant hypersurface.  The two rulings of its rank-one locus
are intrinsically distinguishable over \(\Sigma_0(R)\): the
\(V\)-ruling is the projectively trivial one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a point of projection rank \(r\), invert an \(r\)-minor
and take the Schur complement.  The remaining \((4-r)^2\)
entries are independent coordinates and the rank conditions become
the usual matrix rank conditions, times smooth auxiliary coordinates.
The Jacobian criterion for minors gives
\[
 (\operatorname{Sing}\Sigma_r(R))_{\mathrm{red}}
       =\Sigma_{r-1}(R),\qquad 1\le r\le3.
\]
This also follows directly by differentiating the minors: the
rank-\(r\) stratum is smooth, whereas at rank at most \(r-1\)
the tangent dimension exceeds the local dimension.  Since reduction
and reduced singular locus are preserved by abstract isomorphisms,
\(\Sigma_0(R)\) is intrinsic.

Choose a splitting \(S_R=K_0\oplus S'\).  A Grassmann chart
near \(K_0\) consists of graphs
\[
 (T,B):K_0\longrightarrow V\oplus S'.
\]
The deepest stratum is \(T=0\); \(B\) provides its eight
smooth coordinates.  The Fitting factorization gives precisely
\(d(T)J_R(T)\), independently of \(B\).  All generators of
\(J_R\) have degree twelve in \(T\), so passing to the
\((t_{ij})\)-adic associated graded gives the same homogeneous
ideal.  This proves \eqref{eq:deepest-cone-equation}.  There are
no degree-one relations.  The dual of the conormal bundle is therefore
\(\Hom(\mathcal K_0,V)\), as also follows by quotienting the
Grassmannian tangent bundle along \(\Gr(4,S_R)\).  Determinant
completion, or the sharp bound \(d^4\in J_R\), implies
\(\sqrt{dJ_R}=(d)\).

The rank-one subcone is recovered from the reduced determinant cone
by iterated reduced singular loci in each fibre.  Its projectivization
is the relative Segre variety
\[
 \Pj(V)\times\Pj(\mathcal K_0^*)
          \longrightarrow\Sigma_0(R).
\]
The two components of the relative parameter space of its maximal
linear \(\Pj^3\)'s are \(\Pj(V)\times\Sigma_0(R)\)
and \(\Pj(\mathcal K_0^*)\).  To see that these are exactly
the two components, note that a vector space of rank-one tensors
cannot contain both \(v_1\otimes u_1\) and
\(v_2\otimes u_2\) with both pairs independent: their sum has
rank two.  Thus a maximal such space fixes one tensor factor.

The first parameter bundle is projectively trivial.  The second is
not.  Restrict to a Schubert line in \(\Gr(4,6)\), obtained by
varying \(K_0\) between a fixed three-plane and five-plane.  On
this line
\[
 \mathcal K_0^*|_{\Pj^1}\simeq
 \OO_{\Pj^1}^{\oplus3}\oplus\OO_{\Pj^1}(1).
\]
If its projectivization were a trivial \(\Pj^3\)-bundle, the
vector bundle would be a direct sum of four copies of one line
bundle.  This follows by comparing the relative \(\OO(1)\)
on a projective-bundle isomorphism and pushing forward.  Its
determinant degree would then be divisible by four, contrary to the
degree one displayed above.  Triviality is invariant under an
isomorphism of the base.  Since the base is connected, an isomorphism
of the cone cannot interchange these two parameter bundles on some
fibres but not others.  It must preserve the \(V\)-ruling.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the ruling argument is needed]
A single determinant cone of square matrices permits transposition.
The preceding relative argument removes that ambiguity using only
intrinsic data on \(\Sigma_0(R)\); it does not assume that an
isomorphism of failure schemes extends to the ambient Grassmannian.
A linear isomorphism of tensor spaces preserving the two rank-one
rulings has the form \(g\otimes h\).  Indeed, its restrictions
to the two Segre factors are projective linear, and after lifting
those maps the remaining linear map fixes every decomposable line.
Applying it to sums with one common factor shows that its scalar is
constant.  Thus the coordinate change in the recovered \(V\)
factor is an ordinary projective linear change, as required below.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Reading both Pluecker components from the residual ideal}

\begin{lemma}[The two-component readout]
\label{lem:plucker-component-readout}
At any fibre of the oriented cone in
Lemma~\ref{lem:deepest-normal-cone}, its degree-sixteen ideal
recovers the projective pair \(([Pw_R],[Qw_R])\) whenever both
components are nonzero.  It detects the vanishing of either component
as well.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The equation of the reduced cone recovers \(d\) up to a nonzero
scalar.  In the polynomial ring on its degree-one vector space,
multiplication by \(d\) is injective.  Hence the degree-sixteen
ideal, divided by \(d\), recovers precisely \((J_R)_{12}\).
This uses the ideal of the nonreduced cone, not merely its support.

Let \(U=K_0\), and choose a nonzero volume form on \(S_R\).
Its pullback under \(\gamma_R\) is a decomposable vector
\(\beta_R\in\bigwedge^6W^*\), well-defined up to scalar.
The maximal minors of \(\gamma_R\Sym^2T\) are the matrix
coefficients of \(\beta_R\circ\bigwedge^6\Sym^2T\).
The multiplicity-free decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minor-two-summands}
 \bigwedge^6\Sym^2U
 =\mathbb S_{(5,4,2,1)}U\oplus\mathbb S_{(4,4,4,0)}U
\end{equation}
was used in the corank-four calculation.  It also follows from
\eqref{eq:two-web-components} by exterior duality and
\(\det(\Sym^2U)=(\det U)^5\).  Both identities can be
checked by the exterior character and the Schur character formula;
the dimensions are \(175\) and \(35\).  We use the usual
Cauchy decomposition and its multiplicity-one property
\cite{FultonHarris,DCEP}.

In degree twelve, the polynomial functions on \(\Hom(U,V)\)
decompose as
\[
 \Sym^{12}(V^*\otimes U)
 =\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash12}
          \mathbb S_\lambda V^*\otimes\mathbb S_\lambda U.
\]
For each of the two partitions in
\eqref{eq:minor-two-summands}, the coefficient map into this
polynomial ring is nonzero and injective on the corresponding tensor
product.  One way to verify nonvanishing without a scalar convention
is to evaluate \(\mathbb S_\lambda T\) at an isomorphism \(T\).
The coefficient map is equivariant; its source is irreducible for
\(GL(V)\times GL(U)\), so a nonzero such map is injective.
Writing \(\beta_{R,\lambda}\) for its two components therefore
gives the equality of subspaces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minor-coefficient-readout}
 (J_R)_{12}=
 \bigoplus_{\lambda\in\{(5,4,2,1),(4,4,4,0)\}}
 \C\beta_{R,\lambda}\otimes\mathbb S_\lambda U.
\end{equation}
In particular this is a right-\(GL(U)\)-stable subspace, and its
intersection with each Cauchy summand identifies the left coefficient
line.  It is not just an assertion about which representations occur.

Finally exterior duality gives
\[
 \bigwedge^6W^*\simeq\bigwedge^4W\otimes(\det W)^*.
\]
Under this identification \(\beta_R\) is the Pluecker vector of
\(\ker\gamma_R=R\), with a common determinant twist.  The two
left coefficient lines in \eqref{eq:minor-coefficient-readout}
are exactly \([Pw_R]\) and \([Qw_R]\), with their intrinsic
labels.  A basis change in \(U\) does not change them; a basis
change in \(V\) acts on both by the same projective transformation.
The oriented ruling of the preceding lemma ensures that these are
the only changes relevant to an abstract isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A reduced one-point Pluecker pencil}

We give explicit quadrics establishing nonemptiness of
\eqref{eq:reconstruction-open}.  In particular, the generic assertion
below is not inferred from a numerical sample of a Jacobian map.

Choose a basis \(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\) of \(V\), and order the
monomial basis of \(W\) as
\[
 (b_1,\ldots,b_{10})=
 (x_1^2,x_1x_2,x_1x_3,x_1x_4,x_2^2,
 x_2x_3,x_2x_4,x_3^2,x_3x_4,x_4^2).
\]
For increasing indices put \(e_{ijkl}=b_i\wedge b_j\wedge
b_k\wedge b_l\).  Let
\[
 w_*=e_{1,5,8,10}.
\]
The Jacobian map
\[
 C:E\longrightarrow\det V\otimes\Sym^4V,\qquad
 q_1\wedge\cdots\wedge q_4\longmapsto
 \det(\partial q_i/\partial x_j)
\]
is equivariant, with the determinant factor understood in the chosen
basis.  It is nonzero, since \(Cw_*=16x_1x_2x_3x_4\).
Its kernel is \(E_{175}\).

\begin{lemma}[An exact separating pencil]
\label{lem:exact-plucker-pencil}
The projection of \(w_*\) to \(E_{35}\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-projector-witness}
 Qw_*={1\over3}e_{1,5,8,10}
       -{2\over3}e_{1,6,7,9}
       -{2\over3}e_{2,3,6,10}
       +{2\over3}e_{2,4,7,8}
       +{2\over3}e_{3,4,5,9}.
\end{equation}
On the pencil \(aPw_*+bQw_*\), two Pluecker quadrics restrict to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-separating-quadrics}
 -{2\over9}(b-a)(b+2a),\qquad
 -{4\over9}(b-a)^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\mathscr L_*\cap\Gr(4,W)\) is the single
reduced point \([w_*]\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the Fischer Hermitian forms: a degree-\(r\) monomial
\(x^\alpha\) has squared norm \(\alpha!\), and the exterior
basis has the induced product norm.  The determinant factor has norm
one.  These forms are unitary invariant.  Hence the adjoint \(C^*\)
is unitary equivariant and, by complexification, is \(GL(V)\)-
equivariant.  Schur's lemma gives \(CC^*=c\,\mathrm{id}\).
The only exterior basis vectors contributing to the monomial
\(x_1x_2x_3x_4\) are the five in
\eqref{eq:exact-projector-witness}.  Their Jacobian coefficients
are respectively \(16,-4,-4,4,4\), and their squared norms are
\(16,2,2,2,2\).  The quartic monomial has squared norm one.
It follows that
\[
 c={16^2\over16}+4\,{4^2\over2}=48,
 \qquad Q={C^*C\over48}.
\]
Applying this identity to \(w_*\) gives
\eqref{eq:exact-projector-witness}.

Extend the Pluecker coordinates \(z_{i_1i_2i_3i_4}\)
alternatingly, with repeated-index coordinates zero.  For an ordered
triple \(I\) and an increasing five-tuple \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_5)\),
use the quadratic relation
\[
 F_{I,J}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^5(-1)^{k-1}
              z_{I,j_k}\,z_{J\setminus j_k}.
\]
Substitution of \eqref{eq:exact-projector-witness}, together with
\(Pw_*=w_*-Qw_*\), gives \eqref{eq:two-separating-quadrics}
for
\[
 \begin{split}
 I&=(1,5,8),\quad J=(1,6,7,9,10),\\
 I&=(1,6,7),\quad J=(2,3,6,9,10),
 \end{split}
\]
respectively.  These two formulas can thus be checked with five
nonzero coordinates.  Their linear quotients after removing
\(b-a\) are independent.  As ideal sheaves on \(\Pj^1\),
they generate \((b-a)\): the two remaining linear forms have no
common projective zero.  Every other restricted Pluecker quadric
vanishes at \([1:1]\), so the full restricted ideal sheaf is also
\((b-a)\).  This proves both uniqueness and reducedness, including
at the endpoints and at infinity.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The same condition at the smooth integral witness]
The smooth web \(C_*\) of \eqref{eq:C-star} also satisfies the
rank-two condition.  For its Pluecker vector, in the monomial basis
above, the same two quadrics restrict respectively to
\[
 { (a-b)(339a+89b)\over24},\qquad
 {37(a-b)(31a+17b)\over72}.
\]
The determinant of their linear quotients by \(b-a\) is
\(27787/432\ne0\).  These identities follow by taking the
four-by-four minors of the displayed integral coefficient matrix
\eqref{eq:C-star}, applying \(Q=C^*C/48\), and substituting in
\(F_{I,J}\).  They are reproduced by
\texttt{checks/revision132\_exact.py}, together with equivariance
for all sixteen matrix-unit generators of \(\mathfrak{gl}_4\).
The smoothness and basepoint-freeness of this same web are certified
independently in Lemma~\ref{lem:differential}.  The preceding
five-coordinate proof already establishes the nonempty open without
using this additional computation.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-intrinsic-web}]
On each Pluecker affine chart, the coefficients of the linear forms
in \eqref{eq:plucker-linear-quotient} are regular functions of
\(w_R\).  The condition that they span a two-dimensional space
is the nonvanishing of at least one two-by-two minor.  Together with
nonvanishing of both components, it defines an invariant open in
\(\Gr(4,W)\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-plucker-pencil} proves this
open nonempty.  The witness web of four squares is not asserted to
have smooth Jacobian.  Rather, \(\Gr(4,W)\) is irreducible and
\(G_4^\circ\) is a nonempty open by the earlier smoothness
certificate.  Their two nonempty opens intersect.  This proves the
nonemptiness and density of \(G_4^{\mathrm{rec}}\) without an
unjustified smoothness claim about the witness.

The same ideal-sheaf argument as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-plucker-pencil} proves that
\eqref{eq:reconstruction-open} makes the pencil intersection the
single reduced point \([w_R]\).  Without that rank condition,
the points with the same two nonzero projective components are
precisely the decomposable points \([aPw_R+bQw_R]\) with
\(ab\ne0\), which proves \eqref{eq:exact-plucker-pencil}.
This is an exact characterization of the ambiguity of the
\emph{two-component invariant}; it need not describe every
additional invariant of the full failure scheme on an exceptional
locus.  If the line is not contained in the Grassmannian, its
scheme-theoretic intersection has length at most two, because the
Grassmannian ideal is generated by quadrics.

An abstract isomorphism \(\widehat D_R\simeq\widehat D_{R'}\)
identifies their reductions, deepest strata, and graded normal cones.
Lemma~\ref{lem:deepest-normal-cone} distinguishes the two tensor
rulings.  Choosing one fibre and applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:plucker-component-readout}, there is consequently a
single \(g\in\PGL(V)\) for which the pairs
\(([Pw_{R'}],[Qw_{R'}])\) and
\(([P(gw_R)],[Q(gw_R)])\) agree.  Replace \(R'\) by
\(g^{-1}R'\).  Its Pluecker point belongs to the pencil
\(\mathscr L_R\), and both components are nonzero.  The reduced
one-point intersection gives \([w_{R'}]=[w_R]\), hence
\(R'=R\).  The algebra-orbit equivalence of
Lemma~\ref{lem:algebra-orbits} gives the assertion for \(B_R\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Separation of the generic Torelli packet]
\label{cor:separate-torelli-packet}
After restriction to a nonempty open of the polarized-K3 image in
Theorem~\ref{thm:torelli-monodromy}, its finite etale web cover is
separated by the isomorphism class of the full failure scheme.
Distinct projective web classes in one such fibre have nonisomorphic
failure schemes.  On the regular Reye locus, the full failure scheme
therefore also determines the web's Reye congruence and its Enriques
datum.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The invariant dense open \(G_4^{\mathrm{rec}}\) defines a dense
open in the regular web quotient.  Intersect it with the finite etale
cover of Theorem~\ref{thm:torelli-monodromy}.  The image of its
closed complement is closed under that finite map and misses the
generic point of the base.  Remove this image.  Every point of each
remaining fibre lies in the reconstruction open.  The theorem
separates their web classes.  The Reye and Enriques constructions on
the regular web locus are functorial under projective equivalence,
which proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The result concerns the actual degree-sixteen ideal of the deepest
transverse failure cone.  It uses information that is lost on passing
to the reduced incidence image or to the Jacobian quartic alone.
It neither identifies an incidence-resolution decomposition with a
primary decomposition of \(W_3\) or \(W_4\), nor asserts that
all higher embedded primary components have been classified.  Its
geometric conclusion is instead an intrinsic inverse theorem: on a
dense open, the deeper nonreduced boundary recovers the original web.
