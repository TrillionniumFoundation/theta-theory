\title[Quantitative adaptive Jacobi identification]{Quantitative Adaptive Boundary Identification of Infinite Damped Jacobi Lattices}
\author{Qian Qi}
\date{September 5, 2026}
\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We develop a response-geometry theory for adaptive Bayesian identification of
damped oscillator lattices from one noisy boundary coordinate.  For a
homogeneous bath, six short durations recover five physical coefficients.  A
finite-prefix exploration schedule with predictable durations and fresh signs
gives policy-uniform contrast without resetting the state.  We prove a
random-information quasi-Bernstein--von Mises theorem in total variation,
strong current-state filter jets in deterministic separable dual spaces, and a
functional posterior limit for the inferred memory kernel.

For a semi-infinite inhomogeneous Jacobi bath, every coupling and pinning
coefficient is unknown.  Boundary response reconstructs the complete sequence
by a Weyl--Schur recursion, and the exact posterior under logarithmic washout
satisfies a full good large-deviation principle on the compact coefficient
product.  We give a line-addressable finite algorithm from response jets to
moments, Gram systems, and Jacobi coefficients, with an effective
depth-dependent inverse modulus.  This yields an explicit shrinking radius for
a growing coefficient block, contraction in an exponentially weighted
operator norm, and honest simultaneous confidence cylinders.

A complementary no-washout protocol uses predictable-intercept Rademacher
orthogonalization.  It preserves the guaranteed response-information
posterior exponent, up to an arbitrary fixed exploration fraction, while all
baseline actions may be bounded, adaptive, reward-seeking, or stabilizing.
Its total physical time is \(\Theta(n)\), and its washout-free confidence
cylinders have finite-sample uniform coverage.  Thus the two-sided LDP and the
linear-time adaptive guarantee are both available without weakening either
statement.
\end{abstract}
