\section{Main result and comparison with earlier experiments}
The question left by Round 48 is not whether a rare long probe can identify a
deep coefficient. It is whether retained state can do so when every individual
probe remains in the original bounded window. We give a positive answer for an
explicit admissible choice of the clock and a block-committed control class.
Neither the exploration law nor the fresh independent signs are changed.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-probe logarithmic-depth recovery]
\label{thm:main}
Fix the coefficient box, noise variance, bounded initial-state ball, and positive
exploration probability $\rho$ specified below. There is an explicit diagnostic
atom $q_0$ in the original law $\omega$, with duration $t_0>0$, such that the
following experiment is admissible. Choose baseline gaps $\tau_g=t_0$ and
commit bounded baseline profiles within blocks of $m_n$ stages before seeing
any sign or readout in that block. Profiles may depend arbitrarily on earlier
blocks. On exploration use the original law $\omega$; otherwise use $q_0$.
All signs remain fresh and independent, and no state is reset.

There are explicit constants $C_b,p_b>0$ in \eqref{eq:block-constants} such that,
for $r,s>0$ with $C_b r+p_b s<1$, one can take
$J_n=\lfloor r\log n\rfloor$, $\delta_n=n^{-s}$, and $m_n=O(\log n)$.
For every product coefficient prior with a common positive coordinate-density
lower bound and every working initial-state probability on the declared ball,
the exact posterior based on all completed blocks concentrates on
$d_{J_n}<\delta_n$, uniformly over that block-committed policy class and the
external truth. Coordinatewise posterior medians achieve the same error.
The physical clock for $n'$ used readouts is at most $(t_0+T)n'$ deterministically.

Over the larger class of all bounded-force chronological policies, no uniform
fixed-accuracy estimator can recover depths $J_n/\log n\to\infty$.
Thus the best attainable depth \emph{order} in the bounded-duration unreset
experiment, with the above admissible clock choice, is logarithmic.
\end{theorem}

An explicit choice is $r=1/(4C_b)$, $s=1/(4p_b)$. The result does not say that
every within-block feedback policy achieves this rate. Committing a block is
a control choice, not a reset or an extra observation. A fixed externally
imposed coarse clock is not covered by the sampled-logarithm construction;
its small-step condition is stated rather than an absence of aliasing assumed.

The previous fully adaptive result remains valid with a stronger posterior
bound. For arbitrary bounded predictable baselines, the unchanged step-response
law gives a sufficient region $C_c r+12s<1$ at
$J_n=\lfloor r\log n/\log\log n\rfloor$, $\delta_n=n^{-s}$.
The earlier finite-mean, unbounded-support law gives $C_\infty r+8s<1$
at logarithmic depth. The new block result uses the first, bounded-support
law but extracts additional history information. It therefore cannot be obtained
by equating a one-step exploration floor to actual information.

\subsection{Position relative to the closest prior work}
Mikhaylov and Mikhaylov \cite{MM2019,MM2020} give discrete-time Jacobi boundary
reconstruction and its relation to classical moments. We use the classical
moment and orthogonal-polynomial engine, with the quantitative improvements
proposed in the Round 46 report, not a new inverse spectral principle.
The new bridge here is from delayed bounded pulses in a retained-state noisy
experiment to that engine, with explicit sampling and likelihood costs.

Sarkar, Rakhlin and Dahleh \cite{SRD2021}, Section 3 and Theorem 5.1, estimate
finite-impulse-response and Hankel objects for stable systems of unknown finite
latent order from a single noisy trajectory. Their finite-order realization
and approximation errors, including similarity ambiguity, differ from recovery
of labeled coefficients of an actually infinite Jacobi state. An infinite
Hankel matrix does not by itself assert infinite physical dimension. We do not
claim their results are subsumed; our prior box, common damping, and strict
pinning give stronger structural information, while our target is physical
coordinate depth under a specified clock and feedback convention.

Agapiou, Stuart and Zhang \cite{ASZ2014} obtain logarithmic contraction in
linear severely ill-posed problems with exponentially decaying singular values
and commuting Gaussian structures. Exponential visibility producing a logarithmic
index is therefore not the novelty claim. The new statement concerns a
nonlinear coefficient map, a bounded-probe retained-state design, and the
conditional likelihood of that design. Likewise sampled-generator logarithms
and system aliasing have precedents \cite{YTG2016}; we prove a concrete convergent
operator-series certificate for a boundary pulse channel rather than claiming
that arbitrary coarse sampling determines a continuous generator.

The retained homogeneous nonlinear quasi-BvM is distinct from Du, Nair and
Janson \cite{DNJ2025}: their Theorem 1 permits anisotropic linear information
growth, and their policy-uniform Corollary 1 concerns basis-vector/bandit designs.
Our homogeneous result has nonlinear means and bounded-state nuisance priors,
but stronger smoothness and uniformly nondegenerate root-$n$ information.
It does not solve general weak-regularity or anisotropic nonlinear BvM problems.
Posterior Gaussian approximation is not a sampling limit for the center.
All original homogeneous conclusions and their sources remain in the retained
supplement. Matrix-function decay \cite{BR2007}, inverse-stability transfer
\cite{Vollmer}, and time-uniform martingale methods \cite{Howard} are acknowledged
ingredients. These comparisons are targeted, not an exhaustive priority claim.
