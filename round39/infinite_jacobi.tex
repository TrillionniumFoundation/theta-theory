
\section{Complete recovery of an unknown infinite Jacobi bath}
\label{sec:infinite-jacobi}

The preceding model makes three homogeneous bath coefficients unknown.  We
now allow a different coupling and pinning coefficient at every edge and
site.  This produces a genuinely infinite-dimensional parameter and an
identification statement that cannot be transferred to one fixed finite
chain.

Fix constants \(0<a_-<a_+<\infty\), \(c_->0\), and
\(b_->2a_+\).  Let
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak B=[c_-,c_+]
 \times\prod_{j=0}^\infty[a_-,a_+]
 \times\prod_{j=0}^\infty[b_-,b_+]
 \label{eq:jacobi-parameter}
\end{equation}
with its compact metrizable product topology.  We write
\(\beta=(c,(a_j),(b_j))\).  Define the positive Jacobi operator
\begin{equation}
 (J_\beta q)_0=b_0q_0-a_0q_1,
 \qquad
 (J_\beta q)_j=b_jq_j-a_{j-1}q_{j-1}-a_jq_{j+1},\quad j\ge1.
 \label{eq:jacobi-operator}
\end{equation}
The bound \(b_->2a_+\) implies
\(J_\beta\ge(b_--2a_+)I\).  Let
\[
 A_\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-J_\beta&-cI\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad B=\binom0{e_0},\qquad\ell(q,v)=q_0,
 \qquad h_\beta(t)=\ell\int_0^te^{sA_\beta}Bds.
\]
The common scalar damping is included to keep the reconstruction transparent;
the complete stiffness operator remains unknown.

\begin{lemma}[Product continuity of finite-time response]
\label{lem:product-continuity}
For every \(T<\infty\), the map
\[
 \mathcal R_T:\mathfrak B\to C([0,T]),
 \qquad \mathcal R_T(\beta)=h_\beta|_{[0,T]},
\]
is continuous.  The semigroups are uniformly exponentially stable over
\(\mathfrak B\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The energy proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:five-energy} applies with
\(C=cI\) and \(K=J_\beta\), using the uniform positive and upper bounds.
If \(\beta_n\to\beta\) coordinatewise, then
\(J_{\beta_n}q\to J_\beta q\) for every finitely supported \(q\).  Uniform
operator bounds and density extend this to strong operator convergence on
\(\ell^2\).  Powers of the uniformly bounded generators then converge
strongly, and their exponential series converge uniformly on compact time
intervals.  Applying the result to \(B\), integrating, and observing with
\(\ell\) proves continuity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Weyl reconstruction from the damped step response}

For \(w>0\), let
\[
 m_j(w)=\ip{e_j}{(w+J_\beta^{(j)})^{-1}e_j},
\]
where \(J_\beta^{(j)}\) is the Jacobi tail starting at site \(j\).  Laplace
transformation of the second-order equation gives, for \(z>0\),
\begin{equation}
 \widehat h_\beta(z)
 =\frac1z\ip{e_0}{(z^2+cz+J_\beta)^{-1}e_0}
 =\frac1z m_0(z^2+cz).
 \label{eq:jacobi-transfer}
\end{equation}
The Taylor expansion at the origin also gives
\begin{equation}
 c=-h_\beta'''(0),
 \label{eq:damping-from-response}
\end{equation}
because \(h''(0)=1\) and \(h'''(0)=\ell A_\beta^2B=-c\).

Deleting the first row and column of a Jacobi tail gives the exact Schur
recursion
\begin{equation}
 m_j(w)=\frac1{w+b_j-a_j^2m_{j+1}(w)}.
 \label{eq:weyl-recursion}
\end{equation}
Since \(m_{j+1}(w)=w^{-1}+O(w^{-2})\) as \(w\to\infty\),
\begin{align}
 b_j&=\lim_{w\to\infty}\{m_j(w)^{-1}-w\},
 \label{eq:recover-bj}\\
 a_j^2&=\lim_{w\to\infty}
 w\{w+b_j-m_j(w)^{-1}\},
 \label{eq:recover-aj}\\
 m_{j+1}(w)&=\frac{w+b_j-m_j(w)^{-1}}{a_j^2}.
 \label{eq:recover-tail}
\end{align}
The positive square root selects \(a_j\).

\begin{theorem}[Constructive complete Jacobi reconstruction]
\label{thm:jacobi-reconstruction}
The boundary response map
\[
 \mathcal R:\mathfrak B\to C([0,\infty)),
 \qquad \mathcal R(\beta)=h_\beta,
\]
is one-to-one.  More precisely, \(h_\beta\) determines \(c\) by
\eqref{eq:damping-from-response}, determines \(m_0(w)\) for every \(w>0\) by
\eqref{eq:jacobi-transfer}, and then determines successively every
\(b_j,a_j,m_{j+1}\) by
\eqref{eq:recover-bj}--\eqref{eq:recover-tail}.

The Weyl function of this infinite chain is not rational.  Hence no fixed
finite Jacobi chain has the same boundary response.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Knowledge of \(h_\beta(t)\) for all \(t\ge0\) gives its Laplace transform for
all \(z>0\).  Equation \eqref{eq:damping-from-response} first recovers \(c\).
The map \(z\mapsto z^2+cz\) is a bijection from \((0,\infty)\) onto
\((0,\infty)\), so \eqref{eq:jacobi-transfer} recovers \(m_0(w)\) throughout
that interval.  Equations \eqref{eq:recover-bj}--\eqref{eq:recover-tail} then
recover one coefficient pair and the next tail function at every induction
step.  Positivity of \(a_j\) removes the sign ambiguity.  This proves
injectivity and the constructive assertion.

The vector \(e_0\) is cyclic for \(J_\beta\): from
\(J_\beta e_j\), induction and \(a_j>0\) recover every basis vector in the
closed span of \(\{J_\beta^ne_0:n\ge0\}\).  A rational Stieltjes transform
would correspond to a spectral measure with finite support, whose cyclic
subspace is finite-dimensional.  This contradicts cyclicity on
\(\ell^2(\N_0)\).  A finite Jacobi chain, by contrast, has a rational Weyl
function.  Thus no fixed finite chain has the same response.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is classical and what is used here]
The Weyl recursion and inverse spectral uniqueness are classical.  The theorem
is included with a complete direct proof because it is the identifiability
engine for the noisy posterior result below.  The new statistical statement
is not obtained by calling deterministic inverse uniqueness a posterior
theorem; compact separation and likelihood bounds are proved explicitly.
\end{remark}

\section{A posterior theorem for the complete coefficient sequence}
\label{sec:jacobi-statistics}

Choose a dense sequence \((t_m)_{m\ge1}\) in \((0,T]\) and probabilities
\(\omega_m>0\), \(\sum_m\omega_m=1\).  At stage \(i\), the following
chronological experiment is performed.
\begin{enumerate}
\item An arbitrary bounded feedback segment of uniformly bounded duration may
be run.
\item Set the force to zero for a washout time \(w_i=w_*i\), where \(w_*>0\).
\item Draw \(M_i\) with law \((\omega_m)\), draw an independent Rademacher
sign \(S_i\), apply force \(aS_i\) for duration \(t_{M_i}\), and observe the
boundary displacement with independent \(N(0,\sigma^2)\) readout noise.
\end{enumerate}
All three fresh variables are independent of the observed past.  The
feedback segment may be sample-size-dependent and otherwise arbitrary within
the stated force and duration bounds.

Let \(h_\beta(t)\) be the response from rest.  Uniform stability gives the
exact endpoint mean in the form
\begin{equation}
 Y_i=aS_ih_{\beta_0}(t_{M_i})+\eta_i^{\beta_0}(z_0)+\xi_i,
 \qquad
 \sup_{\beta,\norm z\le R}\abs{\eta_i^\beta(z)}
 \le Ce^{-\lambda w_i}.
 \label{eq:washed-observation}
\end{equation}
All fixed cylinder derivatives have the same summable bound, but consistency
will require only values.

For a working initial-state prior \(\nu_n\) on the radius-\(R\) ball, let
\(L_n^{\nu_n}(\beta)\) be the exact integrated likelihood generated by the
realized full action record.  Let
\[
 L_n^\star(\beta)=
 \prod_{i=1}^n
 \varphi_\sigma\{Y_i-aS_ih_\beta(t_{M_i})\}
\]
be the ideal washed likelihood.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform summable washout correction]
\label{lem:washout-correction}
There are random variables \(C_n\), bounded almost surely by one finite random
variable with moments of every finite order, such that
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\beta\in\mathfrak B}
 \left|\log L_n^{\nu_n}(\beta)-\log L_n^\star(\beta)\right|
 \le C_n
 \label{eq:washout-correction}
\end{equation}
uniformly over \(n\), the working priors, and the bounded feedback segments.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The same Gaussian completion as in \eqref{eq:nuisance-factorization}
expresses the ratio as a log integral of a sum whose \(i\)-th absolute value
is at most
\(Ce^{-\lambda w_i}(1+\abs{\xi_i})\), uniformly in \(\beta,z\).  The series
is almost surely finite and has all finite moments because
\(w_i=w_*i\).  Positivity of the normalized integral bounds its logarithm by
the supremum of the exponent.  This proves \eqref{eq:washout-correction}.
\end{proof}

Define the response metric
\begin{equation}
 D(\beta,\beta_0)=
 \sum_{m=1}^\infty\omega_m
 \{h_\beta(t_m)-h_{\beta_0}(t_m)\}^2.
 \label{eq:response-metric}
\end{equation}
It is continuous by Lemma~\ref{lem:product-continuity} and dominated
convergence.  It separates points: equality to zero gives equality on the
dense duration set, hence on \([0,T]\); analyticity of the bounded-generator
response gives equality on \([0,\infty)\), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:jacobi-reconstruction} gives \(\beta=\beta_0\).

Let \(\Pi\) be any Borel prior with full support on \(\mathfrak B\).  Product
priors with positive continuous one-coordinate densities are examples.  The
exact posterior is
\[
 \Pi_n^{\nu_n}(d\beta)
 =\frac{L_n^{\nu_n}(\beta)\Pi(d\beta)}
        {\int L_n^{\nu_n}(\gamma)\Pi(d\gamma)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Strong posterior consistency for the infinite Jacobi bath]
\label{thm:jacobi-consistency}
For every open neighbourhood \(U\) of \(\beta_0\) in the product topology,
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_n^{\nu_n}(U^c)\longrightarrow0
 \quad P_{\beta_0,z_0}\text{-almost surely}.
 \label{eq:jacobi-consistency}
\end{equation}
The assertion holds for every sequence of working initial-state priors
\(\nu_n\) supported in the fixed Hilbert ball.  It is uniform in probability
over the bounded feedback segments described above.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compactness and point separation give
\begin{equation}
 d_U:=\inf_{\beta\in U^c}D(\beta,\beta_0)>0.
 \label{eq:compact-separation}
\end{equation}
For the ideal likelihood, write
\(\delta_\beta(m)=a\{h_\beta(t_m)-h_{\beta_0}(t_m)\}\).  Under the true law,
\begin{align}
 \frac1n\log\frac{L_n^\star(\beta)}{L_n^\star(\beta_0)}
 ={}&\frac1{n\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^n
 S_i\xi_i\delta_\beta(M_i)
 -\frac1{2n\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_\beta(M_i)^2
 +r_{n}(\beta),
 \label{eq:ideal-log-ratio}
\end{align}
where the true washout residual contributes
\(\sup_\beta\abs{r_n(\beta)}\to0\) almost surely by summability.

The compact family
\[
 \mathcal D=\{m\mapsto\delta_\beta(m):\beta\in\mathfrak B\}
\]
is totally bounded in \(L^2(\omega)\), because
\(\beta\mapsto(h_\beta(t_m))_m\) is continuous from the compact parameter
space into \(\ell^2(\omega)\).  It is uniformly bounded.  Given
\(\varepsilon>0\), choose a finite \(L^2(\omega)\) net.  The strong law at
its finitely many points, followed by the \(L^2\) maximal estimate for the
martingale differences and approximation by the net, yields
\begin{align}
 \sup_{\beta\in\mathfrak B}
 \left|\frac1n\sum_iS_i\xi_i\delta_\beta(M_i)\right|&\longrightarrow0,
 \label{eq:uniform-score-slln}\\
 \sup_{\beta\in\mathfrak B}
 \left|\frac1n\sum_i\delta_\beta(M_i)^2
      -a^2D(\beta,\beta_0)\right|&\longrightarrow0
 \label{eq:uniform-square-slln}
\end{align}
almost surely.  For completeness, the first approximation error has
conditional second moment at most \(C\varepsilon^2n\); apply the estimate on
dyadic \(n\), Borel--Cantelli, and fill the intervening indices by the
martingale maximal inequality.  The second follows by the same finite-net
argument and the ordinary strong law for the bounded square functions.

Equations \eqref{eq:compact-separation}--\eqref{eq:uniform-square-slln} imply
that, eventually,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\beta\in U^c}
 \frac1n\log\frac{L_n^\star(\beta)}{L_n^\star(\beta_0)}
 \le-\frac{a^2d_U}{4\sigma^2}.
 \label{eq:numerator-exponential}
\end{equation}

It remains to lower-bound the denominator without assuming a finite-dimensional
prior density.  By continuity, for every \(\eta>0\) the set
\(B_\eta=\{\beta:D(\beta,\beta_0)<\eta\}\) is an open neighbourhood with
\(\Pi(B_\eta)>0\).  Jensen's inequality gives
\begin{align*}
 \int_{B_\eta}
 \frac{L_n^\star(\beta)}{L_n^\star(\beta_0)}\Pi(d\beta)
 \ge{}&\Pi(B_\eta)
 \exp\left\{
 \Pi(B_\eta)^{-1}\int_{B_\eta}
 \log\frac{L_n^\star(\beta)}{L_n^\star(\beta_0)}\Pi(d\beta)
 \right\}.
\end{align*}
Fubini and the strong law show that the averaged score divided by \(n\)
tends to zero, while the averaged quadratic term is eventually at most
\(a^2\eta/\sigma^2\).  Choose \(\eta<d_U/16\).  The denominator therefore
loses at most \(e^{-na^2d_U/(16\sigma^2)}\), whereas
\eqref{eq:numerator-exponential} gains a strictly larger exponential rate.
Lemma~\ref{lem:washout-correction} changes both logarithms by only \(o(n)\),
uniformly in \(\beta\) and \(\nu_n\).  Their ratio tends to zero almost
surely, proving \eqref{eq:jacobi-consistency}.  All constants in the washout
and strong-law estimates are independent of the bounded feedback segment,
which gives the stated uniform-in-probability version.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Genuine infinite-dimensional content]
Every product neighbourhood constrains finitely many coefficients, and the
theorem learns every such finite block as the sample grows.  The parameter
space contains an infinite sequence, the response is nonrational, and the
reconstruction iterates without a terminal index.  This is not a two- or
five-parameter regression embedded in a known bath.  It is also not claimed
to be a nonparametric Gaussian limit: the proven infinite-dimensional result
is strong posterior consistency in the natural compact product topology.
\end{remark}
