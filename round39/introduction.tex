
\section{Introduction and statement of contribution}
\label{sec:introduction}

Boundary measurements of a distributed mechanical system mix three sources of difficulty.  The input is selected in chronological feedback from previous observations, the unobserved state survives from one experiment block to the next, and different operator coefficients can first appear at very different orders of the boundary response.  In an infinite lattice there is a fourth issue: a complete bath is an operator-valued unknown rather than a finite collection of hidden coordinates.  This paper separates these issues and proves two complementary results.

For a homogeneous half-line bath, the unknown parameter is
\[
 \vartheta=(c,k,\epsilon,c_b,k_b)\in\Theta\Subset(0,\infty)^5.
\]
The entries are, respectively, boundary damping, boundary pinning, coupling strength, bath damping, and bath pinning.  The entire experiment observes only the noisy boundary displacement.  We construct six short forcing durations.  Their response values recover the first eight time jets, and five of those jets form a triangular global coordinate system for \(\vartheta\).  The coupling is bounded away from zero, so division by \(\epsilon^2\) in the last two triangular steps is legitimate.  The statistical policy need not allocate positive mass to all twelve duration--sign words at every block.  Instead, a predictable positive-density subsequence is divided into calibration windows; each window uses every duration once in a data-dependent order and uses a fresh symmetric sign.  The other blocks are unrestricted bounded feedback actions.  This supplies uniform contrast and information without resetting the physical state.

The corresponding likelihood is nonlinear in \(\vartheta\).  It is also a triangular experiment: the policy may depend on the terminal sample size, and the frequentist truth includes a fixed Hilbert-space initial state while the working Bayesian likelihood integrates an arbitrary probability \(\nu_n\) on a Hilbert ball.  The truth need not belong to the support of \(\nu_n\).  We therefore call the resulting parameter law a \emph{quasi-posterior} whenever this external initial-state specification is misspecified.  Exponential damping makes this misspecification summable but does not make its finite-record likelihood identity disappear.

Our first principal theorem proves a uniform random-information Bernstein--von Mises approximation for this experiment.  It is obtained from a general nonlinear adaptive Gaussian theorem proved in Section~\ref{sec:triangular}.  The realized information matrix is allowed to remain random and need not converge.  The proof supplies the experiment index, filtrations, full compact empirical net, uniform stochastic orders, fixed-ball expansion, global localization, relative unnormalized \(L^1\) comparison with a moving Gaussian kernel, and normalization.  The theorem also yields the normalized evidence with the correct five-dimensional factor \(n^{-5/2}\).

The homogeneous model now infers the three coefficients that determine the unresolved bath and hence its memory.  The complete time-domain memory kernel is a smooth Banach-valued functional of \(\vartheta\).  We propagate the parameter quasi-posterior to that function, rather than merely displaying the kernel of a known bath.  We also prove strong, not test-by-test, parameter differentiability of the current-state filter in the norm dual of \(C_b^{r+1}\).  The resulting current-state Gaussian image has rank at most five; it is stated as a finite-rank image theorem and not as an infinite-dimensional Bernstein--von Mises limit.

The second principal result makes the infinite bath itself unknown.  Let
\[
 \beta=\bigl(c,(a_j)_{j\ge0},(b_j)_{j\ge0}\bigr)
\]
parametrize a uniformly positive bounded Jacobi stiffness operator with all off-diagonal coefficients bounded away from zero.  The boundary step response determines \(c\) and the complete Jacobi sequence.  The proof is constructive: the Laplace transform of the response gives the boundary Weyl function, after which a Schur recursion recovers \(b_j\), \(a_j\), and the tail Weyl function successively.  A countable-duration experiment, preceded by increasing zero-force washouts, gives a posterior that is strongly consistent in the compact product topology.  The washout changes the exact likelihood only by a uniformly bounded random factor.  This theorem fails for every fixed finite-chain replacement, because a fixed finite chain has only finitely many Jacobi coefficients and a rational boundary Weyl function.

\subsection{Main theorem map}

The logical structure is as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-bvm} is a nonlinear adaptive Gaussian quasi-Bernstein--von Mises theorem with summably transient nuisance and fully uniform triangular quantifiers.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:jet-embedding} proves the global six-duration embedding of the five homogeneous physical coefficients.
\item Proposition~\ref{prop:balanced-contrast} proves policy-uniform empirical contrast and information from balanced windows.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice-bvm} applies the abstract theorem to the non-resetting half-line lattice.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:filter-jets} proves strong dual filter jets, and Corollary~\ref{cor:memory-posterior} gives posterior uncertainty for the entire memory kernel.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:jacobi-reconstruction} reconstructs the complete unknown Jacobi bath from boundary response.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:jacobi-consistency} proves posterior consistency for that infinite coefficient sequence under a countable diagnostic experiment.
\end{enumerate}
Each arrow in this list is proved in the article.  Computational checks in the repository verify finite algebraic identities and the source build only; they are not used as substitutes for the analytic arguments.

\subsection{Relation to adaptive identification and adaptive-data asymptotics}
\label{sec:literature}

Adaptive identification of infinite-dimensional systems has a substantial history.  Baumeister, Scondo, Demetriou and Rosen~\cite{Baumeister1997} develop online parameter estimation and persistence of excitation for abstract infinite-dimensional dynamical systems, including stiffness and damping examples.  Demetriou and Rosen~\cite{DemetriouRosen1994Inverse,DemetriouRosen1994PE} treat second-order distributed systems and persistence of excitation.  Chattopadhyay, Sukumar and Natarajan~\cite{Chattopadhyay2025} study adaptive identification of stable SISO infinite-dimensional systems and recovery from transfer coefficients.  Sharrock and Kantas~\cite{SharrockKantas2022} study online parameter estimation and sensor placement for a partially observed stochastic PDE.

The conclusions proved here are different from adaptive-estimator consistency.  The homogeneous theorem is a chronological Bayesian likelihood result with nonlinear hidden-state means, arbitrary sample-size-dependent policies in a specified class, an externally misspecified initial-state prior, a random information matrix that need not converge, and a total-variation posterior approximation.  The infinite-Jacobi theorem treats the complete operator coefficient sequence as the statistical parameter and proves Bayesian consistency from noisy boundary data.

Du, Nair and Janson~\cite{DuNairJanson2025} prove Bernstein--von Mises results for adaptively collected data in linear Gaussian models and related adaptive settings.  Their work is the closest adaptive-data asymptotic comparison.  The abstract theorem in Section~\ref{sec:triangular} addresses the nonlinear predictable mean case by imposing and verifying a global contrast condition, controlling a smooth martingale field, and retaining a summable latent-state correction.  It does not claim that posterior Gaussian approximation alone yields frequentist confidence coverage under every policy.  When information and its predictable bracket have limits, the usual additional martingale central-limit argument can be imposed separately.

The treatment of misspecification is related in spirit to general misspecified Bernstein--von Mises theory~\cite{KleijnVanDerVaart2012}.  Here the misspecification is neither independent and identically distributed nor persistent: it enters through one unknown initial state and contributes only an \(O(1)\) log-likelihood perturbation.  We prove directly that every normalized Kullback--Leibler minimizer converges to the physical parameter and that the root-\(n\) center is unchanged.

Inverse spectral recovery of Jacobi matrices is classical; see, for example, Gesztesy and Simon~\cite{GesztesySimon1997}, Teschl~\cite{Teschl2000}, and the dynamic inverse formulation of Mikhaylov, Mikhaylov and Simonov~\cite{Mikhaylov2019}.  The Schur recursion itself is not presented as new.  The contribution of Sections~\ref{sec:infinite-jacobi}--\ref{sec:jacobi-statistics} is to place this complete coefficient recovery inside a damped continuous-time boundary experiment and to prove posterior consistency under noisy countable-duration observations with a persistent, but summably washed out, state.

\subsection{Scope and nonclaims}

The half-line generators used here are bounded.  No conclusion is asserted for an unbounded wave or beam observation without a separate domain argument.  The state-image theorem is finite rank.  The preparation-label result in Section~\ref{sec:preparations} is a finite-mixture evidence theorem, not a phase-transition theorem.  The infinite-Jacobi posterior result is consistency in product topology, not a nonparametric Bernstein--von Mises theorem.  These boundaries are part of the theorem statements rather than qualifications deferred to repository metadata.
