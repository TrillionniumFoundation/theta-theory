\section{Round-seventeen positive closure: direct canonical coefficients and geometric shell asymptotics}
\label{sec:r17-b1}

This section proves the exact-particle-number coefficient theorem directly
from the positive canonical phase-space integral.  Connected trajectory
polymers enter only through analytic error bounds for the grand-canonical
pressure; they are never interpreted as independent random components.  The
proof separates the logically prior grand-canonical B2 input from the
conditioning theorem proved here.

\subsection{Exact-number positive law and the noncircular input}

Fix a short physical horizon $T\le T_*$ and a bounded real path/contact source
$H$ in the grand-canonical source chart constructed in the grand-canonical
part of Paper B2.  For $N\ge1$ put
\[
 Z_{\varepsilon,N}(H,\lambda)
 ={1\over N!}\int_{\mathcal D_{\varepsilon,N}}
 \exp\left\{-\beta E_N+\mu_\varepsilon H_N
 +\lambda\cdot\sum_{i=1}^N C(z_i)\right\}\,dZ_N,
\]
where $C=(v,|v|^2/2,\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_m)$ is the continuous
constraint vector after particle number has been fixed.  For real
$(H,\lambda)$ this is a strictly positive finite integral and therefore
defines the probability $\mathbf P_{\varepsilon,N}^{H,\lambda}$.

The only B2 input used before conditioning is the following: on a fixed
complex source/activity polydisc, the logarithm of the grand-canonical
partition function is normally convergent, its source derivatives through
order four are bounded, and its connected remainder after the singleton term
is $O(\varepsilon)$ in the low-density norm.  No microcanonical conclusion of
B2 is used in this section.

\begin{lemma}[Direct canonical saddle]
\label{lem:r17-b1-saddle}
Let $a$ range over a compact subset of the interior of the one-particle
constraint mean image.  For all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, every real
$H$ in a fixed compact source ball has a unique real multiplier
$\lambda_{\varepsilon,N,H,a}$ satisfying
\[
 {1\over N}\mathbf E_{\varepsilon,N}^{H,\lambda}
 \sum_{i=1}^NC(z_i)=a.
\]
Moreover
\[
 cI\le {1\over N}\operatorname{Cov}_{\varepsilon,N}^{H,\lambda}
 \left(\sum_iC(z_i)\right)\le CI
\]
uniformly at the saddle, and the saddle depends analytically on the source.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the independent one-particle reference measure, the Hessian of the
log-Laplace transform is uniformly positive on compact subsets of the
interior mean image: if $b\ne0$, then $b\cdot C$ is nonconstant on one of a
finite family of velocity-position boxes, and its variance there is bounded
below.  Write the exact canonical integral by integrating particles in a
fixed order.  At each step the forbidden spatial volume is at most
$C N\varepsilon^3=O(\varepsilon)$, uniformly in the previously exposed
configuration.  Differentiating the positive integral under the sign of
integration and comparing conditional one-particle kernels therefore gives
\[
 \left\|{1\over N}D^2_\lambda\log Z_{\varepsilon,N}
       -D^2q_1(\lambda)\right\|\le C\varepsilon
\]
on the compact chart.  The path/contact source changes the conditional kernel
by a uniformly bounded Radon--Nikodym factor; the grand-canonical connected
bound controls its derivative and preserves the same $O(\varepsilon)$
comparison.  Strict convexity and the inverse-function theorem give the
unique saddle and its analytic source dependence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A direct Fourier decomposition of the positive canonical integral}

Let
\[
 S_N=\sum_{i=1}^NC(z_i),\qquad
 \Phi_{\varepsilon,N,H}(u)
 =\mathbf E_{\varepsilon,N}^{H,\lambda_{\varepsilon,N,H,a}}
 e^{iu\cdot(S_N-Na)}.
\]
Choose finitely many compact coordinate boxes $U_1,\ldots,U_J$ away from
hard-core boundaries such that, for every unit vector $e$, one box contains a
coordinate direction $\xi$ with
$|\partial_\xi(e\cdot C)|\ge c_*$.  Partition configuration space according
to the lexicographically first accepted particles lying in those boxes.  This
is a measurable partition of the genuine positive canonical law; it is not a
polymer decomposition.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical cell matching]
\label{lem:r17-b1-cell}
There are $c_0,c_1>0$ and a family of disjoint mesoscopic spatial cells such
that, under every saddle law above, with probability at least
$1-e^{-c_0N}$ at least $c_1N$ cells contain one selected particle whose
velocity ranges over one of the boxes $U_j$.  Conditional on the exterior and
on all selected cell labels, the joint density of the selected velocities is
bounded above and below, together with four derivatives, by tensor-product
one-particle densities times a factor $R$ satisfying
\[
 \|\log R\|_{C^4}\le C\varepsilon n_{
m sel}.
\]
On the exceptional event there is still one $d$-dimensional coarea chart,
and its conditional density has $W^{d+3,1}$ norm bounded by $C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take cells of side $L\varepsilon$ with $L$ fixed and large.  The exclusion
neighborhood generated by previously exposed particles occupies a uniformly
small fraction of a fresh cell.  The exact canonical density is positive and,
on a compact real source chart, its conditional activity in an accepted box
is bounded above and below.  Sequential exposure therefore stochastically
dominates a Bernoulli process with fixed success probability; the bounded-
difference martingale inequality gives the exponential lower-tail estimate.

Given the selected positions and the exterior, hard-core exclusion is
velocity independent.  The remaining dependence among selected velocities
comes from the bounded dynamic source.  Its logarithmic mixed derivatives are
sums of connected trajectory contributions.  The grand-canonical B2 estimate,
used only as an analytic absolute bound, gives the displayed $C^4$ estimate
for $R$.  Finally, the affine-span hypothesis for $C$ provides a finite cover
of configuration space by full-rank coarea charts; disintegrating the positive
canonical integral over the first available chart proves the exceptional
statement.  No independence of connected collision components is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One integrable canonical Fourier majorant]
\label{thm:r17-b1-fourier}
There are $0<\delta<R<\infty$, $c>0$, and $s>d+4$ such that, uniformly in the
compact source/target chart,
\[
 |\Phi_{\varepsilon,N,H}(u)|\le
 \begin{cases}
 \exp(-cN|u|^2),&|u|\le\delta,\\
 \exp(-cN),&\delta\le|u|\le R,\\
 C e^{-cN}(1+|u|)^{-s}
 +C(1+|u|)^{-s-cN},&|u|>R.
 \end{cases}
\]
The estimate holds after up to three source derivatives, with a fixed
polynomial loss absorbed by choosing $s$ larger.  In particular
\[
 \int_{|u|>\delta}|\Phi_{\varepsilon,N,H}(u)|\,du=o(N^{-d/2}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The central estimate follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:r17-b1-saddle}, Taylor's
formula, and the fourth-cumulant bound from the direct positive integral.  On
the compact annulus, every unit direction is detected in one of the boxes
$U_j$.  Conditional integration over one selected velocity gives a
characteristic factor whose modulus is at most $1-c$; applying this successively
to $cN$ selected cells, while absorbing the $C\varepsilon N$ interaction
factor into the exponent, gives $e^{-cN}$.

For $|u|>R$, choose in every selected block a coordinate with phase derivative
at least $c|u|$.  Repeated integration by parts in that physical velocity
coordinate is legitimate because the boxes are interior and the partition is
chosen with a smooth subordinate cutoff.  Boundary terms vanish; derivatives
fall on the one-particle density and on $R$.  On the typical event, applying
$s+cN$ integrations distributed among the selected particles gives
$C(1+|u|)^{-s-cN}$.  On the exceptional event, its probability is
$e^{-cN}$ and the fallback coarea chart supplies the fixed integrable factor
$(1+|u|)^{-s}$.  These are the two displayed terms; there is no
frequency-independent exceptional contribution.  Bounded source insertions
consume only finitely many integrations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Geometric shell theorem}

Let $D_\varepsilon\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a bounded domain with uniformly
$C^2$ boundary, uniformly bounded Gaussian perimeter, and inradius
$\rho_\varepsilon$ satisfying
$N^{1/2}\rho_\varepsilon\to\infty$.  This geometric definition does not
contain a desired Fourier-error conclusion.

\begin{theorem}[Exact-number local coefficient]
\label{thm:r17-b1-coefficient}
Uniformly in the compact source/target chart,
\[
 \mathbf P_{\varepsilon,N}^{H,\lambda_{\varepsilon,N,H,a}}
 \{N^{-1}S_N\in a+D_\varepsilon\}
 =\int_{a+D_\varepsilon}
 {N^{d/2}\exp[-\frac N2(y-a)^T\Sigma^{-1}(y-a)]
  \over(2\pi)^{d/2}\sqrt{\det\Sigma}}\,dy\,(1+o(1)),
\]
where $\Sigma=N^{-1}\operatorname{Cov}(S_N)$.  The error is uniform after
three source derivatives.  Point boxes, mesoscopic windows, and expanding
central boxes follow by the same formula in their natural regimes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fourier inversion is split at $\delta$ and $R$.  In the central ball the
uniform fourth-order cumulant expansion, after $u=N^{-1/2}z$, converges to the
Gaussian integrand and has an integrable Gaussian envelope.  The compact and
large-frequency integrals are $o(N^{-d/2})$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:r17-b1-fourier}.  Approximate the indicator of
$D_\varepsilon$ from above and below by convolution at a scale
$N^{-1/2}\ll h_\varepsilon\ll\rho_\varepsilon$.  The boundary error is its
Gaussian perimeter times $h_\varepsilon/
ho_\varepsilon=o(1)$.  This proves
the statement from geometric hypotheses rather than assuming the needed
error.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Source-dependent microcanonical transfer}

\begin{theorem}[Noncircular positive shell conditioning]
\label{thm:r17-b1-transfer}
Let $Q^{N}(H,\lambda)$ be the exact-number limiting pressure obtained from the
grand-canonical B2-GC pressure by coefficient extraction.  For every regular
constraint target $a$,
\[
 Q^{\mathrm{mc},a}(H)=
 \inf_\lambda\{Q^{N}(H,\lambda)-\lambda\cdot a\}
 -\inf_\lambda\{Q^{N}(0,\lambda)-\lambda\cdot a\}.
\]
The minimizer is reoptimized for every path/contact source $H$, and
\[
 D^2Q^{\mathrm{mc},a}
 =Q_{HH}-Q_{H\lambda}Q_{\lambda\lambda}^{-1}Q_{\lambda H}.
\]
The proof order is strictly
\[
 \text{B2-GC analytic pressure}\longrightarrow
 \text{B1 coefficient and shell theorem}\longrightarrow
 \text{B2-MC LDP}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Tilt the positive exact-$N$ measure by the finite-volume saddle from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r17-b1-saddle}.  The shell is centered under the tilted law,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:r17-b1-coefficient} gives a subexponential, uniformly
positive Gaussian-scale denominator.  Dividing numerator and denominator and
passing to the pressure limit yields the two saddle values.  Differentiating
the exact saddle equation gives the Schur complement.  Only the
grand-canonical B2 input was used before this point; B2's microcanonical
result is therefore downstream rather than circular.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-seventeen B1 closure]
\label{thm:r17-b1-main}
The exact-number hard-sphere law admits a source-dependent saddle, uniformly
nondegenerate constraint covariance, one global integrable Fourier majorant,
a geometric mixed-shell local coefficient theorem, and noncircular
microcanonical transfer.  No signed polymer activity is treated as a
probability and no independent connected-component representation is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Lemmas~\ref{lem:r17-b1-saddle} and~\ref{lem:r17-b1-cell} with
Theorems~\ref{thm:r17-b1-fourier},
\ref{thm:r17-b1-coefficient}, and~\ref{thm:r17-b1-transfer}.
\end{proof}
