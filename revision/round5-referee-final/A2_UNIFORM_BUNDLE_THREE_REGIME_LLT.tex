\section{Round-five positive closure: a fixed cut tower and the three-regime vector--roof limit}
\label{sec:r5-a2}

The radius belongs to
\[
 \mathcal I=[9/20,47/100].
\]
The principal changes are structural.  All moving singularity curves are
placed in one depth-weighted cut tower, the Dolgopyat cancellation operators
are constructed on that tower, and the roof-window theorem is separated into
local, central and wide regimes.  In particular no formula linear in the
window width is asserted when the width is larger than the central-limit
scale.

\subsection{Uniform geometry and the fixed all-depth cut tower}

Let \(T_R\) be the collision map of the triangular periodic Lorentz gas and
let \(\mathcal S_{R,n}\) be the union of singularity and homogeneity curves of
depth at most \(n\).  A component is labelled by a word
\(w=(\ell_0,k_0,\ldots,\ell_n,k_n,\varsigma)\), recording the lifted disk,
homogeneity strip and one-sided orientation.  The label set \(\mathfrak W\)
is independent of \(R\); a label whose geometric component is empty at a
particular radius is assigned zero trace space.

The nearest-neighbour gap and the corridor-blocking margin satisfy
\[
 g_*=1-2(47/100)=3/50,
 \qquad h_*=2(9/20)-\sqrt3/2>0.
\]
Consequently there are uniform flight bounds
\(0<\tau_-<\tau_+<\infty\), invariant cones, distortion constants and an
integer \(n_*\) for which the \(n_*\)-step expansion sum is strictly below
one.

\begin{lemma}[Depth complexity and trace multiplicity]
\label{lem:r5-a2-complexity}
There are \(C_0,C_1>1\) and \(r\ge0\), independent of \(R\), such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a homogeneous stable curve meets at most \(C_0^n\) components of
      \(\mathcal S_{R,n}\);
\item every depth-\(n\) component has, in the moving homogeneous coordinates,
      \(C^2\)-norm at most \(C_1^n(1+n)^r\);
\item the number of depth-\(n\) one-sided traces arriving at any fixed
      depth-\(m\) intersection is at most \(C_0^{n-m}\); and
\item all constants remain valid at births, deaths and tangencies when the
      two one-sided germs are retained separately.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At one collision a smooth unstable or stable curve is cut by the primary
singularity and by homogeneity boundaries.  The number of children is bounded
by a geometric series because the strip of index \(k\) has width
\(O(k^{-3})\), while inverse expansion is \(O(k^{-2})\).  Iterating gives
(i).  The mirror equation and the uniform flight/curvature bounds give one
factor \(C_1\) for each differentiated inverse branch; the polynomial factor
records the two possible differentiations of a product and proves (ii).
For (iii), follow every arriving germ backwards until its first split.  The
same child bound applies independently at each remaining depth.  At a
parameter tangency the normal coordinate
\(u=\operatorname{sgn}(\varphi)(\pi/2-|\varphi|)^{1/2}\) turns both curves
into uniformly \(C^2\) graphs.  Keeping the two germs rather than identifying
them makes the incidence relation upper semicontinuous and proves (iv).
\end{proof}

Choose \(0<\eta<\min(C_0^{-2},C_1^{-2})\).  Let
\(\mathcal B_R^{\rm bulk}\hookrightarrow\mathcal B_{R,w}^{\rm bulk}\) be the
usual strong/weak anisotropic pair.  For every \(w\in\mathfrak W\), let
\(\mathcal T_{R,w}\) be the space of order-zero and order-one oriented traces
on the corresponding singular component, and set
\[
 \mathbb B_R=
 \mathcal B_R^{\rm bulk}\oplus
 \ell^1\!\left(\mathfrak W;
   \eta^{|w|}(1+|w|)^{-r-3}\mathcal T_{R,w}\right).
\]
At an intersection the trace coordinate is the signed inclusion--exclusion
sum of its incident quadrant germs.  Thus no seam mass is counted twice.

The collision transfer operator has the triangular block form
\[
 \mathbb L_R=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  L_R^{bb}&0\\
  L_R^{cb}&L_R^{cc}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here \(L_R^{bb}\) is the ordinary branch transfer operator,
\(L_R^{cb}\) records traces created when a bulk branch is cut, and
\(L_R^{cc}\) pushes an oriented trace to all of its child traces.  For a
vector--roof twist put
\[
 \mathbb L_{R,\xi,s}h
 =\mathbb L_R\bigl(e^{\xi\cdot\kappa_R-s\tau_R}h\bigr),
\]
where multiplication on a trace is by the corresponding one-sided value.

\begin{theorem}[All-depth moving-cut bundle]
\label{thm:r5-a2-bundle}
There is a finite atlas \((U_j,\mathbb B_j)\) over \(\mathcal I\) and
identifications \(J_{R,j}:\mathbb B_R\to\mathbb B_j\) such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(J_{R,j}\) and its inverse are uniformly bounded on the strong and
      weak scales and preserve the embedded physical distribution;
\item the complete block operator \(J_{R,j}\mathbb L_{R,\xi,s}J_{R,j}^{-1}\)
      is bounded on \(\mathbb B_j\), including the current components;
\item on every compact complex source set it satisfies a uniform
      Lasota--Yorke inequality and is quasi-compact with a simple leading
      eigenvalue; and
\item the operator, eigenvalue and Riesz projector are \(C^2\) in \(R\) as
      maps from the strong space to the second weak space.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Continue every finite-depth singular graph in the homogeneous normal
coordinate.  The normal displacement of a depth-\(n\) graph is at most
\(C_1^n(1+n)^r|R-R_0|\).  On the trace summand its contribution is multiplied
by \(\eta^n(1+n)^{-r-3}\), and hence the sum of the first two parameter
derivatives converges absolutely.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-a2-complexity}(iii)
shows that the same is true after all intersection incidences are expanded.
This defines the chart maps and proves (i).

Branch multiplication is bounded on the bulk space by the usual distortion
estimate.  On a trace, the vector cocycle is locally constant and the roof and
its first two tangential derivatives have the homogeneity weights already
present in the trace norm.  The depth weight absorbs the child multiplicity,
so all four blocks are bounded and (ii) follows.  The bulk Lasota--Yorke
estimate and
\[
 \sum_{w':w\to w'}
 \frac{\eta^{|w'|}}{\eta^{|w|}}
 \le C_0\eta<1
\]
give the same contraction on the current block.  A finite-iterate renorming
turns it into a one-step inequality.  Compact embedding of the strong bulk
space and the summable trace tail give quasi-compactness.  Positivity and
mixing give simplicity at real sources; analytic perturbation gives (iii).
The absolutely convergent normal derivatives constructed above are precisely
the missing current-to-current and bulk-to-current derivative blocks.  The
resolvent identity on a contour around the simple eigenvalue then proves (iv).
\end{proof}

\subsection{An explicit temporal and lattice certificate}

Let the three neighbouring disk centres be
\[
 c_0=(0,0),\quad c_1=(1,0),\quad
 c_2=(1/2,\sqrt3/2).
\]
The inward normal points
\[
 p_0=(\sqrt3R/2,R/2),\quad
 p_1=(1-\sqrt3R/2,R/2),\quad
 p_2=(1/2,\sqrt3/2-R)
\]
form an equilateral triangle of side
\(d_R=1-\sqrt3R\).  The oriented words
\[
 w_+=(0,1,2,0),\qquad w_-=(0,2,1,0)
\]
are regular specular polygons for every \(R\in\mathcal I\).  If internal
arclength coordinates are eliminated by the specular equations, the internal
Hessian at the symmetric polygon is
\[
 H_R=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 a_R&-b_R\\-b_R&a_R
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 a_R=\frac{3}{2d_R}+\frac{\sqrt3}{R},
 \quad b_R=\frac{3}{4d_R}.
\]
Thus
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(H_R)=\frac{3}{4d_R}+rac{\sqrt3}{R}>0
\]
uniformly.  The endpoint momentum calculation gives
\[
 \partial_{r'}(\mathscr L_{R,+}-\mathscr L_{R,-})(0,0)=1.
\]

Choose the inducing magnet to contain a fixed small arc around each of the six
oriented endpoints of these polygons and around the four normal nearest-cell
flights with lattice increments \(\pm e_1,\pm e_2\).  The arcs are transported
in the moving-cut atlas.  The first return of each listed flight to this
multi-component magnet is itself a full quotient branch; no unnamed connector
is used.

\begin{lemma}[Certified UNI and full lattice span]
\label{lem:r5-a2-certificate}
After one radius-independent shrinking of the magnet there are two inverse
return branches \(h_{R,+},h_{R,-}\) on a common quotient interval \(J_0\)
for which the temporal distance \(\Psi_R\) satisfies
\[
 \inf_{R\in\mathcal I,x\in J_0}|\Psi_R'(x)|\ge1/4.
\]
The return cocycle also has four regular branches whose displacement
differences are \(\pm e_1,\pm e_2\).  Hence a unit-modulus eigenfunction for
frequency \((u,t)\in[-\pi,\pi]^2\times\mathbb R\) forces \(t=0\) and then
\(u=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalues of \(H_R\) displayed above are uniformly separated from zero,
so the implicit-function theorem supplies the two polygon branches on one
common endpoint interval.  The envelope theorem identifies the endpoint
derivative with the terminal tangential momentum; the two orientations differ
by one.  Stable holonomy changes this derivative by less than \(1/4\) after
shrinking the interval.  The backward temporal tail is a geometric series in
the common expansion constant and is made smaller than \(1/2\) by extending
both return words by the same number of neutral iterates inside the magnet.
The remaining derivative is at least \(1/4\).

For the lattice branches, a normal flight from a disk lift \(c\) to its
nearest lift \(c+e_j\), followed by its reflected reverse inside the magnet,
is regular because the gap is at least \(g_*\).  Reading the first half with
either orientation gives cocycle difference \(e_j\), while the common return
half cancels.  The same construction with the reversed lift gives \(-e_j\).
A peripheral eigenfunction satisfies the periodic branch identities.  Their
derivatives on the UNI pair force \(t=0\); division of the four lattice
identities forces \(e^{iu_j}=1\), hence \(u=0\) in the fundamental domain.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Dolgopyat operators}

Let \(F_R\) be the stable-quotient return map.  For \(|t|\ge1\), let
\(\mathcal C_t\) be the cone of positive pairs \((h,H)\) satisfying
\[
 |h'|\le A|t|H,\qquad |h|\le H,\qquad |H'|\le A|t|H.
\]
Partition \(J_0\) into intervals of length between
\(c_1|t|^{-1}\) and \(c_2|t|^{-1}\).  On each interval the phase difference
between the two UNI branches changes by a fixed angle.  A smooth cutoff
\(\chi_J\), equal to \(1-\delta\) on the subinterval where one branch could
dominate and equal to one off a slightly larger subinterval, defines the
Dolgopyat operator
\[
 \mathcal N_{R,t}H
 =\mathcal L_{R,0,0}^{n_t}(\chi H),
 \qquad n_t\le C\log(2+|t|).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Uniform high-frequency contraction]
\label{thm:r5-a2-dolgopyat}
There are \(A,C,c>0\), uniform in \(R\), such that the cone is invariant,
\[
 \|\mathcal N_{R,t}H\|_{L^2}^2
 \le(1-c)\|H\|_{L^2}^2,
\]
and
\[
 \|\mathbb L_{R,iu,it}^n\|_{\mathbb B_R\to\mathbb B_{R,w}}
 \le C(1+|t|)^A
 \exp\left\{-\frac{cn}{\log(2+|t|)}\right\}
\]
for \(|t|\ge1\) and \(u\in[-\pi,\pi]^2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-a2-certificate} gives phase derivative separation
\(\ge |t|/4\).  On every partition interval van der Corput's estimate makes
the modulus of the paired branches at most
\((1-c_0)\) times their positive majorant on a subinterval of fixed relative
length.  The cutoff \(\chi_J\) implements exactly this loss and its derivative
is \(O(|t|)\), so the cone inequalities are preserved.

The growth lemma for the uniformly finite-horizon family says that a fixed
fraction of every standard family reaches the UNI magnet in at most
\(C\log(2+|t|)\) iterates; families which do not reach it have total weight
at most one half after increasing the constant.  Integrating the cutoff loss
over the reached fraction gives the displayed \(L^2\) contraction.  Iteration
and the Lasota--Yorke inequality convert it to the strong-to-weak operator
bound.  On a depth-\(m\) current coordinate the same argument is applied to
the one-sided branch, while the unreached descendants have norm at most
\((C_0\eta)^m\).  Summing the geometric trace tail proves the estimate on the
complete cut bundle.  Compact nonzero frequency annuli are handled by
Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-a2-certificate} and upper semicontinuity of the spectral
radius.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Covariance and the correct window regimes}

Let
\[
 K_n=S_n\kappa_R,\qquad T_n=S_n\tau_R,
\]
and work under a real simple tilted phase.  Put
\[
 a=\partial_\xi P_R(\xi,s),\qquad
 \bar\tau=-\partial_sP_R(\xi,s),
\]
and write the covariance in block form
\[
 \Sigma=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \Sigma_{KK}&\Sigma_{KT}\\
  \Sigma_{TK}&\Sigma_{TT}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \sigma_c^2=\Sigma_{TT}-
 \Sigma_{TK}\Sigma_{KK}^{-1}\Sigma_{KT}.
\]
For \(k_n-na=O(\sqrt n)\), define the conditional roof centre
\[
 m_n=n\bar\tau+
 \Sigma_{TK}\Sigma_{KK}^{-1}(k_n-na),
 \qquad r_n=t_n-m_n.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Positive joint covariance]
\label{lem:r5-a2-covariance}
The matrix \(\Sigma\) is uniformly positive definite on compact real source
sets and \(R\in\mathcal I\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The spectral gap gives the Gordin decomposition.  Zero variance of
\(b\cdot\kappa+c\tau\) would make this observable a quotient coboundary.
Evaluation on the two UNI return words and differentiation gives \(c=0\).
Evaluation on the four lattice branches of
Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-a2-certificate} then gives \(b\cdot e_1=b\cdot e_2=0\).
Thus \(b=c=0\).  Compactness of the radius and source sets upgrades pointwise
positivity to a uniform lower eigenvalue bound.
\end{proof}

Let \(\Phi\) be the standard normal distribution function.  Fourier inversion
uses the spectral expansion for frequencies of order \(n^{-1/2}\), the compact
aperiodicity gap, and Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-a2-dolgopyat} on the high-frequency
region.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform vector--roof local limit in three regimes]
\label{thm:r5-a2-llt}
Uniformly for \(R\) and sources in compact regular sets, for
\(k_n-na=O(\sqrt n)\), \(r_n=O(\sqrt n)\), and \(b_n=o(n)\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb P\{K_n=k_n,\ |T_n-t_n|\le b_n\}\\
 &\quad=
 \frac{\exp\{-\frac1{2n}(k_n-na)^T
       \Sigma_{KK}^{-1}(k_n-na)\}}
      {2\pi n\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{KK}}}
 \left[
  \Phi\!\left(\frac{r_n+b_n}{\sqrt{n}\sigma_c}\right)
 -\Phi\!\left(\frac{r_n-b_n}{\sqrt{n}\sigma_c}\right)
 \right](1+o(1)).
 \end{aligned}
\]
After undoing the real tilt this expression is multiplied by the exact
exponential change-of-measure factor.

In particular:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item if \(b_n=o(\sqrt n)\), the bracket equals
\[
 \frac{2b_n}{\sqrt{2\pi n}\sigma_c}
 e^{-r_n^2/(2n\sigma_c^2)}(1+o(1));
\]
\item if \(b_n/\sqrt n\to\beta\in(0,\infty)\), the bracket converges to the
      corresponding nontrivial Gaussian interval mass; and
\item if \(b_n/\sqrt n\to\infty\) and \(r_n=O(\sqrt n)\), the bracket tends
      to one, so the joint probability is of order \(n^{-1}\), not
      \(b_nn^{-3/2}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use lattice Fourier inversion in \(u\in[-\pi,\pi]^2\) and a smoothed interval
indicator in the roof frequency.  In the ball
\(|u|+|t|\le n^{-2/5}\), analytic perturbation gives
\[
 \log\lambda_R(iu,it)
 =i u\cdot a-it\bar\tau
 -\tfrac12(u,-t)^T\Sigma(u,-t)+O(|u|+|t|)^3.
\]
Scaling by \(n^{-1/2}\) and integrating first in the roof variable gives the
conditional Gaussian interval mass displayed in the theorem.  The smoothing
boundary has absolute error
\(o(n^{-1})\) by choosing its width
\(n^{-1/2}\gamma_n^{-1}\), with
\(\gamma_n\to\infty\) slower than both
\(\sqrt n/b_n\) in the local regime and \(b_n/\sqrt n\) in the wide regime.
On compact nonzero frequencies the aperiodicity gap is exponential; on the
remaining roof frequencies Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-a2-dolgopyat} is integrable
after two integrations by parts.  These bounds are uniform and prove the main
formula.  The three consequences are respectively the local expansion,
finite scaling limit, and saturation of the same Gaussian bracket.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Physical pressure and conditioned paths}

Let \(Q_R(H)\) be the unique root in \(s\) of
\(P_R(H-s\tau_R)=0\).  The bundle theorem gives analytic dependence, while
Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-a2-covariance} makes the root Hessian a positive Schur
complement.  Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-a2-llt} to cylinder insertions gives
the ratio theorem in each of the three window regimes and hence convergence
of the conditioned path law to the corresponding exposed phase.

\begin{theorem}[Round-five A2 closure]
\label{thm:r5-a2-main}
The Lorentz family has a radius-uniform all-depth cut bundle, a complete
vector--roof Dolgopyat estimate, positive joint covariance, the correct
three-regime lattice--roof local limit theorem, and analytic physical pressure
with source-dependent conditioned path laws.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r5-a2-bundle},
\ref{thm:r5-a2-dolgopyat}, \ref{thm:r5-a2-llt}, and the pressure-root argument
above.
\end{proof}
