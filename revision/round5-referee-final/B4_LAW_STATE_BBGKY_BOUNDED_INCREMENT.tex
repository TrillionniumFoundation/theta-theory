\section{Round-five positive closure: exact law states, full BBGKY correctors, and bounded-increment kinetic semigroups}
\label{sec:r5-b4}

The exact finite system is deterministic on microstates and linear on complete
laws.  The correlation hierarchy is an injective coordinate system for a law,
not an algebra of random states.  The nonlinear kinetic semigroup appears only
after the density--contact contraction proved in B2.

\subsection{The exact microscopic state and its algebra}

Let
\[
 \Omega_\varepsilon=igsqcup_{N\ge0}\Omega_{\varepsilon,N}
\]
be the disjoint hard-sphere phase space and let
\(\Phi_t^\varepsilon\) be its deterministic flow.  A complete ensemble state
is a probability \(\rho\in\mathcal P(\Omega_\varepsilon)\), evolved by
\[
 U_\varepsilon(t)\rho=(\Phi_t^\varepsilon)_\#\rho.
\]
Its factorial correlation hierarchy
\(G(\rho)=(g_k(\rho))_{k\ge0}\) determines \(\rho\) on every exponential
particle-number moment class.  Multiplication is performed in the genuine
algebra \(C_b(\mathcal P(\Omega_\varepsilon))\), not componentwise on the
linear hierarchy coordinates.

For a bounded empirical cylinder \(F\), define the exponential cylinder on law
states by
\[
 \mathbf E_{\varepsilon,F}(\rho)=
 \frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\int e^{\mu_\varepsilon F(\pi(Z))}\rho(dZ).
\]
For an arbitrary bounded functional \(\Phi\) on law states set
\[
 (\mathbf V_\varepsilon(t)\Phi)(\rho)=
 \Phi(U_\varepsilon(t)\rho).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact law-state tower]
\label{prop:r5-b4-exact}
The operators \(\mathbf V_\varepsilon(t)\) form an exact composition semigroup
on \(C_b(\mathcal P(\Omega_\varepsilon))\).  Every exponential cylinder is a
member of this algebra and is computable from the full correlation hierarchy.
No exact Markov or algebra closure on \(g_1\), or on a fixed finite hierarchy
level, is used.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Pushforward satisfies
\(U_\varepsilon(t+s)=U_\varepsilon(t)U_\varepsilon(s)\); composition of
functionals gives the semigroup identity.  Factorial correlations determine
all symmetric exponential moments by their absolutely convergent factorial
series on the declared number-moment class, so they compute
\(\mathbf E_{\varepsilon,F}\).  The hierarchy is used only as coordinates of
\(\rho\), which avoids assigning a random meaning to a deterministic
hierarchy vector.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Full triangular BBGKY correctors}

Let \(S_j^0(t)\) be free transport of \(j\) labels and let
\(C_{j,j+1}^\varepsilon\) be the hard-sphere boundary operator coupling level
\(j\) to \(j+1\).  The complete BBGKY Duhamel propagator is the time-ordered
series
\[
 \mathcal U_{j\leftarrow k}^\varepsilon(t)=
 \int_{0<t_{j+1}<\cdots<t_k<t}
 S_j^0(t-t_{j+1})C_{j,j+1}^\varepsilon\cdots
 C_{k-1,k}^\varepsilon S_k^0(t_k)
 \,dt_{j+1}\cdots dt_k.
\]
Thus no autonomous fixed-\(j\) connected propagator is introduced.

For a density cylinder \(F\), write \(p=\delta F/\delta f\).  Expand the
exponential cylinder in factorial cumulants and define recursively
\[
 \mathfrak c_{j+1}[F](t)
 =-\int_0^tS_{j+1}^0(t-s)
 \mathfrak d_{j+1}[F](s)\,ds,
\]
where \(\mathfrak d_{j+1}\) is the uncancelled connected defect created at
level \(j+1\) by all lower correctors and the coupling operators.

\begin{lemma}[Triangular correlation-corrector expansion]
\label{lem:r5-b4-corrector}
For every cylinder \(F\) and integer \(K\), the corrected functional
\[
 F_{\varepsilon}^{(K)}(G)=
 F(g_1)+\sum_{j=2}^K\mu_\varepsilon^{1-j}
 \mathfrak c_j[F](g_1,\ldots,g_j)
\]
satisfies on hierarchy moment balls
\[
 \mathscr H_\varepsilon F_{\varepsilon}^{(K)}
 =\mathbb H\left(g_1,p,\psi\right)
 +R_{K,\varepsilon},
\]
\[
 |R_{K,\varepsilon}|
 \le C_F(\rho^K+\varepsilon^\alpha).
\]
Every \(\mathfrak c_j\) is the finite sum of time-ordered operators
\(\mathcal U_{r\leftarrow j}^\varepsilon\) with \(r\le j\); it is not evolved
by an isolated fixed-level semigroup.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the full BBGKY generator to the factorial cumulant expansion.  Its
boundary term at level one is
\[
 \int(e^{\Delta p+\psi}-1)dA_{g_1}.
\]
Every other term has a unique highest label count.  At level \(j+1\), collect
all defects generated by the already chosen levels and solve their free
transport equation by the displayed Duhamel integral.  This cancels the whole
level and moves the remainder to higher labels.  Repeated substitution gives
exactly the ordered operators \(\mathcal U_{r\leftarrow j}^\varepsilon\).
B2's tree norm bounds the connected \(j\)-label part by
\(j!C^jT^{j-1}\), so the weighted tail is \(C\rho^K\).  Its genealogical
Gramian estimate gives \(C\varepsilon^\alpha\) for every term containing a
surplus contact.  The same construction with one actual-contact insertion
places \(\psi\) inside the collision exponential.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The bounded-increment Hamiltonian core}

Let \(\mathscr D\) consist of cylinders whose cotangent has the form
\[
 p(t,x,v)=p_b(t,x,v)+a(t,x)+b(t,x)\cdot v+c(t,x)|v|^2,
\]
where \(p_b\) and its transport derivative are weighted bounded and
\[
 \|\Delta p_b+\psi\|_\infty<\infty.
\]
For the affine momentum and kinetic-energy part, elastic conservation gives
\(\Delta(a+b\cdot v+c|v|^2)=0\) when the coefficients are evaluated at the
common collision point.  Hence \(\Delta p+\psi\) is bounded on the core even
though \(p\) contains the unbounded conserved cotangents.

Let
\[
 \mathcal E_M=\left\{f\ge0:
 \int(1+|v|^2)f\le M\right\}
\]
with the weighted weak metric augmented by convergence of the second moment.

\begin{lemma}[Hamiltonian finiteness and continuity]
\label{lem:r5-b4-hamiltonian}
For \(p,q\in\mathscr D\) with a common core bound and
\(f,g\in\mathcal E_M\),
\[
 |\mathbb H(f,p,\psi)-\mathbb H(g,q,\psi)|
 \le \omega_M\left(d_2(f,g)+
  \|p-q\|_{\mathscr D}\right)
\]
for a modulus \(\omega_M(r)\downarrow0\).  In particular the collision
Hamiltonian is finite on energy sublevels without assuming an exponential
one-particle tail.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The exponential factor is uniformly bounded because the collision increment
is bounded.  The remaining collision integrand grows only like
\(|v-v_*|\), which is uniformly integrable under a second-moment bound.
Truncate velocities, use weak continuity on the compact part, and let the
cutoff grow using Cauchy--Schwarz.  The transport term is treated identically
with its declared weighted derivative.  This gives the modulus.  The
conserved polynomial part contributes no collision exponential growth.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Corrected nonlinear-generator convergence]
\label{thm:r5-b4-generator}
For every \(F\in\mathscr D\) there are corrected tests
\(F_\varepsilon\to F\) such that the upper and lower relaxed nonlinear
generators converge locally uniformly on regular energy moment balls to
\[
 \mathbb H\left(f,\frac{\delta F}{\delta f},\psi\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(K=K(\varepsilon)\to\infty\) so slowly that
\(\rho^{K(\varepsilon)}+\varepsilon^\alpha\to0\).  The hierarchy weights and
Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-b4-corrector} make the corrector series Cauchy uniformly on
the moment ball.  Positivity of factorial moment functionals permits
approximation from above and below by finite hierarchy truncations.  The
Hamiltonian continuity lemma identifies both relaxed limits.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The kinetic action semigroup}

For a balanced pair \((f,\Gamma)\) on \([s,t]\), let
\[
 \mathcal A_{s,t}(f,\Gamma)=
 \int_s^t\ell(d\Gamma/dA_f)dA_f.
\]
For bounded uniformly continuous terminal data \(\Phi\), define
\[
 (S_{s,t}\Phi)(f_s)=
 \sup_{(f,\Gamma):f(s)=f_s}
 \{\Phi(f_t)-\mathcal A_{s,t}(f,\Gamma)\}.
\]
This semigroup is the contraction of B2's proved density--actual-contact LDP;
it is not asserted to be the exact finite-\(\varepsilon\) density dynamics.

\begin{lemma}[Action compactness and concatenation]
\label{lem:r5-b4-action-compact}
On each energy sublevel, the sublevels of \(\mathcal A_{s,t}\) are compact in
the weighted path topology.  Balanced pairs concatenate, and their actions
add exactly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Entropy coercivity controls the contact mass relative to \(A_f\); energy
controls its velocity tails.  The balance identity gives time equicontinuity
on a countable core, so weighted Prokhorov compactness applies.  Closedness of
balance and lower semicontinuity of integral entropy give compact sublevels.
At a concatenation time the endpoint measures agree; the two weak balance
identities telescope and the entropy integral splits.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Kinetic Lax--Oleinik semigroup]
\label{thm:r5-b4-action}
The operators \(S_{s,t}\) are monotone, cash additive and satisfy
\[
 S_{r,t}=S_{r,s}S_{s,t}.
\]
For \(\Phi\in\mathscr D\), \(U(s,f)=S_{s,T}\Phi(f)\) is a viscosity solution
of
\[
 \partial_sU+
 \mathbb H\left(f,\frac{\delta U}{\delta f},0\right)=0,
 \qquad U(T,f)=\Phi(f).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Attainment follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-b4-action-compact}.  Splitting an
admissible path gives one dynamic-programming inequality; concatenating two
optimizers gives the other.  On a short interval, Fenchel duality between
\(e^z-1\) and \(\ell\) gives the viscosity upper inequality.  For the lower
inequality use the regular B2 tilted recovery path with source
\(p=\delta F/\delta f\), then approximate a finite-action path by B2's
finite-cell conservative repair.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Comparison on the energy domain}

Use the containment function
\[
 \Upsilon(f)=\int(1+|v|^2)f.
\]
Its collision increment is zero and its transport Hamiltonian is bounded by
\(C(1+\Upsilon)\).  Let \((\phi_j)\) be a countable weighted core and define
finite penalties
\[
 \Psi_{J,\eta}(f,g)=
 \frac1{2\eta}\sum_{j=1}^J2^{-j}
 |\langle f-g,\phi_j\rangle|^2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Comparison and uniqueness]
\label{thm:r5-b4-comparison}
Bounded upper-semicontinuous subsolutions and bounded lower-semicontinuous
supersolutions tested by \(\mathscr D\) and \(\Upsilon\), with the same
terminal data, satisfy \(u\le v\).  Hence the action semigroup is the unique
viscosity solution in this class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume a positive maximum of \(u-v\).  Double variables with
\(\Psi_{J,\eta}\), a time penalty, and
\(\delta(\Upsilon(f)+\Upsilon(g))\).  The containment term keeps maximizers in
one energy sublevel.  Derivatives of the finite penalty belong to
\(\mathscr D\); its unbounded invariant part has zero collision increment.
Apply the sub- and supersolution inequalities.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-b4-hamiltonian}
shows that the Hamiltonian difference tends to zero as
\(\eta\downarrow0\), then \(J\to\infty\).  The containment error vanishes as
\(\delta\downarrow0\), while a standard strict time perturbation leaves a
negative constant, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Microscopic convergence and preparation}

\begin{theorem}[Kinetic excess-pressure convergence]
\label{thm:r5-b4-limit}
For bounded uniformly continuous terminal data and compact regular initial
families, the finite hard-sphere excess pressures converge locally uniformly
to \(S_{s,t}\Phi\).  The same holds with bounded actual-contact work.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition the B2 good joint LDP at the initial density and apply the Laplace
principle.  Its transition cost is exactly \(\mathcal A_{s,t}\), giving the
variational formula.  B2 exponential compact containment makes the convergence
uniform on compact initial families.  The generator theorem and comparison
identify the same limit on the full terminal class.
\end{proof}

Primitive microcanonical preparation is a static initial operation.  Keep its
multiplier \(\lambda\) as a frozen coordinate and define the fixed-\(\lambda\)
kinetic semigroup.  Optimize \(\lambda\) only once in the initial boundary
functional.  Dynamic mass, momentum and energy conservation is distinct: it
follows from \(\Delta(1,v,|v|^2)=0\) and is not an additional repeated
preparation constraint.

\begin{theorem}[Prepared semigroup and Gaussian tangent]
\label{thm:r5-b4-prepared}
The source-dependent B1 saddle contracts the lifted fixed-\(\lambda\) kinetic
semigroup at time zero and preserves its tower.  Under the B3 central-limit
scaling the kinetic semigroups converge to the risk-sensitive Gaussian
semigroup with generator
\[
 b\cdot DF+\frac12A:D^2F+rac\theta2DF^TADF,
\]
where \(A\) contains the B1 conditioned initial covariance and all B3 density--
contact cross brackets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At fixed \(\lambda\), Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-b4-action} gives the tower.  B1
performs the source-dependent initial saddle before the first transition, so
the same \(\lambda\) is retained across every factor.  B3 gives joint Gaussian
convergence and locally uniform analytic pressure.  Expanding the exponential
semigroup at central-limit scale leaves the linear drift, covariance and the
quadratic logarithmic-transform term displayed above.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-five B4 closure]
\label{thm:r5-b4-main}
The exact finite theory is a composition semigroup on complete law states;
correlations are coordinates, not an algebra.  Full triangular BBGKY
correctors converge on the bounded-collision-increment core, and the B2 action
produces the unique energy-domain kinetic viscosity semigroup with static
microcanonical preparation and the B3 Gaussian tangent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Proposition~\ref{prop:r5-b4-exact} and
Theorems~\ref{thm:r5-b4-generator}, \ref{thm:r5-b4-action},
\ref{thm:r5-b4-comparison}, \ref{thm:r5-b4-limit}, and
\ref{thm:r5-b4-prepared}.
\end{proof}
