\section{Round-five positive closure: posterior law states, normalized block games, and canonical LAN}
\label{sec:r5-c1}

The finite controlled state is the regular conditional law of the complete
microstate given the resolved history.  Its conditional correlation hierarchy
is a coordinate representation of that random law.  Every block likelihood is
normalized against this posterior state, and the continuous phase posterior is
a density with respect to a fixed prior measure.

\subsection{The exact information state}

Let \(Z_t^\varepsilon=\Phi_t^\varepsilon Z_0\) be the hard-sphere microstate and
let \(\mathcal F_t^{\rm res}\) be the completed filtration generated by the
resolved empirical history and the declared observed contact marks.  Since the
phase space is standard Borel, choose a regular conditional probability
\[
 \Pi_t^\varepsilon(\omega)=
 \operatorname{Law}(Z_t^\varepsilon\mid\mathcal F_t^{\rm res})(\omega).
\]
Its conditional factorial moments are denoted
\(G_t^\varepsilon=G(\Pi_t^\varepsilon)\).

\begin{lemma}[Measurable posterior hierarchy]
\label{lem:r5-c1-posterior}
The map \(\omega\mapsto\Pi_t^\varepsilon(\omega)\) is measurable as a random
probability measure, and every component of \(G_t^\varepsilon\) is its
conditional factorial moment.  Conversely, on the exponential
particle-number moment class the complete conditional hierarchy determines
\(\Pi_t^\varepsilon\).  Thus a conditional normalizer is a nontrivial random
quantity unless the resolved filtration already reveals the full microstate.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Regular conditional probabilities on standard Borel spaces are measurable in
the weak topology.  Conditional factorial moments follow by applying the
defining disintegration identity to symmetric cylinder functions.  The
factorial moment expansion of the Laplace functional is absolutely convergent
on the number-moment class and determines the conditional law.  If the full
microstate is observed, the posterior becomes the Dirac mass at that state;
otherwise its Laplace functional is generally random and nondegenerate.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exactly normalized actual-contact blocks}

Fix a partition \(0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=T\).  On block \(k\), predictable controls
\(u_k,v_k\) select a bounded running reward and a bounded actual-contact source
\(\psi^{u_k,v_k}\).  For a current microstate \(z\), let
\(Y_k^{u_k,v_k}(z)\) be the block work evaluated along the true deterministic
hard-sphere segment starting from \(z\).  Define the conditional log normalizer
\[
 \Lambda_k^{u_k,v_k}
 =\log\int
 e^{\mu_\varepsilon Y_k^{u_k,v_k}(z)}
 \Pi_{t_k}^\varepsilon(dz)
\]
and the block density increment
\[
 L_{k+1}=L_k
 \exp\{\mu_\varepsilon Y_k^{u_k,v_k}
       -\Lambda_k^{u_k,v_k}\},
 \qquad L_0=1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Posterior-normalized likelihood]
\label{thm:r5-c1-likelihood}
For every predictable piecewise-constant strategy, \((L_k)\) is a positive
mean-one martingale with respect to \(\mathcal F_{t_k}^{\rm res}\).  The Bayes
update of the information state is
\[
 \Pi_{t_{k+1}}^{\varepsilon,u,v}(A)=
 \frac{\int 1_A(\Phi_{\Delta_k}^\varepsilon z)
       e^{\mu_\varepsilon Y_k^{u_k,v_k}(z)}
       \Pi_{t_k}^{\varepsilon,u,v}(dz)}
      {e^{\Lambda_k^{u_k,v_k}}}.
\]
Only actual contacts in the block enter \(Y_k\); no Poisson compensator or
change of elastic geometry is introduced.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditioning at time \(t_k\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E[L_{k+1}/L_k\mid\mathcal F_{t_k}^{\rm res}]
 =e^{-\Lambda_k}
 \int e^{\mu_\varepsilon Y_k(z)}\Pi_{t_k}(dz)=1.
\]
Bayes' formula gives the posterior update.  The functional
\(Y_k(z)\) is evaluated by the deterministic flow and its actual contact
ledger, so the normalization changes only path weights.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform conditional source charts}

Let \(\mathfrak K_{\alpha,\beta}(M)\) be the set of posterior laws whose
complete hierarchy norm and relative entropy with respect to a Maxwellian
reference are at most \(M\).  B2's source-sewing map acts on complete
hierarchies and therefore also on conditional posterior states.

\begin{lemma}[Strategy-uniform posterior stability]
\label{lem:r5-c1-stability}
For compact control sets and uniformly bounded block sources, there are a
partition mesh and \(M'>M\) such that every Bayes update maps
\[
 \mathfrak K_{\alpha,\beta}(M)
 \longrightarrow\mathfrak K_{\alpha',\beta'}(M')
\]
uniformly over all predictable strategies.  The conditional pressures and
their first two source derivatives converge locally uniformly on this compact
posterior class.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The numerator of the Bayes update is the exact conditional Feynman--Kac map
used in the B2 source-sewing theorem.  Its hierarchy loss on a block is
\(C\Delta e^{CR}\); choose the mesh so that the finite sum of losses remains
inside the initial label weight.  The denominator is bounded above by the same
Feynman--Kac estimate and below by Jensen's inequality.  The entropy chain
rule adds at most the bounded block pressure.  Iteration yields one compact
posterior class.  Normal convergence on a slightly larger block source ball
and Cauchy's formula give uniform derivatives.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Three distinct games}

For a frozen preparation phase \(z\), let \(S_{s,t}^z\) be the B4 kinetic
semigroup.

\begin{theorem}[One-time preparation game]
\label{thm:r5-c1-prep}
If \(u,v\) are selected only at time zero and determine a frozen phase
\(z(u,v)\), then
\[
 V_{s,t}^{\rm prep}\Phi=
 \sup_u\inf_v S_{s,t}^{z(u,v)}\Phi.
\]
Across a split time the same pair \((u,v)\) is retained.  Reoptimizing at the
split defines a different game.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After the initial choice, B4 gives the fixed-phase transition tower.  The
outer saddle is taken only after the complete fixed-phase composition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Reward-only feedback game]
\label{thm:r5-c1-reward}
When controls change only a bounded running reward \(c(f,u,v)\), the value is
the unique B4 viscosity solution of
\[
 \partial_tU+\mathbb H_z(f,DU,0)+\sup_u\inf_v c(f,u,v)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The transition action is control independent.  Discrete dynamic programming
and measurable selection optimize only the additive reward; B4 comparison
passes the mesh to zero.
\end{proof}

For adaptive law control, let
\(q^{u,v}\) be a compact positive family with
\(0<q_-\le q^{u,v}\le q_+\).  The block contact source is
\(\psi^{u,v}=\log q^{u,v}\), normalized as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-c1-likelihood}.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive posterior-state game]
\label{thm:r5-c1-adaptive}
On the exact finite system, the dynamic state is
\((H_t^\varepsilon,\Pi_t^\varepsilon)\) and the normalized block game obeys a
dynamic-programming principle.  If the Isaacs equality holds, its values
converge locally uniformly to the unique kinetic viscosity solution
\[
 \partial_tU+\sup_u\inf_v
 \left\{
  \langle f,v\cdot\nabla DU\rangle+
  \int q^{u,v}(e^{\Delta DU}-1)dA_f+c(f,u,v)
 \right\}=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-c1-likelihood} gives exact conditional composition on
posterior states.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-c1-stability} makes the B2 compactness and
source estimates uniform over strategies.  Half-relaxed limits of the block
DPP are viscosity sub- and supersolutions with the displayed Hamiltonians.
The B4 doubled-variable proof is uniform over compact controls, so Isaacs
equality gives comparison.  The posterior coordinate contracts to the
resolved density in the kinetic limit but remains part of the exact prelimit
state.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Smooth saddle response}

Let \(z\) be a finite-dimensional smooth preparation coordinate and put
\[
 G(\phi,z)=\vartheta^{-1}
 \log\mathbb E_z e^{\vartheta\phi}+k(z).
\]
Assume the signed Hessian of \(k\) dominates the B3 phase covariance on the
chosen chart.

\begin{theorem}[Optimizer-response Hessian]
\label{thm:r5-c1-schur}
There is a unique smooth local saddle \(z^*(\phi)\), and
\[
 DV(\phi)[h]=\mathbb E_{Q_{\phi,z^*}}h,
\]
\[
 D^2V(\phi)[h,k]=
 \vartheta\operatorname{Cov}_{Q_{\phi,z^*}}(h,k)
 -G_{\phi z}[h]G_{zz}^{-1}G_{z\phi}[k].
\]
For a finite envelope this formula applies only on a uniquely active smooth
branch; at a crossing the directional active-branch envelope is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Strong signed convexity makes \(G_z=0\) uniquely solvable.  The implicit
function theorem gives
\(Dz^*=-G_{zz}^{-1}G_{z\phi}\); differentiating the envelope twice gives the
formula.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Continuous phase posteriors}

Let \(\Theta\subset\mathbb R^d\) be compact and let the prior have a continuous
density \(r_0\) bounded above and below on a neighborhood of the true
parameter \(\theta_0\).  Let \(L_T^\varepsilon(\theta)\) be the likelihood
relative to one fixed reference phase.  The posterior density is
\[
 r_T^\varepsilon(\theta)=
 \frac{r_0(\theta)L_T^\varepsilon(\theta)}
      {\int_\Theta r_0(\vartheta)L_T^\varepsilon(\vartheta)d\vartheta}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Uniform posterior phase contraction]
\label{thm:r5-c1-posterior-contraction}
Assume the B2 path statistic identifies \(\theta_0\), the relative entropy
rate \(\mathcal K_T(\theta_0\Vert\theta)\) is continuous, and the metric
entropy of \(\Theta\) is polynomial.  For every neighborhood \(O\) of
\(\theta_0\),
\[
 \limsup_{\varepsilon\to0}
 \frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\mathbb E_{\theta_0}
 \int_{O^c}r_T^\varepsilon(\theta)d\theta
 \le-\inf_{\theta\notin O}
 \mathcal K_T(\theta_0\Vert\theta)<0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Cover \(O^c\) by an \(\varepsilon\)-net whose logarithmic cardinality is
\(o(\mu_\varepsilon)\).  Uniform continuity of the sewn B2 pressure controls
the likelihood over every net cell by its centre plus \(o(\mu_\varepsilon)\).
The numerator is therefore bounded by the largest likelihood exponent off
\(O\) times a subexponential covering factor.  The denominator is bounded
below by integrating over a fixed small ball on which the prior density is
positive and the relative entropy is arbitrarily small.  Taking expectation
and applying the uniform pressure bound gives the result.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Canonical LAN and Bernstein--von Mises}

Restrict now to a finite-dimensional canonical source chart
\[
 \frac{d\mathbb P_{\theta,\varepsilon}}
      {d\mathbb P_{0,\varepsilon}}
 =\exp\{\mu_\varepsilon[	heta\cdot X_\varepsilon
       -Q_\varepsilon(\theta)+Q_\varepsilon(0)]\}.
\]
Let \(I(\theta)=D^2Q(\theta)\), positive by the B3 quotient kernel theorem,
and
\[
 \Delta_\varepsilon=\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}
 (X_\varepsilon-DQ(\theta_0)).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Uniform canonical LAN]
\label{thm:r5-c1-lan}
For every compact \(H\subset\mathbb R^d\), uniformly for \(h\in H\),
\[
 \log\frac{d\mathbb P_{\theta_0+h/\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon},\varepsilon}}
          {d\mathbb P_{\theta_0,\varepsilon}}
 =h\cdot\Delta_\varepsilon
 -\frac12h^TI(\theta_0)h+o_{\mathbb P_{\theta_0}}(1),
\]
and
\(\Delta_\varepsilon\Rightarrow N(0,I(\theta_0))\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Taylor-expand the finite-volume analytic pressure on a larger complex chart.
Uniform third derivatives make the remainder
\(O(|h|^3/\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon})\).  B3's joint Gaussian theorem gives the
score limit and identifies its covariance with the Hessian.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical Bernstein--von Mises theorem]
\label{thm:r5-c1-bvm}
Assume in addition local identifiability and the prior thickness stated above.
Under \(\mathbb P_{\theta_0,\varepsilon}\), the posterior law of
\(h=\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}(\theta-\theta_0)\) converges in total variation to
\[
 N\left(I(\theta_0)^{-1}\Delta,
        I(\theta_0)^{-1}\right),
\]
where \(\Delta\sim N(0,I(\theta_0))\).  Equivalently, after centering at the
local efficient estimator, the limit is
\(N(0,I(\theta_0)^{-1})\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On every fixed \(h\)-ball, Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-c1-lan} and continuity of the
prior give uniform convergence of the posterior density to the completed-square
Gaussian density.  Outside a large fixed ball but inside a small parameter
neighborhood, strict Hessian positivity gives the testing bound
\(e^{-c|h|^2}\).  Outside that neighborhood,
Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-c1-posterior-contraction} gives an exponentially small
posterior mass.  These two tail controls make the local density convergence
uniformly integrable and hence total-variation convergence follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-five C1 closure]
\label{thm:r5-c1-main}
The finite controlled model has a measurable posterior law state and exact
posterior-normalized actual-contact likelihoods; one-time preparation,
reward-only feedback and adaptive law control have distinct dynamic programs;
and regular canonical phase charts satisfy uniform posterior contraction,
LAN and Bernstein--von Mises limits.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r5-c1-likelihood},
\ref{thm:r5-c1-prep}, \ref{thm:r5-c1-reward},
\ref{thm:r5-c1-adaptive}, \ref{thm:r5-c1-schur},
\ref{thm:r5-c1-posterior-contraction},
\ref{thm:r5-c1-lan}, and \ref{thm:r5-c1-bvm}.
\end{proof}
