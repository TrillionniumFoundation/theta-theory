\section{Round-five positive closure: genealogical Gramians, future-preserving contact ledgers, and source sewing}
\label{sec:r5-b2}

All marks in this section are attached to actual hard-sphere contacts.  The
first surplus contact is estimated by a quantitative controllability Gramian.
After that contact the true post-collisional state and every later collision
are retained; no collision is deleted from a pseudo-trajectory.  Arbitrarily
large bounded real sources on a fixed short horizon are handled by exact time
slicing on the complete hierarchy state.

\subsection{Actual contacts and scattering coordinates}

Let \(d=3\), let the sphere diameter be \(\varepsilon\), and let
\(\mu_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-2}\).  On the regular hard-sphere phase space
write \(\Phi_t^\varepsilon Z\) for the billiard flow.  For a contact of
\(i<j\), put
\[
 \omega=\frac{x_j-x_i}{\varepsilon},
 \qquad g=(v_i-v_j)\cdot\omega>0
\]
for the incoming convention, and use the elastic reflection
\[
 v_i'=v_i-g\omega,\qquad v_j'=v_j+g\omega.
\]
The empirical particle and actual-contact measures are
\[
 \pi_t^\varepsilon=rac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \sum_i\delta_{(x_i(t),v_i(t))},
\]
\[
 \Gamma_T^\varepsilon=rac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \sum_{i<j}\sum_{t\in\mathcal C_{ij}\cap[0,T]}
 \delta_{(t,x_i(t),v_i(t-),v_j(t-),\omega(t))}.
\]
Their exact weak balance is obtained by integrating a test along free flights
and summing its jumps at every actual contact.

A chronological creation forest is parameterized by root positions and, for
each creation edge \(e\), by time \(t_e\), incoming velocity and transverse
impact coordinate \(\eta_e\in\mathbb R^2\).  The scattering map is canonical
in these coordinates.  Let \(J_e(s)\) be the Jacobi field at time \(s\)
created by varying \(\eta_e\) and retaining all preceding parameters.

\subsection{The genealogical controllability Gramian}

Fix a creation tree and two labels \(a,b\).  Let \(\mathcal P(a,b)\) be the
edge path joining them.  Immediately before a proposed surplus contact at time
\(s\), put \(r_{ab}(s)=x_a(s)-x_b(s)\), let
\(\Pi_{ab}^\perp\) be projection onto the plane perpendicular to
\(v_a(s)-v_b(s)\), and define
\[
 \mathcal G_{ab}(s)=
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal P(a,b)}
 \Pi_{ab}^\perp J_e(s)J_e(s)^T\Pi_{ab}^\perp.
\]
The bad set \(\mathfrak D_\delta\) consists of histories having a relative
speed below \(\delta\), a grazing cosine below \(\delta\), or two consecutive
events separated by less than \(\delta\).

\begin{lemma}[Quantitative genealogical Gramian]
\label{lem:r5-b2-gramian}
For an \(m\)-edge tree, outside \(\mathfrak D_\delta\),
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\mathcal G_{ab}(s))
 \ge c\,\delta^C(1+\mathbb V)^{-C}(1+m)^{-C},
\]
where \(\mathbb V\) is the energy cutoff.  Moreover the Gaussian-weighted
tree measure of \(\mathfrak D_\delta\) is at most
\[
 C^m(1+\mathbb V)^C\delta^\beta
\]
for one \(\beta>0\).  All constants are independent of the collision word.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work in canonical position--momentum Jacobi coordinates.  Across a regular
binary reflection the tangent map is a symplectic shear followed by an
orthogonal reflection.  Its inverse has norm at most
\(C\delta^{-C}(1+\mathbb V)^C\).  Suppose a unit transverse vector \(u\)
annihilates every term in the Gramian.  Propagate the covector
\((u,0)\) backwards along the unique path \(\mathcal P(a,b)\).  Orthogonality
to the impact variation at an edge says that the two backward position
covectors agree in the tangential contact plane.  Orthogonality to the free
flight time variation says that their normal components agree as well.  Thus
they coincide across that edge.  Induction makes the same covector appear at
both terminal roots.  At the first edge on the path the two root translations
are independent, so the common covector must vanish, contradicting
\(|u|=1\).

For a quantitative bound, let \(A_eu=J_e(s)^T\Pi_{ab}^\perp u\).  The preceding
backward argument is a discrete Poincar\'e estimate on a path:
\[
 |u|^2\le C\delta^{-C}(1+\mathbb V)^C(1+m)^C
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal P(a,b)}|A_eu|^2.
\]
Taking the infimum over unit \(u\) proves the Gramian estimate.  Small relative
speed has Gaussian measure \(O(\delta^3)\); grazing impact has collision-flux
measure \(O(\delta^2)\); and an event-time gap below \(\delta\) has ordered
simplex measure \(O(m^2\delta)\).  Tree summation absorbs the polynomial in
\(m\) into \(C^m\), proving the second assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[First-surplus contact tube]
\label{lem:r5-b2-first-cycle}
Fix all variables preceding the first surplus contact, an energy cutoff and a
creation tree.  The integral of the contact condition for its two endpoints,
relative to the tree measure, is bounded by
\[
 C^m(1+\mathbb V)^C(1+T)^C\varepsilon^\alpha
\]
for one \(\alpha>0\) independent of \(m\).  The estimate remains valid after
insertion of any fixed number of bounded actual-contact marks.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the variables in the Gramian simultaneously rather than selecting one
analytic minor.  At fixed event time, the contact equation
\(|r_{ab}(s)|=\varepsilon\) is a codimension-two equation in the transverse
relative position.  The coarea formula and
Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-b2-gramian} bound the regular part by
\[
 C^m\varepsilon^2\delta^{-C}(1+\mathbb V)^C(1+m)^C.
\]
The bad part is bounded by \(C^m\delta^\beta\).  Choose
\(\delta=\varepsilon^{2/(C+\beta)}\); after absorbing the polynomial in
\(m\), both terms are \(C^m\varepsilon^\alpha\).  A bounded mark changes only
the integrand and not the coarea Jacobian.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A future-preserving contact ledger}

For a regular configuration \(Z\), let \(N_t(Z)\) be the number of actual
contacts of its labels in \([0,t]\).  For a nonnegative Liouville density
\(\rho\) supported in an energy ball, define
\[
 M_z(t,\rho)=\int e^{zN_t(Z)}\rho(Z)\,dZ.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Boundary-flux exponential ledger]
\label{lem:r5-b2-ledger}
For a connected \(k\)-label component and \(z\ge0\),
\[
 M_z(t,\rho)
 \le \|\rho\|_1
 \exp\{Ck(1+\mathbb V)^C t(e^z-1)\}.
\]
The same estimate holds conditionally after any stopping contact, with the
actual post-collisional configuration as the new initial state.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the Liouville divergence theorem to the hard-sphere phase domain.  The
outgoing and incoming boundary measures are exchanged by elastic reflection
and have flux density \(g_+\).  Expanding \(e^{zN_t}\) at its first contact
gives the renewal inequality
\[
 M_z(t,\rho)\le\|\rho\|_1+
 (e^z-1)Ck(1+\mathbb V)^C
 \int_0^tM_z(t-s,\rho_s)\,ds,
\]
where \(\rho_s\) is the transported boundary density and has the same
\(L^1\)-mass by Liouville invariance.  Gronwall's inequality proves the bound.
The proof starts from an arbitrary regular incoming or outgoing boundary
state, so it applies after a stopping contact.  In particular the collision
which triggers the restart is reflected and all later velocities and contacts
are the true ones.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole cyclic sector estimate]
\label{thm:r5-b2-cyclic}
For short \(T\), connected \(k\)-label trajectory clusters containing at
least one surplus contact have total marked activity at most
\[
 \varepsilon^\alpha k!C^k(T+\varepsilon)^{k-1}
 \exp\{CkT(e^{r+u}-1)\}
\]
uniformly for bounded particle source \(r\) and contact-mark generating
parameter \(u\).  Only the first-surplus contact supplies a geometric gain;
all later actual contacts are controlled by the future-preserving ledger.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Order events chronologically and stop at the first surplus contact.  Integrate
its existence by Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-b2-first-cycle}.  At that instant retain the
actual reflected state and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-b2-ledger} to the remaining
time.  Creation edges before the stopping contact are summed by the usual
collision coarea tree estimate, giving
\(k!C^k(T+\varepsilon)^{k-1}\).  Multiplying the three independent bounds and
integrating the stopping time proves the formula.  No pseudo-trajectory is
altered after the first surplus contact.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Grand-canonical marked pressure}

Let \(\mathbb P_\varepsilon^{\rm gc}\) be the hard-core grand-canonical law
with Maxwellian velocity activity.  For particle path source \(h\) and actual
contact source \(\psi\), put
\[
 Q_{\varepsilon,T}^{\rm gc}(h,\psi)=
 \frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\mathbb E_\varepsilon^{\rm gc}
 \exp\{\mu_\varepsilon(
  \langle h,\pi^\varepsilon\rangle+
  \langle\psi,\Gamma^\varepsilon\rangle)\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Local analytic all-contact pressure]
\label{thm:r5-b2-local-pressure}
On a complex neighborhood of every bounded real source with sufficiently
small block length, the connected actual-trajectory expansion is normally
convergent with all source derivatives.  Its Boltzmann--Grad limit is the
unique analytic mild solution of the marked Hamilton--Jacobi equation
\[
 \partial_tQ=
 \langle f,v\cdot\nabla_xp\rangle+
 \int (e^{\Delta p+\psi}-1)\,dA_f,
 \qquad f=\frac{\delta Q}{\delta p},
\]
where
\[
 dA_f=rac12ff_*((v-v_*)\cdot\omega)_+
 \,dt\,dx\,dv\,dv_*\,d\omega.
\]
Every derivative in \(\psi\) is a factorial moment of the actual contact
measure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Creation forests are bounded by collision coarea and the ordered time simplex.
Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-b2-cyclic} removes the complete recollision sector with a
uniform \(\varepsilon^\alpha\) factor.  The remaining tree series is normally
convergent when the block length times the source weight is small.  Cauchy's
formula passes all derivatives to the limit.  Cutting a limiting tree at its
last collision gives two independent rooted subtrees and the displayed
Hamiltonian.  Picard contraction in the Gaussian velocity-weight scale gives
uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fixed-horizon real-source sewing}

Let \(\mathfrak X_{\alpha,\beta}\) be the complete correlation hierarchy norm
with label weight \(e^{\alpha k}\) and Maxwellian weight
\(e^{\beta|V_k|^2}\).  A source of size \(R\) on a block of length \(\Delta\)
induces an exact conditional Feynman--Kac map
\[
 \mathfrak T_{\Delta}^{h,\psi}:
 \mathfrak X_{\alpha,\beta}\longrightarrow
 \mathfrak X_{\alpha-C\Delta e^{CR},\beta'}.
\]
This is a map of complete hierarchies, not a reset to a factorized law.

\begin{theorem}[Source-sewing theorem]
\label{thm:r5-b2-sewing}
For every fixed short horizon \(T\) and every bounded real source
\((h,\psi)\), choose
\[
 \Delta_R\le ce^{-CR},
 \qquad m_R=\lceil T/\Delta_R\rceil,
 \qquad \alpha_R=\alpha_0+CTe^{CR}.
\]
The composition of the \(m_R\) exact hierarchy maps is well defined and gives
a finite pressure on \([0,T]\).  On a neighborhood of the source it is
Fr\'echet differentiable, and the finite-volume pressures converge locally
uniformly to a convex functional \(Q_T^{\rm gc}\).  Thus the fixed physical
horizon does not shrink when the bounded source amplitude grows.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The local cluster estimate gives the declared hierarchy loss on one block.
Starting with \(\alpha_R\), the sum of all losses is at most
\(CTe^{CR}\), so every intermediate hierarchy remains in
\(\mathfrak X_{\alpha_0,\beta'}\).  Conditional Feynman--Kac composition is
exact by the Liouville tower, hence the result is independent of the chosen
partition.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-b2-local-pressure} on each block and
compose its normally convergent maps.  A slightly larger source amplitude
uses the same argument after increasing \(\alpha_R\), giving a neighborhood
for differentiation.  Convexity is inherited from finite-volume logarithmic
moments, and local uniform convergence follows by telescoping the block errors.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exponential compactness}

Total kinetic energy is conserved by every collision.  The initial
Maxwellian law therefore gives, for \(0<\eta<\beta\),
\[
 \sup_\varepsilon
 \frac1{\mu_\varepsilon}
 \log\mathbb E e^{\eta\mu_\varepsilon
  \sup_{t\le T}\langle|v|^2,\pi_t^\varepsilon\rangle}<\infty.
\]
Taking a positive constant contact source in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-b2-sewing} gives the same bound for
\(\Gamma_T^\varepsilon(1)\).  The exact balance then controls time increments
on a countable weighted test family.

\begin{lemma}[Joint exponential compact containment]
\label{lem:r5-b2-tight}
The pair \((\pi^\varepsilon,\Gamma^\varepsilon)\) is exponentially tight in
the weighted weak path--measure space at speed \(\mu_\varepsilon\), uniformly
on compact bounded-source sets.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Energy gives velocity tightness, the constant collision source gives contact
mass tightness, and balance bounds the modulus of every compactly supported
smooth test by free transport plus contact mass.  Weighted Prokhorov and the
Jakubowski--Skorokhod compactness criterion give compact sets with arbitrary
exponential complement cost.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Regular tilts and action-dense balance repair}

A smooth pair \((f,\Gamma)\) is regular when it is strictly positive on a
compact velocity set, satisfies the exact balance, and
\(q=d\Gamma/dA_f\) is smooth with
\(0<q_-\le q\le q_+<\infty\).  The source
\(\psi=\log q\), together with the solution of the backward balance adjoint,
defines a normalized time-sliced trajectory likelihood.  Pressure
differentiability gives concentration at \((f,\Gamma)\), and its relative
entropy is
\[
 \int\ell(q)\,dA_f,
 \qquad \ell(q)=q\log q-q+1.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Finite-cell conservative repair]
\label{lem:r5-b2-repair}
Every balanced finite-action pair \((f,\Gamma)\) has regular approximants
\((f_n,\Gamma_n)\) such that
\[
 (f_n,\Gamma_n)\to(f,\Gamma),
 \qquad
 \int\ell(d\Gamma_n/dA_{f_n})dA_{f_n}
 \to\int\ell(d\Gamma/dA_f)dA_f.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First truncate velocities and the density ratio, then add
\(\delta_nM(v)\) and \(\delta_nA_M\) to obtain a positive background.
Mollification produces a balance defect \(r_n\to0\) in the dual of weighted
\(C^1\) tests.  Partition the compact position--velocity--time set into cells.
On each energy--momentum shell, form the finite collision hypergraph whose
incidence vector is
\(e_{v'}+e_{v_*'}-e_v-e_{v_*}\).  Elementary four-collision parallelograms
show that the span of these incidence vectors is the orthogonal complement of
mass, momentum and energy.  The mollified defect has zero pairing with these
invariants, so the finite incidence matrix has a solution whose norm is
bounded by the inverse spectral gap of the connected finite graph.  Add the
corresponding signed cell current to \(\Gamma\) and a transport correction to
\(f\).  The positive background dominates its negative part once the mesh is
fine.  The correction tends to zero in total variation; uniform convexity on
bounded ratios and then monotone truncation give convergence of the entropy.
A diagonal choice of cutoff, background and mesh proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The grand-canonical and microcanonical LDPs}

\begin{theorem}[Grand-canonical density--actual-contact LDP]
\label{thm:r5-b2-gc-ldp}
On the fixed short horizon, the pair
\((\pi^\varepsilon,\Gamma^\varepsilon)\) satisfies a good LDP with speed
\(\mu_\varepsilon\) and rate
\[
 I_T^{\rm gc}(f,\Gamma)=I_0^{\rm gc}(f_0)+
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\int\ell(d\Gamma/dA_f)dA_f,
 &\Gamma\ll A_f\text{ and }(f,\Gamma)\text{ is balanced},\\[1ex]
 +\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The source-sewing pressure on a countable dense set of bounded tests and
Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-b2-tight} give the projective upper bound.  Fenchel duality
in the contact source gives \(\ell\), while the density cotangent enforces the
closed weak balance.  For the lower bound, use the normalized regular tilt for
a regular pair and identify its entropy by differentiating the sewn pressure.
Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-b2-repair} extends the result to every finite-action pair.
Open-set approximation and exponential tightness finish the proof.
\end{proof}

The finite-volume source-dependent shell coefficient proved in Paper B1 may
now be applied to the grand-canonical theorem, since B1 uses only the
Grand-canonical pressure above.  Conditioning gives the prepared initial rate
and does not alter the dynamic entropy integrand.

\begin{theorem}[Microcanonical joint LDP]
\label{thm:r5-b2-mc-ldp}
For a compatible primitive particle-number, momentum and energy shell, the
conditioned pair satisfies a good LDP with rate
\[
 I_T^{\rm mc}(f,\Gamma)=I_0^{\rm mc}(f_0)+
 \int\ell(d\Gamma/dA_f)dA_f
\]
on balanced pairs and \(+\infty\) otherwise.  The dependency order is
\[
 B2\text{-GC}\longrightarrow B1\longrightarrow B2\text{-MC}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the exact B1 shell coefficient uniformly to every bounded source used in
the sewn pressure.  The coefficient is subexponential at speed
\(\mu_\varepsilon\), so the conditional Laplace principle is the
source-dependent saddle contraction of the grand-canonical one.  Duality gives
the displayed initial rate and leaves the dynamic contact entropy unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-five B2 closure]
\label{thm:r5-b2-main}
The actual hard-sphere contact ledger has a word-uniform first-surplus estimate
based on a genealogical controllability Gramian, retains the true future after
the first surplus contact, has a fixed-horizon pressure for every bounded real
source by exact hierarchy sewing, and satisfies the full grand-canonical and
prepared microcanonical density--actual-contact LDPs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r5-b2-cyclic},
\ref{thm:r5-b2-sewing}, \ref{thm:r5-b2-gc-ldp}, and
\ref{thm:r5-b2-mc-ldp}.
\end{proof}
