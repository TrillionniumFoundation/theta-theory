\section{Round-eleven positive closure: coupled natural extension and exact augmented suspension}
\label{sec:r11-a1}

The physical generalized baker map is kept separate from its symbolic natural
extension.  In particular, no inverse of a one-sided shift is used, and no
spectral gap is assigned to the bilateral Koopman shift.

\subsection{The coupled natural extension}
Let $\mathcal A=\{1,2,3,4\}$, let
$\Sigma^+=\mathcal A^{\mathbb N_0}$, and let
$\Sigma^-=\mathcal A^{\mathbb N}$.  If
$x=(x_0,x_1,\ldots)$ and $y=(y_1,y_2,\ldots)$, set
\[
 \widehat\sigma(x,y)=(\sigma x,x_0y)
 =((x_1,x_2,\ldots),(x_0,y_1,y_2,\ldots)).
\]
This is the invertible natural-extension map; its inverse removes the first
past symbol and prefixes it to the future.

\begin{theorem}[Correct physical--symbolic factor]
\label{thm:r11-a1-factor}
For every regular point of the four-strip section there is a coding
$\mathfrak c_a=(\mathfrak c_a^+,\mathfrak c_a^-)$ such that
\[
 \mathfrak c_a\circ B_a=\widehat\sigma\circ\mathfrak c_a.
\]
The complement of the regular set is the countable union of forward and
backward seam iterates and has Lebesgue measure zero.  The coding pushes
Lebesgue measure to the two-sided Bernoulli law with weights $w_i(a)$.
Every mixed finite cylinder is therefore evaluated on one coupled natural
extension rather than by two independent shifts.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The future itinerary records the successive vertical strips.  The first
symbol used by $B_a$ is $x_0$; after one iteration it is removed from the
future and becomes the newest past symbol.  This is exactly
$(x,y)\mapsto(\sigma x,x_0y)$.  Iterating the inverse branches gives nested
rectangles of area equal to the product of their strip widths, proving the
Bernoulli cylinder formula.  Seam iterates are finite unions of line segments
at each depth, hence null.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A smooth augmented Hamiltonian suspension}
Cut the square along every primary seam and keep the four open strips as
separate smooth sheets.  On sheet $i$, the affine branch map is an exact
symplectomorphism from a vertical rectangle to a horizontal rectangle.  Keep
the target sheet label as part of the section, so different incident germs
never have to be identified.

\begin{lemma}[Exact branch isotopy]
\label{lem:r11-a1-isotopy}
Each affine branch map admits an exact symplectic isotopy, constant in collars
of the rounded sheet boundary.  The four isotopies combine to one smooth
isotopy on the disjoint sheet section.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Translation and the diagonal map
$(q,p)\mapsto(q/w_i,w_i p)$ lie in the affine symplectic group and have
explicit quadratic generating Hamiltonians.  Multiply those Hamiltonians by
a collar cutoff after first rounding the sheet corners; exactness permits the
cutoff error to be removed by a compactly supported Hamiltonian correction.
The sheets are disjoint, so the four constructions do not overlap.
\end{proof}

Adjoin a clock pair $(s,E)$ and a work pair $(W,J)$, with symplectic form
\[
 dq\wedge dp+ds\wedge dE+dW\wedge dJ.
\]
During the routing layer on sheet $i$, translate $W$ by $c_i$ while
keeping $J$ fixed.  This translation is generated by a Hamiltonian linear
in $J$.

\begin{theorem}[Global autonomous augmented suspension]
\label{thm:r11-a1-suspension}
There is a smooth autonomous Hamiltonian on a finite-dimensional manifold
with boundary whose regular Poincare section is the disjoint sheet section
and whose first return is the exact branch symplectomorphism.  Forgetting the
sheet and work coordinates factors this return map onto $B_a$ almost
everywhere.  The work increment on a return through sheet $i$ is exactly
$c_i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:r11-a1-isotopy} as a periodic time-dependent Hamiltonian on
the sheet section and autonomize it with $(s,E)$.  Since the sheet label is
retained, the mapping torus is an ordinary smooth manifold, not a branched
quotient.  Add the Hamiltonian $\dot\chi(s)Jc_i$ in the routing layer; its
flow translates $W$ by $c_i$.  The one-form $dW$ is globally defined
and its endpoint difference gives the work, so no unmatched local channel
forms have to descend across a gluing.  The factor which forgets the sheet
label identifies the regular return with the original baker branch.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Refinement-independent cut currents}
Let $\mathscr P_n$ be the depth-$n$ rectangular partition.  A current
presentation is a compatible family of bulk $C^1$ densities on cells and
oriented trace densities on their faces.  Two presentations are equivalent
when their common refinement differs by cancelling traces on internal faces.
For $0<\varpi<1/4$, define
\[
 \|F\|_{\rm cur}
 =\sup_n\sum_{R\in\mathscr P_n}\varpi^n
 \left(\|F_R\|_{C^1(R)}+
       \sum_{e\subset\partial R}\|F_e\|_{C^0(e)}\right).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Current quotient and Hadamard derivative]
\label{prop:r11-a1-current}
The refinement quotient completed in $\|\cdot\|_{\rm cur}$ is a Banach
space.  Moving a seam defines a bounded Hadamard current map from one extra
bulk derivative into this space.  Iterated derivatives are defined by
successive pullback and trace, never by multiplying distributions on
intersecting cuts.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The factor $4^n\varpi^n$ is summable.  Refinement maps are contractions and
the internal orientation relations form a closed subspace, so the quotient
completion is Banach.  Reynolds' formula on every cell expresses one material
derivative as a bulk derivative plus an oriented boundary trace.  At higher
order, pull back to the newest common refinement before taking the next trace;
this produces a compatible flag current and avoids undefined products of
currents.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Response on the coupled extension}
The future transfer operator $P_a^+$ and the reversed conditional operator
$P_a^-$ act on one-sided Holder spaces and have the ordinary Bernoulli gap.
A mixed cylinder is a finite sum of tensors after conditioning on the present
symbol.  This finite-rank coupling is part of the operator, not an independent
product dynamics.

\begin{theorem}[Typed physical response]
\label{thm:r11-a1-response}
Let $G_a$ be a physical observable transported by a declared material map
and represented in the current quotient.  Then $a\mapsto\int G_a\,dm$ is
as differentiable as the transport.  Its derivative is the sum of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the material bulk derivative;
\item the Hadamard seam-current term of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:r11-a1-current}; and
\item the Bernoulli score response of the coupled finite-cylinder coding,
      computed with the reduced resolvents of $P_a^+$ and $P_a^-$ and
      the present-symbol coupling operator.
\end{enumerate}
If the physical observable itself is fixed, the integral against Lebesgue
measure is constant and the three transported terms cancel.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First prove the formula on a finite common refinement.  The usual
one-sided Poisson equations control future and past tails, while conditioning
on the present symbol accounts for mixed cylinders.  Reynolds' formula gives
the seam term.  Uniform summability in $\varpi$ permits passage to the
current completion.  For a fixed physical observable, the material pullback
identity is merely a change of variables under the same Lebesgue measure, so
its derivative is zero and yields the asserted cancellation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-eleven A1 closure]
\label{thm:r11-a1-main}
The driven benchmark has a correct coupled natural extension, a smooth
finite-dimensional autonomous Hamiltonian realization on an augmented sheet
section, a global work coordinate, and a refinement-independent physical
response calculus.  No inverse one-sided shift, bilateral Koopman gap,
branched symplectic quotient, or untyped product of cut distributions is used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r11-a1-factor},
\ref{thm:r11-a1-suspension}, and \ref{thm:r11-a1-response} with
Proposition~\ref{prop:r11-a1-current}.
\end{proof}
