\section{Round-eleven positive closure: exact soft phase disintegration}
\label{sec:r11-d1}

Phase components are constructed from the original finite-volume law on one
sample space.  They are not arbitrary exponential tilts.  The conditional
pressure is normalized by the phase weight, so phase cost is counted once.

\subsection{A positive exact latent label}
Let $M_n$ be a finite-dimensional order parameter whose limiting rate has
finitely many isolated compact minimizer components $K_1,\ldots,K_J$.
Choose nonnegative continuous functions $\chi_{n,j}$ with
$\sum_j\chi_{n,j}=1$, each equal to one near $K_j$, and with overlap
contained in a shrinking rate-gap region.  Given the microscopic state, draw a
latent label with probabilities $\chi_{n,j}(M_n)$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact microscopic phase disintegration]
\label{thm:r11-d1-disintegration}
Define
\[
 w_{n,j}=\mathbb E_n\chi_{n,j}(M_n),
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_{n,j}(dx)=w_{n,j}^{-1}\chi_{n,j}(M_n(x))\mathbb P_n(dx).
\]
Then
\[
 \mathbb P_n=\sum_jw_{n,j}\mathbb P_{n,j}
\]
exactly.  The labels are defined once from the full order parameter and hence
are compatible under every finite path projection.  Their overlap has the
explicit order-parameter rate-gap cost.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The partition of unity gives the exact identity and positivity.  Since the
same full-space label is subsequently projected, projective consistency is
automatic.  The LDP upper bound for $M_n$ controls the overlap region; no
sharp basin boundary or superexponential uncovered set is assumed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Component laws and phase pressure}
The normalized conditional pressure is
\[
 \widetilde Q_{n,j}(\theta)=
 \frac1n\log\mathbb E_{n,j}e^{n\langle\theta,X_n\rangle},
\]
while $-n^{-1}\log w_{n,j}\to\alpha_j$.

\begin{theorem}[Phase-resolved LDP]
\label{thm:r11-d1-components}
On every regular component, the platform source theorem and the soft
localization give a phase-local zero-free chart, a good conditional LDP with
rate $I_j$, and boundary-face recovery by a source/soft-window diagonal
limit.  The joint labelled pair satisfies
\[
 \mathcal J(j,x)=\alpha_j+I_j(x),
\]
and the physical law satisfies the generally nonconvex rate
\[
 I(x)=\min_j\{\alpha_j+I_j(x)\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The soft weight is positive and bounded, so on a phase neighborhood it changes
real and complex partition functions only by a subexponential factor; the
platform's local operator chart therefore persists.  Component exponential
tightness and exposed recovery give the conditional LDP.  At a face, send the
normal source and the soft-window radius on a diagonal for which the overlap
cost remains negligible.  Apply the ordinary joint LDP to the exact latent
label, then contract it to obtain the minimum rate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Correct pressure formula]
\label{thm:r11-d1-pressure}
The physical pressure is
\[
 Q(\theta)=\max_j\{-\alpha_j+\widetilde Q_j(\theta)\}.
\]
Equivalently, if one uses the unnormalized restricted pressure
\[
 Q_j^{\rm un}(\theta)=\lim_n n^{-1}\log
 \mathbb E[e^{n\theta\cdot X_n}\chi_{n,j}],
\]
then $Q=\max_jQ_j^{\rm un}$.  The phase cost is never subtracted twice.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert the exact decomposition of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r11-d1-disintegration} into the moment generating function.
A finite log-sum-exp has the maximum exponential rate.  The two conventions
are related by $Q_j^{\rm un}=-\alpha_j+\widetilde Q_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Phase-compatible shell and likelihood theorem]
\label{thm:r11-d1-shell}
Suppose the A2 or B1 coefficient is uniform on the regular component charts.
Then thin conditioning of the original physical law decomposes into the exact
phase coefficients, including overlap terms.  Away from a phase boundary the
conditional law and Gaussian likelihood are those of the unique minimizing
component.  At coexistence, every subsequential tangent is the mixture selected
by the actual finite-volume weights
\[
 w_{n,j}e^{-nI_j(\text{shell})}
\]
and the corresponding component coefficients.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the platform coefficient under each positive component measure and sum
the exact identities.  The overlap is another positive labelled component
with its own explicit order-parameter cost, so it cannot silently dominate a
shell.  Laplace comparison selects the minimum exponential cost.  If costs tie,
retain the finite-volume subexponential weights and local coefficients; their
subsequential limits give precisely the stated mixture.  Component LAN then
gives the Gaussian likelihood within each selected phase.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nontrivial commutation theorem]
\label{thm:r11-d1-commute}
For the exact soft disintegration, the following operations commute as maps of
the joint labelled law: source-dependent shell conditioning, finite path
projection, phase contraction, and local Gaussian likelihood formation.
Contracting the phase label before the LDP generally convexifies or hides
coexistence, whereas carrying the label gives the nonconvex minimum rate and
the correct tangent mixture.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every operation is first performed on the same positive joint law
$(J_n,X_n)$.  Projection and conditioning are ordinary measurable kernels,
so their finite-volume diagrams commute exactly.  Apply the joint LDP and the
uniform component coefficients before contracting the label.  The resulting
rate and likelihood formulas are those of
Theorems~\ref{thm:r11-d1-components} and \ref{thm:r11-d1-shell}.  Early label
contraction loses this information, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-eleven D1 closure]
\label{thm:r11-d1-main}
The paper now constructs a genuine positive finite-volume phase label on the
original microscopic sample space, proves its projective compatibility and
component LDP, counts phase weights exactly once, and establishes a
phase-resolved conditioning and likelihood commutation theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r11-d1-disintegration},
\ref{thm:r11-d1-components}, \ref{thm:r11-d1-pressure},
\ref{thm:r11-d1-shell}, and \ref{thm:r11-d1-commute}.
\end{proof}
