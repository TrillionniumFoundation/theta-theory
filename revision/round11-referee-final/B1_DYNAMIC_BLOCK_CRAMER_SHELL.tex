\section{Round-eleven positive closure: dynamic block Cramer theorem and exact shell}
\label{sec:r11-b1}

B1 uses only the grand-canonical theorem of B2.  The multiplier is selected at
the complete particle/contact source and at finite volume.

\subsection{Mean image and typical covariance}
Let $C$ be the lattice particle-number coordinate together with continuous
momentum, energy, and declared macro constraints.  Assume their one-particle
marks have the stated affine-span and transverse-submersion property on a
finite collection of positive activity patches.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform finite-volume mean image]
\label{thm:r11-b1-saddle}
For targets in a compact relative-interior set and sources in a fixed bounded
B2 ball, the finite-volume map
$\lambda\mapsto D_\lambda Q_\varepsilon(H,\lambda)$ is proper on a common
multiplier domain, has degree one, and has Hessian between two positive
constants.  It therefore has a unique exact saddle
$\lambda_{\varepsilon,H,a}$, converging with all local source derivatives.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
B2 normal convergence controls the Hessian from above.  For the lower bound,
use linearly many typical insertion patches in the full compound sector; the
affine-span hypothesis gives a positive single-block covariance and the B2
connected correction is bounded by the short-time cluster norm.  Boundary
values of the gradient point outward on the common multiplier domain, so
Brouwer degree gives existence for every target; strict convexity gives
uniqueness.  Normal convergence and the implicit-function theorem give the
limit.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dynamical regenerative blocks}
Truncate velocities at
$V_\varepsilon=K\sqrt{\log\mu_\varepsilon}$.  On the truncated event,
collision influence has finite range $TV_\varepsilon$; its complement is
exponentially negligible after $K$ is chosen on the source chart.

\begin{lemma}[Source-uniform block decoupling]
\label{lem:r11-b1-blocks}
There are $c\mu_\varepsilon$ colored space--velocity blocks and a full-rank
block mark such that, under the complete dynamically tilted law and after
conditioning on previously exposed colors, each selected block has a uniform
minorization by a smooth compactly supported mark density.  The conditional
characteristic contraction is uniform on every compact nonzero frequency
annulus.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Color boxes separated by more than twice the truncated propagation radius.
B2's connected trajectory expansion expresses the conditional density as a
product block weight times a polymer correction.  Polymers joining two boxes
of the same color contain a long connection and their total norm is made less
than one half by the color separation and short-time source chart.  The local
affine-span patches then survive with a uniform Radon--Nikodym lower bound.
Unbounded-velocity configurations are restored by their exponential tail.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full mixed characteristic estimate]
\label{thm:r11-b1-fourier}
At the exact saddle, the centered joint characteristic function satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a nondegenerate Gaussian estimate near zero;
\item an $e^{-c\mu_\varepsilon}$ bound on every compact annulus in the full
      lattice--continuous frequency fundamental domain; and
\item for large continuous frequency,
\[
 |\varphi_\varepsilon(t,u)|
 \le e^{-c\mu_\varepsilon}+(1+|u|)^{-s c\mu_\varepsilon}
\]
for one $s>0$.
\end{enumerate}
These regions cover the entire Fourier domain, including
$t=0$ and $\delta<|u|\le R$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Taylor expansion and Theorem~\ref{thm:r11-b1-saddle} give (i).  On a
compact nonzero annulus, Lemma~\ref{lem:r11-b1-blocks} contracts each of
$c\mu_\varepsilon$ conditional block factors by a fixed number, proving (ii).
Repeated integration by parts in the smooth block mark and multiplication over
the same linear number of blocks gives (iii).  Polymer and velocity-tail
errors are absorbed into the exponential term.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Regular shell coefficients}
Let the exact number be prescribed and let the continuous shell be a family of
bounded Lipschitz domains $W_\varepsilon$ with uniformly controlled boundary,
\[
 \delta_\varepsilon\downarrow0,
 \qquad \sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}\delta_\varepsilon\to\infty,
 \qquad \log\operatorname{vol}(W_\varepsilon)=o(\mu_\varepsilon).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Uniform exact-number shell coefficient]
\label{thm:r11-b1-coefficient}
Under the source-dependent saddle law,
\[
 \mathbb P\{N=N_\varepsilon,
 \sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}(Y-a)\in W_\varepsilon\}
 =\mu_\varepsilon^{-1/2}
 \left(\mathfrak g_{H,a}(W_\varepsilon)+o(
       \mathfrak g_{H,a}(W_\varepsilon))\right),
\]
where $\mathfrak g_{H,a}$ is the conditional Gaussian measure after the
lattice coordinate is fixed.  The relative error is uniform whenever the
Gaussian shell mass is at least $e^{-o(\mu_\varepsilon)}$; in the displayed
wide intensive regime it tends to one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fourier invert the exact lattice coordinate and smooth upper/lower
approximations of the Lipschitz domain.  Theorem~\ref{thm:r11-b1-fourier}
makes every noncentral region
$o(\mu_\varepsilon^{-1/2}\mathfrak g(W_\varepsilon))$, including growing
frequency cutoffs.  The central expansion gives the conditional Gaussian
integral.  Boundary regularity controls smoothing error, and the
subexponential mass hypothesis prevents the main term from falling below the
Fourier error.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Source-dependent microcanonical transfer]
\label{thm:r11-b1-transfer}
For the full particle-path and actual-contact source,
\[
 Q^{\rm mc,a}(H)=
 Q(H,\lambda_{H,a})-\lambda_{H,a}\cdot a
 -Q(0,\lambda_{0,a})+\lambda_{0,a}\cdot a.
\]
The conditioned initial LDP and covariance are the affine constraint of the
grand-canonical rate and its exact Schur complement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Change measure in numerator and denominator at their separate exact finite
saddles.  Theorem~\ref{thm:r11-b1-coefficient} shows that both shell
coefficients have subexponential logarithm uniformly on every bounded source
ball.  Pass to the limit and differentiate the saddle equation; first-order
multiplier terms cancel and the second derivative is the Schur complement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-eleven B1 closure]
\label{thm:r11-b1-main}
The dynamically tilted hard-sphere law has a quantified source-uniform block
Cramer theorem covering every Fourier frequency, a global exact saddle on the
relative-interior target chart, a relative shell coefficient for a declared
regular shell class, and the full source-dependent microcanonical transfer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r11-b1-saddle},
\ref{thm:r11-b1-fourier}, \ref{thm:r11-b1-coefficient}, and
\ref{thm:r11-b1-transfer}.
\end{proof}
