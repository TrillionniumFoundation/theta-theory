\section{Round-eleven positive closure: deterministic-clock renewal LDP}
\label{sec:r11-a3}

The empirical law is indexed from the start by a deterministic collision or
physical horizon.  No random quantity is called an LDP speed, and no
countable process is replaced by a non-lumpable cemetery Markov chain.

\subsection{The renewal coefficient state}
For a first-return edge $a$, retain its initial and terminal vertices,
collision length $r(a)$, roof path $\tau_a(\cdot)$, Gibbs weight, and the
complete graph-completed excursion profile $K_a$.  For deterministic collision
horizon $n$, let $N(n)$ be the number of completed excursions, let $A_n$ be
the age in the terminal excursion, and retain the pointed prefix
$K^{\rm pt}_{a,A_n}$.

\begin{definition}[Deterministic-horizon state]
\label{def:r11-a3-state}
The state consists of the recurrent edge-flow measure, the length-weighted
excursion-profile measure, and the pointed terminal prefix.  Their total
collision mass is exactly one.  The physical-time state additionally retains
the continuous roof age inside the final free flight.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-cylinder renewal coefficient theorem]
\label{thm:r11-a3-finite}
For every finite family of bounded edge/profile cylinders and every bounded
clock source, the coefficient of collision total $n$ in the original
countable Gibbs renewal operator has a locally uniform logarithmic limit and a
matching exposed-point lower bound.  The finite-dimensional empirical state
therefore satisfies an LDP with deterministic speed $n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert the sources into the original countable return operator and introduce a
complex renewal variable $z$ conjugate to $r(a)$.  Exponential return tails
make the operator analytic for $|z|<e^\eta$, while A2 controls every bounded
cylinder insertion.  Cauchy's coefficient formula and the simple real saddle
give the upper bound and local coefficient.  Exponential tilting at the same
saddle yields concentration and the lower bound.  At no stage are omitted
edges identified, so hidden-state lumpability is irrelevant.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One-big excursions at the same speed}
For $L$, split the length-weighted profile into $r\le L$ and $r>L$ parts, but
retain the latter as a measure rather than deleting it.

\begin{theorem}[Projective recurrent--recession LDP]
\label{thm:r11-a3-recession}
The finite-cylinder LDPs, augmented by all tail-profile coordinates
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{j<N(n)}r(a_j)1_{\{r(a_j)>L\}}\delta_{K_{a_j}},
\]
are projectively consistent.  Their Dawson--Gartner limit is a good LDP at
speed $n$.  The recession cost is the lower-semicontinuous epigraph limit of
actual long-excursion coefficients divided by the same deterministic
collision horizon $n$.  Thus recurrent and one-big-excursion costs are
components of one joint rate, not rates at incompatible speeds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every finite projection is obtained by an exact measurable map from the same
renewal path law and hence has the contraction rate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r11-a3-finite}; consistency is automatic.  Exponential
moments give projective exponential tightness after all tail coordinates are
retained.  The lower bound uses diagonal source tilts on finitely many tail
profile cells.  Taking the epigraph closure supplies both liminf and recovery
for actual excursions, including convex mixtures through concatenation with
quantified Gibbs connector cost.  All terms are normalized by $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Full recurrence classification]
\label{prop:r11-a3-recurrence}
Each irreducible component is classified as strongly positive recurrent,
positive recurrent without a pressure gap, null recurrent, or transient.  A
tight equilibrium law is retained in either positive-recurrent case; loss of
clock mass is asserted only for sequences whose renewal mass actually escapes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the Vere--Jones classification to the component renewal series.  Tight
normalized eigenvectors characterize positive recurrence, while a strict tail
pressure gap is the additional strongly-positive-recurrent property.  The two
notions are not collapsed into a false dichotomy.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Recurrent and recession singularity shield]
\label{thm:r11-a3-shield}
For every finite-rate recurrent--recession state, singularity neighborhoods
can be chosen so that their profile mass and logarithmic approximation cost
vanish.  The estimate is uniform separately on bounded excursions and on the
normalized long-excursion epigraph recovery family.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On bounded excursions use the standard complexity--neighborhood ledger.  On a
long excursion, divide by its own collision length and include the resulting
singularity-exposure coordinate in the tail profile.  Absolute continuity of
homogeneous branch measures and the epigraph definition show that a positive
limiting exposure has its explicit finite cost rather than being declared
superexponentially impossible.  Truncating only the residual exposure gives an
exponentially good approximation on every rate sublevel.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Collision and physical deterministic-time contractions]
\label{thm:r11-a3-clocks}
The collision empirical path at time $n$ and the physical empirical path at
time $T$ are continuous contractions of the recurrent--recession state once
the pointed terminal collision prefix, roof prefix, and age inside the final
flight are retained.  Both satisfy full good LDPs with the corresponding
contraction of the single joint rate from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r11-a3-recession}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The completed excursions contribute their full profiles.  The pointed state
determines exactly the prefix of the last excursion at collision time, and the
roof path plus flight age determines it at physical time.  These maps are
continuous off shielded singular sets; Theorem~\ref{thm:r11-a3-shield} gives
exponentially good continuous approximations.  Extended contraction proves
both LDPs, including terminal one-big-excursion events with order-one mass.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-eleven A3 closure]
\label{thm:r11-a3-main}
The Sinai inducing process has a deterministic-clock, recession-inclusive
empirical-path LDP constructed directly from the original countable Gibbs
renewal law.  No random speed, cemetery Markovization, unnormalized recession
cost, incompatible rate addition, or negligible macroscopic terminal cut is
used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r11-a3-finite},
\ref{thm:r11-a3-recession}, \ref{thm:r11-a3-shield}, and
\ref{thm:r11-a3-clocks}, together with
Proposition~\ref{prop:r11-a3-recurrence}.
\end{proof}
