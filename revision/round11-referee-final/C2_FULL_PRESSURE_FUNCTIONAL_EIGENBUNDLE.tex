\section{Round-eleven positive closure: full pressure functionals and finite eigenbundles}
\label{sec:r11-c2}

Three notions are separated: equality of invariant integrals, equality of one
pressure number, and equality of the entire perturbed pressure functional.
Only the third can imply thermodynamic cohomology.

\subsection{The two closed quotients}
Let
\[
 \mathscr N_{\rm inv}=
 \overline{\operatorname{span}}
 \{U-U\circ\Theta_t:t\in\mathbb Q\}
\]
and let $\mathscr N_{\rm pr}=\mathbb R\mathbf1+\mathscr N_{\rm inv}$.

\begin{theorem}[Invariant-integral quotient]
\label{thm:r11-c2-invariant}
On each platform, two weighted potentials have equal integrals under every
invariant finite-weight probability exactly when their difference belongs to
$\mathscr N_{\rm inv}$.  The quotient is Hausdorff and its continuous dual is
the compactly generated set of invariant Radon probabilities.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every coboundary integrates to zero.  Conversely, separate a class outside the
closed span by a weighted Radon functional.  The platform-specific Lyapunov
estimate holds for every Dirac initial state and hence for the positive and
negative parts of the separator.  Cesaro averaging is weighted tight; every
limit is invariant.  Its Jordan parts remain invariant and one normalized part
separates the class, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full perturbation pressure rigidity]
\label{thm:r11-c2-pressure}
Let $F,G$ lie in one mixing regular phase.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for every sufficiently small potential $H$,
\[
 P(F+H)-P(F)=P(G+H)-P(G);
\]
\item the equilibrium path laws of $F$ and $G$ coincide;
\item $F-G\in\mathscr N_{\rm pr}$.
\end{enumerate}
Equality of the single numbers $P(F)=P(G)$ alone has no such consequence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the full perturbation identity in every cylinder direction to
obtain equality of equilibrium measures.  Equality of all periodic orbit
integrals follows from the Gibbs specification, and the platform Livsic
theorem gives a constant plus a coboundary.  Conversely such a difference
changes every pressure by the same constant.  The two-symbol counterexample
with equal scalar pressures is excluded because it fails the perturbation
identity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-dimensional spectral transport without scale loss}
Choose fixed strong vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_d$ and weak dual vectors
$\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_d$ whose projected Gram matrix is invertible at the base
parameter.

\begin{theorem}[Finite eigenbundle trivialization]
\label{thm:r11-c2-eigenbundle}
On a parameter chart, the vectors $e_i(\eta)=\Pi_\eta v_i$ and their dual
Gram inverse give an analytic frame of the Riesz range in the common weak
space.  Material derivatives may lose one strong level, but no Picard
iteration of a regularity-losing Kato commutator is used.  The resulting
finite-dimensional connection transports spectral data and has bounded
covariant derivatives at every prescribed order with the corresponding
strong-level budget.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Riesz projector is bounded on the weak space and maps the chosen strong
vectors into the finite-dimensional range.  Its matrix coefficients against
the fixed weak duals are differentiable with the declared one-level loss.
Invertibility of the finite Gram matrix persists on a smaller chart.  All
transport is then finite-dimensional matrix algebra; higher derivatives use
fresh strong regularity but never iterate a loss on one Banach space.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The physical resolved Hilbert bundle}
For a fixed finite family of physical observables, define their phase
correlation Gram matrix using the Doob equilibrium law.  Positivity and A4
correlation differentiability give a separate finite-dimensional Hilbert
bundle and orthogonal projection $R_\eta$.

\begin{theorem}[Covariant resolved memory]
\label{thm:r11-c2-memory}
The resolved Gram bundle is differentiable and nondegenerate on a regular
chart.  Away from the transmission-zero divisor, the compressed resolvent
\[
 C_\eta(z)=R_\eta(z-L_\eta^D)^{-1}R_\eta
\]
and
\[
 \widehat K_\eta(z)=zR_\eta-R_\eta L_\eta^DR_\eta-C_\eta(z)^{-1}
\]
have covariant derivatives including the derivatives of the Doob data,
correlation pairing, generator domain, and projection.  At a zero, the A4
descriptor mode is included and the formula is applied to the minimum-phase
residual block.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A4's renewal-resolvent theorem gives differentiable correlation entries and a
common graph domain after conjugation by the finite spectral frame of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r11-c2-eigenbundle}.  Differentiate the finite Gram matrix to
obtain $R_\eta$.  The resolvent identity gives the generator derivative, and
$D(C^{-1})=-C^{-1}(DC)C^{-1}$ gives the memory derivative off the zero
divisor.  Smith--McMillan descriptor modes remove the finite zeros before the
same calculation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Source and state derivatives under contraction}

\begin{theorem}[Typed contraction chain rules]
\label{thm:r11-c2-chain}
For a measurable contraction $\mathcal C:X\to Y$,
\[
 Q_{\mathcal C}(\varphi)=Q(\varphi\circ\mathcal C).
\]
A source variation $h:Y\to\mathbb R$ satisfies
\[
 DQ_{\mathcal C}(\varphi)[h]
 =DQ(\varphi\circ\mathcal C)[h\circ\mathcal C].
\]
Separately, if $\varphi$ and $\mathcal C$ are state differentiable, the state
derivative of the composite observable is
$D\varphi(\mathcal Cx)D\mathcal C(x)\xi$.  These two derivatives are never
identified.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The pressure identity is exact at finite volume.  Differentiate it in the
linear source direction to obtain the first formula.  The second is the
ordinary chain rule in the state variable.  Since their domains are different,
keeping the parameters explicit prevents the previous ill-typed expression.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Optional-projection stability]
\label{thm:r11-c2-optional}
Under the A4 quenched kernel convergence and uniform exponential moments,
finite-history likelihood martingales and their optional projections converge
stably in the coarse history filtration.  Their diffusion limit is the
Girsanov exponential and the associated quadratic BSDE on the declared
resolved bundle.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A4 supplies convergence of conditional kernels on compact history sets and the
correct centered rough martingale.  Uniform exponential moments give uniform
integrability.  Approximate the optional projection by a finite cylinder,
pass to the limit there, and remove the approximation uniformly.  Ito's
formula identifies the Girsanov and BSDE representations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-eleven C2 closure]
\label{thm:r11-c2-main}
The synthesis now has the correct invariant quotient, a rigidity theorem for
the full perturbed pressure functional rather than one scalar pressure, a
finite-dimensional spectral transport avoiding regularity-loss Picard
iteration, a separately constructed resolved Hilbert bundle, and typed source
and state contraction derivatives.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r11-c2-invariant},
\ref{thm:r11-c2-pressure}, \ref{thm:r11-c2-eigenbundle},
\ref{thm:r11-c2-memory}, \ref{thm:r11-c2-chain}, and
\ref{thm:r11-c2-optional}.
\end{proof}
