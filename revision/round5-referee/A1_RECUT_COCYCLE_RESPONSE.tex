\section{Round-five referee closure: recut suspensions, descended work, and a common response fibre}
\label{sec:r5-a1}

The construction below replaces the port-identification paragraph reviewed at
commit \texttt{cbee394}.  Incoming and outgoing faces are never identified
without first being recut to a common section, and the mechanical work is
defined by an exact-symplectic cocycle rather than by the non-descending
primitive \(p\,dq\).

\subsection{The recut port category}

Let \((M,\omega=d\vartheta)\) be an exact symplectic surface with boundary.
For each directed branch \(e\) let \(U_e^-\) and \(U_e^+\) be compact
rectangles with piecewise smooth boundary and let
\[
 S_e:U_e^-\longrightarrow U_e^+
\]
be an exact symplectic diffeomorphism.  The horizontal sides are impact walls
and the vertical sides are ports.  We assume the port graph is balanced: for
every outgoing port there is a unique incoming port with the same transverse
coordinate interval.  This is a combinatorial equality of labelled ports,
not an identification inside \(M\).

\begin{lemma}[Common recut section]
\label{lem:r5-a1-recut}
There are compact rectangles \(\widehat U_e\), exact symplectic
diffeomorphisms
\[
 i_e^-:\widehat U_e\to U_e^-,
 \qquad
 i_e^+:\widehat U_e\to U_e^+,
\]
and a permutation \(\sigma\) of the labelled rectangles such that
\[
 R_e=(i_{\sigma(e)}^-)^{-1}\circ S_e\circ i_e^+
\]
is defined on all of \(\widehat U_e\).  The boundaries can be chosen so that
\(R_e\) maps impact sides to impact sides and port sides to port sides.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take the finite common refinement of the incoming and outgoing port
partitions, including every preimage under \(S_e\) of an endpoint of an
incoming partition.  Exact symplecticity preserves area and orientation, so
each refined atom has a canonical area coordinate
\[
 q=\frac{\operatorname{area}(\text{left part})}
          {\operatorname{area}(\text{atom})},
 \qquad
 p=\frac{\text{signed transverse flux}}{\operatorname{area}(\text{atom})}.
\]
Cut each atom along the finitely many refined vertical curves and use
\((q,p)\) to identify it with a standard rectangle.  At a corner two adjacent
charts have the same oriented flux coordinate, hence the identifications
agree on the common side.  The balanced port graph pairs the refined outgoing
atoms bijectively with refined incoming atoms; this gives \(\sigma\).
Because every cut includes the complete preimage of the target boundary,
\(R_e\) has no partially defined branch.  Impact sides are carried to impact
sides by the specular branch map and port sides are carried to port sides by
construction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Autonomous suspension with an exact first return}

For every \(e\) form the cylinder
\(\widehat U_e\times[0,1]\) with coordinate \(s\).  Near \(s=0,1\) use the
Hamiltonian
\[
 H_e(z,s,\eta)=\eta+\chi(s)K_e(z),
\]
on the extended exact symplectic manifold with primitive
\(\vartheta+\eta\,ds\), where \(\chi\) is supported away from the endpoints
and \(K_e\) generates an exact symplectic isotopy from the identity to
\(R_e\).  Such an isotopy exists after the recutting because a rectangle has
trivial first cohomology.  Glue \((z,1)\) to \((R_ez,0)\) in the cylinder
labelled \(\sigma(e)\).  The horizontal boundary is completed by the standard
specular double; the vertical port boundary is already paired by the gluing.

\begin{theorem}[Single-valued Hamiltonian first return]
\label{thm:r5-a1-return}
The quotient is a Hausdorff exact symplectic manifold with a piecewise smooth
autonomous Hamiltonian flow.  The section
\[
 \Sigma=\bigsqcup_e\widehat U_e\times\{0\}
\]
is transverse outside the usual grazing set, every regular orbit starting in
\(\Sigma\) returns to \(\Sigma\) before meeting any other boundary component,
and its first-return map is exactly
\[
 R|_{\widehat U_e}=R_e.
\]
In particular, the return relation is neither branched nor multivalued.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the endpoint collars, Hamilton's equation gives
\(\dot s=\partial_\eta H_e=1\).  Thus an orbit crosses each cylinder once and
cannot turn around or leave through an \(s\)-side.  The specular double makes
the horizontal impact boundary an interior mirror; regular trajectories
cross its doubled collar uniquely, while grazing trajectories form a
Liouville-null hypersurface.  The refined vertical sides are identified in
pairs and therefore are not boundary in the quotient.  Consequently the
only next intersection with the union \(s=0\) occurs after \(s\) has advanced
from \(0\) to \(1\).  The time-one map in the \(z\)-coordinate is \(R_e\) by
the defining isotopy, and the endpoint gluing places the point on the unique
section component \(\sigma(e)\).  Local gluing charts are products of a
boundary chart with an interval, which proves Hausdorffness and uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The descended mechanical cocycle}

Exactness gives unique functions \(F_e\), normalized by
\(\int_{\widehat U_e}F_e=0\), such that
\[
 R_e^*\vartheta-\vartheta=dF_e.
\]
For a suspended orbit segment \(\gamma_e(z)\) from \((z,0)\) to
\((R_ez,1)\), define
\[
 {\mathcal W}_e(z)
 =\int_{\gamma_e(z)}(\vartheta+\eta\,ds-H_e\,dt)-F_e(z).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Descent and concatenation]
\label{prop:r5-a1-work}
The value \({\mathcal W}_e\) is independent of the choice of exact primitive
and of the representative of the quotient orbit.  For an admissible word
\(e_0\ldots e_{n-1}\),
\[
 {\mathcal W}^{(n)}(z)
 =\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}{\mathcal W}_{e_j}(R^jz)
\]
is the action of the corresponding quotient trajectory, with no seam term.
Changing \(\vartheta\) to \(\vartheta+dG\) changes each one-step expression
by \(G(R_ez)-G(z)\), which telescopes on every finite path and vanishes on
periodic paths.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On an overlap, the pullback of the primitive differs by
\(dF_e\).  Stokes' theorem shows that the raw action on the two
representatives differs by exactly \(F_e(z)\); the correction in the
definition cancels it.  Under a change of primitive the action integral
changes by the endpoint difference of \(G\), while the normalized transfer
function changes by the same coboundary.  Concatenation then follows by
telescoping all internal endpoints.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One Banach fibre for all response channels}

Let \(\Omega\) be the common labelled path space of the recut return map and
let \(\mathscr B_\alpha\) be the Banach space of dynamically
\(\alpha\)-H\"older functions with norm
\[
 \|f\|_\alpha=\|f\|_\infty+
 \sup_{n\ge0}\alpha^{-n}\operatorname{osc}_n(f).
\]
For every parameter \(a\), recutting supplies a path-space conjugacy
\(J_a:\Omega\to\Omega_a\).  Transfer, score, pathwise, and finite-difference
observables are pulled back by \(J_a\) before differentiation.  At a moving
impact seam the derivative is the saltation operator
\[
 {\mathsf S}_a
 =D\Phi_a^+
 +\frac{(X_a^+-D\Phi_a^+X_a^-)\,n_a^T}
        {n_a^TX_a^-+\partial_a h_a},
\]
written in the same reference coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Common-fibre response identity]
\label{thm:r5-a1-response}
On every compact parameter interval avoiding grazing bifurcations, the four
operators
\[
 D_a{\mathcal L}_a,\qquad
 \text{score derivative},\qquad
 \text{pathwise derivative with }{\mathsf S}_a,\qquad
 \text{centred finite difference}
\]
are bounded maps from \(\mathscr B_\alpha\) to
\(\mathscr B_{\alpha'}\), \(0<\alpha'<\alpha\), after conjugation by \(J_a\).
For every \(f\in\mathscr B_\alpha\) their expectations coincide.  The common
value is the derivative of the recut path pressure and the work direction is
the descended cocycle of Proposition~\ref{prop:r5-a1-work}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite recutting gives uniformly bounded distortion and a common
separation metric.  Differentiating each inverse branch in that metric gives
a bounded operator with the usual one-unit loss from \(\alpha\) to
\(\alpha'\).  Leibniz differentiation of the branch Jacobian is the score
formula.  Differentiating the actual trajectory gives the variational
equation on smooth pieces and the displayed saltation operator at each seam.
A dominated second-order Taylor estimate, uniform on the compact parameter
interval, passes the centred finite difference to the same derivative.
All four calculations are therefore different representations of
\(D_a(J_a^{-1}{\mathcal L}_aJ_a)\) on one Banach fibre.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-five A1 closure]
\label{thm:r5-a1-main}
The driven branch family admits an autonomous, single-valued Hamiltonian
impact suspension; its physical work is a well-defined exact-symplectic
cocycle; and every response channel is typed on one common Banach fibre.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-a1-recut},
Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-a1-return},
Proposition~\ref{prop:r5-a1-work}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-a1-response}.
\end{proof}
