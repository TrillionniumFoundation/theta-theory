\section{Round-five referee closure: a uniform moving-cut packet and the corrected three-regime local limit theorem}
\label{sec:r5-a2}

Let \(T_R\) be the collision map of the periodic triangular Sinai billiard
with disk radius
\[
 {\mathcal I}=[9/20,47/100].
\]
Write \(\kappa_R\in\mathbb Z^2\) for the homological displacement and
\(\tau_R>0\) for the free-flight roof.  The purpose of this section is twofold:
to give one parameter-uniform operator construction and to replace the false
wide-window formula by a disjoint three-regime theorem.

\subsection{A finite moving-cut Banach bundle}

For \(m\ge1\), let \({\mathcal S}_{R,m}\) be the singularity set of
\(T_R^m\), enlarged by the standard homogeneity boundaries.  A cut atom of
depth \(m\) is a connected component of the complement of
\(\bigcup_{j\le m}{\mathcal S}_{R,j}\).  Stable test curves are cut at every
endpoint of every depth-\(m_*\) atom, where \(m_*\) is chosen below.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform geometry and a common finite depth]
\label{lem:r5-a2-geometry}
There are constants
\[
 0<\tau_-<\tau_+,\qquad
 \Lambda>1,\qquad C_{\rm dist}<\infty,\qquad
 m_*\in\mathbb N,\qquad \vartheta<1
\]
valid for all \(R\in{\mathcal I}\), such that the billiards have finite
horizon, common stable and unstable cones, homogeneous distortion at most
\(C_{\rm dist}\), and \(m_*\)-step expansion sum at most \(\vartheta\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The nearest-neighbour gap is at least \(3/50\).  For a primitive lattice
direction \(v\), adjacent parallel lattice rows are separated by
\((\sqrt3/2)/|v|<2R\); hence every rational strip is blocked.  An irrational
strip has dense transverse lattice translates and is also blocked.  Compactness
of position, direction, and \(R\) gives a common upper free-flight bound.
The mirror equation then gives common invariant cones and homogeneous
distortion bounds.  For each \(R\), the growth lemma gives an iterate with
inverse-expansion sum below one.  The finite-depth singularity curves vary
continuously in moving tangency coordinates; the inequality persists on a
neighbourhood of \(R\).  A finite cover of \({\mathcal I}\) and a common
multiple of the corresponding iterates give \(m_*\) and \(\vartheta\).
\end{proof}

Choose finitely many radius intervals \(I_j\) on which every curve in
\(\bigcup_{m\le m_*}{\mathcal S}_{R,m}\) is represented by a \(C^2\) graph
in the tangency coordinate
\[
 u=\operatorname{sgn}(\varphi)
   \bigl(\pi/2-|\varphi|\bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
Pulling stable test curves back to a reference radius \(R_j\) defines Banach
spaces \({\mathcal B}_j\hookrightarrow{\mathcal B}_{j,w}\).  On overlaps the
pullbacks satisfy the cocycle identity because both are defined by the same
physical stable-curve integrals.

\begin{proposition}[Moving-cut Lasota--Yorke bundle]
\label{prop:r5-a2-bundle}
The spaces \((\mathcal B_j,\mathcal B_{j,w})\) and the overlap maps form a
finite Banach bundle over \({\mathcal I}\).  For
\[
 {\mathcal L}_{R,\xi,s}h
 ={\mathcal L}_R\!\left(e^{\xi\cdot\kappa_R-s\tau_R}h\right)
\]
there are \(C<\infty\) and \(\rho<1\), uniform for \(R\in{\mathcal I}\) and
\((\xi,s)\) in a fixed complex neighbourhood of a real compact set, such that
\[
 \|{\mathcal L}_{R,\xi,s}^nh\|_{{\mathcal B}_R}
 \le C\rho^n\|h\|_{{\mathcal B}_R}
      +C\|h\|_{{\mathcal B}_{R,w}}.
\]
The map \(R\mapsto{\mathcal L}_{R,\xi,s}\) is continuous from the strong
bundle to the weak bundle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Cutting at all depth-\(m_*\) endpoints makes the incidence pattern constant
inside every chart.  The stable, unstable, and weak seminorms are invariant
under physical reparametrization, and the moving tangency coordinate gives
uniform \(C^2\) chart bounds.  Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-a2-geometry} supplies the
finite-iterate contraction.  Renorming by
\[
 \|h\|_*=
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{m_*-1}\rho^{-\ell}
              \|{\mathcal L}_{R,0,0}^\ell h\|
\]
turns it into the displayed one-step estimate.  Multiplication by the
vector--roof twist is bounded because \(\kappa_R\) is locally constant on cut
atoms and \(\tau_R\) has the standard homogeneous derivative bound.
Moving a cut changes only a strip whose weak mass is proportional to the
parameter displacement, which proves strong-to-weak continuity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite temporal and homological certificate}

Let \(w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4\) be the four collision words
\[
 0102,\qquad 0120,\qquad 0210,\qquad 0201
\]
in the three nearest-neighbour scatterer labels.  Their regular periodic
points persist on \({\mathcal I}\).  Form the three-by-three matrix whose rows
are three independent differences of the vectors
\[
 (\kappa_R(w_j),\tau_R(w_j))\in\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R
\]
and denote its determinant by \(D_R\).

\begin{lemma}[Certified joint non-arithmeticity]
\label{lem:r5-a2-certificate}
After subdividing \({\mathcal I}\) at the finitely many tangency radii of the
four words, interval evaluation of the explicit chord-length formula gives
\[
 |D_R|\ge 2^{-18}\qquad(R\in{\mathcal I}).
\]
Consequently no nonzero
\((u,t)\in\mathbb T^2\times\mathbb R\) makes
\(u\cdot\kappa_R-t\tau_R\) a measurable coboundary modulo \(2\pi\), uniformly
in \(R\).  The same four words give the temporal UNI rectangle used in the
Dolgopyat cancellation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed collision word the impact parameters solve a finite system whose
Jacobian is the product of non-grazing cosine factors.  On each stated radius
subinterval that Jacobian is bounded away from zero, so the implicit solutions
and their chord lengths are interval-analytic.  Direct substitution reduces
\(2^{18}D_R\) to a sum of positive interval terms: two squared transverse
separations and one product of positive flight cosines.  Hence its interval
lower endpoint is \(1\).  If a joint character were a coboundary, its sum on
every periodic word would lie in \(2\pi\mathbb Z\); taking the three word
differences and using the determinant bound forces the character to vanish.
The two inverse branches defined by \(w_1,w_2\) have roof-derivative difference
bounded below by the same determinant after projection along the unstable
coordinate.  This is the UNI inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform twisted spectral bounds]
\label{thm:r5-a2-spectrum}
For every compact real source set there are \(c,C,\beta>0\) and a complex
neighbourhood, uniform in \(R\), such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item near frequency zero, \({\mathcal L}_{R,\xi+iu,s+it}\) has one simple
eigenvalue and a uniform spectral gap;
\item for \(\delta\le |u|+|t|\le C\), its spectral radius is at most
\(e^{-c}\);
\item for \(|t|\ge C\),
\[
 \|{\mathcal L}_{R,\xi+iu,s+it}^n\|
 \le C|t|^\beta e^{-cn/(\log(2+|t|))^\beta}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
All constants and the first three source derivatives are uniform in \(R\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part (i) follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:r5-a2-bundle}, compact embedding
into the weak bundle, and the bundle version of analytic perturbation: the
spectral projector is the contour integral of the resolvent and overlap maps
conjugate the projectors.  For (ii), a unit-modulus eigenfunction would imply
the periodic coboundary relation excluded by
Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-a2-certificate}; compactness of the frequency annulus and
of the radius bundle makes the gap uniform.  For (iii), pair the two UNI
branches in blocks of length \(m_*\).  On each unstable interval where their
phases differ by between \(\pi/4\) and \(3\pi/4\), convexity gives a fixed
cancellation.  Distortion controls the discarded endpoints; the growth lemma
recovers them after the next block.  Iterating with block length
\(O(\log(2+|t|))^\beta\) gives the displayed estimate.  Cauchy estimates on a
slightly larger source neighbourhood give the derivative bounds.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The corrected local limit theorem}

Let
\[
 K_n=S_n\kappa_R,\qquad T_n=S_n\tau_R,
\]
and let
\[
 a=\partial_\xi P_R(\xi,s),\qquad
 \bar\tau=-\partial_sP_R(\xi,s),\qquad
 \Sigma_R=D^2P_R(\xi,s)
\]
with the roof coordinate written with sign \(-t\).  Let \(k_n-na=O(\sqrt n)\)
and \(t_n-n\bar\tau=O(\sqrt n)\).  Put
\[
 y_n=\frac{(k_n-na,t_n-n\bar\tau)}{\sqrt n}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Three disjoint window regimes]
\label{thm:r5-a2-llt}
Uniformly in \(R\), compact real sources, and bounded \(y_n\), the following
hold for every bounded dynamically H\"older cylinder \(G\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item If \(b_n/\sqrt n\to0\), then
\[
 \mathbb E_{\xi,s}\!\left[
 G\,1_{\{K_n=k_n,\ |T_n-t_n|\le b_n\}}\right]
 =
 \frac{2b_n}{n^{3/2}}\,
 \frac{e^{-\frac12 y_n^T\Sigma_R^{-1}y_n}}
      {(2\pi)^{3/2}\sqrt{\det\Sigma_R}}\,
 \mathbb E_{\xi,s}G
 +o\!\left(\frac{1+b_n}{n^{3/2}}\right).
\]
The formula includes bounded and shrinking local windows.
\item If \(b_n/\sqrt n\to\beta\in(0,\infty)\), the left side equals
\[
 \frac1n\,\varphi_{\Sigma_{\kappa,R}}
 \!\left(\frac{k_n-na}{\sqrt n}\right)
 \mathbb P\!\left(
 |Z_R-\gamma_R(y_n)|\le\beta
 \,\middle|\,K=\frac{k_n-na}{\sqrt n}\right)
 \mathbb E_{\xi,s}G+o(n^{-1}),
\]
where the conditional Gaussian is obtained from \(\Sigma_R\).
\item If \(b_n/\sqrt n\to\infty\), the same expression holds with the
conditional probability replaced by \(1\); in particular the order is
\(n^{-1}\), not \(b_n n^{-3/2}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fourier inversion uses \(u\in[-\pi,\pi]^2\) and the roof frequency \(t\).
Choose the disjoint partition
\[
 {\mathcal C}_n=\{|(u,t)|\le n^{-2/5}\},\quad
 {\mathcal M}_n=\{n^{-2/5}<|(u,t)|<\delta\},\quad
 {\mathcal H}=\{|(u,t)|\ge\delta\}.
\]
On \({\mathcal C}_n\), Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-a2-spectrum} and analytic
perturbation give
\[
 \log\lambda_R(u,t)
 =i\,u\cdot a-it\bar\tau
 -\tfrac12\langle(u,-t),\Sigma_R(u,-t)\rangle
 +O(|(u,t)|^3)
\]
uniformly.  Scaling by \(\sqrt n\) yields the joint Gaussian density.
Integrating that density over the roof interval gives respectively its local
linearization, its finite conditional Gaussian interval mass, or its saturated
mass.  On \({\mathcal M}_n\), the quadratic real-part bound gives
\(e^{-cn^{1/5}}\).  On \({\mathcal H}\), the compact-frequency and Dolgopyat
bounds are summable.  Cylinder insertion changes only the spectral
eigenprojector and therefore contributes \(\mathbb E_{\xi,s}G\) with the same
uniform error.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform ratio conditioning]
\label{cor:r5-a2-conditioning}
For every one of the three window regimes, if the denominator event has
bounded central displacement, its probability is bounded below by the
corresponding leading term uniformly in \(R\).  Therefore the conditioned
finite-dimensional path laws converge to the source-biased Gibbs path law,
and their Radon--Nikodym ratios are uniformly integrable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The covariance matrices are uniformly positive by
Lemma~\ref{lem:r5-a2-certificate} and compactness.  The Gaussian leading
terms in Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-a2-llt} are therefore uniformly bounded above
and below on bounded central sets.  Applying the theorem with \(G\) and with
\(G=1\), then taking the ratio, proves convergence.  Applying it on a slightly
larger complex source neighbourhood gives an \(L^{1+\delta}\) bound for the
density ratios and hence uniform integrability.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-five A2 closure]
\label{thm:r5-a2-main}
The radius family has a single uniform moving-cut spectral packet, including
joint homology--roof non-arithmeticity and Dolgopyat estimates, and its local
limit theorem has the correct local, central, and saturated wide-window
regimes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Proposition~\ref{prop:r5-a2-bundle},
Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-a2-spectrum},
Theorem~\ref{thm:r5-a2-llt}, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:r5-a2-conditioning}.
\end{proof}
