\section{Round-ten reconstruction: deterministic-speed marked flows and a projective renewal LDP}
\label{sec:r10-a3}

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the countable first-return alphabet of a fixed Young
base, with admissible edge set \(\mathcal E\subset\mathcal A^2\), return length
\(r(a)\), roof \(\tau(a)\), Gibbs potential \(\phi(a,b)\), and actual
excursion path profile \(K(y,a)\).  Branch labels and transition edges are
retained in the state; a branch average is never substituted for the realized
excursion.

\subsection{The deterministic block-flow state}

For the first \(N\) complete excursions define
\[
 L_N^{\rm e}=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
 \delta_{(a_j,a_{j+1},K(y_j,a_j),r(a_j),\tau(a_j))}.
\]
The speed is the deterministic integer \(N\).  Let \(\mathfrak E\) be the
Polish space of probability measures on the marked edge space with the weak
topology plus convergence of the weight \(1+r+\tau+|\phi|\).  Its stationary
subset has equal source and target alphabet marginals.

\begin{lemma}[State sufficiency and exponential tightness]
\label{lem:r10-a3-state}
The entropy rate, Gibbs-potential average, collision clock, physical clock,
transition admissibility and realized excursion-path barycenter are Borel
functions of \(L_N^{\rm e}\).  Moreover \((L_N^{\rm e})\) is exponentially
tight at speed \(N\) in \(\mathfrak E\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The source and target branch labels are explicit coordinates, so the
conditional transition kernel and its entropy are recovered by disintegration.
The remaining quantities are coordinate integrals.  The Young return tail and
finite-horizon bound give an exponential moment of \(r+\tau\); summable
variations control the negative part of \(\phi\).  Chernoff bounds give
uniform integrability of the weight.  Tightness of finite-window path profiles
follows from the singularity shield.  Weighted Prokhorov compactness gives
exponential tightness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite cores and exact contractions}

Let \(\mathcal A_L\) contain the first \(L\) branches together with a fixed
finite connector set.  Write \(\pi_L\) for the projection which keeps all
edges inside \(\mathcal A_L\) and sends the complement to a marked cemetery
state without forgetting its total weight.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-core marked-flow LDP]
\label{thm:r10-a3-finite}
For every \(L\), \(\pi_LL_N^{\rm e}\) satisfies a full LDP at speed \(N\)
with good rate
\[
 I_L(\lambda)=
 \begin{cases}
 D(q_\lambda\Vert q_\phi\mid\lambda_1),&
 \lambda\text{ stationary and supported on admissible edges},\\
 +\infty,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\]
including the potential normalization and retained cemetery weight.  If
\(L<L'\), then
\[
 I_L(\lambda)=\inf_{\pi_{L,L'}\lambda'=\lambda}I_{L'}(\lambda').
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the finite core, the transfer matrix with cylinder sources is a positive
finite matrix.  Perron--Frobenius perturbation gives its log moment generating
function on all finite sources.  Exponential tilting gives the lower bound and
finite-state entropy duality gives the rate.  The cemetery mark records every
discarded edge and its weight, so projection from \(L'\) to \(L\) is an
actual continuous contraction of the same empirical flow.  The contraction
principle proves exact consistency of the finite rates.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Projective marked-flow LDP]
\label{thm:r10-a3-projective}
The deterministic-speed flows \(L_N^{\rm e}\) satisfy a full good LDP on
\(\mathfrak E\) with
\[
 I_{\rm e}(\lambda)=\sup_L I_L(\pi_L\lambda).
\]
Every finite-rate \(\lambda\) admits finite-core stationary recovery flows
using only edges in \(\mathcal E\), with convergence of entropy, potential,
clock moments and realized path profiles.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Dawson--G\"artner gives the projective weak LDP.  Exponential tightness from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r10-a3-state} upgrades it to the weighted topology and makes the
rate good.  For recovery, truncate a finite-rate stationary edge flow where
its weighted tail is small.  Balance lost in/out mass along the fixed legal
connector network by a finite circulation.  Mixing with a small positive
circulation puts the retained kernel in the relative interior without
introducing forbidden edges.  Conditional entropy converges by uniform
integrability of its information density; profiles and clocks converge by
weighted truncation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Recession as many actual excursions}

For \(M\ge1\), let \(\mathcal R_M\) be the empirical law of \(M\) consecutive
actual excursions conditioned to have total collision clock at least \(L\).
Define the finite-window logarithmic costs and take their lower-semicontinuous
projective epigraph limit, first as \(L\to\infty\), then as \(M\to\infty\).
Call the result \(J_\infty\).

\begin{theorem}[Actual recession principle]
\label{thm:r10-a3-recession}
The rate \(J_\infty\) exists and is convex.  Every sequence of actual
long-excursion blocks obeys its Gamma-liminf bound, and every finite-cost
recession law has a recovery sequence made of legal consecutive excursions
and the fixed connector network.  No convex mixture is asserted to be one
first-return branch.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At a fixed finite path window, log probabilities are subadditive under
concatenation of blocks of complete excursions; visits to the Young base are
allowed between excursions and are precisely the renewal delimiters.  The
connector network has bounded clock and bounded logarithmic Gibbs cost, so it
contributes \(o(L)\).  Fekete's lemma gives the finite-window limits.
Projective consistency and exponential clock tightness give a unique
lower-semicontinuous epigraph limit.  Concatenating proportions of two long
blocks proves convexity.  A diagonal choice over path windows yields actual
recovery blocks and both Gamma bounds.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Collision and physical deterministic horizons}

For a deterministic collision horizon \(n\), stop at the first excursion
crossing \(n\) and retain the cut terminal excursion.  Define the analogous
object at deterministic physical time \(T\), cutting the last suspension
segment at exactly \(T\).

\begin{lemma}[Clock inversion with a retained terminal profile]
\label{lem:r10-a3-clock}
On every rate sublevel, the complete-excursion count divided by \(n\),
respectively by \(T\), is exponentially equivalent to the reciprocal mean
collision, respectively physical, clock.  The terminal cut contributes at
most one excursion and its normalized path mass is exponentially negligible;
it nevertheless hits the prescribed physical time exactly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Exponential moments of \(r\) and \(\tau\) control the maximum excursion among
linearly many renewals.  A terminal excursion of macroscopic size carries the
recession cost already represented by
Theorem~\ref{thm:r10-a3-recession}; otherwise its normalized mass vanishes.
Renewal inversion gives the deterministic clock ratios.  Cutting a suspension
leg changes neither its preceding collision profile nor more than \(o(T)\)
path time.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full collision- and physical-time empirical-path LDP]
\label{thm:r10-a3-main}
The collision-time empirical path law at deterministic horizon \(n\) satisfies
a good LDP at speed \(n\), and the physical-time law at deterministic horizon
\(T\) satisfies a good LDP at speed \(T\).  Their rates are the contractions
of
\[
 I_{\rm e}(\lambda)+J_\infty(\zeta)
\]
under exact recurrent/recession clock normalization and the realized-path
barycenter.  The upper and lower bounds include all finite-rate recurrent and
recession phases.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine the deterministic block-flow LDP with
Theorem~\ref{thm:r10-a3-recession}.  The joint state is exponentially tight
because escaped clock mass is recorded explicitly.
Lemma~\ref{lem:r10-a3-clock} makes collision and physical inversion an
exponentially good map.  The extended contraction principle gives the upper
bound.  For the lower bound, concatenate a finite-core recurrent recovery
flow with an actual recession recovery block in prescribed proportions, use
legal connectors, and cut only the terminal excursion.  Connector and cut
costs are sublinear.
\end{proof}
