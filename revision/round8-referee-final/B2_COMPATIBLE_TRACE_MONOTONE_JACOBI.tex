\section{Round-eight reconstruction: compatible kinetic traces and monotone fork Jacobi fields}
\label{sec:r8-b2}

Boundary flux is not an independent coordinate.  We define the hierarchy as a
closed graph of transport states satisfying the Green identity and collision
compatibilities.  The recollision estimate is proved in the physical Jacobi
metric of a dispersing hard-sphere flow; no coordinate reset is used to erase
singular values.

\subsection{The compatible graph-trace state}

For $k$ particles let $\mathcal D_k$ be smooth compactly supported densities on
nonoverlapping configurations, smooth up to non-grazing collision faces, and
satisfying specular reflection and creation/annihilation compatibility.  Let
$T_k=v\cdot\nabla_x$ and let $\gamma_k^-g$ be the incoming kinetic trace.  Set
\[
 \|g\|_{\mathcal X_k}
 =\|g\|_{L^1_w}+\|T_kg\|_{L^1_w}
 +\|\gamma_k^-g\|_{\mathcal M_w},
\qquad
 \mathcal X_k=\overline{\mathcal D_k}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal X_k}}.
\]
The trace in the completion is the limit of the traces of the approximants.

\begin{lemma}[Closed kinetic trace and Green identity]
\label{lem:r8-b2-trace}
The map $g\mapsto\gamma_k^-g$ is closed on $\mathcal X_k$.  Every
$g\in\mathcal X_k$ satisfies
\[
 \int (T_kg)\phi+\int g(T_k^*\phi)
 =\int_{\partial^-}\phi\,d\gamma_k^-g
 -\int_{\partial^+}\phi\,d\gamma_k^+g
\]
for every smooth test function, together with the reflection and contact
compatibility inherited from $\mathcal D_k$.  Conversely an arbitrary pair of
interior and boundary measures is not a state unless it satisfies these
relations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identity holds on $\mathcal D_k$.  Each term is continuous in the graph
norm, so it passes to the completion.  If $g_n\to g$, $T_kg_n\to h$, and
$\gamma^-g_n\to\gamma$, the identity identifies $h=T_kg$ distributionally and
$\gamma=\gamma^-g$; hence the trace graph is closed.  The boundary
compatibilities are linear closed relations and also pass to the limit.
\end{proof}

Let
\[
 \mathcal X_\beta=\left\{G=(g_k)_{k\ge0}:
 \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{\beta^k}{k!}\|g_k\|_{\mathcal X_k}<\infty\right\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Positive boundary-renewal semigroup]
\label{thm:r8-b2-semigroup}
The free transport with specular reflection and the creation operators
$B_{k,k+1}$ define a closed dissipative generator on $\mathcal X_\beta$ and a
positive strongly continuous semigroup.  Collision-time Dirac traces belong to
its graph closure, and
\[
 \|B_{k,k+1}g_{k+1}\|_{\mathcal X_k}
 \le C(k+1)\|g_{k+1}\|_{\mathcal X_{k+1}}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Solve the resolvent along backward characteristics until the first boundary
hit.  The reflection map is flux preserving and the contact creation kernel is
a positive boundary integral.  For sufficiently large resolvent parameter the
boundary renewal is a contraction; its Neumann series gives the resolvent and
preserves positivity.  The flux measure of one selected partner contributes at
most $C$ and there are $k+1$ partners, proving the bound.  Factorial weights
make the triangular hierarchy operator relatively bounded.  Lumer--Phillips
then gives the semigroup.  Approximating a collision trace by incoming thin
layers shows that its Dirac limit belongs to the graph closure.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Monotone Jacobi geometry from the youngest fork}

For an orthogonal Jacobi field write $(J,V)$ for position and velocity
variations and
\[
 \mathcal Q(J,V)=\langle J,V\rangle.
\]
The unstable cone is $\mathcal Q\ge0$ with positive collision curvature.  A
free flight of length $t$ sends $(J,V)$ to $(J+tV,V)$.  At a dispersing
collision the curvature operator gains a positive term
$2\mathcal K/|v\cdot n|$.

\begin{lemma}[Dispersing monotonicity]
\label{lem:r8-b2-monotone}
Along a non-grazing hard-sphere collision path, the unstable Jacobi norm
\[
 \|(J,V)\|_*^2=|V|^2+\mathcal Q(J,V)+|J|^2
\]
does not lose a transverse exterior two-volume by more than a factor
$C\delta^C$ depending only on the minimal angle and event gap $\delta$, not on
the number of later collisions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
During free flight,
$\mathcal Q(J+tV,V)=\mathcal Q(J,V)+t|V|^2$.  At collision, the mirror formula
adds the positive quadratic form
$2\langle\mathcal KJ,J\rangle/|v\cdot n|$ to the curvature.  Hence the
associated positive Lagrangian graph is monotone.  Apply the same calculation
to the second exterior power: the Gram determinant of two unstable fields is
nondecreasing in the Jacobi metric.  On a finite-horizon chart with the final
free-flight gap at least $\delta$, the Riccati formula
$B(t)=B_0(I+tB_0)^{-1}$ gives
$0\le B(t)\le\delta^{-1}I$ before the final impact.  Thus the Jacobi norm and
the physical position exterior norm are equivalent with a fixed
$C\delta^{-C}$ factor.  Conversion to impact coordinates uses only the final
non-grazing collision chart and is independent of the number of earlier
collisions.
\end{proof}

At the youngest separating fork of two labels choose the two root variations
consisting of transverse relative translations of the two child clusters.
They form an unstable two-plane immediately after the fork.

\begin{theorem}[Depth-independent first-surplus Gramian]
\label{thm:r8-b2-gramian}
On the good chronological set where all speeds, angles, and event gaps exceed
$\delta$, the derivative from the two fork variables to the relative contact
coordinate at the first surplus collision has Gramian
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\mathcal G_{ab}\ge c\delta^C,
\]
with $C$ independent of ancestral depth.  The complementary set has flux
measure at most $C^m\delta^\alpha$ in a depth-$m$ sector.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At the fork the two relative translations span the contact transverse plane
with a uniform determinant.  They lie in the unstable Jacobi cone.  Subsequent
hard-sphere collisions and free flights preserve their exterior volume by
Lemma~\ref{lem:r8-b2-monotone}; later dispersing impacts therefore cannot focus
them to zero.  The final impact-coordinate conversion costs only
$\delta^{-C}$.  The bad-set estimate follows by integrating one speed, angle,
or event-gap variable at the first failing event; the number of possible
locations is exponential in $m$ but is later absorbed by the hierarchy
factorial.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Summable recollision sectors]
\label{cor:r8-b2-recollision}
The cyclic depth-$m$ contribution is bounded by
\[
 \frac{(CT)^m}{m!}\bigl(\varepsilon^2\delta^{-C}+\delta^\alpha\bigr).
\]
Choosing $\delta=\varepsilon^{2/(C+\alpha)}$ and summing over $m$ gives
$C_T\varepsilon^{\eta}$ for some $\eta>0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Coarea at the surplus contact gives the first term using
Theorem~\ref{thm:r8-b2-gramian}; the bad chronological set gives the second.
The exponential genealogy count is absorbed by $m!^{-1}$.  Optimize in
$\delta$ and sum the exponential series.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Source-uniform one-block convergence}

Let $\mathcal S_{h,\varepsilon}^H$ be the exact microscopic graph semigroup with
a bounded contact source $H$ and let $\mathcal S_h^H$ be the Boltzmann graph
semigroup.

\begin{theorem}[One-block graph-domain estimate]
\label{thm:r8-b2-one-block}
For every reached graph radius and source ball,
\[
 \|\mathcal S_{h,\varepsilon}^H-\mathcal S_h^H\|_{\mathcal X_\beta\to
 \mathcal X_{\beta'}}
 \le C_R(h\varepsilon^\eta+\varepsilon^\eta),
\]
and the same bound holds for every fixed number of source derivatives.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand both semigroups by the boundary-renewal Duhamel series.  Noncyclic trees
are matched by the Boltzmann--Grad collision change of variables in the graph
trace coordinates.  Cyclic trees are bounded by
Corollary~\ref{cor:r8-b2-recollision}.  Differentiating the bounded source only
marks collision vertices and is absorbed by a smaller factorial radius.  The
remainder from two events inside one block is $O(h\varepsilon^\eta)$ by the
same coarea estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stable source sewing]
\label{thm:r8-b2-sewing}
Products of one-block operators converge, uniformly over partitions whose mesh
tends to zero, to one nonlinear pressure semigroup.  The limit is independent
of the partition and retains the actual reflected future after every contact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The graph semigroups are uniformly Lipschitz on reached source balls.  A
standard telescoping sum uses
Theorem~\ref{thm:r8-b2-one-block}; consistency of adjacent blocks follows from
the exact Markov semigroup law in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r8-b2-semigroup}.  The state is never stopped or deleted at a
surplus contact, so the reflected future remains in every factor.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Balance-preserving regularization}

Let $\mathfrak G_h$ be a heat kernel on the compact group generated by spatial
translations and small canonical velocity transformations preserving total
momentum, energy, and the hard-sphere collision manifold.  Average an entire
microscopic path law under this group and then take its density/contact
coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[Commuting path-law smoothing]
\label{lem:r8-b2-smoothing}
The regularization $R_h$ obtained in this way preserves positivity, total mass,
momentum, energy, and the exact weak density--contact balance.  It maps finite
action states to smooth compatible graph states and
$R_h(f,\Gamma)\to(f,\Gamma)$ in the action topology.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every group element conjugates the hard-sphere flow and its contact current,
so pushforward commutes with the microscopic continuity identity.  Averaging
therefore preserves the identity exactly.  Heat-kernel convolution on the
group smooths every noninvariant direction.  Convexity of relative entropy
gives action contraction, and approximate-identity convergence gives the
limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Grand-canonical and microcanonical dynamic LDP]
\label{thm:r8-b2-ldp}
The sewn pressure has the full good convex dual on compatible density--contact
paths.  Smooth positive exposed paths give the lower bound by source tilting;
Lemma~\ref{lem:r8-b2-smoothing} extends it to every finite-action path.  B1
coefficient transfer then yields the microcanonical LDP in the order
GC$\to$B1$\to$MC.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound follows from exponential tightness and the source Laplace
principle.  On a smooth compatible path, finite projected exposing equations
have uniformly positive covariance on the quotient of collision invariants;
solve them and pass through the source sewing theorem.  Approximate a general
finite-action path by $R_h$ and use lower semicontinuity plus entropy
contraction.  Finally apply the exact shell ratio of B1 to every finite source
projection and then use exponential tightness.
\end{proof}
