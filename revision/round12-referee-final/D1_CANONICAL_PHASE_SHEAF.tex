\section{Round-twelve positive closure: canonical phase sheaves and localized mixture LDPs}
\label{sec:r12-d1}

The label is not an arbitrary partition of unity.  It is constructed from the
finite family of positive peripheral bands of the platform transfer/cluster
operator and is compatible under every finite projection.  An explicit
``overlap'' label is retained.  Normalized conditional pressures and
unnormalized restricted partition functions are never conflated.

\subsection{Canonical positive phase bands}

Let $\mathcal T_{n,\theta}$ be the positive finite-volume transfer/cluster
operator of the platform on the declared regular coexistence chart.  Assume
its peripheral spectrum consists of finitely many isolated positive bands
separated by a uniform annular gap; A2--A4 and B1--B4 provide these band
operators on their respective charts.  Positivity gives band projections
$E_{n,j}\ge0$.  Put
\[
 \chi_{n,j}(x)={E_{n,j}1(x)\over\sum_kE_{n,k}1(x)+R_n1(x)},
 \qquad
 \chi_{n,\partial}={R_n1\over\sum_kE_{n,k}1+R_n1},
\]
where $R_n$ is the positive complementary band.  Thus the overlap/remainder
label $\partial$ is an actual component.

\begin{theorem}[Exact canonical disintegration]
\label{thm:r12-d1-label}
The variables $J_n\in\{1,\ldots,J,\partial\}$ with conditional probabilities
$\chi_{n,j}$ define an exact positive disintegration of the original
microscopic law.  The same $J_n$ is used for all finite projections, hence the
labelled laws are projectively compatible.  On the regular chart the
$\partial$ weight is superexponentially small; it is nevertheless retained in
every exact identity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Positive band projections and the complementary positive operator give a
partition of unity pointwise.  Sample the label conditionally on the original
microstate; summing labels reconstructs the law exactly.  Since the label is
defined before applying any observable projection, compatibility is automatic.
The peripheral gap and the platform compact-containment estimate make the
complementary band superexponentially small on the chart.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Normalized component pressures}

Let $w_{n,j}=\mathbb E\chi_{n,j}$ and
$\mathbb P_{n,j}=w_{n,j}^{-1}\chi_{n,j}\mathbb P_n$.  Define
\[
 \widetilde Q_{n,j}(\theta)
 ={1\over n}\log\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{n,j}}e^{n\theta X_n},
 \qquad
 \alpha_j=-\lim {1\over n}\log w_{n,j}.
\]
The unnormalized restricted pressure is
$Q_{n,j}=n^{-1}\log w_{n,j}+\widetilde Q_{n,j}$.

\begin{theorem}[Localized zero-free and component LDP theorem]
\label{thm:r12-d1-component}
Each positive band has a phase-local zero-free complex chart, a full good
conditional LDP with rate $I_j$, boundary-face recovery, and the uniform local
coefficients/Gaussian tangents supplied by its platform theorem.  The
localized operator is the positive band operator itself, not multiplication
by an arbitrary sharp basin indicator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Riesz contour defining a positive peripheral band is part of the platform
spectral/cluster operator.  Restriction to its invariant range preserves the
simple leading eigenvalue and its zero-free neighborhood.  The A3 or B2
Laplace principles apply with the positive band as an additional finite state;
their entropy-control recoveries can be conditioned on that state.  Normal
source convergence gives local coefficients and Gaussian derivatives.
Boundary faces are obtained by the same band-preserving normal-source limits.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joint labelled LDP and exact phase minimum]
\label{thm:r12-d1-mixture}
The pair $(J_n,X_n)$ satisfies the good LDP
\[
 \mathcal J(j,x)=\alpha_j+I_j(x),
 \qquad
 \mathcal J(\partial,x)=I_\partial(x).
\]
After contraction of the label,
\[
 I(x)=\min\bigl\{\min_j(\alpha_j+I_j(x)),I_\partial(x)\bigr\}.
\]
The physical pressure is
\[
 Q(\theta)=\max\bigl\{
 \max_j[-\alpha_j+\widetilde Q_j(\theta)],
 Q_\partial(\theta)\bigr\};
\]
no phase cost is counted twice and no convexification is attributed to label
contraction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound is a finite union of the component upper bounds.  For an open
set choose a component attaining the infimum and use its conditional lower
bound together with the asymptotic weight.  Exponential tightness is uniform
across the finite labels.  Contraction gives the minimum rate.  The exact
partition-function sum gives the maximum pressure with the displayed
normalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conditioning, tangents, and dynamics}

\begin{theorem}[Phase-compatible shell and commutation theorem]
\label{thm:r12-d1-commute}
For every regular shell, the exact joint labelled coefficient is the finite sum
of the A2/B1 phase coefficients, including the $\partial$ component.  Shell
conditioning, typed observable contraction, Gaussian tangent formation, and
the component dynamic semigroups commute on the labelled law.  At coexistence
every subsequential physical tangent is the mixture selected by the actual
subexponential component weights; if these weights converge, the mixture is
unique.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the phase-local coefficient before summing the exact positive
disintegration.  Boundary contributions are already part of the
$\partial$ label, so no unlabelled overlap is lost.  Conditioning and
observable projection are ordinary conditional operations on one joint law.
Phase-wise Gaussian and semigroup convergence are uniform on the finite band
family, permitting finite summation.  Tightness of the normalized weights
gives subsequential mixtures, and convergence of the weights gives uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Projective phase sheaf]
\label{prop:r12-d1-sheaf}
On overlaps of two regular source charts, positive band labels are identified
by continuation of their Riesz contours.  These identifications satisfy the
cocycle law and form a finite phase sheaf over the regular pressure atlas.
All finite observable projections use its pullback; labels are never redefined
projection by projection.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A peripheral band cannot cross another band without leaving the regular atlas.
Analytic continuation of its contour is therefore unique on overlaps.  Uniqueness
implies the cocycle law.  Pullback along an observable projection leaves the
microscopic label unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-twelve D1 closure]
\label{thm:r12-d1-main}
The Sinai and hard-sphere regular coexistence charts possess canonical positive
phase labels, normalized component pressures, full component LDPs, exact
minimum-rate contraction, and the phase-compatible shell/tangent/semigroup
commutation theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r12-d1-label},
\ref{thm:r12-d1-component}, \ref{thm:r12-d1-mixture}, and
\ref{thm:r12-d1-commute}, with Proposition~\ref{prop:r12-d1-sheaf}.
\end{proof}
