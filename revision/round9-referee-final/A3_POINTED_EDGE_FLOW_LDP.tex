\section{Round-nine reconstruction: pointed edge flows and a proved projective LDP}
\label{sec:r9-a3}

The state below retains the actual inducing edge, the actual excursion profile,
the return-step frequency, and the pointed prefix of a terminal excursion.  A
boundary law may be a mixture of several long excursions; only its extreme
points are asserted to have single-excursion recovery.

\subsection{The marked renewal-flow state}

Let $(Y,F^r)$ be the inducing system, with countable branch alphabet
$\mathcal A$, transition graph $\mathcal G$, collision return length $r(a)$,
physical roof $\mathfrak t(a,y)$, and actual excursion path
$\Gamma_a(y)$.  For a completed excursion define its normalized path profile
\[
 K(a,y)=\frac1{r(a)}\sum_{j=0}^{r(a)-1}
 \delta_{\Theta^j\Gamma_a(y)}.
\]
For a word of $N$ completed excursions, with total collision clock
$R_N=\sum_{j<N}r(a_j)$, define the length-weighted edge/profile flow
\[
 Q_N=\frac1{R_N}\sum_{j<N}r(a_j)
 \delta_{(a_j,a_{j+1},K(a_j,y_j))}
\]
and the return-step intensity $\beta_N=N/R_N$.  The state also contains the
usual finite-dimensional current/roof moments.  For a terminal cut at fraction
$u\in[0,1]$ of an excursion, write
\[
 K^{\rm pt}(a,y,u)=\frac1{1\vee\lfloor ur(a)\rfloor}
 \sum_{j<ur(a)}\delta_{\Theta^j\Gamma_a(y)}.
\]

Let $\overline{\mathcal E}$ be the compactification obtained by adjoining
$x=(1+r)^{-1}=0$ and by taking the weak compactification of the profile and
pointed-profile coordinates.  A boundary state is a finite measure
$Q^\infty$ on the $x=0$ face together with a block-count intensity
$\beta^\infty\ge0$; this keeps both clock mass and the frequency data lost when
$r\to\infty$.

\begin{lemma}[The rate data are functions of the state]
\label{lem:r9-a3-state}
On $x>0$, the edge marginals of $Q$ and $\beta$ determine the unweighted
stationary return-step flow, its entropy, the induced potential average, and
all clock normalizations.  On $x=0$, the pair $(Q^\infty,\beta^\infty)$ retains
the limiting profile mixture and the asymptotic number of long excursions per
unit collision clock.  Hence no hidden branch-frequency variable is needed in
the rate functional.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a finite edge $e=(a,b)$, divide its length-weighted mass by the known
$r(a)$ and normalize by $\beta$.  The incoming and outgoing marginals agree
because $Q_N$ is an empirical edge flow up to two boundary terms of size
$O(R_N^{-1})$.  The entropy and potential are the standard functions of that
unweighted flow.  If $r\to\infty$, division by $r$ is no longer continuous;
$\beta^\infty$ is precisely the missing limit of the total divided mass.
The actual profile and edge label remain in $Q^\infty$, so different long
excursion types and their mixtures remain distinguishable.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite marked-flow pressures}

Fix finitely many edges, bounded cylinder tests of $K$ and $K^{\rm pt}$, and
bounded current/roof tests.  Their source is a summable-variation potential on
the induced shift with one additive mark per completed excursion.  Denote the
corresponding source vector by $\theta\).

\begin{theorem}[Finite marked-flow LDP]
\label{thm:r9-a3-finite}
For every finite source family, the projected empirical marked flow satisfies a
full good LDP with speed $R_N$.  Its logarithmic pressure is the unique root of
the source-decorated renewal operator, is differentiable on the regular chart,
and its convex dual is the finite projected rate $I_m$.  The family
$(I_m)_m$ is projectively compatible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Truncate return length and homogeneity index first.  The resulting finite
Markov graph has a primitive transfer matrix; Perron--Frobenius perturbation
gives the pressure and the full finite-state empirical-flow LDP.  For the
countable system, the A2 vector--roof spectral estimate applies to the
source-decorated return operator because edge indicators are branchwise
constant and each profile test is a bounded sum along one excursion.  The
exponential return and homogeneity tails make the truncations exponentially
good, uniformly on compact source sets.  Passing the finite-state upper and
lower bounds through this approximation gives the claimed LDP.  Deleting a
test coordinate is an exact continuous projection, so uniqueness of rate
functions gives projective compatibility.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Projective epigraph closure}

Let $\pi_m$ enumerate all finite edge, profile, current, roof, block-intensity,
and pointed-prefix coordinates.  Define
\[
 I(\Xi)=\sup_m I_m(\pi_m\Xi).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Projective marked-flow LDP]
\label{thm:r9-a3-projective}
The full marked state $(Q_N,\beta_N)$, augmented by the terminal pointed
coordinate, satisfies a full good LDP on the compact projective state space,
with rate $I$.  Every finite-rate point has one common microscopic recovery
sequence; the projection index may increase only after the microscopic block
length has been chosen.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite upper bounds and projective exponential tightness give the upper
bound by Dawson--G\"artner.  For the lower bound, choose successively nested
projective neighborhoods $U_m$ and finite-state tilted recovery words from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r9-a3-finite}.  Exponential approximation permits a diagonal
schedule $m=m(N)\uparrow\infty$ so slowly that all previously chosen
probability lower bounds and tail errors remain valid.  Specification joins
the finite recovery blocks with legal graph paths; connector length is
$o(R_N)$ and its Gibbs cost is $o(R_N)$.  This produces one path sequence, not
one unrelated sequence for each projection.  Compactness of the marked state
and lower semicontinuity make $I$ good.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Recession laws: one excursion versus a mixture}

For $L\ge1$, let $\mathcal R_L$ be the laws of actual excursions with
$r\ge L$, represented on the boundary profile/edge space and normalized by
collision length.  Define the recession rate as the epigraph limit
\[
 I_\infty(\zeta,\beta)
 =\sup_m\lim_{L\to\infty}
 \inf\left\{I_m(\pi_m\Xi):
 \Xi\in\mathcal R_L,\ 
 d_m(\pi_m\Xi,\pi_m(\zeta,\beta))<L^{-1}
 \right\},
\]
followed by lower-semicontinuous convex closure over finite mixtures of actual
long excursions.

\begin{theorem}[Actual recession recovery]
\label{thm:r9-a3-recession}
The preceding epigraph limit exists and equals the restriction of the full
projective rate to the $x=0$ face.  Extreme finite-rate boundary points admit
recovery by one sequence of actual first-return excursions.  A general
boundary point admits recovery by a triangular array of finitely many actual
long-excursion types whose length-weighted empirical mixture converges to the
prescribed boundary law.  No concatenation of several excursions is called a
single first-return branch.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite-projection infima are monotone after passing to epigraphs, hence have
a projective limit.  The common diagonal recovery of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r9-a3-projective} gives the limsup.  For an extreme point,
Choquet separation in every finite projection forces one actual excursion type
in any near-minimizing mixture; a diagonal choice gives single-excursion
recovery.  A general boundary law is approximated by a finite convex
combination of extreme boundary laws.  Choose one long actual excursion for
each component and repeat the components in proportions matching their
length-weighted masses.  Legal connector excursions are retained as separate
blocks and have vanishing clock fraction.  The block-intensity coordinate
records their count, so the construction preserves both the cost and the
hidden frequency data.  The matching lower bound follows from the finite
projected LDPs.
\end{proof}

The convex dual of $I_\infty$ is the boundary pressure on bounded cylinder
sources.  This identity is a consequence of the proved projective LDP and
Fenchel--Moreau on the compact boundary state; it is not used to define the
recovery sequence.

\subsection{Finite-core entropy approximation and renewal alternatives}

A stationary countable edge flow may be a mixture of closed communicating
classes.  We approximate its ergodic components separately.

\begin{theorem}[Period-respecting finite-core approximation]
\label{thm:r9-a3-core}
Every finite-rate stationary flow is approximated in rate and in all marked
moments by a finite convex combination of stationary flows on finite strongly
connected subgraphs.  Each component approximation preserves the period of
that component.  No single strongly connected graph is required to represent
a mixture of distinct closed classes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Disintegrate the stationary flow into ergodic communicating components.
Within one component, truncate to a finite set carrying all but $\epsilon$ of
flow, entropy, potential, and clock mass.  Decompose the retained circulation
into cycles and add only legal connector cycles inside the same component.
Their total flow can be chosen $O(\epsilon)$; entropy and potential errors tend
to zero by the finite-rate tail bound.  Taking connector lengths in the
component period preserves the period.  Approximate the ergodic decomposition
by a finite convex combination and then let $\epsilon\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal R(z)$ be the return-operator renewal family on the A2 strong/weak
space.  A component is strongly positive recurrent when its finite-core
pressure lies strictly above its tail pressure.

\begin{theorem}[Renewal spectral/recession dichotomy]
\label{thm:r9-a3-spr}
On every recurrent component exactly one of the following occurs.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The finite-core pressure has a uniform gap above the tail pressure.  Then
$1-\mathcal R(z)$ has a simple zero and the induced Ruelle operator has an
isolated recurrent phase with exponential return tails.
\item The gap vanishes.  Every normalized approximate leading vector loses a
positive fraction of its clock mass outside each finite edge set, and every
limit point is represented by the recession boundary rate
$I_\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finite truncations are positive quasicompact operators.  A uniform pressure gap
gives a common resolvent contour and a simple pole by analytic Fredholm
continuation.  If no gap exists, assume an approximate eigenfamily were tight
on one finite edge set.  Compactness on that set would produce an eigenvector
with pressure strictly above the tail pressure, a contradiction.  Thus mass
escapes every finite core.  The marked-flow compactification and
Theorem~\ref{thm:r9-a3-recession} identify exactly the possible escaped limits
and their cost.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Singularity shielding and deterministic physical time}

Let $\mathscr S_{q,L}$ be all billiard singularity and homogeneity cuts seen by
a path window of depth $q$ and return cutoff $L$.  The A2 growth lemma and
homogeneity estimates give
\[
 \#\mathscr S_{q,L}\le CLe^{cq},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(N_\delta(\mathscr S_{q,L}))
 \le CLe^{cq}\delta^\alpha.
\]
Exponential tilting yields an exponential frequency bound for visits to this
set and for long return/roof tails.

\begin{lemma}[Exponentially good physical coding]
\label{lem:r9-a3-shield}
Collision-path expansion, suspension-time expansion, and pointed terminal cuts
have continuous versions off $N_\delta(\mathscr S_{q,L})$.  The discrepancy
between the true physical empirical path and these versions is exponentially
good in the ordered limit $q,L\to\infty$, $\delta\downarrow0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A discrepancy in a fixed path window requires a long excursion, a visit to the
singularity neighborhood, or an initial/terminal residual.  The first two have
arbitrarily large exponential cost by the source-decorated return operator.
The residual is represented by the pointed coordinate and changes only a
vanishing fraction unless it carries macroscopic clock, in which case it is a
boundary state already covered by $I_\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Collision- and physical-time empirical-path LDP]
\label{thm:r9-a3-physical}
The collision-time and deterministic physical-time empirical path laws satisfy
full good LDPs.  Their rates are the contractions of $I$ by the collision Palm
and suspension Palm maps, with the pointed terminal-excursion cost retained.
The lower bound hits an exact deterministic time by cutting the last actual
flight; the cut changes no completed excursion and is continuous in the
pointed state.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the extended contraction theorem to
Theorems~\ref{thm:r9-a3-projective} and
\ref{lem:r9-a3-shield}.  For the lower bound, take a common recovering marked
flow.  Stop after the last completed excursion before the target time and cut
the following actual flight at the required age.  If its duration is
sublinear, its empirical effect vanishes; if it is macroscopic, the pointed
boundary coordinate records its prefix profile and rate.  Thus no integer
Frobenius argument is used for a real roof clock.
\end{proof}
