\section{Round-thirteen positive closure: invariant duals and a common-domain response bundle}
\label{sec:r13-c2}

Invariant-integral equivalence, pressure equivalence, spectral response, and
state contraction are four different notions.  They are stated separately.
The phase-dependent generators are compared through a common closed form
domain built from the cylinder algebra; no finite correlation matrix is used
to identify entire mutually singular $L^2$ spaces.

\subsection{The invariant-integral quotient}

Let $\mathscr A_W$ be the weighted dynamically Hölder cylinder algebra and
let
\[
 \mathscr N_{\rm inv}
 =\overline{\operatorname{span}}
 \{U-U\circ\Theta_t:U\in\mathscr A_W,
                         t\in\mathbb Q_+\}^{\mathscr A_W}.
\]
Constants are not included in $\mathscr N_{\rm inv}$.

\begin{theorem}[Signed invariant-Radon dual]
\label{thm:r13-c2-dual}
The continuous dual of
$\mathscr A_W/\mathscr N_{\rm inv}$ is the vector space of finite invariant
signed Radon measures with finite $W$-moment.  Invariant probabilities form
its normalized positive slice.  Consequently
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\int Fd\mu=\int Gd\mu$ for every invariant probability iff
      $F-G\in\mathscr N_{\rm inv}$;
\item the two integrals differ by one common constant $c$ for every invariant
      probability iff $F-G-c1\in\mathscr N_{\rm inv}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A finite signed invariant Radon measure annihilates every time coboundary and
therefore acts on the quotient.  Conversely, lift a quotient functional to
$\mathscr A_W$.  Weighted strict duality represents it by a countably additive
signed Radon measure.  Average its time translates.  The uniform Lyapunov
bound proved on each platform makes these averages weighted-tight; every
limit is invariant and preserves the pairing.  Hahn--Banach separation then
shows that the annihilator of all invariant signed measures is exactly the
closed coboundary space.  Evaluating constants gives the two distinct
statements (i)--(ii).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Full pressure-functional rigidity}

For a potential $F$, write
\[
 \mathscr P_F(H)=P(F+H)-P(F)
\]

after fixing the platform and one regular phase chart.

\begin{theorem}[Platform-typed pressure rigidity]
\label{thm:r13-c2-rigidity}
Suppose $\mathscr P_F(H)=\mathscr P_G(H)$ for every $H$ in a neighborhood of
zero.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item On the mixing Sinai symbolic/suspension chart,
$F-G=c+U-U\circ\Theta$ in the weighted Hölder closure.
\item On the finite-horizon hard-sphere source chart, the density/contact
source difference is a constant plus the closed B3 endpoint--balance gauge,
including the direct contact coordinate.
\end{enumerate}
Equality of one scalar pressure value is not used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating the full perturbation identity at every nearby $H$ shows that
all nearby equilibrium linear functionals coincide.  On the Sinai chart,
periodic closing and the returned-branch spectral theorem imply equality of
all periodic sums; the weighted Livšic theorem gives the constant plus
coboundary representation.  On the hard-sphere finite-time chart, strict
convexity of the exact cumulant on the B3 quotient says that its full family of
first and second variations separates every class except the closed
endpoint--balance gauge.  The contact coefficient is $\Delta p+\psi$, so pure
contact directions are included.  This yields (ii) without invoking a
hyperbolic Livšic theorem for hard spheres.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A common closed form domain}

Let $\eta$ range over a compact regular phase chart.  Start from one common
cylinder algebra $\mathcal C$.  On $\mathcal C$ define
\[
 \|F\|_{\mathcal V}^2
 =\sup_\eta
 \left(\|F\|_{L^2(\pi_\eta)}^2+
       \mathfrak a_\eta(F,F)+
       \|F\|_{\rm tr,\eta}^2\right),
\]
where $\mathfrak a_\eta$ is the Doob/kinetic graph form and the null modes are
removed before completion.  Let $\mathcal V$ be the completion and let
$\mathcal V\hookrightarrow\mathcal H_\eta\hookrightarrow\mathcal V^*$ be the
resulting continuous field of Hilbert spaces.

\begin{theorem}[Common-domain type-(B) bundle]
\label{thm:r13-c2-form}
On the regular chart, the norms above are uniformly equivalent on
$\mathcal C$, each form $\mathfrak a_\eta$ is closed sectorial on the common
domain $\mathcal V$, and
$\eta\mapsto\mathfrak a_\eta(F,G)$ is $C^q$ for
$F,G$ in the corresponding fresh strong levels.  The associated resolvents
satisfy, as maps $\mathcal V^*\to\mathcal V$,
\[
 D_\eta(z-L_\eta)^{-1}
 =(z-L_\eta)^{-1}(D_\eta L_\eta)(z-L_\eta)^{-1},
\]
where $D_\eta L_\eta$ is interpreted as a bounded form
$\mathcal V\to\mathcal V^*$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A2/A4 give uniform graph and transfer estimates on the Sinai chart; B2/B3
give the corresponding transport/contact energy estimates on the hard-sphere
chart.  On a compact regular chart these bounds make the displayed norms
equivalent and the cylinder algebra complete to one common Gelfand domain.
Closedness and sectoriality follow by the uniform form estimate.  Material
source derivatives are bounded from the next strong level into
$\mathcal V^*$, which gives type-(B) differentiability.  Differentiate the
weak resolvent equation
\[
 z\langle u,v\rangle+\mathfrak a_\eta(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle
\]
for every $v\in\mathcal V$ to obtain the typed formula.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Resolved projections and memory response}

Choose physical observables $g_1,\ldots,g_d\in\mathcal C$ after quotienting
constants, conservation laws, and covariance null modes.  Their correlation
Gram matrix is uniformly positive on the chosen chart.  Let $R_\eta$ be the
orthogonal projection onto their span in $\mathcal H_\eta$.

\begin{theorem}[Covariant compressed-memory derivative]
\label{thm:r13-c2-memory}
The finite-rank sections $R_\eta$ are $C^q$, and on the common form bundle
\[
 C_\eta(z)=R_\eta(z-L_\eta)^{-1}R_\eta,
 \qquad
 \widehat K_\eta(z)=zR_\eta-R_\eta L_\eta R_\eta-C_\eta(z)^{-1}
\]
have typed derivatives obtained by the product rule and
Theorem~\ref{thm:r13-c2-form}.  After adjoining the A4 descriptor modes, the
regular memory kernel and all declared source derivatives decay
exponentially.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Uniform positivity of the finite Gram matrix gives a smooth inverse and hence
a smooth finite-rank projection section.  The form-resolvent derivative is
bounded $\mathcal V^*\to\mathcal V$; composition with the finite-rank sections
is therefore an ordinary matrix-valued differentiable function.  Differentiate
$C^{-1}$ by $D(C^{-1})=-C^{-1}(DC)C^{-1}$.  A4 gives integrable vertical
bounds after all pole/zero Jordan chains are promoted, and these bounds are
stable under the finite number of form derivatives.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Optional projections, Girsanov, and the entropic BSDE}

Assume the A4 conditional kernels converge on a determining weighted cylinder
class, the finite likelihood martingales are uniformly bounded in
$L^{1+\delta}$, and they converge jointly with the driving martingale rough
path.  These hypotheses are now proved in A4 on the regular chart.

\begin{theorem}[Filtration-stable likelihood limit]
\label{thm:r13-c2-filter}
The optional projections of the microscopic likelihoods onto the resolved
history filtration converge stably to
\[
 \mathcal E\left(\int_0^t\theta_s^T\,dW_s\right).
\]
Under the tilted law the limiting resolved diffusion has the corresponding
Girsanov drift.  For bounded terminal value $\Phi$, the pair
$(Y,Z)$ is the unique bounded solution of
\[
 Y_t=\Phi(X_T)+\int_t^T{1\over2}|Z_s|^2ds
      -\int_t^TZ_s\,dW_s,
\]
after absorbing the chosen risk coefficient into $Z$ and the driver.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditional-kernel convergence gives extended weak convergence of the
resolved filtrations on the determining class.  Joint martingale convergence
and the $L^{1+\delta}$ bound pass conditional expectations and optional
projections to the limit.  The limiting exponential is a true martingale and
Girsanov's theorem identifies the drift.  Exponential transformation of the
bounded terminal martingale gives the displayed quadratic BSDE; boundedness
and the standard convex energy estimate give uniqueness in
$\mathcal S^\infty\times\mathrm{BMO}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Source and state chain rules}

Let $\mathcal C:X\to Y$ be a typed continuous contraction.  For a source
$\varphi:Y\to\mathbb R$,
\[
 Q_\mathcal C(\varphi)=Q(\varphi\circ\mathcal C).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Separated contraction derivatives]
\label{prop:r13-c2-chain}
A source perturbation $h:Y\to\mathbb R$ satisfies
\[
 DQ_\mathcal C(\varphi)[h]
 =DQ(\varphi\circ\mathcal C)[h\circ\mathcal C].
\]
If instead the state is varied through $x_s$ with tangent $\dot x$, then
\[
 {d\over ds}\varphi(\mathcal Cx_s)|_{s=0}
 =D\varphi(\mathcal Cx)D\mathcal C(x)\dot x.
\]
These are different derivatives and are never combined into one formula.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first identity is the Fréchet chain rule on the source Banach space; the
second is the ordinary state-space chain rule.  Their domains are distinct.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-thirteen C2 closure]
\label{thm:r13-c2-main}
The series has a correctly typed invariant signed-measure cotangent dual,
platform-specific full-pressure rigidity, a common-domain form bundle for
source and memory response, a filtration-stable likelihood/Girsanov/BSDE
limit, and separated source/state contraction derivatives.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r13-c2-dual},
\ref{thm:r13-c2-rigidity}, \ref{thm:r13-c2-form},
\ref{thm:r13-c2-memory}, and \ref{thm:r13-c2-filter}, with
Proposition~\ref{prop:r13-c2-chain}.
\end{proof}
