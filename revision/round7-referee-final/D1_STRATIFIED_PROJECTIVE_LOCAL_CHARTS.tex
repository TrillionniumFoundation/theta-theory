\section{Round-seven reconstruction: stratified projective pressures, coexistence charts, and exact local likelihoods}
\label{sec:r7-d1}

This section replaces the round-six controlling module.  No global complex
logarithm is assumed.  Each real compact source set is covered by finitely many
zero-free complex tubes.  Bounded projections are treated by convex-support
face stratification rather than by an impossible gradient blow-up at infinity.
Nondifferentiable coexistence pressures are retained as maxima of phase charts
and their lower bounds are obtained by phase-wise recovery.

\subsection{Local zero-free pressure atlases}

Let $\pi_m:E\to\mathbb R^{d_m}$ be a finite coordinate projection.  For every
compact real source set $K\subset\mathbb R^{d_m}$ assume the platform spectral
or cluster theorem gives finitely many open sets $U_{m,j}\supset K_j$ covering
$K$ such that the finite-volume moment generating function has no zero on
$U_{m,j}+iB(0,\eta_{m,j})$.  Fix the logarithm by its real value at one point of
$K_j$.  The limiting phase pressure on that chart is analytic and denoted
$Q_{m,j}$.

The physical pressure is the upper envelope
\[
 Q_m(\theta)=\max_{j\in\mathcal J_m(\theta)}Q_{m,j}(\theta).
\]
At coexistence more than one phase may maximize and $Q_m$ need not be
differentiable.

\begin{lemma}[Zero-free atlas normalization]
\label{lem:r7-d1-atlas}
On overlaps, two logarithm branches differ by an integer multiple of
$2\pi i/\mu_\varepsilon$ and agree on the real axis; hence their normalized
finite-volume pressures and all real derivatives agree.  Local uniform
convergence on the tubes implies convergence of every finite real derivative
on compact subsets.  No single logarithm on all of $\mathbb C^{d_m}$ is used.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The ratio of two nonvanishing analytic moment generating functions is one,
so logarithm branches differ by a constant $2\pi i k$.  Division by the speed
and agreement at the real base point fix $k=0$ for the normalized branch used
on the overlap.  Cauchy's formula gives derivative convergence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-dimensional rates with bounded support and faces}

Let $C_m$ be the closed convex hull of the support of $\pi_m(X)$.  For a phase
chart $j$ define
\[
 I_{m,j}(x)=\sup_{\theta}\{\theta\cdot x-Q_{m,j}(\theta)\}.
\]
Interior points of the phase mean image are reached by finite tilts.  Boundary
points of $C_m$ are organized by exposed faces.  If $F$ is a face exposed by
$n_F$, first send the normal source $rn_F$ to infinity and then use a finite
tangential tilt inside $F$.  The tilted means remain bounded, as they must for
bounded cylinders.

\begin{theorem}[Face-stratified finite-dimensional LDP]
\label{thm:r7-d1-finite}
For every finite projection, the laws of $\pi_m(X_\varepsilon)$ satisfy a full
good LDP with rate
\[
 I_m(x)=\min_j I_{m,j}(x)
\]
on phase-attainable points and its lower-semicontinuous extension to $C_m$.
Interior lower bounds use finite analytic tilts.  Boundary lower bounds use
normal face concentration followed by tangential tilts.  At coexistence the
lower bound is the minimum over the individually recovered phase costs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The local pressure atlas gives the exponential upper bound by finite covering
of compact source sets.  For an interior differentiability point of one phase,
the exact normalized tilt and its pressure gap give the local lower bound.  For
an exposed face, the source $rn_F$ concentrates the law exponentially on that
face because all points outside have a uniform support-function gap.  After
that concentration, the tangential local pressure atlas gives the LDP inside
the lower-dimensional affine span.  Iterate over a flag of faces for
non-smooth boundary points.  Straszewicz approximation of compact convex sets
and lower semicontinuity complete the boundary lower bound.  If several phase
pressures maximize, choose the phase-specific microscopic recovery supplied by
the platform theorem; summing or mixing phase packets changes no exponential
cost.  Thus global differentiability is unnecessary.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Projective passage and topology upgrade}

Let $E_{\rm proj}$ carry the initial topology generated by the maps $\pi_m$ and
let $E$ be the desired weighted path topology.  Assume the projections separate
points and agree with the $E$ topology on every compact rate sublevel.

\begin{lemma}[Exponential-tightness topology upgrade]
\label{lem:r7-d1-topology}
If the laws are exponentially tight in $E$ and satisfy the projective LDP in
$E_{\rm proj}$, then they satisfy the same LDP in $E$.  The rate is
\[
 I(x)=\sup_m I_m(\pi_mx).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a closed $E$ set, intersect with an exponentially good compact set.  On
that compact set the two topologies coincide, so the projective upper bound
applies.  The compact complement has arbitrarily large exponential cost.  An
$E$-open neighborhood of $x$ contains a projective cylinder neighborhood after
restriction to a compact rate sublevel; the projective lower bound then gives
the $E$ lower bound.  Let the compact level increase.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stratified projective full LDP]
\label{thm:r7-d1-projective}
Suppose every finite projection satisfies
Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-d1-finite} and the platform laws are exponentially tight in
$E$.  Then the full laws satisfy a good LDP in $E$ with the projective rate
above.  The theorem permits bounded cylinder coordinates, local complex
charts, phase coexistence, and nondifferentiable pressure envelopes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Dawson--Gartner gives the LDP in the initial projective topology.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-d1-topology}.  Goodness follows from exponential tightness and
the projective compactness of rate sublevels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conditioning on thin constraints}

Let $C:E\to\mathbb R^r$ be a continuous finite coordinate constraint.  A
source-uniform coefficient theorem means that, at every regular constrained
phase, exact finite saddles exist and the shrinking shell probability equals
$e^{o(\mu_\varepsilon)}$ uniformly on bounded pulled-back source charts.  For
hard spheres this is B1; for a Sinai roof/homology shell it is A2.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional projective LDP]
\label{thm:r7-d1-conditioning}
Under the source-uniform coefficient theorem, conditioning on the exact lattice
coordinate and the admissible shrinking continuous shell yields the good rate
\[
 I^a(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 I(x)-\inf_{Cy=a}I(y),&Cx=a,\\
 +\infty,&Cx\ne a.
 \end{cases}
\]
The conclusion is obtained from numerator and denominator shell asymptotics;
it is not inferred from the unconditioned LDP alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the coefficient theorem after every finite source tilt.  The shell factor
is subexponential and cancels between numerator and denominator, so the finite
conditional pressures are the constrained envelopes.  Their face-stratified
lower bounds pass through the projective system.  Exponential tightness on the
constraint surface gives the full conditional LDP.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Contraction and primal--dual commutation}

Let $T:E\to F$ be continuous.  At the primal level both conditioning then
contraction and contraction of the constrained rate give
\[
 J^a(y)=\inf\{I(x)-I_a:Cx=a,\ Tx=y\}.
\]
For dual equality assume $T$ and $C$ are continuous linear maps on the finite
projection and that the projected rate is proper, lower semicontinuous, convex,
with a relative-interior feasible point.

\begin{theorem}[No-gap commutation]
\label{thm:r7-d1-commutation}
Under the stated finite-dimensional constraint qualification, the conjugate of
the constrained contracted rate equals the constrained pressure evaluated on
$T^*\eta$.  At coexistence the equality is applied phase by phase and the lower
semicontinuous envelope is taken afterwards.  For nonlinear contractions only
the primal contraction and the full C2 Frechet chain rule are asserted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The primal identity is the associativity of infima.  On a finite linear
projection, Fenchel--Rockafellar applies because the relative-interior point is
a continuity point of the indicator of $Cx=a$.  Phase-wise application avoids
pretending that a nondifferentiable pressure envelope has one global Hessian.
Projective passage follows by monotone enlargement of the finite source
families.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact local likelihoods and Gaussian tangents}

Fix one real regular source $\Theta$ and a zero-free complex neighborhood from
Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-d1-atlas}.  Put
\[
 m_{\varepsilon,\Theta}=DQ_\varepsilon(\Theta),
 \qquad
 Z_\varepsilon=\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon}
 (X_\varepsilon-m_{\varepsilon,\Theta}).
\]
For fixed $h$ define
\[
 L_\varepsilon(h)=
 \exp\left\{
 h\cdot Z_\varepsilon
 -\mu_\varepsilon\left[
 Q_\varepsilon(\Theta+h/\sqrt{\mu_\varepsilon})
 -Q_\varepsilon(\Theta)
 -\mu_\varepsilon^{-1/2}DQ_\varepsilon(\Theta)h
 \right]\right\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-centred local likelihood]
\label{thm:r7-d1-likelihood}
The variable $L_\varepsilon(h)$ is exactly the Radon--Nikodym density of the
local tilted law and has expectation one.  If the finite pressure Hessians
converge, then
\[
 \log L_\varepsilon(h)
 =h\cdot Z_\varepsilon-rac12h^T\Sigma_\Theta h+o_P(1).
\]
Process versions follow whenever the platform-specific B3 or A4 tightness
theorem is available.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the definition of $Z_\varepsilon$; the finite mean terms cancel
algebraically, leaving the exact exponential-family likelihood.  Taylor's
theorem inside the fixed zero-free chart gives the quadratic expansion.  No
entire pressure is needed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dynamic contractions}

If a proved good path action is additive and has compact sublevels, define
\[
 V_tF(x)=\sup_{\gamma_0=x}
 \{F(\gamma_t)-I_{[0,t]}(\gamma)\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Rate--pressure--semigroup commutation]
\label{thm:r7-d1-dynamic}
For each platform satisfying the preceding model interfaces, contraction,
conditioning, local likelihood tangents, and the Lax--Oleinik semigroup commute
on their common finite source charts.  Phase coexistence is represented by a
minimum of phase actions and a maximum of phase pressures; the dynamic value
selects the optimal phase path rather than assuming global differentiability.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Additivity and compactness give dynamic programming.  The finite projected
pressure/rate identities commute with endpoint contractions by
Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-d1-commutation}.  Projective limits are justified by
Lemma~\ref{lem:r7-d1-topology}.  Local Gaussian tangents are
Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-d1-likelihood}.  Taking minima or maxima over the finitely
active phase charts preserves the stated identities.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-seven D1 closure]
\label{thm:r7-d1-main}
The projective theorem uses local zero-free complex atlases, bounded-support
face stratification, phase-wise recovery at coexistence, an explicit topology
upgrade, genuine thin-shell conditioning, complete finite-dimensional duality
hypotheses, and exact finite-centred local likelihoods.  It requires neither an
entire complex log-mgf nor a gradient diverging at infinity and provides a
positive standalone stratified projective commutation theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Lemmas~\ref{lem:r7-d1-atlas} and
\ref{lem:r7-d1-topology} with
Theorems~\ref{thm:r7-d1-finite},
\ref{thm:r7-d1-projective},
\ref{thm:r7-d1-conditioning},
\ref{thm:r7-d1-commutation},
\ref{thm:r7-d1-likelihood}, and
\ref{thm:r7-d1-dynamic}.
\end{proof}
