\subsection{Defect-completed inducing and direct collision pressure}

The induced return length has only an exponential tail.  It is therefore not
legitimate to discard a single excursion of order \(N\) at superexponential
cost.  We retain that sector through a direct collision-time pressure.  The
induced ratio formula is recovered on phases which visit the magnet with
positive frequency, while zero-frequency phases are represented by the escape
branch of the direct pressure.

Let \(\Omega_R^c\) be the compact graph-completed two-sided collision path
space and let \(\Theta\) be the shift.  For a bounded continuous cylinder
potential \(F\), put
\[
 Q_R^c(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \log\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_R^c}
 \exp\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}F\circ\Theta^j\right\},
\]
whenever the limit exists.  If \(\widehat F\) is the sum of \(F\) over one
complete return block, define the induced renewal operator
\[
 \mathscr R_{R,F}(z)g
 =\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 e^{\widehat\phi_R(a)+\widehat F(a)-z r(a)}
 g\circ h_a .
\]
For \(z>\|F\|_\infty-\eta/2\) this series is normally convergent on the
weighted variation space.  Let \(q_{Y,R}(F)\) be the largest real value for
which its spectral radius is one.  Let
\[
 q_{\infty,R}(F)
 =\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \log\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_R^c}
 \left[
 e^{\sum_{j<n}F\circ\Theta^j};\
 T_Y>n
 \right]
\]
be the escape pressure, where \(T_Y\) is the first hit of the magnet.  The
limit is obtained from the same homogeneous growth operator restricted to
branches avoiding \(Y\).

\begin{theorem}[Defect-completed renewal pressure]
\label{thm:r3-a3-block}
For every bounded dynamically H\"older collision cylinder \(F\), the direct
pressure exists and
\[
 \boxed{Q_R^c(F)=\max\{q_{Y,R}(F),q_{\infty,R}(F)\}.}
\]
Both branches are locally Lipschitz in \(F\).  Whenever the maximum is strict,
the direct pressure is Fr\'echet differentiable and its derivative is the
unique equilibrium collision-path law.  If the induced branch wins, that law
has positive magnet frequency and is the Palm lift of the corresponding
induced Gibbs law.  If the escape branch wins, it is supported on paths of
zero magnet frequency.  Equality of the two branches gives precisely the
recession-phase coexistence omitted by an ordinary empirical block measure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Decompose every collision word into its initial no-return segment, complete
return blocks, and terminal no-return segment.  Taking generating functions in
the collision length gives the renewal identity
\[
 \mathscr G_F(z)=
 \mathscr E_F^{\rm in}(z)
 (I-\mathscr R_{R,F}(z))^{-1}
 \mathscr E_F^{\rm out}(z)+\mathscr G_{F,\infty}(z).
\]
The exponential return tail and boundedness of \(F\) make all four operator
series analytic for large \(\Re z\).  The first term loses analyticity when
the leading eigenvalue of \(\mathscr R_{R,F}(z)\) reaches one; the second loses
analyticity at the escape abscissa.  Positivity and aperiodicity exclude any
earlier cancellation.  The abscissa of convergence of the scalar partition
function is therefore the maximum of the two critical values, proving the
formula.

On the induced branch, the weighted Doeblin--Fortet estimate and finite
connectors give a simple eigenvalue.  On the escape branch, finite
homogeneity/depth truncations give a finite positive matrix, and the limits of
its Perron eigenvalues are monotone under refinement.  A strict winning branch
therefore has a simple normalized eigenvector and analytic perturbation.
Differentiating its eigenvalue gives the equilibrium path law.  Kac's formula
identifies the induced derivative with the Palm lift; the escape derivative
has zero mass on \(Y\).  At equality the subdifferential is the convex hull of
the two phase sets.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dense exposed phases and the full collision-path LDP}

Let \(\mathscr D_R\) be the algebra generated by dynamically H\"older
collision cylinders and by finite-depth singularity-shield functions.  It is
uniformly dense in \(C(\Omega_R^c)\): first approximate a continuous function
by a finite cylinder, then use the graph completion to approximate that
cylinder from the regular components on both one-sided singular limits.

\begin{lemma}[Rate-dense exposed phases]
\label{lem:r3-a3-exposed}
For every invariant collision-path law \(\nu\) of finite pressure-dual cost,
there are potentials \(F_m\in\mathscr D_R\) with unique equilibrium laws
\(\nu_m=DQ_R^c(F_m)\) such that
\[
 \nu_m\Longrightarrow\nu,
 \qquad
 \sup_F\{\nu_m(F)-Q_R^c(F)+Q_R^c(0)\}
 \longrightarrow
 \sup_F\{\nu(F)-Q_R^c(F)+Q_R^c(0)\}.
\]
The approximation includes zero-magnet-frequency laws.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(\nu(Y)>0\), induce \(\nu\) on \(Y\), cut simultaneously at return length
\(L\) and homogeneity index \(K\), and reconnect discarded entrance--exit
pairs with the finite connector family.  This gives a mixing finite-state
Markov phase.  Summable distortion and finite mean return imply convergence
of its free-energy cost after collision-time normalization.  A small generic
finite-cylinder perturbation makes its equilibrium law unique.

If \(\nu(Y)=0\), approximate \(\nu\) by periodic orbit measures in the
finite-depth survivor sets.  The graph-completed survivor sets have the
specification property after adjoining one bounded connector outside a
vanishing fraction of the word.  A pinning cylinder potential selects each
periodic approximation uniquely.  Monotone convergence of the survivor
pressures gives convergence of the escape free-energy cost.  Convex
concatenation with lengths tending to infinity treats a nonergodic \(\nu\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full collision empirical-path LDP]
\label{thm:r3-a3-collision-ldp}
The collision empirical path measures
\[
 L_n^c=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
 \delta_{\Theta^j\omega}
\]
satisfy a full LDP on \(\mathcal P(\Omega_R^c)\), with speed \(n\) and good
rate
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal I_R^c(\nu)=
 \sup_{F\in C(\Omega_R^c)}
 \{\nu(F)-Q_R^c(F)+Q_R^c(0)\}.}
\]
The rate is infinite off invariant probabilities.  If \(\nu(Y)>0\) and
\(\widehat\nu\) is its induced law, then
\[
 \mathcal I_R^c(\nu)
 =\frac{\widehat{\mathcal I}_R(\widehat\nu)}
       {\widehat\nu(r)}.
\]
If \(\nu(Y)=0\), its cost is the escape-pressure dual and no unrecorded
macroscopic return block is discarded.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The compactness of \(\Omega_R^c\) gives exponential tightness automatically.
For any finite family of continuous cylinders, exponential Chebyshev and the
direct pressure give the projective upper bound.  Compactness upgrades it to
the full closed-set upper bound.  At a differentiability point
\(F\in\mathscr D_R\), change measure by the normalized direct transfer
operator.  Its law of large numbers gives a neighborhood lower bound at the
unique equilibrium law with cost
\(\nu_F(F)-Q_R^c(F)+Q_R^c(0)\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-a3-exposed} extends the
lower bound to every finite-rate invariant law.  The induced ratio is the
Abramov--Kac pressure identity on positive-frequency phases.  The escape
branch in Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a3-block} gives the zero-frequency assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Singularity exclusion and continuous graph factors}

Let \(\mathcal S_q\) be the finite union of singularity faces which can enter
a collision window of radius \(q\).  For a bounded continuous approximation
\(b_{q,\delta}\) of its \(\delta\)-neighborhood, homogeneous distortion gives
\[
 Q_R^c(sb_{q,\delta})-Q_R^c(0)
 \le C(e^s-1)e^{\kappa q}\delta^\alpha.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Infinite cost of singular-frequency mass]
\label{lem:r3-a3-frequency}
For every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 \lim_{q\to\infty}
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0\atop e^{\kappa q}\delta^\alpha\downarrow0}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \log\mathbb P_R^c\left(
 L_n^c(N_\delta(\mathcal S_q))>\epsilon
 \right)=-\infty.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Chernoff's bound is
\[
 -s\epsilon+C(e^s-1)e^{\kappa q}\delta^\alpha.
\]
Choose
\(s=\frac12\log((e^{\kappa q}\delta^\alpha)^{-1})\) and take the ordered
limit.  Unlike the old return-length truncation, the bad observable is bounded,
so its source may tend to infinity without invoking an unbounded mark moment.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exponentially good graph-completed path coordinates]
\label{thm:r3-a3-factor}
Every bounded continuous collision or physical path cylinder has continuous
regular-component approximants whose discrepancy has arbitrarily large
collision-time exponential cost.  In particular the one-sided values at a
grazing or multiple-collision face are retained by the graph completion rather
than identified or deleted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed window, remove a \(\delta\)-neighborhood of \(\mathcal S_q\).
On the remaining finitely cut regular components, collision expansion and
physical suspension are uniformly continuous.  Extend each component value
to the graph completion and use a partition of unity.  Any discrepancy larger
than a fixed tolerance requires a positive empirical frequency in the removed
neighborhood, whose cost tends to infinity by
Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-a3-frequency}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Bounded-roof passage to physical time}

Finite horizon gives
\[
 0<\tau_-\le\tau_R\le\tau_+<\infty.
\]
For an invariant collision-path law \(\nu\), define its physical suspension
Palm law by
\[
 \int F\,d\mathscr S_p\nu
 =\frac1{\nu(\tau_R)}
 \int\int_0^{\tau_R(\omega)}
 F(\Theta_t\Gamma_R^p\omega)\,dt\,d\nu(\omega).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Bounded terminal clock surgery]
\label{lem:r3-a3-surgery}
For a deterministic physical horizon \(T\), the number of complete collision
flights differs from \(T/\nu(\tau_R)\) by the empirical clock fluctuation, and
the two terminal residual pieces have total duration at most \(2\tau_+\).
After division by \(T\), the residual contribution is uniformly \(o(1)\) and
has no separate exponential cost.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Stop at the last complete free flight before \(T\).  There is one initial and
one terminal partial flight, each bounded by \(\tau_+\).  The remaining clock
is the sum of the bounded roof observable, so its empirical mean is already a
continuous coordinate in the collision-path LDP.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full collision- and physical-time empirical-path LDP]
\label{thm:r3-a3-two-clock}
The collision LDP is Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a3-collision-ldp}.  The physical
empirical path measures satisfy a full LDP with speed \(T\) and good rate
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal I_R^p(\rho)=
 \inf_{\mathscr S_p\nu=\rho}
 \frac{\mathcal I_R^c(\nu)}{\nu(\tau_R)}.}
\]
Both rates are independent of the chosen finite-connector inducing magnet.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Jointly contract the collision empirical-path LDP with the bounded continuous
roof average and with the graph-completed suspension coordinates of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r3-a3-factor}.  On a clock-mean slice
\(\nu(\tau_R)=a\), collision speed \(n\) and physical speed \(T\) satisfy
\(n/T\to1/a\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:r3-a3-surgery} removes the bounded residuals.
The extended contraction upper and lower bounds give the displayed rate.
Because \(\tau_R\) is bounded away from zero and infinity, compact collision
rate sublevels have compact physical images, so the rate is good.  The direct
pressure and its dual are microscopic collision-path objects; changing the
inducing magnet changes only the renewal representation, proving
presentation independence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Presentation independence]
\label{prop:r3-a3-independent}
Two admissible inducing magnets yield the same direct pressures
\(Q_R^c\), collision rate \(\mathcal I_R^c\), and physical rate
\(\mathcal I_R^p\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both renewal decompositions equal the logarithmic moment limit of the same
collision empirical path law.  Equality of direct pressures on the dense
cylinder algebra, continuity, and convex duality give equality of the rates.
\end{proof}
