\begin{lemma}[Span-one particle-number gap]
\label{lem:r3-b1-span-one}
Uniformly on every compact real source--multiplier set, the tilted hard-core
grand partition function has positive weights at two consecutive particle
numbers in each mesoscopic safe region.  Consequently the particle-number
lattice has span one and, for every \(\delta\in(0,\pi)\),
\[
 \sup_{\delta\le |t|\le\pi}
 \left|
 \mathbb E_{H,\lambda,\varepsilon}
 e^{it(N-\mathbb EN)}
 \right|
 \le e^{-c_\delta\mu_\varepsilon}.
\]
The estimate remains valid jointly with continuous constraint frequencies in
a fixed central neighborhood.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Partition the torus into mesoscopic cubes of side \(L\varepsilon\).  Since
\(N\varepsilon^3=O(\varepsilon)\), outside an event of
\(e^{-c\mu_\varepsilon}\) probability a fixed fraction of the cubes has an
insertion subcube separated by more than \(\varepsilon\) from all existing
centers.  Conditional on the exterior configuration, the local grand
partition in such a cube has a zero-particle term one and a one-particle term
\(z_B\) satisfying \(0<c\le z_B\le C\) on the compact source set.  Its
normalized number characteristic factor therefore obeys
\[
 \frac{|1+z_Be^{it}|}{1+z_B}
 \le1-c\sin^2(t/2).
\]
Select \(c\mu_\varepsilon\) disjoint safe cubes.  The hard-core Mayer
interaction between their insertion subcubes is zero; interactions with the
exterior are already conditioned.  Multiplication of the factors gives the
stated exponential gap.  A central continuous frequency changes each
one-particle weight by \(1+O(|u|)\), so the same bound holds after reducing
\(c_\delta\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Density of local balanced variations]
\label{lem:r3-b3-local-density}
Let \((f,\Gamma)\) be a smooth strictly positive regular pair.  The closed
linear span of tangent variations generated by compactly supported changes of
one free-flight density and one non-grazing binary collision is the full
weighted tangent space satisfying the linearized balance and the five
collision-invariant constraints.  Its annihilator in the declared dual pair
is exactly
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{Ran}\mathfrak G},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak G r=(r,-\Delta r).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A compact density variation away from collision times is generated by changing
the incoming one-particle activity on a short free-flight tube.  A compact
collision-current variation on a non-grazing box is generated by changing the
actual-contact source on that box.  Their balance defects are paired by the
transport endpoint and \(\Delta r\).  A partition of unity in space--time and
the finite collision-box right inverse of Paper B2 approximate every smooth
balanced variation; weighted truncation and Orlicz uniform integrability give
the closure.

If a dual pair \((p,\psi)\) annihilates all local collision variations, then
\(\Delta p+\psi=0\) on every non-grazing box and hence almost everywhere by
weighted density.  It is therefore \((p,\psi)=\mathfrak Gp\).  Conversely
every gauge pair annihilates all balanced variations by the linearized balance
identity.  Closedness of the graph of \(r\mapsto-\Delta r\) completes the
annihilator statement.
\end{proof}
