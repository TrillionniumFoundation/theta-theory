\subsection{The weighted bounded-strict path-potential topology}

Let \(\Omega\) be a Polish path space and let \(W\ge1\) be continuous with
compact sublevels.  Put
\[
 C_W(\Omega)=\{F\in C(\Omega):\|F/W\|_\infty<\infty\}.
\]
The weighted bounded-strict topology \(\beta_W\) is the mixed topology whose
convergent nets are exactly the sup-\(W\)-bounded nets converging uniformly on
every compact set after division by \(W\).  Equivalently, it is generated on
sup-\(W\)-bounded sets by the seminorms
\[
 F\longmapsto\sup_{x\in K}|F(x)|/W(x),
 \qquad K\Subset\Omega.
\]
Let \(\mathcal M_W(\Omega)\) be the countably additive signed Radon measures
with \(\int Wd|\mu|<\infty\).

\begin{theorem}[Weighted strict dual and compact phase sets]
\label{thm:r3-c2-strict}
The continuous dual of \((C_W(\Omega),\beta_W)\) is
\(\mathcal M_W(\Omega)\) under integration.  If \(I\) is a good weighted path
rate and
\[
 Q(F)=\sup_\nu\{\nu(F)-I(\nu)\},
\]
then \(Q\) is convex and \(\beta_W\)-continuous on every bounded source set,
and
\[
 \partial_{\beta_W}Q(F)
 =\operatorname*{argmax}_\nu\{\nu(F)-I(\nu)\}.
\]
The subdifferential is a compact set of countably additive path
probabilities; no finitely additive dual element occurs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiplication by \(W^{-1}\) identifies sup-\(W\)-bounded subsets of
\(C_W\) with sup-norm bounded subsets of \(C_b(\Omega)\).  A
\(\beta_W\)-continuous functional restricts on every compact sublevel
\(\{W\le n\}\) to a sup-norm continuous functional and hence to a Radon
measure.  Compatibility of these restrictions and bounded-set continuity give
a countably additive global measure with finite \(W\)-moment.  Conversely,
weighted tightness and dominated convergence make integration against every
member of \(\mathcal M_W\) continuous for \(\beta_W\).

Goodness and the weight coercivity restrict the variational supremum to a
compact rate sublevel.  Every maximizer satisfies the subgradient inequality.
Conversely a strict-continuous subgradient is a Radon measure by the first
part; testing constants and nonnegative compactly supported functions makes it
a probability, and Fenchel equality makes it a maximizer.  Compactness follows
from the compact rate sublevel.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Linearized history pressure and compressed memory}

Fix one exposed prepared phase \(\nu_\Psi\).  Let
\(P_t^{\Psi,\rm D}\) be its normalized Doob transition semigroup on histories
and let \(L_\Psi^{\rm D}\) be the corresponding generator on
\(L^2(\nu_\Psi)\).  For the normalized nonlinear history semigroup
\[
 \mathcal E_t^\Psi F
 =\log P_t^\Psi(e^F)-\log P_t^\Psi\mathbf1,
\]
its first derivative at zero is the linear normalized transition:
\[
 D\mathcal E_t^\Psi(0)A=P_t^{\Psi,\rm D}A.
\]
Let \(P\) be the finite resolved orthogonal projection.

\begin{theorem}[Memory is the Laplace transform of the pressure tangent]
\label{thm:r3-c2-memory}
For resolved \(A,B\in PL^2(\nu_\Psi)\) and \(\Re z>0\),
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-zt}
 \left\langle B,D\mathcal E_t^\Psi(0)A\right\rangle_{L^2(\nu_\Psi)}dt
 =\left\langle
 B,P(z-L_\Psi^{\rm D})^{-1}PA
 \right\rangle.
\]
Consequently the compressed memory transform is
\[
 \widehat K_\Psi(z)
 =zP-PL_\Psi^{\rm D}P-
 [P(z-L_\Psi^{\rm D})^{-1}P]^{-1}.
\]
Linearizing the nonlinear history pressure and then forming the compressed
memory gives the same operator as forming A4's compressed-resolvent identity
under the prepared phase.  A further source derivative on either route is the
same Duhamel insertion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the normalized Feynman--Kac formula at \(F=0\).  Division by
\(P_t^\Psi\mathbf1\) is exactly the Doob normalization, so the derivative is
\(P_t^{\Psi,\rm D}A\).  Pair with \(B\) and integrate its semigroup in time to
obtain the compressed resolvent.  A4 defines memory by the displayed Schur
complement, proving the first commutation statement.  Differentiating the
resolvent identity gives
\[
 D(z-L_\Psi^{\rm D})^{-1}
 =(z-L_\Psi^{\rm D})^{-1}
 (DL_\Psi^{\rm D})
 (z-L_\Psi^{\rm D})^{-1},
\]
which is the Laplace transform of the same Duhamel insertion obtained by
source-differentiating the history semigroup.
\end{proof}
