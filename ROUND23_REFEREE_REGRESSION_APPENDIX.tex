\section{Referee counterexamples as permanent mathematical regression tests}
\label{sec:r23-regression-appendix}

This appendix records the eight decisive Round-Twenty-Two counterexamples and
the exact object that replaces each contradicted construction.  The purpose is
not rhetorical.  Each calculation is short enough to be rechecked before any
future revision is accepted into the proof dependency graph.

\subsection{Regular points do not create resolvent residues}

Let $R(z)=(z-L)^{-1}$ be holomorphic in a neighbourhood of $z_0$.  For a
sufficiently small positively oriented circle $\Gamma$ around $z_0$, Cauchy's
theorem gives
\[
 {1\over2\pi i}\int_\Gamma R(z)\,dz=0.
\]
The same holds after left or right composition with a bounded projection.
Consequently a zero of a compressed transfer map at a point where the full
resolvent is regular cannot be promoted to a nonzero Riesz vector.  A genuine
Riesz projection is defined only at an isolated pole of the full resolvent.
Round Twenty Three therefore uses the Schur/Feshbach compression and records
transmission zeros as zeros.

\subsection{The generic Feshbach memory has a nonzero inverse-frequency term}

Let $P$ be a bounded projection, $Q=I-P$, and suppose the displayed products
are defined between graph spaces.  For large $z$ in a common sector,
\[
 (z-QLQ)^{-1}=z^{-1}I+z^{-2}QLQ+O(|z|^{-3}).
\]
Hence
\[
 PLQ(z-QLQ)^{-1}QLP
 =z^{-1}PLQLP+z^{-2}PLQ(QLQ)QLP+O(|z|^{-3}).
\]
Already the scalar block matrix
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
has memory $bc/(z-d)=bc/z+bcd/z^2+O(z^{-3})$.  Unless $bc=0$, the leading
coefficient is present.  Round Twenty Three proves memory decay from the
orthogonal semigroup $e^{tQLQ}$ and never from a fictitious generic
$O(z^{-2})$ cancellation.

\subsection{The raw canonical factorial is not a normalized pressure}

At fixed volume, even before hard-core exclusion, the quantity
\[
 p_N^{\rm raw}(0)={1\over N}\log {V^N\over N!}
\]
satisfies, by Stirling's formula,
\[
 p_N^{\rm raw}(0)=\log V-\log N+1+o(1),
\]
and therefore tends to $-\infty$.  It cannot be the finite pressure used for
a local saddle theorem.  If instead
\[
 \nu_{N,\lambda_0}(dX)=
 {1\over Z_N(\lambda_0)}{1\over N!}
 1_{\Omega_N}(X)e^{\lambda_0\cdot C_N(X)}dX,
\]
then the normalized log moment generating function
\[
 q_N(t,H)={1\over N}\log
 \mathbb E_{\nu_{N,\lambda_0}}
 e^{t\cdot(C_N-Nc_0)+NH_N}
\]
satisfies $q_N(0,0)=0$ exactly.  Particle-number extraction is performed from
a normalized activity ensemble, so the factorial and volume normalization
are incorporated before the saddle.

\subsection{The collision Hessian is normal to the zero-cost manifold}

Let
\[
 \mathscr I(f,\Gamma)=\int
 \ell\!\left({d\Gamma\over dA_f}\right)dA_f,
 \qquad \ell(q)=q\log q-q+1.
\]
At a balanced point $\Gamma=A_f$, take
$f_\epsilon=f+\epsilon u+o(\epsilon)$ and
$\Gamma_\epsilon=A_f+\epsilon\delta\Gamma+o(\epsilon)$.  Since
\[
 A_{f_\epsilon}=A_f+\epsilon DA_f[u]+o(\epsilon),
 \qquad
 h=\delta\Gamma-DA_f[u],
\]
one has
\[
 {d\Gamma_\epsilon\over dA_{f_\epsilon}}
 =1+\epsilon{h\over A_f}+o(\epsilon),
 \qquad
 \mathscr I(f_\epsilon,\Gamma_\epsilon)
 ={\epsilon^2\over2}\int{h^2\over A_f}+o(\epsilon^2).
\]
Thus every tangent to $\Gamma=A_f$ has $h=0$ and zero dynamic collision cost.
Any proposed Hessian that is positive at
$\delta\Gamma=DA_f[u]$ is incompatible with the exact zero-cost manifold.

\subsection{An uncountable deterministic family cannot have one positive-density dominator}

Suppose a probability $\lambda$ dominates every
$\delta_{\Phi(x)}$ and each density is positive at its supporting point.  Then
$\lambda\{\Phi(x)\}>0$ for every distinct image point.  A finite measure has
at most countably many positive-mass atoms: for each $n$, there are at most
$n$ atoms of mass at least $1/n$, and every positive atom belongs to one such
class after changing $n$.  Hence an uncountable injective deterministic image
contradicts the assumed domination.  Round Twenty Three leaves the hidden
Dirac transition undominated and dominates only the actual observation
strata.

\subsection{Empirical transitions do not encode path order}

Let $A$ and $B$ be two legal excursion marks with the same duration.  The
ordered words $AB$ and $BA$ have the same empirical mark measure
$\frac12(\delta_A+\delta_B)$, the same total clock, and the same unordered edge
multiset.  If their physical segments do not commute under concatenation, the
resulting paths are different.  Therefore no measurable reconstruction of the
ordered path from only those empirical contractions can be correct on both
words.  Round Twenty Three stores the stopped word and its concatenated path
as state coordinates; empirical flows are downstream contractions.

\subsection{Atomic representatives have infinite relative entropy against a non-atomic kernel}

Let $K(x,\cdot)$ be non-atomic and let $m$ be a point.  Since
$K(x,\{m\})=0$ but $\delta_m(\{m\})=1$, one has
$\delta_m\not\ll K(x,\cdot)$ and therefore
\[
 H(\delta_m\mid K(x,\cdot))=+\infty.
\]
A finite-cell recovery must instead use a density.  For a cell $C$ with
$K(x,C)>0$, the restricted conditional reference
\[
 q_C(x,dm)={1_C(m)\over K(x,C)}K(x,dm)
\]
is legal whenever $K$ is legal, is absolutely continuous, and has entropy
$-\log K(x,C)$.  The manuscript uses conditional cell averages of the target
density, which recover the full entropy by martingale convergence.

\subsection{A second moment does not compactify quadratic-growth tests}

Let $\varphi$ be a centred probability density with finite moments and put
\[
 f_n=(1-n^{-2})\varphi+n^{-2}\varphi(\,\cdot-ne_1).
\]
Then $f_n\rightharpoonup\varphi$ and the second moments remain bounded, but the
translated component carries an order-one amount of kinetic energy to
infinity.  In particular the family is not uniformly integrable for
$|v|^2$, so it is not compact in a topology testing all continuous functions
of quadratic growth.  For every $\delta>0$,
\[
 \int |v|^{2+\delta}f_n(dv)\asymp n^\delta\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Round Twenty Three places a superquadratic de la Vall\'ee--Poussin moment in
the state and action sublevels, excluding this sequence before any semigroup
compactness argument.
