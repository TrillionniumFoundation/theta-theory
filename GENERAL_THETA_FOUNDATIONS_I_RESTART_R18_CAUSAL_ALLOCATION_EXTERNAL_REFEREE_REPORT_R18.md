## 2. Executive assessment of revision R18

R18 changes the mathematical status of the paper. R15 gave a strong joint horizon--memory law on a regular block-stable class, but its main theorem remained a sufficient theorem. R18 now asks the more intrinsic question:

> Given the actual predictive acquisition laws generated by a finite observer, which finite retained laws can be realized causally, and when can one stationary finite programme realize all phases simultaneously?

The answer is exact within the declared compact predictive category.

For the external interface, the paper proves the chain

```text
actual retained law and action distribution
        -> actual pre-pooling acquisition law
        -> finite barycentric contraction
        -> measurable report-to-label kernel
        -> actual next retained law.
```

Convex order is necessary because conditional expectations contract convex functions. It is sufficient because finite-target weak-star separation produces a measurable stochastic allocation whose label masses and barycenters are exactly prescribed. The construction is calibrated against the full incoming mixture under the deployed observer; it does not update an uncharged exact posterior on another controlled path.

For the autonomous interface, phasewise convex order is not enough. A label reused at different cuts must invoke the same action kernel and the same report-to-label kernel. The manuscript sums the phase measures into one dominating report measure for each label/action pair and eliminates the unknown common kernel through a simultaneous support-function inequality. This is the central new theorem. Its mathematical content is the placement of

```text
max over next labels
```

outside the phase sum. Placing it inside the sum would authorize a different hidden programme at each phase and would solve a different, externally clocked problem.

The exact risk formulas then follow by optimizing over feasible flows. The deployed-law Bellman telescope identifies the excess risk as the sum of nonnegative action slacks and nonnegative Jensen defects. Thus exact zero-loss completion is neither identified with zero formal dimension nor forced by a contraction hypothesis: it is the existence of a feasible flow with zero local defects.

The manuscript adds two useful consequences. First, compactness gives an optimal external-width flow and a sequence of finite test relaxations whose values increase to the exact optimum. Any strictly smaller target is ruled out by finitely many tests. Second, the blind-transport experiment shows that even a free external clock does not remove all intermediate memory costs: once a captured variable is transported without further information, the smallest intermediate alphabet determines the final quantization error.

This is a significant advance. It directly addresses the strongest conceptual criticism in the R15 report: the absence of an intrinsic or near-intrinsic completion criterion. The qualification is that the criterion is intrinsic only relative to the chosen compact affine predictive realization and machine interface. It is an exact variational characterization, not a simple numerical invariant, an effective general algorithm, or a theorem covering arbitrary standard-Borel predictive quotients.

## 4. Statement and conceptual status of the main theorem

At phase `t`, the predictive carrier `K_t` is compact, metrizable and convex. Continuous affine future-test expectations separate its points. For each legal action, the raw experiment induces a report law and normalized predictive update

```text
J_t(q,a),        F_t(q,a,y),
```

and hence an actual post-report law

```text
Lambda_t(q,a)=Law(F_t(q,a,Y)),   Y~J_t(q,a).
```

The unnormalized instrument is affine under physical mixtures. The terminal conditional loss is affine in the predictive state for every decision, and its Bayes envelope is concave.

For externally clocked widths `m_t`, an allocation flow consists of finite retained laws `nu_t` and action probabilities such that the next actual acquisition mixture is

```text
lambda_{t+1} = integral over retained states and actions of Lambda_t(q,a),
```

and the next retained law satisfies

```text
nu_{t+1} <=_cx lambda_{t+1}.
```

The paper proves that this is necessary and sufficient for causal realization with the declared widths. It also proves the equivalent finite-target affine support-function inequalities.

For an autonomous `M`-state tuple, one proposes phase occupancies `p_{t,z}`, phase-conditional predictive states `q_{t,z}`, one action distribution `alpha_z`, and one terminal subset. For every reused label/action pair, the phase report measures are dominated by their sum. The proposed moments are realizable by one phase-independent next-label kernel if and only if the simultaneous affine inequality holds for every finite family of tests. The kernel itself does not appear among the variational variables.

The theorem then gives

```text
R_ext = B_n^* + Phi_ext,
R_aut = B_n^* + Phi_aut.
```

The excess of any calibrated observer with optimal terminal readout is exactly

```text
sum over phases (action slack + pooling Jensen defect).
```

This is an exact theorem, but its conceptual status should be stated with precision:

- it is a characterization on a compact affine predictive category obtained from a fixed preparation and executable-test interface;
- it is not a claim that every standard-Borel predictive equivalence relation has such a quotient;
- it is not a closed-form scalar rate formula;
- it is not generally computable;
- it permits conditional predictive states of a reused label to vary with phase as law parameters, while the executed programme cannot read those states or the phase;
- it characterizes the stated finite-state machine interfaces, not all possible computational models with writable tapes, unbounded programmes or external clocks; and
- its quantitative consequences require additional actual-law and task hypotheses.

The manuscript mostly observes these distinctions. They should remain visible wherever the word “intrinsic” or “exact” is used.

## 5. Audit of the principal arguments

### 5.1 Predictive quotient and raw mixture structure

Appendix A does not quotient an arbitrary measurable equivalence relation. It starts with compact raw state spaces and a separable closed space of continuous executable-test expectations closed under legal unnormalized report pullbacks. A countable dense family maps preparation measures into a compact convex subset of a countable product.

The proof uses equality of unnormalized report/test measures to show representative independence of the report law and outgoing predictive coordinates. A standard-Borel conditional-probability construction supplies jointly measurable versions, and an explicit nested compact-cover argument supplies a Borel section of the compact quotient map. Mixing selected representatives gives the affine instrument identities.

I found this route coherent under the stated hypotheses. The manuscript correctly limits minimality to the fixed preparation/test/legal-interface structure and does not promote the result to simultaneous frequentist sufficiency for an inaccessible parameter family.

Two qualifications should remain prominent. Weak continuity of the acquired-law kernel is additional and is not obtained from measurability alone. Also, the operational statement that finite linear combinations of determining tests suffice for the later affine duals relies on the closed test space and compact affine realization; it would be useful to cross-reference this point explicitly at Theorem 3.1.

### 5.2 Finite barycentric allocation

Lemma 4.1 is a finite-target form of the Strassen martingale/convex-order mechanism. The simplex of stochastic allocations is weak-star compact. The mass and affine-moment map has compact convex image. If the target moments lie outside, finite-coordinate separation yields affine functions whose support function is the integral of their pointwise maximum. Conversely, convex order bounds that maximum.

The proof correctly retains random allocation. An acquired atom can be split into several labels with the same barycenter; replacing the allocation by deterministic cells would be false. The use of Borel versions on the compact standard-Borel carrier is legitimate.

This lemma is classical in substance, as the manuscript says. Its role is not novelty by itself but as the exact one-step engine in the causal composition.

### 5.3 All-incoming calibration and external causal realization

The load-bearing point is that a retained state is a conditional predictive barycenter under the actual deployed observer law. The proof mixes all incoming labels, actions and reports assigned to a next label before taking the barycenter. The affine instrument identity then identifies every determining future-test expectation at that barycenter.

This avoids a common circularity: the construction does not run an exact full-history filter, quantize it, and then claim that the quantized controller has the same acquisition law. It constructs the observer forward from its own retained law. Duplicate predictive atoms may be merged for the marginal flow after averaging their action distributions; this does not assert equality of the old labelled trajectories.

I found the necessity and sufficiency arguments for the external interface correct. The synthesized programme can be noncomputable and real-valued; only its persistent alphabet is bounded at this stage, and the manuscript states this.

### 5.4 Simultaneous allocation for one autonomous programme

This is the most original part of the manuscript and deserves the greatest scrutiny.

For each reused label `z` and action `a`, the proof forms the finite dominating report measure

```text
eta_{z,a} = sum over phases p_{t,z} alpha_z(a) J_t(q_{t,z},a).
```

The Radon--Nikodym weights record the phase contribution at a report. A candidate stationary next-label kernel is a simplex-valued function of `(z,a,y)`. Its image under all phase mass and affine-moment maps is compact and convex in a product space. Finite-coordinate separation yields exactly the displayed simultaneous inequality. The support function is

```text
integral max_j { sum_t h_t(y) phi_{t,j}(F_t(...,y)) } d eta.
```

The same maximizing label must serve all phase terms at that report. This is the correct dual for one common kernel. The proof then uses the resulting kernel and one common action distribution to reproduce the proposed occupancies by induction. The conditional states `q_{t,z}` are certificate variables and are not runtime registers.

I did not find a flaw in this separation argument. Zero-measure label/action pairs and null reports are harmless because they can be completed arbitrarily. Constants among the affine tests enforce occupancy masses as well as barycenters.

For exposition, I strongly recommend adding a finite matrix formulation immediately after the theorem. In a finite report experiment, the simultaneous condition is a finite linear feasibility problem over one stochastic matrix per label/action. Displaying that form would make clear both the theorem's force and its relation to bounded finite-state-controller synthesis.

### 5.5 Exact risk and Bellman--Jensen decomposition

The baseline `B_n^*` is the full-history optimum for the same preparation, horizon, legal actions and terminal task. It may vary with `n`. At each cut, using the observer's actual conditional predictive state, the difference between the chosen action's continuation value and the Bellman minimum is nonnegative. Pooling replaces the pre-pooling predictive state by its label-conditional barycenter; concavity makes the Jensen defect nonnegative. Telescoping gives the exact excess.

This proof remains valid at nonsmooth action switches because it compares the actual chosen action distribution with the Bellman minimum rather than differentiating the value. It also avoids comparing separately optimized shadow controllers.

The zero/epsilon completion corollary follows correctly from nonnegativity and realizability. It distinguishes an attained zero from an infimum of zero. This is conceptually important: an affine or piecewise-affine Bayes risk may have zero Jensen loss on a nontrivial cell even when the acquired law is high-dimensional.

### 5.6 Compact attainment and global finite obstruction

For fixed external widths, the candidate laws and action assignments can be represented by measures on `K_t x A_t` whose first marginals have bounded atomic support. This set is compact, including sequences in which atoms collide. Weak continuity of the acquisition kernel makes the law recursion continuous; convex-order inequalities are closed; terminal risk is continuous.

The countable family of finite maxima of finite-coordinate affine functions is used to exhaust continuous convex tests. Nested finite-test feasible sets are compact and nonempty. Their minima increase to the full optimum. Therefore any target strictly below the optimum is excluded by finitely many tests.

I find the compactness argument sound. The result is useful, but its terminology needs care. It gives a finite family of inequalities excluding all candidates below a strict target. It does not give one universal affine witness, an effective bound on the number of tests, or an algorithm for finding the obstruction. The manuscript states these limitations; the abstract phrase “every infeasible proposed allocation has a finite affine dual witness” should not be read as a uniform effective certificate for the whole optimization problem.

The finite polyhedral convex tests are mathematical functions of the predictive carrier. When the paper calls them “future-test inequalities,” it should make explicit whether each affine component is represented by an executable test or by a uniform limit of the determining test span. This appears available from Appendix A but should be tied into the theorem statement more directly.

### 5.7 Timing conventions and the clock converse

The direct conversions are elementary and correct: an externally clocked observer can run an autonomous one, while an autonomous implementation of an external profile can attach the phase to each label.

Under action-wise common report null sets, the reverse argument shows that a positive-probability label cannot occur at two different cuts of an internally exact-deadline machine. Starting from that label, the same stationary programme has equivalent actual and reference trace null sets; a stopping event of probability one after one remaining length would force premature stopping at the earlier occurrence. Thus cut supports are disjoint and the exact conversion identity follows.

This theorem is valid under its information pattern and is more precise than merely declaring phase-labelled codebooks necessary. Its significance is nevertheless interface-dependent. Most finite-horizon control formulations supply the stage index to the controller. The manuscript now clearly separates that convention from the stationary allocation theorem, which is the right editorial choice. The top-four case should not rely principally on the `M-n` clock charge.

### 5.8 Quantitative regular law

Section 6 re-proves the R15 mechanism rather than importing it as a black box. The global convex Lipschitz extension of the negative continuation value, distributional Laplacian estimate, actual density bound and sensitivity-weighted integer allocation produce the external and autonomous upper rates. The final acquired-law density and strong task curvature give the matching lower.

I found no elementary error in the boundary-valid curvature estimate or in the budget calculation. The proof keeps norm-comparison constants, incomplete block factors and the one-label baseline visible. The lower is based on actual terminal acquisition laws under every admissible policy, not on packing the formal reachable carrier.

This theorem remains a strong sufficient theorem. It assumes a uniform all-state/all-action acquired density, strong curvature in every acquired direction and a summable block sensitivity profile. R18 correctly presents these as estimates of the exact frontier rather than axioms of the general allocation criterion.

### 5.9 Gaussian and quantum realizations

For finite-state Gaussian sensing, a positive transition floor keeps the predicted coordinates away from zero before observation. Gaussian likelihood ratios give a full-rank logit chart. The logit density has Gaussian tails, while the inverse softmax Jacobian grows only exponentially, yielding a bounded posterior density up to approached simplex faces. The positive-instrument coupling gives report continuity and the Wasserstein coefficient. The Brier Bayes risk is strongly concave.

The quotient argument uses linear independence of the Gaussian emission densities and an invertible legal transition to recover the incoming predictive state. This is sufficient for the declared tests. The example includes untruncated report tails and does not condition away rare observations.

For the quantum example, the prepared state remains uniformly positive. The normalized congruence map has the displayed positive inverse, so the report-to-posterior map is one-to-one with full differential on the compact positive domain. A fixed informationally complete POVM gives an injective affine probability forecast and hence a strongly concave Brier Bayes risk.

I found these calculations plausible and internally consistent. They are finite-dimensional, known-kernel verification theorems. They do not support broader language about arbitrary quantum memory, unrestricted measurement design or infinite-dimensional instruments. The manuscript observes this boundary.

Both mechanisms substantially predate R18 in the restart pipeline and are identified as inherited/re-proved. They strengthen readability and applicability but should not be counted again as the principal novelty of the current revision.

### 5.10 Blind transport and singular acquisition

The blind experiment makes one exact paid capture of `X`, then gives no further information. At every later cut, conditional on the retained label, all future randomization is independent of `X`. Thus the terminal output is downstream of every intermediate label in the Markov sense, and squared-loss projection gives a quantization lower for each intermediate alphabet.

The external optimum is attained by quantizing once at the narrowest width and carrying the label. For the autonomous interface, exact stopping and identical blind reports make phase supports disjoint, while the initial capture label is also distinct because it must choose a different legal action. The exact floor `floor((M-1)/n)` follows and is attained by phase copies.

This proof is correct and makes an important conceptual point: summable sensitivity is not necessary for completion, and a free clock does not restore information discarded at an intermediate cut. The Cantor and accumulating-atom examples correctly use actual small-ball and atomic mass profiles rather than ambient dimension.

The class is deliberately simple. It is a sharp counterexample and exact resource law, not yet a solution of a natural noncontracting nonlinear filtering problem. That distinction matters to the journal-level assessment but not to correctness.

### 5.11 Common-task transfer, numerical execution and finite rational compilation

The common-task transfer proposition uses a parameter-independent simulator of the complete stopped and scored law. Product persistent state and bounded-loss total-variation error then give the stated upper. The manuscript correctly refuses to infer a reverse lower from a one-way simulator.

The first-mismatch numerical estimate is standard and correctly conditions mismatch probabilities on the ideal prefix rather than on the event of prior successful comparisons. Coordinate error for selecting a cell is kept separate from terminal readout error.

For finite rational experiments, after finite discrete choices of initial label, stopping subset and readouts are enumerated, the stationary programme probabilities lie in a compact product of simplexes. Exact legality and exact stopping are polynomial path constraints; expected loss is polynomial. Real-algebraic decision therefore gives decidability and an algebraic optimizer. Rounding within the original support cannot activate a forbidden path. The finite-horizon total-variation estimate and storage/sampling accounts then follow.

I find this theorem correct at the stated level. It is a decidability and feasible-compilation theorem, not an efficient synthesis result. The relation to existing finite-state-controller parameter synthesis is close and currently underdeveloped in the literature section; this is a major revision request below.

The native build runs ordinary and optimized Python modes, performs two isolated three-pass TeX builds, checks the recorder inputs, and requires within-environment byte identity. ## 6. Correctness assessment

Subject to the stated compactness, measurability, affine-instrument and machine-interface hypotheses, I found no elementary fatal error in the new load-bearing arguments.

The most delicate proof is the simultaneous autonomous allocation lemma. Its functional-analytic structure is appropriate: one weak-star compact simplex-valued kernel is mapped simultaneously to all phase moments, and the displayed dual is exactly its support function. The proof neither exchanges the maximum with the phase sum nor assumes phase-dependent programmes. The forward calibration induction then closes the construction under the observer's own law.

The next most delicate point is the passage from raw experiments to a compact predictive carrier. Appendix A is a sufficient theorem, not a universal quotient result. Within that scope, the countable continuous test closure, compact image, Borel section, disintegration and unnormalized instrument identities appear adequate.

There remain technical details that would benefit from fuller exposition, particularly the identification of all continuous affine tests with the closed executable-test span, the precise topology in the compact candidate set of Theorem 4.4, and the measurable handling of algebraic support in the finite compiler. I regard these as requests for strengthening and readability, not as presently demonstrated counterexamples.

The finding “no elementary fatal error found” is not a formal verification or proof of every assertion. It records the outcome of this referee audit of the principal arguments.

The current literature map nevertheless omits several bodies of work close enough to affect the novelty and positioning claims.

### 7.1 Conditional convex order and measurable martingale kernels

Leskelä and Vihola, *Conditional convex orders and measurable martingale couplings*, Bernoulli 23 (2017), 2784--2807, DOI `10.3150/16-BEJ827`, prove conditional versions of Strassen's theorem and measurable martingale couplings in kernel language. The present finite-target construction is self-contained and its serial/controller constraint is different, but this is a natural comparison for measurable conditional allocation. The paper should explain whether Lemma 4.1 is merely a specialized proof of that machinery and exactly where the autonomous simultaneous constraint goes beyond it.

### 7.2 Finite-state-controller synthesis

The autonomous tuple is, mathematically, a randomized finite-state controller with a stopping subset and a common observation-dependent transition programme. At minimum the revision should compare with:

- S. Junges, N. Jansen, R. Wimmer, T. Quatmann, L. Winterer, J.-P. Katoen and B. Becker, *Finite-State Controllers of POMDPs using Parameter Synthesis*, UAI 2018;
- R. Andriushchenko, M. Češka, S. Junges and J.-P. Katoen, *Inductive synthesis of finite-state controllers for POMDPs*, UAI 2022;
- C. Amato, B. Bonet and S. Zilberstein, *Finite-State Controllers Based on Mealy Machines for Centralized and Decentralized POMDPs*, AAAI 2010; and
- the broader bounded-controller and parametric-Markov-chain synthesis literature.

Junges et al. explicitly identify randomized FSC synthesis with parameter synthesis for parametric Markov chains. This is particularly close to Theorem 9.3. R18's contribution is not merely decidability of a fixed finite controller class; it gives an exact law-valued elimination of the controller transition kernel through predictive barycentric moments and a common-programme dual. That distinction should be demonstrated rather than left implicit.

The comparison should address:

- finite hidden-state POMDP occupancy equations versus compact predictive carriers;
- reward/specification synthesis versus the same prepared decision task;
- controller-node probabilities versus conditional predictive barycenters;
- finite polynomial feasibility versus the infinite affine-test dual;
- time-homogeneous Mealy/Moore controller conventions; and
- whether the finite rational compiler is new beyond the standard parametric-controller reduction or is chiefly an execution corollary tailored to exact stopping and support preservation.

### 7.3 Sequential information design

The paper cites static Bayesian persuasion and coarse information design. Its external flow is also close in spirit to sequential Bayes-plausible information design. Relevant comparisons include Gan, Majumdar, Radanovic and Singla, *Bayesian Persuasion in Sequential Decision-Making* (AAAI 2022), and the Markov/sequential persuasion literature. Those models include strategic obedience constraints and different objectives, so they do not subsume R18. The useful comparison is structural: serial posterior laws, Bayes plausibility, action-dependent state evolution and commitment to a signalling programme.

The manuscript should say whether its exact external flow is a nonstrategic finite-message dynamic information-design problem with the physical acquisition kernel fixed, and whether its autonomous simultaneous dual has an analogue in stationary signalling schemes.

### 7.4 Causal transport and controller memory

The paper already explains that causal transport enforces nonanticipation rather than a hard finite programme alphabet. This is correct. The comparison could be sharpened by identifying the extra factorization imposed by a finite retained label and by the reuse of one transition kernel. Conversely, the manuscript should avoid suggesting that finite support alone is an intrinsic computational notion without a programme-description or execution certificate.

### 7.5 Significance of the literature gap

These omissions do not show that the main theorem is already known. They do mean that the current claim of a new exact finite-state causal frontier is not yet positioned against the most directly adjacent controller-synthesis literature. For a specialist publication, this is fixable. For a top-four general journal, the missing comparison makes it difficult to judge whether the simultaneous dual is a broadly new mathematical principle or an elegant convex-analytic elimination of familiar controller variables.

## 8. Assessment against the controlling targets F1--F4

### F1 — Causal acquired-geometry transfer

**Achieved in a substantial exact form on the declared compact affine predictive class.**

The theorem begins with raw experimental kernels through Appendix A, constructs finite observers from actual acquisition laws, and gives necessity and sufficiency. The converse is not merely a terminal packing bound. The qualification is that compact affine realization and finite horizon/actions are genuine structural assumptions, and the criterion remains law-valued rather than a universal scalar invariant.

### F2 — Resource--resolution--risk composition

**Substantially achieved for persistent labels and selected error coordinates; partially achieved for the full programme.**

The exact variational formulas give persistent-label risk frontiers for external and autonomous interfaces. Common-task simulators, numerical defects, actual erasure, inaccessible calibration and physical calls are separately accounted for. The finite rational compiler gives one effective class.

There is still no matching optimal region for programme length, workspace, arithmetic precision, planning time, physical time, reset count or simulator state. The exact-real compact-carrier theorem can require noncomputable kernels. Thus the full multi-resource objective remains incomplete.

### F3 — Singular extension

**Achieved in an exact but deliberately structured class.**

Blind transport permits arbitrary compactly supported actual acquisition laws, including singular continuous and countably atomic laws, with no density or contraction. The exact rate depends on actual quantization error. This is a meaningful demonstration that the general criterion is not secretly a smoothing theorem.

It is not a general theorem for singular nonlinear filtering, changing-support random dynamics or naturally arising noncommutative singularities.

### F4 — Realization theorem

**Achieved.**

Gaussian categorical filtering and a finite-dimensional noncommuting instrument verify the regular hypotheses for conventional Brier tasks. Blind transport verifies a fundamentally different noncontracting regime. None of these realization conclusions is used as a premise of the exact allocation theorem.

## 9. Major issues before specialist-journal publication

### 9.1 Recast the exact theorem's novelty against finite-state-controller synthesis

This is the most important revision request. The paper presently compares mainly with finite-window approximation and zero-delay coding, while the closest object to Definition 2.1 is a randomized finite-state controller. The literature comparison should include bounded FSC synthesis, Mealy/Moore controller conventions, parametric-Markov-chain reductions and inductive FSC synthesis.

The authors should state precisely what Theorem 3.1(b) adds:

- a compact predictive-state formulation rather than a finite hidden-state path formulation;
- elimination of one common observation-dependent controller kernel through an infinite affine moment dual;
- exact same-task risk optimization over actual conditional predictive states; and
- a separation between external phase, stationary programme memory and predictive resolution.

Theorem 9.3 should then be positioned as a finite rational specialization and support-preserving execution theorem, not as though decidability of bounded randomized controllers were absent from the literature.

### 9.2 Clarify “intrinsic criterion” versus “exact variational restatement”

Theorem 3.1 is exact and nontrivial, but its variables include the full phase-indexed family `(p_{t,z},q_{t,z})` and all affine moment inequalities. The paper should explain in the introduction that the theorem eliminates the unknown report-to-label kernel but does not eliminate the law-valued trajectory. It is an exact convex feasibility characterization, not a finite list of experiment invariants.

A useful addition would be a proposition showing the finite-state/report specialization as an explicit linear or polynomial feasibility system. That would expose what is gained by the dual elimination and what remains to be optimized.

### 9.3 Strengthen the operational interpretation of the dual tests

The manuscript alternates among “affine tests,” “future-test inequalities” and “finite dual witnesses.” It should state exactly when an affine function on `K_t` is the expectation of a physically executable continuation, when it is a finite linear combination of determining tests, and when only uniform approximation is used.

Theorem 4.4's finite obstruction is a finite family of polyhedral convex inequalities. It is not necessarily a single continuation experiment with one bounded score. The prose should preserve that distinction.

### 9.4 Give the central autonomous theorem a worked nontrivial finite example

Remark 5.2 shows a two-phase contradiction with an identical deterministic report. This is useful but minimal. Add a finite experiment in which:

- every phasewise convex-order contraction is feasible;
- common-programme feasibility is nontrivial rather than immediately impossible;
- the simultaneous inequality determines a sharp risk or state threshold; and
- the implementing common kernel is displayed.

This would make the theorem accessible to readers outside functional analysis and clarify that it is more than a no-gluing counterexample.

### 9.5 Separate the conventional external-clock theorem from the internally timed deadline law in all summaries

The abstract does this reasonably well, but the paper's strongest quantitative-looking formula remains the autonomous `(M-n)^{-2/d}` law. For most finite-horizon control models the stage is externally known. The conventional result is the external `M^{-2/d}` rate and the exact external allocation frontier. The internal clock charge is a valuable interface theorem, not a universal memory penalty.

### 9.6 Expand the conditional-convex-order and sequential-information-design comparison

The one-step measurable allocation should be compared with conditional martingale coupling results, and the serial external flow with dynamic/sequential information design. The point is not to diminish the result but to isolate the new composition constraint cleanly.

### 9.7 Tighten the status of the finite-obstruction theorem

Theorem 4.4 is a compactness theorem. It gives no computable witness length or rate of convergence of finite relaxations. The paper should avoid language that could be read as an algorithmic finite certificate for arbitrary compact Borel data. A short example where the required number of tests has no supplied a priori bound would help.

### 9.8 Preserve the finite-dimensional scope of the quantum theorem

The quantum verification is mathematically sound as a finite-dimensional example. Its role should remain one realization of a compact affine carrier with a full-rank positive instrument and a strongly curved probability task. It does not establish a quantum analogue of the exact theorem beyond the ordinary affine-state formalism, nor an optimal quantum-memory law under general measurements.

### 9.9 Distinguish cardinality synthesis from implementable computation throughout

The general theorem synthesizes Borel kernels whose descriptions can be infinite. The finite rational theorem gives one effective specialization but may be computationally prohibitive. This distinction appears in Section 9 and the audits; it should be stated at the first theorem summary as prominently as the label bound.

### 9.10 Reconsider the title or add a sharper subtitle

The title may be retained, but “Foundations” still creates an expectation of a broad invariant theory of finite-memory causal experiments. A subtitle emphasizing “finite barycentric allocation and stationary programme compatibility” would better identify the new mathematical center. At minimum, the abstract should include the phrase “on compact affine predictive carriers” in its first sentence about the exact criterion.

## 11. What would materially change the top-four assessment

R18 has already crossed the threshold from a sophisticated sufficient theorem to an exact compact-class characterization. More polishing, more finite checks, or another purpose-built realization would not by itself change the top-four recommendation. A materially different assessment would require at least one advance of broader external force, for example:

1. **A natural hard application.** Use the exact criterion to solve a recognized open finite-memory problem for a nonuniformly stable filter, partially observed expanding system, constrained information-design problem, or quantum-memory task not constructed around the theorem.

2. **A computable or finitely parameterized intrinsic frontier.** Derive a finite or effectively convergent dual with quantitative witness bounds for a broad natural class, rather than only compact existence of a finite obstruction below a fixed target.

3. **A structural classification beyond compact affine quotients.** Extend the necessity-and-sufficiency theorem to a significantly broader standard-Borel or noncompact class while retaining measurable implementability, or prove a sharp obstruction showing why such an extension fails.

4. **A general online/checkpoint equivalence or separation theorem.** Characterize when terminal finite support can be maintained through every intermediate cut without relying on a special blind protocol or the internal-clock convention.

5. **A genuinely matched multi-resource region.** Prove upper and lower bounds for several intrinsic coordinates among persistent labels, programme description, workspace, precision, acquisition count, simulator state and physical time in one natural experiment class.

6. **A substantive unknown-kernel/adaptive-acquisition theorem.** Combine the exact causal allocation frontier with learning or experimental design under a raw acquisition budget, with matching risk or regret and no access to the true parameter.

7. **A sharp connection to another major theory.** Establish an equivalence or duality with causal transport, dynamic information design, martingale optimal transport or finite-state-controller synthesis that yields new theorems on both sides rather than only a terminology comparison.

Any one of these could concentrate the architecture into a result of broad general-mathematics significance.

## 12. Final evaluation

Revision R18 is a serious and successful response to the preceding external report. Its exact external allocation theorem places the actual deployed acquisition law, rather than a formal reachable set, at the center of finite causal compression. Its autonomous theorem correctly identifies the missing compatibility condition: the same persistent programme location must use one action kernel and one report-to-label kernel at every phase, and the simultaneous dual tests precisely that constraint. The Bellman--Jensen decomposition, compact finite obstruction and blind intermediate bottleneck make the framework mathematically coherent rather than purely definitional.

The manuscript is also unusually careful about scope. It distinguishes compact predictive realization from arbitrary measurable quotients, exact cardinality from finite-bit execution, external time from internal time, actual singular acquisition from ambient dimension, one-way simulation from reverse risk comparison, and delivery evidence from proof.

I found no elementary fatal error in the load-bearing arguments checked. The mathematical and provenance pipeline is disciplined and reproducible within the limits it states.

The paper nevertheless remains below the exceptional threshold of the four leading general mathematics journals. The exact theorem is an elegant compact convex characterization, but its principal new step is an elimination/separation theorem for one common finite-state programme. The quantitative natural-looking laws are inherited; the new singular and compiler consequences are transparent model classes; and no widely recognized difficult external problem is resolved. The most directly adjacent finite-state-controller and conditional-kernel literature also needs substantially fuller treatment before the novelty can be judged confidently.

Accordingly:

**Top-four general-journal recommendation:** reject in the present form.  
**Reason:** insufficient concentration of broadly transformative impact for that exceptional level, not an identified fatal correctness defect.  
**Specialist-journal outlook:** strongly favorable after a focused major revision addressing finite-state-controller synthesis, conditional convex-order and sequential-information-design positioning; sharpening the operational meaning of the dual tests; adding a nontrivial finite worked example; and preserving the exact compact-class and computational scope boundaries.
