\section{Increasing-depth recovery and simultaneous confidence cylinders}
\label{sec:growing-depth}
The following argument uses the finite-sample likelihood inequality, including
its constants, at the shrinking separation supplied by the inverse theorem.
The prior-thickness requirement is imposed only for these quantitative
conclusions, not for the full-support posterior LDP.

\subsection{Effective covers and prior mass}
Assume a product prior on \(c,(a_j),(b_j)\), with all coordinate densities
bounded below by a common \(p_->0\). Let \(w_->0\) be the minimum of the
three interval lengths and \(W\ge1\) their maximum, enlarged to one.
Use either the washed response metric
\(d_h(\beta,\gamma)=a\|h_\beta-h_\gamma\|_{C([0,T])}\), or the metric
\(d_*\) of \eqref{eq:policy-controlling-metric}. The following construction specifies
covering numbers and prior-mass bounds for both metrics.

\begin{lemma}[Boundary locality and explicit finite-cylinder bounds]
\label{lem:jacobi-boundary-locality}
If two parameters agree in \(c,a_0,b_0,\ldots,a_K,b_K\), then
\begin{align}
 \|h_\beta-h_\gamma\|_{C([0,T])}
 &\le2T e^{\Lambda T}\frac{(\Lambda T)^{K+1}}{(K+1)!},\notag\\
 \|g_\beta-g_\gamma\|_{L^1(0,\infty)}
 &\le2S e^{\Lambda S}\frac{(\Lambda S)^{K+1}}{(K+1)!}
       +2C_*e^{-\lambda_*S}/\lambda_*\quad(S>0).
 \label{eq:factorial-boundary-locality}
\end{align}
If only those coordinates are changed by at most \(r\), with the tail
unchanged, the additional error in \(d_h\) is at most
\(C_hr\), where \(C_h=\max(1,2aT^2e^{\Lambda T})\); the additional
error in \(d_*\) is at most \(C_*^{\rm met}r\), where
\[
 C_*^{\rm met}=\max\{1,4(U_*+a)C_*^2/\lambda_*^2\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The matrix entries of \(A_\beta\) only connect a site to itself or an
adjacent site. A word of length at most \(K\), starting at the boundary,
cannot involve a coefficient below depth \(K\). Thus the boundary
entries of the first \(K+1\) powers agree. Subtract the two exponential
series and use
\(\sum_{r\ge K+1}x^r/r!\le e^x x^{K+1}/(K+1)!\).
Integration yields the first bound and the finite-\(S\) portion of the
second. Uniform stability bounds the remaining integral by
\(2C_*e^{-\lambda_*S}/\lambda_*\).

For the finite-coordinate perturbation, the generator difference has
operator norm at most \(4r\), by the tridiagonal row-sum bound and the
damping entry. Duhamel's formula gives a bound \(4rte^{\Lambda t}\)
on the semigroup difference. Integrating it proves the stated \(d_h\)
bound. Alternatively stability in both factors gives
\(4rC_*^2te^{-\lambda_*t}\); its integral is
\(4rC_*^2/\lambda_*^2\). Finally
\(\|h_\beta-h_\gamma\|_{C([0,T])}\le\|g_\beta-g_\gamma\|_1\).
For a general pair, insert the admissible hybrid with the first parameter's
tail and the second parameter's finite coordinates. This proves both
locality-plus-perturbation estimates without a factor growing with \(K\).
\end{proof}

For \(d_h\), let \(K_h(\epsilon)\) be the least nonnegative integer such
that the first right side of \eqref{eq:factorial-boundary-locality},
multiplied by \(a\), is at most \(\epsilon/4\).
For \(d_*\), choose
\[
 S(\epsilon)=\max\left\{1,T,
 \lambda_*^{-1}\log\frac{16(U_*+a)C_*}{\lambda_*\epsilon}\right\}
\]
and let \(K_*(\epsilon)\) be the least nonnegative integer making
\[
 2(U_*+a)S(\epsilon)e^{\Lambda S(\epsilon)}
 \frac{(\Lambda S(\epsilon))^{K+1}}{(K+1)!}\le\epsilon/8.
\]
The remaining stability tail is at most \(\epsilon/8\).
For either metric \(d\), use its corresponding \(K=K_d(\epsilon)\)
and \(C_d\in\{C_h,C_*^{\rm met}\}\), and set
\begin{equation}
 r_d(\epsilon)=\min\{w_-,1,\epsilon/(4C_d)\},\quad
 \overline{\mathcal H}_d(\epsilon)=(2K+3)
       \log(2+W/r_d(\epsilon)),\quad
 \mathcal P_d(\epsilon)=(2K+3)\log\frac1{p_-r_d(\epsilon)}.
 \label{eq:effective-entropy-prior}
\end{equation}
Then \(\mathcal H_d(\epsilon)\le\overline{\mathcal H}_d(\epsilon)\),
and every \(d\)-ball of radius \(\epsilon\) has prior mass at least
\(e^{-\mathcal P_d(\epsilon)}\). Indeed a coordinate mesh of radius
\(r_d(\epsilon)\), with one fixed tail, covers the response image;
each matching coordinate interval has length at least \(r_d(\epsilon)\)
even at a boundary truth. Independence of the coordinates gives the mass
bound, while the unrestricted tail has mass one. The resulting metric error
is at most \(\epsilon/2\), so the bound holds for open balls as well.
In particular \(\mathcal P_d\) is an upper bound on minus log prior mass.

These explicit formulas imply, with box-dependent constants,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_{d_h}(\epsilon)+\mathcal P_{d_h}(\epsilon)
 &\le C\frac{(1+|\log\epsilon|)^2}
                   {\log(2+|\log\epsilon|)},\notag\\
 \mathcal H_{d_*}(\epsilon)+\mathcal P_{d_*}(\epsilon)
 &\le C(1+|\log\epsilon|)^2\qquad(0<\epsilon<1/2).
 \label{eq:response-entropy-bound}
\end{align}
For the first assertion use \(m!\ge(m/e)^m\) with fixed \(\Lambda T\):
\(m=C|\log\epsilon|/\log(2+|\log\epsilon|)\), for sufficiently large
fixed \(C\), makes the factorial tail smaller than \(\epsilon/4\).
For the second, \(S(\epsilon)=O(1+|\log\epsilon|)\). Taking
\[
 m\ge\max\left\{2e\Lambda S,
 \frac{\log(16(U_*+a)S/\epsilon)+\Lambda S}{\log2},1\right\}
\]
makes the required tail at most \(\epsilon/8\), because
\((\Lambda S)^m/m!\le2^{-m}\). Thus \(K_*=O(1+|\log\epsilon|)\).
In both cases \(\log(1/r_d(\epsilon))=O(1+|\log\epsilon|)\), proving
\eqref{eq:response-entropy-bound}; finitely many larger radii are absorbed
in the constant. The finite-radius bounds remain the explicit formulas
\eqref{eq:effective-entropy-prior}.

\begin{theorem}[Growing-depth recovery under washed or no-washout policies]
\label{thm:growing-depth-recovery}
Let \(J_n\to\infty\), \(0<\delta_n\le1\), and
\(q_n=\rho_n a^2\underline\kappa_{J_n}(\delta_n)\), where
\(\rho_n\in(0,1]\) is the exploration floor. Use \(d=d_h\) for the
washed protocol, including adaptive nonexploration durations, and \(d=d_*\)
for the no-washout protocol. If
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_n(q_n/16)\to0,\quad nq_n\to\infty,\quad
 \mathcal P_d(\sqrt{q_n}/4)=o(nq_n),
 \label{eq:growing-depth-conditions}
\end{equation}
then, uniformly over the corresponding horizon-dependent policy class,
\[
 \Pi_n^{\nu_n}\{d_{J_n}(\beta,\beta_0)\ge\delta_n\}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\]
A directly checkable sufficient replacement for the first condition is
\(\mathcal H_d(e(q_n/16))=o(nq_n^2)\) and \(nq_n^2\to\infty\).
For \(\inf_n\rho_n>0\), fixed \(0<\eta<1\), and
\begin{equation}
 (J_n+1)^3=o(\log n),\qquad
 \delta_n=\exp\{-\eta\log n/[80(J_n+1)]\},
 \label{eq:explicit-shrinking-radius}
\end{equation}
all the conditions hold and \(\delta_n\to0\). This includes the earlier
\(J_n=o((\log n)^{1/5})\) regime and also permits
\(J_n=o((\log n)^{1/3})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The squared conditional response contrast on the separated cylinder is at
least \(q_n\), by the exploration identity and the inverse theorem.
The hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-n-posterior-separation} hold with
the deterministic metric just specified. Its failure probability tends to
zero under \eqref{eq:growing-depth-conditions}, including the transient term
by \eqref{eq:finite-n-transient-tail}; its posterior upper bound tends to
zero by the prior-mass condition. This proves the assertion without a
fixed-resolution-to-shrinking-resolution interchange.

For the concrete choice, put \(j_n=J_n+1\). The new inverse bound gives
\[
 q_n\ge (\inf_m\rho_m)a^2e^{-A_0j_n^3}n^{-\eta/4}
       =n^{-\eta/4-o(1)}.
\]
Thus \(nq_n^2\ge n^{1-\eta/2-o(1)}\), whereas both entropy and negative
log prior mass at the required radii are \(O((\log n)^2)\).
These verify the sufficient conditions with all fixed likelihood constants
retained. Also \(\log n/j_n\to\infty\), so \(\delta_n\to0\).
All estimates are uniform in the policy and true coefficient sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exponentially weighted operator-norm contraction]
\label{cor:weighted-operator-recovery}
Let \(W_\tau e_j=e^{-\tau j}e_j\), \(\tau>0\). With
\(C_{\rm box}=2b_++4a_+\),
\begin{equation}
 \|W_\tau(J_\beta-J_{\beta_0})W_\tau\|_{\rm op}
 \le3d_J(\beta,\beta_0)+C_{\rm box}e^{-2\tau(J+1)}.
 \label{eq:weighted-operator-bound}
\end{equation}
Consequently the preceding theorem with \(\delta_n\to0\) gives contraction
at radius \(3\delta_n+C_{\rm box}e^{-2\tau(J_n+1)}\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Split the tridiagonal difference into the entries involving the known
coefficients \(b_0,\ldots,b_J,a_0,\ldots,a_J\), including the edge from
\(J\) to \(J+1\), and the remaining entries. The first part has row-sum
norm at most \(3d_J\), also after multiplication by \(W_\tau\).
Every index in the second part is at least \(J+1\), so its weighted
row sum is at most \(C_{\rm box}e^{-2\tau(J+1)}\).
The Schur bound proves the inequality. This is explicitly a weighted
operator topology, not an assertion of ordinary operator-norm recovery.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Anytime frequentist confidence sets}
Let \(p_q>0\) be weights on the countable labeled diagnostic cells, summing
to one; one may use \(p_q=\omega_q\). Fix \(0<\alpha<1\).
For the washed protocol count all visits to cell \(q\); for the no-washout
protocol count exploration visits only. Write \(m=N_q(n)\) and
\(\widehat h_q=(am)^{-1}\sum_{i\in q,\ i\le n}S_iY_i\) when \(m>0\).
Set \(\ell_{q,m}=\log\{2m(m+1)/(\alpha p_q)\}\). Define radii
\begin{align}
 R^{\rm wash}_{q,n}&=\frac\sigma a\sqrt{2\ell_{q,m}/m}
        +\frac1{am}\sum_{i\in q,\ i\le n}r_i,\notag\\
 R^{\rm lin}_{q,n}&=\frac{\sqrt{2(\sigma^2+B_*^2)\ell_{q,m}}}{a\sqrt m},
 \qquad B_*=C_*\{R+U_*/\lambda_*\},
 \label{eq:response-confidence-radius}
\end{align}
with radius \(+\infty\) and no constraint when \(m=0\). Here \(r_i\)
is the undifferentiated washout envelope, not a derivative envelope.

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous honest response and coefficient cylinders]
\label{thm:honest-jacobi-cylinders}
For either protocol and any finite collection \(\mathcal Q_n\) of cells,
let
\[
 \mathcal C_n=\{\beta\in\mathfrak B:
 |h_\beta(t_q)-\widehat h_q|\le R_{q,n}\ (q\in\mathcal Q_n)\}.
\]
Then \(P_{\beta_0,z_0}\{\beta_0\in\mathcal C_n\text{ for all }n\}\ge1-\alpha\)
for a fixed infinite admissible policy. At each horizon the bound is uniform
over horizon-dependent policies. It is also valid for data-selected finite
cell collections, since the underlying event controls all cells and times.
These are frequentist confidence sets, not posterior credible sets.

For any \(J\ge0\), \(0<\delta\le1\), suppose \(\mathcal Q_n\) includes
exactly the required \(R_J\) positive-time cells
\(\{(J,s_J(\delta),k):1\le k\le R_J\}\), with any additional cells
allowed. Put \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{J,s_J(\delta)}\). If
\begin{equation}
 2\max_{1\le k\le R_J}R_{(J,s_J(\delta),k),n}
 <\frac{\delta\varepsilon^{R_J}}{2C_{R_J}L_J},
 \label{eq:honest-cylinder-explicit-threshold}
\end{equation}
then \(\operatorname{diam}_{d_J}\mathcal C_n\le\delta\). The unmeasured
origin is supplied by \(h_\beta(0)-h_\gamma(0)=0\). An empty confidence
set has diameter zero; emptiness is excluded on the coverage event.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a selected cell, its visit indicator is known before the fresh sign
and readout. In the washed experiment the sum of selected \(S_i\xi_i\)
has conditional moment generating function bounded by the Gaussian one;
the remaining signed residual is deterministically bounded by the sum of
\(r_i\)'s. In the no-washout experiment, at a visit,
\[
 Y_i=aS_ih_{\beta_0}(t_q)+B_i^0+\xi_i,\qquad |B_i^0|\le B_*.
\]
The intercept is measurable before \(S_i\), so
\[
 E[e^{\lambda S_i(B_i^0+\xi_i)}\mid\text{pre-sign information}]
 =e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2/2}\cosh(\lambda B_i^0)
 \le e^{(\sigma^2+B_*^2)\lambda^2/2}.
\]
Here \(Z_n\) denotes the sum of the selected centered increments just
described. In either case \(\exp\{\lambda Z_n-\lambda^2vN_q(n)/2\}\) is a
nonnegative supermartingale, where \(v=\sigma^2\) or \(\sigma^2+B_*^2\).
Stop it at the \(m\)th visit, first intersecting with a deterministic time
bound and then letting that bound increase. Markov's inequality and
optimization in \(\lambda\) show that a two-sided error exceeding
\(\sqrt{2vm\ell_{q,m}}\) at that visit has probability at most
\(2e^{-\ell_{q,m}}=\alpha p_q/[m(m+1)]\). This argument does not assert
that a sum conditioned on an adaptively chosen visit count is Gaussian.
Summing over all \(m\ge1\) and all cells gives total failure probability
at most \(\alpha\). Division by \(am\), and the washed residual budget,
prove simultaneous coverage.

For two elements of \(\mathcal C_n\), the response difference on every
required positive grid point is at most twice its radius. The zero point
has difference zero. The finite-grid certificate and scale choice bound
all derivatives by a value strictly less than \(\delta/L_J\).
The all-depth inverse then gives \(d_J<\delta\), proving the diameter
bound. Every threshold uses the same corrected \(L_J\).
\end{proof}

For each fixed grid, exploration-only counts divided by \(n\) converge
to \(\rho\omega_q>0\) under a fixed positive floor. Therefore the visit
counts in either protocol satisfy \(\liminf_n N_q(n)/n\ge\rho\omega_q\),
and the
radii tend to zero, and the fixed-block diameter condition eventually
holds. The coverage event remains the single event of probability at least
\(1-\alpha\); it is not obtained by intersecting fixed-time coverage claims.
For growing grids the explicit threshold remains a sufficient condition.
In the no-washout experiment the entire construction costs at most
\(\tau_+n\) physical time.
