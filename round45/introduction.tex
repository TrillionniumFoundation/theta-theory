\section{Response geometry and the two experimental clocks}
\label{sec:introduction}
The unknown coefficients of a damped lattice enter the boundary response
through repeated propagation into its unobserved bath. Deterministic inverse
geometry and chronological statistical information must therefore be proved
separately and then quantitatively connected. This article makes that
connection for a homogeneous five-parameter bath and for a complete
inhomogeneous Jacobi coefficient sequence.

In the homogeneous model, the response jets form an explicitly invertible
coordinate system for boundary damping and pinning, coupling strength, and
bath damping and pinning. A six-duration interpolation converts the jets
into a global response embedding. Calibration occupies a positive fraction
of every finite prefix, with the duration fixed before a fresh random sign.
This chronology prevents an existing hidden state from cancelling the
conditional information. Uniform smooth Gaussian likelihood estimates give
a total-variation quasi-Bernstein--von Mises theorem without requiring a
limit for the realized information matrix. The initially unknown state is
integrated against an arbitrary probability on a fixed Hilbert ball; its
likelihood contribution has a summable derivative budget. The same estimates
control current-state filter jets in fixed separable dual spaces and push
the finite-dimensional posterior through the entire memory-kernel map.

In the inhomogeneous model every positive off-diagonal coefficient and every
pinning coefficient is unknown, as is one common damping parameter. The
parameter space is a compact product, not a finite-dimensional truncation.
Classical Weyl--Schur reconstruction supplies identifiability. For quantitative
inversion we use the exact response-to-moment generating identity, the
coefficient norm of monic orthogonal polynomials, and a ledger of each finite
Gram solve, quadratic form, ratio, and square root. The resulting bound is
\[
 d_J(\beta,\gamma)\le Q^{64(J+1)^2}
 \max_{r\le4J+8}|h_\beta^{(r)}(0)-h_\gamma^{(r)}(0)|.
\]
A finite Taylor grid and an explicitly weighted multiscale design give
\[
 d_J(\beta,\gamma)\ge\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 D(\beta,\gamma)\ge e^{-A_0(J+1)^3}\delta^{20(J+1)}
 \quad(0<\delta\le1).
\]
Both constants are uniform in the infinite tail, and the proof includes the
shallow depths. The rates are sufficient rates; no optimality is asserted.

The central probability estimate is finite-sample and policy-uniform. With
\(\mathcal H_d\) the entropy of a deterministic metric controlling every
candidate mean field, it bounds the score and centered conditional-square
errors by
\[
 4\exp\{\mathcal H_d(e(t))-nt^2/(8V)\}+e^{-n}.
\]
Together with an explicit integrated-likelihood transient tail and a
finite-cylinder prior-mass bound, this yields a posterior separation
inequality at shrinking \(t\). It proves coefficient recovery at the
concrete radius
\(\delta_n=\exp\{-\eta\log n/[80(J_n+1)]\}\) whenever
\((J_n+1)^3=o(\log n)\), for any fixed \(0<\eta<1\).

The article distinguishes two clocks. Logarithmic zero-force washouts give
summable residuals, a full good posterior LDP with sampled-response rate,
and total physical time \(n\log n\). A complementary no-washout protocol
uses a predictable baseline followed by a fresh signed pulse. It has linear
physical time and conditional information at least the same exploration
floor. Its actual information can be larger and need not converge. We
therefore prove a Laplace approximation following the entire conditional
information sequence, with full LDPs along each uniform cluster subsequence;
a full-sequence adaptive LDP follows when that sequence converges. The
universal closed-set information-floor bound requires no such convergence.
Finally, stopped exponential supermartingales give confidence sets
simultaneous over all times and countably many diagnostic cells. These are
frequentist confidence sets, not claims about Bayesian credible-set coverage.

\subsection{Relation to established theory}
Inverse spectral reconstruction, the Jacobi moment problem, and orthogonal
polynomial recurrences are classical; Teschl~\cite{Teschl2000} is a standard
reference. We use these identities as a deterministic engine and provide the
finite-map condition calculation needed for the present statistical bounds.
The general principle of transferring forward regression consistency through
inverse stability also predates this work; see Vollmer~\cite{Vollmer2013}.
Here the forward data arise from one chronological boundary trajectory, and
a common metric controls all bounded feedback convolutions.

Posterior asymptotics for dependent data and misspecified models, including
posterior large-deviation behavior, have a broader existing theory, notably
Shalizi~\cite{Shalizi2009}. Our compact-space Laplace step is elementary
once the uniform likelihood estimate is established. The detailed work here
is the explicit shrinking-net bound, the uniform transient correction,
and their mechanical verification. Time-uniform confidence sequences and
exponential-supermartingale methods likewise have an established literature;
see Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe and Sekhon~\cite{HowardEtAl2021}. We use a
countable visit-time allocation to connect that mechanism to the same
finite-grid inverse, rather than interpreting a credible set as a confidence
set. The finite-dimensional Gaussian theorem is a separate regular
likelihood result; its state and memory images are finite-rank Gaussian
images, not an infinite-dimensional Bernstein--von Mises claim.
