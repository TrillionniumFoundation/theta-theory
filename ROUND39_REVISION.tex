\title[Adaptive boundary identification]{Adaptive Boundary Identification and Bayesian Asymptotics for Infinite Damped Jacobi Lattices}
\author{Qian Qi}
\date{September 4, 2026}
\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We construct and analyse two boundary experiments for damped oscillator lattices on a half-line.  First, for a homogeneous bath we infer five physical coefficients---boundary damping and pinning, coupling strength, bath damping, and bath pinning---from one noisy displacement coordinate and bounded, non-resetting feedback forcing.  Six short durations and fresh randomized signs form balanced calibration windows; no duration is required to have positive probability at every block.  A triangular inversion of the first eight response jets proves global identifiability.  We prove a policy-uniform random-information Bernstein--von Mises theorem for nonlinear adaptive Gaussian triangular experiments with sample-size-dependent policies and an arbitrarily misspecified, sample-size-dependent working prior on a summably transient Hilbert-state nuisance.  The proof gives the full empirical net, uniform stochastic quantifiers, relative unnormalized \(L^1\) Laplace estimates, and normalization.  It yields a five-dimensional posterior approximation without assuming convergence of the realized information, strong filter derivatives in test-function dual norm, and functional posterior limits for the entire time-domain memory kernel.

Second, we let every Jacobi coefficient of the stiffness operator be unknown.  The continuous-time boundary response determines the damping and the complete semi-infinite Jacobi sequence by an explicit Weyl-function recursion.  A countable-duration, randomized-sign experiment with summable washout error has a posterior that is strongly consistent in the compact product topology for this genuinely infinite-dimensional parameter.  Thus the infinite bath is both an inferential object and the source of a theorem that no fixed finite-chain replacement can carry.
\end{abstract}
