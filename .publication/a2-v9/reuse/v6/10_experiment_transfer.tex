\section{Relative boundary laws for physical experiments}
\label{sec:v6-transfer}
The two-boundary law also approximates finite experiments, rather than
only their limiting offset coefficients. It is important to retain the
probability of obtaining a selected record in this comparison. We use
$\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sup_B|P(B)-Q(B)|$ for probability measures.
For finite signed measures, $|\mu|(\Omega)$ denotes the total variation
mass. Constants below are uniform on the compact geometric families of
Theorem~\ref{thm:g-stability}. Neither symmetry nor finite horizon is
added in this section.

Fix a channel, suppress its index, and put
\[
 t_j=-W_{j,uv}(0,0)>0,\qquad p=j\bmod2.
\]
\[
 E_\infty(u,v)=S_0(u)+S_p(v),\quad
 b_\infty(u,v)=B_0(u)B_p(v).
\]
The physical time from preparation to the first impact is denoted by $r$.
On a selected event it lies between zero and the unused excess time.
At $d>0$ use the common coordinates
\[
 (z_0,z_1,s)=(u/\sqrt d,v/\sqrt d,r/d),\qquad z=(z_0,z_1).
\]
The finite and boundary subprobability measures on this space are
\begin{align}
 \dd\mu_{j,d}
 &=\frac{d^2t_j}{2\pi A}\,b_j(\sqrt d\,z)
   \one_{\{s\ge0,\ s+E_j(\sqrt d\,z)/d<1\}}\dd z\dd s,
       \label{eq:v6-finite-experiment}\\
 \dd\mu^\partial_{j,d}
 &=\frac{d^2t_j}{2\pi A}\,b_\infty(\sqrt d\,z)
   \one_{\{s\ge0,\ s+E_\infty(\sqrt d\,z)/d<1\}}\dd z\dd s.
       \label{eq:v6-boundary-experiment}
\end{align}
Their densities are zero outside the common contact box. After decreasing
the offset collar, all their nonzero supports lie in a fixed bounded
$z$-ball and in $0\le s\le1$. The first measure is exactly the
full-phase selected-record measure, by the physical flux formula.
Write $p_{j,d}=\mu_{j,d}(\R^3)$ and
$p^\partial_{j,d}=\mu^\partial_{j,d}(\R^3)$, and normalize them to
$\Pi_{j,d}$ and $\Pi^\partial_{j,d}$. Finally set
\[
 P_{j,d}=(1-p_{j,d})\delta_{\partial}+\mu_{j,d},\qquad
 P^\partial_{j,d}=(1-p^\partial_{j,d})\delta_{\partial}
                         +\mu^\partial_{j,d}.
\]
Here $\partial$ records that the preparation did not produce the selected
event. Thus $P_{j,d}$ is a coarsening of a raw physical record, not an
experiment in which unsuccessful preparations have been discarded for free.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform transfer to the boundary experiment]
\label{thm:v6-transfer}
There are $d_*>0$, $C<\infty$ and $0<\tau<1$ such that, for all
$j\ge1$ and $0<d\le d_*$, the displayed measures are well defined and
\begin{align}
 p_{j,d}&\asymp p^\partial_{j,d}\asymp d^2e^{-j\gamma},
                                                  \label{eq:v6-rare-mass}\\
 |\mu_{j,d}-\mu^\partial_{j,d}|(\R^3)
       &\le C p_{j,d}\tau^j,\qquad
 \|\Pi_{j,d}-\Pi^\partial_{j,d}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       \le C\tau^j,                              \label{eq:v6-tv}\\
 \|P_{j,d}-P^\partial_{j,d}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       &\le C p_{j,d}\tau^j.                     \label{eq:v6-raw-tv}
\end{align}
For $n$ independent preparations, respectively $k$ independently
prepared successful records, these imply
\begin{align}
 \|P_{j,d}^{\otimes n}-(P^\partial_{j,d})^{\otimes n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       &\le \min\{1,Cnp_{j,d}\tau^j\},           \label{eq:v6-product-raw}\\
 \|\Pi_{j,d}^{\otimes k}-(\Pi^\partial_{j,d})^{\otimes k}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
       &\le \min\{1,Ck\tau^j\}.                 \label{eq:v6-product-selected}
\end{align}
The same bounds hold after applying any common measurable observation
map, including retention of the endpoint positions alone. At $d=0$,
the conditional statement holds for the corresponding quadratic right
limits in the scaled coordinates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The positive endpoint Hessians and the uniform nonlinear bounds give,
on a sufficiently small common box,
\[
 c|Y|^2\le E_j(Y),E_\infty(Y)\le C|Y|^2,
 \qquad c\le b_j(Y),b_\infty(Y)\le C.
\]
These inequalities give the asserted common bounded scaled support and
upper and lower bounds on the scaled integrals. They also imply that
both masses are less than $1/2$ after decreasing $d_*$, since $t_j$ is
uniformly bounded. The Jacobi formula gives $t_j\asymp e^{-j\gamma}$,
which proves \eqref{eq:v6-rare-mass}.

The action difference has zero value and gradient at $Y=0$.
Taylor's integral formula and the $C^2$ factorization estimate imply
\[
 |E_j(Y)-E_\infty(Y)|\le C\tau^j|Y|^2,
 \qquad |b_j(Y)-b_\infty(Y)|\le C\tau^j.
\]
Consequently, on the common scaled ball,
\[
 \left|\frac{E_j(\sqrt d\,z)-E_\infty(\sqrt d\,z)}d\right|
                   \le C\tau^j|z|^2.
\]
For each fixed $z$, the two allowed $s$-fibres have lengths
$(1-E_j(\sqrt d\,z)/d)_+$ and
$(1-E_\infty(\sqrt d\,z)/d)_+$. They start at the same point zero.
Since the positive-part map is 1-Lipschitz, the length of their symmetric
difference is at most $C\tau^j|z|^2$. Integrating the bounded amplitudes
on this difference, and the amplitude difference on the common part,
proves
\[
 |\mu_{j,d}-\mu^\partial_{j,d}|(\R^3)
                       \le C d^2t_j\tau^j/A.
\]
This proves the first part of \eqref{eq:v6-tv}. In particular
$|p_{j,d}-p^\partial_{j,d}|$ obeys the same bound. Dividing by the
positive masses and adding the error of their normalizers gives the
conditional estimate. Adding the missing-event atom instead gives
\eqref{eq:v6-raw-tv}. This argument estimates a moving domain by its
fibres; it does not differentiate its indicator function.

For probability measures $P_i,Q_i$, telescope their product measures,
or couple each pair maximally, to obtain
\[
 \left\|\bigotimes_{i=1}^nP_i-\bigotimes_{i=1}^nQ_i\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
                   \le\sum_{i=1}^n\|P_i-Q_i\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
This proves the two product bounds. A measurable pushforward cannot
increase total variation. Lastly $E_j(\sqrt d\,z)/d$ and its boundary
analogue converge to their positive quadratic forms as $d\downarrow0$,
and the amplitudes converge to one. The same fibre argument proves
$L^1$ convergence of these bounded densities and of their normalizations.
Passing to the limit proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Transfer of bounded risks]\label{cor:v6-risk}
For any estimator or test based on the stated coarsened records, and any
measurable loss taking values in $[0,1]$, replacing the finite experiment
by its boundary model changes its risk by at most the right-hand side
of \eqref{eq:v6-product-raw} or \eqref{eq:v6-product-selected}, respectively.
The estimate is uniform in the parameter family. More generally, a
fixed design of independent preparations at $(j_i,d_i)$ has risk error
at most $C\sum_i p_{j_i,d_i}\tau^{j_i}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Integrate the bounded loss against the difference of the laws, using
its layer-cake representation. Apply the product estimate, with possibly
different factors, and then take the supremum over parameters.
\end{proof}

The comparison also applies in unscaled physical endpoint-position and
residual-time coordinates, by using the same boundary embeddings for
both laws at each parameter. For predetermined physical windows $t_i$,
set $d_i(\xi)=t_i-j_i g_e(\xi)$ and restrict the parameter family so that
$0<d_i(\xi)\le d_*$; the bounds hold uniformly at these offsets. Scaling
is then only a device in the proof, not an extra observation of an
unknown gap. In a design programmed directly at fixed excess $d$, the
onset must instead be supplied or calibrated. No onset-calibration cost
is removed by the total-variation comparison.

The effective sample size in \eqref{eq:v6-product-raw} is the expected
number $np_{j,d}$ of selected records. In particular, an experiment with
$np_{j,d}=O(k_j)$ is approximated uniformly whenever $k_j\tau^j\to0$.
This is a simultaneous large-record and large-sample conclusion that
separate fixed-flight Taylor expansions do not supply. It concerns the
specified endpoints and residual time. The full growing collision array
lies on an embedded surface; no total-variation comparison of distinct
such surfaces is asserted. Nor does this theorem supply a
parameter-free sampling algorithm for an unknown table. It compares the
physical statistical families in common observation coordinates and
preserves the cost of obtaining a selected event.
