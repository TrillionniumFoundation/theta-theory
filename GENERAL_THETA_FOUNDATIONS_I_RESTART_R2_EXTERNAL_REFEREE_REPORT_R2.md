## Recommendation

**Reject at the level of the Annals of Mathematics, Inventiones Mathematicae, the Journal of the American Mathematical Society, or Acta Mathematica in the manuscript's present form.**

This recommendation is **not a technical-correctness rejection**. The revision is mathematically substantial. It answers most of the previous report's seven major requests, and I did not find an elementary error that invalidates the principal displayed inequalities in the parts I checked. In particular, the predictive factorization repair, the anisotropic small-ball argument, the innovation recurrence, the reverse-edge moment recursions, the positive resolvents, the continuation-tag state lower bound, the joint robust-family lower and upper bounds, and the three realization calculations are internally coherent under their stated hypotheses.

The remaining negative recommendation is instead about **mathematical scale, structural breadth, and the location of the hard work**.

1. The main general theorem is still a certificate theorem over a finite-chart, finite-horizon, fixed-exploration class. Its new structural route reduces forced online stability to a bounded Lyapunov weight for a time-homogeneous stationary marked factor, followed by an explicit normalization against terminal acquired weights. This is useful and correct, but in the multi-chart setting the difficult acquisition/transport coupling is not classified: it remains in the normalized matrix, and it may diverge.
2. The resource layer is now formally typed and materially stronger, but the matching converse is a total-state theorem generated by an exact semantic continuation tag. Workspace, program length, physical time, pilot cost, calibration acquisition, and numerical interface costs remain upper-accounted rather than generally lower-characterized.
3. The common minimax theorem is now genuine, not merely an informal collection of examples. Nevertheless its five terms are assembled through independent blocks and an independently selected terminal menu. This proves simultaneous presence, but not a natural nonseparable interaction among acquired geometry, recurrent transport, causal simulation, and implementation complexity.
4. The paper combines several solid mechanisms, but it does not yet contain one theorem whose breadth and depth, independently of the framework surrounding it, appears commensurate with a four-leading-general-journal contribution. The positive-kernel calculation is classical in mathematical form; the new content is its placement in the acquired-geometry accounting. The mandatory-state lower is sharp for its exact challenge interface but not a general memory-complexity classification.

For a strong specialist journal in probability, mathematical statistics, stochastic control, information theory, nonlinear filtering, or quantization, I would now give a **positive major-revision recommendation**. The present r2 is far stronger than r1 and, after tightening the positioning and several technical interfaces listed below, could form a serious specialist submission. Reaching the four-leading-general-journal level would require a further theorem of genuinely broader scope, not another round of editorial polishing.

## 3. Summary of the contribution

The paper starts from a prepared causal experiment with standard-Borel hidden states, legal actions, visible reports, and published resource increments. A countable family of executable future tests induces predictive equivalence. Under a compact realization certificate, the quotient is compact metrizable and one-step report/update instruments descend with declared conditional versions.

For a finite terminal squared-prediction menu, the full-history conditional prediction vector has actual law `nu_N`. A checkpoint encoder retains at most `m` labels after seeing the complete history. An online encoder carries at most `m` persistent labels recursively and has no readable discarded-report tape. Conditional-mean projection identifies checkpoint excess risk with the optimal `m`-point quadratic quantization distortion of `nu_N`.

The acquired law is decomposed into finitely many actual weighted component charts. Each chart is the image of an anisotropic box with widths

```text
a_{j,1} >= ... >= a_{j,d_j} > 0,
```

and the conditional law has a density bounded above by a constant multiple of inverse box volume. For positive allocation `k`, the scale

```text
psi_j(k)
  = max_{1 <= l <= d_j}
      ((a_{j,1} ... a_{j,l})/k)^(2/l)
```

captures all anisotropic regimes. A zero allocation receives a discard penalty. Minimizing the acquired-weighted sum gives `Psi_N(m)`.

A specified online representative machine satisfies an innovation-localized recurrence

```text
e_{t+1}
  <= gamma_{t+1} e_t
     + C_0 sum_j B_{t+1,j} r_j
     + u_{t+1},
```

where `r_j=sqrt(psi_j(k_j))`. Exact holds inject no new rounding error. Unrolling gives transport coefficients `Z_{N,j}`. The transfer theorem assumes either the acquisition-weighted energy inequality

```text
E (sum_j Z_{N,j} z_j)^2
  <= Gamma_N sum_j w_{N,j} z_j^2
```

or a structural marked-kernel condition from which such an inequality is derived.

The structural theorem introduces reverse-edge positive operators `K_1,K_2`, assumes a bounded Lyapunov weight satisfying

```text
K_2 v <= lambda v,   lambda < 1,
```

and solves two positive resolvent equations for first and mixed-second forced moments. Their integrated matrix `H` is normalized by the actual acquired weights. This permits active expansion and exact state-independent suspension.

The central conclusion is

```text
B_N + c Psi_N(m)
  <= R_cp(N,m)
  <= R_on(N,m)
  <= B_N
     + (C sqrt(Gamma_N Psi_N(m))
        + L ||U_N||_2
        + v_N^num)^2.
```

The paper then supplies:

- a typed causal-morphism composition theorem with complete transcript defects and a full implementation ledger;
- a continuation-tag theorem proving that exact future distinctions force disjoint persistent-state supports and hence a total-state resolution lower bound at scale `floor(M/L)`;
- one explicit robust experiment with a matching five-term minimax law on a declared finite-bit feasible region;
- a controlled binary filter, a singular absorbing acquisition model, and a recurrent fold/refresh model as independent raw-kernel realizations.

The organizing insight is sound: geometric resolution must be weighted by probability mass actually acquired under the experiment, and causal approximation cost must be localized to genuine innovations rather than to elapsed clock ticks.

## 4. Assessment against targets F1–F4

### F1 — Causal acquired-geometry transfer

**Achieved for the certified finite-chart class.**

The paper proves a genuine class theorem rather than a calculation in one model. The checkpoint lower and online upper use the same actual component weights. The risk optimum is not assumed. The local machine and the transport certificate are explicit theorem hypotheses.

The important qualification is that the theorem is a transfer principle, not a classification theorem. The chart decomposition, global valid cover, allocation-uniform local recurrence, and either the full energy inequality or the stationary marked-kernel structure must already be supplied. This is acceptable mathematics; it limits the scale of the foundational claim.

### F2 — Resource–resolution–risk composition

**Achieved in a constructive upper sense and in one explicit total-state minimax class; not achieved as a universal full-ledger converse.**

The resource types are now formal. Theorem `thm:morphism` keeps raw calls, representative states, simulator states, controller states, phase states, workspace, program, calibration precision, numerical precision, and time. It correctly distinguishes pathwise, mean, and tail semantics.

Theorem `thm:tag` proves necessity of growing total state when exact continuation distinctions are part of the legal interface. Theorem `thm:robust` then gives a common-task minimax result with an explicit feasible finite-bit upper.

What remains open is a general law in the entire resource vector. No theorem characterizes optimal workspace, program length, runtime, pilot acquisition, or calibration acquisition across a broad experiment class. The manuscript states this honestly.

### F3 — Singular or nonuniformly stable extension

**Achieved in two nontrivial directions.**

The absorbing sensor includes intersecting finite strata, atoms, changing rank, arbitrarily small acquisition probability, and exact waiting. The fold/refresh realization permits recurrent active expansion with a bounded forced second moment. Neither extension introduces inverse powers of vanished widths or of the suspension probability.

The remaining limitation is that the recurrent theorem is one-chart and the multi-chart singular theorem is absorbing. The paper does not yet treat the genuinely combined problem: recurrent transport among multiple rare acquired strata with a nontrivial acquired-weight normalization derived directly from the raw dynamics.

### F4 — Distinct realizations

**Achieved.**

The binary filter, absorbing sensor, and fold/refresh model use structurally different mechanisms. None is a premise of the general theorem. This logical separation is one of the strongest aspects of the revision.

## 5. Audit of the principal proof chain

### 5.1 Predictive factorization and quotient descent

Lemma `lem:factor` now states the standard-Borel hypotheses needed for completed Doob–Dynkin factorization and supplies a proof by measurable versions of countably many coordinates. This repairs the r1 overstatement.

Proposition `prop:quotient` also repairs the pointwise conditional-version issue. Equality of event-instrument integrals yields equality of finite signed measures; equality of continuous densities almost everywhere under a full-support dominating measure upgrades to equality everywhere; normalized fibres therefore agree at every positive-density report. Zero-density values are used only when a separately verified continuous fibre-compatible extension is given.

Under these hypotheses the compact-image and quotient-map arguments are sound. The proposition is deliberately not a theorem about arbitrary measurable equivalence relations, noncompact domains, or unspecified regular conditional versions.

A presentation improvement remains desirable: the paper should distinguish even more sharply between the general standard-Borel experiment at the beginning and the much stronger compact/full-support realization certificate used for the pointwise quotient. The current prose is correct, but a reader can miss how large that specialization is.

### 5.2 Conditional projection

Lemma `lem:projection` is standard and correct. Conditioning removes the cross term, decoder randomization is eliminated by convexity, encoder randomization by nearest-center selection, and independent shared randomness only averages deterministic codebook costs. The use of separately executable terminal probes avoids constructing a nonexistent joint counterfactual outcome vector.

### 5.3 Anisotropic actual-mass lemma

Lemma `lem:geometry` is one of the cleanest parts of the paper.

For the upper bound, the scale `h=sqrt(psi_j(k))`, active coordinate subdivision, and the estimate on the product of cell counts yield at most `k` valid representatives with radius of order `h`. Zero widths are deleted rather than inverted. Discarded components are charged by the diameter.

For the lower bound, the score-chart inverse Lipschitz estimate turns a score ball into short coordinate projections; the true conditional density upper bound gives an actual small-ball estimate; a union bound over arbitrary decoder centers gives the quantization lower. The separated-component argument correctly handles centers off the chart and components receiving no label. The intersecting-strata corollary uses all centers in every conditional small-ball estimate and loses only a finite `J`-dependent factor.

This is a finite-chart theorem. It is not a result for arbitrary doubling, rectifiable, Ahlfors-regular, or multifractal measures. The paper no longer suggests otherwise.

### 5.4 Innovation transport and the main theorem

The positive recurrence unrolls exactly to the displayed `Z_{N,j}` and `U_N`. Minkowski's inequality and the nonnegative-cone energy bound give the root-mean-square error estimate without an independence assumption. Exact holds are correctly assigned zero forcing.

Conditional on `(G)`, `(I)`, `(C)`, and `(T)` or `(K)`, the proof of `thm:main` is sound. The checkpoint lower follows from projection and geometry. An online output is a checkpoint output, giving the middle inequality. The specified representative machine, terminal task Lipschitz bound, and conditional projection give the upper. The zero-distortion case is handled as equality rather than as a ratio of zeros.

The central limitation is structural rather than logical. Assumption `(I)` already asks for a single allocation-independent recursive implementation with pathwise coefficients. In difficult models, constructing this machine and its common valid envelope can be most of the approximation problem. Assumption `(T)` is almost exactly the energy statement needed to match the lower. The new kernel theorem reduces this burden for an important stationary marked class, but does not eliminate it generally.

### 5.5 Forced moment resolvent

Theorem `thm:kernel` is the principal new mathematical addition.

The reverse-edge conditional recursions

```text
m_{t+1}=K_1 m_t+f,
C_{t+1}=K_2 C_t+D_j m_{t,k}+D_k m_{t,j}+g_{jk}
```

are correct under the stated Markov-factor conditional independence and stationary environment law. The inequality `K_2 v<=lambda v` implies `K_1 sqrt(v)<=sqrt(lambda)sqrt(v)` by conditional Cauchy–Schwarz. The two positive Neumann resolvents are therefore finite in their weighted spaces.

Starting from zero forcing, positivity bounds the finite-time first and mixed-second profiles by the fixed points. The limiting conditional second-moment matrices are positive semidefinite; integration preserves this; entrywise domination is sufficient on the nonnegative cone. A bounded initial density with respect to the stationary environment law multiplies nonnegative path expectations by the domination constant. The normalization

```text
|| diag(w_N)^(-1/2) H diag(w_N)^(-1/2) ||_op
```

then yields `(T)` on positive-weight coordinates, with the necessary zero-row proviso.

The finite-state example and the one-state scalar formula are arithmetically consistent. In the fold/refresh realization,

```text
E G   = 17/40,
E G^2 = 73/160,
H     = 3040/667.
```

The exact suspension calculation correctly cancels the state-independent activity probability in both fixed-point equations while retaining all physical calls and time.

Two points should nevertheless be emphasized.

First, this theorem is a forced affine moment-stability theorem in positive-operator form. Its mathematical machinery is classical in spirit, as the paper itself acknowledges. The new contribution is the acquired-weight normalization and its placement in the causal approximation chain. That is useful, but not by itself a top-four-scale positive-operator result.

Second, in more than one chart the theorem does not derive bounded acquired normalization from the raw dynamics. The matrix `H` can put mass in a forcing channel whose terminal acquired weight is small or zero. The manuscript exposes this rather than hiding it, but this is precisely the hard acquisition/transport interaction suggested by the title. No nontrivial multi-chart recurrent realization closes it.

### 5.6 Causal morphisms and complete resource accounting

Section 5 is much improved. A morphism now has an initial simulator state, causal microsteps, nondecreasing stopping indices, legal source actions, report/clock/cost transformations, terminal-command semantics, a lifted controller, and a monotone full-ledger resource map. The defect is measured on the complete target-visible law, including the common terminal readout and independent controller seed. This is the correct object for a bounded common-loss transfer.

The serial-composition proof is the total-variation triangle inequality plus data processing, with the needed strategy-class inclusions made explicit. The pathwise resource composition is correctly distinguished from conditional expected-cost and tail versions.

The theorem is valuable as a typed accounting result. Its probabilistic core is standard. It does not establish a categorical classification, an optimal resource monotone, or a converse for arbitrary resource coordinates. The title should not be read as claiming those stronger results.

### 5.7 Mandatory continuation state

Theorem `thm:tag` is correct for its exact interface. With a fixed independent random seed, two distinct tags cannot reach the same positive-probability persistent state and still answer a fresh identical challenge with zero error. The state supports are therefore disjoint. Conditioning on each tag reduces the prediction problem to a codebook with `k_c` centers, and averaging yields the exact allocation formula. The scale lower at `floor(M/L)` follows from a counting argument and the anisotropic lower bound.

This theorem properly distinguishes a semantic lower bound from a mere product implementation. It does not prove that every internal simulator, controller, or clock register is mandatory. The manuscript states that limitation.

The exact zero-error challenge is strong. Quantitative noisy challenges, approximate continuation equivalence, and information-theoretic state lower bounds would be natural next steps and would considerably broaden the result.

### 5.8 Joint robust minimax theorem

Theorem `thm:robust` is now a legitimate minimax statement. The experiment family, unknown parameter, report order, common task, block selection, oracle baseline, audited tag, exact-report model, finite-bit input model, total-state budget, and feasible computational region are all explicit.

The lower proof is consistent:

- no acquisition leaves the geometry at its prepared variance;
- successful acquisition followed by the exact tag requirement invokes the total-state quantization lower;
- identical calibration packets force the `delta^2` floor;
- two probabilities in one width-`2^{-b}` input cell force the `2^{-2b}` floor;
- an erasure probability `2 epsilon` gives squared-prediction excess of order `epsilon` and exact deficiency `epsilon` under the paper's total-variation convention.

The upper retains the tag, a three-valued bit state, and either an unresolved geometry state or a grid index. Its state count and constant-factor allocation scaling are correct. The finite-bit scan, guard-bit, program, workspace, and time bounds are conservative but coherent.

The conceptual limitation is that the five terms are deliberately separable. They live in independent prepared variables and in a terminal block chosen independently after encoding. This is a valid way to prove simultaneous minimax terms. It does not show a natural experiment in which calibration uncertainty changes the acquired geometry, recurrent instability changes the optimal state allocation, and causal deficiency interacts nonadditively with resource resolution. For a foundational top-four claim, such coupling would be far more compelling.

### 5.9 Realizations

#### Binary filter

The posterior update, positive denominator, invariant interval, log-odds contraction, report total-variation coefficient, and posterior-density Jacobian bound are correct. Mixtures over histories preserve the density upper bound. The exact Borel and finite-bit algorithms are now separated. The resulting `m^{-2}` checkpoint/online law is credible uniformly over the stated compact parameter rectangle and fixed command rules.

#### Singular absorbing sensor

The unresolved mass is `(1-s)^N`; conditional on acquisition, the target retains the prepared mixture law; total variance gives the displayed acquisition baseline. The quotient glues equal absorbed predictions and does not reveal the latent chart mark. A success overwrites once, failures and post-success attempts are exact holds, and the transport coefficients are disjoint indicators. Intersecting finite strata and rank collapse are handled without inverse separation or inverse width.

#### Recurrent fold/refresh model

The fold has two inverse branches and preserves the density supremum; the refresh creates a conditional density at most `4/(3a)`; the report type is independent of the current state; the active Lipschitz gains are `2` and `1/4`. The second active gain moment is below one despite recurrent expansion. The kernel theorem and suspension proposition therefore give the stated uniform online bound. This is a real improvement over r1's contractive/absorbing examples.

The remaining breadth issue is that all three models are low-dimensional and only the recurrent model exercises the new resolvent theorem. The model in which the acquired-weight normalization is genuinely difficult is not yet present.

## 6. Why the top-four threshold is not met

### 6.1 The hard multi-chart coupling remains external

The central conceptual novelty is supposed to be the coupling of actual acquisition mass and causal error transport. In the theorem, that coupling is represented by `(T)` or by the normalized stationary matrix under `(K)`. The kernel theorem computes `H`, but the comparison between `H` and the terminal weights `w_N` remains an additional quantitative requirement.

For one chart this is automatic. For the absorbing mixture it follows from a special disjoint-indicator identity. The paper does not prove a structural theorem for recurrent transitions among several charts, rare terminal components, or changing strata that forces the required normalization. That is the point at which the two sides of the theory would interact nontrivially.

A result based on regeneration, conditioned transfer operators, a space-time Lyapunov function, or a finite-horizon bridge localization that derives the correct terminal weights would materially raise the paper's scale.

### 6.2 The resource converse is semantic and one-coordinate

The continuation-tag theorem is rigorous, but it forces state by putting an exact tag-recovery problem into the legal continuation interface. It establishes a lower in total persistent cardinality. It does not characterize the cost of causal simulation or prove a general tradeoff among persistent state, transient workspace, program length, calibration information, raw calls, and time.

The complete ledger is therefore primarily an upper-accounting language. The robust theorem keeps the other coordinates visible on its feasible region, but does not prove their optimality. This is enough for a well-typed specialist paper, not yet for a foundational resource theory in the strongest sense suggested by the title.

### 6.3 The joint minimax class is engineered rather than structurally coupled

The robust class successfully repairs the logical defect of r1: there is now one class, one minimax functional, and one common oracle. However, the proof deliberately decomposes into independent geometry, calibration, numerical-cell, and erasure blocks.

Such direct-sum witnesses are legitimate. Their novelty is lower than a theorem showing that the same raw dynamics necessarily produces the full resource–resolution law through one coupled mechanism. The present construction demonstrates compatibility of terms, not an intrinsic interaction theorem.

### 6.4 The geometry remains finite and charted

The anisotropic box lemma is effective and width-uniform, but it is elementary finite-dimensional geometry. Intersections are finite; dimensions are uniformly bounded; every component has an onto bi-Lipschitz parametrization and an actual density upper bound against box volume. The paper explicitly leaves infinitely accumulating strata and general singular measures open.

A broad theorem in terms of metric entropy, doubling profiles, multiscale mass functions, rectifiable decompositions, or local dimension spectra—while retaining the online causal conclusion—would be a substantially more general contribution.

### 6.5 Fixed exploration and finite horizon remain central

The principal matching theorem fixes the exploration independently of the encoder and works at a deterministic finite horizon or a declared bounded stopping time. This is mathematically clean. It does not address adaptive information acquisition optimized jointly with compression, unknown-kernel learning, or infinite-horizon experiment comparison.

Again, these limitations do not make the theorem false. They place it in a specialist rather than a universal-foundational scope.

### 6.6 The paper has no single dominant theorem of top-four depth

The manuscript is a carefully assembled architecture:

- standard-Borel factorization and quotient descent;
- finite-chart quantization;
- positive recurrence unrolling;
- a Markov-factor moment resolvent;
- total-variation composition;
- a disjoint-support tag lower;
- an explicit direct-sum minimax construction;
- three low-dimensional examples.

The synthesis is coherent and potentially useful. The individual mathematical ingredients, however, are mostly elementary or classical in form once the certificates are in place. The submission does not yet produce a theorem with the breadth, surprise, or difficulty normally expected for the four leading general journals.

## 7. Revisions recommended before a specialist submission

These revisions would not by themselves change my top-four recommendation, but they would materially improve the paper.

### 7.1 State the seeded kernel theorem with separate notation

The theorem first defines `Z_0=0` and then states that the same inequality holds with a nonnegative seed satisfying first- and second-moment bounds. Give the seeded recurrence a separate symbol or explicitly set

```text
Z_0=S,
Z_{t+1}=gamma_{t+1}Z_t+b_{t+1}.
```

Then align this seed with the `t=0` innovation term of the main theorem. The present meaning is recoverable, but the index interface is too important to leave implicit.

### 7.2 Make the geometry-channel/forcing-channel identification explicit

In `(I)` and `(K)`, the index `j` simultaneously labels terminal acquired charts, allocation radii, and forcing channels. State precisely how a one-step raw mark chooses or bounds these channels, especially when a path can move among chart domains before the terminal horizon. This is automatic in the one-chart and absorbing examples, but not in a general recurrent multi-chart model.

### 7.3 Separate theorem conclusions for exact Borel and finite-bit machines

The paper is much better on this distinction, but the main theorem still carries both models in one long statement. A clearer architecture would state:

1. an exact Borel finite-label theorem under `(G),(I),(T)`;
2. an effective finite-bit corollary under explicit computability, input, and numerical-error conditions;
3. a complexity corollary only where quantitative routines are supplied.

This would make the mathematical core easier to read.

### 7.4 Clarify time indexing in the initialized recurrent realization

The recurrent theorem counts the paid initial reveal as one of the `N` calls and then applies the main theorem to `N-1` continuation steps. State the convention immediately before the formula for `B_N` and use one notation consistently for the preforecast raw-call count. The appendix resolves the issue, but the theorem should be self-contained.

### 7.5 Distinguish framework novelty from classical sublemmas

The introduction should identify one or two precise claims as the paper's principal new theorems. Standard projection, finite-box quantization, positive Neumann resolvents, total-variation data processing, and disjoint-support state counting should be labeled as ingredients rather than allowed to share equal rhetorical weight.

### 7.6 Expand and sharpen prior-art positioning

The current fifteen references are a meaningful improvement. For a final specialist submission, the authors should verify publication data and broaden the theorem-level comparison in predictive-state minimality, finite-state nonlinear filtering, controlled quantization, Markov jump affine recursions, information-constrained control, automata/state complexity, and experiment deficiency. The goal should be to isolate exactly which combined statement was not previously available.

### 7.7 Shorten repository-process material in the article-facing narrative

The audit files, source receipts, negative controls, and branch policy are valuable repository artifacts. The mathematical paper should remain focused on definitions, theorems, proof ideas, and comparisons. Build integrity should not compete with mathematical motivation.

## 8. What would change the top-four assessment

A future version could plausibly change the assessment if it added at least one genuinely broad result of the following kind.

1. **A multi-chart recurrent acquisition theorem.** Starting from raw nonstationary kernels, derive the terminal acquisition-weighted transport inequality with constants uniform through rare components and chart transitions, rather than assuming bounded normalization of `H` by `w_N`.
2. **A natural coupled minimax model.** Prove a sharp law in which acquisition, recurrent instability, calibration, simulation defect, and memory resolution interact in one dynamics and cannot be separated into independent terminal blocks.
3. **A true multi-resource converse.** Establish a nontrivial state–workspace–time, state–program, or acquisition–calibration tradeoff for a broad causal experiment class, not merely an exact semantic tag.
4. **A multiscale singular-geometry theorem.** Replace finite bi-Lipschitz box charts by a broad measure-geometric condition and retain both the checkpoint lower and causal online upper with sharp scales.
5. **Adaptive exploration or unknown-kernel learning.** Optimize or lower-bound the joint cost of acquiring the geometry and compressing it, with pilots and calibration charged.

Any one of these would be a major new paper-level theorem. Without such an addition, further refinement would strengthen presentation but would not change the general-journal scale.

## 10. Positive assessment

The negative top-four recommendation should not obscure the progress made in this revision.

- The r2 paper genuinely answers the previous report rather than relabeling the old manuscript.
- The predictive quotient now treats continuous conditional versions correctly under an explicit instrument certificate.
- The geometric lower uses actual acquired mass and arbitrary decoder centers.
- Exact holds are not charged repeated fictitious rounding errors.
- Recurrent expansion is handled by a real forced-second-moment theorem rather than by an example-specific cancellation.
- The full resource ledger is now typed, and pathwise, expected, and tail semantics are separated.
- State-product necessity is proved only where semantic continuation distinctions justify it.
- The robust lower and upper use one class, one task, and one oracle.
- Exact Borel and finite-bit machines are no longer conflated.
- The singular and recurrent realizations are logically downstream of the general theorem.
- The scope audit states the remaining open responsibilities without disguising them as completed results.

This is the strongest version of the restart paper I have reviewed. It is a serious specialist-level mathematical manuscript. My rejection recommendation is based on the exceptional breadth and originality threshold of the four leading general journals, not on a conclusion that the work lacks value or that its central inequalities are unsound.

## 11. Final recommendation to the editor

**Decision: reject for a four-leading-general-journal venue.**

**Reason:** the revision is technically coherent and substantially improved, but the main theorem remains a finite-chart, certificate-based transfer result; the new kernel theorem does not yet solve the hard multi-chart acquisition/transport coupling; the resource converse is confined to exact semantic total-state distinctions; and the joint minimax construction is deliberately separable. The combined contribution is promising and publishable in spirit, but does not yet reach the breadth or depth expected at this venue.

**Specialist-journal outlook:** favorable after major revision, especially after tightening the central novelty claim, clarifying the kernel/geometry channel interface, and adding a nontrivial multi-chart recurrent example or theorem.
