## Recommendation

**Reject at the level of the Annals of Mathematics, Inventiones Mathematicae, the Journal of the American Mathematical Society, or Acta Mathematica in the manuscript's present form.**

This is **not a technical-correctness rejection**. R53 is a coherent and substantial paper. It proves a precise stopped-protocol theorem that keeps together three quantities that are often separated incorrectly: the occupation needed to reach a favorable report, the task geometry actually acquired at commitment, and the persistent controller state needed to execute the legal protocol. It also gives an exact realization of every finite feasible flow after removing unvisited circulation, a sharp paid-gate example, classical and quantum raw verifications, and a useful killed-potential perturbation theorem for modes in which stopping is forbidden.

In the load-bearing proofs I checked, I did not find an elementary defect invalidating:

- the conditional risk comparison after the full current report, including the informative auxiliary report `V`;
- domination of each terminal primary-report measure by the corresponding acquired report-count measure;
- the killed-flow balance equations for arbitrary legal history-dependent actions and stopping;
- the stationary realization of every finite feasible flow;
- removal of unreachable recurrent circulation without changing any terminal submeasure;
- compact attainment of the acquired-flow profile on the bounded count slice;
- the common unknown-model upper based only on the common primary projection and calibration certificate;
- the checkpoint and autonomous profile bounds;
- the mandatory-live-label lower under incompatible action signatures;
- the reliability–resolution geometry under the stated rectangle estimates;
- the exact gate-cost identity `kappa(p)=1+1/p` and the rescaling by `N/kappa(p)`;
- the hidden-sign and noncommuting quantum calibrations;
- the killed-potential resource/risk perturbation theorem;
- the same-task calibration/erasure construction; or
- the source, artifact, and independent-rebuild claims at the level at which they are stated.

The negative top-four judgment concerns **intrinsic breadth, distance from classical finite occupation-measure Markovization, naturality of the assumptions, and external mathematical impact**, rather than a fatal proof gap.

The central theorem assumes an exact, known, finite observable protocol driver whose primary transition/report row is common to every hidden model and every surviving history. Under that assumption, the reachability and expected-cost component is already a finite total-cost controlled Markov system. Hidden dynamics and the informative report enter the terminal decision risk through a strong history-uniform calibration certificate, but they do not alter the primary flow. The resulting theorem is clean and useful, yet it is a sufficient reduction for a structured class rather than an intrinsic or near-necessary classification of causal acquisition.

The exact autonomous matching also depends on mandatory modes with pairwise disjoint action signatures. The lower bound is correct for the typed interface as written, but it is not invariant under harmless action aliasing or alternative presentations of physically equivalent ports. Thus the strongest memory conclusion is presently interface-relative.

The gate theorem is sharp within its class and correctly retains the factor `1+1/p`. Its uniformity as `p` tends to zero is, however, a uniformity on the feasible rescaled budget `N >= 1+1/p`; no fixed finite budget survives the limit. The raw examples honestly verify the assumptions, but both impose a fixed positivity floor `q`, and the constants are not uniform as that floor vanishes.

For a strong specialist journal in stochastic control, applied probability, information theory, quantization, mathematical statistics, or mathematical quantum information, my assessment is **favorable after a focused major revision**.

## 2. Executive mathematical assessment

The physical report is `(J,Y,V)`. The pair `(J,Y)` has a known common conditional row `P_ia`; the auxiliary report `V` and hidden state remain model- and history-dependent. Stopping may use the whole report. A history-uniform certificate compares the conditional audit variance and conditional audit mean with a proxy `f_ia(j,y)` and reliability mark `w_ia(j,y)` even after selection using `V`.

For a terminal dictionary `C`, the marked distortion is

```text
d_ia,C(j,y)=w_ia(j,y)+min_b ||f_ia(j,y)-r_b||^2.
```

The profile `Psi_m(N)` minimizes terminal marked distortion over finite mode-action counts and terminal densities subject to:

1. domination by actual report counts;
2. legal stopping support;
3. killed-flow balance from the initial mode;
4. terminal mass one; and
5. the expected resource budget.

Theorem 3.1 compares both checkpoint and autonomous risk with this profile. The general autonomous widths are bracketed by `M-1` and `M-L`. Under mandatory incompatible modes, both bounds use `M-L`.

The principal proof innovation is the exact converse realization. A finite balance solution may contain an unreachable invariant circulation. The paper constructs the minimal Green-series occupation and proves that the residual circulation can lie only in rows with zero acceptance. It therefore contributes no terminal measure and is removed without increasing nonnegative costs.

This is exact and useful. Its limitation is that the finite primary driver is assumed rather than derived from a general hidden experiment.

## 4. Raw projection and calibration

### 4.1 Exact common primary row

The assumption

```text
Law_theta(J_t,Y_t | H_(t-1),A_t=a)=P_ia
```

is sufficient to identify the acquired report-count measure exactly as `x_ia P_ia`. This step is correct and carries much of the theorem's tractability.

It also means that model uncertainty, hidden-state evolution, and `V` do not influence the primary mode/report flow. A natural experiment with history-dependent gate success or posterior-dependent primary reports is outside the theorem unless the observable mode is enlarged to restore exact closure.

The assumption should appear in the theorem title or first sentence. The paper would benefit from a counterexample showing precisely how the flow reduction fails when two histories in one visible mode have different primary rows.

### 4.2 History-uniform calibration

The certificate

```text
a_v w <= v-B_0 <= A_v w,
||mu-f||^2 <= a_b w
```

holds after the full current report, including `V`. It therefore survives arbitrary stopping selection based on `V`. The risk sandwich and its constants are correct.

The hypothesis is strong. Repeated informative rejections may drive residual uncertainty below every current-report mark. The displayed realizations avoid this through a fixed positivity-improving floor `q`. A more conceptual version should distinguish primitive minorization, exact task identities, externally supplied calibration, and failure of all history-uniform marks.

## 5. Actual flow induced by arbitrary procedures

For each model and legal procedure, define expected pre-call counts `x_ia` and terminal primary-report submeasures `zeta_ia`. Predictability and the common primary row give

```text
acquired report-count measure = x_ia P_ia.
```

The terminal submeasure is dominated by it, hence has density `h_ia` with `0<=h_ia<=x_ia`. The visit balance correctly counts only continued reports as new live visits. Almost-sure termination gives terminal mass one, and positive call cost bounds all counts.

The lower is modelwise. Although a common program may induce different flows under different hidden models, every flow lies in the same profile set. The upper selects one profile minimizer and implements it with a common program that ignores `V`. No minimax interchange occurs.

The compactness argument is valid on the finite count box and weak-star density balls. The manuscript should either formulate it with nets or state the separability needed for a sequential argument.

## 6. Exact flow realization and circulation removal

The continuation matrix `Q` satisfies

```text
x=e_i0+xQ.
```

The minimal Green-series occupation

```text
xbar=sum_{n>=0} e_i0 Q^n
```

obeys `xbar<=x`. The residual `z=x-xbar` satisfies `z=zQ`. Summing coordinates yields

```text
sum_i z_i(1-Q_i 1)=0.
```

Each summand is nonnegative, so every row carrying residual circulation has zero acceptance probability. Such rows have zero terminal density. On each accepting row `z_i=0`, so the realized terminal density is exactly the prescribed density. Counts can only decrease.

I find this proof correct. It is the most distinctive central argument and should be isolated as a standalone finite killed-flow theorem.

The paper should compare it directly with Piunovskiy–Zhang's results on finite extreme occupation measures in MDPs with an absorbing state, not only with compactness/chattering papers.

## 7. Autonomous state accounting

Every autonomous observer needs at least one live label, hence has at most `M-1` terminal values. The upper uses one live label per mode plus the terminal dictionary, giving `M-L` terminal values.

If `b` modes are mandatory under one common model and their legal action sets are pairwise disjoint, one live label cannot serve two such modes. The proof is correct for the typed interface.

The lower is not invariant under action recoding. Physically identical operations may be duplicated as mode-specific names, artificially making the action sets disjoint. Conversely one name may denote mode-dependent operations. A foundations theorem should formulate incompatibility semantically through physical kernels/ports or through the minimal legal protocol automaton.

At minimum the paper should add an action-aliasing example and explicitly call the present lower interface-relative.

## 8. Reliability–resolution geometry

The marked law is `sigma=(f,sqrt(w))_#nu`. Rectangle mass of order `r^d s^alpha` produces two obstructions:

```text
b^(-2/alpha),
(bm)^(-2/(d+alpha)).
```

The lower bounds follow from density-limited selection and a union of `m` marked distortion rectangles. The upper chooses separated location centers and low-reliability rectangles at scale `t~(bk)^(-1/(d+alpha))`, where `k~min(m,b^(d/alpha))`.

The proof is consistent with the inherited stopped-experiment theorem and retains the actual mass. The “large `b`” threshold, admissible radius range, and compact remaining parameter region should be written explicitly.

## 9. Metastable gate law

The gate and acquisition counts satisfy

```text
x_A=p x_G.
```

Terminal mass requires `x_A>=1`, so the expected raw cost is

```text
x_G+x_A=(1+1/p)x_A.
```

Every terminal density of mass one bounded by `b=N/kappa(p)` is implemented by accepting on an acquisition call with probability `h/b`. Thus

```text
Psi_m(N)=Q_m(N/kappa(p)).
```

This is correct for arbitrary history-dependent procedures; it is a predictable-count identity, not an independent-cycle shortcut.

The interpretation should be “uniform after rescaling by the active-attempt budget.” At fixed `N`, the problem is infeasible once `kappa(p)>N`. The known `p` is part of the protocol; this is not unknown-rate learning.

## 10. Raw realizations

### 10.1 Hidden sign

Binary transition entries in `[q,1-q]` and sign-report likelihood floor `gamma` imply posterior probabilities bounded below by

```text
a=q gamma/(1-gamma).
```

For audit `Z=Y+T E e_1`, the conditional variance satisfies

```text
4a(1-a)T^2 <= v <= T^2,
```

and the proxy bias is at most `T^2`. This verifies calibration after observing `V`.

The derivation is correct. It is not uniform as `q` tends to zero. A lower example should show whether the blow-up is necessary.

### 10.2 Quantum instrument

The positivity-improving channel gives `D_q(rho)>=qI`. Weak effects have eigenvalues in `[gamma,1-gamma]`. After normalization the state has an eigenvalue floor `a=q gamma/(1-gamma)`, giving the same Pauli-variance lower bound.

The verification is correct, genuinely noncommuting, and finite-dimensional. It does not cover nearly projective effects or rank-deficient limits uniformly.

The paper correctly distinguishes a fixed-model carrier from an unknown-model predictive family; the observer is not supplied the true model.

## 11. Killed-potential perturbation

Reference potentials satisfy

```text
W=c+QW,
U=b+QU.
```

The implementation inequalities make the expected drop of `W` dominate `(1-rho)c_i`. Stopped telescoping bounds cumulative reference cost, positive minimum call cost implies finite expected duration and almost-sure stopping, and separate drift inequalities control actual physical cost and terminal loss.

The proof is correct and avoids the false inference that a small terminal-law TV distance controls expected stopping time.

It remains an a posteriori certificate rather than a general compiler. The implementation must prove history-uniform inequalities and exact action compatibility. A revised paper should give one full finite-bit gate example with explicit arithmetic precision, boundary mass, `rho,eta,zeta`, budget slack, and final intervals.

## 12. Joint calibration/deficiency law

The joint task adds a paid early sign, the independent gate core, a fixed unobservable calibration sign, and a paid final audit. The lower terms are simultaneous: core geometry holds after every preceding history, the erased sign contributes `2epsilon`, and the two calibration alternatives contribute at least `delta^2`.

The upper uses one initial label and three copies of the two-live-state core. The count `1+3(2+m_M)<=M` is correct for `M>=13`. Conditional on erasure, any immediate virtual sign is independent of the true fair sign; the erased branch has mass `2epsilon` and conditional TV distance one half, giving exact deficiency `epsilon`.

This is a correct selected same-task product construction, not a universal additive resource theorem.

## 13. Literature position

The manuscript appropriately credits constrained occupation methods, projection, trimmed quantization, selective classification, belief compression, Bregman Bayes risk, and weak quantum measurement.

The following comparisons should be added.

1. **Piunovskiy–Zhang**, *Extreme Occupation Measures in Markov Decision Processes with an Absorbing State*, SIAM J. Control Optim., DOI `10.1137/23M1572398`: every finite extreme occupation measure is generated by a deterministic stationary strategy, with constrained-mixture consequences. R53 differs by preserving terminal report submeasures and coupling them to a terminal dictionary, but the realization results are adjacent.
2. **Piunovskiy–Zhang**, arXiv:`2311.14043`: continuity of strategic-to-occupation projection versus uniform absorption. R53 avoids generic compactness through a bounded finite dominated slice.
3. **Dufour–Prieto-Rumeau**, arXiv:`2510.04808`: stationary/chattering sufficiency and constrained extreme occupation measures in uniformly absorbing models.
4. **Chatterjee–Chmelík–Gupta–Kanodia**, arXiv:`1411.3880`: optimal-cost almost-sure reachability in POMDPs, including undecidability or double-exponential behavior depending on cost assumptions.
5. **Asadi–Chatterjee–Saona–Shafiee**, PMLR 286 (2025): NP-completeness of limit-sure reachability with a fixed memory budget.

These last two do not contain R53's geometric calibration theorem. They clarify that R53's tractability comes from the exact common finite driver, not merely from finite observer memory.

I found no cited source with the exact combination of common primary driver, informative auxiliary report, history-uniform task calibration, terminal-submeasure-preserving circulation removal, typed live-state lower, and paid gate geometry. This supports a focused novelty claim, not an exhaustive priority certificate.

## 14. Why the top-four bar is not met

1. **The main control reduction is assumed.** Exact common primary closure already gives a known finite MDP for reachability and cost.
2. **Calibration is strong and sufficient.** No near-necessary criterion is supplied, and selected histories can destroy it in natural models.
3. **The strongest memory lower is presentation-dependent.** Pairwise disjoint action names are not yet an intrinsic invariant.
4. **The gate result is a sharp two-state balance rescaling.** It is not a theory of unknown or history-dependent metastability.
5. **The applications are certificate-verifying constructions.** They do not solve an externally recognized open problem.
6. **Computational optimality is open.** Weak-star existence and potential certificates do not yield efficient synthesis or matched bit/time/workspace lower bounds.
7. **The title remains broader than the theorem.** The native result is a calibrated finite-protocol theorem, not a classification of general causal experiments.

## 18. Final editorial assessment

R53 is compact, self-contained, and mathematically serious. Its strongest contribution is the combination of exact terminal-submeasure flow realization with calibrated task comparison and explicit controller-width accounting. That combination appears technically sound and potentially useful.

In its present form, however, it remains a specialized sufficient-class theorem built on a known finite observable driver and a strong conditional calibration certificate. The naturality and broad external impact do not yet meet the standard of a top-four general mathematics journal.

**Final recommendation:** reject at the top-four level in the present form; encourage a focused major revision for a strong specialist journal.
