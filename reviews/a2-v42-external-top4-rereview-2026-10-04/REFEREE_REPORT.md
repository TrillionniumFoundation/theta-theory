## 3. Audit of the exact two-field theorem

Fix `a=te`, `t>0`, and one unknown compactly supported stationary probability `mu`. The only commands are `a` and `-a`, and the exact data are the two whole-plane mean fields

\[
F_+(x)=\int B_a(x+z)\,d\mu(z),
\qquad
F_-(x)=\int B_{-a}(x+z)\,d\mu(z).
\]

A solid start and a free miss both return zero and both remain in the denominator.

### 3.1 Finite prefix occupation inverse

The endpoint identity

\[
B_a(y)-B_{-a}(y+a)
 =\mathbf1_{\mathcal O}(y+a)-\mathbf1_{\mathcal O}(y)
\]

is valid pointwise under the closed-solid convention, including atoms placed on obstacle or swept boundaries. After averaging against the same stationary law,

\[
r(x):=F_+(x)-F_-(x+a)=v(x+a)-v(x).
\]

With `M=floor((D+Delta)/t)+1`,

\[
v(x)=\max_{0\le m\le M}
 \left\{-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}r(x+ka)\right\}.
\]

Every prefix equals `v(x)-v(x+ma)` and is at most `v(x)`. If `v(x)>0`, the arithmetic progression exits the containing expanded component before step `M`; the next point cannot lie in another component because the component gap exceeds `t`. The attaining endpoint therefore has zero occupation. The stability bound follows immediately. I found this proof correct.

### 3.2 Supports for arbitrary singular launch laws

The v41 support lemmas correctly formulate supports as supports of nonnegative measures, not pointwise positivity sets. For compact nonnegative measures, convolution support is the Minkowski sum of supports. Tonelli's theorem passes planar-null changes in the strip indicator through convolution even for atomic or lower-dimensional laws.

For each obstacle `C`,

\[
\operatorname{supp}(v_C(x)\,dx)=P_C=C+(-A),
\]

and the two collision-field components are

\[
K_{\pm,C}=E_{\pm,C}+(-A).
\]

The positive gaps, connectedness, local finiteness, and incidence with `P_C` give a canonical matching between occupation and collision components. The proof also explains why almost-everywhere field data determine the same support components and period group as the physical representatives. I found no missing density assumption in this passage.

### 3.3 Footprint cancellation and nonsmooth Jordan decomposition

The section-based argument proves, without a normal parametrization,

\[
h_{\Gamma_+}+h_{\Gamma_-}=h_C+h_{L_C},
\]

where `L_C` is the chord joining the two transverse support contacts. Combining the three observable support functions yields

\[
H_C=2h_{P_C}-h_{K_+}-h_{K_-}+t|u\cdot e|
     =h_C-h_{L_C}.
\]

For a planar convex body, `h''+h` is the surface-area measure. The v41 proof derives its positivity and shows that an atom at a normal has mass equal to the length of the exposed face. Strict convexity is therefore exactly what is required to make the obstacle contribution nonatomic; smoothness is unnecessary, and corners or singular-continuous curvature are allowed.

Consequently

\[
(\partial_\phi^2+1)H_C
 =\mathsf S_C-\ell(\delta_n+\delta_{-n})
\]

has mutually singular positive and negative parts. The two negative atoms recover the centered chord, and adding its support to `H_C` recovers the obstacle up to translation. I found the sign, normalization, and face-length convention consistent.

### 3.4 Canonical geometry, law, fiber, and periods

The Steiner point of `P_C` places the recovered obstacle shape in the common canonical frame:

\[
C_P^0=(C-s(C))+s(P_C)=C-s(A).
\]

Support subtraction recovers the centered footprint and hence the canonical table. For one isolated component,

\[
v_P=\mathbf1_{C_P^0}*\check\mu_0.
\]

The Fourier transform of the compact nonzero obstacle indicator is entire and nonzero at the origin. Its real zero set has empty interior; division on the dense nonzero set and continuity determine the characteristic function everywhere. Fourier uniqueness recovers the finite Borel probability exactly. This is an exact uniqueness argument and is not represented as stable deconvolution through zeros.

The complete observational fiber is one common translation of table, footprint, and law. The same canonical triple determines every finite-length forward response. Almost-everywhere and pointwise period definitions agree, and the common period group equals that of the obstacle configuration. I found these conclusions sound.

## 6. Audit of the v42 protected linear moment acquisition

Version 42 uses the recovered complete expanded component `P` to construct a rational outer polygon

\[
P\subset Q\subset P+\rho\overline B,
\qquad \rho+t<g.
\]

For `x in Q`, let `m_Q(x)` be the first index at which `x+m a` exits `Q`. Because the preceding point is in `Q`, the exit point remains within `rho+t` of `P`; because it is outside `Q`, it is outside `P`; and because `rho+t<g`, it belongs to no other expanded component. Therefore the physical endpoint increment telescopes to the pointwise linear identity

\[
v_C(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbf1_{\{k<m_Q(x)\}}
 \bigl(F_-(x+(k+1)a)-F_+(x+ka)\bigr).
\]

The exit index is computed from rational polygon membership and is independent of observed bits.

For each block, the controller selects a grid point `X` and an index `L`, obtains two fresh independent bits, and forms

\[
Z=K\mathbf1_{\{L<m_Q(X)\}}(Y_--Y_+).
\]

Averaging first over the bits and then over `L` gives

\[
\mathbb E(Z\mid X)=v_C(X).
\]

The sign is essential; negative records are allowed and must not be clipped. All monomials share the same blocks. Uniform boundary-cell quadrature for translated convex indicators gives bias `C(n+1) ell` for an arbitrary compact launch probability. Bounded-variable concentration and a union bound over `d_n=binom(n+2,2)` moments yield

\[
N_{\rm lin}\le 2\left\lceil
 8|W|^2K^2\tau^{-2}
 \log\frac{2d_n}{\delta}\right\rceil.
\]

I checked the index shifts, sign, first-exit isolation, rationality, arbitrary-law quadrature, and simultaneous confidence allocation and found them consistent.

The corresponding joint bound becomes

\[
N\le N_{\rm geom}(c a_m,\delta/2)
   +C a_m^{-2}\log\frac{Cm}{\delta}.
\]

This improves only the separated moment-acquisition term. The exponentially small tolerance, positive moment-factor conditioning, and fine geometric accuracy remain; the overall explicit joint bound is still exponential and is not presented as minimax. This is the correct interpretation.
