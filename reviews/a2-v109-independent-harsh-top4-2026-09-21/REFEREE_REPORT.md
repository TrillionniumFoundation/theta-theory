## 1. Executive assessment

Revision 109 is a materially stronger paper than revision 108. The change is not cosmetic. The manuscript has finally moved beyond complete-conormal tomography and beyond the low-dimensional determinant-line transversality that dominated the preceding version. Its new organizing result is the exact fibre theorem for partial contact data:

\[
  \{\theta' : \text{same observed second jets as }\theta\}
  =
  (\theta+\mathcal U^\perp)\cap\Omega,
\]

where \(\mathcal U\) is generated by within-contact products of normal polynomials. The subsequent construction produces, for every shared dimension \(d\ge 2\), a one-contact experiment with \(k=3d+2\) for which \(\mathcal U=\mathcal P_{2k-2}\), even though arbitrary ambient metrics retain a high-dimensional invisible fibre and, for \(d\ge 6\), the full symmetric quadratic measurement map is necessarily noninjective.

That directly addresses the strongest conceptual criticism in my previous report. The native information structure now genuinely changes an underdetermined inverse problem in every dimension, rather than only removing the classical determinant ambiguity when \(d=2\). I regard this as real progress.

I also find the paper substantially more disciplined about scope. The complete-conormal discriminant calculation is explicitly identified as classical Veronese/discriminant geometry; Hankel multiplication duality is attributed; the exact-real scalar oracle is separated from the noisy experiment; the quantifier-elimination endpoint statement is demoted to an effective companion result; and the principal manuscript is now organized around one dependency spine.

I did **not** find a fatal algebraic counterexample to the principal partial-contact fibre theorem or to the displayed all-dimensional construction in the scope I checked. The normal second-jet formula, the Hankel duality calculation, the exponent-sum spanning argument, and the local stability estimate are internally coherent.

Nevertheless, I still recommend rejection at a general top-four journal.

The reason is now sharper and more mathematical than in revision 108. The new central realization theorem is an engineered witness for a much more natural unresolved problem. Once one writes the proof in its essential form, the principal combinatorial requirement is:

> choose a coefficient set \(S\subset\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\) of size \(k-d\) such that
> \[
>    S+S=\{0,\ldots,2k-2\}.
> \]

This is a finite additive-basis problem in disguise. The manuscript's choice
\[
 S=\{0,\ldots,d\}\cup\{2d+1,\ldots,3d+1\},\qquad k=3d+2,
\]
is a convenient construction, but it is far from a convincing threshold theorem and is not positioned against the classical literature on finite/restricted additive 2-bases. The paper itself says that \(k=3d+2\) is only sufficient, but at a top-four level that disclaimer does not cure the issue: the headline all-dimensional theorem is currently driven by a deliberately wasteful sumset construction, while the natural sharp or asymptotically sharp geometry is left untouched.

Indeed, the same proof architecture admits much smaller exact examples. For example:

- for \(d=2\), \(k=7\) and
  \[
  S=\{0,1,3,5,6\}
  \]
  already give \(S+S=\{0,\ldots,12\}\), whereas the manuscript uses \(k=8\);

- for \(d=6\), \(k=14\) and
  \[
  S=\{0,1,2,5,8,11,12,13\}
  \]
  give \(S+S=\{0,\ldots,26\}\), whereas the manuscript uses \(k=20\).

The complements contain an exponent of the parity needed for the manuscript's positivity/sign argument, so these are not merely abstract sumset counterexamples to minimality; they fit the same normal-space realization mechanism after relabelling the tangent exponents. I am not claiming that these values are optimal. The point is the opposite: the manuscript has not reached the mathematically natural problem yet.

The second remaining issue is statistical significance. The finite-noise theorem is a valid finite-dimensional Gaussian regression theorem **for the contact values postulated as observations**. But those Gaussian contact readings are not the original calibrated binary observations from which the native Fisher geometry was derived. Consequently the statistical theorem does not yet provide a finite-sample inverse theorem for the motivating experiment. Once the mean map is shown to be bi-Lipschitz, the LAN, least-squares efficiency, and \(N^{-1}\) local minimax statements are essentially classical smooth Gaussian regression. They strengthen the paper technically, but they do not supply the missing conceptual leap.

Finally, the exact revision branch currently contains no committed \`article/v109/evidence/verification.json\`. The verifier is carefully written to distinguish source-bound from local execution, but the authoritative source-bound receipt described by the manuscript is absent at the reviewed snapshot. Thus the source-integrity defect of v108 has been improved at the source level—the principal v109 TeX is self-contained—but the claimed exact build/diagnostic replay is still not closed on the reviewed object.

My overall assessment is therefore:

- correctness of the core linear-algebraic mechanism: **plausible and substantially improved**;
- closure of the main v108 conceptual objection: **yes, materially**;
- sharpness/generality of the new realization theorem: **not established**;
- statistical connection to the motivating raw experiment: **still incomplete**;
- literature positioning of the sumset/product-space mechanism: **insufficient**;
- source-bound execution evidence at the reviewed branch: **absent**;
- top-four novelty/significance: **not yet established**;
- recommendation: **reject in the present form**.

## 2. What revision 109 genuinely adds

The manuscript should receive credit for making a real structural change.

Let \(R_\theta=JH(\theta)J^{\mathsf T}\) be the native inverse-information family, with \(H(\theta)\) Hankel and \(\theta\in\mathbb R^{2k-1}\). At a prescribed smooth contact point \(x_\nu\), let \(N_\nu\) be the Euclidean normal space. Mapping a normal vector \(n\) to the polynomial whose coefficient vector is \(J^{\mathsf T}n\) produces a polynomial subspace \(U_\nu\subset\mathcal P_{k-1}\).

The manuscript then forms

\[
 \mathcal U
 =
 \sum_\nu \operatorname{span}\{pq:p,q\in U_\nu\}
 \subset\mathcal P_{2k-2}.
\]

Because
\[
  n^{\mathsf T}R_\theta n'
  =
  \ell_\theta(p_np_{n'}),
\]
the normal second jets identify precisely the restriction of the moment functional \(\ell_\theta\) to \(\mathcal U\). The exact native fibre is therefore the affine annihilator \((\theta+\mathcal U^\perp)\cap\Omega\), and identifiability is equivalent to \(\mathcal U=\mathcal P_{2k-2}\).

This is the correct abstraction for the partial-data problem. It is notably better than the v108 organization because the observation geometry and the structured-information geometry now meet in one clean algebraic object.

The native realization theorem then chooses a synchronized quadratic image
\[
 q_A(\eta)=((a_i^{\mathsf T}\eta)^2)_i
\]
whose tangent space at a positive point is engineered so that the corresponding normal-polynomial subspace is a prescribed monomial span. The chosen exponent set has a full two-fold sumset, hence one second jet determines all native coordinates.

This is a real interaction theorem. I no longer regard the paper's central mechanism as merely “classical determinant ambiguity plus one native linear constraint.”

## 3. The partial-contact fibre theorem is clean, but much of it is formal once the model is linearized

Theorem 2.2 is one of the best statements in the manuscript. It is exact, intrinsic to the chosen structured family, and gives the whole fibre rather than a sufficient rank test.

However, its proof is also extremely short for a reason. Once Lemma 2.1 identifies the second jet with the normal restriction of \(R\), and once \(R\) is written as \(JH(\theta)J^{\mathsf T}\), the theorem is finite-dimensional duality:

\[
 n^{\mathsf T}(R_{\theta'}-R_\theta)n'
 =
 \ell_{\theta'-\theta}(p_np_{n'}).
\]

Everything else—rank, fibre dimension, equivalence with injectivity—is linear algebra.

I do not consider that an objection to correctness. But it matters for venue-level novelty. The main theorem becomes significant only if the geometry of attainable spaces \(U_\nu\), their product spans, and the resulting sharp recovery thresholds are themselves deep. Revision 109 gives one engineered family; it does not yet develop that geometry into a classification or sharp theorem.

The natural next questions are immediate:

1. For fixed \((d,k)\), which \((k-d)\)-planes \(U\subset\mathcal P_{k-1}\) satisfy \(\operatorname{span}(UU)=\mathcal P_{2k-2}\)?
2. What is the least \(k\) for which such a \(U\) can be realized by the native synchronized experiment at one globally valid contact point?
3. Is full product rank generic in the appropriate Grassmannian once the dimension inequality holds?
4. What additional restrictions does the native loading map \(A\mapsto U(A)\) impose?
5. What is the codimension/geometry of the rank-deficient locus?
6. Can one characterize multi-contact tradeoffs between the dimensions and relative positions of the \(U_\nu\)?

The present paper answers none of these sharply. Instead it provides one convenient family in a regime where there is substantial slack.

For a specialized paper, that may be enough. For a top-four paper, I would expect the theorem to expose the actual geometry rather than stop at one construction.

## 4. The all-dimensional construction is an additive-basis construction in disguise

This is my main new objection.

The proof of Theorem 3.2 chooses coefficient indices \(0,\ldots,3d+1\), a tangent band
\[
 E=\{d+1,\ldots,2d\},
\]
and normal exponents
\[
 S=\{0,\ldots,d\}\cup\{2d+1,\ldots,3d+1\}.
\]

The decisive sentence is that the pairwise sums of \(S\) contain the intervals
\[
 [0,2d],\quad[2d+1,4d+1],\quad[4d+2,6d+2],
\]
which together cover every coefficient index \(0,\ldots,2k-2\).

That is exactly an additive 2-basis condition.

There is a substantial classical literature on finite additive bases, including restricted bases where the basis is constrained to the first half of the represented interval. At minimum the authors should engage with this literature before presenting \(k=3d+2\) as the canonical all-dimensional regime. Relevant entry points include:

- Gang Yu, *Upper bounds for finite additive 2-bases*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. **137** (2009), 11–18, DOI 10.1090/S0002-9939-08-09430-6.
- Jukka Kohonen, *An improved lower bound for finite additive 2-bases*, J. Number Theory **174** (2017), 518–524, DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2016.11.011.

The point is not that these papers solve the present loading-realization problem verbatim. They do not. The point is that the combinatorial heart of the manuscript's construction belongs to an established problem class, and that problem class immediately suggests much denser bases than the two-block set used here.

There is also adjacent literature on product spaces of binary forms and Gram-type geometry; for example Claus Scheiderer, *Extreme Points of Gram Spectrahedra of Binary Forms*, Discrete Comput. Geom. **67** (2022), 1174–1190, studies subspaces of binary forms through the span/independence of their pairwise products. Again, this is not the same theorem, but it is much closer to the manuscript's product-space language than the current bibliography indicates.

The current bibliography has only a handful of references and does not situate the new \(\operatorname{span}(UU)\) problem in either of these neighboring literatures.

## 5. The value \(k=3d+2\) is visibly nonsharp even within the paper's own proof method

The manuscript correctly states that \(k=3d+2\) is only a constructive sufficient size. But the gap is not merely a theoretical possibility.

The one-contact necessary dimension condition is

\[
 \frac{(k-d)(k-d+1)}2\ge 2k-1.
\]

If \(c=k-d\) is the normal dimension, this becomes

\[
 c^2-3c-4d+2\ge0,
\]

so dimension counting only forces \(c\) to be on the order of \(2\sqrt d\). In contrast the manuscript sets \(c=2d+2\). Thus the theorem operates in a normal dimension linear in \(d\), far above the dimensional obstruction.

More importantly, exact smaller monomial constructions already exist.

### Example: \(d=2\), \(k=7\)

Take
\[
 S=\{0,1,3,5,6\}.
\]
Then \(|S|=5=k-d\), and direct enumeration gives
\[
 S+S=\{0,1,\ldots,12\}=\{0,\ldots,2k-2\}.
\]
The complement \(E=\{2,4\}\) has the required dimension \(d=2\). Since \(k-1=6\), choosing the target column with exponent \(2\) has the same parity required by the sign argument used in the manuscript to obtain a strictly positive target.

Thus the manuscript's construction principle already works one clock/root coordinate earlier than its headline \(k=8\) in shared dimension two.

### Example: \(d=6\), \(k=14\)

Take
\[
 S=\{0,1,2,5,8,11,12,13\}.
\]
Then \(|S|=8=k-d\), and
\[
 S+S=\{0,1,\ldots,26\}.
\]
The complement has six exponents, as required. Since \(k-1=13\), an odd tangent exponent such as \(3\) has the parity needed for the same positivity construction.

Thus the same architecture gives a one-contact full-product monomial space with \(k=14\), not \(k=20\).

These examples do not prove the optimum. They show that the stated \(3d+2\) regime is an artifact of one easy interval-cover construction.

This matters at a top-four standard because the main theorem is sold as the new conceptual answer to the v108 objection. If the new theorem is really a first nonsharp witness for a richer additive/product-space problem, then the paper should say so more forcefully and solve more of that richer problem.

## 6. The natural sharp problem appears substantially more interesting than the theorem actually proved

There are at least three layers that should be separated.

### 6.1 Abstract product-space threshold

Ignore the native realization temporarily. Given \(d\) and \(k\), when does there exist a \((k-d)\)-dimensional subspace
\[
 U\subset\mathcal P_{k-1}
\]
with
\[
 \operatorname{span}(UU)=\mathcal P_{2k-2}?
\]

The dimension inequality is necessary. Is it generically sufficient? If not, what are the exceptional pairs? What is the rank-deficient determinantal variety in the Grassmannian?

This is already a natural algebraic-geometric problem.

### 6.2 Monomial/product-basis threshold

If \(U\) is restricted to a monomial span, the problem becomes: find a set \(S\subset\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\), \(|S|=k-d\), with full interval sumset.

That is an additive-basis problem. The manuscript uses one elementary basis but does not investigate optimal or asymptotic density.

### 6.3 Native realizability

Even if an abstract \(U\) has full product span, can it be realized as
\[
 U=J^{\mathsf T}N
\]
for the normal space of a synchronized loading image at a positive globally isolated contact point, with \(J\) coming from the actual calibrated experiment?

Revision 109 gives one powerful realization trick using the sign pattern of the interpolation matrix. This is arguably the most model-specific part of the paper. It deserves to be turned into a theorem describing a broad class of realizable subspaces, not just one exponent band.

A top-four version of this project would, in my view, connect these three layers and establish a sharp or asymptotically sharp threshold. The current version stops after producing one convenient point in parameter space.

## 7. Genericity is proved only locally around an engineered witness

The theorem gives a nonempty open set of loading matrices \(A\) near a constructed \(A_0\). That is enough for the stated existence theorem.

But it is not enough to understand the geometry of the problem.

The full-product condition is a nonvanishing maximal minor and hence a Zariski-open condition in any chart where the normal space varies rationally/algebraically. If the relevant parameter map is dominant enough, one might hope for a generic theorem. The manuscript does not pursue this.

Likewise the global single-fibre condition is handled by excluding finitely many algebraic sign-incidence sets. This part is elegant and probably reusable. But the combination is only used to show that one Euclidean-open neighborhood meets a dense open separation set.

What I want to know is:

- Is the full-product condition itself generic among full-column-rank loadings in the natural regime?
- What is the closure of the bad locus?
- Is the image of the loading-to-normal-space map contained in a special subvariety of the Grassmannian?
- Does generic full-product rank occur as soon as the dimension count permits?
- If not, what is the true native obstruction?

Without an answer, the all-dimensional theorem remains an existence construction rather than a structural rigidity theorem.

## 8. The global separation argument is plausible and substantially better than a purely local germ claim

I do not see a fatal problem in the global fibre argument as written.

For fixed \(A\), if
\[
 q_A(\eta)=q_A(e_1),
\]
then coordinatewise squares imply
\[
 A\eta=\operatorname{diag}(s)Ae_1
\]
for some sign vector \(s\in\{\pm1\}^k\).

The manuscript excludes nonconstant sign vectors by the algebraic incidence condition
\[
 \operatorname{diag}(s)Ae_1\in\operatorname{ran}A.
\]
There are finitely many \(s\), and each condition is shown to be proper. The surviving fibre is therefore \(\{\pm e_1\}\). Properness of \(q_A\) and full derivative rank then give a single embedded image germ near the target, with the two parameter points producing the same local image because \(q_A\) is even.

That is a sensible argument.

I would nevertheless ask the authors to make one point more explicit in a final version: the finite sign-fibre argument is not merely an auxiliary technicality. It is what upgrades the local normal-space construction to a statement about the **global distance function** \(C_R\). The current proof compresses that upgrade into a paragraph. Given how often earlier versions blurred local and global contact, I would isolate it as a lemma.

This is a presentation request, not a basis for rejection.

## 9. The native moment-coordinate derivation appears coherent, but the model remains highly specialized

Proposition 3.1 derives
\[
 R=JH(\theta)J^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \theta_a=\sum_j\omega_jT_j^a,
\]
from the actual square score system and a nuisance Schur complement. That is preferable to postulating the structured metric.

The interpolation argument is also correctly separated from the later loading choice: \(J\) is determined by roots/clocks and the calibrated experiment before \(A\) is chosen.

However, the resulting native class still depends on a special square-clock design:

- \(k\) root pairs,
- \(2k+1\) clocks,
- the particular rational score system,
- independently variable positive exposures,
- a calibrated binary mixture,
- and an exact Hankel congruence after profiling.

The paper explicitly avoids claiming that arbitrary clock systems have this structure, which is good.

But that leaves the broader conceptual question unanswered: which parts of the theorem survive for structured inverse-information families other than a Hankel moment cone? The exact fibre theorem does, trivially, for any linear structured family after replacing products by the appropriate dual measurement span. The difficult part is the native realization.

For a top-four claim, I would want either a much more general structured-family theorem or a sharper theorem showing that the special Hankel experiment exhibits a genuinely exceptional phenomenon.

## 10. The stability theorem is correct-looking but essentially condition-number bookkeeping

Theorem 4.1 says that if the linear observation map \(\mathcal L\) has least singular value \(\gamma>0\), and the normal Hessian blocks are uniformly positive, then inversion and pseudoinversion give
\[
 \|\widehat\theta-\theta\|
 \lesssim
 \gamma^{-1}\|E\|.
\]

This is exactly what one should prove, and the constant \(2M^2/\gamma\) is transparent.

The matching statement that rank failure precludes uniform consistency on an open parameter set is also correct and useful.

But this is not, by itself, a deep inverse-problem stability theorem. It is the local conditioning of a finite-dimensional linear map composed with matrix inversion.

The interesting quantitative problem would be to understand \(\gamma\) for natural loading/root/clock ensembles:

- sharp lower bounds;
- scaling in \(d,k\);
- robustness under perturbations;
- optimal designs maximizing \(\gamma\);
- tradeoffs between number of contacts and conditioning;
- behavior near the product-rank discriminant.

The manuscript does not address these. The theorem therefore verifies that the exact algebraic criterion has the expected stable version, but it does not add a major quantitative result.

## 11. The noisy theorem is a theorem about an imposed Gaussian contact-value experiment, not the original binary data

This distinction is crucial.

The manuscript defines observations
\[
 Y_j=\mu_h(\theta)+\sigma\xi_j,
 \qquad
 \xi_j\sim N(0,I),
\]
where \(\mu_h(\theta)\) is the vector of exact symmetric contact values at finitely many offsets.

For this model, the paper proves a global bi-Lipschitz bound for sufficiently small fixed \(h\), a nonasymptotic least-squares risk bound, LAN, asymptotic efficiency, and a local minimax lower bound.

I believe this is internally coherent. The use of the **exact** finite-offset mean, rather than its quadratic Taylor approximation, correctly avoids fixed-\(h\) bias. The compact convex parameter set and uniform analytic tube are used to upgrade local derivative control to global injectivity.

But the experiment is invented at the contact-function level.

The motivating statistical model is the calibrated binary observation law from which the Fisher metric is derived. A reader naturally asks:

> How are the contact values \(C_{R_\theta}(b_0\pm hv_a)\) observed from a finite sample of those binary data?

Revision 109 does not answer this. It assumes those contact values are available with additive Gaussian noise.

That is a legitimate secondary observation model, but it should not be confused with a finite-sample theorem for the original experiment. Once the exact mean map is a smooth injective finite-dimensional map and the noise is Gaussian, the LAN and efficient nonlinear least-squares theory is classical.

Therefore R108.4 is only **partly** closed. The paper now has a real noisy theorem, but not yet a statistical inverse theorem for the underlying calibrated experiment.

For top-four significance, I would need one of the following:

1. derive the contact readings from the original binary/categorical sample with an explicit finite-sample procedure and propagate its error through the inverse map; or
2. formulate a more intrinsic statistical experiment in which the contact functional arises as an estimable quantity and prove its minimax rate; or
3. demote the Gaussian-contact theorem to a robustness appendix and make the algebraic/geometric theorem substantially sharper.

## 12. The \(N^{-1}\) minimax statement is classical after injectivity is established

The local asymptotic information
\[
 I_h(\theta_0)
 =
 \sigma^{-2}D\mu_h(\theta_0)^{\mathsf T}D\mu_h(\theta_0)
\]
is positive because the mean map has full rank.

The asymptotic covariance of least squares and the local asymptotic minimax risk
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{tr}I_h(\theta_0)^{-1}}{N}
\]
then follow from standard smooth Gaussian regression/LAN theory.

This is mathematically appropriate and the manuscript now credits van der Vaart rather than presenting LAN as a new principle.

But it also means the statistical section should not be used to inflate the novelty accounting. The nontrivial part is the geometric proof that \(D\mu_h\) remains injective for small \(h\), not the \(N^{-1}\) conclusion itself.

A stronger paper would identify a nonstandard rate or singular asymptotic regime near \(\gamma=0\), where higher contact orders start carrying information. The final remark gestures toward that phenomenon but does not develop it.

That singular regime is potentially much more interesting than the regular LAN theorem currently proved.

## 13. The manuscript now handles the classical discriminant material correctly

This is one place where revision 109 fully resolves a previous objection.

Section 5 explicitly identifies the rank-one cone with the quadratic Veronese geometry and the dual discriminant with the determinant hypersurface. It cites GKZ. The paper no longer presents the degree-two vanishing calculation as a new universal conormal phenomenon.

Likewise, the Hankel/polynomial-multiplication relation is attributed to Mourrain–Pan.

I would keep the elementary coordinate proofs because they make the paper self-contained. But the novelty accounting is now much more honest.

R108.1 is closed to my satisfaction.

## 14. The higher-dimensional native interaction objection from v108 is materially closed

R108 criticized the fact that in \(d\ge3\), complete conormal data already recovered an arbitrary ambient metric, making the native Hankel restriction irrelevant.

Revision 109 fixes this by changing the observation category.

At **one** contact point, arbitrary ambient positive matrices have invisible dimension
\[
 \frac{d(2k-d+1)}2,
\]
which becomes \(5d(d+1)/2\) at \(k=3d+2\). The native structure can nevertheless identify all \(2k-1\) moment coordinates.

For \(d\ge6\), the measurement map on \(\operatorname{Sym}_d\) is dimensionally noninjective, so the construction also leaves the old full-symmetric phase-retrieval regime.

This is exactly the kind of change I asked for in R108.2/R108.3. It should be recognized as a substantive success.

My rejection is therefore **not** a repetition of the v108 criticism. The problem has moved: the new interaction theorem exists, but the theorem is not sharp or structurally developed enough for the target venue.

## 15. The paper should formulate the rank-deficient locus as a geometric object

The condition
\[
 \operatorname{span}(UU)=\mathcal P_{2k-2}
\]
is the nonvanishing of maximal minors of a multiplication matrix.

Thus the bad locus is determinantal.

This invites a much stronger geometric treatment:

- degree/codimension of the bad locus in the appropriate Grassmannian;
- irreducible components;
- intersection with the realizable normal-space image of the loading map;
- transversality/genericity;
- singular strata corresponding to partial recovery;
- conditioning as distance to that determinantal locus.

This would connect the exact fibre theorem and the stability theorem conceptually: \(\gamma\) is a quantitative distance-to-degeneracy parameter.

At present the paper observes openness and then moves on. That leaves much of the natural geometry unexplored.

For a project titled around “tomography through polynomial multiplication,” this determinantal geometry should be central.

## 16. The exact scalar query theorem remains a dimension theorem for a bespoke oracle

The manuscript now states the scope correctly.

Under full product rank, the moment dual space has dimension \(2k-1\). Polarization lets one choose \(2k-1\) exact normal quadratic queries spanning that dual. Fewer queries leave a common kernel at an interior point, and adaptivity does not remove the deterministic dimension obstruction.

This is fine.

It is not a statistical sample complexity theorem, and the manuscript mostly refrains from saying otherwise.

I would keep it as a corollary, not as a major pillar of the paper.

## 17. The reciprocal-fibre and endpoint-QE material is now appropriately secondary

The reciprocal fixed-budget fibre discussion is mathematically clean. Strict convexity on a two-dimensional affine weight fibre gives circular level sets, and the recovered coordinates transport them into observable space.

The endpoint representation statement is also now honestly described as a fixed-dimension semialgebraic decision/construction result obtained from KKT conditions and real quantifier elimination.

These are useful preserved consequences.

Neither changes my venue-level assessment. I agree with the present manuscript's decision not to make them the central novelty claim.

The principal bibliography is currently extremely short relative to the territories touched:

## 20. Independent exact checks and stress tests

I performed several independent checks of the displayed mechanism.

### 20.1 Product-space coverage

For the manuscript's set
\[
 S=\{0,\ldots,d\}\cup\{2d+1,\ldots,3d+1\},
\]
the sumset indeed covers every exponent \(0,\ldots,6d+2=2k-2\). Thus the monomial product span has rank \(2k-1\).

### 20.2 Ambient fibre dimension

At one contact point of a \(d\)-dimensional submanifold of \(\mathbb R^k\), fixing the normal-normal block leaves
\[
 \frac{k(k+1)-(k-d)(k-d+1)}2
 =
 \frac{d(2k-d+1)}2
\]
ambient symmetric directions. Substituting \(k=3d+2\) gives \(5d(d+1)/2\), as claimed.

### 20.3 Noninjective threshold

For \(k=3d+2\), the map
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}_d\to\mathbb R^k
\]
cannot be injective once \(3d+2<d(d+1)/2\), whose first integer solution is \(d=6\). This threshold is correct.

### 20.4 Smaller exact additive-basis realizations

As noted above, the sets
\[
 \{0,1,3,5,6\}\subset\{0,\ldots,6\}
\]
and
\[
 \{0,1,2,5,8,11,12,13\}\subset\{0,\ldots,13\}
\]
have complete two-fold sumsets. Their complements have dimensions \(2\) and \(6\), respectively, and contain exponents of the parity needed by the sign construction.

These checks reinforce, rather than refute, the manuscript: they show the mechanism works. But they also show that the current \(3d+2\) theorem is only one loose witness.

### 20.5 No proof certification claim

These finite calculations are diagnostic only. They do not certify the universal all-dimensional proof and do not determine journal-level originality.

## 22. Required changes for any top-four reconsideration

I do not recommend another revision whose main act is to increment \(v109\) with more local lemmas. The next version would need to solve a more natural problem.

### R109.1 — Replace the \(k=3d+2\) witness by a sharp or asymptotically sharp recovery threshold

At minimum:

1. formulate the optimization problem for
   \[
   \dim U=k-d,\qquad \operatorname{span}(UU)=\mathcal P_{2k-2};
   \]
2. connect the monomial case to finite/restricted additive 2-bases;
3. prove substantially better upper bounds than \(3d+2\);
4. give meaningful lower bounds beyond raw dimension counting;
5. identify the gap between abstract subspace existence, monomial existence, and native realizability.

A theorem of the form
\[
 k_{\min}(d)=d+\Theta(\sqrt d)
\]
with explicit constants, if true and natively realizable, would change the mathematical scale of the paper.

I am not asserting that this exact asymptotic must hold without proof; I am pointing out that the current dimension bound and additive-basis structure make this the natural question.

### R109.2 — Determine genericity and the geometry of the bad locus

Prove, in a natural parameter regime, whether full product rank is generic.

Describe the determinantal failure locus and its intersection with the loading-realizable family.

Ideally connect the smallest singular value \(\gamma\) to distance from this locus and derive robust generic lower bounds.

### R109.3 — Connect the noisy theorem to actual observations from the calibrated experiment

Do not stop at additive Gaussian noise on already-formed contact values.

Give a procedure that starts from finite samples of the underlying binary/categorical observations, constructs estimators of the needed contact quantities, and proves the resulting recovery risk.

If the contact functional is itself an asymptotic object, make that two-stage limit explicit and identify the correct joint scaling.

A singular regime near product-rank failure would be especially interesting.

### R109.4 — Broaden the structured-information theorem or make the Hankel case genuinely sharp

Either:

- generalize the native realization mechanism to a meaningful class of structured information matrices beyond the square-clock Hankel congruence; or
- prove a sharp theorem showing that this special Hankel model has a distinctive extremal recovery property.

At present the abstract fibre theorem is broad but formal, while the nontrivial realization theorem is specialized and nonsharp.

### R109.5 — Expand and correct the literature positioning

The next version should directly discuss:

- finite/restricted additive 2-bases;
- product spans of polynomial/binary-form subspaces;
- structured matrix recovery from compressed quadratic data;
- relevant algebraic-statistical inverse problems;
- singular/minimax inverse theory if the statistical claims remain central.

The novelty section should say explicitly which part is not contained in these literatures.

### R109.6 — Commit exact source-bound evidence for the pinned reviewed source

Create the immutable mathematical source commit first.

Then run the native verifier against that exact commit and commit a separate evidence receipt containing:

- reviewed source SHA;
- source manifest;
- v109/v108 build logs;
- PDF hashes;
- exact diagnostic outputs;
- workflow/run URL;
- compiler versions.

The report should never infer execution from the existence of \`verify.py\`.

## 23. Minor and proof-level comments

1. Theorem 2.2 would benefit from an explicit basis-free formulation: the observed bilinear forms define the restriction of the structured dual functional to the image of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 U_\nu\) under polynomial multiplication.

2. The phrase “complete partial-contact fibre” is accurate but potentially confusing; “exact fibre for prescribed contacts” may read more naturally.

3. In Corollary 2.3, solve the dimension inequality asymptotically in a remark. It immediately reveals how far \(k=3d+2\) is from dimension counting.

4. In Theorem 3.2, separate the proof into (i) algebraic normal-space realization, (ii) product spanning, (iii) generic global separation, and (iv) transfer to global distance. The current single proof hides the modular structure.

5. State explicitly that the interval choice of \(S\) is only one additive basis and is not intended to optimize normal codimension.

6. The rational-example paragraph should not be used as evidence for all-dimensional optimality. The manuscript already avoids that overclaim; keep this restraint.

7. The nonorthonormal formula \(B=KG^{-1}K\) is important and correctly fixes a common normalization error. Keep it.

8. The stability constant \(\gamma\) depends on the chosen coefficient norm and contact configuration. Consider reporting a scale-normalized version if quantitative comparisons are later made.

9. The full-contact perturbation bound is useful but peripheral to the core theorem. It could move to an appendix unless used downstream.

10. In the noisy theorem, distinguish “globally injective on a chosen compact convex \(\Theta\)” from “globally identifiable on the entire moment cone.”

11. The proof of the bi-Lipschitz lower bound uses convexity of \(\Theta\) in an essential way. Keep that assumption visible in the theorem statement.

12. “All targets are positive” depends on the coordinatewise positivity convention for the admissible target region; make this connection explicit.

13. The local asymptotic minimax statement should continue to be attributed to standard LAN theory, not described as a new minimax principle.

14. The statement that higher contact orders may recover information when \(\gamma=0\) is important. It suggests a much richer singular problem and deserves either development or restraint in the introduction.

15. Section 5 is now appropriately labelled classical. I would shorten its proof in the main text and move the coordinate proof to an appendix.

16. The reciprocal-fibre discussion can be shortened further unless it is used to derive a new observable consequence.

17. The endpoint-QE discussion should remain clearly separate from the principal theorem; the current version mostly succeeds.

18. The title “Single-contact recovery of information metrics through polynomial multiplication” is much more accurate than the previous “contact tomography” framing. I would keep this narrower title.

19. The paper should avoid implying that “one contact point” means one scalar observation. The observed second jet is a full normal block, or in the noisy model many offset contact values.

20. The repository response letters are admirably explicit about what finite diagnostics do not prove. Preserve that discipline in the journal manuscript itself.

## 24. Final recommendation

Revision 109 is the strongest version of this A2 line that I have seen.

It genuinely resolves the central conceptual deficiency of revision 108. Native Hankel information is now essential in an all-dimensional **partial-contact** inverse problem, and the theorem remains meaningful even when unrestricted symmetric measurements are noninjective. The exact fibre theorem is clean, the realization is explicit, and the source organization is much improved.

That progress changes my reason for rejection.

The present obstacle is that the flagship realization theorem has not yet reached the natural mathematics exposed by its own proof. The condition \(\operatorname{span}(UU)=\mathcal P_{2k-2}\) leads directly to a product-space/determinantal problem, and the monomial realization leads directly to a finite additive-basis problem. The chosen \(k=3d+2\) construction is convenient but visibly nonsharp, and substantially smaller constructions already fit the same mechanism. The paper does not determine the sharp threshold, genericity, failure locus, or conditioning geometry, and it does not engage the relevant additive-basis/product-space literature.

The new noisy theorem is useful but operates on an imposed Gaussian contact-value experiment rather than on finite samples from the motivating calibrated binary model. Its regular LAN/minimax conclusions are classical once geometric injectivity is proved.

Finally, the reviewed branch still lacks the source-bound verification receipt that the manuscript itself designates as authoritative.

I therefore regard revision 109 as a credible foundation for a strong specialized paper, and as meaningful progress toward a deeper general-journal result. I do **not** regard the current theorem package as sufficient for Annals / Inventiones / JAMS / Acta.

**Recommendation: reject in the present form. A top-four reconsideration should be based on a sharp/generic product-space recovery theorem (with the additive-basis structure properly incorporated) and/or a genuine finite-sample theorem for the underlying statistical experiment, not another local expansion of the current \(k=3d+2\) construction.**
