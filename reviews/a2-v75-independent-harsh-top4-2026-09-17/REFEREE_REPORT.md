Nevertheless, rearranging the same results does not itself strengthen them. It is not an unmarked reconstruction theorem, a global smooth-boundary rigidity theorem, or an improvement of a marked-length theorem with the same observations. The paper now says this accurately. Section 6 below identifies the exact locations. This is not a missing premise of the smooth theorem and should not be inflated into one.

**Decision categories must remain separate:** the identified narrative defect requires correction; the central proofs have not been refuted by this review; exceptional significance has not been established to this referee's satisfaction; the entire 369-page technical catalogue has not received a new complete theorem-by-theorem certification.

## 4. Mathematical examination

### R75-M1. The reference twist and relative determinant argument are coherent

**Locations:** Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 2.3, principal pp. 6–9; S2, `lem:v64-reference`, `thm:v64-periodic-relative`.

The signed/scaled reference Jacobi operator has diagonal `k_(i-1)+k_i+m_i`, off-diagonal `-k_i`, and positive mass `m_i=2 kappa_i/nu_i`. The transfer matrix

$$Q_i=\begin{pmatrix}1+m_i/k_i&1/k_i\\m_i&1\end{pmatrix}$$

has determinant one. With `N=nP`, the boundary transfer gives `-W_uv(0)=1/(M_b^n)_{12}`. Cayley–Hamilton then yields exactly

$$D_{nP,b}=\frac{\sinh\chi}{(M_b)_{12}\sinh(n\chi)}.$$

The flight/period distinction is correctly retained. The corner cofactor independently gives the product of negative mixed chord derivatives divided by the interior determinant. The included symbolic tests verify these identities on a heterogeneous positive fixture, not merely on a symmetric two-contact example.

The important step is the subsequent **relative** comparison. After cancelling `D_N` exactly, the logarithm contains the localized mixed-chord sum and

$$-\log\det(I+G_N\Delta H_N).$$

The proof uses endpoint decay to bound the sum of absolute perturbation entries, hence its trace norm, uniformly in the growing matrix dimension. Deleting the middle third costs an exponentially small trace norm; finite/half-line Green differences and opposite-end Green blocks control the remaining coupling. The logarithmic series is differentiated only to each separately fixed order, with summable polynomial losses in its power index. A slower exponential base absorbs the fixed polynomial losses in flight number.

I do not find a missing factor of `D_N`, an illicit dimension-independent determinant bound, or a division of an absolute error by an exponentially small success probability. The argument is compact but contains the necessary two-end mechanism. Its continuum estimates are supported by the written localization argument, not by the finite matrix tests.

### R75-M2. The physical law restores the clock and normalizer correctly

**Locations:** Theorem 2.5, pp. 10–11; S2, `thm:v64-physical-law`; measured-coordinate transfer in Lemma 3.1, pp. 12–13.

The gauged action is not the physical length. The residual time is

$$r=d-E_N(u,v)+p_bu-p_bv.$$

On the fixed positive gate the manuscript places this residual strictly below both adjacent free-flight lengths. Splitting it into the time before the first collision and after the last therefore produces an interval of length `r`, with no extra multiplicity from repeated words. The canonical endpoint Jacobian is `-W_uv`; multiplying by that time interval and normalized Liouville volume gives

$$\frac{D_N}{2\pi\mathcal A}\,\beta_N(u,v)
       \{d-E_N(u,v)+p_bu-p_bv\}.$$

The scaled arclength calculation is transferred to the measured tangent coordinate with the appropriate endpoint Jacobians; the derivative at the reference contact is one, so the exact central twist is unchanged. The unknown graph conversion is not falsely supplied as an observation.

Thus the conditional law legitimately eliminates the area normalizer, whereas recovering area from counts legitimately needs the stated normalized Liouville preparation. An arbitrary unknown reset measure, noisy gate membership, or a single correlated unreset orbit would define a different experiment. They are not covered, and are not silently used. The current statement makes these restrictions explicit rather than hiding them in a proof.

### R75-M3. Action separation and the positive quadratic inverse are not circular

**Locations:** Proposition 3.2, p. 14; Theorem 4.2, pp. 17–18; S3–S4.

For `h=A(u)+C(v)`, the anchored ratio of the two offset laws is

$$Q=\frac{1-h/d_1}{1-h/d_2},\qquad
 h=\frac{d_1d_2(1-Q)}{d_2-d_1Q}.$$

The common positive residual and separated offsets provide the denominator floor. Axis evaluation recovers the two actions without supplied amplitudes or curvature.

For the quadratic inverse the actual Schur relation is

$$s_i=k_i+c_i-\frac{k_i^2}{k_i+c_{i+1}+s_{i+1}}.$$

The positive-part map on the nonnegative orthant has contraction constant at most `max_i[k_i/(k_i+s_(i+1))]^2<1`. Positivity of its fixed point characterizes the positive-curvature image. This provides a genuine global inverse on that image, not the invalid inference that an everywhere nonsingular Jacobian must be globally injective.

A direct two-candidate check is useful. For equal `s`, subtraction gives

$$c_i-\widetilde c_i=-a_i\widetilde a_i
                  (c_{i+1}-\widetilde c_{i+1}),$$

where all factors have modulus below one. Iterating around the cycle forces equality. The source's quantitative pairwise estimate is compatible with this mechanism. The independent rational fixture has `k=(1,6/5,4/5)`, `c=(1/5,79/50,29/25)` and `s=(1,121/50,41/25)`, and satisfies both the forward and inverse equations exactly. This is a Jacobi-algebra check, not a substitute for geometric realization hypotheses.

### R75-M4. The signed jet argument and actual smooth inverse are distinct, and both are present

**Locations:** Lemma 3.3 / Theorem 3.4, pp. 14–16; Proposition 5.2 / Theorem 5.5, pp. 20–23; S3, S5.

The signed cyclic blocks retain the orientation data:

$$C_n=(I+T_n)(I-T_n)^{-1},\qquad T_n^r=\Lambda^n I,$$

with

$$\det C_n=\frac{1-(-1)^r\Lambda^n}{1-\Lambda^n}.$$

The odd-period sign is important; it cannot be discarded by claiming this determinant is always one. The source keeps the sign, and exact odd/even negative-orientation fixtures pass. The finite-jet factorization concerns actual smooth graphs and supplies equality through the required finite order. It does not conclude equality of smooth functions from equality of Taylor series.

That final step instead integrates the actual stationary envelope along local graph interpolation:

$$g=\overline\alpha h+Kh,\qquad \overline\alpha\ge\tfrac12.$$

The key estimate is the constant-one visit contraction `|x_(b,j)(u)|<=a^j|u|`. A bound with an uncontrolled prefactor at every visit would not suffice for the printed argument. Here it follows from one-step maps fixing zero and uniformly satisfying `|X_i'|<=a<1` on the smaller collar. With `h` vanishing through order `m-1`, the weighted norm gives

$$\|\overline\alpha^{-1}K\|\le
 \theta_m=\frac{6a^m}{1-a^m}<1.$$

Consequently the envelope separates actual functions, including flat differences. The finite stationary envelope controls its terminal term before the half-line limit. No interpolation of globally closed billiard tables is required: local positive graph tuples suffice for this comparison of two actual endpoints.

For unequal data, the polynomial jet correction is essential. The source first controls the quadratic parameters and successively higher jets, forms `G^sharp=G+P`, proves the needed forward `C^m` action bound for `P`, then applies the weighted estimate to the aligned pair. It does not infer curvature closeness from a `C^0` graph estimate. The stated `C^(m+3)` graph prior, enlarged positive class, smaller common collar, and sufficiently small data discrepancy remain part of the conclusion.

**Finding:** no flat-function gap or local-Jacobian/global-injectivity gap is established. This functional step, together with R75-M1, is the strongest part of the paper. Its use of elementary contraction does not make the actual nonlinear geometric implementation vacuous.

### R75-M5. Locality and the complete record identify a precise, limited object

**Locations:** Sections 6–8, pp. 24–37; S6–S8.

The equal-area remote-completion construction varies genuine closed obstacles outside the observation neighborhood while preserving the local stationary bridges and adjacent-flight margins. The conditional local laws remain the same; when the free areas agree, the charged finite records also agree. This verifies the converse direction and the spatial limitation of the invariant. It is not a counterexample to the stated local theorem.

At oblique phases, the single-law cross ratio is `R=1-U(u)V(v)` and

$$\partial_{uv}R(0,0)=p_b^2/d_b^2.$$

The marked nonzero slope selects the positive offset. Fixed nonzero slices then recover `U,V` without an additional functional derivative at every inverse order. This needs the uniform obliquity and slice denominator floors; normal incidence is covered by the separate two-offset route, not by pretending this formula remains invertible there.

The finite-clock correction is retained: the mixed logarithmic derivative contains `D_N/d` and the finite amplitude term as well as `p_b^2/d^2`. Substituting the limiting identity as an exact finite identity would be wrong, but that is not what the current proof does.

For the count-augmented record the exact central formula is

$$\mathcal A(T)=\frac{D_{N,b}(c)d_b}
 {2\pi\pi_{N,b}f_{N,b}(0,0)}.$$

Its density and count use the same full gate. The finite density is that of the fitted actual table, not an empirical or limiting rank-two surrogate. The retained sensitivity

$$\nabla\log D_{nP,b}=
 [\coth\chi-n\coth(n\chi)]\nabla\chi-
 \nabla\log(M_b)_{12}$$

explains the factor `1+N` in area stability. Neither an unknown local amplitude nor an unknown curvature has been treated as already measured. The resulting invariant is contact germs, offsets, and one free-area scalar, not the previously unvisited boundary.

### R75-M6. Moment compression and charged estimation retain their necessary error terms

**Locations:** Section 9, pp. 37–43, and Appendix A, pp. 43–48; S9–S11.

For a rank-two kernel `f=a(u)^T C b(v)`, the measured moment factors satisfy `H=LCR_0`, `M=a^TCR_0`, `N=LCb`, and therefore

$$f(u,v)=M(u)H^{-1}N(v).$$

The matrix is not assumed symmetric. Differentiation to total order `m` introduces derivatives of the two profiles, not an additional derivative of the data. The determinant floor comes from opposite signed covariances of the physical actions. The generic cancellation is classical low-rank algebra; the current attribution and subordinate role are appropriate. [L4]

The following negative control was independently rechecked. On `[-1,1]^2`, let

$$f_0=\frac{4+u/2-v/2}{16},\qquad
 f_1=f_0+\frac{(u^2-1/3)(v^2-1/3)}{100}.$$

Both are positive normalized densities with the same four profiles and moment matrix. Their ranks are two and three. Thus compression is not injective on arbitrary finite densities. These are algebraic kernels, **not** an asserted pair of billiard tables. Proposition 9.5 handles precisely the needed distinction: it compares the physical finite laws with their rank-two limits first, and retains `exp(-omega N)` in the final comparison. That defect must not disappear in any later exposition of the result.

For the weighted one-dimensional kernel estimate, an order-`k` summand has envelope `O(h^(-k-1))` and variance `O(h^(-2k-1))`. The grid extension, bias cancellation, and bounded readout perturbation give the printed bound

$$C\left[h^s+\sqrt{\frac{L_n}{nh^{2m+1}}}
 +\frac{L_n}{nh^{m+1}}+n^{-2}+\delta h^{-m-2}\right].$$

The noise comparison is pathwise and permits sample-dependent errors, but only after exact acceptance. The estimator selects a near-minimum from a countable dense actual-table image. This proves measurable existence; it does not supply an effective enumeration or a computationally practical inverse. The count event and latent conditional-mark construction account for all failed preparations without needing independence between the estimated profiles and count errors.

Balancing the displayed terms gives `alpha_1=s/[2(m+s)+1]`, `gamma_1=s/(m+s+2)`, and `beta_1=alpha_1 omega/(omega+alpha_1 Gamma)`. The returning-length floor and the logarithmic area loss are retained. The two-dimensional appendix uses the corresponding dimension-two powers and preserves the normal-incidence protocol. These are sufficient upper bounds under explicit count, bandwidth, class, and small-error conditions, not minimax claims. I find no new exponent or realization-interface error in these statements.

## 5. Conditioning: a useful interpretation, not a new counterexample

**R75-C1 — recommended clarification.** The new affine conditioning example correctly displays one source of ill-conditioning. A second, independent source is the weighted inverse order itself. From the printed sufficient condition,

$$\theta_m<1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a^m<1/7\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 m>\frac{\log 7}{-\log a}.$$

For illustration, the independent checker gives:

| Chosen contraction bound `a` | Smallest permitted `m>=3` | `theta_m` | Sufficient `alpha_1` when `s=1` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `1/2` | 3 | 0.857143 | `1/9` |
| `9/10` | 19 | 0.937099 | `1/41` |
| `99/100` | 194 | 0.995514 | `1/391` |

The weighted inverse prefactor is `2/(1-theta_m)`; increasing `m` improves this particular prefactor but increases the required differentiation/smoothness order. Rarity further reduces the preparation-budget exponent from `alpha_1` to `beta_1`.

These calculations interpret the **sufficient proof conditions**, not sharp lower bounds, optimal choices of geometric coordinates, or an impossibility theorem for weakly hyperbolic tables. The paper already disclaims degenerating-class uniformity. It would therefore be false to announce a mathematical contradiction here. Nevertheless, a single integrated discussion of this order dependence together with gate, obliquity, offset separation, and rarity would make the quantitative content much easier to assess. A positive exponent alone should not be presented as evidence of an efficient reconstruction procedure.

## 6. Required principal-text correction

**R75-P1 — definite narrative inconsistency, nonfatal to the central proof.** The exact-prefix strategy preserves proofs but also preserves introductions written for a different subsequent text.

At `journal/core/periodic_contact_v75.tex`, lines 3–11, principal p. 12, the opening promises an inverse of the complete analytic action map. The principal slice actually ends after the signed finite-jet blocks; the analytic inverse is in the full entry.

At `journal/core/positive_curvature_v75.tex`, lines 3–10, principal p. 16, the opening invokes “the local analytic coordinate theorem of the preceding section.” There is no such theorem in the preceding principal section. Section 4 now proves the positive quadratic inverse, while actual smooth germ identification is completed in Section 5.

The end of S2 and its opening also retain language about the subsequent alternating two-contact, analytic, and global-lattice material, although the principal continuation is now the general periodic smooth route. At the end of S2, “that follow” is inaccurate for material that follows only in the full technical manuscript.

**Required action:** rewrite these transitions for the principal article, explicitly distinguish quadratic identification from full smooth identification, and name any full-entry extension as such. A title such as “Global identification of the positive quadratic parameters” would describe the present Section 4 more precisely. Preserving the historical wording in an archive is compatible with correcting the active prose.

The six `F` references are declared and resolvable. Their actual roles in the central dependency chain are comparisons/extensions, not omitted analytic premises. I therefore do **not** infer from these stale sentences that Theorem 1.1 lacks its proof. Equally, a successful label checker cannot establish semantic consistency of a sentence about a nonexistent preceding theorem.

## 7. Why the placement recommendation remains negative

**R75-E1 — significance, not correctness.** The relative determinant comparison and the actual smooth envelope inverse constitute a coherent local mechanism. The flat perturbation construction shows that the invariant contains more than formal jet data; that point deserves credit. The new organization now exposes this mechanism rather than burying it.

My reservation is that the demonstrated advance remains tied to a highly structured observation: an already supplied clear marked polygon, supplied contact frames, exact itinerary/gate selection, and full functions of continuously varying endpoints, with a phase-resolved collection of laws. Two-offset elimination first reconstructs complete half-line action functions; the positive Jacobi structure and contracted later visits then yield the local inverse. That is a legitimate problem, but the paper has not convinced me that its solution has the breadth or decisiveness required at the requested general-journal level.

This is not a claim that rich observations trivialize the proof. They do, however, matter to the significance comparison. The paper should not receive implicit credit for recovering marks it assumes, recovering remote arcs its own completion theorem leaves free, or solving another inverse problem whose data are different. The revised introduction largely avoids these overclaims; its accuracy is an improvement, not by itself a demonstration of exceptional impact.

**R75-E2 — the literature comparison is now accurate, but does not establish a dominance result.** The corrected Noakes–Stoyanov Theorem 1.1 determines finite planar unions of strictly convex `C^3` bodies from almost-everywhere exterior travelling-time or scattering-length data. The travel-time datum there is indexed by incoming boundary phase points; the scattering-length family is indexed by incoming/outgoing directions. The manuscript's local-completion comparison uses this correctly. [L1]

De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil address analytic open billiards with symmetry/genericity hypotheses and marked lengths. Finamore–Leguil's cited version treats finite-horizon Sinai billiards with an **enriched** marked length spectrum and an isometry conclusion. Neither supplies the present local conditional-law inverse, and neither is strengthened merely by changing to the present data and target. [L2–L3]

These distinctions answer a literature-accuracy objection. They do not establish that the present theorem surpasses those results. I found no primary-source basis for declaring the present central theorem already known, but a targeted comparison is not an exhaustive originality certificate. The appropriate claim is a particular physical-law-to-local-function inverse, not generic priority over smooth billiard rigidity.

**R75-E3 — do not respond by adding unrelated obligations.** The moment identity, covariance estimate, quotient formulas, concentration, and measurable minimum-distance selection are useful components. Their number does not multiply the number of independent principal breakthroughs. Revision 75 now recognizes this. Another observation corollary or another successful workflow would not, by itself, answer the remaining editorial reservation.

A stronger presentation of the reusable analytic principle behind the two-end relative comparison and weighted actual-envelope inverse could help a reader assess its reach. This is an invitation to clarify the demonstrated mathematics, not a requirement to solve unmarked recovery, unknown reset measures, noisy gates, minimax lower bounds, or global remote-boundary determination. None of those is an identified repair obligation for Theorem 1.1.

## 8. Requested response and final assessment

A responsible response to this report should correct **R75-P1**, state whether the integrated conditioning clarification **R75-C1** is adopted, and address **R75-E1–E3** as questions of mathematical positioning. It should retain the actual smooth proof, the normal-incidence two-offset route, the finite-flight defect, and the exact finite-area anchor. It should not declare all remaining issues “closed” by counting new labels or compiling another version.

I am not recommending arbitrary deletion of the technical programme, and I am not claiming that a fatal theorem gap has been located. The required prose repair is bounded and concrete. The unfavorable top-four judgment is separate and is not promised to disappear when that repair is made.

**Final assessment:** the principal manuscript is markedly better organized, its examined central argument is credible, and several historically dangerous interfaces are now handled explicitly. I nevertheless decline to recommend acceptance at the requested highest general-journal level on the evidence of this revision. This is a negative placement recommendation with a substantive mathematical audit, not a proof of impossibility, a formal certificate, or a blanket judgment on every retained technical theorem.
