# Recommendation

## Reject in the present form at the requested top-four general-mathematics standard

This recommendation is substantially more favorable mathematically than the seventh report, even though the final editorial verdict is unchanged.

Revision 24 has crossed the main mathematical threshold that the previous report identified as missing. There is now an honest outer-architecture theorem for the declared reset/feedback class. It is not merely a fixed-schedule SOS certificate. The manuscript gives an explicit one-preparation impossibility certificate, a two-preparation construction, and a common theorem that matches them to prove

\[
N_c^{\rm phys}(W,Q)=2
\]

for every \(W\ge 12\) and for the stated nonempty interval of thresholds. The lower bound allows unlimited memory. This is a genuine physical preparation theorem, not just a promise that a generic certificate could eventually be searched for.

The paper also supplies quantitative finite-architecture certification, an autonomous noisy theorem with explicit finite-time/precision bookkeeping, a substantially improved current literature discussion, and a genuinely focused 14-page article.

Those changes invalidate several central objections from v23. In particular, I no longer accept the following criticisms as descriptions of the current paper:

- “there is no outer optimization theorem over physical schedules”;
- “there is no actual physical upper certificate”;
- “the physical section contains only attainable points”;
- “autonomous finite-memory testing is only encoded and never structurally solved”;
- “the journal manuscript is a 96-page cumulative repository snapshot”;
- “the paper has not separated inner and outer optimization.”

All of these were fair criticisms of v23. They are no longer fair criticisms of v24.

The remaining problem is different and, at the requested venue level, more demanding.

> The new architecture-uniform theorem becomes possible because every admissible training experiment in the physical class is a causal garbling of one dominating fresh experiment. Under precisely that structural hypothesis, the outer choice of schedule can be pulled back to a response kernel on the dominating experiment and then collapsed by standard minimax duality. This is elegant and useful. It is not yet a general resource theory for controlled statistical experiments, and the exact physical result determines only the preparation coordinate on a high-width segment, not the joint sample–memory frontier.

The physical theorem is mathematically real. The issue is the level of generality, sharpness, novelty, and foundational necessity required for Annals/Inventiones/JAMS/Acta.

My current assessment is:

1. the most general new duality is powerful only in a common-dominating-row regime in which the hard architecture choice is converted into post-processing;
2. the exact physical theorem is sharp in the integer preparation count but not in peak width, decision width, the one- or two-preparation optimal score, or the joint \(N\)-\(W\) Pareto frontier;
3. the generic quantitative theorem is an effective discretization/Bernstein/coupling theorem for fixed finite architectures, not a sharp statistical complexity law;
4. the autonomous stationary optimum is correctly attributed to Hellman–Cover, while the new finite-time dyadic estimate is a conservative construction without a matching resource lower bound;
5. the physical embedding still delegates crucial model-specific statements to a large unpublished predecessor companion;
6. the pipeline remains explicitly multiple-root: A2 is independent, B4 and broad C2 remain unresolved, and the eleven-paper program is not closed.

I therefore regard v24 as a serious and substantially stronger finite statistical resource paper. I do not yet regard it as a “General Theta Foundations I” theorem of top-four general-mathematics depth.

# 2. Targeted source-level mathematical checks

I record these because the next revision should not waste effort repairing arguments that I do not currently find defective.

## 2.1 Posterior-predictive minimax dual

The response space is a finite-dimensional compact cube and probability measures on compact \(\Theta\) are weakly compact. Under continuity of \(\theta\mapsto P_\theta\), the bilinear integrated payoff is continuous in the required variables. Replacing a pointwise minimum over \(\theta\) by a minimum over probability measures and applying Sion is legitimate.

For fixed prior, optimization of each \(h_z(\omega)\in[0,1]\) gives the positive part of its coefficient. Grouping the coefficient vector for fixed \(z\) gives the posterior-predictive total-variation expression.

I do not see a minimax gap in Theorem thm:dual as stated.

## 2.2 The common-dominating-row simulation

For the inherited physical subfamily, the current feedback gate has the form

\[
T_g(x_1,x_2,w)=(x_1,g(x_1)x_2,w),
\]

with the private all-on row \(b(p,q)\) having independent Bernoulli report bits and an independent fair mark. This exact pullback structure is already present in the inherited feedback-completion argument.

Given \(N\) independent all-on draws, simulating each requested feedback row by applying the corresponding parameter-independent garbling to the next unused all-on draw is valid. Stopping simply leaves some generated draws unused. Conditioning on independent auditor randomness and then averaging produces a response kernel \(h_z\).

Under these hypotheses, I do not see a defect in the architecture-uniform upper bound.

The phrase “under these hypotheses” is important and becomes the central conceptual issue below.

## 2.3 One-preparation rational certificate

I independently reconstructed the displayed coefficient matrix from the stated support points, weights, and ideal target. The entries agree with the manuscript.

The sum of all positive entries is exactly

\[
\frac{4035777}{10240000}=u_1.
\]

The manuscript is also correct to emphasize that the four-point prior is a dual certificate and is not itself a legal simulator resampled between preparations.

At \(\beta=1/300\) and \(c=2/5\), the lower-end separation margin is exactly

\[
\frac25-u_1-\frac1{300}
=\frac{78269}{30720000}>0.
\]

Thus the certificate really does exclude every at-most-one-preparation auditor covered by the outer theorem at the physical threshold.

## 2.4 Two-preparation polynomial minimum

The five-event selector produces the stated polynomial \(H(u,v)\). The derivative

\[
\partial_v H(u,v)
=\frac{3(u-v)^2-8u+4v+15}{32}
\]

is bounded below by \(7/32\) on the feasible region used in the proof, so minimizing in \(v\) at \(v=0\) is justified.

The remaining one-variable cubic has its relevant interior critical point at \(u=2-\sqrt3\), and substitution gives

\[
m_2=\frac{12-3\sqrt3}{16}.
\]

The manuscript correctly says this is the exact minimum for the specified selector, not the optimum over all two-preparation selectors.

I do not see an algebraic flaw in the main separation calculation.

## 2.5 Residual-state compilation

The residual-function argument is the right way to justify state merging. The paper does not merely count syntactic labels; it checks closure of the identified residuals under subsequent observations.

The twelve-state peak is therefore a genuine implementation bound for this selector. The five event labels and the seven final residual-difference functions are internally consistent with the event table.

I have not found a hidden free selector or a hidden uncharged first-bit buffer in this compilation.

## 2.6 Quantitative Bernstein subdivision theorem

The key proof idea is sound. Conditioning independent vertex lists on their count vectors gives the Bernstein coefficients. The resulting without-replacement row sequence need not itself be an admissible uncharged-memory controller; it is being used as a coefficient representation, and the manuscript says so.

A first-disagreement coupling over at most \(H\) controller updates gives a total-variation error of order \(HS/m\), and a reward in \([-1,1]\) doubles the path-law variation in the expected-reward estimate. This supports the advertised \(HS/m\) coefficient control.

The inequalities

\[
L_m\le V(A)\le U_m\le L_m+\frac{HS}{m}
\]

then follow in the stated finite-parameter, finite-architecture setting.

I do not see a shared-row independence error in the proof.

## 2.7 Autonomous stationary theorem

The Markov-chain-tree likelihood-ratio argument yields an oscillation bound of \(r^{2(W-1)}\) for stationary state likelihood ratios. The elementary likelihood-range lemma then gives the stationary total-variation bound corresponding to minimax error

\[
\frac{1}{1+r^{W-1}}.
\]

The matching birth–death approximation is consistent with the classical Hellman–Cover formula, as the paper itself states.

The common-minorization estimate gives a valid, if very conservative, finite-time convergence term. I do not see a contradiction in the stated upper construction.

## 2.8 No fatal counterexample found in the new core

After these targeted checks, I do not currently have a fatal counterexample to the 14-page v24 proof chain.

That fact matters. My rejection is principally a judgment about structural depth, generality, sharpness, self-containedness, and top-four significance—not a disguised claim that the displayed rational table is wrong.

# 3. The central remaining issue: architecture-uniformity is bought by a dominating experiment

The most important theorem in v24 is the dominating-row converse. It is also the theorem whose hypothesis most sharply limits the foundational interpretation.

The theorem assumes that every permitted training row is a parameter-independent causal garbling of one fresh draw from a common experiment \(P_\theta\), and that the validation rows are the corresponding garblings of the candidate and target laws.

Under this assumption, the outer architecture problem simplifies dramatically.

The controller can choose feedback schedules, stop adaptively, randomize, and maintain arbitrary memory, but none of these choices can create an experiment more informative about \(\theta\) than the pre-generated all-on samples. The entire adaptive architecture can therefore be pulled back to a response kernel on \(P_\theta^{\otimes N}\).

This is elegant. It is exactly why the proof succeeds.

It is also much narrower than a general theory of controlled statistical experiments.

In a genuinely controlled experiment, different actions may generate statistically incomparable experiments. There may be no single fresh row from which every action is obtained by parameter-independent garbling. The action sequence can then alter the information geometry rather than merely post-process a common dominating sample.

Those are precisely the settings in which adaptive experimental design, controlled sensing, active learning, and causal comparison become structurally difficult.

V24 does not solve that problem.

The paper's own proof-status file essentially acknowledges this by leaving a generic controlled \(K\)-state minimax recursion unproved. The current theorem should therefore be understood as a strong theorem for a common-dominating-row reset class, not as a general elimination of architecture from causal finite-resource statistics.

For a specialized paper, that is entirely respectable.

For a paper titled “General Theta Foundations I,” the distinction is decisive.

# 4. The physical theorem is exact in preparation count, not a joint resource frontier

The headline equation is mathematically correct within its declared region:

\[
N_c^{\rm phys}(W,Q)=2,\qquad W\ge12.
\]

But the interpretation must be exact.

The lower bound excludes one preparation even with unlimited memory. It therefore gives no information that distinguishes \(W=12\) from \(W=10^6\).

The upper bound gives one particular implementation at peak twelve. The paper does not prove:

- that twelve is the least feasible peak at \(N=2\);
- that five decision labels are necessary at \(N=2\);
- the value for any \(3\le W\le11\);
- the optimal score among all two-preparation controllers;
- the exact one-preparation value \(U_1\);
- the exact two-preparation value \(U_2\);
- a nontrivial lower curve coupling \(N\) and \(W\);
- any asymptotic law as either resource grows.

The exactness is therefore one-dimensional: the integer preparation coordinate is exactly two once the width budget is at least the width of the displayed construction.

This is a real theorem. It is not yet a Pareto theorem.

The phrase “sharp physical preparation segment” is defensible if “sharp” is always read as “sharp in the preparation coordinate.” It becomes misleading if a reader hears “sharp sample–memory frontier.”

The manuscript is mostly careful about this. The title and foundational narrative remain more ambitious than the theorem.

# 5. The threshold separation does not solve either neighboring optimization problem

The proof of the preparation theorem uses two nonmatching quantities:

- \(u_1\), obtained from a four-point prior that is expressly not claimed to be least favorable;
- \(m_2\), the exact worst-case score of one displayed two-preparation selector that is expressly not claimed to be globally optimal.

The theorem then chooses thresholds between them.

This is perfectly valid for proving that one preparation is impossible and two preparations suffice.

But it means the paper has not solved the underlying minimax values on either side.

The logical form is:

\[
\text{best one-preparation score}
\le u_1
< c
< m_2
\le \text{best two-preparation score}.
\]

What is not known is whether either outer inequality is equality.

Thus the exact preparation count is extracted from a certified gap between a non-sharp upper bound and a non-sharp lower construction.

That is a clever and efficient proof strategy. It is also evidence that the deeper statistical optimization remains unsolved.

A top-four-level structural theorem would be more compelling if it identified one of the following:

1. the exact \(U_N\) for a nontrivial range of \(N\);
2. the least-favorable prior and a matching optimal selector;
3. a general invariant characterizing when \(N\) preparations cross a threshold;
4. an asymptotic law with matching constants or exponents.

At present the threshold \(2/5\) and the chosen certificate are very tightly adapted to the existing physical task.

# 6. Width twelve is an implementation achievement, not yet a resource obstruction

The residual-state compilation is one of the cleanest parts of v24.

But the number twelve enters only on the constructive side.

There is no theorem saying that a two-preparation successful controller needs twelve states, eleven states, five states, or any nontrivial number above the inherited decision obstruction.

Consequently the current result does not yet explain a sample–memory tradeoff. It explains how much memory one successful two-preparation audit happens to use.

The missing theorem would couple the two resources.

For example, any one of the following would materially deepen the paper:

- prove \(N_c^{\rm phys}(W,Q)>2\) for some interval \(W\le W_0\);
- determine the least peak width at \(N=2\);
- prove a lower bound \(W\ge f(N)\) on a range of \(N\);
- prove a matching or constant-factor Pareto curve;
- prove that reducing the decision alphabet forces additional preparations.

Without such a result, the paper's strongest exact resource theorem is still fundamentally a sample-complexity theorem with a finite-memory implementation attached.

# 7. The posterior-predictive dual is useful, but its top-four novelty burden remains high

The identity

\[
U_N(P,Q)
=
\min_\mu
\sum_z m_\mu(z)\,
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(Q,\overline P_\mu(\cdot\mid z)\right)
\]

is conceptually attractive. It gives the dual variables a genuine statistical meaning: a prior and its posterior-predictive validation law.

That is a significant improvement over generic SOS multipliers.

However, the proof is short because the mechanism is classical:

- convexify the adversarial parameter by probability measures;
- apply a minimax theorem;
- optimize coordinatewise over \([0,1]\);
- recognize total variation.

The genuinely new burden is therefore not the existence of the algebraic identity but the structural range in which it produces new mathematics.

In v24, that range is the common-dominating-row reset class and one carefully engineered physical consumer.

I would not regard the positive-part/Sion identity by itself as a top-four general statistical duality theorem. To reach that level, I would expect substantially more extraction from it, for example:

- an exact characterization of optimal priors/selectors for a broad family;
- a controlled-action extension without a common dominating experiment;
- a canonical deficiency or information invariant with composition laws;
- a nontrivial stability theorem under experiment perturbation;
- a general finite-support theorem with sharp support size and operational consequences;
- a scaling law derived directly from the posterior-predictive geometry.

The current corollary giving at most \((N+2)^2\) support points is useful but still a Carathéodory moment reduction, not such a classification.

# 8. The quantitative \(HS/m\) theorem is effective, but not the missing structural resource law

The subdivision theorem is mathematically sound-looking and more informative than “some SOS degree eventually works.”

It gives explicit:

- mesh \(1/m\);
- error \(HS/m\);
- row-block degrees \(d_r\);
- total degree;
- coefficient counts;
- rational certificates;
- dyadic precision loss.

This deserves inclusion.

But the theorem's mechanism is ultimately a uniform discretization of a finite stochastic parameter space plus a pathwise coupling bound.

Its complexity is generally exponential in the row dimensions and visit counts. It does not produce a sharp dependence of statistical performance on memory, horizon, or precision. It does not exploit special statistical structure to beat generic approximation. It does not provide a finite exactness theorem.

Thus it answers request 24.3 from the previous report in a literal and useful sense, but not in the stronger sense that would transform the paper into a new theory of resource frontiers.

I would describe it as an effective verification theorem, not the central conceptual breakthrough.

# 9. The autonomous section is well handled but contributes limited new novelty

The authors now do the right thing: the stationary formula is explicitly attributed to Hellman and Cover.

That attribution substantially changes how the section should be counted in the paper's novelty ledger.

The new contribution of the section is then principally:

- a proof written in the paper's notation/resource language;
- an explicit approximating birth–death construction;
- a conservative finite-time convergence bound;
- a dyadic precision ledger;
- a fair-bit state implementation bound.

These are useful clarifications and operational refinements.

But there is no matching finite-time lower bound, no sharp precision–state tradeoff, and no theorem that the displayed \(4W(b+2)\) implementation is close to optimal.

Accordingly the autonomous section successfully repairs a conceptual omission from v23. It does not by itself add a new top-four-level theorem.

The manuscript is appropriately modest here. The referee should be equally modest in counting the section as new significance.

# 10. The headline physical connection is not yet self-contained in the 14-page paper

The current article is admirably short, but the shortening has a mathematical cost.

The corollary for the positive-noise collision family depends on inherited facts from the unpublished companion, especially:

1. the exact characterization of the private all-on candidate row as the independent product \(b(p,q)\) with independent actual mark, together with the legality of the feedback pushforward used in the outer reduction; and
2. the uniform physical estimate
   \[
   \operatorname{TV}(Q_{d,\sigma},Q_*)<1/300.
   \]

These are not cosmetic references. They are the bridge from the abstract finite experiment to the claimed physical collision theorem.

The current article sketches the gate and candidate family, but it does not re-establish the microscopic estimate. Instead it cites the retained companion.

For an ordinary sequel paper, reliance on an already published predecessor would be unproblematic.

Here the “Companion” is an internal prior revision preserved in the same repository, not an independent published theorem that a journal reader can simply treat as established literature.

A top-four submission should therefore do one of two things:

- make the physical theorem self-contained by importing the precise interface lemma and the quantitative physical approximation proof needed for the corollary; or
- state the main paper as an abstract statistical theorem and present the collision statement explicitly as conditional on a separately submitted companion theorem.

At present the paper simultaneously wants the physical theorem to carry significance and the companion to carry much of its physical proof burden.

# 11. The literature audit is improved, but the novelty boundary is still not closed

The current literature section is much stronger than v23.

The paper correctly places the stationary autonomous result in the Hellman–Cover line, recognizes that randomization/state tradeoffs are classical phenomena, and discusses the recent Managoli–Prabhakaran work without claiming theorem identity.

That is sufficient to remove the previous objection that directly adjacent current work had been omitted.

The unresolved point is Norberg.

The paper itself says that the proof-level comparison with filtered statistical experiments remains incomplete and that no non-overlap claim is justified from metadata alone.

I agree with this caution.

But the consequence is not merely bibliographic. A paper whose title advertises general foundations in causal/statistical comparison should know very precisely what the established filtered-experiment comparison theory already provides.

I am not asserting that Norberg contains the v24 theorem. I have not established that.

I am saying that the paper cannot simultaneously leave the proof-level comparison unresolved and ask the referee to credit a broad originality claim at the “general foundations” level.

The safest current novelty claim is narrower: an explicit posterior-predictive preparation dual and a matched preparation threshold for this reset/common-row marked experiment, together with compatible finite-resource realizations.

That narrower claim is credible.

The v24 article gives a clear local chain:

\(\to\) likelihood

But then the word “Foundations” cannot derive significance merely from the size of the downstream repository.

At present neither route is fully realized.

# 13. The multiple-root pipeline remains an editorial fact, not a mathematical defect

The repository is commendably honest that A2 is independent.

This matters because one of the strongest potential justifications for “General Theta Foundations I” would be that the paper provides the indispensable root from which later geometry necessarily follows.

V24 does not claim that, and the pipeline does not show it.

Similarly, B4 and C2 still contain their own analytic/operator-theoretic obligations. The current finite-state/resource theorem does not repair them.

This does not make v24 false.

It simply means the paper should be judged as the theorem it currently proves rather than as the gateway to a much larger program.

Under that judgment, I see a strong specialized contribution but not yet the breadth/depth combination typical of the requested four journals.

# 14. The title still promises more generality than the theorem delivers

The current subtitle—“Statistical Resource Duality and Two-Preparation Physical Certification”—is substantially better than the old broad frontier framing.

The prefix “General Theta Foundations I” still raises the expected theorem class.

The actual central theorem is most naturally described as follows:

> For reset experiments whose admissible rows are causal garblings of one dominating fresh experiment, posterior-predictive minimax duality yields architecture-uniform preparation lower bounds; for one marked collision family an explicit dual certificate and a five-event selector determine the minimum number of training preparations above a finite peak-width threshold.

That is a mathematically meaningful paper.

It is also much more specific than “general foundations” normally signals.

If the authors keep the current title, I would expect the next revision to prove a theorem that survives beyond the common-dominating-row class or a genuinely joint resource law.

If they instead keep the mathematics essentially as is, a narrower title and a specialized venue would be more natural.

# 15. The paper should distinguish three different senses of “sharp”

The manuscript currently uses “sharp” carefully in many places, but the next revision should make the distinction impossible to miss.

There are three different statements:

1. **Sharp preparation count at fixed width range:** yes.  
   For the stated threshold interval and \(W\ge12\), the minimum number of training preparations is exactly two.

2. **Sharp width at two preparations:** no.  
   The least feasible peak width is unknown.

3. **Sharp minimax score at one or two preparations:** no.  
   The four-point prior is not proved least favorable and the two-preparation selector is not proved optimal.

The abstract, introduction, theorem captions, README, and response should all preserve this trichotomy.

I would avoid any unqualified phrase such as “sharp sample–memory frontier.”

# 16. The common-row theorem should be foregrounded as an assumption, not hidden as a proof device

The entire outer converse depends on the fact that every permitted row is a garbling of the all-on row.

This should appear earlier and more prominently.

At present a casual reader can absorb “regardless of feedback schedule” and miss that this universality is conditional on a common dominating experiment.

I recommend stating in the abstract itself that the architecture-uniform result is for a **common-dominating-row reset class**.

Likewise, the introduction should explicitly contrast:

- post-processing control, where every action is a garbling of one experiment and the theorem applies; and
- informative control, where actions correspond to incomparable experiments and the theorem does not apply.

This would sharpen both the theorem and the research frontier.

# 17. Specific mathematical requests for the next revision

The following are not cosmetic. They are the shortest routes I see toward changing the editorial assessment.

## 17.1 Determine a genuine joint preparation–memory obstruction

The highest-value next theorem would prove a lower bound involving both \(N\) and \(W\).

The cleanest target is probably the unresolved \(N=2\) width threshold.

For example, prove either:

\[
N_{2/5}^{\rm phys}(W,Q)>2
\quad\text{for all }W\le W_0
\]

for a nontrivial \(W_0\), or determine the exact least feasible peak width at \(N=2\).

This would turn the current high-width sample theorem into an actual Pareto theorem.

## 17.2 Solve one of the minimax values exactly

Either determine \(U_1\) with a least-favorable prior and optimal response, or determine \(U_2\) for the marked family.

The current gap method is enough for the threshold theorem but leaves the intrinsic statistical optimization hidden.

An exact dual/primal pair would make the posterior-predictive dual feel like a structural theorem rather than a certificate generator.

## 17.3 Extend the outer dual beyond a single dominating experiment

The most conceptually ambitious route is to permit a finite or compact family of genuinely incomparable controlled experiments and derive an architecture-uniform dual that retains causal action choice.

# 22. Final assessment

Revision 24 is not a cosmetic response to the seventh report.

It contains a real new theorem.

The authors identified the precise structural property that lets the physical outer problem collapse: every legal feedback row is a parameter-independent garbling of a dominating all-on experiment. They then used posterior-predictive minimax duality to turn a finite prior into an all-architecture one-preparation obstruction. They matched this obstruction with a two-preparation marked selector whose continuum worst case can be solved exactly and whose entire causal register profile is explicitly compiled.

That is substantial progress.

The paper has therefore moved beyond the v23 description “exact finite semialgebraic framework without an extracted physical theorem.”

The correct v24 description is closer to:

> an architecture-uniform preparation theorem for a common-dominating-row reset class, with one exact physical preparation threshold and compatible finite-resource realizations.

My remaining objection is that this is still narrower than the title and narrower than the structural reach usually required for the four general-mathematics journals named by the author.

The architecture is eliminated because the experiment class has a dominating row.  
The preparation count is sharp, but the memory coordinate is not.  
The dual separates one from two preparations, but neither minimax value is solved exactly.  
The generic quantitative theorem is effective but not sharp.  
The autonomous exact stationary formula is classical.  
The physical embedding still depends materially on an unpublished companion.  
The larger program remains multiple-root and unresolved in the branches the metadata itself identifies.

I therefore do **not** recommend acceptance at Annals/Inventiones/JAMS/Acta in the present form.

At the same time, I would strongly discourage the next revision from adding more diagnostics, more historical modules, or another isolated finite example. The v24 core is now clean enough that the decisive next move is mathematical:

**prove a genuinely joint resource obstruction, extend the dual beyond a common dominating experiment, or solve the minimax family rather than only separating one resource threshold.**

That is the theorem-level step that would change the status of the paper.

**Recommendation: reject in the present form at the requested top-four general-mathematics standard.**
