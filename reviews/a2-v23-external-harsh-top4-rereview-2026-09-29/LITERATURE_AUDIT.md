# Focused literature audit for the external A2 v23 rereview

**Date:** 29 September 2026  
**Scope:** theorem- and information-level comparison for the new v23 preparation, discovery, and stopping claims. This is not an exhaustive priority search and does not establish mathematical correctness or editorial significance.

## 1. Local inverse geometry

### Stefanov--Uhlmann--Vasy

P. Stefanov, G. Uhlmann, and A. Vasy, *Local and global boundary rigidity and the geodesic X-ray transform in the normal gauge*, Annals of Mathematics 194 (2021), 1--95; arXiv:1702.03638v3.

Their local and global results concern recovery of an unknown Riemannian metric from local boundary distances or lens data under strict convexity/foliation hypotheses, modulo the relevant gauge. The ambient metric in A2 is fixed Euclidean geometry; the unknowns are reflecting obstacle arcs, incidence, and a period lattice. The data are also different: two selected endpoint-density functions are first converted to a local generating action and an absolute phase normalization.

This source does not obviously subsume A2's explicit two-reflector curvature formulas, missing-area defect, or period-group descent. Conversely, A2's elementary affine elimination and travel-time differentiation should not be presented as a new general lens-rigidity principle. Version 23 states this distinction correctly.

### Gurfinkel--Noakes--Stoyanov and Noakes--Stoyanov

T. Gurfinkel, L. Noakes, and L. Stoyanov, *Travelling Times in Scattering by Obstacles in Curved Space*, Journal of Differential Equations 269 (2020), 9508--9530; arXiv:2003.12261v1.

L. Noakes and L. Stoyanov, *Travelling times in scattering by obstacles*, Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications 430 (2015), 703--717; arXiv:1404.4147v3.

These works use exterior travelling-time observations and derive flow or obstacle information in their own geometric settings. Their enclosing-boundary data are not a finite list of selected three-impact intrinsic endpoint records, and their results do not supply the geometric record producer used by v23.

They nevertheless identify the closest information category for the local part of A2: endpoint travel time and its tangential derivatives encode directions and local scattering geometry. The manuscript now acknowledges this comparison and does not claim that its local action quotient is conceptually isolated from the established travel-time/lens framework.

## 2. Time-uniform inference

S. R. Howard, A. Ramdas, J. McAuliffe, and J. Sekhon, *Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences*, Annals of Statistics 49 (2021), 1055--1080; arXiv:1810.08240v9.

This paper develops general time-uniform concentration and confidence-sequence methods. A2 v23 does not invoke a new martingale boundary. Its proof uses a simpler mechanism:

1. the producer proposes before validation;
2. every epoch uses fresh conditional samples;
3. the finite-list theorem is applied conditionally;
4. failure probabilities are summable over epochs; and
5. a separate hazard bound controls the appearance of an unknown finite witness.

The novelty, if any, lies in carrying this elementary time-uniform device through analytic continuation, type clustering, rational subgroup decoding, volume calibration, and the physical completion defect. It does not lie in a new general sequential-probability theorem. The manuscript's comparison is appropriately restrained.

## 3. Periodic preparation and volume information

Classical Santaló-type identities relate invariant flow measure, travel time, and geometric volume. A2's preparation theorem uses a different elementary mechanism: complete lattice cells inside a large laboratory square project exactly to the quotient law, while boundary tiles contribute an `O(L^{-1})` contamination. The argument is a spatial periodic boundary-layer estimate, not a mixing or equidistribution theorem for a single billiard trajectory.

The absolute normalization is central to A2's missing-area certificate. A hidden obstacle changes the accepted free-position law even when it never intersects the selected successful branch. This is mathematically meaningful, but it also makes the sensor richer than success-conditioned scattering data. Any comparison with volume identities should preserve that distinction.

## 4. Marked-length billiard rigidity

J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin, and M. Leguil, *Marked length spectral determination of analytic chaotic billiards with axial symmetries*, Inventiones Mathematicae 233 (2023), 829--901; arXiv:1905.00890.

P. Bálint, J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin, and M. Leguil, *Marked length spectrum, homoclinic orbits and the geometry of open dispersing billiards*, Communications in Mathematical Physics 374 (2020), 1535--1575; arXiv:1809.08947.

D. Finamore and M. Leguil, *A CAT(0)-approach to the marked length spectral rigidity of Sinai billiards*, arXiv:2510.18983v1.

These works use marked-length or enriched global dynamical observations. A2 v23 uses selected local endpoint densities, absolute phase normalization, local branch gates, and a separate geometric producer. The observations are not equivalent. The new sequential theorem does not produce a conventional marked-length rigidity result, and the marked-length literature does not immediately imply the local density quotient or the missing-area/period-index certificate.

The comparison therefore supports a distinction, not a priority claim. The strongest A2 statements should continue to be called rigidity/certification for an intrinsic marked local-scattering experiment.

## 5. Analytic continuation

L. N. Trefethen, *Quantifying the ill-conditioning of analytic continuation*, BIT Numerical Mathematics 60 (2020), 901--915; arXiv:1908.11097.

The A2 whole-image step is conditional on uniform strip-width and magnitude bounds. Its two-constants estimate has a class-dependent exponent. Version 23 does not remove this instability; it embeds the existing continuation bound in a sequential stopping theorem. No universal or optimal continuation rate follows from the new preparation or discovery arguments.

This matters editorially because the whole-table theorem remains a compact-class result even after the reference table and named witness have been removed.

## 6. Proximity, visibility, and integer arithmetic

The retained Toussaint and Jaromczyk--Toussaint proximity-graph comparisons explain the ancestry of the obstruction-descent idea, while Pocchiola--Vegter visibility complexes concern different tangent/free-bitangent primitives. The strict shortening argument for blocked shortest body gaps is proved directly in A2 and does not require transferring a finite point-set triangle inequality.

Kannan--Bachem and standard Hermite/Smith normal-form references justify finite integer computation once rational coordinates have been separated. They do not make the preceding tasks—complete analytic-body representation, congruence testing, geometric gate installation, physical fitting, or analytic continuation—polynomial-time.

Version 23 correctly keeps oracle work, proposal count, phase preparations, rejected launches, and laboratory aperture as separate resource categories.

## 7. Closest comparison for the new v23 claim

The new v23 theorem combines five familiar ingredients:

- periodic boundary-layer approximation;
- finite-dimensional histogram reconstruction;
- finite-index subgroup saturation;
- conditional coupon/hazard arrival bounds; and
- summable error spending over validation epochs.

The combination is nontrivial because it is coupled to the A2 completion defect and reference-free geometric inverse. I did not find in the scoped sources a theorem with exactly the same sensor, missing-area certificate, period-group arithmetic, and stopping rule.

This does not by itself imply top-four significance. The concrete discovery mechanism is oracle-relative and the probability layer uses standard tools. The conceptual weight therefore remains concentrated in the specialized physical information contract and the completion defect, rather than in a broadly reusable new inverse or sequential principle.

## 8. What this audit does not establish

This audit does not establish:

- exhaustive novelty or priority;
- correctness of the analytic continuation or geometric producer;
- effective construction of periodic gates from unmarked trajectories;
- a complexity theorem for the full inverse;
- a whole-table minimax lower bound;
- exact analytic count-only rigidity or nonrigidity;
- formal proof certification; or
- suitability for a particular journal.

Those questions require independent mathematical and editorial review. The referee report uses this audit only to frame the information category and the remaining top-four significance question.
