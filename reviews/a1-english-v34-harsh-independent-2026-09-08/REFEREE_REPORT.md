## 3. Audit of the publication-bearing collision theorem

### 3.1 The operational experiment is not an arbitrary posterior family

The detector cells span the sparse monomial space and the failure-command cube contains a neighborhood of the constant function one half. This follows from a fixed right inverse of the cell coefficient map. It provides physically admitted all-failure factors for the tangent construction. The future-only continuation definition excludes a discarded-prefix oracle; finite executable products of failure factors span the future test space. For a fixed future report word, an adaptive continuation chooses a fixed sequence along that word, so its likelihood is still a product in the same space. The randomized case follows by averaging an independent seed. [S5]

Consequently equality of the relevant posterior moments is an operational equivalence for the specified future tasks. It is not equivalence for arbitrary losses inspecting the latent parameter, or for tasks that can reread a discarded prefix. The fixed query menu and its revelation after compression are integral to the theorem, not cosmetic conventions. The main theorem retains these restrictions. [S5, S6]

### 3.2 Attained tangent and loss of exactly one rank

At the binomial tuple, put $D=a_{r-1}$ and $z=t^D$. The polynomials

$$
P_i(z)=\prod_{j\ne i}(1+c_jz)
$$

span all polynomials through degree $n-1$ when the positive $c_i$ are distinct. Evaluation at the distinct points $-1/c_i$ proves their independence. Constant and $t^D$ variations give all multiples $t^{jD}$ through $j=n$; variations in each interior exponent give $t^{a+jD}$ through $j=n-1$. The listed exponents are distinct because the interior exponents lie strictly between zero and $D$. The unnormalized tangent therefore has dimension $n(r-1)+1$, and contains the product itself. [S7, `lem:binomial-tangent`]

The mixed-moment determinant is strictly positive for every fixed full-support prior. The determinant integration identity reduces it to products of generalized Vandermonde determinants; disjoint ordered intervals of positive prior mass make the integral strictly positive. No prior density is needed. Pairing the tangent with the future tests gives the required rank. In

$$
Q\longmapsto Z^{-1}\{LQ-p\,e_0(LQ)\},\qquad e_0p=1,
$$

the vector $p$ belongs to the image being projected, so normalization loses exactly one rank. This establishes the acquired dimension, rather than substituting the dimension of the whole test space. [S7]

The continuous-state lower bound uses a local section and invariance of domain. Its upper bound uses normalized chronological factors until future moments are the smaller representation, then makes a single changeover. It does not claim that every curved attainable variety embeds into its dimension. A sufficient encoding is allowed to distinguish histories with equal future predictions. These distinctions are important, and the printed proof respects them.

### 3.3 Confluent flags: why a rank larger than the exact physical rank is not a contradiction

The Newton construction retains formal exponent labels at collisions. Its initial divided differences span complete Hermite evaluation systems, including multiplicities at nonadjacent positions. The functions involved are uniformly bounded at latent value zero because their positive exponents stay away from zero while the logarithmic factors have finite order. The mixed confluent pairing against the acquired tangent then has the needed rank. Finitely many orderings and compactness give uniform constants. [S8]

There are two different ranks in this argument. In the two-parameter example at $(u,v)=(0,0)$, the nine positive formal two-fold labels evaluate to only six distinct exponents. The physical future rank is six. The auxiliary complete confluent flag can nevertheless have a nine-dimensional differential against the degree-nine tangent. Its additional directions are multiplied by zero Newton scales in the physical metric. This is not a proof that nine physical directions remain distinguishable at the collision.

Our exact diagnostic verifies this distinction: the physical distinct-node count is six, while the normalized complete confluent pairing has rank nine at checkpoint $(n,m)=(3,2)$. It is an especially useful stress test of the proof's zero-pivot logic. A numerical test cannot supply the uniform lower singular-value bound; the analytic complete-system positivity and compactness argument supply it.

The acquired minorization is also essential. The proof augments the output coordinates by kernel coordinates, applies a quantitative local inverse argument, and integrates over the kernel box. Its density includes the all-failure evidence. The locally absolutely continuous variables are acquisition commands, not latent parameters. Thus a singular full-support prior is compatible with the stated positive acquired mass. The proof does not confuse a feasible image with a set carrying positive experiment probability. [S8, `lem:newton-attainment`]

### 3.4 Global anisotropic covering

For fixed calibration and a report word, the scaled prediction coordinates are rational functions of command variables with a positive evidence denominator. Prior integrals and exponent values occur as real coefficients. Bounded semialgebraic format therefore does not require semialgebraic dependence on the calibration. The whole image is a finite union over report words, not just the local all-failure chart. [S9]

The thin-rectangle lemma uses identifiable external inputs. Comte–Halupczok explicitly recalls the classical real variation and entropy formulas, and Zhang–Kileel's Lemma 2.18 supplies coefficient-independent semialgebraic regularity. Their respective uses here are legitimate; the argument does not import a nonarchimedean result into the real problem. [L1, L2]

Given these inputs, sections of codimension above the image dimension contribute zero almost everywhere. For lower codimensions, the uniform component bound and the projected rectangle's zonotope volume bound give products of the largest side lengths. This yields

$$
\mathcal N(S,\epsilon)\le C\sum_{j=0}^{p}
 \epsilon^{-j}\prod_{i=1}^{j}a_i.
$$

The separate small-integer-budget argument is needed: a big-Oh covering number alone would not enforce at most $M$ representatives for every integer $M\ge1$. Moving nonempty covering centers onto the reachable image at a factor-two radius cost is also legitimate. I find no identified gap in these steps. [S9, `lem:tame-rectangle`]

### 3.5 Matching lower bounds and a single causal filter

Finite Leja ordering gives uniformly bounded triangular coordinate changes on the nonzero block, and

$$
\prod_{j\le\ell}d_j\le\mathcal V_{m,\ell}
 \le\ell!\prod_{j\le\ell}d_j.
$$

The lower bound uses the acquired mass of an initial flag scaled by its nonzero $d_j$. A union bound on the volume of $M$ balls leaves a fixed fraction uncovered at the asserted radius. Randomized assignment does not beat the distance to the nearest center. The argument remains valid after fixing and then averaging independent coding randomness. [S8, S9]

For the upper bound, the controller stores raw reachable representatives. Updating a representative with the current command/report gives another reachable point. The Bayes quotient denominator is bounded below on the segment between two reachable raw vectors because the segment represents a mixture of their posterior laws. The resulting Lipschitz bound contains no inverse exponent gap. A finite-horizon error recurrence therefore propagates the checkpoint covers using one label process. No prefix tape or divided-difference state is hidden in that recurrence. The checkpoint lower bounds use prefixes of the same exploration law, so taking their maximum is legitimate. [S9, `thm:intrinsic-streaming`]

These are genuine experiment-level steps. It would be inaccurate to dismiss the theorem as a generic quantization bound applied to an assumed rectangle. Conversely, the theorem is an equivalence up to constants depending on a fixed experiment and horizon, not a sharp leading-constant result or a growing-horizon stability theorem. The current title and abstract no longer obscure that distinction.

### 3.6 Collision phases, including a useful boundary of the tree statement

The two-parameter calculation survives scrutiny. At $(n,m)=(3,2)$ there are six separated groups and three possible within-group gaps. The largest two gaps are comparable, by the linear relation among the signed differences. The seven-dimensional term is controlled by geometric interpolation between the six- and eight-dimensional terms. Other checkpoints are dominated by the six-dimensional floor. This gives the printed three-term law and the 6/8/9 dimension pattern. [S10]

The collision-tree recurrence also follows from the pair-order ultrametric inequality and the decomposition of total pair energy into cluster increments. Its power-law hypothesis matters. An arbitrary smooth flat approach need not have finite orders relative to powers of the path parameter. This is not a defect: the determinant theorem still applies without the tree's additional hypothesis.

For illustration, the following is a referee-derived consequence of the printed two-parameter law, not an additional result claimed by the author. Set, for sufficiently small positive $\theta$,

$$
u(\theta)=e^{-1/\theta^2},\qquad
v(\theta)=u(\theta)+e^{-1/\theta^4}.
$$

Then $\rho\asymp e^{-1/\theta^2}$ and $\tau\asymp e^{-1/\theta^4}$, and the three terms are

$$
M^{-1/3},\qquad e^{-1/(2\theta^2)}M^{-1/4},\qquad
 e^{-4/(9\theta^2)-2/(9\theta^4)}M^{-2/9}.
$$

Their crossover budgets have orders $e^{6/\theta^2}$ and $e^{8/\theta^4-2/\theta^2}$. The finite-power collision-tree hypothesis does not hold for these gaps, but the original determinant classification gives the answer directly. This illustrates the distinction between the scope of the invariant theorem and one convenient phase-computation corollary. No new observable jet or enlarged memory resource is involved. [S10]

## 4. Audit of the new finite-cell realization theory

### 4.1 The Blackwell interpretation is now exact and appropriately attributed

Proposition `prop:v34-blackwell` identifies the body as the image of the auxiliary experiment under one common Markov postprocessing. The auxiliary raw-cell index is not revealed after failure; the physical observation law is its mixture with weights $k_k(t)$. The accepted contribution is a separately weighted average, while all failure contributions use the same kernel. Thus the proposition describes exactly the admitted controller, not a relaxation. [S2]

The support interpretation is a finite-action Bayes reward, and the overlap inequality follows by pushing a common failure submeasure through that same kernel. The subsequent weak-star compactness proof and explicit support calculation are valid. This satisfies the earlier conceptual-positioning request. Calling these mechanisms classical no longer constitutes an objection to the revised novelty claim, because v34 expressly makes that attribution. The present report does not claim that Blackwell's article contains the whole finite-horizon realization theorem.

### 4.2 Zero evidence and zero occupancy are handled correctly

The extension permits zero cells and arbitrary latent priors. Conditional occupancies still satisfy

$$
q_{n,j}(t)=\sum_{i,k}q_{n-1,i}(t)k_k(t)A_{n,kj}(i).
$$

This is necessary by integrating the fresh prescribed command and report, and sufficient by realizing each matrix with an admitted transition kernel. Formal coefficients propagate before evaluation, so linear relations among cell products do not obstruct realization. Summing coefficients over the retained labels gives the multinomial identity. [S2, `thm:v33-moment-program`]

Conditional on a positive-evidence full history, the latent likelihood factor and state-path factor separate. This gives the cross-term identity needed for the mean-risk formula; it does not assert sufficiency of the retained label. Completing the square gives the centroids. With $w=\mu(q)$ and $b=\mu(qH)$, the bound $0\le b^2/w\le w$ provides a continuous zero-occupancy extension and compact attainment.

The baseline now uses a second perspective convention:

$$
0\le\frac{\mu(LH)^2}{\mu(L)}\le\mu(L),
$$

with value zero when $\mu(L)=0$. It justifies ignoring impossible histories and integrability without borrowing the positive monomial denominator bound. The degree accounting remains at most $N$: squaring an already evaluated moment is not taking a new latent moment of twice the degree. This is a valid extension, not a change of the main collision theorem's assumptions.

The body still depends on the prescribed command law, and its membership is not presented as a finite list of polynomial inequalities for every abstract law. The assertion is an exact finite-dimensional representation with finitely many prior moments, not a universal effective solver. That distinction should remain explicit wherever the formula is summarized.

### 4.3 Evaluation, purification, and scalarized policies

The evaluation identity follows by padding shorter products with $\sum_k k_k=1$, and by expressing monomials through the spanning cells. It identifies the future test algebra, not the acquired image dimension. The two-valued positive-cell example makes this distinction concretely: its algebra is two-dimensional, and the nonzero derivative of a posterior class weight gives an attained interval when a distinguishing query is included. The derivative printed in the proof is correct. [S2, `prop:v34-evaluation`, `prop:v34-two-cell`]

Purification preserves finitely many accepted and common-failure integrals. Atomlessness belongs to the command law. Preserving every coefficient then preserves every conditional state law, and hence the full mean-risk vector for fixed decoder tables. It does not preserve each history's risk or eliminate a persistent decoder-indexing seed. No jointly measurable purification of arbitrary calibration-indexed kernels is claimed.

The polyhedral theorem starts from a global minimizer, freezes decoders, and minimizes an affine functional in one time-state matrix at a time. Each path uses that matrix once, so the affine claim is justified. Replacing a row cannot go below the global minimum, and successive replacements retain global optimality. The explicit strict/weak comparisons assign ties to the least index, including positive atomic mass on a tie face. This closes a real precision issue in the wording, without requiring atomlessness.

It remains a scalarized theorem, not a convex minimax theorem for the maximum of checkpoint risks. V34 says so. The subsequent fixed-$M$ collision-continuity statement also explicitly returns to the positive compact monomial family. Its continuity and convergent-minimizer argument are sound within that scope. Its all-budget comparison is imported from the principal classification, and the revision now acknowledges exactly that dependency.

## 5. Remaining substantive requirement: compare with nearer collision-spectrum results

The current main bibliography and comparison include classical confluent interpolation, Leja ordering, total positivity, real entropy, finite controllers, and finite approximation. They do not discuss the closer modern clustered Vandermonde results identified below. This matters now that collision geometry, rather than a new general control principle, is the declared principal contribution. [S3]

Batenkov–Diederichs–Goldman–Yomdin study clustered nodes on the unit circle. Their Theorems 2.2–2.3 compare multicluster singular values with cluster spectra and, under their single-cluster hypotheses, give scales proportional to $\sqrt{N}(Nh)^{j-1}$. Batenkov–Demanet–Goldman–Yomdin prove sharp smallest-singular-value bounds for partially clustered Fourier matrices and derive sparse super-resolution minimax consequences. These are directly relevant comparators for a hierarchy of collision-dependent resolution scales. [L3, L4]

They must not be misrepresented as an immediate theorem about A1. Their sampling/bandwidth parameter is not A1's retained-label budget. The observation models and error criteria differ. A matrix estimate does not, by itself, provide the probability minorization of A1's acquired posterior family, its truncation by $n(r-1)$, or the compatibility of all checkpoint codes with one charged transducer. The work on super-resolution does have statistical consequences; it should not be dismissed as having none. [L3, L4; S6–S10]

**Required revision R34.1:** add a concise theorem-level comparison that matches the parameter regimes, geometric objects, and statistical resources, then identifies the precise acquired-geometry conclusions not obtained by simply invoking those estimates. A bibliography insertion alone is insufficient. An exhaustive proof of worldwide priority is neither requested nor realistically certified by this review. I have not established prior publication of the exact A1 theorem, and I make no plagiarism allegation.

The strongest case for the paper is already visible: collision data are converted into a jointly calibration-and-budget-uniform operational memory law through an actually acquired family. The author should make that case against nearby results, rather than merely explain why each classical tool is not the entire proof. This is a specific remaining task, not a demand that another large theory be appended.
