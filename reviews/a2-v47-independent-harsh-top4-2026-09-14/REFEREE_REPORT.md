## Recommendation to the editor

**I do not recommend acceptance at the requested highest-level general-journal standard. My recommendation is rejection at that level on the demonstrated mathematical contribution, not technical rejection supported by a newly discovered false theorem.** Within the fresh proof coverage recorded here, I have not found a fatal error in Lemmas 19.7–19.8, Theorem 19.9, or Corollary 19.10. The concrete calibration clarification in the preceding report has been supplied and should be marked resolved.

This distinction is not a courtesy qualification. Revision 47 correctly handles two potentially serious interfaces: the amplification of onset error by the final flight number, and the passage from coordinate error to hard histogram categories. It also makes a finite joint choice of grid, final flight number, same-flight pilot resolution, successful sample size, and preparation cap. These are actual proved statements, not a relabelling of build checks as mathematics. [S02–S05]

Nevertheless, the new contribution is an acquisition extension of an existing inverse. Its ingredients are normalization of positive-part densities, a coupling bound for grid crossings, the previously established physical pilot, categorical concentration, and the previously established quantitative law-to-table inverse. Their combination is useful and correctly scoped, but I do not regard it as a further rigidity mechanism, a new information threshold, or a conceptual advance sufficient to change the placement assessment. The overall paper contains more substantial mathematics than these four added results; the nonlinear relative-law construction and its signed smooth finite-remainder inverse must be assessed on their own merits. I credit those mechanisms, but the contribution as presented still does not persuade me at the exceptionally selective level requested.

This is an evaluative judgment, not a theorem about what a journal must publish. Another referee or editor may assign greater significance to the structural inverse. It is also not a demand to abandon the program, weaken correct theorems, or add an indefinitely moving list of new research achievements. No amount of source preservation or successful compilation can settle the placement question, and an adverse placement judgment must not be converted into an invented proof gap.

## 2. The actual observation model

The reconstruction target is the complete labelled periodic table and its unknown marked Euclidean lattice, modulo one common proper Euclidean motion. The fixed design supplies obstacle labels, channel and deck marks, and an invertible integer gain matrix; it does not supply Euclidean deck vectors or the poses of different channel frames. The quantitative class also supplies analytic width and size bounds, positive geometric and density margins, finite-order forward bounds, and quantitative finite asymmetry witnesses. These are substantial conditional-stability priors, not measured values of the unknown support functions. [S04]

There are two records in the acquisition result. The physical pilot uses both coordinates of noiseless physical endpoint positions in oriented channel-specific frames, together with timing and preparation outcomes. After that pilot, the law-to-table reconstruction uses estimated gaps and transverse grid categories. Longitudinal positions are not passed into that inverse. Thus the coarsened inverse is genuinely different from direct reconstruction using the richer position record, but the complete acquisition protocol is not calibration discovery from histograms alone. Corollary 19.10 explicitly retains the richer sensor for the pilot and the observable projection. [S03, S05]

Theorem 19.9 permits bounded groupwise programming error. Corollary 19.10 sets that clock error to zero and inherits the exact physical clock and position sensor of Part III. Neither statement, as inspected, establishes robustness to an additional arbitrary per-shot sensor-noise or clock-jitter model. Those would be different experiments, not omitted premises of the experiment actually stated.

For each prescribed positive geometric accuracy, the finite grid and number of observations may increase. The manuscript does not claim exact determination of an arbitrary analytic boundary from a single fixed finite scalar vector. Nor does it claim that the quantitative modulus holds uniformly over the entire earlier relative-C2 open dense locus without the additional analytic and conditioning bounds. These restrictions are printed and must not be rediscovered as purported contradictions.

## 3. Fresh mathematical audit of the new results

### R47-C1 — offset continuity correctly includes normalization and moving support

Lemma 19.7 writes the limiting density on a common bounded endpoint box U as

$$
f_d(x)=Z_d^{-1}w(x)(d-A(x))_+\mathbf 1_U(x),
\qquad A\geq 0,\quad 0\leq w\leq W_+,\quad Z_d\geq Z_->0.
$$

For d and d' in the common positive-offset interval, the positive-part inequality gives an unnormalized L1 error at most W_+|U||d-d'|. The same bound controls the change of normalizer. Dividing by the positive lower normalization bound gives twice this quantity in L1, hence

$$
\|P_d-P_{d'}\|_{\rm TV}\leq \frac{W_+|U|}{Z_-}|d-d'|.
$$

The proof does not differentiate a moving zero set and does not assume a fixed support smaller than the box containing all selected supports. Its density ceiling is a full-box bound, including neighborhoods outside the recorded square. That last point is essential for the next lemma: an interior C1 bound alone would not control probability entering the square through an outer edge. [S03, p. 94]

The onset error is also correctly amplified. Programming at t=j g_hat+d0 produces the true offset d0+j(g_hat-g), not d0+(g_hat-g). The independent functional control accompanying this report verifies the exact positive-offset quadratic-cap identity TV(P_d,P_D)=(D-d)/(D+d). With d=1, gap error 1/10000 and j=100, the actual value is 1/201, whereas omitting j would give 1/20001. This is a diagnostic of the error model, not a billiard counterexample. The manuscript includes the amplified term and passes this control.

**Finding:** the offset bound is supported under the stated uniform forward bounds. There is no missing normalization term and no unsupported differentiation across the cap boundary.

### R47-C2 — the hard-category argument handles outer edges and singular image maps

Lemma 19.8 defines the edge set to include every internal grid edge and the four outer edges of the recorded square. Coupling x and its perturbed coordinate Phi(x), a category mismatch implies that the joining segment crosses an edge. If the displacement in sup norm is at most r, the mismatch event lies in the r-tube around that edge set. A global density ceiling H_* therefore bounds the categorical vector L1 error by

$$
2\min\{1,H_*V_\delta(r)\}.
$$

The factor two is the conversion from total variation to vector L1. No smoothness or invertibility of Phi is needed. The original absolutely continuous law gives zero mass to the old edges, while the image map may put mass on an edge; the half-open convention still defines the category and does not invalidate the coupling. [S03, pp. 94–95]

For the q-by-q square grid, the printed bound

$$
V_\delta(r)\leq 8r(r_1+r)(q+1)
$$

follows by taking the union of tubes about 2(q+1) full segments of length 2r1. Intersections reduce area. Independently, the exact tube area is the expanded outer-square area minus the q-squared eroded cell interiors:

$$
V_\delta(r)=(2r_1+2r)^2-q^2\max\{\delta-2r,0\}^2.
$$

The independent script checks the claimed upper bound in 500 exact rational cases, including zero displacement and tubes wider than a cell. The geometric proof, not the number of cases, justifies the general inequality.

Three functional controls clarify why the hypotheses matter. A right translation of the uniform unit-square law crosses only an outer edge when q=1, giving vector L1 error 2r; deleting outer edges would falsely predict zero. Collapsing a strip onto an internal edge creates singular image mass but still obeys the stated estimate. Finally, for an even q, move each odd vertical column of a uniform unit-square law left by one cell and leave each even column fixed. The displacement 1/q tends to zero, but the categorical L1 discrepancy remains one. Thus shrinking chart error alone is not sufficient when the grid is simultaneously refined. None of these examples is asserted to be a realizable billiard perturbation. They test the general measurable-map lemma, and the current formulation survives them.

**Finding:** the argument is genuinely a hard-category estimate, not a bounded-Lipschitz estimate incorrectly applied to indicators. Its dependence on the grid resolution is necessary in this generality.

### R47-C3 — the calibrated confidence theorem compares laws in the correct order

Theorem 19.9 fixes the final even flight number before the pilot. Conditional on a good pilot history, the experimental design and estimated projection maps are fixed. The actual offsets lie in the prescribed common interval because j v_g+v_t is bounded by its radius. The finite-flight estimate is uniform over that interval; a bound at the nominal offset alone would not justify its use at these pilot-dependent offsets. [S03, p. 95]

The comparison then has three steps: finite law at the actual offset to limiting law at the same offset; limiting law there to limiting law at the nominal offset; estimated coordinates to true coordinates at that nominal offset. The first two comparisons apply the same estimated projection and grid map to both measures. Data processing, offset continuity, and the grid lemma give respectively

$$
2C_0\tau^j,\qquad 2C_{\rm off}(jv_g+v_t),\qquad 2H_*V_\delta(r).
$$

Their sum is precisely the stated b_cal. The argument takes place on a fixed true boundary. It does not assert total-variation continuity of ambient position laws supported on different curves, and it does not ask the statistician to apply the unknown true chart. The true chart is used to analyze the observable estimated projection.

Conditional on the pilot history, fresh uncapped successful categories form iid streams with their actual laws. The inherited concentration bound applies to these streams. Bias plus concentration puts the true limiting vectors in the confidence set. Two triangle inequalities give the arguments

$$
\Omega\bigl(2v_g,\;2(u_n+b_{\rm cal})+C_{\rm bin}\delta\bigr).
$$

The finite-dimensional total-variation/L1 factors are consistent. The approximate measurable selector adds a positive tolerance, and the theorem explicitly requires the probability-error argument to be in the range of the inherited modulus. Integrating the uniform conditional probability over good histories adds the calibration failure probability; no independence between the pilot events is required. [S03, pp. 95–96; S04]

The deterministic preparation cap is treated separately. Each capped experiment is coupled with the uncapped successful stream; cap failure is added after applying concentration. The proof does not condition on successful cap completion and then declare the resulting record iid. The uniform lower success probability gives the binomial lower-tail control, and all unsuccessful preparations remain charged.

**Finding:** the calibrated confidence statement has the required bias decomposition, conditioning order, and cap accounting. I found no invalid change of observation space or circular use of an unknown geometric chart at this interface.

### R47-C4 — the joint acquisition prescription leaves a strictly positive error margin

Corollary 19.10 is the most consequential new synthesis. It first obtains positive tolerances e_* and eta_* from the existing finite accuracy prescription, then fixes the grid, then chooses the final even J, and only then chooses the pilot resolution at that same J. The ordering matters: J is not increased after estimating the gaps, and the chart tolerance is allowed to depend on the already fixed grid. [S03, pp. 96–97]

The printed choices imply

$$
C_{\rm bin}\delta\leq\eta_*/4,\quad
2C_0\tau^J\leq\eta_*/32,\quad
2C_{\rm off}h\leq\eta_*/32,\quad
2H_*V_\delta(C_\chi\sqrt h)\leq\eta_*/32.
$$

For the last inequality, the restriction C_chi sqrt(h) <= r1 gives V <= 16 r r1(q+1), and the displayed h bound supplies the remaining constant. Thus b_cal <= 3 eta_*/32. The sample-size choice gives u_n <= eta_*/8. Together with a positive selector tolerance at most eta_*/4, the second inverse argument is at most

$$
2(\eta_*/8+3\eta_*/32)+\eta_*/4+\eta_*/4
=15\eta_*/16<\eta_*.
$$

The gap argument is bounded separately by e_*. There is therefore genuine slack after selection; the proof has not spent the same tolerance twice. The independent script checks this budget with exact rational synthetic constants and checks the sample and cap arithmetic separately. These constants are not presented as a certified periodic-table instance.

I also inspected the pilot on which the corollary depends. At the selected J, successful observations in a short onset window localize endpoints within a constant times sqrt(h), while the bracketing rule gives J times the gap error at most h. The construction searches a finite time grid and gives a uniform positive success lower bound and a deterministic total pilot charge. Both collision types occur in the same oriented channel frame, permitting the required observable projection. This is an existing physical-sensor construction, not calibration conjured from the compressed categories. [S05, especially the localization, pilot, and observable-projection proof]

The total charge adds that pilot cap to the post-pilot caps. The arbitrary minimum flight number is compatible with all choices because the final J is selected before taking the pilot. The proof does not claim a useful practical complexity bound or an optimal rate.

**Finding:** the finite acquisition corollary follows at the stated physical sensor and quantified-class level. The simultaneous grid/pilot limit, amplified timing error, selector slack, and failed-preparation accounting are not left as gaps.

## 4. Checks on the inherited inverse: what should not be erased by this review

The new theorem is meaningful only if its inherited inverse contains actual geometry. I therefore checked the following interfaces rather than treating an earlier favorable report as a proof certificate.

The single-offset identity uses the density ratio R(u,v)=f(u,v)f(0,0)/(f(u,0)f(0,v)) to recover a separated rank-one product. Choosing a nonzero anchor determines the positive normalization once, then recovers the signed action as a function of the signed coordinate. It does not take an ambiguous pointwise square root at the minimum or discard odd contact terms. The geometric consequence also requires the half-line action construction and the finite-jet inverse; it cannot fairly be reduced to this density identity alone. [S06]

The smooth finite-jet factorization is an actual remainder argument. It interpolates two graph functions with matching finite jets, differentiates finite stationary actions, cancels interior variations, bounds the terminal orbit term, and passes to the limit under a summable estimate. The resulting action difference has the required order in the endpoint coordinate. This establishes dependence on finite jets before polynomial representatives are used. I freshly examined this argument, but did not independently reconstruct the entire weighted orbit theory supplying its estimates. [S07, source lines 298–410]

In the finite Bellman recursion, the order-n leading block is accompanied by a computable lower-order remainder. The omitted term of the stationary-coordinate expansion contributes beyond order n for n at least three, while quadratic recovery is handled separately. The determinants 1-exp(-2n gamma) are positive, and the order-dependent constants need not be uniform in n. The bounded analytic continuation argument subsequently fixes a sufficiently high finite order before shrinking observational errors. It does not exchange these choices or pretend that total variation alone controls arbitrary derivatives. The finite histogram-to-density step uses explicit forward regularity, and the continuation step uses a common analytic bound. [S04]

Finally, the selected clear-channel graph construction is more than a special example. If a closest segment is blocked, splitting through the intermediate obstacle reduces the individual subgaps by the positive separation margin. Induction gives a finite clear-channel path. Finite seed paths supply a connected quotient graph with full gain group; a spanning tree and two independent non-tree gains give the selected N+1-channel skeleton. The quantitative registration uses the actual inverse of its integer gain matrix, not an unjustified unimodular-pair assumption. The lattice remains unknown until common-frame registration reconstructs it. My fresh examination of the skeleton is limited to the indicated geometric construction, not a new proof audit of every later generic-asymmetry argument. [S08, source lines 1–230; S04]

These are favorable findings. They do not imply formal certification of the entire inherited paper, but they exclude several tempting and inaccurate criticisms of the current revision.

## 5. Contribution and significance

### R47-E1 — a correct acquisition extension does not by itself change the structural theorem

The author distinguishes the central nonlinear relative-law mechanism from the classical estimates used to propagate it. This is the correct distinction. The new four environments remove ambiguity at a genuine implementation interface, but the recoverable geometric object, the local rigidity mechanism, and the analytic continuation principle remain those of the inherited inverse. The extension neither replaces the rich physical pilot by histogram-only calibration nor establishes a new observation threshold. [S01–S05]

The sufficient cost prescription makes the nature of the addition concrete. For fixed class constants and eta=eta_*, its chosen grid has q=O(eta^-1) and K=O(eta^-2), and its successful sample prescription is

$$
n=O\bigl(\eta^{-4}+\eta^{-2}\log(\mathcal L/\alpha)\bigr).
$$

From the printed pilot grid and success lower bound, its pilot charge has a sufficient bound of the form

$$
B_{\rm pil}=O\bigl((1+J/h)h^{-2}e^{\gamma_+J}\log(2|E|/\beta)\bigr),
$$

with the fixed class constants absorbed. The hard-grid condition allows a pilot resolution of order eta^4 or smaller when that restriction governs the choice; the independent gap tolerance may require a still smaller h. These are my deductions from the displayed sufficient choices, not claimed optimal complexities or lower bounds. The relation between eta and the requested geometric accuracy still passes through the inherited analytic inverse. [S03–S05]

This is finite-resolution existence with fully charged acquisition, not a newly established sharp statistical theory. The manuscript does not claim otherwise. Poor sufficient complexity is not, on its own, a reason to reject a pure mathematics paper; the relevant point is that the added sampling theorem should not be counted as a separate major structural breakthrough merely because its bookkeeping is now complete.

At the requested level, the decisive case must therefore rest on the conceptual reach of the relative long-bridge inverse and the resulting rigidity of full analytic tables with an unknown marked lattice. These are genuine contributions. My adverse assessment is that the current presentation and inspected mathematical scope do not establish the breadth or conceptual force needed for this placement. That assessment cannot be discharged by adding another routine acquisition corollary, nor can it justify suppressing the existing mathematics.
