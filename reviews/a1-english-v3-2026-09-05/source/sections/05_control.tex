\section{Attained control on the reduced state}\label{sec:control}
\subsection{A decision problem on the actual readouts}
Let $\lambda\in\R$ be fixed. On an accepted raw report $x$ the reward is
$r_a(x)$; on censoring it is $r_a^\dagger$. These functions are jointly
continuous on their compact component domains and bounded in absolute value
by $B$. Let $D_0$ be a compact metric terminal-decision space and let
$G(R,d)$ be a continuous payoff with $0<G_-\le G\le G_+$. The objective is
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\text{admissible policies and final decision}}
 \E_{\pi_0}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{j=1}^N r_j\right)G(R,d)\right].
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Exactly $N$ cartridges may be scheduled; a smaller predetermined horizon is
a separate instance. Stopping after a censoring cap is the explicitly
analyzed protocol in Section~\ref{sec:attainability}. Rewards and decisions
may charge unsuccessful placement, censoring, or the chosen detector mode.
The criterion does not require that a hidden rejected coordinate be known.

Let $n$ be the consumed count and $b$ the remaining count, $n+b\le N$.
For a coefficient state $c\in\cS_n$ define
\[
 \bar k_c^a(x)=\int k_R^a(x)\pi_c(dR),\quad
 \bar f_c^a(m)=\int H_\dagger(R;m)/a_0\,\pi_c(dR).
\]
Write $c^{a,x}$ and $c^{a,m,\dagger}$ for the positive updates of
Theorem~\ref{thm:filter}. The accepted update is independent of $g(x)$,
because this scalar is independent of $R$ and nonzero.

\subsection{An exact five-dimensional feasible body}
Assume for the moment that $V_{n+1,b-1}$ is continuous. Put
\[
 w_{c,a}(x)=\bar k_c^a(x)e^{\lambda r_a(x)}
                    V_{n+1,b-1}(c^{a,x}),\qquad
 F_{c,a}(x)=(v_a(x),w_{c,a}(x))\in\R^5.
\]
Define
\begin{equation}
 \cZ(c,a)=\left\{(m,u)=\int_X g(x)F_{c,a}(x)\nu(dx):
                               \eta\le g\le1-\eta\right\}.
 \label{eq:gate-body}
\end{equation}
All four coordinates of $m$ and the success-continuation coordinate $u$
are optimized jointly. Separate interval bounds on them are not a substitute
for this body.

\begin{lemma}[Compactness, support, and measurable representatives]\label{lem:body}
The body $\cZ(c,a)$ is compact and convex, and for $z\in\R^5$ its support is
\begin{equation}
 h_{\cZ(c,a)}(z)=\eta\int z\cdot F_{c,a}\,d\nu
       +(1-2\eta)\int (z\cdot F_{c,a})_+\,d\nu.
 \label{eq:support}
\end{equation}
A body point is realized by a measurable comparator gate. On the compact
state and mode domains these realizations can be selected Borel measurably.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\cG$ be the order interval $\eta\le g\le1-\eta$ in $L^\infty(\nu)$,
with the weak-star topology. It is weak-star closed and bounded, hence
compact; it is metrizable because $L^1(\nu)$ is separable. Integration
against the five $L^1$ coordinates of $F$ is continuous, so its image is
compact and convex. Maximizing $g(x)z\cdot F(x)$ pointwise chooses $1-\eta$
where $z\cdot F>0$ and $\eta$ where it is negative, proving
\eqref{eq:support}, including attainment and the zero set.

For clarity, an $L^\infty$ class is not evaluated at a point without a
representative. Take fixed nested dyadic partitions on the two circle
components and leave $\partial$ as an atom. The conditional averages
$E_jg(x)$ are jointly Borel in $(g,x)$: each cell average is a weak-star
continuous coordinate. The clipped limsup of these averages is jointly
Borel, stays in $[\eta,1-\eta]$, and equals $g$ almost everywhere by the
Lebesgue differentiation theorem. This gives a common measurable
representative map for the physical comparator.

The map $(c,a,g)\mapsto\int gF_{c,a}\,d\nu$ is continuous: $F$ depends
continuously in $L^1$ on $(c,a)$, uniformly on the compact domains. Its
nonempty fibers over the image are compact with a closed graph. Here is a
direct selection construction. Cover the compact gate space successively
by finitely many closed sets of diameters tending to zero, refining previous
covers. At each level choose the first child whose intersection with the
fiber is nonempty. The test is Borel, since the projection of the compact
closed fiber graph intersected with a fixed closed child is closed. The
nested compact choices have a unique limit, which is a Borel selection.
Composing it with the representative map gives an actual Borel comparator.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An attained reduced Bellman recursion]\label{thm:control}
The optimal conditional values in \eqref{eq:objective} depend on the past
only through $(n,c,b)$. They are continuous on $\cS_n$ and satisfy
\begin{align}
 V_{n,0}(c)&=\max_{d\in D_0}\int G(R,d)\pi_c(dR),\notag\\
 V_{n,b}(c)&=\max_{a\in\cA,\ (m,u)\in\cZ(c,a)}
 \left\{u+e^{\lambda r_a^\dagger}\bar f_c^a(m)
                         V_{n+1,b-1}(c^{a,m,\dagger})\right\}.
 \label{eq:bellman}
\end{align}
There is an optimal Borel feedback controller in the declared ideal
comparator class. The same apparatus, with the same failure records,
implements it. Randomizing the first command cannot improve this value.
Moreover, for every $\delta>0$, finite interval gates and a finite command
menu attain value within $\delta$ of the ideal optimum, uniformly on the
finite-budget compact state strata.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The terminal value is continuous and attained, by compactness and continuity
of $G$ and of the posterior. Suppose the assertion holds one step later.
Condition on the next report. Its accepted predictive density is
$g(x)\bar k_c^a(x)$ and its posterior is $\pi_{c^{a,x}}$. Its failure
probability is $\bar f_c^a(m)$ and its posterior is
$\pi_{c^{a,m,\dagger}}$. Therefore the conditional value of a command is
\[
 Q(c,a,g)=\int g(x)w_{c,a}(x)\nu(dx)
  +e^{\lambda r_a^\dagger}\bar f_c^a(m)
                         V_{n+1,b-1}(c^{a,m,\dagger}).
\]
This expression is jointly continuous on the compact product
$\cS_n\times\cA\times\cG$. Continuity of the failure update follows from
its positive coefficient and evidence bounds, not from an unproved
continuity of a general posterior kernel. It follows directly from uniform
continuity that $\max_{a,g}Q(c,a,g)$ is continuous and is attained. Replacing
$g$ by its five integrals gives exactly \eqref{eq:bellman}.

An optimal command can be chosen Borel. To see this without assuming a
pointwise gate selector, use the nested closed-cover argument of
Lemma~\ref{lem:body} on the compact command space. For any fixed compact
subset $C$ of commands, the condition
$\max_{(a,g)\in C}Q(c,a,g)=V_{n,b}(c)$ is closed in $c$, so each recursive
choice is Borel. The representative construction makes the resulting gate
jointly Borel in state and raw report. Theorem~\ref{thm:attainable} then
pastes the commands into the actual finite cartridge preparation. Backward
induction proves sufficiency against every full-history policy: given the
past, the unknown radius has posterior $\pi_c$, the unused cartridges are
fresh, and the admissible commands and resource count are identical.
Past multiplicative rewards factor out as positive constants. A randomized
command only averages the same numbers $Q$, and cannot exceed their maximum.

It remains to verify the finite-program approximation, rather than infer
it from ideal measurability. For the fixed dyadic partition used above put
$g_J=E_Jg$, with the atom unchanged. This is a finite lookup gate in the
same bounds. For any integrable component $F_i$,
\[
 \left|\int(g-g_J)F_i\,d\nu\right|
 =\left|\int g(F_i-E_JF_i)\,d\nu\right|
 \le(1-\eta)\norm{F_i-E_JF_i}_{L^1}.
\]
On compact state and mode sets, the family of continuous functions
$F_{c,a}$ is uniformly continuous on the two circles. Consequently the
right side tends to zero uniformly in $(c,a,g)$ for all five coordinates.
The success value and the four failure moments are therefore approximated
uniformly. Uniform continuity of the failure posterior and continuation
value proves a uniform one-step value error $e_J\to0$ when replacing any
gate by a finite lookup gate. This argument is not the false claim that
$\norm{g-E_Jg}_{L^1}\to0$ uniformly over all measurable gates.

The Bellman operator is $e^{|\lambda|B}$-Lipschitz in the sup norm of its
continuation value, because predictive probabilities sum to one. Backward
induction bounds the finite-budget value error by a finite weighted sum of
the one-step errors, hence it tends uniformly to zero. For a fixed finite
partition the gate values form a finite compact cube. Compact mode grids
and finite grids in this cube approximate its continuous command objective
uniformly; terminal decisions can likewise be restricted to a finite net.
Taking the smallest maximizing index in a finite menu gives a Borel rule.
Thus a sufficiently fine finite menu and finite interval gate implementation
achieve any prescribed $\delta>0$. These statements establish existence and
approximation, not a polynomial-time global optimization algorithm.
\end{proof}

The failure term is essential. Deleting it would both lose posterior
information and optimize over a different resource experiment. The moment
body is five-dimensional for every budget, but its continuation coordinate
depends on the current value function; it is not a fixed universal polytope.
For a fixed budget the filter uses $O(N)$ storage and $O(N^2)$ total update
arithmetic. Computing the continuous value function and its support
integrals can have much greater complexity.

The four failure moments do not determine the entire accepted-report
kernel, and the fifth coordinate is specific to the current continuation
value. A selected body point is realized by a gate, not substituted for the
gate in subsequent observation generation. This is an exact reduction of
the Bellman optimization, not a universal five-number encoding of every
measurable instrument.

\subsection{Boundary-complete gates without an acceptance floor}
The positive floor simplifies some intermediate formulas, but it is not an
intrinsic restriction of the apparatus or of the exact state theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Closure for the entire comparator interval]\label{thm:boundary}
The realizability, finite-budget likelihood state, dimension lower bound,
Bellman attainment, finite-command approximation, and fixed-controller
response extend to the full class of Borel gates $0\le g\le1$. A posterior
update is required only at a history with nonzero likelihood; zero-evidence
branches may be assigned any fixed next state. The support formula and
Bellman formula are unchanged with $\eta=0$, interpreting the failure term
as zero when its predictive probability is zero. The state strata can still
be chosen compact with strictly positive normalized likelihood coefficients,
uniformly over this larger gate class at each fixed budget.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The comparator construction, necessity of a common gate for every radius,
and fresh-cartridge pasting use only $0\le g\le1$. Let
$0<h_*\le h_j(a,x)\le H_*$ be uniform raw coefficient bounds, and put
$s(g)=\int(1-g)\,d\nu$. If censoring has positive probability, $s(g)>0$
and its cubic coefficients satisfy
\[
 h_*s(g)\le h_j(a,g,\dagger)\le H_*s(g).
\]
Normalize each likelihood factor by its coefficient sum before multiplying.
Its normalized coordinates are then at least $h_*/(4H_*)$, both for raw
accepted observations and for every possible censored observation. An
accepted observation with $g(x)=0$ is impossible at every radius. Censoring
has zero probability exactly when $g=1$ almost everywhere, also independently
of the radius. Thus on the common nonzero-likelihood histories the same
positive convolution applies. Products of the compact set of normalized
cubic vectors with coordinates at least $h_*/(4H_*)$ give the claimed compact
state strata. Arbitrarily small failure probability does not destroy the
normalized coefficient margin.

The gate space $\{0\le g\le1\}$ remains weak-star compact metrizable. The
moment body and its support proof therefore apply with $\eta=0$. Where
$\bar f>0$, the failure posterior and its value contribution are continuous.
At $\bar f=0$ the posterior need not have a limit, but
\[
 0\le e^{\lambda r_a^\dagger}\bar f\,
        V_{n+1,b-1}(c^{a,m,\dagger})
 \le \bar f\,G_+e^{b|\lambda|B}\longrightarrow0.
\]
So the failure-weighted contribution, extended by zero, is jointly
continuous on the entire compact feasible domain. It is this contribution,
not a fictitious everywhere-continuous zero-evidence posterior, that the
Bellman maximum needs. The compact command objective remains continuous;
the same selection and induction prove an attained Borel controller.
The dyadic gate approximation approximates the five moments uniformly.
Uniform continuity of this extended failure-weighted objective replaces
the positive-evidence posterior estimate in that approximation proof.
Finite command menus therefore still approach the full optimum uniformly.

The sharp dimension lower bound holds already in the interior gate subclass
used in Theorem~\ref{thm:dimension}, hence holds in the larger class.
For response, the observed conditional densities have a common support
independent of $R$. On that support their derivative ratios are bounded by
the same raw $\beta_j$: successful gate factors cancel, and a failure ratio
is bounded by integrating against $1-g$. Between any two radii their density
ratio lies between $h_*/H_*$ and $H_*/h_*$, for accepted and failed reports
alike. Their chronological products therefore have common support and
bounded relative derivatives and likelihood ratios. The polynomial
variation proof and the relative square-root proof in
Theorem~\ref{thm:response} apply without a density floor relative to $\mu^N$.
In particular, all compared readout path laws remain equivalent on their
common support and have finite relative entropies. No such equivalence is
asserted for the ideal collision-coordinate record.
\end{proof}

For the full interval a bounded-source tilt is always available by taking
$g=e^{V-\sup V}$. Its conditional accepted law is the tilt, and the actual
censoring and cartridge counts are still those derived in
Section~\ref{sec:attainability}. Allowing the endpoint gates does not turn a
conditional output law into an unconditional successful preparation.
