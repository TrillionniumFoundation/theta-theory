\begin{abstract}
We develop a finite-time foundation for deterministic mechanical experiments with partial observation. Mechanical evolution, preparation, admissible intervention, and measurement are specified before any path law, nonlinear evaluation, or effective state. Joint state--observation instruments retain measurement back-action and apparatus memory. We separate probability tilting from physically attainable changes: the exact deficit of a restricted Gibbs variational problem is its relative-entropy distance to the unrestricted tilted law. For preparation restricted to a coarse variable we compute the optimizer, the loss of hidden selection, density-cap constraints, and causal acceptance protocols with their trial budgets. Predictive states retain the entire parameter experiment and admissible continuation resources. We prove finite-alphabet update and response formulas, a continuous compact-alphabet extension under explicit positive-density hypotheses, and resource-constrained dynamic programming. Error bounds are given both in total variation and in observable-dependent test classes; singular deterministic path perturbations need not be close in total variation. Mechanical differentiation is developed through flow jets, event-time recursion, saltation, moving-domain currents, and complete-preparation Cauchy criteria. Two direct Lorentz-gas calculations verify the framework. The first treats a parameter-independent positive-volume preparation crossing a terminal collision interface. The second covers the entire natural equilibrium preparation, including arbitrarily grazing incidences, for a horizon shorter than the minimum intercollision flight. An exact collision-tube assembly gives all finite orders of strong cylindrical response without inverse-grazing multipliers. The collision indicator itself, without Gaussian readout noise, identifies the radius. This corrected edition retains the bias in constrained entropy, both branch density traces in shape response, and explicit fixed or transported preparation hypotheses in chamber integration.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} deterministic mechanics; realizable path ensembles; measurement instruments; relative entropy; predictive sufficiency; hybrid sensitivity; Lorentz gas.\\
