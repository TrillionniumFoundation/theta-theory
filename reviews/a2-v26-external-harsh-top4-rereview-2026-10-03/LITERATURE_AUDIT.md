# Focused literature audit for A2 v26

**Scope:** theorem/input comparison for the new collision-cloud and whole-table certification mechanism. This is not an exhaustive priority search.

## 1. Boundary and lens rigidity

P. Stefanov, G. Uhlmann and A. Vasy, *Local and global boundary rigidity and the geodesic X-ray transform in the normal gauge*, Annals of Mathematics 194 (2021), 1–95; arXiv:1702.03638.

Their data are the boundary distance function or lens relation of a Riemannian metric, and the unknown is the metric modulo the natural gauge. The v26 problem has known Euclidean ambient metric, unknown periodic reflecting bodies, actively chosen interior launches, collision positions and selected endpoint-return laws. The cited work supplies a nearby inverse-geometry paradigm, not the collision-cloud theorem or the completion defect.

## 2. Obstacle travelling-time rigidity

T. Gurfinkel, L. Noakes and L. Stoyanov:

- *Uniqueness of Obstacles in Riemannian Manifolds from Travelling Times*, arXiv:2309.11141;
- *Rigidity of Travelling Times for Strictly Convex Obstacles in Riemannian Manifolds*, arXiv:2311.07813.

These works compare obstacle configurations from exterior travelling-time sets. Their observations consist of boundary endpoints and travel lengths of billiard trajectories. Version 26 instead uses resettable spatial preparations, complete first-hit collision clouds, selected local return branches, endpoint-arclength distributions and absolute cell-volume normalization. Neither input obviously contains the other. The v26 paper should therefore present itself as a different controlled tomography experiment, not as a general improvement of travelling-time rigidity.

## 3. Random approximation of convex bodies

R. Schneider, *Random approximation of convex sets*, J. Microscopy 151 (1988), 211–227.

J. Prochno, C. Schütt, M. Sonnleitner and E. M. Werner, *Random approximation of convex bodies in Hausdorff distance*, Mathematische Annalen 392 (2025), 4525–4542; arXiv:2404.02870.

This literature studies convex hull approximation from random points sampled on or in a convex body, including Hausdorff rates. The v26 first-hit points are not asserted to be uniform boundary samples. Instead the paper proves a physical lower mass bound for every short boundary arc and uses a direct coupon estimate plus curvature to obtain a hull enclosure. That is a legitimate specialized argument, but the broad convex-hull and support-smoothing mechanism belongs to a mature approximation literature. Version 26 does not claim an optimal random-polytope constant or minimax cloud rate.

## 4. Classical mechanisms used in the new proof

Several ingredients are standard in isolation:

- convex-hull Hausdorff approximation from boundary coverage;
- support-function smoothing and moment cancellation;
- Newton/contraction certification under positive Hessian bounds;
- finite separation on compact generic classes;
- rational reconstruction with bounded denominators and Hermite normal form;
- conditional concentration, coupon bounds and summable error spending.

The novelty claim should rest on their integration with the billiard endpoint law, absolute free-area recovery and the nonnegative completion defect, not on presenting any one of these tools as a new general principle.

## 5. Marked-length and count-only distinctions

The v26 experiment is not a marked length spectrum, collision-count process or passive unmarked trajectory. It supplies controlled initial positions, collision localization, auxiliary body clouds and two function-valued endpoint laws. The retained count-fiber results and formal Taylor examples do not settle exact analytic count-only rigidity or nonrigidity. These boundaries are stated correctly in the primary and should remain prominent.

## 6. Editorial comparison

Version 26 has a clearer novelty profile than v25:

1. it removes deterministic boundary/distance queries;
2. it removes analytic continuation from the new whole-table theorem;
3. it reconstructs complete bodies directly from collision observations;
4. it gives quantitative finite fingerprints;
5. it preserves an omission-safe completion certificate.

The closest literatures confirm that these are nontrivial synthesis results. They also show why the top-four case remains difficult. The sensor is a specially engineered active tomography experiment, while the strongest classical rigidity questions usually seek recovery from a more intrinsic or standard global invariant. The new theorem is therefore best viewed as a strong conditional reconstruction/certification result rather than a universal rigidity theorem.

## 7. Audit conclusion

I found no primary source that obviously subsumes the exact v26 package of collision clouds, endpoint-law action recovery, rational period reconstruction and completion certification. I also found no basis for treating the convex-hull, support-smoothing, travelling-time or sequential-probability components individually as unprecedented.

The literature assessment supports a serious specialist-journal contribution. It does not independently supply the exceptional breadth or naturality needed for an Annals/Acta/Inventiones/JAMS recommendation.
