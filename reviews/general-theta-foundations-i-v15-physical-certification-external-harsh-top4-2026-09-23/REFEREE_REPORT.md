# 3. Independent audit of the v14 local-certificate theorem

The fixed-resource local certificate theorem is one of the stronger pieces of the inherited manuscript.

For finite marked causal experiments and a fixed private or finite-selector signature, the policy space is a product of stochastic-row simplexes. The deficiency is represented as

delta = min over q in P of F(q), with F(q) the maximum of finitely many marked feedback discrepancies f_i(q).

The key technical point is correctly handled: a fixed test f_i is affine separately in each time-indexed policy row, while the maximum F need not be. The proof does **not** commit the common fallacy of treating the global maximum as separately affine.

The clipped-simplex construction then gives, on each product cell C,

- an exact vertex formula for the minimum of each fixed f_i;
- a cellwise lower test value ell(C);
- a genuine same-budget upper witness u(C) obtained from an actual row table;
- global bounds L_M <= delta <= U_M.

The coupling modulus is also conceptually appropriate. Two rows in one grid cell are maximally coupled; mismatches accumulate along time, not over all unused histories. Hidden and visible selectors are treated differently, with the visible selector paying for the reference joint interface as well as the simulated side.

I do not see a short contradiction in this argument.

The dyadic monotonicity and convergence statements are plausible at the finite scope. The exact-boundary clause properly invokes classical real quantifier elimination rather than pretending that the supplied certificate script implements such elimination.

However, the theorem remains an **exhaustive localization theorem**. The lower witness can require one test per policy cell. The number of cells grows in the full stochastic-row dimension. The theorem deliberately makes no polynomial-time claim, which is mathematically honest, but it also means that “history-independent modulus” must not be confused with “history-independent certificate complexity.”

At the requested venue level, this is not yet the structural nonconvex finite-memory theorem I would expect to carry the paper. The proof recipe is powerful but generic: compact semialgebraic policy set, multiaffine local interpolation, subdivision, and finite test enumeration.

A decisive strengthening would expose intrinsic geometry, for example through a sparsified witness theorem, a dimension bound, a semialgebraic stratification, a nonconvex dual object, or a sharp complexity transition.

# 4. Independent audit of the v14 residual theorem

The residual theorem is also technically better than a naive Galerkin argument.

The finite trial space V is contained in D(L). The compressed generator J is skew-adjoint. The interventions are unitary but are **not** assumed to preserve D(L). The residuals

R = ((I-P)L iota)^* ((I-P)L iota)

and

S_a = ((I-P)C_a iota)^* ((I-P)C_a iota)

are positive semidefinite by construction.

The proof performs the variation-of-constants step before the intervention. This is important: it does not differentiate a kicked vector whose membership in D(L) was never proved. The intervention error is then added as an orthogonal projection residual.

The controlled-word estimate uses exact prefixes and approximate suffixes. The probabilistic comparison keeps the matched-history weights until taking the maximum over possible action words and only then applies Cauchy-Schwarz. The actual latent mark is coupled from the original microscopic state and remains part of the matching event.

I again do not see a short fatal counterexample in the declared finite-space inequality.

But the theorem is not yet what the title “physical certification” would need.

The manuscript explicitly admits two crucial limitations:

1. a finite-dimensional vector model is not automatically a finite-report experiment to which the rational local certificate theorem applies;
2. the residual majorants need not converge along every graph-core sequence even though the actual controlled words converge strongly.

Those are not side remarks. They are exactly the missing links in the advertised synthesis.

# 5. Main mathematical blocker: no end-to-end physical-to-finite-rational certificate

The local theorem is exact for finite marked causal experiments; its effective equality statements require rational finite data.

The active hard-sphere model is not such an experiment:

- the physical carrier is nonfinite;
- the finite approximation state is a real vector;
- the Gaussian reports are continuous;
- the preparation and Gram data are generally real, not rational;
- a finite-dimensional observable model is not a finite report alphabet.

The residual theorem supplies a two-sided marked comparison between the exact active acquisition and a finite-dimensional Gaussian acquisition. That is useful. It still does not produce the finite rational marked experiment needed by the local algorithm.

The missing theorem must control the whole feedback interface, not merely a fixed open-loop Gaussian law. A serious bridge must:

1. choose a finite report truncation and quantization;
2. construct a finite marked causal experiment with the same feedback timing and action interface;
3. prove a two-sided marked feedback deficiency bound;
4. make the error uniform over the declared finite consumer resource signature;
5. preserve the actual latent mark and every exposed selector;
6. rationalize the resulting finite probabilities with an additional two-sided comparison error;
7. compose those errors with the physical residual and presentation-stability theorems;
8. finally output a certified lower/upper interval for the intrinsic deficiency of the **original physical experiment at the original resource budget**.

No such v15 theorem is present because no v15 mathematical source is present.

Even in inherited v14, the corollary explicitly says that a separate finite-report bridge “must actually be proved.” I agree.

This remains the single largest mathematical blocker.

# 6. The a posteriori residual bound is not an asymptotically complete certification scheme

The inherited graph-core theorem proves that the true finite-dimensional controlled-word approximation converges strongly at each fixed horizon.

The residual theorem proves that, for one chosen finite space V, a computable expression bounds the error if the necessary finite Gram data are rigorously known.

These are different assertions.

The paper does not prove that there exists a sequence of trial spaces for which the **computable residual bounds themselves** tend to zero. Indeed it explicitly warns that cancellation can make the true error small while the residual majorant remains large.

That prevents the paper from claiming a convergent a posteriori certification algorithm.

There are at least two mathematically substantive ways forward:

- prove a verifiable graph-norm/intervention approximation property under which the present residual majorants vanish;
- construct an enriched or equilibrated estimator whose reliability and asymptotic completeness are proved.

Merely adding more floating-point residual examples would not address this point.

# 7. Rigorous microscopic Gram data remain an assumption

The residual certificate needs inner products involving the trial functions, L applied to them, the interventions, and the observables.

The graph-core construction is elegant as an existence argument: it smooths a dense family under the exact hard-sphere unitary flow. But an abstract smoothed graph-core basis is not automatically a constructive family for which collision-domain integrals and intervention residuals can be rigorously enclosed.

The current diagnostics explicitly acknowledge this boundary. Their finite operator tests are floating-point diagnostics, not analytic certification of the hard-sphere Gram data.

A physical-certification paper should exhibit at least one genuinely nontrivial active microscopic instance with:

- an explicit admissible trial family;
- a proof of domain membership;
- rigorous interval enclosures for the needed Gram matrices;
- propagation of interval uncertainty through the residual estimate;
- and a resulting nonvacuous resource-deficiency interval.

Without this, the theorem begins after one of the hardest analytic/numerical steps has already been assumed solved.

# 8. The private-resource converse is still exhaustive rather than structural

The finite nonconvex feasible set is treated honestly. That is a strength.

But top-four significance requires more than correctness of an exhaustive certification method.

The present theorem localizes the whole policy product into cells and may assign a different test to every cell. The certificate is complete because compactness plus refinement eventually resolves strict inequalities.

What is still absent is an invariant explaining **why** a small or structured witness should exist.

The paper would become substantially stronger if it proved one of the following types of results:

- witness support bounded by an intrinsic dimension rather than the number of grid cells;
- a finite stratification of the policy image on which one small family of tests is sufficient;
- a sharp lower/upper complexity theorem for private finite-memory certification;
- a richer dual object for the nonconvex feasible set;
- a theorem linking positive-error state complexity to the exact compatible-cover width and universal continuation state.

This is not a request for polynomial time as such. It is a request for mathematical structure.

At the requested venue level, the nearest-neighbour theorem comparison should be closed at the level of hypotheses and conclusions, including:

- hidden versus visible randomization;

- compactness/existence assumptions;
- zero-error state minimization;

Until then, the paper is right not to claim an exhaustively established priority boundary. But that restraint also weakens the case for top-four novelty.

Completeness is proved for fixed finite experiments and fixed resource signatures by exhaustive localization. It is not completeness for arbitrary nonfinite causal experiments.

## 11.5 Error modulus is not certificate size

The feedback oscillation bound avoids counting histories. The number of policy cells and local tests can still be enormous.

## 11.6 Rationalization is part of the theorem

Writing floating probabilities as decimal or rational literals is not a proof of two-sided marked causal closeness. The rounding error must enter the comparison inequality.

## 11.7 Keep v13 and v14 novelty accounting separate

The intrinsic deficiency, endogenous common-task theorem, regenerative average theorem, and active graph-core construction are inherited from v13. The v14 mathematical additions are the local-certificate and residual-certificate layers. This version accounting is currently clear and should not be blurred by a new branch label.
