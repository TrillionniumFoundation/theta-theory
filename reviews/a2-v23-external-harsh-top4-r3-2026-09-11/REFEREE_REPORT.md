**Manuscript:** *Boundary laws, intrinsic rigidity, and multiscale physical information in dispersing billiards*

**Standard applied:** a deliberately severe external-referee standard appropriate to Annals / Inventiones / JAMS / Acta-level consideration.

## Recommendation

**Reject in the present form at a top-four mathematics journal.**

I do **not** regard v23 as a cosmetic or incremental revision of v22. It makes serious progress on the two most important structural defects of the preceding version: it formulates an actual intrinsic periodic gluing problem rather than silently assuming common Euclidean registration, and it separates the endpoint-time, count-endpoint, and endpoint-only statistical experiments instead of conflating distinct observation sigma-fields. The all-order signed inverse remains potentially substantial, and the new lattice-holonomy formulation is conceptually cleaner than the registered v22 global statement.

Nevertheless, at the standard requested here, the revision still does not close the paper at the level advertised in the title, abstract, and concluding global-statistical narrative. The principal issue is no longer a missing local calculation. It is an architectural gap: the paper proves strong fixed-order local information statements and a conditional compactness/diagonal lemma, but it does not prove the uniform physical estimability hypothesis needed to turn those ingredients into the claimed global statistical reconstruction theorem. The final global statistical consequence is therefore conditional on precisely the sort of growing-complexity control that the manuscript emphasizes it does not establish. In addition, the meaning of “intrinsic” still includes a substantial prior calibration—the marked abstract lattice carries a fixed Euclidean Gram form—and the headline formulation should make this impossible to miss.

I would be willing to look at a substantially strengthened successor because the deterministic inverse component appears materially stronger than in earlier rounds. I would not recommend another referee cycle on essentially the same theorem package with only expository changes.

## 1. What v23 genuinely fixes

### 1.1 The registration problem is now mathematically formulated

The new intrinsic datum is channel-local: for each lifted channel `e=(a,b,ell)` the law is recorded in its own channel-adapted frame, while the Euclidean placement of that frame relative to other channels and to the lattice is not supplied. The paper then introduces an unknown oriented realization `iota` of the marked abstract lattice and edge placements `A_e`, imposes deck-corrected incidence matching, and defines the admissible periodic gluing space. This is a substantial improvement over a global theorem whose registration was effectively built into the input.

The classification statement

`periodic table realizations modulo global SE(2) <-> admissible periodic gluing space`

is the correct kind of object to formulate if one wants to distinguish true inverse ambiguity from coordinate convention.

### 1.2 Symmetry is treated as an actual inverse ambiguity

The analytic-signature lemma correctly acknowledges circles and finite rotational symmetries instead of suppressing them through a hidden registration choice. This is important. An inverse theorem at this level must state what data fail to distinguish and why.

### 1.3 The observation hierarchy is much better

The manuscript now distinguishes:

1. endpoint-time records, whose moving ceiling has positive trace and produces a non-dominated Poisson boundary-shift experiment at the fastest scale;
2. count-endpoint records, in which negative-binomial waiting counts expose a hyperbolic direction on a faster Gaussian scale while endpoint marks carry the slower moving-support information;
3. endpoint-only records, which recover the previously derived Gaussian moving-boundary experiment.

This is a mathematically meaningful hierarchy, not just repackaging. The statement that these are different experiments generated by coarsening is a substantial conceptual correction.

### 1.4 The v23 finite-to-global compactness lemma is useful

The finite-coordinate resolution theorem is a legitimate compactness consequence of injectivity of the full data map on a compact analytic class. Likewise, the diagonal argument is a standard but useful way to convert uniform fixed-order consistency into one slowly growing order sequence. This closes a logical gap of the form “all jets imply exact analytic recovery, while statistics only estimates a fixed finite number of jets.”

However, as explained below, it does **not** by itself close the statistical problem generated by the actual physical experiment.

# Major objections

## M1. The advertised global statistical reconstruction remains conditional on the central missing uniform-estimability theorem

This is the most important issue.

Theorem `thm:v23-diagonal-global-reconstruction` assumes that for every fixed `M` there exists an estimator `\widehat{\mathcal D}_{n,M}` satisfying

`sup_{T in K} P_{n,T}( ||Dhat_{n,M}-D_M(T)|| > eta ) -> 0`

for every `eta>0`.

The physical finite-order results developed elsewhere in the paper are local asymptotic experiments around anchored finite-dimensional models, with designs, support geometry, rates, collars, transfer constants and information matrices depending on the local model and on the fixed order. The corollary then says, in effect, that **whenever** the compact analytic class admits a common finite acquisition atlas for which each fixed `D_M` is uniformly consistently estimable, the diagonal theorem yields global recovery.

That “whenever” contains the main unresolved statistical theorem.

The manuscript therefore has the following logical structure:

- fixed-dimensional local limit experiments are proved;
- exact all-order deterministic injectivity is proved;
- a compactness lemma says finitely many sufficiently accurate coordinates resolve any prescribed global tolerance;
- a diagonal lemma says that *if* every fixed finite coordinate vector can be estimated uniformly over the compact class, one can let the order grow slowly.

What is **not** shown is that the actual finite-bridge acquisition experiment provides those uniform estimators over the declared compact analytic class. In particular, the manuscript does not presently derive, from its local experiments, a globally valid finite covering/atlas construction with all comparison kernels, positive designs, identifiability constants, cap choices and finite-to-boundary errors controlled uniformly over that cover.

For a top-four claim, this distinction is decisive. Local asymptotic normality or a local Poisson limit at every point does not automatically imply uniform global consistency over a nonlinear compact model, especially when the experiment itself has moving support and when the useful design may depend on the unknown table.

### What is required

At least one of the following must be supplied.

**Route A — prove the global physical theorem.** Construct a finite acquisition atlas on `K`, prove uniform local identifiability and uniform comparison/transfer bounds on each chart, specify a parameter-independent implementable design or an adaptive scheme, and derive the required uniform consistency of every fixed `D_M` from the actual physical experiment.

**Route B — lower the headline claim.** State the compactness/diagonal result explicitly as a conditional abstract bridge and remove language suggesting that the physical experiment itself has already been proved to yield global analytic reconstruction.

At a top-four venue I strongly prefer Route A. Route B would make the paper more honest but substantially weaker.

## M2. “Intrinsic periodic rigidity” still presupposes the full Euclidean Gram form of the marked abstract lattice

Section `23b_intrinsic_multichannel_rigidity_v23.tex` begins with a marked abstract lattice `Lambda_abs ≃ Z^2` **with its fixed Euclidean Gram form**. The unknown `iota` is then only an orientation-preserving linear isometry into `R^2`.

Thus the intrinsic theorem does not recover the lattice metric (shape and scale) from the channel laws. It recovers the orientation/placement of an already metrically calibrated lattice relative to the channel network. One nonzero holonomy vector is enough to determine `iota` precisely because the Euclidean structure of the domain lattice has already been supplied.

This is mathematically legitimate, but the abstract/introduction language “without a common laboratory placement” and “fully intrinsic periodic table rigidity” can easily be read more strongly than the theorem actually says. The paper should sharply distinguish:

- no common Euclidean registration of channel frames;
- known marked lattice metric but unknown lattice orientation/placement;
- unknown lattice metric.

Only the first two are treated here.

For a truly stronger intrinsic result one should ask whether two independent non-collinear cycle holonomies can recover the lattice Gram data itself (or characterize the residual ambiguity if not). If that can be done from the observable channel data, it would materially strengthen the global theorem and remove the principal calibration hidden in the present use of “intrinsic.”

At minimum, the title theorem and abstract should say “relative to a known marked Euclidean lattice” or equivalent.

## M3. The singleton-gluing hypothesis in the global statistical bridge is too close to assuming away the difficult global inverse stability problem

The compact class `K` in Section 25 is assumed to have singleton admissible periodic gluing throughout. The paper gives a sufficient qualitative mechanism—an anchoring cycle plus a signature-rigid spanning tree—but the statistical theorem does not establish a **uniform quantitative separation** from loss of signature rigidity, cycle degeneration, channel switching, or competing admissible gluings.

This matters because the diagonal argument uses only topological compactness. It guarantees existence of `M(q,epsilon)` and `eta(q,epsilon)`, but gives no quantitative control as the class approaches a symmetry locus or a gluing bifurcation. The theorem is therefore a continuity/identifiability result on a preselected well-behaved class, not a stability theorem for the natural inverse problem.

For pure consistency this may be formally sufficient once the assumptions are granted. For the top-four narrative, however, the manuscript should not conflate this compactness statement with robust global inverse stability.

A stronger theorem should formulate explicit uniform nondegeneracy assumptions—e.g. quantitative signature separation, persistence margins for the selected closest-pair channels, and a quantitative isolation of the admissible gluing—and propagate them into an actual modulus of continuity for the finite data map. Even a non-sharp modulus would be significantly more informative than the current contradiction argument.

## M4. The Poisson asymptotic-equivalence proof needs a more rigorous global deficiency argument; the current “generate the bulk from the reference conditional law” step is too compressed

The endpoint-time theorem claims asymptotic equivalence, uniformly on compact local parameter sets, between the complete conditional successful sample and a boundary Poisson process plus a parameter-free factor.

The rare-layer Poissonization is plausible and the codimension-two corner estimate is useful. The weak point is the reverse deficiency argument. The proof says that after removing a reference ceiling layer, the bulk likelihood ratio has centered variance `O(k_n^{-1})`, its deterministic first-order term matches the Poisson compensator, and one may therefore generate the asymptotically parameter-free bulk from the reference conditional law and attach it to the Poissonized boundary layer.

For a Le Cam equivalence theorem with moving support, this deserves a precise statement and proof. In particular:

1. the Markov kernel reconstructing the bulk must be parameter independent;
2. the joint law of bulk size, boundary-layer count and locations must be controlled, not merely the marginal likelihood fluctuation in the bulk;
3. the uniformity in `z` over compact sets must survive the random split between layer and bulk;
4. the projective passage as `R_K` increases should be stated in a standard experiment topology, rather than described informally;
5. the effect of the `o_K(k_n^{-1})` moving-boundary error on the symmetric difference of supports should be quantified explicitly.

I am not asserting that the theorem is false. I am saying that for the central non-dominated experiment of the paper, the proof as written is too abbreviated for a top-four journal. This is exactly the place where a reader will demand a full deficiency bound or a clean citation to an applicable Poissonization theorem whose hypotheses are verified one by one.

## M5. The count-endpoint product limit relies on a first-order hyperbolic decomposition whose uniformity and coordinate invariance are not developed sufficiently

Section 18d chooses `v_gamma` with `D_vartheta gamma(v_gamma)=1` and takes `H_gamma=ker D_vartheta gamma`. This is a sensible tangent decomposition at one reference point. But the theorem is presented as an intrinsic multirate information statement, while several issues remain local-coordinate dependent:

- `v_gamma` is noncanonical;
- the endpoint information is restricted to the tangent hyperplane `H_gamma` at the reference point;
- the slow local path is only first-order iso-hyperbolic, so second-order changes of `gamma` enter through the condition `j_n delta_n -> 0`;
- the proof does not formulate the resulting product experiment invariantly as a normal/tangent decomposition of the level set of `gamma` with a metric-independent comparison map.

This is not fatal, but the current exposition makes the “two-speed information geometry” sound more canonical than the theorem establishes. A stronger version should formulate the fast normal direction as the one-dimensional quotient `T/H_gamma`, prove invariance of the Gaussian count factor under admissible reparameterizations, and state exactly how the endpoint quadratic form descends/restricts on the tangent space.

In addition, the assertion `lambda != 0` because the coordinate contains the two contact curvatures and `gamma` depends nontrivially on them should be supported by an explicit derivative formula or a previously proved proposition with hypotheses that rule out cancellation at the reference table.

## M6. The paper is still carrying too many theorem layers for one article without a sufficiently sharp central theorem

The manuscript combines:

- nonlinear relative boundary laws;
- an all-order two-sided jet inverse;
- analytic continuation;
- global periodic graph gluing and lattice holonomy;
- endpoint-time non-dominated Poisson asymptotics;
- count-endpoint multirate Gaussian asymptotics;
- endpoint Gaussian coarsening;
- finite-to-boundary experiment transfer;
- compact analytic global reconstruction.

Breadth is not itself a defect. At a top-four venue, however, breadth must resolve into one or two unmistakable theorems whose hypotheses and consequences are memorable. At present, the paper still reads partly as the accumulated closure of successive referee blockers. Several components are individually interesting, but the reader has to reconstruct the hierarchy from a large number of modules and inherited versions.

The v23 introduction is better, but I would still require the paper to identify one deterministic flagship theorem and one statistical flagship theorem, each stated in the introduction in final native variables with no dependence on revision history. The rest should be organized explicitly as lemmas needed for those two results.

The provenance paragraph is acceptable, but the source architecture (`v3`, `v4`, `v5`, `v6`, `article/..._v18`, `..._v22`, `..._v23`) should not leak into the mathematical presentation of a submission. A journal referee should be able to read the compiled article as a coherent paper, not as a repository stratigraphy.

## M7. Exact-head build status remains unresolved and therefore the submission is not mechanically closed

The response correctly does **not** mislabel the zero-step GitHub Actions failure as a TeX failure. Nevertheless, the exact-head native build has not been certified. For a manuscript of this size, with a large dependency graph of `\input` files and many cross-references, this matters.

This is not a mathematical reason for rejection, but before resubmission I would require an independently reproducible clean build of the exact reviewed commit, with unresolved references/citations checked and the diagnostic scripts run on that exact source tree. The current repository record says this has not yet happened.

# Detailed comments on the deterministic inverse theorem

## D1. Clarify what the measured graph is allowed to know

The lifted labels `(a,b,ell)` are treated as part of the data/design. This is stronger than merely knowing that two obstacles participate in a channel: the deck displacement `ell` is already identified in the marked lattice. The manuscript should explain operationally how `ell` is known in the physical acquisition protocol and whether mislabelling or unknown deck class is outside scope. Otherwise the inverse theorem risks importing combinatorial information that is nontrivial to obtain from unregistered observations.

## D2. Admissibility is necessary but partly tautological

The classification theorem is correct in spirit: one constructs all algebraic gluings and then retains those that actually form dispersing tables with the declared measured channels. But because admissibility includes “the declared measured edges remain the prescribed facing closest-pair channels,” the bijection is partly a reformulation of realization compatibility.

The real content lies in the uniqueness corollary from holonomy and signature-rigid propagation. The paper should make that distinction explicit and avoid presenting the classification bijection itself as the deep rigidity result.

## D3. The analytic-signature rigidity condition is qualitative and may be fragile

A contact curvature jet that occurs at a unique framed point provides exact uniqueness. Statistically, however, near-repeated signatures are ill-conditioned. If Section 25 is meant to connect to noisy reconstruction, a quantitative separation condition should appear somewhere in the global statistical class. Exact uniqueness alone is not enough to explain robust recovery.

## D4. Analytic continuation from a germ is exact-data rigidity, not stable reconstruction

The manuscript is careful not to claim stable numerical analytic continuation, which is good. The top-four narrative should maintain this distinction throughout the abstract and introduction. Exact all-order recovery of a germ plus analyticity gives global uniqueness; it does not give useful finite-sample stability without the additional compactness assumptions of Section 25.

# Detailed comments on the statistical experiments

## S1. Conditional-on-success versus unconditional experiment should be displayed at theorem level

The endpoint-time Poisson theorem is first stated for successful records conditioned on success, and the complete stopped theorem then restores negative-binomial waiting counts and caps. This architecture is mathematically reasonable, but the introduction should state the unconditional observation experiment first and then identify the conditional factorization. Readers should not have to infer which random quantities have been conditioned away at each theorem.

## S2. The design is finite for each fixed jet order, but the growing-order design problem is unresolved

The v22 finite positive design may depend on the fixed order `M`. Section 25 lets `M_n -> infinity`, yet no single physical design sequence is constructed that simultaneously realizes these growing-order estimators. This is another manifestation of M1 and should be treated explicitly.

If the number of design points or their placement must change with `M`, the acquisition cost and the uniform transfer bounds become part of the global theorem. They cannot be hidden inside “every fixed finite vector is uniformly consistently estimable.”

## S3. Rate compatibility is nonempty but not yet an optimization theorem

The explicit regime `delta_n=n^{-1}`, `k_n=floor(n^2/log n)`, `j_n≈C log n` is useful as a feasibility witness. It should not be sold as more than that. The manuscript is mostly careful here; retain that caution.

## S4. The fastest endpoint-time scale dominates only for the declared record

The manuscript now explicitly says it does not analyze the full growing collision array. Good. This limitation should remain in the abstract-level claims whenever “complete physical information” is discussed. The complete theorem is complete for the stopped endpoint-time transcript, not for all dynamically observed collision data.

# Top-four significance assessment

If I separate correctness from significance, my assessment is as follows.

### Potentially top-tier component

The strongest part is the combination

`signed endpoint laws -> all-order unsymmetrized contact-jet inverse -> analytic obstacle recovery -> periodic holonomy/gluing rigidity`

provided the all-order inverse from v22 is fully correct and self-contained. Removing reflection/equal-contact assumptions and handling unregistered periodic channel geometry through a genuine holonomy argument could be a significant deterministic inverse result.

### Interesting but not yet top-tier as a global statistical theorem

The statistical hierarchy is conceptually valuable, particularly the distinction between a non-dominated Poisson boundary experiment and slower Gaussian coarsenings. But the manuscript currently establishes this in fixed finite-dimensional local models. The step from those local models to global analytic table estimation is conditional. Until that is repaired, I would not use the global-statistical conclusion as a principal top-four selling point.

### Venue-level problem

The paper presently asks the reader to regard a very broad chain as one finished theory. The deterministic chain is close to being convincing at that level; the statistical-global chain is not. A top-four paper needs the latter either completed or clearly demoted so that the former can carry the paper on its own.

# Required changes before I would recommend reconsideration

I would require the following, in roughly this order.

1. **Close the uniform-estimability gap.** Derive fixed-`M` uniform consistency over the compact analytic class from the actual finite-bridge experiment, including a finite/common acquisition atlas, parameter-independent or implementably adaptive designs, uniform caps, and uniform transfer bounds. If this cannot be done, rewrite the global statistical bridge as explicitly conditional and narrow the headline statistical claims.

2. **State the lattice calibration honestly and prominently.** Make clear that the marked abstract lattice includes a known Euclidean Gram form. Better: investigate recovery of the Gram form from multiple independent holonomy cycles and state the strongest theorem actually supported by the data.

3. **Strengthen the global stability side.** Add quantitative nondegeneracy/separation assumptions for signature matching, channel persistence, and gluing isolation, and derive at least a qualitative explicit modulus rather than relying solely on compactness by contradiction.

4. **Expand the proof of the endpoint-time Le Cam equivalence.** Give an explicit two-sided deficiency bound or invoke and verify a theorem that provides one. Treat the bulk/layer joint reconstruction and moving-support remainder rigorously and uniformly.

5. **Make the count-endpoint decomposition invariant.** State the fast information on the quotient normal direction and the endpoint information on the tangent space, and verify `D_vartheta gamma != 0` through an explicit formula/proposition.

6. **Solve the growing-order acquisition-design problem.** If `M_n` grows, specify how the finite positive designs grow, what the resulting sample allocation is, and why all finite-to-boundary approximations remain valid along that sequence.

7. **Reorganize around flagship theorems.** Present one deterministic and one statistical main theorem in final variables, strip revision-era architecture from the mathematical narrative, and move auxiliary experiment variants after the principal theorem chain.

8. **Produce a clean exact-head build certificate.** This is mechanical, but for a manuscript of this complexity it is necessary before serious resubmission.

# Minor and presentation comments

- Define at the first global theorem whether the lattice Gram form includes absolute scale or only shape; as written it appears to include the full Euclidean inner product and hence scale.
- In the holonomy discussion, explicitly state how reversing a channel changes the data-determined incidence congruence and verify orientation conventions around a cycle.
- In the singleton-gluing corollary, separate “at most one algebraic gluing” from existence; existence follows because one assumes the data are realized by an actual table, and this should be stated as a hypothesis rather than buried in the proof.
- In Section 25, specify a concrete compact metric topology on the quotient rather than saying the topology is “strong enough.” A fixed holomorphic collar model is a good candidate; use it as the theorem hypothesis if possible.
- The measurable minimum-distance selection in the diagonal theorem is fine, but the estimator is not constructive. Say this explicitly.
- Distinguish consistently between local parameter dimension `r_M`, order `M`, number of designs `L(M)`, and total acquisition cost. These quantities will matter if the growing-order problem is repaired.
- Keep the current caution that no sharp analytic minimax rate is proved.
- The phrase “complete local limit” should always carry the observation sigma-field in the same sentence.

---

# Final verdict

V23 is a serious revision and, in my view, the first version in this sequence whose deterministic global inverse architecture looks plausibly commensurate with a very high-level journal submission. The intrinsic gluing/holonomy framework and the explicit hierarchy of physical experiments are genuine improvements.

But the manuscript is **not yet closed at the level it advertises**. The central unresolved issue is the jump from fixed-order local physical experiments to uniform global analytic reconstruction. Section 25 proves a compactness/diagonal principle conditional on fixed-order uniform estimability; it does not prove that hypothesis for the actual finite-bridge experiment on the global analytic class. This is not a technical footnote. It is the missing theorem that would make the deterministic and statistical halves of the paper meet.

Accordingly my recommendation is **reject in the present form at a top-four journal, with encouragement to resubmit only after a mathematically substantive strengthening rather than another presentational revision**.

If the authors close M1 and give a fully uniform physical-to-global reconstruction theorem, while clarifying or reducing the lattice calibration in M2 and making the non-dominated equivalence proof fully rigorous, I would regard the resulting paper as deserving a fresh high-level evaluation.
