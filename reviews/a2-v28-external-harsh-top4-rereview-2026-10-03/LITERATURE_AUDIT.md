# Focused literature audit for the external A2 v28 rereview

**Date checked:** 3 October 2026  
**Scope:** theorem/input comparison relevant to the v28 fixed-aperture and local-period claims. This is not an exhaustive priority search.

## 1. Local periodicity and Delone-set symmetry

### Nikolay Dolbilin, *Delone Sets: Local Identity and Global Symmetry*

The cited paper concerns local criteria under which a Delone set has a crystallographic symmetry group, including locally antipodal sets and regular systems. That is a crystallinity/forced-symmetry problem for general Delone sets.

A2 v28 assumes from the outset that the obstacle union is periodic under some bounded rank-two presentation. Its central-patch lemma does not deduce periodicity. It uses the presentation bound to ensure that every unknown component orbit is represented in a finite patch, and then decides whether a candidate translation belongs to the full intrinsic translation group. The comparison in the manuscript is accurate.

### Pyry Herva and Jarkko Kari, *Periodicity and local complexity of Delone sets* (2025)

This more recent work studies periodicity and complexity of Delone sets and coloured Delone configurations using algebraic/symbolic-dynamical methods, including conditions under which finite local complexity and low pattern complexity force periodic structure.

It is relevant to the broad theme of local patterns versus global periods and should be added to the manuscript's comparison. The information and conclusions differ sharply: A2 v28 assumes a bounded periodic presentation, observes complete convex bodies in a finite physical patch, and reconstructs the full translation subgroup of that already periodic union. It does not prove forced periodicity from patch complexity.

## 2. Random approximation of convex bodies

### Joscha Prochno, Carsten Schütt, Mathias Sonnleitner and Elisabeth M. Werner, *Random approximation of convex bodies in Hausdorff metric/distance*

This work studies Hausdorff approximation by random polytopes. A2 v28's boundary coupon, hull and support estimates belong to the wider random/convex approximation tradition. The paper correctly treats these estimates as ingredients rather than claiming a new general random-polytope theorem.

The physical contribution in A2 v28 is that controlled first-hit launches supply a uniform lower mass on every short boundary arc in the protected finite patch. The subsequent `q`-coverage and hull approximation remain classical in character.

### Rolf Schneider, *Random approximation of convex sets*

The older Schneider reference is likewise an appropriate general comparator for random convex approximation. The manuscript does not rely on an unsupported priority claim concerning the hull rate.

## 3. Boundary/lens rigidity

### Plamen Stefanov, Gunther Uhlmann and András Vasy, *Local and global boundary rigidity and the geodesic X-ray transform in the normal gauge*

That work concerns recovery of an unknown Riemannian metric from boundary distance or lens data, modulo the natural diffeomorphism gauge, under convexity and related geometric assumptions.

A2 v28 has a known Euclidean metric and an unknown periodic reflecting set. Its controlled internal first-impact positions directly reveal boundary points in a finite laboratory aperture. It is not a special case of boundary/lens rigidity, nor does it supply a new general lens-rigidity mechanism. The manuscript's distinction is appropriate.

## 4. Obstacle travelling-time rigidity

### Tal Gurfinkel, Lyle Noakes and Luchezar Stoyanov, *Uniqueness of Obstacles in Riemannian Manifolds from Travelling Times*

This work studies uniqueness of strictly convex obstacles from exterior travelling-time triples under curvature and trajectory-intersection assumptions.

### Tal Gurfinkel, Lyle Noakes and Luchezar Stoyanov, *Rigidity of Travelling Times for Strictly Convex Obstacles in Riemannian Manifolds*

The later paper removes a tangency-equivalence condition in dimension at least three and proves obstacle equality from equality of travelling-time data.

These observation sets are substantially different from A2 v28. Travelling-time data relate exterior entry/exit points and lengths of reflected trajectories. A2 v28 uses controlled interior laboratory launches and only first-impact positions. The new theorem should not be marketed as an improvement of travelling-time rigidity; its novelty is the fixed-aperture physical coverage and full-period recognition under a bounded periodic prior.

## 5. Information-category conclusion

The closest conceptual ingredients divide into two groups:

1. local-pattern/global-period criteria for Delone or coloured configurations; and
2. direct convex-body approximation from sampled boundary points.

A2 v28 combines a physical first-impact mechanism with a finite two-sided translation test. The combination appears distinctive in the stated periodic billiard model. At the same time, the exact support datum exposes the relevant boundary patch directly, and the group step assumes periodicity under a bounded presentation. Those facts limit the top-four significance claim even though the theorem is mathematically nontrivial.

No literature source inspected here was found to state the exact v28 theorem. Conversely, this audit does not establish priority, and the absence of a located theorem is not evidence of uniqueness or four-journal significance.
