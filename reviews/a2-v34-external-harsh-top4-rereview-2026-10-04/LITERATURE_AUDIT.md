# Literature audit for the external A2 v34 rereview

## Scope

This is a focused theorem-level comparison for the new fixed-footprint stationary-noise result. It is not an exhaustive priority search, and it does not infer a journal decision from bibliographic novelty.

## 1. Active smooth-boundary estimation

Castro and Nowak study minimax active learning under boundary-fragment smoothness and binary class-label queries. Locatelli, Carpentier and Kpotufe study adaptive line search and interpolation for smooth decision boundaries in a membership-query model.

These works are directly relevant to the geometry of the v34 finite upper bound after an approximate membership label has been constructed. Their apparatus returns a class label at a selected feature location. The v34 collision sensor does not: a solid start and a free miss both return zero. The reciprocal paired experiment, killed-Bellman inversion and repeated pooled means are needed before the manuscript obtains an indeterminate-layer boundary label.

The manuscript appropriately credits line search, interpolation and active boundary estimation rather than presenting those abstract mechanisms as new. Its additional contribution is their implementation and accounting in the stipulated collision-bit experiment, followed by periodic-table recognition.

## 2. Convex support estimation under noisy observations

Brunel, Klusowski and Yang study estimation of a convex support from noisy continuous vectors with additive Gaussian errors, with Hausdorff loss and a geometric estimator avoiding direct Fourier deconvolution.

That setting is not the v34 observation model. V34 observes only pooled collision bits at controlled nominal centers. Conversely, v34 assumes a known compact launch footprint, a quantitative boundary-mass lower bound and stationary reciprocal use of the same unknown density. It does not treat Gaussian or unbounded noise, an unknown support, or direct noisy point observations.

The comparison is nevertheless useful: both problems recover geometry in the presence of a nuisance displacement law, and both exploit geometry rather than naïve Fourier inversion. No rate ordering is justified across the two different experiments.

## 3. Classical convex geometry

The identity

`support(C+(-K)) = support(C) + support(-K)`

and the subtraction of a known Minkowski summand at the support-function level are classical convex geometry. So are inner parallel bodies under a rolling-radius condition and Brunn--Minkowski concavity of the square root of planar intersection area. Schneider's monograph is an appropriate general reference.

V34 does not claim these facts as abstract novelties. Its mathematical contribution is the way they remove an unknown stationary density from this particular reciprocal collision experiment and feed a finite reconstruction theorem.

## 4. Killed random walks and dynamic programming

The stopped quadratic-martingale estimate, killed Green sum and monotone positive-part Bellman iteration are classical potential-theoretic and dynamic-programming mechanisms. The retained v31/v32 manuscripts and the current article give the required special-case proofs and cite standard random-walk sources.

The v34 improvement is not a new Green theorem. It uses the expected exit time from the complete fixed aperture to control accumulated forcing and update error independently of the iteration depth. This prevents a spurious logarithmic-depth multiplier in the probability accuracy.

## 5. Novelty boundary

I did not identify in the manuscript's focused citations a theorem that already contains all of the following:

- reciprocal first-hit collision bits with solid starts and misses both retained as zero outcomes;
- one fixed unknown stationary launch density on a calibrated compact support;
- recovery of a blurred occupation by a killed Bellman inverse;
- identification of the original obstacles by positivity support and known Minkowski subtraction;
- finite active boundary reconstruction and periodic-lattice recognition.

This combination appears mathematically meaningful. The audit was not exhaustive, so this is not a priority certificate.

The closest conceptual description is a synthesis of active boundary estimation, support estimation with nuisance displacement, classical convex geometry and killed-walk inversion. That synthesis supports specialist-journal novelty. It does not by itself establish the broad conceptual transformation expected at Annals, Acta, Inventiones or JAMS.

## 6. Comparisons still useful in a specialist submission

A final specialist version would benefit from a compact comparison table separating:

- directly observed membership labels versus collision-derived labels;
- continuous noisy vectors versus pooled collision bits;
- known versus unknown noise support;
- compact versus unbounded noise;
- Hausdorff versus `C^2` loss;
- passive versus active inputs;
- sample count versus physical input precision and apparatus calibration.

This would make the exact information-theoretic contribution visible without overstating universality.
