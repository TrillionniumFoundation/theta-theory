# Focused literature audit for the external A2 v40 rereview

This audit positions the new two-field theorem by mathematical mechanism and observation model. It is not an exhaustive priority search. The principal conclusion of the referee report is conditional on a broader human literature review before publication at the requested level.

## 1. Signed support functions and planar curvature measures

The exact collision identity reduces the observable geometry to

\[
        H_C=h_C-h_{L_C},
\]

where `L_C` is a contact chord. The operator `d^2/dphi^2+1` sends a planar support function to its surface-area measure and sends a segment support to two atoms. For a strictly convex body the positive surface-area measure has no atoms, while the chord contributes two negative atoms. This is classical support-measure calculus after the collision-specific identity has been derived.

The primary references inspected for this layer are:

- R. Schneider, *Convex Bodies: The Brunn–Minkowski Theory*, second expanded edition, Cambridge University Press, 2014;
- Y. Martinez-Maure, *Geometric study of Minkowski differences of plane convex bodies*, Canadian Journal of Mathematics **58** (2006), 600–624.

Martinez-Maure treats signed length measures associated with differences of support functions and uniqueness up to translation. Schneider supplies the surface-area-measure, Steiner-point, support-addition, and planar segment identities.

The new claim is not the signed-measure calculus itself. It is the experimentally observable cancellation

\[
2h_{C-A}-h_{K_+}-h_{K_-}+t|u\cdot e|
       =h_C-h_{L_C},
\]

together with matching the three support components from the two collision fields. This step should be compared explicitly with any prior inverse problem in which several morphological images cancel an unknown probe.

## 2. Unknown-probe morphology and blind geometric factorization

A close geometric antecedent is:

- J. S. Villarrubia, *Algorithms for scanned probe microscope image simulation, surface reconstruction, and tip estimation*, Journal of Research of the National Institute of Standards and Technology **102** (1997), 425–454.

That work represents probe imaging through morphological dilation and develops blind tip estimation. It is directly relevant to the problem of separating an unknown Minkowski summand from observed supports.

The A2 theorem differs in three important ways:

1. the two collision supports are generated by entering strips, not by two ordinary dilations of one set;
2. their support combination leaves an obstacle-minus-chord function whose negative atoms identify the missing chord;
3. after the geometry is recovered, the occupation amplitude identifies the full probability measure, not merely the probe support.

A final journal version should compare its identifiability assumptions and complete fiber with the strongest available blind-probe or blind morphological-reconstruction theorems, not only with algorithmic microscopy practice.

## 3. Compact convolution and Fourier zeros

The exact law step uses

\[
 v_P=\mathbf 1_C*\check\mu.
\]

The obstacle transform is entire and nonzero at the origin, so its real nonzero set is dense. The quotient determines the continuous characteristic function on that dense set and therefore everywhere.

A useful comparison and warning is:

- A. Delaigle and A. Meister, *Nonparametric function estimation under Fourier-oscillating noise*, Statistica Sinica **21** (2011), 1065–1092.

That paper studies statistical deconvolution when the known error transform has zeros. It illustrates why exact division on a dense set does not imply stable statistical inversion. The A2 manuscript correctly separates these issues: the Fourier quotient establishes exact uniqueness only, while finite recovery uses a moment method with explicit exponential conditioning.

The final exposition should keep this separation prominent. In particular, exact completion of all responses should not be described as a finite-cost extrapolation through the Fourier zero set.

## 4. Moment comparison and transportation recovery

The finite probability theorem uses triangular moment identities, polynomial approximation of Lipschitz functions, and Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality. Relevant primary sources include:

- P. Rigollet and J. Weed, *Uncoupled isotonic regression via minimum Wasserstein deconvolution*, Information and Inference **8** (2019), 691–717;
- G. Peyré and M. Cuturi, *Computational Optimal Transport*, Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning **11** (2019), 355–607;
- C. Villani, *Optimal Transport: Old and New*, Springer, 2009;
- C. Caillerie, F. Chazal, J. Dedecker, and B. Michel, *Deconvolution for the Wasserstein metric and geometric inference*, Electronic Journal of Statistics **5** (2011), 1394–1423.

The A2 proof supplies a bivariate triangular inversion with perturbation of the recovered geometric factor and obtains

\[
 W_1(\mu,\widetilde\mu)
     \le C/m+a(Cm)^{Cm}.
\]

The finite positive output is then constructed by a rational linear programme. The novelty lies in acquiring the required moments from two Bernoulli collision fields while the convolution factor is itself reconstructed from the same data. The factorial/exponential conditioning is not claimed as optimal.

## 5. Joint support and distribution inference with unknown noise

A closer blind statistical comparison is:

- J. Capitao-Miniconi, E. Gassiat, and L. Lehericy, *Support and distribution inference from noisy data*, arXiv:2304.09452.

That work studies simultaneous support and distribution inference in additive models without known noise or auxiliary noise samples, under structural independence and analytic/geometric assumptions. It belongs to the same broad family of joint signal/noise identification problems.

A2 obtains identification from a different source: the collision geometry produces three support components with an exact cancellation, and the same-law endpoint identity produces occupation. Its launch law need not have independent coordinate blocks and may be singular. Conversely, the A2 data are actively controlled whole-plane fields and use strict obstacle separation. A theorem-level comparison should make these tradeoffs explicit.

## 6. Bounded variation and response prediction

The weak prediction theorem uses finite-perimeter collision indicators, coupling in `W_1`, and the translation inequality for functions of bounded variation. The strong density and uniform-response result adds a known `BV` bound on the zero-extended density.

Primary references include:

- B. Galerne, *Computation of the perimeter of measurable sets via their covariogram. Applications to random sets*, Image Analysis and Stereology **30** (2011), 39–51;
- L. Ambrosio, N. Fusco, and D. Pallara, *Functions of Bounded Variation and Free Discontinuity Problems*, Oxford University Press, 2000.

The local spatial `L^1` prediction for arbitrary compact laws is the natural conclusion obtainable from `W_1` and bounded variation of the collision indicator. Pointwise uniform prediction requires the additional density regularity and is correctly stated separately.

## 7. Relation to classical billiard rigidity

The data in v40 are two complete active collision-probability fields. They are not a marked length spectrum, a scattering relation, a trajectory law, a count germ, or another standard passive billiard invariant. Comparisons with classical billiard rigidity therefore concern scope and motivation, not direct theorem inclusion.

The exact data reduction is nevertheless literal and substantial: only two fixed displacement vectors of one positive length are commanded. Whole-field data are common in inverse problems, and the theorem should not be dismissed merely because the input is infinite-dimensional. The relevant editorial question is whether the support cancellation and joint law/table factorization establish a sufficiently reusable inverse principle.

## 8. Literature conclusion

The focused search did not identify a cited predecessor containing the complete chain

1. two fixed opposite forward collision fields;
2. finite-prefix occupation recovery;
3. cancellation of one unknown launch support through three observable supports;
4. recovery of a strictly convex obstacle from the negative atoms of a body-minus-chord curvature measure;
5. exact identification of an arbitrary compact launch probability;
6. finite geometric, transportation, and predictive reconstruction from the same two commands.

This observation does not establish priority. Before acceptance at a top-four journal, the author should complete a broader search across blind morphology, inverse source/noise factorization, signed Minkowski reconstruction, and deconvolution with unknown compact factors. The final paper should then state a restrained novelty claim at theorem level.
