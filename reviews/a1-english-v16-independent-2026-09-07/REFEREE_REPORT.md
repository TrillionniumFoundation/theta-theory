## 4. Principal mathematical findings

### 4.1 The monomial theorem has actual attainable content

At the separated binomial witness, the true unnormalized product tangent has dimension \(n(r-1)+1\). This calculation survives additive collisions in future sums; it is not the dimension of a hypothetical unrestricted posterior family. Normalization then removes exactly the radial product direction. The mixed pairing with complete Newton prefixes remains full rank, including when repeated nodes require Hermite multiplicities. Compactness is applied over finitely many formal orders, not through an unjustified continuously chosen Leja order. [S5–S7]

The conversion to a measure lower bound is also real. The inverse-function argument augments the normalized moments by kernel coordinates and integrates over a whole kernel-coordinate box. A local section alone would have zero ambient command measure. The proof retains both the command density and the likelihood of the specified failures. [S4, S7]

For the upper bound, the entire posterior image is a bounded-format rational image of commands; the prior integrals are coefficients. No semialgebraicity or density of the prior is required. The thin-rectangle estimate truncates exterior products at the attainable dimension rather than at the full number of future formal labels. The real entropy input is correctly distinguished from the nonarchimedean results in the cited paper. [S6, S7; L3, L4]

Thus the profile

\[
\Psi_{n,m}(M,a)=
\max_{1\le\ell\le p_{n,m}}
\left(\frac{\mathcal V_{m,\ell}(a)}{M}\right)^{2/\ell}
\]

is supported by a local lower measure and a separate global upper construction. It is not inferred from a rank count. The raw-moment update has a uniformly positive evidence denominator and no reciprocal additive gap. Updating a reachable representative and quantizing again gives an actual causal filter. These are significant technical merits, even though they do not settle the editorial assessment. [S7; audit A2–A3]

### 4.2 The general criterion is correct as a sufficient criterion

Proposition 3.1 correctly uses \(P\in T\), not merely the dimension of an ambient test space. On \(L(T)\), the normalization map has kernel exactly \(\operatorname{span}(LP)\), after the stated scaling. Theorem 3.2 applies determinant integration to two strict Chebyshev systems, one lying in the true product tangent. Full support supplies positive mass to disjoint interior intervals; a Lebesgue density is unnecessary. The dominated-prior compactness argument is valid. [S4]

This is not a universal classification of positive detectors. Positivity alone neither identifies the true tangent nor provides the requisite test system or observation scales. The manuscript acknowledges this. That limitation belongs to an assessment of its reach, not to a charge that the theorem fails to prove its stated conclusion.

### 4.3 Both circular contrast factors are necessary and correctly retained

The Laurent product gives

\[
p_j=\tau^jP_{n,j}(\tau^2),\qquad
c_j=\tau^jS_j,\qquad Y_j=\tau^{2j}S_j.
\]

At zero contrast the normalized \(S\)-map is the elementary-symmetric coefficient map. Its differential at distinct interior complex coordinates has full complex rank, hence the required real ranks for initial flags. Continuity supplies a small contrast interval for each fixed finite horizon. The physical state nevertheless collapses to zero at \(\tau=0\); the auxiliary extension is not information acquired at zero contrast. [S8]

The finite physical query menu gives an exact discrete Fourier norm, uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean norm of \(Y\). Consequently the real side orders occur in pairs:

\[
\tau^2,\tau^2,\tau^4,\tau^4,\ldots,\tau^{2k},\tau^{2k},
\qquad k=\min(n,m).
\]

The even exterior products yield

\[
\mathcal H_{n,m}(M,\tau)
=\max_{1\le j\le k}\tau^{2(j+1)}M^{-1/j};
\]

the odd products add no larger branch. The new one-past/one-future computation gives \(\rho^2\tau^4|z-z'|^2/2\), and agrees with the theorem. Replacing the acquisition factor by one would give the wrong power, but the submitted proof does not do so. [S3, S8; audit A4]

The weighted update

\[
Y'_j=
\frac{Y_j+\tau^2zY_{j-1}+\bar zY_{j+1}}
 {1+z\overline{Y_1}+\bar zY_1}
\]

is correctly indexed and normalized. Its denominator is bounded below by \(1-\tau\) on mixtures of reachable posterior states. The shrinking future index contains the needed coordinates; harmonics above past degree are exactly zero. A finite-horizon error recurrence, not an illicit exact-prefix restart, proves the causal assertion. The theorem bounds the maximum-checkpoint profile, not the separate optimum at every checkpoint simultaneously. [S8, S13]

### 4.4 Prior ambiguity is distinct from acquired memory

The finite-radius dual construction correctly normalizes the pulled-back prior:

\[
\frac{d\mu'}{d\mu}
 =\frac{1+s f_v}{1+s\mu f_v},\qquad
\frac{d\nu'}{d\nu}=1+s f_v.
\]

Centering \(f_v\) under the posterior does not generally center it under the prior. The denominator is essential at a nonconstant history, and it is present. The simultaneous cube construction, followed by an arbitrary linear observation map, also remains valid when that map has a kernel. The complete-flag covariance argument supports all stated width orders. [S9, S10]

Exact raw-prefix constraints justify exact triangular substitution; they are not noisy data. The physical event-weighted example retains its probability \(2^{-n}\). In the common-name problem the two priors instead share one imperfect supplied name and have overlapping, not identical, history laws. The posterior-separation calculation is correct. The common-program upper bound does not secretly consult the unknown center: its representatives and numerical decisions use the same supplied data. I checked the construction contract, stopping-scale argument, and profile-stability reduction, but not every implementation and workspace certificate. [S10–S12; audit A5]

## 5. E16.1 — Correct the command-alphabet wording

**Severity: minor expository correction with a resource-model consequence; not a counterexample to the formally stated theorem.**

In `sections/structural_comparison.tex`, lines 79–80, the new text says that the four-cell interface admits “the specified finite detector commands.” In `sections/circular.tex`, lines 18–19 and 37–55, however, the controller uses four real acceptance probabilities ranging over a cube, and the lower bound uses independent uniform commands on that continuous cube. A finite detector, a finite report alphabet, a finite query menu, and a finite command alphabet are different notions. [S1, S8]

A literal finite-command reading would change the result. With \(B<\infty\) available commands and five reports, there are at most \((5B)^n\) command-report histories at checkpoint \(n\). At fixed horizon, an index alphabet of size \((5B)^{N-1}\) can store every acquisition prefix exactly. With the model in read-only data it gives zero prediction loss, contradicting any strictly positive lower profile for all finite \(M\) and \(\tau>0\). This elementary argument applies to that alternative finite-alphabet model, **not** to the continuous-command model actually defined in the paper. [Audit A6]

Replace the offending phrase by, for example, “commands acting on the specified four-cell detector, with acceptance probabilities in the continuous gate cube.” Keep the finite query menu and the finite persistent-label alphabet separate. No change to the theorem, command cube, proof, or scope is called for.

## 6. E16.2 — Why the four-journal recommendation remains negative

**This is an editorial assessment, not an additional correctness defect.**

The new comparison successfully narrows the originality claim. Van den Berg already develops exact Fourier posterior representations and coefficient updates; de Neeve and coauthors treat reduced-contrast likelihoods and Fourier recursions in adaptive phase estimation. Those works do not establish the present finite-label minimax theorem, because the resource and objective differ. The submission neither subsumes them nor becomes unoriginal merely by sharing their representation. [L1, L2; S1]

The strongest demonstrated contribution is the matching of *attainability*, *observation scales*, *global entropy*, and *causal compatibility* uniformly through arbitrary additive collisions. This is more substantial than a standalone Vandermonde bound, and I give it full credit. The circular example genuinely adds acquisition-side degeneration, rather than merely changing the final observation matrix. The criticism must concern this actual contribution, not an earlier, weaker version of the paper.

Nevertheless, the formulas become a general rectangular coding calculation once three obligations are met: a minorized attainable flag, a dimension-controlled global cover, and a uniformly Lipschitz stage update in the physical coordinates. Audit A3 spells out that reduction. In the monomial model the substantive verification rests on the separated binomial tangent and complete Chebyshev–Newton pairing. In the circular model it rests on elementary-symmetric coefficients, Haar extraction, and a deliberately orthogonal finite query menu. The general positive-history proposition isolates a useful step but does not remove those experiment-specific obligations.

In my judgment, the resulting work is a technically coherent specialized theory, not yet a contribution of the demonstrated breadth or decisive conceptual impact needed for the requested venue. The ambiguity and common-advice developments add careful consequences and resource distinctions, but do not, in this submission, change that judgment about the central advance. This is not an argument that classical methods, a fixed horizon, or a special prior are intrinsically disqualifying. Any of them may occur in an outstanding paper. It is an assessment of the strength of the actual theorem package after its reductions have been made explicit.

The updated exposition has made the case assessable; it has not compelled a positive decision. I have not located a primary source containing the identical collision-uniform attainable classification or circular finite-label law, and I make **no exhaustive priority determination**. Another qualified specialist may weigh the significance differently. That uncertainty belongs to the editorial recommendation and must not be converted into a fabricated mathematical error.

I therefore recommend rejection rather than another incremental “major revision” whose real condition would be to persuade this referee of importance. Correcting E16.1 is warranted, but cannot honestly be presented as the change that would by itself reverse the recommendation. No deletion of valid results, weakening of theorem statements, or serial addition of applications is prescribed.
