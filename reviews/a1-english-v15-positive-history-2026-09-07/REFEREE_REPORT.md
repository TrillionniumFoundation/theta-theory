## 4. Mathematical findings

### 4.1 Proposition 3.1 has the correct normalization and measure argument

Let \(T\) be the true product tangent, \(P\in T\), \(Z=\mu P\), and \(LQ=(\mu Q,\mu(Q\phi))\). With \(v=LP/Z\), the normalized derivative is

\[
Z^{-1}(LQ-v\,e_0LQ).
\]

Since \(e_0v=1\) and \(v\in L(T)\), the map on \(L(T)\) loses exactly the one-dimensional space spanned by \(v\). The proof is not an unjustified subtraction of one from an ambient dimension. [S3]

For the quantitative assertion, the square map consisting of the prediction coordinates and orthonormal kernel coordinates has a uniformly invertible central derivative. Its local inverse has a Jacobian bounded below using the forward derivative bound. The proof then integrates over a full kernel-coordinate box and multiplies by the actual command density and report evidence. This is precisely what is needed to convert a local section into a subprobability minorization. A section alone would have zero ambient command measure; the submitted proof does not make that error.

The constants depend on the finite dimensions, interior margin, likelihood lower bound, command density and derivative bounds. They do not depend on a later observation map's small singular values. Nothing in this proposition claims that positivity alone guarantees a submersion.

### 4.2 Theorem 3.2 is valid under its actual-tangent hypotheses

The determinant-integration argument pairs two strict Chebyshev systems. Their evaluation determinants have compatible fixed signs on ordered interior tuples, and full support gives positive mass to a product of disjoint interior intervals. This proves nonvanishing of the mixed pairing without assuming a prior density. The detector's command-to-factor surjection then transfers the conclusion to actual controls. [S3]

The compact-family statement also withstands scrutiny. For dominated priors, a weak limit remains between the same measure envelopes and hence retains full support. Uniform-norm continuity of tests, likelihood factors and command derivatives makes the normalized Jacobian continuous in the varying prior and calibration. A sequence of vanishing least row singular values would contradict the pointwise determinant conclusion at the limit. A continuously selected Chebyshev basis is not needed for that compactness argument.

The result is a useful sufficient criterion. It is not a classification of arbitrary positive finite-dimensional detectors: the existence of an appropriate subsystem in the true product tangent and the Chebyshev property of the future tests remain substantive hypotheses. The manuscript says so, and this scope is not a defect.

### 4.3 The principal monomial theorem retains the essential attainable content

The separated binomial tangent has dimension \(n(r-1)+1\), even when future additive sums collide. Complete Newton prefixes, including all required Hermite multiplicities, preserve the mixed pairing. Normalization removes exactly one direction. Compactness is taken over finitely many formal orders, so discontinuities of a greedy Leja choice do not invalidate uniformity. [S6, S7]

The global upper bound is not inferred from the local lower cube. The scaled posterior image is a rational image of a bounded command cube, with positive polynomial evidence and bounded semialgebraic format. Prior integrals are coefficients, so no definability of the prior is required. Its dimension bound is \(n(r-1)\), and the real thin-rectangle estimate truncates the products accordingly. The cited real variation inequality is indeed a real statement recalled in equations (4)–(5) of Comte–Halupczok; no transfer from their nonarchimedean theorem is needed. The rational-image and semialgebraic regularity inputs are present in Zhang–Kileel, Lemmas 2.17–2.18. [S7, S8; L1, L2]

Finally, the recurrence uses raw moments and a denominator bounded below on mixtures of reachable posteriors. It has no reciprocal collision gap. Updating a reachable representative remains within the next reachable set, and the finite error recurrence provides one stage-compatible filter. This is a genuine additional obligation beyond a covering theorem. [S7, S10]

### 4.4 The new circular acquisition lemma is not an ambient-rank argument

Writing

\[
P(w)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\{1+\tau(z_iw+\bar z_iw^{-1})\}
       =\sum_jp_jw^j,
\]

one obtains the exact factorization \(p_j=\tau^jP_{n,j}(\tau^2)\). Haar normalization gives

\[
c_j=p_j/p_0=\tau^jS_j,
\qquad Y_j=\tau^jc_j=\tau^{2j}S_j.
\]

At zero contrast the normalized map \((S_1,\ldots,S_n)\) is the elementary-symmetric coefficient map. At a fixed tuple of distinct interior complex coordinates its complex differential is invertible, and hence its real differential has rank \(2n\). This proves all initial harmonic flags have the required real ranks. Continuity yields a common small interval for the finitely many checkpoint dimensions. The command right inverse is physical, and the evidence is \(p_0\prod_i a_i\), not one. [S4]

The nonsingular normalized extension at \(\tau=0\) must not be confused with physical information at zero. The physical state \(Y\) is identically zero there. The proof correctly makes this distinction.

### 4.5 The physical metric and the contrast powers are correct

The fixed menu of \(2m+1\) attainable repeated-failure queries yields an affine Fourier prediction vector. Its nonconstant coefficient factors \(B_{m,j}(\tau^2)\) have positive constant terms. Exact discrete orthogonality therefore gives

\[
\frac1{2m+1}\sum_l|q_l(h)-q_l(h')|^2
 =2^{1-2m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}B_{m,j}(\tau^2)^2
                 |Y_j(h)-Y_j(h')|^2.
\]

This is a uniformly conditioned metric in \(Y\), not in the unweighted posterior coefficients. Both the acquisition and future attenuation have been retained. Orthogonal projection of arbitrary decoder vectors onto the affine Fourier span is legitimate for the converse. [S4]

The real rectangle has paired side orders

\[
\tau^2,\tau^2,\tau^4,\tau^4,\ldots,\tau^{2k},\tau^{2k},
\qquad k=\min(n,m).
\]

Its even exterior products give exactly \(\tau^{2(j+1)}M^{-1/j}\). Odd orders do not create an omitted phase: each interior odd log-term is a convex combination of its neighboring even log-terms, and the first odd term is bounded by the second-order term for \(M\ge1\). The small-integer-budget treatment is present. The lower bound uses the image measure of actual acquisition commands and failures, not a hypothetical uniform posterior law. [S4; audit A5]

### 4.6 The circular causal theorem is proved in the right coordinates

The submitted update

\[
Y'_j=\frac{Y_j+\tau^2zY_{j-1}+\bar zY_{j+1}}
                 {1+z\overline{Y_1}+\bar zY_1}
\]

is correct. Its denominator is at least \(1-\tau\), including on the segment joining two reachable posterior states. The coefficients are uniformly bounded, so the update is Lipschitz without dividing by contrast. The shrinking future index contains every coordinate required by the next update, and harmonics above the past degree are exactly zero before the changeover. [S4]

Nearest-representative quantization after each exact representative update gives \(e_{n+1}\le Le_n+r_{n+1}\). At fixed horizon, this proves the maximum-checkpoint law. It does not assert that the same filter achieves each checkpoint's separately optimal profile; the theorem and proof ledger correctly distinguish those statements. The converse uses prefixes of the same independent exploration law. The exact continuous dimension follows from the local physical prediction patch and the Fourier sufficiency argument. I found no missing causal lower or upper step here. [S4, S0]

### 4.7 The prior-ambiguity and common-name conclusions remain separately scoped

Proposition 8.1 has the correct finite-radius pullback

\[
d\mu'=\frac{1+s f_v}{1+s\mu f_v}\,d\mu,
\qquad d\nu'=(1+s f_v)\,d\nu.
\]

The prior denominator is essential at a nonconstant history. The bound on \(f_v\) is uniform over an entire normalized cube, so the result is a simultaneous body inclusion and not a collection of incompatible directional alternatives. Applying an arbitrary observation map then gives the stated inclusions even when the map has a kernel. [S5]

The retained complete-flag covariance argument and active triangular block give all width orders. Exact prefix constraints permit exact back substitution through nonzero pivots; this is not a claim of stable inversion from noisy data. The event-weighted constant-failure realization counts its probability \(2^{-n}\). The local problem is not identified with the later common-name minimax experiment. [S5]

In the common-name section, the two tilted priors share the supplied name because the center has the stated interior and accuracy slack. Their history laws overlap rather than coincide. The posterior-separation identity is correct, and the memory lower bound and prior-ambiguity lower bound combine through a maximum. The upper argument explicitly invokes a common construction and profile stability; it does not supply the unknown center to the program. I checked that logical reduction, not a new implementation-level proof of the invoked appendices. [S9]

## 5. Why I do not recommend acceptance at the requested level

### E15.1 — The new circular result needs comparison with the closest Bayesian Fourier literature

The introduction now compares its analytic inputs at theorem level, which answers an earlier request. For the new circular application, however, clustered or nonuniform Fourier evaluation matrices are not the closest comparison for the Bayesian representation and update. Neither of the following works appears in the two inspected bibliography inputs. [S2, S11]

Van den Berg's *Efficient Bayesian phase estimation using mixed priors*, Quantum 5 (2021), 469, Section 2.2 and equation (15), already treats exact Fourier posterior representations under trigonometric likelihoods and their coefficient updates. Its treatment of truncation addresses a different computational issue from the present fixed-label minimax problem. [L3]

De Neeve, Lebedev, Negnevitsky and Home's *Time-adaptive phase estimation*, Physical Review Research 7 (2025), 023070, has reduced-contrast likelihoods and an explicit Bayesian Fourier-coefficient recursion; see Section II, equations (1)–(3) of the inspected preprint. Its adaptive phase-estimation objective and coefficient-storage discussion are not the same resource or loss as Theorem 7.1. [L4]

These comparisons do **not** show that the submitted minimax theorem is already known. They show why Fourier closure, neighboring-coefficient updates and the possibility of storing a finite Fourier state cannot carry its originality claim. The distinction to establish is the uniform acquisition-side minorization, the resulting paired physical metric, and the matching fixed-\(M\) causal coding law under this actual finite detector. The absence of that comparison is a material positioning deficiency, not a counterexample.

**Required response:** add a compact comparison stating what is inherited at the representation/update level, what differs in the physical experiment and information convention, and what new quantitative claim is proved. Do not equate “number of Fourier coefficients” with “number of persistent labels.” Do not claim either priority or subsumption without a matching theorem. Adding citations alone, without that comparison, would not settle the question of incremental content.

### E15.2 — A correct second example does not yet establish the paper's exceptional mathematical significance

This is an editorial assessment, not a second independent correctness defect. The strongest part of the paper remains the identification of actual past-limited directions uniformly through arbitrary additive collisions. It is more than a Vandermonde estimate: the complete-flag pairing, global dimension-truncated cover and causal compatibility have to match. That deserves serious credit.

The general theorem isolates the rank-to-measure step, but it assumes the difficult geometric ingredient in the form of two suitable test systems, one inside the actual tangent. In the circular example, the problem-specific additional identification reduces to a degree-filtered Laurent product, an elementary-symmetric submersion, and a finite orthogonal query menu. Once those facts are established, the profile follows from rectangular quantization and a bounded finite-horizon recurrence. The audit gives that reduction explicitly; the assessment is not based on dismissing unfamiliar mathematics as “routine.” [S3, S4; audit A1–A6]

There is a real advance over revision 14: acquisition and observation now degenerate together within one experiment. Nevertheless, the manuscript still reads to me as a coherent, specialized quantitative theory whose central structural calculation generates a number of carefully developed consequences. The ambiguity result is now correctly presented as finite-dimensional bounded duality, and the noisy-name floor as a visible prior-uncertainty direction. The second application broadens the evidence, but does not, in my judgment, yet demonstrate mathematical force commensurate with the requested publication level. [S4, S5, S9]

This conclusion does not follow merely from the use of classical tools, a fixed horizon, or special hypotheses. Any of those can occur in an outstanding paper. It follows from my assessment of the actual incremental contribution after the reductions and distinctions above. I have not found a primary source containing the identical positive-history classification or circular finite-label law, and I do not assert that either theorem is unoriginal. A different expert may assess their significance more favorably; that uncertainty belongs to the recommendation itself.

A response should make the best case for the proved results, not start an endless sequence of extra applications or manufacture a stronger claim than the proof supports. More source-preservation counts, more passing arithmetic tests, or another equivalent phase formula would not answer this reservation. Nor does this report ask for deletion or weakening of complete valid results.

## 6. Limited editorial guidance

Keep the distinction between the two meanings of “uniform” visible: the circular comparison is uniform in \(M\) and \(\tau\) on an interval chosen for each fixed \(N\), not uniform as \(N\to\infty\). The manuscript currently makes the necessary restriction; preserve it in any shorter abstract or introduction.

A concise comparison of the monomial and circular coordinate chains would improve exposition: the former has a uniformly attained normalized flag followed by collision-dependent observation scales, whereas the latter has acquired coefficients \(c_j=\tau^jS_j\) and physical coordinates \(Y_j=\tau^{2j}S_j\). This is a suggestion about explanatory economy, not a missing lemma. The read-only program and known-contrast convention should remain adjacent to the causal claim whenever it is summarized. [S2–S4, S10]

Retain all-width-orders language and the exact-prefix/common-name distinction. Do not describe the circular intrinsic code as an effective compiler: no such implementation is claimed or audited here. I make no criticism of the PDF's typography or layout, which I did not inspect.
