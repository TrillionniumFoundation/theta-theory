# 3. Independent proof audit of the higher-defect theorem

## 3.1 Stabilization after unit normalization appears sound

The argument chooses a local unit a in the universal generating bundle and normalizes W=a^{-1}A so that 1 is in W.

The claimed stabilization

\[
W^{d-1}=\mathcal O[W],\qquad W^m=W^{d-1}\quad(m\ge d-1)
\]

then identifies the image of the original multiplication map with a^m times the generated algebra.

Within the manuscript's stated finite locally free setting, this is a legitimate way to make all Fitting ideals of the stable cokernel independent of m.

I found no contradiction here.

## 3.2 The canonical stable algebra E is a useful construction

On an exact-rank-s stratum the image is locally free, and the manuscript identifies it with a unital subalgebra bundle E.

The change-of-unit argument using Cayley-Hamilton is the correct issue to check. If b is another unit in A, then b/a is a unit in B and lies in E; Cayley-Hamilton expresses its inverse in E because the constant term of its characteristic polynomial is a unit. This gives equality of the generated algebras on overlaps.

That is a real relative statement, not merely a fibrewise classification.

## 3.3 The conductor kernel is correctly identified on exact-rank strata

For M=B/E the action

\[
\theta:E\longrightarrow \mathcal E nd(M)
\]

has kernel equal to

\[
J=\{b\in B:bB\subset E\}.
\]

The manuscript correctly notes that bB subset E forces b in E by multiplying by 1, so the displayed set is indeed the action kernel.

On an exact-rank-t stratum, pivot reduction gives a locally free kernel and image, so the base-change statement is appropriately restricted to the stratum.

This restriction is essential and is one of the strongest formal improvements in v118.

## 3.4 The quotient-flag equivalence is meaningful but narrower than the top-level narrative

The flag

\[
C=E/J\subset Q=B/J
\]

retains more information than a list of closed-point subalgebra types. In particular, it keeps the specified inclusion and the faithful action on Q/C.

However, this equivalence describes stable generated subalgebras together with their conductor quotient data.

It does not classify the whole multiplication-failure scheme.

That distinction is not semantic. It is exactly the distinction between the theorem the paper has and the theorem needed to support a broad “higher-defect failure geometry” claim.

# 4. The Fitting-index gap remains the central mathematical limitation

Proposition prop:extreme-corank is unusually important because it states, in effect, the limit of the new theory.

For a codimension-r unital subbundle W, the subalgebra locus is

\[
V\!\left(
\operatorname{Fitt}_{r-1}
\operatorname{coker}(\operatorname{Sym}^mW\to B)
\right),
\qquad m\ge2.
\]

For r=1 this is the ordinary nonsurjectivity scheme.

For r>1 it is not.

The full multiplication-failure scheme is controlled by Fitt_0. The new conductor theorem instead naturally controls an extreme-corank stratum at a different Fitting index.

This is the single most important mathematical boundary in the paper.

A top-four advance would need to show how the conductor flag controls substantial geometry of Fitt_0 itself:

- irreducible components;
- generic coranks;
- closures of exact-rank strata;
- singularities;
- normalizations;
- associated primes;
- embedded structure;
- or a dense-open normal form.

v118 does not yet do this.

No amount of terminology can convert Fitt_{r-1} into Fitt_0.

# 5. Codimension two: correct low-rank mechanism, incomplete failure geometry

## 5.1 The rank-at-most-two argument appears valid

When M=B/E has rank two, the manuscript decomposes the image matrices into scalar and trace-zero parts.

Because the trace-zero parts commute and

\[
\bigwedge^2\mathfrak{sl}_2\longrightarrow\mathfrak{sl}_2
\]

is an isomorphism over the characteristic-zero base, their wedges vanish.

Adding the scalar identity line gives the scheme-theoretic rank bound I_3(theta)=0.

I find this mechanism convincing in the stated setting.

## 5.2 The cyclic-vector step should be promoted to a formal relative lemma

On the exact-rank-two stratum, the manuscript writes C=<1,T> locally and uses the fact that a nonscalar 2 by 2 matrix has a cyclic vector.

Fibrewise this is standard.

For publication at the claimed level, I would isolate the relative assertion:

- define the determinant of the map C -> M, c |-> cv;
- show the cyclic-vector locus is open;
- prove these opens cover the exact-rank-two stratum;
- record base-change compatibility explicitly.

I do not regard the present argument as obviously false. I regard it as too compressed for a theorem whose importance is being used to justify a new quotient geometry.

## 5.3 The result is still a 2 by 2 representation-theoretic dichotomy

The two cases are:

- action rank one: a rank-three quotient with scalar subalgebra;
- action rank two: a rank-four quotient Q locally free of rank two over a rank-two algebra C.

This is elegant.

It is also special.

The classification is driven by the unusually rigid algebra of commuting endomorphisms in dimension two. Once rank(B/E)>=3, commuting matrix algebras have much richer moduli.

Thus the codimension-two theorem should be sold as a complete low-rank quotient-module case, not as evidence that general higher-defect geometry has been solved.

## 5.4 The theorem has not yet been converted into codimension-two Fitt_0 geometry

This is the missing theorem.

The current paper should be able to answer, but does not answer, questions such as:

- Are the rank-three and rank-four quotient mechanisms generic points of distinct components of the full nonsurjectivity scheme?
- Is one in the closure of the other?
- What is the local equation at their meeting locus?
- Does the nonreduced s^2=0 action-rank jump occur as a systematic embedded or tangential structure in Fitt_0?
- What are the expected dimensions and actual dimensions?
- Which component dominates which quotient incidence?
- What is the primary structure at a generic boundary point?

Without this, the quotient dichotomy is a structure theorem about a selected locus, not a classification of the failure scheme.

# 6. The nonreduced multigenerator theorem is correct-looking but lives in the easiest primary regime

The truncated local algebra is

\[
B_{e,h}=\mathbb C[z_1,\ldots,z_e]/(z_1,\ldots,z_e)^h.
\]

On the ideal-generating (e+1)-plane Grassmannian, after choosing a unit and normalizing,

\[
A=\langle 1,x_1,\ldots,x_e\rangle.
\]

The stable multiplication matrix becomes the substitution map

\[
T_x:B_{e,h}\to B_{e,h},\qquad z_i\mapsto x_i.
\]

The m-adic filtration gives blocks Sym^j M, hence

\[
\det T_x
=
\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}\det(\operatorname{Sym}^jM)
=
(\det M)^N,
\qquad
N=\binom{e+h-1}{e+1}.
\]

The exponent identity is consistent with the standard symmetric-power determinant character and the hockey-stick sum.

I found no algebraic contradiction in this calculation.

But the geometric consequence is exactly the limitation:

\[
\operatorname{Fitt}_0\operatorname{coker}(\operatorname{Sym}^mA\to B_{e,h})
=
\mathcal I_\Delta^N.
\]

The entire stable primary geometry is a power of one integral Cartier divisor.

Once that identity is known, the following conclusions are essentially formal:

- one associated prime;
- no embedded associated point;
- primaryity of every positive power;
- nilradical index N;
- generic transverse length N.

This is nonreduced geometry, but it is the least complicated nonreduced geometry available.

# 7. The support Delta is first-order algebra-generation failure

The paper's word “generating” is dangerous here.

The open X is an ideal/module-generating open: augmentation is surjective, equivalently A contains a unit locally.

It is not the open of subspaces that generate B as a unital algebra.

After normalization, the map

\[
K\to \mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2
\]

has determinant Delta.

Off Delta, the classes of x_1,...,x_e span m/m^2. By Nakayama they generate m, hence 1,x_1,...,x_e generate the truncated local algebra.

Therefore Delta is precisely the first-order failure of minimal algebra generation inside the larger unit-generating parameter space.

This makes the theorem clean, but it also locates its novelty boundary:

- the support is the familiar determinant rank-drop locus;
- the new information is the exact multiplicity N as a multiplication Fitting scheme;
- higher coefficients of the x_i do not change the stable ideal.

This should be stated in the abstract-level novelty claim, not buried in proof mechanics.

# 8. Higher jets disappear completely from the flagship primary law

The proof explicitly says that no coefficient of degree at least two is used in the determinant calculation.

That is mathematically attractive.

It is also a severe significance constraint.

Arbitrarily high nilpotence order h changes only the exponent N. It does not introduce:

- new support components;
- new associated primes;
- an embedded component;
- a non-Cartier primary support;
- a higher-jet-sensitive equation;
- a new normalization problem;
- or an interaction of multiple conductor strata.

In other words, the family grows in dimension and multiplicity without growing in qualitative primary complexity.

For a specialist paper, that rigidity may be the point.

For a top-four paper, it is not enough to say “arbitrary e and h” if the geometry remains the same determinant divisor with a larger power.

# 9. The paper already knows a harder local primary phenomenon but does not systematize it

The earlier/hyperplane material contains an example with embedded structure of the form

\[
(ab,b^2)=(b)\cap(a,b)^2.
\]

That tiny example is qualitatively more interesting as primary geometry than the flagship power I_Delta^N.

This creates an obvious challenge for the paper.

A genuine next theorem should take such phenomena out of isolated charts and produce a systematic multigenerator family with:

- multiple associated primes;
- embedded components;
- non-Cartier support;
- higher-jet-dependent equations;
- or interacting conductor strata.

Until then, the phrase “complete nonreduced multigenerator geometry” is too broad.

# 10. The global fat-point transport theorem is a strong transport lemma, not a higher-dimensional classification

The theorem claims, for n>=2h-1,

\[
WU^{m-1}=H^0(\mathbb P^e,\mathcal I_p^h(mn)).
\]

The proof covers the degree interval by the integer intervals [jh,jn], factors monomials and uses a unit u to construct a triangular basis.

I find the basic combinatorial argument plausible and internally coherent.

But one should describe what has been achieved accurately.

The support is one standard homogeneous fat point. The proof is monomial and triangular. The local algebra is maximally symmetric.

The theorem does not yet treat:

- unions of fat points;
- mixed multiplicities;
- nonmonomial Artin quotients;
- local complete intersections with varying tangent data;
- families with changing Hilbert function;
- or nonhomogeneous local algebras where the associated graded object varies.

Thus it is a transport theorem for one exceptionally tractable class, not a classification theorem in higher-dimensional projective geometry.

# 11. The advertised uniform sharpness is not sharpness for the flagship rank

At the boundary n=2h-2, the counterexample chooses A=<1>, hence k=1.

The flagship nonreduced theorem uses k=e+1.

The manuscript responsibly says that no rank-specific sharpness is asserted.

That sentence should be given more weight.

The theorem establishes a uniform bound over all generating dimensions and shows that no smaller universal bound works because the rank-one case fails.

It does not show that 2h-1 is the optimal threshold for the actual (e+1)-plane family driving the main primary corollary.

A top-level next result would determine the rank-sensitive threshold, ideally with a formula analogous in spirit to the sharp rank dependence already available in the curve setting.

# 12. The two principal v118 mechanisms still do not explain one another

The higher-defect mechanism produces:

- a stable generated algebra E;
- a conductor J;
- a quotient flag C subset Q;
- an action-rank stratification.

The truncated-local-algebra mechanism produces:

- a tangent matrix M;
- a substitution endomorphism T_x;
- a symmetric-power determinant character;
- a pure power I_Delta^N.

At present these are two separate successful calculations.

The conductor flag does not compute N.

The fat-point determinant does not reveal a nontrivial interaction of conductor strata.

The action-rank stratification does not explain the primary decomposition of a difficult multigenerator failure scheme.

For a theory paper, this is an architectural weakness.

The title now suggests a unified geometry of conductor strata and nonreduced failure schemes. The strongest new theorems remain parallel rather than causally linked.

## 13.1 Anna Torstensson, 2026

Anna Torstensson, *Subalgebras of the univariate polynomial algebra*, Applicable Algebra in Engineering, Communication and Computing, published 19 January 2026.

The paper develops the finite-codimension subalgebra theory further and contains an explicit conductor-ideal theorem: the conductor is the largest K[x]-ideal contained in the subalgebra, generated by a minimal polynomial, with the finite quotient A/I(A) playing a structural role.

This does not automatically imply the manuscript's relative conductor flags or Fitting identities.

It does mean that any novelty discussion of univariate conductor/subalgebra structure in September 2026 must compare against this paper explicitly.

## 13.2 Erik Leffler, 2026

Erik Leffler, *Describing multivariate polynomial subalgebras using equations*, Applicable Algebra in Engineering, Communication and Computing 37 (2026), 1081-1114, published 19 June 2026, DOI 10.1007/s00200-026-00743-8.

The paper studies finite-codimension subalgebras of multivariate polynomial rings, including filtrations by kernels of linear functionals and descriptions by equality/derivative conditions.

Again, this does not by itself anticipate the manuscript's universal Grassmannian Fitting schemes.

But it is too close to the paper's higher-defect/subalgebra narrative to omit from a current top-four priority audit.

# 17. Proof-level revisions required even for a specialist submission

I did not find a fatal proof error, but I would require the following formal improvements.

## 17.1 Rename the fat-point parameter open

Use “unit-generating” or “ideal-generating” rather than unqualified “generating” whenever the distinction matters.

State explicitly that Delta is the complement of the algebra-generating locus inside that larger open.

## 17.2 Make Delta intrinsically determinantal

Define Delta globally from

\[
K\to(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\otimes\mathcal O_X
\]

and identify it with the standard determinant/Schubert divisor.

Compute its determinant line bundle or divisor class.

This is cleaner than proving primeness primarily by passing to a frame chart with a generic determinant and then descending.

## 17.3 Isolate the relative cyclic-vector lemma

The codimension-two rank-two action case deserves its own lemma with:

- an explicit cyclicity determinant;
- the open cover;
- rank-one freeness over C;
- and base-change compatibility.

## 17.4 State the geometric meaning of Fitt_0=0 below threshold

For m<h-1 the zeroth Fitting ideal is zero.

Since the failure scheme is defined by that ideal, the failure scheme is all of X.

Say so explicitly.

## 17.5 Construct the moving-fat-point space canonically

For moving p in P^e, write the relative jet algebra, relative augmentation ideal, relative Grassmannian, universal kernel K and determinant line map.

Then prove integrality and Cartier structure of the total Delta from the relative construction, not only from coordinate-local descriptions.

# 18. What a genuine v119 would need to prove

A real v119 should not be another source-repackaging round.

The branch name “full-failure-embedded-conductor” suggests the right ambition, but the theorem is currently absent.

The next revision would materially change the assessment if it supplied one major theorem of the following type.

## 18.1 Full codimension-two Fitt_0 geometry

Use the rank-three/rank-four conductor quotient dichotomy to prove a theorem about the actual zeroth-Fitting nonsurjectivity scheme.

At minimum determine:

- major irreducible components;
- dimensions;
- generic coranks;
- closure relations;
- and primary behavior at their intersection.

This is the most direct way to turn the current quotient formalism into failure geometry.

## 18.2 An embedded/conductor primary theorem

Produce a systematic multigenerator class whose stable failure scheme has an embedded associated component or multiple associated primes, and prove the primary decomposition.

This would justify the phrase “embedded conductor” far more than a branch name does.

## 18.3 Higher-rank quotient geometry

For rank(B/E)>=3, replace the bare action-rank stratification by an actual moduli/equation theorem for faithful commutative subalgebras of End(M) arising from the multiplication problem.

The result must control equations or components, not merely define strata.

## 18.4 Conductor-to-primary mechanism

Prove that conductor/action data predicts primary exponents or associated primes in a nontrivial family.

This is the missing bridge between the two headline mechanisms.

## 18.5 Rank-sensitive global transport

Determine the optimal global conductor threshold for the flagship k=e+1 family, or a meaningful formula depending on e,h,k.

A counterexample only at k=1 is not enough for flagship sharpness.

## 18.6 Variable local algebra transport

Extend the global theorem beyond the homogeneous fat point to a family in which the local algebra genuinely varies.

Mixed fat points, unions, nonhomogeneous Artin algebras or local complete intersections would all test whether the method is structural or model-specific.
