## 2. What is observed, and what is actually claimed

For each selected marked clear channel, the exact datum supplies its gap and the two same-type signed conditional endpoint laws at a known sufficiently small positive excess time. The obstacle labels, deck gains, channel choices and signed intrinsic coordinate conventions belong to the design. The observation does not supply the Euclidean lattice vectors, their Gram form, an inter-channel pose, the unknown flux amplitudes, or a global registration. It is not an unmarked discovery theorem. [S01, S02, S08]

Each exact endpoint law is function-valued. A finite number of channels is not a finite vector of observed real numbers. The number N+1 is minimal for the stipulated architecture consisting of an obstacle spanning tree and two independent gain cycles; the manuscript expressly does not claim a universal information-theoretic lower bound.

The derivative experiment keeps the excess fixed in smoothly moving contact-centered coordinates. This choice provides the axis slices and nonzero interior anchors used in the density inverse. It is not automatically an experiment in one fixed laboratory chart with unknown, parameter-independent contact origins. Similarly, Theorem 20.6 selects scalar tests locally at a base table in an immersed finite-dimensional model; it does not supply one fixed finite vector identifying the entire infinite-dimensional analytic class, a uniform conditioning constant over that class, or a finite-sample efficiency theorem. These qualifications are present in the manuscript and must be respected in evaluating it. [S01, S03]

The quantitative histogram and physical acquisition results retain their own analytic, separation and asymmetry margins. Their calibration pilot may use richer planar-position information. Revision 49 does not turn those results into uniform statistical recovery over all noncircular obstacles, or into calibration from histograms alone. This separation is correct, not an omitted consequence of the new exact theorem. [S09, S10]

## 3. Fresh mathematical findings

### R49-C1 — finite congruence torsors and the actual local branch

**Location:** Lemmas 19.1–19.2, pages 78–79; S02, lines 14–85.

Centering at the first support harmonic is proper-Euclidean equivariant. A noncircular centered strictly convex body has a nonzero support harmonic z_k with k at least two. Its proper symmetry group is therefore contained in the finite set of k-th roots of unity. Intersecting the conditions from all nonzero harmonics gives a cyclic group of order

$$m(K)=\gcd\{k\ge2:z_k(K)\ne0\}.$$

The gcd of this nonempty infinite set is attained by a finite subset: a sequence of positive integer gcds stabilizes. The set of proper congruences between two copies is a torsor under this group, and the center identity determines the translation once the rotation is selected. This proves the finite count without a genericity assumption.

The local statement uses a different fact. Along the continuously followed actual congruence, for one nonzero harmonic,

$$r(t)=\frac{z_k(C_t(t))}{z_k(C_s(t))}=e^{-ik\alpha(t)}.$$

On the local logarithm through the actual base alignment,

$$U(t)=U_0\exp\{-k^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(r(t)/r(0))\},\qquad
\frac{\dot r}{r}=-ik\dot\alpha.$$

The exponent has the correct sign. This formula proves differentiability of the local branch from differentiability of the images; it is not a global phase selection from arbitrary image data. Stationary images give zero relative angular and translational velocity.

The explicit actual-branch premise matters. For a support function with a second harmonic, adding a small third harmonic can destroy its half-turn symmetry. An arbitrary second base congruence need not extend. The manuscript expressly excludes that incorrect inference. The independent control includes precisely this symmetry-breaking case and checks the angular-velocity sign.

**Finding:** both lemmas are valid in the stated setting. A finite exact ambiguity does not create an infinitesimal rotational kernel.

### R49-C2 — finite periodic enumeration: completeness, not merely a count

**Location:** Theorem 19.3, pages 79–80; S02, lines 89–182; the skeleton input in S08.

The proof does more than multiply symmetry orders. First the law inverse and analytic continuation recover the entire ordered pair of images in each channel frame. It then builds a spanning tree on channels, not just on obstacle labels, fixing the particular shared occurrences at each link. The connected obstacle graph makes this channel-incidence graph connected, including the one-obstacle case with loop channels.

Rooting one channel rotation removes the common rotational gauge. Every tree link offers exactly the finite number of congruences belonging to the shared obstacle, and the inverse rotation propagates the chosen global channel frame. All other repeated occurrences are subsequently checked. Thus inconsistent cycles are not silently assigned independent phases.

For a compatible assignment the center displacements D_e give the finite cochain

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_a&=\sum_{[1,a]}\epsilon_eD_e,&m_a&=\sum_{[1,a]}\epsilon_e\ell_e,\\
v_i&=p_{a_i}+D_{f_i}-p_{b_i},&\eta_i&=m_{a_i}+\ell_{f_i}-m_{b_i},\\
L&=(v_1\ v_2)M^{-1},&M&=(\eta_1\ \eta_2),\qquad c_a=p_a-Lm_a.
\end{aligned}
$$

The use of the actual M inverse is essential: independence of two integer gains over the reals is not unimodularity. The independent three-obstacle algebraic control takes det M=6 and detects omission of this inverse. Telescoping then verifies the displacement equations on every tree edge and on both remaining edges.

The determinant sign, periodic disjointness, closest-contact conditions and clear collars are genuinely checked, not inferred from the gain matrix. Once a candidate lattice is nonsingular, bounded representative bodies and the smallest singular value give a finite bound on lattice marks relevant to possible intersections. This is a finite family of exact geometric tests; it is not an effective numerical decision procedure for arbitrary finite-precision analytic input. The manuscript makes that distinction.

Finally, every realization induces an enumerated congruence assignment. Conversely, each retained candidate contains exactly the recovered ordered pairs up to proper motions, so it has their local actions, amplitudes and normalized conditional laws. The global phase-volume constant cancels under conditioning. These two directions close the exact-fiber argument. The product bound counts assignments and may overcount; no sharpness is asserted.

The earlier skeleton construction also survives the relevant check. Replacing an obstructed closest segment by two shorter obstacle connections reduces the length by a positive separation margin, giving finite descent; the resulting connections are graph edges, not claimed billiard trajectories. Selecting an obstacle tree and two independent gain cycles supplies the stated architecture without treating the Euclidean lattice as known. [S08]

**Finding:** I found no missing continuous placement parameter, untested periodic consistency, or hidden supplied registration in this proof. Section 4 below supplies an independent noncircular example showing that its finite ambiguity is real.

### R49-C3 — differentiating the moving reference and the normalization

**Location:** Lemma 20.1, pages 82–83; S03, especially lines 101–180; S07.

The C1 common-strip support hypothesis controls every separately fixed real smooth norm by Cauchy estimates. The moving contact equations have Hessian

$$\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_0+g^{-1}&-g^{-1}\\-g^{-1}&\kappa_1+g^{-1}\end{pmatrix},$$

whose determinant is strictly positive. The contact frame and gap therefore have the required local dependence. The weighted half-line inverse supplies finite-order endpoint and family derivatives after restricting to a common collar.

The new amplitude calculation now retains all moving-reference terms. Writing

$$H_t^0=H_t(0),\quad G_t=(H_t^0)^{-1},\quad
\Delta H_t=H_t(u)-H_t^0,\quad T_t=G_t\Delta H_t,$$

one obtains

$$\dot G=-G\dot H^0G,\qquad
\dot{\Delta H}=\dot H(u)-\dot H^0,\qquad
\dot T=\dot G\,\Delta H+G\dot{\Delta H}.$$

Subtraction occurs before the entrywise summability claim. Thus the constant infinite-volume variation of the reference Hessian is not erroneously called trace class. Endpoint decay and tridiagonality give a summable majorant for the subtracted perturbation and its fixed-order derivatives; fixed powers of indices are absorbed by a strict exponential margin. Multiplication by the bounded Green operators preserves the trace-class factor.

On a smaller collar, the operator norm is bounded by q<1. Cyclicity in each finite trace power and the bound q^(n-1) times the trace norm of dot T justify summing the differentiated logarithmic determinant. The edge-product reference contributes no residual infinite constant because its normalized value is identically one. Consequently

$$\frac{\dot B}{B}=\dot U-\operatorname{tr}((I+T)^{-1}\dot T).$$

Fixed endpoint derivatives add fixed polynomial factors to a convergent series. This is a legitimate functional-family argument at the interface used here, not differentiation of an arbitrary convergent sequence of finite-flight amplitudes. The independent noncommuting three-by-three control agrees with direct differentiation of the determinant quotient and detects omission of dot G. That control checks the algebra, not the infinite-operator estimates.

For the normalized endpoint density, with A=S(u)+S(v),

$$Z_t=\int w_t(d-A_t)_+,\qquad
\dot Z=\int\bigl[\dot w(d-A)_+-w\dot A\,\mathbf1_{\{A<d\}}\bigr].$$

At a positive regular cap level the boundary has planar measure zero. The positive-part difference quotient is uniformly dominated on the fixed integration box, so there is no missing first-order boundary Dirac term. The strong finite smooth density derivative is asserted on positive interior squares, where the cutoff is inactive, not indiscriminately across the moving support boundary.

**Finding:** the requested moving-reference expansion is now explicit and supported. R48-C1 should be closed, not recycled.

### R49-C4 — signed smooth inversion, analytic variation and the derivative kernel

**Location:** Lemmas 20.2–20.4 and Theorem 20.5, pages 83–86; S03–S06.

The four-density ratio eliminates both amplitudes and the normalizer. For a nonzero anchor a, put q=sqrt(1-R(a,a)) and t(u)=(1-R(u,a))/q. The derivative is

$$\dot t(u)=-\frac{\dot R(u,a)}q+
\frac{t(u)\dot R(a,a)}{2q^2},\qquad
\dot S(u)=\frac{d\dot t(u)}{(1+t(u))^2}.$$

The second term has a positive sign. The anchor is a scalar strictly away from the degeneracy at zero; there is no pointwise square-root operation at the action minimum. The contact-centered square contains the required slices. Zero density derivative yields zero action derivatives at every separately fixed finite order, including odd signed terms. The independent rational-function control tests cancellation with varying amplitudes and normalizer, and detects the wrong sign in the anchor derivative.

The finite graph-jet inverse is not merely formal. The smooth remainder argument interpolates actual graph representatives with the same finite jet, differentiates finite action sums, cancels the interior orbit variations by stationarity, and retains the terminal term until its exponential decay has been proved. The resulting remainder is bounded by a constant times |u|^(M+1) summed along a geometric tail. It factors the finite action jet through the finite graph jet before the polynomial inverse is used. Bounds on a list of coefficients alone are not substituted for the stipulated functional bounds. [S05]

At degree n the differentiation of s_n=M_n q_n+R_n retains both dot R_n and dot M_n q_n. Finite induction then gives zero graph-jet derivative. No inversion of an unrestricted infinite series, exchange with infinite differentiation, or uniform-in-order bound for the inverse blocks is needed.

The next identity-theorem step applies to the support variation itself. A C1 map into a fixed-strip holomorphic-support space gives a holomorphic derivative on a smaller strip after moving the contact frame. The derivative consists of translated support variations, rotation terms involving h prime, and first harmonics. Vanishing of all contact derivatives therefore kills the whole channel-frame image variation. Merely assuming that every parameter slice is analytic would not establish this step. Nor does exact analytic continuation give a bounded noisy inverse. [S03, S06, L4]

The local branch of R49-C1 now propagates zero relative angular velocities between the stationary images even when a finite rotational symmetry is present. The same cochain then gives zero gauge-fixed center and lattice derivatives. Undoing the gauge yields exactly common Euclidean infinitesimal motion,

$$\dot h_a(\theta)=b\cdot n(\theta)-\omega h_a'(\theta),\qquad
\dot L=\omega J L.$$

Zero derivatives of all compactly supported smooth interior expectations imply zero continuous interior density derivative: otherwise a small ball has a strict sign and admits a test detecting it. Thus the expectation formulation does not lose the needed density information.

**Finding:** the noncircular kernel statement follows by an actual differentiated inverse. It is not the invalid general inference that an injective nonlinear map must have an injective derivative.

### R49-C5 — finite coordinates and the boundary of the statistical claim

**Location:** Theorem 20.6, pages 86–87; S03, lines 425–505; S09–S10.

On an immersed p-dimensional gauge-fixed model, the preceding kernel theorem says that the covectors furnished by gaps and smooth interior expectations have zero common annihilator. Finite-dimensional duality then selects p actual covectors spanning the dual. The associated C1 map has invertible derivative and hence gives local coordinates. On a smaller convex parameter ball, a derivative bound relative to that invertible matrix gives the claimed lower Lipschitz estimate by integration along the segment. This is the appropriate quantitative local conclusion; an inverse function theorem alone would not give a uniform statement over arbitrary models.

The result is a consequence of the kernel theorem, not a new universal finite embedding theorem. There is no contradiction between these local coordinates and a finite global fiber. Nor is there a new promise of uniformly stable statistical inversion as the nonzero harmonics tend to zero or symmetry classes change. The quantified histogram theorem still imposes its original margins, and the hard-category calibration proof still accounts for internal and outer cell edges, offset amplification, conditioning on the pilot, and separately charged preparation failures.

**Finding:** the finite-coordinate conclusion is sound in its announced local sense. The broader readings just listed would be unjustified, but they are not claims I found in the revised statement.

## 4. Independent geometric control: a genuinely noncircular two-table fiber

The manuscript prints a circular example to explain the continuous stabilizer. A complementary control tests the actual finite-symmetry theorem **inside** its noncircular class. The following example is derived for this review; it is not a counterexample to a theorem in the manuscript, and no literature-priority claim is made for it.

Let K have support function

$$h(\theta)=\frac1{10}+\frac1{1000}\cos(4\theta).$$

It is entire analytic and noncircular, has proper rotational symmetry group C4, and is strictly convex because

$$h+h''=\frac1{10}-\frac{15}{1000}\cos(4\theta)\ge\frac{17}{200}>0.$$

Its center is zero and it lies in the disk of radius 101/1000. Put a=1/sqrt(2) and consider the one-obstacle periodic tables with marked lattices

$$L_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&a\end{pmatrix},\qquad
L_-=\begin{pmatrix}1&-a\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.$$

Both have positive determinant a. Select the two marked channels with gains e1 and e2. The first ordered obstacle pair is identical in the two tables. For the second, rotation by pi/2 maps the ordered pair (K,K+L_+e2) to (K,K+L_-e2), since it preserves K and takes (a,a) to (-a,a). This is a proper motion, so it preserves the signed intrinsic channel convention. Both same-type conditional laws, as well as each gap, agree at any common sufficiently small allowed offset.

The gaps are explicitly 399/500 in the first channel and 401/500 in the second. At the axis and diagonal contact normals h prime is zero, so the supporting endpoints lie on the unit center segments; strict convexity verifies the normal closest pairs.

These are legitimate periodic tables and clear channels, not merely diagrams of two local pairs. The smallest eigenvalue of either Gram matrix is 1-a, so every nonzero lattice vector has length at least sqrt(1-a)>2(101/1000). Thus all lifted obstacles are disjoint. Relative to either selected unit center segment, a lattice point off its supporting line has perpendicular distance at least a: the relevant coefficient transverse to that line is a nonzero integer. A nonincident lattice point on the line is at least distance one from the segment. Every third obstacle is therefore at distance at least a-101/1000>3/5 from the center segment. The actual closest segment is a subset, and small gates have uniform clearance. No finite-horizon claim is needed.

Nevertheless,

$$L_+^TL_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\a&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
L_-^TL_-=\begin{pmatrix}1&-a\\-a&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

The marked Gram forms differ, so a common proper Euclidean motion cannot identify the two tables. A change of integer lattice basis is not an allowed removal of the supplied deck marks.

In the root-channel gauge the four possible relative C4 alignments rotate the second unit displacement by q pi/2, q=0,1,2,3. Their determinants are a,a,-a,-a. Exactly the first two pass the orientation condition, and the preceding separation and clearance argument validates both. The recovered images and the cochain leave no additional continuous placement freedom. Hence this exact datum has **two** realizations in the announced marked class, while the product bound in Theorem 19.3 is four.

This control makes three points concrete: noncircularity does not imply global uniqueness; a finite exact fiber can coexist with an injective local derivative modulo common motion; and the geometric filtering stage is essential rather than decorative. It supports the theorem's distinction. Adding such an example would improve its exposition, but its absence is not a proof defect and its addition would not mechanically settle the placement question.

## 5. Contribution, primary literature and journal-level assessment

The strongest chain in this manuscript remains

**relative long-bridge boundary law → amplitude-free signed action inverse → actual smooth contact jets → analytic obstacle images → intrinsic periodic reconstruction.**

The first three steps cannot accurately be dismissed as ordinary finite-dimensional algebra. In particular, the relative amplitude estimates address an exponentially small reference twist, and the actual smooth remainder argument is what licenses geometric interpretation of the coefficient inverse. The ensuing analytic uniqueness, harmonic registration and finite incidence reconstruction are consequences of this machinery. Revision 49 improves the last stage without supplying a different observation or a new long-orbit mechanism. [S04–S08]

The primary literature comparison remains observation-specific. Finamore–Leguil's current arXiv record concerns finite-horizon Sinai billiards and an enriched marked length spectrum [L1]. De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil concern analytic open billiards under their non-eclipse, symmetry and genericity assumptions [L2]. Neither record is a reason to assert that the signed conditional-law inverse here has already been proved, and I have not established a reduction between those data and this manuscript's data. Conversely, recovering the table from this deliberately selected, function-valued record should not be represented as automatically removing assumptions from a marked-length theorem.

The fifth version of Florio–Leguil explicitly records removal of an earlier geometric spectral-rigidity assertion affected by an error, while retaining dynamical conjugacy conclusions [L3]. The current manuscript does not invoke that removed statement as a premise. Its correction is not evidence against the present density inverse. The targeted comparison conducted here is not an exhaustive priority investigation.

My unfavorable placement judgment rests on the demonstrated scale of the conceptual advance, not on a false claim that no substantial mathematics is present. The exact observation is designed around contact-centered, marked conditional laws carrying full functional information. The paper develops a powerful inverse for that observation and many consequences under different observation and regularity regimes. The newly added finite matching and local-coordinate consequences strengthen that result but do not, in my assessment, make a decisive additional case for exceptional general-journal importance.

Nor does a 259-page corpus establish exceptional scope merely by accumulating related conclusions. The reorganization has substantially improved navigation; that deserves explicit credit. The remaining question is whether the conceptual return of the central inverse justifies this unusually extensive combined treatment of exact rigidity, several local statistical experiments, physical pilots, finite-resolution inversion, and auxiliary comparisons. I am not persuaded by the current submission. There is no universal page limit being imposed here, and no demand to delete valid proofs. Preservation of technical work and selection of a compelling article-level argument are different matters.

## 6. Bounded advice for a further submission

There is no newly established fatal theorem error in the fresh coverage of this report requiring an algebraic patch. These are boundaries to preserve, not errors I am asserting have occurred.

Finally, do not portray a sequence of author-requested AI-assisted reviews as actual journal evaluation. The present report, like its predecessor, supplies auditable technical criticism and an explicitly nonbinding placement assessment. It does not substitute for independent journal refereeing.
