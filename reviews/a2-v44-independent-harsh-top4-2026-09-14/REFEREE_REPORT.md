## 2. The mathematical statement being judged

The strongest deterministic claim is not reconstruction from an unmarked trajectory or from an ordinary marked length spectrum. Its data consist of a separately observed gap/onset, two same-type signed limiting endpoint laws at a known positive offset for each selected channel, and the marked obstacle/deck incidence. Inter-channel Euclidean placements and the lattice Gram form are not supplied. The global conclusion additionally uses connected real-analytic boundaries, realizability, rank-two anchoring and signature-rigid propagation. Those are genuine hypotheses, not quantities manufactured by the final lattice identity. [S03, S07–S09]

The principal chain is

\[
\text{physical alternating bridges}
\longrightarrow \text{relative nonlinear boundary laws}
\longrightarrow \text{two action germs}
\longrightarrow \text{two complete contact jets}
\longrightarrow \text{analytic images and marked periodic placement}.
\]

The first arrow must survive normalization by an exponentially small twist. The third must be proved for actual smooth functions, not only formal polynomial coefficients. The fourth has analytic and incidence requirements that do not follow from the first three. The paper recognizes these distinctions. My criticism below is not that its introduction fails to state them.

The richer physical acquisition theorem uses planar position records and a charged pilot before discarding coordinates in its post-pilot criterion. That is a different experiment from being handed only the intrinsic limiting transverse laws. The same richer experiment also permits direct graph sampling. One must not use the physical theorem to imply that the difficult law inverse is necessary for every form of physical reconstruction. The manuscript explicitly supplies the comparison. [S03, S12–S14]

## 3. Fresh audit of the new Section 17

### 3.1 Convexity, separation and symmetry

The construction uses

\[
h_1(\theta)=1+\epsilon\{\cos 2\theta+\tfrac14\cos(3\theta+\pi/4)\},\qquad
h_2(\theta)=\tfrac34+\delta\{\cos 2\theta+\tfrac14\cos(3\theta+\pi/6)\},
\]

with both amplitudes in \([1/200,1/100]\), a lattice \(L=T\operatorname{diag}(8,10)\), and second-type center \(T(4,5)^t+w\), where \(\|T-I\|\le1/100\), \(|w|\le1/100\). These are construction parameters, not inputs secretly supplied to the inverse. [S09, lines 11–43]

For a support of the displayed form, the curvature radius is

\[
h+h''=r-3z\cos2\theta-2z\cos(3\theta+\phi).
\]

The two radius intervals are respectively \([19/20,21/20]\) and \([7/10,4/5]\); their reciprocal curvature ranges are disjoint. The radius bound \(R=81/80\) and the reference-center separation \(\sqrt{41}\) give

\[
\operatorname{dist}(D_a+Lm,D_b+Ln)
\ge .99\sqrt{41}-407/200>43/10.
\]

This is an all-pairs estimate, not a finite spatial search. The free-area calculation is consistent with the support-area integral. [S09, lines 74–104; pp. 73–74]

The symmetry exclusion is also substantive. The translation-covariant first-harmonic center forces any symmetry of an individual centered body to fix the origin. Simultaneously nonzero modes 2 and 3 exclude every nontrivial proper rotation. A reflection at angle \(\alpha\) would require both \(2\alpha\in\pi\mathbb Z\) and \(3\alpha+\phi\in\pi\mathbb Z\); the two specified phases prohibit it. Unequal perimeters exclude exchange of obstacle types. Consequently the full periodic table has no additional Euclidean symmetry modulo its period translations. I found no hidden reflection assumption in this argument. [S09, lines 106–122]

### 3.2 Clearance is proved for the actual contact segments

For the reference segment from \((0,0)\) to \((4,5)\), every third center is at least 4 from its bounding rectangle. Applying \(T\), perturbing second-type centers, allowing arbitrary endpoints in the two radius-\(R\) bodies, and finally subtracting the third body's radius gives

\[
4(.99)-.02-2(81/80)=383/200.
\]

The other three channels use the reflected rectangles. This proves clearance from every third translate. It is stronger evidence than the author's numerical table, and it must not be replaced by that table. [S09, lines 124–140]

The contacts are not assumed to lie on the center directions. They are recovered by maximizing

\[
F_{ij}(\theta)=v_{ij}\cdot n(\theta)-h_1(\theta)-h_2(\theta+\pi).
\]

The comparison outside the \(\pi/3\) arc and the bound \(F_{ij}''\le-|v_{ij}|/2+1/8<0\) give a unique maximizer. Its stationarity equation yields \(q_{ij}-p_{ij}=g_{ij}n(\theta_{ij})\); the supporting half-planes then prove that these points realize the closest distance. This checks the geometric event actually used by the bridge theorem. The fixed-gate estimates leave a positive margin. No finite-horizon theorem is asserted or needed here. [S09, lines 142–198; pp. 74–75]

### 3.3 Anchoring and image-based registration

The four marks are \(e_{ij}=(1,2,-i\mathsf e_1-j\mathsf e_2)\). The two cycles \(e_{00}e_{10}^{-1}\) and \(e_{00}e_{01}^{-1}\) have the required independent deck displacements and common starting channel. The edge \(e_{00}\) reaches both obstacle orbits. The absence of proper obstacle symmetries supplies signature uniqueness at the incidences. These are simultaneous facts about one table, not independently plausible assumptions assembled after the fact. [S09, Theorem 17.1]

The Fourier registration in Proposition 17.2 starts only after the established contact inverse and analytic continuation have recovered complete boundary images in their separate frames. For the same unknown first-type image in two frames, the relative third and second Fourier coefficients cancel its unknown intrinsic coefficients. Their ratio gives the relative rotation without choosing a branch of an argument. The support center supplies translation. The mode bounds \(|z_2|\ge1/400\), \(|z_3|\ge1/1600\) prevent division by zero. Registered second-type centers then recover the period vectors by differences, and the fourth channel supplies a redundant holonomy identity. [S09, lines 200–272; pp. 75–76]

This is a valid registration mechanism on the recovered-image space. It is not a newly proved Lipschitz inverse from empirical laws, finite jets or total variation to entire analytic boundaries. The proposition makes the correct distinction. The explicit Gram matrix in the displayed example is consistent with the stated generating lattice; the independent diagnostic below also recovers Gram matrices without passing their generating matrices to its registration routine.

### 3.4 Persistence and the full observation-to-table composition

Corollary 17.3 composes eight signed laws and four onsets with the existing action and contact inverses, analytic continuation, registration and deck recovery. Its statement does not replace the observed laws by supplied support functions. Nor does its numerical illustration establish that full composition by simulation: the illustration starts at the recovered-image stage. The manuscript's own diagnostic appropriately disclaims such certification. [S09, lines 274–342]

Proposition 17.4 allows independent higher-harmonic real-analytic perturbations controlled in \(C^2\). Such a bound controls the support and curvature-radius errors. Nonzero modes 2 and 3 persist, and the reflection-phase obstruction remains separated from the necessary congruence class. Thus the example is not confined to fitting its original two trigonometric amplitudes. The strict geometric margins also persist. [S09, lines 343–404; p. 77]

However, an infinite-dimensional analytic \(C^2\) neighborhood is not automatically a compact analytic class with a uniform complex-extension bound. The proposition explicitly invokes the statistical theorem only on compact analytic subfamilies satisfying that theorem's regularity conventions. I credit this qualification; I do not accuse the author of a compactness claim the text does not make.

## 4. Fresh examination of the main local proof chain

### 4.1 Relative, rather than absolute, bridge control

The local Euclidean flight action has positive gap, curvature and twist. The finite stationary bridge is constructed with a uniformly invertible weighted Jacobi operator. The cofactor identity expresses its endpoint twist as a product of local twists divided by the interior Hessian determinant. Since the reference endpoint twist decays exponentially in the number of flights, an absolute approximation alone would be insufficient. [S04]

The argument instead bounds the sum of absolute perturbation entries, hence the trace norm, independently of the number of sites. Small operator norm permits a logarithmic determinant series with one trace-class factor in each term. This is the right mechanism for a relative estimate; no growing matrix dimension is substituted for the trace bound.

The two-half-line comparison uses a summable gluing residual, uniform inverse-Hessian bounds in the sum norm, and separated endpoint blocks. Middle tails, reflected Green terms and off-diagonal block interactions are controlled before comparing logarithmic determinants. For the latter comparison the telescoping estimate

\[
|\operatorname{tr}(A^m-B^m)|\le m q^{m-1}\|A-B\|_1,
\qquad \|A\|,\|B\|\le q<1,
\]

provides dimension-independent summability. Fixed differentiated orders add only polynomial factors absorbed by a slower exponential. The subsequent fixed-domain Morse integration, rather than formal differentiation of a moving indicator, supplies the offset derivatives at zero. These inspected steps support the claimed relative mechanism. They do not independently certify every complete-event or historical response theorem in the auxiliary compendium. [S04–S05]

### 4.2 Smooth remainders are the decisive inverse step

The determinant-one matrix is not, by itself, a proof that finite action jets depend only on finite graph jets. Differentiating stationarity can expose apparently higher graph derivatives. The paper now resolves the relevant issue before introducing the finite-dimensional inverse. [S06, pp. 48–52]

For a finite action truncation, the interior orbit variations cancel. The remaining terminal term is a product of exponentially decaying factors and vanishes as the truncation recedes, including at every fixed differentiated order under the stated smooth bounds. For graph pairs with equal jets through order \(M\), interpolate their actual smooth graphs on a common local interval. At fixed endpoints,

\[
\partial_t\ell_{r,t}(y,z)=\frac{h_{r,t}(y,z)}{\ell_{r,t}(y,z)}
\{\Delta\psi_r(y)+\Delta\psi_{1-r}(z)\}.
\]

The ratio is bounded and the bracket is a genuine functional Taylor remainder. Along the weighted orbit it is dominated by a summable multiple of \(|u|^{M+1}\rho^{(M+1)i}\). Integrating the exact finite envelope identity before passing to the infinite limit gives

\[
|S_b^{[1]}(u)-S_b^{[0]}(u)|
\le \frac{C_M}{1-\rho^{M+1}}|u|^{M+1}.
\]

That estimate treats flat variations as well as polynomial ones. Its constants require smooth functional bounds on a common interval; bounded finite coefficients alone are not substituted for them. Analyticity is not used to hide this step.

Only after this filtration is established is the first occurrence of a new order-\(n\) graph jet isolated on the linear half-line orbit. The starting contact appears once, interior contacts twice, and the two geometric series give the displayed \(\coth(n\gamma)\) and \(\mathfrak r_b^n\operatorname{csch}(n\gamma)\) coefficients. Their determinant is one because \(\mathfrak r_0\mathfrak r_1=1\). This gives an invertible finite block-triangular map. It does not give uniform conditioning at unrestricted infinite order. The source preserves that limitation. I found no reason to reopen the old finite-remainder objection. [S06, Lemmas 12.2–12.5 and Propositions 12.6–12.7]

### 4.3 The density inverse and the common frame

For the interior density

\[
f(u,v)=Z^{-1}B(u)B(v)\{d-S(u)-S(v)\},
\]

the four-density ratio satisfies \(1-R(u,v)=t(u)t(v)\), where \(t=S/(d-S)\). The inverse takes a square root at one fixed nonzero anchor, not pointwise through the double zero of the action. Strict convexity supplies a positive scalar anchor margin. The algebra applies to non-even actions and unknown nonconstant amplitudes. Local stability is in an interior \(C^M\) density topology, not total variation. The finite-flight argument normalizes on the whole box and differentiates only on a positive interior square. These are appropriate safeguards. [S07, Theorem 13.1 and Corollary 13.3]

The global proof then fixes the anchoring-cycle frame and reroots the supplied tree at an incident signature-rigid obstacle. Lattice holonomies and later deck corrections consequently use the same Euclidean realization. The equation \(L=VM^{-1}\) completes that construction; it does not supply its missing hypotheses. Neither a new registration datum nor rerooting at an arbitrary symmetric leaf is justified or requested. [S07–S08]

## 5. Statistical and physical claims: fresh checks and boundaries

### 5.1 A genuine two-sided Poisson comparison

I examined the complete proof of Section 30 afresh, rather than treating a previous favorable report as its verification. Its assumptions include a support approximation at order \(k^{-1}\), convergence of the smooth density factor, and a separate uniform \(O(k^{-1})\) relative-density bound on the common bulk. The last hypothesis is essential: bare convergence of a boundary trace would not control the product experiment. [S10, pp. 117–120]

The reference layer \(|k(w-r)|\le R\) is independent of the local parameter. Its probability is \(O(k^{-1})\). After rescaling, the intensity error is \(O(\varepsilon_{n,K}+k^{-1})\). At the intersection with the fixed face \(r=0\), the endpoint strip has measure \(O(k^{-1})\), and the residual interval has length \(O(k^{-1})\); its one-record mass is therefore \(O(k^{-2})\), not an uncontrolled boundary contribution.

On the common bulk, normalization preserves a relative density ratio \(1+O(k^{-1})\), giving squared Hellinger distance \(O(k^{-2})\). The product cost is thus \(O(k^{-1/2})\) in total variation. The forward kernel keeps and rescales layer points. The reverse kernel maps Poisson points back, fills the remaining records from the reference bulk law, and randomly interleaves them. It includes conventions for invalid corner points and for more than \(k\) Poisson points. No unknown local parameter enters either kernel. These steps support

\[
\Delta(\mathsf E_{n,K}^R,\mathsf P_K^R)
\le C_K\{\varepsilon_{n,K}+k^{-1/2}\}.
\]

This is stronger than point-process convergence. The favorable conclusion here concerns this abstract comparison under its displayed assumptions and the inspected stated dependency on the anchored model. It is not a fresh derivation of every preceding LAN, alternative-moment, quadratic-loss, count-product or adaptive-transfer result. Those remaining dependencies are explicitly outside the fresh proof certificate claimed by this report; no such comprehensive certificate is issued.

### 5.2 Calibration and capped estimation

The inspected physical pilot uses the first success in a finite onset scan. A witness grid point has success probability bounded below by a constant times \(h^2e^{-J\gamma_+}\). The union bound therefore gives simultaneous localization after charging all attempted preparations. The absence of success is never treated as proof of zero probability. [S12]

The final long flight number \(J\) is chosen before the pilot, which is run at that same \(J\). Thus the subsequent offset error really controls \(J|\widehat g-g|\), rather than multiplying an earlier fixed-flight gap error by an uncontrolled later flight number. The positive gap margin controls the estimated normal and tangent. Separate channel frames remain unregistered.

The finite-template estimator uses compact separation by gaps and bounded law tests. Concentration is applied to the uncapped iid success sequence, and the cap-failure event is added separately. Conditioning on successful completion of a cap is not used to assert iid sampling. Its budget-indexed version runs the selected stage afresh, so its claimed diverging minimum flight number is consistent with the acquired transcript. These details survive fresh inspection. [S12–S13, pp. 141–148]

The result remains an existence and consistency theorem with potentially non-effective inverse moduli, libraries and large rare-event caps. It is not a sharp analytic minimax theorem or an efficient channel-discovery algorithm. The source says so. The same-record direct-position benchmark remains relevant to significance even though the estimator under discussion subsequently discards normal coordinates. [S03, S13–S14]

## 7. Literature and the remaining editorial reservation

The version-sensitive comparison with Florio–Leguil is correct: their v5 notice removes an earlier geometric spectral-rigidity assertion affected by an error, while the revised work retains dynamical smooth-conjugacy conclusions. The manuscript uses the revised distinction. This is not an outstanding correction. [L1, S03]

De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil treat analytic open dispersing billiards under symmetry and genericity assumptions, with marked length data. Finamore–Leguil use enriched marked length data for finite-horizon Sinai billiards. Neither is simply the present collection of onsets, signed channel laws and marked incidences. The present absence of reflection symmetry therefore cannot, by itself, be called removal of a hypothesis from those theorems: the observation map has changed. The manuscript already avoids that overstatement. Conversely, these comparisons do not establish that its new law inverse is already contained in those papers. [L2–L3, S03]

Meister–Reiß provide a prior Poisson boundary-experiment equivalence in nonregular regression. The general Gaussian/Poisson contrast is therefore not the new principle to be credited here. The potentially new work lies in the specific billiard densities, nonlinear inverse and justified comparison of the declared records. The manuscript acknowledges this background. The literature examination in this report is limited to the named primary sources; it is not an exhaustive priority search. [L4, S03]

### R44-E1 — significance, not a newly invented proof defect

My adverse recommendation rests on the following assessment of the actual mathematical hierarchy.

**First, the local law-to-jet mechanism is the strongest contribution.** The relative determinant and smooth-remainder steps deserve attention. I do not dismiss them as the two-line rank-one identity or the identity \(\det M_n=1\). But the manuscript has not persuaded me that their present formulation and consequences constitute an exceptional general-mathematical advance, rather than a substantial inverse result for this carefully specified family of observations.

**Second, the global theorem should receive credit for exactly what it adds.** It passes from recovered analytic germs to complete boundary images, resolves marked matching under signature rigidity, and reconstructs the lattice from two independent cycles. It is not an unmarked reconstruction theorem, and the proof does not supply a reduction from classical spectral data. Section 17 now proves nonvacuity and robust realizability, including higher analytic harmonics. That is a genuine improvement, but it does not itself establish generic availability of the required channel architecture throughout a much broader class. Such a genericity theorem is not a missing premise of the stated result; it is simply not among its demonstrated consequences.

**Third, the physical theorem cannot carry the full exceptional-significance burden.** Its charged pilot and cap analysis are careful. Its inverse modulus and finite template construction are non-effective, however, and its richer observation model admits the paper's own direct graph-sampling route. The difficult intrinsic inverse remains meaningful precisely because its data are coarser; the stronger position sensor does not magnify the novelty of every resulting consistency statement.

**Fourth, volume and preservation are not mathematical significance.** The 228-page article contains several substantial technical strands and historical auxiliary material. Their inclusion is not an error, nor is length alone a rejection ground. Nevertheless, adding every correct consequence does not make the central advance more decisive. The existing introduction already explains the hierarchy fairly well. Another novelty paragraph, another version number or a further packaging revision would not resolve my reservation.

These considerations support my recommendation, but they are not a claim that a top journal has a mechanical criterion violated by a special observation model, compactness, or non-effective existence. Such results can be important. The assessment is that the present manuscript has not made a sufficiently compelling case in this instance.
