## 3. Mathematical examination of the new material

### R71-M1. Exact locality is proved for the actual record, with the correct area factor

**Locations:** Proposition 7.1, principal p. 39; S8, lines 1–109; Theorem 2.5, principal p. 13; S2, lines 424–494.

The recorded outcome is either a successful endpoint pair in the fixed gate or one failure symbol. It does not retain the trajectory of a rejected preparation. The preparations are independent resets from each table's normalized Liouville measure. These specifications are mathematically operative, not optional experimental prose.

For two local completions, the single-edge length functions agree on common contact collars. The weighted uniqueness construction identifies the same stationary bridge for every permitted returning length. Clearance and free-segment margins make that stationary bridge a physical trajectory in both tables. Therefore its stationary length, mixed twist, gauge terms, and residual time agree as functions, not merely as Taylor series.

The physical formula gives

$$
\frac{d\mu_{N,b,d}}{du\,dv}
=\frac{D_{N,b}}{2\pi\mathcal A}\,\beta_{N,b}(u,v)
\{d-E_{N,b}(u,v)+p_bu-p_bv\}_+.
$$

Only the total free area can change. Consequently

$$
\widetilde\mu_{N,b,d}
=\frac{\mathcal A}{\widetilde{\mathcal A}}\mu_{N,b,d}.
$$

The direction of this ratio is correct. Conditioning cancels it; equality of the *charged* records additionally uses equal area so that the success probability and failure mass coincide. No ignored rejected-ray information is smuggled into the comparison. The preceding/following free-segment margin is what prevents truncation of the time-split interval; equality of endpoint length functions alone would not have sufficed without this event specification. [S2, S8]

The adaptive remark is also justified within its stated scope. Conditional on a common recorded history, a common selection rule has the same next-setting distribution and encounters the same record kernel. Induction proves equality for a fixed finite number of preparations. It does not prove anything about a single unreset orbit, a different failure record, or table-dependent readout kernels. These limitations are expressly retained. This is an exact finite-experiment result, not a conclusion inferred from convergence of conditional densities.

### R71-M2. The contact-germ equivalence is not a normalization sleight of hand

**Location:** Corollary 7.3, principal pp. 40–41; S8, lines 110–202.

In the forward direction, equal two-offset law germs give equal anchored actions, then equal positive quadratic data and higher jets, and finally equal actual smooth graphs through the contracted-visit envelope. In the converse direction, common contact germs permit common compact collars and sufficiently small endpoint gates, with the necessary flight margins. Proposition 7.1 then applies.

The manuscript correctly distinguishes two operations: normalizing a new smaller-gate experiment, and restricting a density previously normalized on a larger gate. The former is used for equality of experiment germs; the latter is used in the finite-estimation theorem. Treating them as interchangeable would change unknown normalization constants and could invalidate a comparison. That error is not present here. Equality is asserted on sufficiently small common gates, not on a fixed larger domain where the two graphs might differ. [S6, S8]

The result identifies the equivalence class of visited contact germs. It does not reconstruct the unvisited smooth boundary or a global lattice from this already marked experiment. Nor does it prove that two offsets or all phases are minimal. Those stronger claims are not needed for the stated result.

### R71-M3. The equal-area family consists of actual closed obstacles

The realization uses the clear three-obstacle configuration of Proposition 2.6, not a collection of freely assigned jets. Two disjoint smooth normal bumps are supported on boundary arcs away from the contact collars and closed trajectory tubes. Small perturbations preserve strict convexity, disjointness, lattice separation, and the local clearance margins.

If the deformation is parametrized by $(s,t)$, its area function satisfies

$$
\partial_t V(0,0)=\int\psi_2\,d\sigma>0.
$$

The ordinary implicit function theorem supplies $t=t(s)$ with exactly constant obstacle area. The first bump's support is disjoint from the compensator, so its displacement $s\psi_1$ cannot be canceled. Thus distinct sufficiently small parameters give genuinely different closed boundaries while preserving every specified local charged record. The common small neighborhood is available uniformly in the returning length because all visits and short free segments were localized before the remote perturbation. [S2, S8]

The compact unions obtained by removing lattice copies meet the hypotheses of the cited corrected planar theorem: finite disjoint strictly convex compact domains with $C^3$ boundaries. That theorem says that either of its two almost-everywhere data equalities implies equality of the compact obstacles. Its contrapositive therefore distinguishes the two exterior observations for distinct unions. I checked Theorem 1.1 and the data definitions in the primary PDF. The correction specific to dimension two matters here. [L1]

This is a valid **one-direction failure of implication**: equality of the selected periodic local records does not supply equality of complete compact exterior observations. The manuscript does not assert two-way incomparability, an equality of full marked length spectra, or a quantitative information ordering between the two differently configured experiments. The comparison is useful and answers the preceding report. It is not, by itself, a new global inverse theorem.

## 4. Re-examination of the retained central mechanism

### R71-M4. The relative theorem controls the quantity needed for conditioning

**Locations:** Section 2, especially Theorem 2.3; S2, lines 1–423.

The positive Jacobi operator gives exponentially decaying Green kernels. The finite/half-line comparison includes a reflected-end contribution, and weighted stationary equations construct the actual nonlinear bridges. Gluing the two half-lines leaves an exponentially small residual; fixed-order differentiation can produce polynomial factors in the flight count, which a strict exponential margin absorbs.

The decisive normalization precedes the nonlinear determinant estimate:

$$
D_{N,b}=\frac{\sinh\chi}{(\mathcal M_b)_{12}\sinh(n\chi)},
\qquad
\log\beta_N=
\sum_i\log\frac{-\ell_{i,12}}{k_i}
-\log\det(I+G_N\Delta H_N).
$$

The Hessian perturbation is localized at the two ends with uniformly controlled trace norm. Cutting off the middle, comparing Green operators, and using the convergent trace expansion separates the two endpoint contributions. This is the substantive step that turns an exponentially small physical numerator into a controlled relative limit. It is not an absolute remainder divided by an exponentially small denominator. [S2]

The physical measure then restores the linear momenta in the actual residual time. Gauging a stationary action does not change the physical clock. The event specifies the collisions, collars, and no-additional-collision rule; it is not an arbitrary maximal-count event. All these ingredients remain in the source. The differentiability statements are for each separately fixed finite order, not for an unweighted infinite-jet norm. My finite exact checks agree with the cofactor/monodromy normalization, but those checks do not certify the infinite-dimensional estimates. [S1, S2, D2]

### R71-M5. The law-to-action and action-to-jet passages use the stated observations

**Locations:** Sections 3–4; S3, lines 1–350; S4.

The measured graph coordinate is a supplied-frame tangent projection. It is not an unknown arclength parametrization or a measurement of normal graph height. The arclength conversion retains endpoint Jacobians and gauge terms.

For $H=A(u)+C(v)$, the anchored ratio of the two limiting densities gives

$$
q=\frac{f_{d_1}(u,v)f_{d_2}(0,0)}{f_{d_2}(u,v)f_{d_1}(0,0)},
\qquad
H=\frac{d_1d_2(1-q)}{d_2-d_1q}.
$$

The normalizers and common flux amplitudes cancel. The denominator equals $d_2(d_2-d_1)/(d_2-H)$ and is bounded away from zero under the stated interior margins. Anchoring separates $A$ and $C$ along the axes, and the supplied momenta restore the gauged actions. This is a legitimate inverse on the realizable law image, not a claim that every arbitrary fitted density has this factorization. [S3]

The positive quadratic inverse uses the Schur equation

$$
s_i=k_i+c_i-\frac{k_i^2}{k_i+c_{i+1}+s_{i+1}}
$$

and a projected contraction on the nonnegative orthant. Its fixed point lies in the positive-curvature image precisely when all components are positive. Thus the global uniqueness statement does not assume a preselected nearby inverse branch or identify every positive action vector as realizable. The excluded example $k=(1,1)$, $s=(1/10,10)$ correctly has projected fixed point $(0,109/11)$. [S4]

For higher degrees the response is $(I+T_j)(I-T_j)^{-1}$, with $(T_jz)_i=\sigma_i^jz_{i+1}$ and the signed contractions retained. Finite-jet factorization follows from the actual stationary envelope, including control of the terminal variation before taking the half-line limit. The finite curvature iteration also retains its propagated error multiplier $L_M E_m$ in the higher-jet reconstruction and specifies the off-fixed-point algebraic extension. It does not treat a nonstationary approximate tuple as an exact physical solution. [S3, S4]

### R71-M6. The last step identifies functions, not just their Taylor series

**Locations:** Section 5, especially Theorems 5.3 and 5.5; S5.

The shifted tail of an actual half-line is identified with the next phase's half-line by uniqueness. On a common collar along the segment between two candidate graph tuples, one-step derivatives are bounded by $a<1$. This gives the essential constant-one estimate

$$|x^t_{b,j}(u)|\le a^j|u|.$$

An estimate $C\rho^j|u|$ with an uncontrolled prefactor would not justify the same large-weight contraction. The source proves the stronger statement it actually uses.

Integrating the actual variation formula yields

$$
\mathcal S(G)-\mathcal S(F)=\overline\alpha(G-F)+K(G-F),
\qquad \overline\alpha\ge\tfrac12.
$$

On differences vanishing below degree $m$,

$$
\|\overline\alpha^{-1}K\|_{m,R}
\le\frac{6a^m}{1-a^m}<1
$$

for a sufficiently large finite $m$. Equality of the actions then forces equality of the actual graph functions. Equality of finite jets is used to put the difference in this weighted space; it is not substituted for equality of smooth functions. The operator depends on both candidates, which is appropriate for separation but not a directly observable numerical inverse. [S5]

For stability, the low-degree polynomial alignment is performed *before* applying the weighted norm. The aligned action difference has the vanishing required for Taylor's remainder estimate. Uniform differentiated envelope bounds control the polynomial correction, yielding complete-profile $C^0$ stability from a finite $C^m$ discrepancy between realizable laws on a bounded positive class. The derivative loss and deterioration near weak hyperbolicity are visible. [S5]

The actual flat family is also correctly delimited. It keeps contact and action Taylor jets fixed while changing full actions and laws. A remote area correction leaves the local construction unchanged. The text does not assert equality of normalized density jets or full marked length spectra. Its finite test concerns fixed nonzero separation; it is not cheap uniform access to arbitrarily small flat effects. Those distinctions must remain in every significance claim. [S1, S5, S6]

I additionally examined the analytic Banach-inverse construction in S3, lines 353–541. It combines a finite lower-triangular quotient with an independently bounded high-vanishing tail inverse, after a protected holomorphic half-line construction. It does not infer a bounded infinite triangular inverse merely from bounded invertible diagonal blocks. This additional check is not certification of the later real-to-disc estimates or every global analytic consequence.

### R71-M7. The finite experiment reaches the differentiated topology and charges rarity

**Locations:** Theorem 1.2 and Section 6; S6, lines 1–358.

The forward bounds precede statistical inversion: a positive residual integral gives uniform conditional derivative bounds, a $C^m$ finite-flight bias $O(e^{-\omega N})$, and a success floor $c_0e^{-\Gamma N}$. The free-area upper bound is in the correct direction for this floor. Densities are normalized on $Q_+$ and restricted to the interior $Q$ without renormalization.

In endpoint dimension two, the derivative-kernel summand has envelope $Ch^{-m-2}$ and variance $CHh^{-2m-2}$. The resulting terms

$$
h^s+\sqrt{\frac{L_n}{nh^{2m+2}}}
+\frac{L_n}{nh^{m+2}}+n^{-2}+\delta h^{-m-3}
$$

have the appropriate scaling. Interior gating handles the support issue; the bounded readout perturbation is controlled pathwise. Independence is needed for the latent accepted samples, not for an adversarial choice of bounded perturbations after acceptance. [S6]

The fitted density is not inverted as if it were automatically physical. A countable dense subset of the actual law image gives a measurable near-minimum-distance selection taking values in actual tables. The triangle inequality then invokes geometric stability only between realizable laws. This is an existence construction; no efficient enumeration is proved or claimed.

With $B$ preparations per phase/offset group, the binomial tail bound supplies enough successes. The returning-length choice balances the finite-flight bias against the effective accepted-sample size. In the notation of the source,

$$
\alpha=\frac{s}{2(m+s)+2},\qquad
\beta=\frac{\alpha\omega}{\omega+\alpha\Gamma},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{s}{m+s+3},
$$

and the sufficient error bound is $C\{(L_B/B)^\beta+\delta^\gamma\}$. The floor in the returning length changes constants, and the explicit lower-count, confidence, bandwidth, and small-error conditions remain necessary. All $JB$ preparations, including failures, are charged. The result does not concern noisy itinerary labels, uncertain gate decisions, unknown offsets, an unreset correlated trajectory, or minimax optimality. [S1, S6]

It would therefore be incorrect to repeat an objection that the manuscript merely assumes access to an exact differentiated limiting density. Section 6 supplies a finite-data route in the stipulated model. My criticism below is not that obsolete objection.

## 5. Originality and significance at the requested level

### R71-E1. The comparison is now adequate; it does not settle exceptional significance

The corrected planar lens theorem no longer constitutes a missing comparison. The reader can now see what is supplied, what is observed, and which part of the geometry is determined in each problem. The v71 construction proves why one data-equality premise cannot simply replace the other. It would be unfair to call this response cosmetic or to infer that the local theorem is a corollary of the exterior theorem without a data-recovery argument. None has been established in this review. [S7, S8, L1]

The marked-length comparisons likewise concern different problems. Bálint–De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil extract geometric/dynamical information from marked lengths in open dispersing billiards. De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil obtain analytic determination under their stated symmetry and genericity hypotheses. Finamore–Leguil concern an **enriched** marked length spectrum in finite-horizon Sinai geometry; that qualifier must not be dropped. These primary records do not establish that A2's complete local law theorem is already known. My check of those three papers' records is targeted, not an exhaustive audit of their proofs or of all priority claims. [L2–L4]

The strongest positive account is the one the revised introduction now gives: an actual nonlinear relative physical limit, followed by an actual functional inverse that detects flat contact information, and a finite charged experiment reaching the required topology. This is a coherent mathematical chain. The new locality theorem identifies its target precisely.

My reservation is that the demonstrated gain remains concentrated in a heavily marked, deliberately selected local observation. The internal polygon and frames are supplied, every relevant phase is resolved, and the experiment is restricted to a calibrated itinerary. The finite theorem removes the exact-law oracle but retains those geometric and experimental priors. This is a meaningful inverse problem; it is not yet, in my judgment, an exceptional general advance commensurate with the very highest requested placement. That assessment is about the theorem actually proved, not a demand to replace it by an unmarked whole-table problem. [S1, S6]

### R71-E2. What the new comparison contributes—and what it does not

The hard analytic work remains in the retained relative-law and smooth-envelope modules. Once the exact physical measure and bridge uniqueness are available, locality is a direct consequence; the remote realization uses a standard two-parameter area adjustment; the exterior distinction then uses the cited rigidity theorem. Their combination is useful, correct within the examined scope, and responsive. It does not introduce another comparably deep analytic mechanism. The response itself appropriately avoids advertising it as one. [S2, S8, S9]

Similarly, the finite-preparation theorem is a genuine improvement of the observation model, but its kernel approximation, measurable minimum-distance selection, and binomial balancing are classical components applied to the proved geometric stability estimate. The paper states this accurately. Neither a large number of theorem labels nor the length of the retained catalogue adds independent evidence for exceptional originality. [S1, S6]

These points explain why I remain unconvinced despite closing the concrete v70 objection. They do **not** show that the principal theorem is routine, false, or devoid of originality. Nor do they establish a no-go statement about the research program. An editor or specialist may reasonably weigh the relative-law/functional-inverse synthesis more favorably. The negative recommendation here should be read as my assessment, not a consensus or an objective defect count.

### R71-E3. No moving target for the next response

I do not prescribe an efficient estimator, a minimax lower bound, recovery without supplied marks, a same-full-spectrum example, or a whole-smooth-obstacle theorem as a mandatory repair. Such results would be separate research contributions. It would be dishonest to describe their absence as a contradiction in the present theorem.

The concrete comparison request has been answered. A further response on placement should argue the significance of the demonstrated mechanism, with precise prior-art comparisons or genuinely relevant mathematical consequences, rather than promise more version increments or declare all objections closed. Another cosmetic revision solely to overturn the same subjective judgment is not warranted by this report. No reduction of the mathematical scope or deletion of valid technical material is requested.
