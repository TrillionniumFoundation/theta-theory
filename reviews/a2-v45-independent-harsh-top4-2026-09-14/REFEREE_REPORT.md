## Recommendation and principal finding

**I do not recommend acceptance at the requested highest-level general-journal standard. My recommendation is editorial rejection of this submission at that level, not a claim that the new finite-channel theorem has been refuted and not a technical-major-revision verdict resting on an invented proof gap.**

This recommendation requires an important change from the preceding assessment. Revision 45 supplies a genuine new theorem, not another worked example or another delivery certificate. The geometric skeleton exists for every table in the stated separated periodic convex setting. Proper obstacle asymmetry then makes the intrinsic law inverse globally determining on an open dense class. The earlier reservation that the required architecture had only been demonstrated in a specially arranged realization is therefore no longer valid. It must be withdrawn rather than repeated under another issue number. [S02–S04]

Within the fresh proof coverage recorded below, I have not found a fatal counterexample to Theorem 18.7, an unjustified substitution of a unimodular cycle basis, or a missing inter-channel registration oracle. The selected-channel extension of the local bridge argument is supported by an actual locality proof. The smooth finite-remainder step remains substantially stronger than a merely formal jet calculation. These are favorable mathematical findings.

My adverse recommendation is instead a judgment about the central contribution and its demonstrated consequences at the exceptionally selective level requested. The new theorem materially broadens the geometric domain of the inverse. It does not change its observation map, turn finitely many laws into finitely many scalar measurements, or make the image-stage stability into a quantitative law-to-boundary inverse. The manuscript already distinguishes these statements. My concern is not that the qualifications are absent; it is that the additional global and statistical conclusions, even correctly qualified, do not in my assessment make the underlying contribution sufficiently decisive for this placement.

That assessment is fallible. I do not claim an exhaustive priority search, that observation-specific inverse theorems cannot merit such publication, or that the programme should be abandoned. A different mathematical editor could give greater weight to the relative nonlinear law and the new generic determination theorem. The recommendation below should not be represented as a mathematical impossibility result or an actual journal decision.

## 2. Precisely what the new theorem determines

For a labelled real-analytic periodic table with N obstacle orbits and trivial proper symmetry of each obstacle, the theorem selects N+1 marked channels. Its data are their separately observed onsets, their two same-type signed limiting endpoint laws at a known sufficiently small positive offset, and their obstacle/deck incidence labels. The Euclidean placements of different channel frames, contact curvatures, support coefficients and lattice Gram form are not inputs. The conclusion determines the complete labelled table and the Euclidean realization of the marked lattice modulo one simultaneous proper Euclidean motion. Competing realizations need not separately be assumed asymmetric. [S03–S04, Theorem 18.7]

The relevant quantifiers are: for each table in the open dense class, there exists a finite marked design and an offset, with the design persisting in a neighborhood of that table. This is not one universal unmarked acquisition scheme for all tables. Corollary 18.9 applies the physical theorem on compact analytic subfamilies within one such persistent-design neighborhood, with its original uniform analytic assumptions. Those distinctions are explicitly stated and are not missing hypotheses.

The conclusion is also stronger than a finite-dimensional shape fit: arbitrary higher analytic harmonics are permitted. Conversely, each exact endpoint law is an infinite-dimensional datum. Proposition 18.5 proves minimality of N+1 within the connected two-cycle graph mechanism, not an information-theoretic lower bound for arbitrary sensors. Both sides of this distinction matter when evaluating the result.

## 3. Fresh audit of Section 18

### 3.1 Finite sublevels, tangencies and obstruction descent

Lemma 18.1 uses bounded representatives and the least singular value of the lattice realization to obtain

\[
 d(K_a,K_b+L\ell)\ge s_{\min}(L)|\ell|-2R.
\]

Only finitely many translate labels can lie below a fixed distance threshold. Disjoint compact lifted bodies therefore have a uniform positive separation. The same local finiteness argument makes the clearance of a genuinely clear compact closest segment positive against the union of all third bodies. A finite numerical picture is not being used to exclude remote translates. [S04, lines 25–55; p. 79]

The descent in Lemma 18.2 treats tangency as obstruction. If a third body C meets the closest segment from A to B at z, then

\[
 d(A,C)+d(C,B)\le d(A,B),\qquad
 \max\{d(A,C),d(C,B)\}\le d(A,B)-\sigma.
\]

The second inequality follows because each endpoint-to-z distance is at least the uniform separation. Induction on the integer part of d/σ terminates. Concatenation and erasure of repeated-vertex subwalks yield a finite path of clear pairs with the claimed total edge-gap bound. This is a valid incidence-graph argument. It would be false reasoning to interpret that sum as the travel time of a specular itinerary through intermediate bodies; the source expressly does not do so. [S04, lines 58–93]

### 3.2 The finite graph really carries two independent deck gains

The seed pairs reach every obstacle type and two primitive translates of a root obstacle. Applying descent to these pairs produces a finite connected quotient graph whose closed gains generate the marked deck group. With a rooted spanning tree and tree-path labels m_a, the fundamental gain of an oriented edge is

\[
 \eta_e=m_a+\ell_e-m_b.
\]

The tree gains vanish, and the non-tree gains generate every closed gain. Two of the latter are independent over the reals. Keeping the tree and these two edges gives exactly N+1 channels, including the one-obstacle case with two loops. [S04, Theorem 18.3; p. 80]

A generating collection of integer vectors need not contain a unimodular pair. The manuscript correctly avoids that inference: it retains the actual matrix M of the two selected gains and uses L=VM^{-1}. Real independence is sufficient for recovering the Euclidean linear realization; generation of the whole integer lattice by the selected pair is not necessary. This is a substantive safeguard, not cosmetic notation.

### 3.3 Clear graph edges are legitimate local observations

At a closest pair the two-endpoint distance Hessian is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\kappa_a+g^{-1}&-g^{-1}\\-g^{-1}&\kappa_b+g^{-1}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det=\kappa_a\kappa_b+(\kappa_a+\kappa_b)/g>0.
\]

The nondegeneracy, all-translate clearance and finite selected incidence set give persistent contact patches. Distinct clear incidences on one obstacle cannot have the same closest point: the nearer target along the common normal ray would obstruct the farther segment. Fixed reference gates can consequently contain the continued contacts with positive margins. These gates are not translated during acquisition using an unknown lattice. [S04, lines 150–194; pp. 80–81]

Lemma 18.4 addresses the essential interface with the old local theorem. An already specified alternating event between the selected lifted pair has every complete flight at least its own gap g_e. An excess d over jg_e bounds each nonnegative flight excess, as well as the unused entrance and exit times. Uniqueness of the minimum and its positive Hessian localize every impact for sufficiently small d, independently of j. The channel need not minimize the gap of the entire table. The proof therefore does not confuse a selected event with the complete count event. Its differentiated uniformity retains the required higher functional bounds; a C² neighborhood is not made to supply arbitrarily high uniform derivatives. [S04, lines 196–233; S05]

### 3.4 The genericity statement is broader than the two-harmonic example

For the support centered at its translation-covariant first-harmonic center, proper rotational asymmetry is equivalent to the greatest common divisor of the nonzero harmonic indices being one. A finite subset of those indices already has gcd one; its nonzero coefficients persist. This proves openness, including asymmetric supports whose second or third coefficients vanish. [S04, Proposition 18.6; p. 82]

The more explicit sublocus uses nonzero second and third coefficients and

\[
 \operatorname{Im}\bigl(z_3^2\overline{z_2}^{\,3}\bigr)\ne0.
\]

Independent sine and cosine perturbations at orders two and three avoid the relevant nonzero real-polynomial zero sets. Such perturbations can be arbitrarily small in every fixed C^r topology while preserving positive curvature, separation and analyticity. The phase polynomial also excludes an individual reflection symmetry. No finite harmonic truncation of the unknown obstacle is imposed. Theorem 18.7 needs only proper asymmetry, not the extra reflection exclusion of this convenient explicit sublocus.

### 3.5 The local inverse and global placement are composed in one frame

The four-density inverse recovers both unsymmetrized action germs. The smooth finite-remainder theorem then recovers both complete graph jets. Analytic continuation determines the connected full boundary images in each measured channel frame. For a properly asymmetric analytic obstacle, equal complete oriented curvature signatures imply a proper Euclidean symmetry, hence a unique matching framed point. This supplies the incidence congruences used in the tree and cycles. [S07–S10]

The competing table has the same recovered full images. It therefore inherits their proper asymmetry; the proof is not secretly restricting its competitors after obtaining the data. The root incidences of both cycles can be matched to the same channel frame. Their translation vectors then define the same V in two realizations, and the same marked gains define the same M. Thus L=VM^{-1} and the subsequent deck corrections use one Euclidean realization. No independently chosen frame is inserted into either column of V. [S04, lines 330–396; S08, lines 178–259]

The harmonic formula of Proposition 18.8 independently checks this placement mechanism on the explicit sublocus. Under the manuscript's Fourier convention,

\[
 U_{s\to t}=\frac{z_3(C_s)z_2(C_t)}{z_3(C_t)z_2(C_s)}.
\]

For centered edge displacements d_e in the registered frame, tree sums p_a satisfy p_a=c_a+Lm_a. Consequently the two cycle defects are Lη_i, and c_a=p_a-Lm_a recovers the representative centers. The formula does not receive the generating lattice or an already registered table as input. Its stability is correctly stated on the recovered-support-image space. [S04, lines 400–490; pp. 83–84]

**Finding R45-C1:** the new geometric availability and generic intrinsic-determination results survive this audit. This is a bounded favorable finding about the inspected proof, not a formal certification of every theorem in the 235-page main.

## 4. Fresh scrutiny of the inherited analytic core

### 4.1 Relative normalization is doing real work

The finite bridge endpoint twist is a product of local twists divided by the interior Jacobi determinant. Its reference value is exponentially small in the number of flights. An absolute perturbation estimate would not justify the normalized limiting law. The finite weighted-orbit construction instead bounds the sum of absolute Hessian perturbation entries, hence their trace norm, independently of the length. A convergent logarithmic determinant series then contains one trace-class factor in every term; its cost is not the matrix dimension. [S05]

The two-half-line argument controls the gluing residual in a summable norm, separates the endpoint blocks, bounds middle tails and reflected Green terms, and compares logarithmic determinants before exponentiation. For contractions A and B, the estimate

\[
 |\operatorname{tr}(A^m-B^m)|\le m q^{m-1}\|A-B\|_1
\]

supplies summability. Fixed differentiated orders add polynomial factors that are absorbed by a slower exponential. The offset analysis uses fixed Morse coordinates and disk integration rather than an unjustified formal differentiation of moving support indicators. These are credible mechanisms for the stated relative estimates. They should not be dismissed as an elementary density-ratio identity, but neither are they a fresh proof of every auxiliary historical statement. [S06]

### 4.2 Finite jets are justified by functional remainders, not notation

Theorem 12.1 depends critically on proving that the action jet of order M factors through the graph jet of order M. Differentiated stationarity alone can appear to introduce higher graph derivatives. The actual smooth argument addresses this issue. It interpolates graph pairs with equal finite jets on a common interval, applies the finite-action envelope identity, and lets the exponentially decaying terminal term tend to zero. Along the weighted orbit, the graph difference is bounded by a summable multiple of |u|^{M+1}ρ^{(M+1)i}. Thus

\[
 |S_b^{[1]}(u)-S_b^{[0]}(u)|
 \le C_M|u|^{M+1}/(1-\rho^{M+1}).
\]

This estimate covers flat perturbations and uses actual functional bounds, not only bounded coefficients of a formal polynomial. Analyticity is not used to conceal the smooth step. [S07, lines 298–404; pp. 50–52]

After that filtration is proved, the first occurrence of a new order-n graph coefficient is evaluated on the linear half-line orbit. The initial contact occurs once and the interior contacts twice. The resulting geometric series give the coth and csch coefficients, whose 2-by-2 determinant is one. This establishes the finite block-triangular inverse. It does not give unrestricted infinite-order conditioning, and the manuscript does not claim it. [S07, lines 406–598]

### 4.3 Single-offset cancellation and analytic globalization

For the same-type endpoint density, write

\[
 f(u,v)=Z^{-1}B(u)B(v)(d-S(u)-S(v)).
\]

On an interior square the four-density ratio satisfies 1-R(u,v)=t(u)t(v), where t=S/(d-S). The inverse uses the positive square root at a fixed nonzero anchor. It does not differentiate a pointwise square root through the double zero at the contact. The unknown amplitude and normalizer cancel, and neither evenness nor a constant amplitude is needed. Finite-flight stability differentiates only on an interior square while normalizing on the full box. Its topology is an interior C^M density topology, not total variation. [S08, Theorem 13.1 and Corollary 13.3; pp. 54–56]

Equality of the recovered analytic germs then gives equality of the complete connected obstacle images. That is an exact identity argument, not a stable continuation theorem for noisy finite jets. The new generic theorem legitimately invokes the exact argument; Proposition 18.8 does not repair or claim to quantify the preceding continuation step. [S09]

**Finding R45-C2:** the inspected local-to-global chain retains the substantive repairs credited previously. I found no basis for reissuing the old smooth-remainder, scalar-anchor or independent-frame objections.

## 5. Physical reconstruction: what was checked and what it contributes

I freshly read the common-record calibration and the finite-template reconstruction proofs. A pilot witness grid point has success probability bounded below by a constant times h² exp(-Jγ_+). The first success cannot precede onset. Charging all attempted preparations and taking a union bound therefore yields the simultaneous contact and gap estimates. The final even flight number J is chosen before the pilot, which is run at that same J; the proof controls J times the gap error rather than multiplying an earlier error by an uncontrolled later flight number. [S11, Section 40]

For post-pilot acquisition, concentration is applied to the uncapped iid sequence of successful marks, and cap failure is then added separately. The proof does not assert iid sampling conditional on completing a cap. The finite separators include the onset coordinates. Compactness supplies a finite template library and a positive separating margin, not an effective method for finding them. The budget-indexed theorem runs its selected stage afresh, so its claimed diverging minimum flight number is consistent with its entire acquired transcript. [S12, Section 41]

The physical result is meaningful as a measurable, charged consistency construction. It is not an efficient design-discovery algorithm or a sharp analytic minimax theorem. More importantly for its role in this paper, its sensor observes planar endpoint positions before the estimator discards longitudinal coordinates. The manuscript's direct graph-interpolation benchmark uses that same richer record and also permits long even flights. Thus the difficult intrinsic law inverse is not necessary for reconstruction with every record in the physical experiment. The new Corollary 18.9 retains rather than erases this distinction. [S13; S04, pp. 84–85]

The present report does not freshly recertify the entire inherited LAN, Poisson deficiency, adaptive-transfer, deconvolution or count-experiment collection. Reading the preceding referee's conclusions about those results is not a substitute for another complete proof audit. None of those unrefreshed claims is used here as an independently certified reason for acceptance or as an invented reason for rejection.

## 6. One concrete, minor clarification

### R45-P1 — say “zero-net-gain backtrack,” not “zero-gain edge”

In the proof of Theorem 18.7, source lines 368–375 and native p. 83, the common-frame explanation says to prepend a zero-gain edge followed by its reverse. The needed object is a backtrack ee^{-1}, whose **total** gain is zero, regardless of the gain of e. There need not be a selected edge with individually zero marked gain. For N=1, every admissible selected self-channel joins distinct translates and therefore has a nonzero deck mark.

Replace that phrase by “prepend the zero-net-gain backtrack ee^{-1}.” This avoids conflating an original edge label with a tree-adjusted gain or assuming an extra measured edge. The preceding unique-congruence construction already gives the common frame. This is a wording clarification, not a counterexample to the theorem and not the basis of my adverse recommendation. [S04]

## 7. Significance and comparison with existing work

### R45-E1 — an adverse editorial assessment, with the new contribution credited

The contribution should be evaluated at three distinct levels.

**The analytic inverse is the strongest part.** Relative nonlinear normalization and a smooth finite-remainder law-to-jet inverse are substantial. A fair assessment must not reduce them to the final rank-one cancellation or determinant-one formula. Nevertheless, after examining their mechanisms, I remain unconvinced that the manuscript demonstrates an exceptional advance of sufficiently broad conceptual consequence for the requested placement. This is not a claim that the proof is routine or that a known cited theorem already subsumes it.

**The generic theorem is a genuine extension, but its additional mechanism is comparatively economical.** Obstruction descent removes the architecture hypothesis at the geometric level; asymmetry removes the incidence ambiguity on an open dense class. Once the local inverse has supplied complete analytic boundary images, the remaining tree, cycle and Fourier arguments are finite registration algebra. Their utility is clear, but their difficulty and conceptual content should not be conflated with a new reconstruction principle from coarser data. N+1 is an informative graph count, not a finite-information recovery theorem. This changes the domain of the result significantly without changing the basic informational strength of its input.

**The statistical extension does not independently settle the significance question.** It gives honest charged existence and consistency, but the library and inverse moduli may be non-effective. The richer position sensor also supports the direct acquisition benchmark. The intrinsic theorem has value precisely because its limiting law datum is coarser than that sensor record; the physical implementation does not make all of its statistical consequences independent breakthroughs. These are limitations of what is demonstrated, not corrections to claims the author has already carefully qualified.

The relevant spectral comparisons must remain observation-specific. De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil concern analytic open billiards with symmetry/genericity assumptions and marked length data. Finamore–Leguil concern finite-horizon Sinai billiards and enriched marked length data. The latter observation is not simply the present set of selected endpoint laws. No reduction between those observations is established here. Accordingly the new lack of a reflection assumption cannot be advertised as removal of a hypothesis from those different theorems, but neither do those papers establish that the present local inverse is known. The manuscript observes this distinction. [L1–L2; S03]

The version-sensitive Florio–Leguil citation is also not an outstanding defect: the v5 notice withdraws the earlier geometric spectral assertion affected by an error while retaining dynamical conjugacy results. Meister–Reiß already provide a Poisson boundary-experiment equivalence in a nonregular regression setting; the general Gaussian/Poisson contrast is not the new principle to credit here. The paper-specific density analysis and justified observation comparison must bear that credit. The literature check here is limited to these named primary sources. [L3–L4]

Length itself is not a rejection criterion, and preservation of previous proofs is not an error. But the 235-page main also does not make the contribution more exceptional merely by accumulating correct downstream statements and historical auxiliary material. The introduction already differentiates several levels of observation and proof. Adding another summary or changing the version number will not resolve this assessment. A publication decision must be made on the actual theorems, not on perpetual nominal revision.

## Final disposition

Revision 45 makes a real mathematical advance over revision 44: finite clear skeletons are no longer confined to the explicit example, and full intrinsic periodic determination holds on the stated open dense class. That finding should survive any later editorial decision. The local proof mechanisms inspected here are substantially more credible than a recurring generic assertion that “gaps remain” would suggest.

I nevertheless do not support acceptance at the requested highest-level general mathematics journal on the present contribution and evidence. The adverse recommendation is editorial; the concrete wording clarification is minor, and no fatal mathematical defect has been established in the audited core. This report does not demand unmarked discovery, finite-scalar determination, a quantitative analytic minimax theorem, and a spectral-data reduction as a cumulative new checklist. Those would be different research contributions, not retroactive premises of the theorem submitted.

Correct the identified wording, preserve the valid qualifications and full proofs, and make the submission judgment on the actual strength of the local inverse and its now genuinely generic consequence. No arbitrary deletion of mathematics, weakening of valid claims, additional registration oracle, or abandonment of the programme is requested.
