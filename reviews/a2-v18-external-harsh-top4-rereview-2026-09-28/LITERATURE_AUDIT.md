# Targeted literature audit for A2 v18

## 1. Purpose and limit

This note records the closest theorem-level comparisons used in the external rereview of A2 v18. It is not an exhaustive priority search and does not claim that an earlier paper contains the complete v18 theorem.

The central v18 observation is not an ordinary marked length spectrum. It is a selected near-onset subprobability law marked by two intrinsic endpoint arclengths. At two strictly active times, its densities explicitly recover a stationary two-flight action. That change of information category makes two literatures relevant:

1. rigidity from marked periodic-orbit lengths in dispersing billiards;
2. rigidity from boundary travel times, lens/scattering relations, and obstacle travelling-time data.

The manuscript discusses the first category but does not adequately discuss the second.

## 2. Marked-length billiard rigidity already cited by the manuscript

### De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil

J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin, and M. Leguil, *Marked length spectral determination of analytic chaotic billiards with axial symmetries*, Invent. Math. 233 (2023), 829–901; arXiv:1905.00890v4.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00890v4

The paper proves determination of analytic open dispersing billiards from the marked length spectrum under stated symmetry and genericity assumptions. Its observation consists of periodic-orbit lengths labelled by the symbolic dynamics. This is much poorer in local endpoint information than the v18 endpoint-density law.

A2 v18 is therefore not subsumed by this theorem. Conversely, the difference in data richness must be explicit when global rigidity claims are compared.

### Finamore–Leguil

D. Finamore and M. Leguil, *A CAT(0)-approach to the marked length spectral rigidity of Sinai billiards*, arXiv:2510.18983v1 (2025).

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18983v1

This work introduces an enriched marked length spectrum for finite-horizon Sinai billiards and proves isometric rigidity from that enriched periodic-orbit data. The geometry, horizon hypothesis, and information set differ from A2 v18.

### Bálint–De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil and Osterman

The manuscript also cites the period-two/homoclinic marked-length analysis of Bálint–De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil and Osterman's relation between marked lengths, homoclinic conjugacy, and determination of a third scatterer when two scatterers are known. These remain appropriate comparisons for the older coefficient and global-dynamics parts of the A2 programme.

They are not the closest comparisons for the new two-density quotient.

## 3. Boundary-distance and lens rigidity

### Stefanov–Uhlmann–Vasy

P. Stefanov, G. Uhlmann, and A. Vasy, *Local and global boundary rigidity and the geodesic X-ray transform in the normal gauge*, arXiv:1702.03638.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03638

This paper treats boundary distance and lens data near strictly convex boundary points and globally under convex-foliation hypotheses. The data are endpoint travel times and, equivalently in the local strictly convex setting, the associated lens relation. Tangential derivatives of the boundary distance encode endpoint directions.

The geometry is Riemannian rather than a two-reflection external billiard, and its theorem does not directly recover two unknown obstacle arcs from the A2 density. It is nevertheless conceptually close because v18 first reconstructs a generating/travel-time action as a function of two boundary variables and then differentiates it to recover geometry.

A top-four presentation should explain:

- why the v18 action is not merely standard local lens data in disguised coordinates;
- which part of the two-reflection stationary reduction is genuinely new;
- how the explicit recovery of both reflecting boundaries compares with local boundary/lens rigidity;
- whether the Liouville-density origin supplies information beyond the recovered action once two times are used.

## 4. Obstacle travelling-time rigidity

### Gurfinkel–Noakes–Stoyanov

T. Gurfinkel, L. Noakes, and L. Stoyanov, *Travelling Times in Scattering by Obstacles in Curved Space*, J. Differential Equations (2020); arXiv:2003.12261v1.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12261v1

The paper studies travelling times of billiard trajectories in the exterior of an obstacle on a two-dimensional Riemannian manifold. It proves a time-preserving conjugacy result from almost-equal travelling times and, under non-positive curvature and strict convexity, equality of obstacles.

Its data and global setting differ from A2 v18. It does not appear to contain the particular two-window elimination, the stationary two-reflection Schur complement, or the periodic network assembly of A2.

It is nevertheless a closer conceptual comparator than a marked length spectrum because its primary information is obstacle-scattering travel time. A2 should distinguish selected local three-impact endpoint densities from the travelling-time data used there and state what is gained or lost.

## 5. Analytic continuation

L. N. Trefethen, *Quantifying the ill-conditioning of analytic continuation*, BIT Numer. Math. 60 (2020), 901–915.

The manuscript's citation is appropriate for the general phenomenon that continuation from a real interval under a uniform complex bound has a conditional power-type loss governed by continuation geometry. The v18 proof uses an elementary two-constants/harmonic-measure argument rather than claiming a new complex-analysis theorem.

The exponent `theta` is therefore a class- and geometry-dependent continuation exponent. It should not be presented as a universal statistical exponent of the billiard problem.

## 6. Assessment of novelty after these comparisons

The specific combination below may well be new:

- derive the full intrinsic endpoint density for a selected three-impact billiard event;
- use two absolute times to eliminate flux and area normalization and recover the stationary two-flight action;
- extract both obstacle curvatures from a diagonal stationary Schur complement;
- reconstruct both smooth arcs in relative placement;
- acquire the densities by finite histograms;
- propagate local arcs to complete analytic obstacles;
- synchronize independently reversed channel pairs by quantitative asymmetry;
- recover the marked lattice from two cycle labels.

This targeted audit found no earlier result establishing that complete package.

The top-four novelty question is narrower. Once the densities are retained, the action recovery is algebraically immediate, and inverse geometry from travel-time/lens data is a mature subject. The paper must therefore identify a conceptual theorem beyond the package construction, rather than relying on the fact that no cited marked-length paper uses precisely the same sensor.

## 7. Required literature revision

A revised manuscript should add a subsection comparing:

1. marked periodic-length data;
2. full travelling-time or lens/scattering data;
3. the v18 selected near-onset endpoint-density law;
4. the two densities after their quotient has recovered the action;
5. finite histograms approximating those densities.

For each category it should list:

- what is marked;
- whether endpoint positions and directions are observed;
- whether absolute travel time or only periodic length is observed;
- whether the obstacle network and lattice-copy labels are supplied;
- what regularity and convexity are assumed;
- what geometry is reconstructed;
- what stability is proved.

Without this comparison, the manuscript's current literature section understates the closeness of v18 to established travel-time and lens rigidity questions.
