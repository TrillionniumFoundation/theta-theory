# Targeted primary-source literature audit for A2 v16

**Review date:** 28 September 2026  
**Scope:** theorem-level comparison relevant to the top-four significance assessment. This is not an exhaustive priority search or a complete bibliography of dispersing-billiard inverse problems.

## 1. Observation categories must not be conflated

A2 v16 uses a near-onset **endpoint-arclength subprobability law** for selected three-impact itineraries. It retains failures, marked contact origins, two endpoint arclengths and odd information modulo one coherent reversal. Its global theorem additionally uses signed contact registration and integer copy labels on a finite normal-channel network.

The papers below primarily use periodic-orbit lengths, enriched marked spectra, local conjugacy data, formal normal forms or deformation spectra. They do not directly contain the A2 observation, and A2 does not directly prove their theorems. The relevant comparison is therefore one of naturality, global reach and mechanism, not literal theorem inclusion.

## 2. Bálint–De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil

P. Bálint, J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin and M. Leguil, *Marked length spectrum, homoclinic orbits and the geometry of open dispersing billiards*, Communications in Mathematical Physics 374 (2020), 1535–1575, DOI `10.1007/s00220-019-03448-x`.

The paper extracts geometric and hyperbolic information, including period-two curvature/Lyapunov data, from a global marked length spectrum and homoclinic asymptotics for open dispersing billiards.

**Comparison with A2 v16.** A2's curvature calibration instead comes from quadratic moments of a local intrinsic endpoint law. This is a different mechanism. The marked length spectrum is more conventional and global; the A2 datum is more localized but records substantially richer endpoint information.

## 3. De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil

J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin and M. Leguil, *Marked length spectral determination of analytic chaotic billiards with axial symmetries*, Inventiones Mathematicae 233 (2023), 829–901, DOI `10.1007/s00222-023-01191-8`, arXiv `1905.00890v4`.

Their theorem gives global analytic table determination from marked periodic lengths in a symmetric/generic open-dispersing setting. Their local hyperbolic normal-form material also provides the closest classical comparator for the forward relative-product mechanism in earlier A2 versions.

**Comparison with A2 v16.** A2 removes symmetry for its marked local contact inverse and, after adding explicit registration, reconstructs a periodic table. It does not use only a marked length spectrum. The v16 registered network carries contact correspondences, arclength offsets and lattice copy labels that are not part of the ordinary marked length data.

## 4. Osterman

O. V. Osterman, *On Length Spectrum Rigidity of Dispersing Billiard Systems*, arXiv `2208.12244v1` (25 August 2022; version checked 28 September 2026).

For three analytic scatterers under the non-eclipse condition, the paper relates equality of marked length spectra to analytic conjugacy near a homoclinic orbit and proves that two known scatterers together with the marked spectrum determine the third.

**Comparison with A2 v16.** Osterman's data are global periodic lengths and two complete known obstacles in the reconstruction statement. A2 locally reconstructs participating obstacle images from endpoint-arclength germs, then propagates them through a supplied registered network. Neither theorem subsumes the other.

## 5. Finamore–Leguil

D. Finamore and M. Leguil, *A CAT(0)-approach to the marked length spectral rigidity of Sinai billiards*, arXiv `2510.18983v1` (21 October 2025; version checked 28 September 2026).

The paper introduces an enriched marked length spectrum for finite-horizon Sinai billiards and proves that equality of that spectrum implies isometry. The proof uses approximating geodesic flows and CAT(0) limit geometry.

**Comparison with A2 v16.** This is a genuine whole-table rigidity theorem from a global enriched dynamical spectrum. A2 has no finite-horizon hypothesis and uses a different local residual-flux mechanism, but its whole-table conclusion requires a purpose-built registered channel network and integer cycle labels. The contrast is central to the editorial naturality assessment.

## 6. Koval

I. Koval, *Billiard tables with analytic Birkhoff normal form are generically Gevrey divergent*, arXiv `2403.14448v1` (21 March 2024; version checked 28 September 2026).

The paper studies formal reconstruction from billiard normal-form data, treats odd table derivatives and the normal form as parameters to make the formal solution unique, and proves generic Gevrey divergence in the enlarged problem.

**Comparison with A2 v16.** A2's highest-jet recursion comes from physical residual-flux moments rather than formal Birkhoff normal-form equations. Koval is nevertheless an important warning against turning an all-order formal recurrence into an analytic convergence claim. Version 16 respects that warning in its count-fiber section: it proves formal fibers and smooth realizations but leaves exact analytic count-germ convergence unresolved.

## 7. De Simoi–Kaloshin–Wei

J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin and Q. Wei, *Dynamical spectral rigidity among Z2-symmetric strictly convex domains close to a circle*, Annals of Mathematics 186 (2017), 277–314, DOI `10.4007/annals.2017.186.1.7`.

This work proves deformation rigidity from dynamical spectral data in a symmetric near-circle convex-billiard class.

**Comparison with A2 v16.** The geometry, observation and dynamical regime differ from periodic dispersing billiards. It remains a relevant benchmark for the breadth and naturality expected of a top-four rigidity theorem.

## 8. What appears new in A2 v16

The targeted search did not locate an earlier theorem with the exact combination of:

- a selected near-onset three-impact endpoint-arclength subprobability law;
- intrinsic recovery of gap, two curvatures and area from onset and low moments;
- explicit all-parity contact-jet blocks without curvature separation;
- passage from local analytic obstacle recovery to a periodic table through signed contact registration and rank-two lattice-cycle labels;
- positive-dimensional finite count fibers and smooth tables with identical onset Taylor series.

This combination is plausibly new. The report does not claim complete historical priority.

## 9. Significance consequence of the comparison

The literature does not show that A2 v16 is already known. It does sharpen the question of what level of information is doing the work.

A2's intrinsic arclength law is richer than a length spectrum or a count law, and its registered network explicitly supplies the cross-channel identifications needed to assemble the table. The local filtration and jet blocks are technically nontrivial, while the final lattice recovery is finite linear algebra once registered obstacle images are known.

Accordingly, the top-four question is not whether the theorem is merely a corollary of the cited papers. It is whether this customized local marked law and supplied registration reveal a sufficiently broad new rigidity principle. The accompanying referee report answers that editorial question negatively while recognizing the theorem as a substantial and plausibly original specialist contribution.
