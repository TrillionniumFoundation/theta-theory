# Targeted literature audit for A2 v15

**Review object:** Qian Qi, *Nonlinear boundary laws and asymmetric two-contact rigidity in dispersing billiards*, mathematical manuscript commit `00f27ebd0071d75504995b61f9a15c67f896188f`.

**Date checked:** 28 September 2026.

This is a targeted theorem-and-mechanism comparison, not an exhaustive priority search. The search concentrated on inverse problems for dispersing/Sinai billiards, recovery of boundary jets, analytic normal forms, and enriched dynamical observations. No claim is made that the list below exhausts all local moment inverse problems.

## 1. De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil

J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin and M. Leguil, *Marked Length Spectral determination of analytic chaotic billiards with axial symmetries*, arXiv:1905.00890v4; *Inventiones Mathematicae* 233 (2023), 829–901.

- Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00890v4
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-023-01191-8

The paper studies analytic open dispersing billiards and proves global determination from the marked length spectrum under stated symmetry and genericity assumptions. Its symmetry class requires matching jets at the endpoints of a selected period-two orbit and even curvature jets there. It also uses a local analytic hyperbolic normal form near that orbit.

A2 v15 correctly distinguishes three matters:

1. the local normal-form comparator;
2. the global marked-length information set and conclusion of De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil;
3. A2's local four-germ observation and framed participating-contact conclusion.

The new signed-moment block is not contained in the cited theorem merely because both arguments use Taylor reconstruction near a period-two orbit.

## 2. Bálint–De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil

P. Bálint, J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin and M. Leguil, *Marked Length Spectrum, homoclinic orbits and the geometry of open dispersing billiards*, arXiv:1809.08947; *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 374 (2020), 1535–1575.

- Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08947
- DOI linked by arXiv: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-019-03448-x

This work shows that the marked length spectrum recovers the curvature at period-two collision points and the Lyapunov exponent of each periodic orbit. It is directly relevant to A2's convention that the two separate curvatures and the hyperbolicity parameter are leading geometric data. The observation and target are nevertheless different from A2's near-onset probability and signed-moment germs.

## 3. Osterman

O. V. Osterman, *On Length Spectrum Rigidity of Dispersing Billiard Systems*, arXiv:2208.12244.

- Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12244

For three analytic scatterers satisfying non-eclipse, the paper relates equality of marked length spectra to analytic conjugacy near a homoclinic orbit and proves that two scatterers together with the marked length spectrum determine the third. This gives a useful comparison with A2's much more local conclusion: A2 determines only the two participating analytic boundary images in supplied contact frames and leaves unvisited obstacles unconstrained.

## 4. Finamore–Leguil

D. Finamore and M. Leguil, *A CAT(0)-approach to the marked length spectral rigidity of Sinai billiards*, arXiv:2510.18983v1 (21 October 2025).

- Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18983v1

The paper introduces an enriched marked length spectrum for finite-horizon Sinai billiards and proves that equality of that enriched spectrum implies isometry. This result is not a direct competitor to A2: its data are global periodic-orbit data and its finite-horizon geometry is different. It is nonetheless important context whenever A2 argues that enriching an information set yields rigidity. A2's enrichment is a local, orientation-sensitive first moment and its conclusion is correspondingly local.

## 5. Koval

I. Koval, *Billiard tables with analytic Birkhoff normal form are generically Gevrey divergent*, arXiv:2403.14448.

- Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14448

This work relaxes a symmetry condition in a formal normal-form problem, treats odd table derivatives and the normal form as parameters, and reconstructs Taylor coefficients successively. It does not prove A2's signed near-onset moment inverse. It does show that triangular all-order Taylor reconstruction with odd derivatives belongs to an active and technically developed normal-form literature. A2 should therefore compare mechanisms and information sets explicitly rather than presenting the mere existence of an all-degree recursion as sufficient novelty evidence.

## 6. Targeted novelty conclusion

I did not find, in this targeted search, an earlier theorem with exactly A2 v15's data:

- two oriented raw two-flight probability germs;
- two unconditional signed germs `E[(u+v) 1_E]`;
- supplied labelled gap, separate curvatures, contact points and transverse-axis orientation;
- all-degree recovery of both framed contact jets;
- a physical support-function realization of all odd and even directions.

The explicit odd block and its determinant separation therefore appear plausibly new. This is not an exhaustive novelty clearance.

The principal literature problem in the submitted primary article is presentation: its bibliography contains only De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil and the internal companion volume. A top-journal article making a new inverse-rigidity claim should directly discuss at least the marked-length, homoclinic, enriched-spectrum, spectral-rigidity and formal Taylor-reconstruction lines above. The complete companion's historical bibliography does not remove the need for a focused literature account in the theorem-led primary article.
