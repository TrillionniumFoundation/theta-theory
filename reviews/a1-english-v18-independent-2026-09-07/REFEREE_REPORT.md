## 1. Recommendation

**REJECT for the requested four-journal standard.** The principal reasons are the weight of the mathematical contribution and a specific missing comparison with an existing necessity-and-sufficiency theorem. This is not a rejection based on a manufactured counterexample.

**Mathematical disposition:** I found no blocking counterexample or essential missing step in the eleven new results, under their stated hypotheses, or in the principal inherited collision, circular, and operational-inverse chains examined here. This does not certify all 99 proof blocks, every appendix, the numerical compiler, or every uniform constant by formal verification.

The revision is a genuine advance over v17. In particular, it would be inaccurate to repeat the previous report's observation that the addition only inverts an already represented scale envelope. The new rank theorem starts with an input-defined class of experiments; the affine theorem derives a forward profile for an entire acquisition class; and the square-pairing theorem has a genuine necessity direction. Those changes answer the mathematical substance of the previous request. The negative recommendation below is an assessment of their contribution, not a denial that they exist.

The strongest part of the submission remains the joint identification of attainable mass, global approximation, and causal memory through exponent collisions. The new material improves the architecture and scope. Nevertheless, after examining how the new assertions are proved and how the universal-pairing criterion relates to the existing matrix literature, I am not persuaded that the cumulative advance has the conceptual force and depth of consequence needed for these journals. A different referee might place greater weight on the collision-uniform synthesis. The present recommendation is a reasoned editorial judgment, not a mathematical theorem about the paper's possible importance.

## 2. What has actually changed since v17

The general setting now permits any compact metric latent space and any probability prior. The horizon, report alphabets, and command dimensions remain finite; likelihoods are polynomial in the commands, with continuous latent coefficients and a fixed positive lower bound. A finite list of physical future-word queries spans the relevant test space. The query is selected after the memory label is formed. Commands are continuous, and the prefix is not available outside the charged state.

For each report word, evidence and future numerators form the vector

$$Y_a=(Z_a,Z_ap_n)^t.$$

The new invariant is

$$d_n(\mu)=\max_a\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb R(\boldsymbol u)}[Y_a,DY_a]-1.$$

For positive rank the checkpoint risk is comparable to $M^{-2/d_n}$, and a single causal filter has maximum-checkpoint risk comparable to $M^{-2/d_*}$, where $d_* = \max_n d_n$. The actual prior enters the rank; exceptional priors are not excluded. The zero-rank case is finite, not necessarily a singleton. Rank strata are given by polynomial equations in finitely many prior moments.

For positive binary affine acquisition, the manuscript identifies

$$C_\mu=\operatorname{Cov}_\mu(h,f),\qquad
R_M\asymp\max_{1\leq\ell\leq\min(b,q)}
\left(\frac{\|\bigwedge^\ell C_\mu\|_{\rm op}}{M}\right)^{2/\ell}.$$

Here $h$ contains the square-root query weights. The constants are independent of the nonzero covariance singular values and remain valid through rank loss. This is a one-acquisition-step classification with arbitrary subsequent physical tests, not a claimed multi-step covariance formula.

Finally, in the square regime $\dim\widetilde T=\dim\Phi=p+1$, where $\widetilde T=\operatorname{span}\{P\}+\operatorname{im}DP$, the paper characterizes full normalized rank under every full-support prior by a weak common sign and nonvanishing somewhere of the paired evaluation determinants. The square hypothesis is explicit. The paper does not assert an unproved reduction of arbitrary rectangular pairings to this case.

These are genuine statements derived before assuming the risk representation. Their scope must be credited, but their novelty and significance must still be evaluated separately.

## 4. Assessment of the new proofs

### 4.1 Augmentation, image dimension, and unconditional mass

The normalized-rank identity is correct. Row subtraction transforms $[Y,DY]$, with $Y=(Z,Zp)^t$, into a matrix with one nonzero evidence column and the block $ZDp$. Thus its rank is $1+\operatorname{rank}Dp$. There is no need to assume that $P$ is a physically available radial derivative. The finite diagnostic explicitly includes a case where it is not.

The global image argument also survives inspection. With an actual prior fixed, the prior integrals are real coefficients, and the command graph is described by polynomial equations with a nonvanishing evidence denominator. Projection and a finite union over report words give a compact semialgebraic image. The dimension is the maximum interior differential rank. A nonzero minor at a boundary point persists at nearby interior points, so the boundary does not invalidate this identification.

For the lower bound the proof does not put probability on a section of zero ambient measure. It augments an onto output projection with kernel coordinates, applies the inverse function theorem, and integrates the whole kernel-coordinate box. The density of the specified report word includes $Z_a$, bounded below by $\kappa^n$. Consequently the measure minorization is genuinely unconditional. The cube-volume argument then applies to arbitrary prediction centers and to the permitted independent randomization.

For the upper bound, the whole-image covering estimate is used, not merely the local minorized patch. Bounded semialgebraic format is what prevents unbounded oscillatory complexity from invalidating the dimension-based cover. The relevant geometric input is classical and is already attributed in the manuscript; see [R2]. The use of that input here is appropriate.

### 4.2 The causal assertion and constant-rank uniformity

Multiplication by the next report likelihood sends the next future test space into the current one. This produces a well-defined rational update on the exact prediction state. The denominator remains bounded below on segments between reachable states because such segments are represented by posterior mixtures. At fixed experiment and horizon, the expansion coefficients and update Lipschitz constants are bounded.

Updating a reachable representative and quantizing in the next reachable set gives

$$e_{n+1}\leq L_ne_n+A M^{-1/d_*}.$$

The finite iteration includes the accumulated effect of previous lossy updates. It does not reconstruct the discarded prefix, retain an uncharged command tape, or use a different retrospective encoder at every checkpoint. Small budgets and zero-dimensional stages can be absorbed into the stated finite-horizon constants. The lower bound applies at a checkpoint attaining $d_*$ under the same exploration law.

The compact constant-rank uniformity statement is also defensible. At each parameter a nonzero maximal minor and an interior command tuple can be selected locally. A finite cover of the compact parameter set supplies uniformly positive chart data. Positivity supplies uniform derivative bounds, while the covering format is coefficient independent. This is not uniformity over arbitrary sequences approaching a lower-rank stratum; the text correctly does not assert that stronger conclusion.

### 4.3 Prior moment strata

The polynomial-minor criterion is exact: a command polynomial vanishes identically precisely when all its command coefficients vanish. The $(k+2)$-minors therefore characterize $d_n\leq k$. No equality test based on approximate moments is silently substituted.

The statement about full-support moment vectors is correctly phrased as an equality of sets of attainable moment vectors. It does not assert that an interior moment vector has only full-support representations. A supporting hyperplane proves that a full-support probability has an interior moment vector. Conversely, a small mixture with a fixed full-support probability realizes every interior moment vector with full support.

For bounded-density perturbations, the covariance of $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ is positive definite when $1,g_1,\ldots,g_s$ are independent and the prior has full support. The tilted moments $m+Gt$ therefore fill a neighborhood. The generic simultaneous rank assertion follows from a finite product of nonzero minor coefficients. There is no claim that the moment body itself must be semialgebraic.

### 4.4 Affine covariance geometry

The decisive identity is

$$p=\bar h+C_\mu\theta,\qquad
\theta=\frac{v}{1+m^tv},\quad v=yu,\quad m=\mu f.$$

Both report branches contribute to the law of $v$, whose density is $(1+m^tv)/2^b$. The Jacobian determinant is $(1+m^tv)^{-(b+1)}$. Combining the Jacobian and report evidence gives the exponent $b+2$ in the transformed density, not $b+1$. The independent symbolic calculations checked this distinction in dimensions one, two, and three.

The inner-ball inclusion, outer-ball inclusion, and positive density bound depend only on the stated $a,b$. A linear image of this thick distribution is therefore controlled by the unwhitened covariance singular values. The lower volume estimate remains valid even when a comparison ball is wider than a short ellipsoid axis: it bounds the volume of an intersection from above and does not require the ball to fit inside. The grid proof handles all integer budgets, zero singular values, and reachable centers. The query weights are already in $h$, so no inverse minimum weight is needed.

The interior degeneration example is correct:

$$\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_t}(x^2,x)=\frac{4t}{45},\qquad
 d\mu_t=(1+tx)\,dx/2.$$

At $t=0$ the prescribed affine query task is constant; at nonzero $t$ its rank is one. This should not be confused with the zero-rank endpoint of the general finite-report theorem. An independently checked command-independent informative detector has two prediction values $3/8,5/8$, zero command rank, and one-label mean excess $1/64$. It becomes exact with two labels. The manuscript correctly permits this possibility.

### 4.5 Universal pairing

The reduction from normalized rank to the determinant of the square moment pairing is correct, even without $P\in\operatorname{im}DP$. Adjoining $P$ does not enlarge the normalized derivative image because that direction is annihilated by normalization.

Sufficiency follows from the paired-determinant integration formula and full support. Necessity uses the convexity of the set of full-support probabilities, continuity of the pairing determinant, and approximation of the equally weighted measure on any $p+1$ points by full-support measures. At that atomic measure the determinant is $(p+1)^{-(p+1)}$ times the paired evaluation determinant. This identifies its sign pointwise. The matched-space corollary follows from a squared evaluation determinant.

The compact-family quantitative assertion has the necessary qualifications: a common positive likelihood bound, uniform interior neighborhood, command density bound, controlled derivatives, and priors dominating a fixed full-support probability. These hypotheses make the relevant parameter-measure set compact and the Jacobian uniformly onto. Full support alone, without a compact dominated envelope, would not give these quantitative constants, but that stronger assertion is not made.

The mathematical argument is sound as inspected. The novelty comparison, however, is incomplete; this is the first substantive concern below.

## 5. E18.1 — a necessary comparison for the universal criterion

**Classification: significant attribution/novelty-positioning issue, not a counterexample.**

For finite $X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_m\}$, write $A_{ik}=v_i(x_k)$ and $B_{jk}=\phi_j(x_k)$, with $d=p+1$. Both have row rank $d$. Then

$$D(w)=\det(A\operatorname{diag}(w)B^t),\qquad w_k>0.$$

The paired sign condition is precisely common weak sign, with at least one nonzero entry, of the products of corresponding maximal minors. Banaji–Pantea [R1, Definition 2.24 and Lemma 2.36] provide the corresponding strong-compatibility criterion. To specialize their lemma, take its matrices to be $A,B^t$. Since $D_2A$ is onto,

$$\operatorname{rank}(AD_1B^tD_2A)=d
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\det(AD_1B^t)\ne0.$$

Positive diagonal weights and positive probability weights are equivalent by scalar normalization. Thus the finite-space algebraic criterion follows directly from that prior result.

The revision's bibliography does not include this comparison. It should distinguish that inherited core from its compact-space extension, posterior normalization, and history-mass conclusion. This is not a claim that [R1] contains the entire manuscript, nor an allegation of misconduct. Attribution of Andréief's identity alone does not settle the novelty of the necessity direction.

## 6. E18.2 — significance after the new structural results

**Classification: publication-decisive editorial assessment.**

The general rank theorem has broad hypotheses, and it is not vacuous. Nevertheless, its mechanism is a comparatively direct synthesis: an exact normalized Jacobian rank identifies a finite-dimensional semialgebraic image; a regular chart gives a quantization lower bound; a whole-image entropy estimate gives the upper bound; positivity and the closure of future tests give a finite Lipschitz recurrence. The moment-stratum theorem then expands the same minors into coefficient equations. The mathematical novelty must be located in the experiment-level synthesis and its consequences, not in the rank criterion, determinant equations, or dimension-to-quantization principle individually.

The breadth of the latent space should also be interpreted precisely. Arbitrary compact metric spaces genuinely enlarge the formal domain. At each fixed horizon, however, the argument factors through finitely many continuous coefficient functions and their prior integrals. This is a useful finite reduction, not the solution of an independent infinite-dimensional geometric difficulty. The theorem gives an exact symbolic characterization for specified exact moments; it does not produce structural normal forms for the resulting rational maps or describe their anisotropic degeneration near every exceptional locus. The author appropriately disclaims the latter claim. Its absence is not a proof gap, but it prevents that stronger interpretation from carrying the significance case.

The affine theorem is the cleanest addition. It supplies a complete uniform profile for a class defined by its physical likelihoods. Its proof shows why a future observation spectrum alone is insufficient. Yet the exact projective change of variables leaves a bounded-thickness distribution followed by one linear map. Once this reduction is made, the full profile follows from elementary ellipsoidal volume and rectangular covering estimates. This simplicity is not a defect by itself. The issue is that the paper does not establish a further consequence of this reduction that, in my assessment, changes the level of its central contribution. The full-support example is illuminating but remains a scalar covariance cancellation, not such a further consequence.

The universal theorem adds a useful quantifier characterization, but E18.1 materially changes the novelty accounting for its algebraic core. Its matched-space corollary is the positive Gram-pairing case, and its quantitative component uses the compactness/minorization mechanism already present in the earlier positive-history section.

These observations must not erase the inherited achievement. The multi-step collision theorem still has to establish actual acquisition of complete confluent flags, a dimension-truncated cover of the entire reachable image, and stable causal updates with no inverse gap. A generic entropy bound or a Vandermonde spectrum alone supplies none of those three assertions together. The circular theorem similarly has to account for attenuation on both acquisition and observation. I have not located a primary source that simply contains these exact combined finite-memory laws in the manuscript's model.

Even giving full credit to that synthesis, I judge v18 to be a technically careful consolidation and enlargement of the same framework rather than an advance of the magnitude required for the requested recommendation. This judgment rests on the mathematical roles just described, not on the number of pages, the number of lemmas, a dislike of elementary proofs, or a requirement that every paper solve an unrelated famous problem.

There is no correction-sized checklist that guarantees reversal of this assessment. In particular, I do not ask the author to append one more example, prove an unbounded-horizon result that was never claimed, change the resource model, or remove the existing collision theorem. Correcting E18.1 is necessary for accurate positioning, but that correction alone would not change my present recommendation.

## 7. Inherited results and limits of this review

I re-examined the mixed-moment positivity argument and the binomial product tangent in `core/03_transversality.tex`; the complete Newton–Hermite prefix attainment, global-cover, and causal-update chain in `core/06b_collision_geometry.tex`; the thin-rectangle input in `core/06a_attainable_filtration.tex`; the positive-history criterion; the circular acquisition, query metric and weighted update; and all four operational-reconstruction results.

The distinctions that survived this inspection are important. Exact state dimension is not an anisotropic profile. A local onto patch is not a global cover. Independent coding randomness is not a seed that generated and remembers the commands. Fixed-horizon constants are not horizon-uniform constants. A known-calibration representative filter is not a calibration-blind compiler. The inverse of a checkpoint envelope is not an inverse of a maximum over checkpoints or of a risk curve with a positive uncertainty floor.

The source index lists the exact coverage. I did not independently audit every retained numerical-resource theorem, every prior-uncertainty appendix, or every historical derivation in this round. I did not execute the author's complete validators or rebuild and visually inspect the submitted manuscript PDF. Those limitations should not be mistaken for specific errors in those materials.

## 9. Final disposition

The eleven new results survive the mathematical checks performed here. The response to the earlier experiment-classification concern is substantive and should be recorded as such. The universal criterion nevertheless needs the precise prior-work comparison in E18.1. After accounting for that comparison and the actual mathematical role of each new theorem, my recommendation remains **rejection at the requested four-journal level**.

The author should receive a clear distinction between three matters: proofs that survived inspection, a concrete novelty-positioning correction, and an unfavorable significance judgment. Neither successful diagnostics nor a previous negative recommendation is a substitute for any of them.
