## 3. Mathematical examination of the new experiment

### R69-M1. Forward bounds and normalization are obtained before conditioning

**Location:** S1, lines 13–90, principal p. 58; S4, relative theorem and physical law.

The experiment fixes the marked polygon, the phase labels and frames, two separated offsets, and nested squares $Q\Subset Q_+$. Successful trajectories are gated on $Q_+$; the conditional laws are normalized there and merely restricted to $Q$. This restriction must not be renormalized in the proof. The manuscript observes that distinction. Its two-offset quotient cancels arbitrary positive scalar normalizers, so a density normalized on the larger square remains a valid input on the smaller one. [S1, S3]

The class assumptions produce, at the stipulated fixed derivative orders,

$$\|f_N-f\|_{C^m(Q_+)}\le C_0e^{-\omega N},\qquad
\|f_N\|_{C^{m+s}(Q_+)}+\|f\|_{C^{m+s}(Q_+)}\le H,$$

and the uniform success lower bound $p_N\ge c_0e^{-\Gamma N}$. These are not consequences of an absolute action approximation. The exact reference mixed twist is

$$D_{N,b}=\frac{\sinh\chi}{(\mathcal M_b)_{12}\sinh(N\chi/P)}.$$

The retained relative cofactor theorem controls the normalized numerator and its derivatives; a fixed positive-residual square bounds the conditional normalizing integral below. Uniform quadratic bounds control the prefactor and $\chi/P$, while the cell-area upper bound converts the positive residual integral into a probability lower bound. This is the appropriate direction of the area bound. [S1, S4]

I re-examined why the retained forward theorem is relative: reference Green kernels have uniform exponentially weighted bounds, the finite and half-line constructions retain their endpoint errors, and the log cofactor/determinant comparison is formed after removing the exact reference twist. Trace-series continuity then separates the two ends. Dividing an absolute error by the exponentially small twist would be invalid; it is not the argument used here. The selected full-phase volume is the mixed twist times the available residual time, with the physical linear momenta restored. The small positive window prevents an extra reflection and makes the itinerary representation unambiguous. [S4]

The stipulated higher graph bounds are finite-order geometric priors, not a density-error oracle. Conversely, the theorem is not uniform over all smooth tables with no such bounds. This restriction is essential and explicitly stated.

### R69-M2. The derivative-estimation lemma has the correct dimension and boundary treatment

**Location:** Lemma 12.1, principal pp. 59–60; S1, lines 92–187.

For a derivative of total order at most $m$ in dimension two, the kernel summand has envelope $Ch^{-m-2}$ and variance at most $CHh^{-2m-2}$. Thus the square-root and Bernstein linear terms are respectively

$$\sqrt{\frac{L_n}{nh^{2m+2}}},\qquad \frac{L_n}{nh^{m+2}}.$$

The derivative order has not been omitted from the variance. Moment cancellation through order $s-1$ gives bias $Ch^s$ from $C^{m+s}$ regularity. The required integration by parts takes place strictly inside $Q_+$ because the kernel support stays away from its boundary for evaluation points in $Q$. No smooth zero extension of a discontinuously gated density is assumed. [S1]

The spatial grid of mesh $n^{-2}h^{m+3}$ has at most $Cn^4h^{-2m-6}$ points. The field and its expectation have the necessary Lipschitz bound $Ch^{-m-3}$, leaving interpolation error $Cn^{-2}$. Since $h\ge n^{-1}$, the grid logarithm is bounded by a fixed multiple of $\log(CJn/\zeta)$. A union bound over the finite derivatives and groups yields the printed probability. [S1]

The recording-error contribution is a pathwise mean-value estimate, $C\delta h^{-m-3}$. Dependence or adversarial choice of bounded readout errors therefore does not invalidate this step. But this argument applies **after exact acceptance**: it gives no protection against misclassified success/gate labels. That limitation is printed, not a hidden loophole discovered by this report.

The estimates may be signed. This is harmless because they are used for approximation, not asserted to be physical probability densities. The lemma is a sufficient classical fixed-bandwidth argument, not a new empirical-process theorem. The supplied proof is needed: Giné–Guillou's classical uniform-density result alone would not establish all of these derivative, interior-gate and bounded-readout details. [S1, L1]

### R69-M3. The measurable realizable selection resolves a genuine logical interface

**Location:** S1, lines 189–245, principal p. 60.

Applying the exact two-offset identity directly to a noisy kernel estimate would not be justified: the estimate need not factorize, be positive, or lie in the image of a billiard table. Revision 69 does not do this. It chooses a countable dense subset of the actual law image in $C^m(Q)^J$, retaining an actual table for each element, and selects the first candidate within $n^{-1}$ of the countable infimum distance. [S1]

The ambient finite-order function space is separable and metrizable, hence so is its subspace. Each norm is a supremum over a fixed countable dense set of derivative evaluations. The countable infimum and first-index selection are measurable. Neither compactness of the image nor existence of an exact distance minimizer is needed. The selector can be computationally unusable without affecting this existence statement; the paper explicitly makes no efficient-search claim.

If $e$ bounds the kernel error, the true limiting tuple is within $e+C_0e^{-\omega N}$ of the estimate. Density of the candidate image and the triangle inequality give

$$\|f^{\widehat T}-f^T\|_{C^m(Q)}
\le 2e+2C_0e^{-\omega N}+n^{-1}.$$

Only then is the geometric inverse invoked, between **two realizable limiting tuples**. This does not assume that the finite-flight density itself satisfies a limiting identity. Nor does it supply the estimator with the unknown true curvature. The proof crosses the required interface correctly. [S1, S2]

### R69-M4. The accepted-sample rate and the noise exponent balance correctly

**Location:** Theorem 12.2, principal pp. 60–61; S1, lines 210–264.

With

$$\alpha=\frac{s}{2(m+s)+2},\quad
\gamma=\frac{s}{m+s+3},\quad
h=\max\{(L_n/n)^{1/[2(m+s)+2]},\delta^{1/(m+s+3)}\},$$

the stated bound is

$$\|F^{\widehat T}-F^T\|_{C^0([-R,R])}
\le C\{e^{-\omega N}+(L_n/n)^\alpha+\delta^\gamma\}.$$

At the sampling bandwidth $h_n$, the square-root term equals $h_n^s$, whereas the linear term is $h_n^{m+2s}\le h_n^s$. Increasing the bandwidth decreases both stochastic terms. The larger bandwidth contributes at most $h_n^s+\delta^\gamma$ to the bias and at most $\delta^\gamma$ to the readout term. The selection and grid tolerances are smaller at sufficiently large $n$. The small-error threshold and the upper bandwidth restriction remain necessary. [S1]

This is a uniform upper bound on a specified bounded class. It is neither minimax optimality nor a guarantee of exact recovery under a fixed nonzero readout error. The source does not make either claim. Estimating the whole profile in $C^0$ must also not be confused with estimating all Taylor coefficients in an unweighted norm.

### R69-M5. Failed preparations and stopping do not disappear from the rate

**Location:** Theorem 12.3, principal p. 61; S1, lines 256–340.

An independently reset preparation has a failure atom and a successful mark of mass $p_Nf_N$. It can be generated by an independent Bernoulli stream and an independent conditional-mark stream. Consequently, the first $n$ successful latent marks have the required product law. With a fixed budget, their law remains independent of the event that enough successes occurred. This is an exact mixture argument, not a mixing assertion for a single billiard trajectory. [S1]

For $B$ attempts in each of the $J=2r$ groups, the manuscript uses $n_B=\lfloor Bp_*(N)/2\rfloor$ and requires $Bp_*(N)\ge8\log(J/\zeta)$. The binomial lower-tail estimate and union bound cost at most $\zeta$; the density event costs another $\zeta$. Hence the final confidence is $1-2\zeta$, as printed. Dependent bounded readout errors still enter only through the pathwise comparison.

The returning flight number is rounded **down** to a multiple of $P$:

$$N_B=P\left\lfloor\frac{\alpha\log(B/L_B)}{P(\omega+\alpha\Gamma)}\right\rfloor.$$

This changes the bias by at most the fixed factor $e^{\omega P}$. It yields

$$\beta=\frac{\alpha\omega}{\omega+\alpha\Gamma},\qquad
\mathbb P_T\!\left(\|F^{\widehat T}-F^T\|_{C^0}>
C\{(L_B/B)^\beta+\delta^\gamma\}\right)\le2\zeta.$$

The effective sample size is bounded below by a constant times
$B^{\omega/(\omega+\alpha\Gamma)}L_B^{\alpha\Gamma/(\omega+\alpha\Gamma)}$.
Substitution gives the displayed exponent. Since $\Gamma>0$, $\beta<\alpha$: the rarity cost has not been omitted. All $JB$ preparations are charged; bounded period length gives time at most $CJB(1+N_B)$. The conditions for varying confidence are kept explicit rather than silently absorbed into a fixed-confidence asymptotic. [S1]

The rational diagnostic checks 225 parameter cases and 372 finite conditional-mark identities, with controls that deliberately omit the dimension or rarity cost. These corroborate algebraic bookkeeping only; the probability and geometric arguments above remain the proof. [D2]

### R69-M6. The retained smooth inverse is the substantive non-statistical input

**Location:** S2, Section 11; S3 and S5.

The constant-one visit contraction is important. Actual tail uniqueness identifies later visits with compositions of one-step maps whose derivatives are uniformly below one on a common collar. A bound $C\rho^j|u|$ with uncontrolled $C$ would not by itself justify increasing the vanishing order. The retained proof supplies $|x_{b,j}(u)|\le a^j|u|$ instead. [S2, S3]

Integrating the actual envelope along the segment between two candidates gives $g=\overline\alpha h+Kh$, with $\overline\alpha\ge1/2$ and
$\|K\|_{m,R}\le3a^m/(1-a^m)$. The condition $6a^m/(1-a^m)<1$ is therefore sufficient. The operator depends on the candidate pair; using it to prove separation is valid, while calling it a directly computable observation-only inverse would not be. The paper makes the former use.

For exact smooth identity, global curvature uniqueness and the signed finite-jet recursion first show flatness. Equality of the **full actions** is then used again in the weighted estimate to eliminate the flat difference. For stability, the low-order polynomial correction is made before taking the high-vanishing norm. The differentiated envelope controls the action change caused by that polynomial, and Taylor's integral remainder bounds the aligned tail. Thus the argument does not place arbitrary unequal jets in an infinite weighted norm, or equate smooth functions with their Taylor series. [S2]

The global quadratic inverse uses the positive Schur fixed point and a two-point comparison, not merely pointwise nonsingularity of a Jacobian. The finite-iteration reconstruction retains
$C_M(\tau^N+\varepsilon)+L_ME_m$ and its off-fixed-point higher-jet recursion. No earlier stopping error has been reopened by the present revision. [S5]

These mechanisms deserve credit. They also locate the true burden of originality: it lies in the relative physical-law/actual-contact combination, not in counting kernel regularization, triangle inequalities and concentration as separate breakthroughs.

### R69-M7. The flat-family test is valid at fixed separation, not a spectral or uniform-resolution theorem

**Location:** S1, lines 342–358, principal pp. 61–62; S2, Proposition 11.6.

At a fixed nonzero contact coordinate where the cutoff is one, the actual equal-area family has profile separation at least $\Delta=\varepsilon e^{-1/u_*^2}$. If the uniform profile-error radius is below $\Delta/3$, nearest-candidate comparison gives a test with error at either table at most $2\zeta$. The remote area correction leaves that contact value unchanged. This is a legitimate consequence for finite charged observations, including a genuinely flat geometric distinction. [S1, S2]

The phrase “at every fixed nonzero scale” matters. The displayed rate supplies no inexpensive or uniform resolution as $u_*\to0$, and a fixed noise floor may prevent meeting the threshold. Equal contact jets do not imply equal complete laws or equal normalized density jets. No equal-full-marked-length-spectrum example has been constructed here. These distinctions are correctly retained.

## 5. Exceptional significance and presentation

### R69-E1. A real acquisition advance does not by itself settle the placement case

The previous review's observation objection has materially changed. The paper now recovers a complete smooth contact profile from finitely many independent physical preparations, rather than assuming an exact function-valued density input. The realizable selection and the charging of failures are indispensable interfaces. Dismissing the revision as a cosmetic change would be inaccurate. [S1, S6]

Nevertheless, after the retained relative forward law and deterministic smooth inverse are granted, the new section is principally a composition of finite-order kernel approximation, a countable minimum-distance construction, a binomial tail, and a logarithmic bias/sample-size balance. This makes the existing mechanism operational as an existence theorem. It does not introduce a new general statistical inverse method, prove a sharp acquisition threshold, or expose a further geometric rigidity mechanism. Those are descriptions of what the proof actually does, not requirements imposed to repair a false statement.

The deepest part of the submission remains the exact mixed-twist normalization combined with the actual stationary envelope and its smooth separation argument. I have reconsidered that combined theorem with the new consequence, but the manuscript still does not persuasively demonstrate why the mechanism has the exceptional mathematical reach claimed by the requested placement. The author acknowledges the classical statistical ingredients; that acknowledgment is correct, but it also prevents the addition of three numbered results from carrying an independent significance argument.

### R69-E2. The observation class and quantitative constants limit what can be inferred

The main experiment still supplies the collision polygon, contact labels and tangent frames, two exact separated offsets, and exact success/gate tags. It uses independent full-phase resets and known uniform geometric/smoothness margins. Only endpoint values after acceptance receive the stated bounded-noise treatment. Unknown registration, label errors, offset uncertainty and single-trajectory dependence are not covered. These are declared assumptions, not undisclosed errors. They nevertheless limit claims of broadly applicable physical recovery. [S1, S6]

The rate is a sufficient upper bound for this class. Its small exponent must not be represented as a sharp difficulty theorem or as an efficient algorithm. As an algebraic illustration, the sufficient weighted condition gives $m=3$ for $a=1/2$, but $m=38$ for $a=19/20$. With $s=1$ and the illustrative ratio $\Gamma/\omega=1$, the latter formulas give $\alpha=1/80$, $\beta=1/81$ and $\gamma=1/42$. These are substitutions in the sufficient estimates, not an exhibited table realizing all these constants or a lower bound. They show why positivity of a rate alone is not a quantitative optimality or practicality claim. [S1, S2, D2]

Likewise, contact-local smooth identity does not imply whole-obstacle smooth identity on unvisited arcs. The retained whole-obstacle conclusion still uses its analytic continuation, fixed placement/lattice and visitation assumptions. One should not combine conclusions from different observation models into a single apparently assumption-free recovery theorem. The current qualifications substantially avoid that mistake. [S5, S6]

### R69-E3. The principal article still carries a high reader-facing burden

The new argument is integrated immediately after the smooth inverse, rather than appended as an unrelated abstract theorem. That is an improvement. However, the 163-page principal article and 337-page technical manuscript still require the reader to distinguish several different inverse problems and norms: exact contact identity, finite-real-smoothness stability, analytic norm inversion, whole-boundary continuation, finite-jet computational stopping, and sampled recovery. The enlarged synthesis and retained catalogue do not themselves establish that every component belongs to one indispensable principal proof. [S6, D1]

My editorial request is a sharper comparative mathematical thesis and dependency presentation: which obstruction is solved by the central mechanism, what exact observation supplies it, and which consequences genuinely change the inverse problem rather than merely compose known estimates. This can be addressed while retaining all valid mathematics in the repository and technical companion. It is not a demand to delete proofs, weaken correct statements, manufacture an unrelated theorem, or restart a nominal repair loop.

The recommendation therefore remains adverse at the requested level. Another specialist may judge the geometric mechanism more significant; the present report records a reasoned placement judgment, not a proof that such a judgment is uniquely correct.
