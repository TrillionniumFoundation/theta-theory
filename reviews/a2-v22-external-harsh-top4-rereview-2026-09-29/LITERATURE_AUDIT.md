# Focused literature audit for the external A2 v22 rereview

**Date checked:** 29 September 2026  
**Reviewed manuscript:** Qian Qi, *Reference-free certification from intrinsic boundary laws*  
**Scope:** theorem/input comparison for the top-four rereview; not an exhaustive novelty or priority search.

## 1. Purpose of the comparison

The local A2 observation is not a collision-count germ or an ordinary marked length spectrum. For one selected three-impact return branch it records two complete endpoint-arclength subprobability densities at two absolute times. Their affine dependence on time recovers a local stationary action. The global theorem then uses complete recovered obstacle images, physical cycle displacement vectors, absolute cell normalization, and a completion defect.

The nearest literature therefore falls into four different categories:

1. boundary-distance, lens, scattering, and travel-time inverse problems;
2. marked-length rigidity for dispersing billiards;
3. conditional analytic continuation;
4. integer subgroup computation.

The distinctions below are important for novelty and information-category claims.

## 2. Boundary and lens rigidity

### Stefanov–Uhlmann–Vasy

P. Stefanov, G. Uhlmann, and A. Vasy, *Local and global boundary rigidity and the geodesic X-ray transform in the normal gauge*, Annals of Mathematics 194 (2021), 1–95; arXiv:1702.03638.

The primary theorem concerns recovery of a Riemannian metric, modulo the natural boundary-fixing gauge, from boundary/lens information under strict convexity and foliation hypotheses, in dimensions covered by the paper. A2 instead has a known Euclidean ambient metric and unknown reflecting curves, local multiple-reflection actions, periodic copies, and a period group.

The paper does not obviously contain A2's explicit two-reflection curvature identities or its missing-area/period-index certificate. It does show that recovery of geometry from endpoint travel-time data and their derivatives belongs to an established inverse-geometric paradigm. A2's local theorem should therefore be presented as a billiard realization with an explicit stationary Schur-complement inverse, not as the first general observation that endpoint travel times determine nearby geometry.

Primary source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03638
- https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2021.194.1.1

## 3. Obstacle travel-time and scattering rigidity

### Gurfinkel–Noakes–Stoyanov

T. Gurfinkel, L. Noakes, and L. Stoyanov, *Travelling Times in Scattering by Obstacles in Curved Space*, Journal of Differential Equations 269 (2020), 9508–9530; arXiv:2003.12261.

This work studies exterior scattering/travel-time data for obstacle systems and derives geometric rigidity under its hypotheses. The data, flow setting, and conclusions differ from A2's selected near-onset three-impact subprobability laws. In particular, A2 recovers an intermediate-reflection stationary action from two physical endpoint densities and then assembles a periodic table.

The source does not obviously subsume the v22 completion defect or reference-free lattice recovery. It is nevertheless a closer information comparison than ordinary marked-length rigidity because A2's two-density quotient first produces a function of endpoint travel times/actions.

Primary source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12261
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jde.2020.08.012

### Noakes–Stoyanov and retained comparisons

The retained v21 source discusses local endpoint derivative relations and related obstacle-scattering results. Version 22 preserves those comparisons rather than claiming that its selected Liouville law is equivalent to a standard scattering relation. The current rereview did not independently re-read every retained source in full.

## 4. Marked-length rigidity for dispersing billiards

### Bálint–De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil

P. Bálint, J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin, and M. Leguil, *Marked length spectrum, homoclinic orbits and the geometry of open dispersing billiards*, Communications in Mathematical Physics 374 (2020), 1535–1575.

This line extracts curvature and dynamical information from periodic and homoclinic marked lengths. It uses a global dynamical spectrum, not endpoint-resolved near-onset Liouville densities.

Primary record:

- https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-019-03448-x

### De Simoi–Kaloshin–Leguil

J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin, and M. Leguil, *Marked length spectral determination of analytic chaotic billiards with axial symmetries*, Inventiones Mathematicae 233 (2023), 829–901; arXiv:1905.00890v4.

The theorem determines an analytic open dispersing billiard in a symmetric/generic class from marked periodic lengths. A2 uses a different, richer local observation and removes symmetry in its generic asymmetric class, but it also retains selected branches, endpoint marks, absolute phase normalization, and compact analytic priors. Neither theorem should be described as a special case of the other.

Primary sources:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00890v4
- https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-023-01191-8

### Finamore–Leguil

D. Finamore and M. Leguil, *A CAT(0)-approach to the marked length spectral rigidity of Sinai billiards*, arXiv:2510.18983v1.

The observation is an enriched marked-length spectrum in a finite-horizon Sinai-billiard setting. It is not the selected endpoint-density experiment of A2. The manuscript should retain the distinction between a conventional global dynamical invariant and a purpose-built local sensor.

Primary source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18983v1

### Osterman

O. V. Osterman, *On length spectrum rigidity of dispersing billiard systems*, arXiv:2208.12244v1.

The paper relates marked lengths, homoclinic analytic conjugacy, and obstacle recovery under its stated information and geometric assumptions. It belongs to the marked-spectrum comparison, not to the reference-free completion-certificate mechanism.

Primary source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12244v1

### De Simoi–Kaloshin–Wei

J. De Simoi, V. Kaloshin, and Q. Wei, *Dynamical spectral rigidity among Z2-symmetric strictly convex domains close to a circle*, Annals of Mathematics 186 (2017), 277–314.

This is a deformational spectral-rigidity theorem for a symmetric focusing-billiard class. Its observation and geometry differ from periodic dispersing tables and endpoint Liouville laws.

Primary source:

- https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2017.186.1.7

## 5. Formal billiard inverses and count-data scope

### Koval

I. Koval, *Billiard tables with analytic Birkhoff normal form are generically Gevrey divergent*, arXiv:2403.14448v1.

The paper is relevant to formal reconstruction and the distinction between formal coefficient recovery and convergent analytic geometry. A2 v22 does not use a formal all-order count recursion for its completion theorem. Its local full-density inverse reconstructs functions on arcs, while its exact analytic count-only problem remains unresolved.

Primary source:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14448v1

The v22 defect theorem should not be summarized as count-only rigidity. The hidden-area signal uses absolute probabilities, and the local geometry uses endpoint-resolved laws.

## 6. Conditional analytic continuation

### Trefethen

L. N. Trefethen, *Quantifying the ill-conditioning of analytic continuation*, BIT Numerical Mathematics 60 (2020), 901–915.

The source quantifies the geometry-dependent instability of analytic continuation. A2 proves the two-constants/harmonic-measure estimate needed for its bounded periodic-strip class and makes no universal or optimal continuation-rate claim. The comparison supports the conditional nature of the exponent `theta`; it does not establish optimality of the full inverse.

Primary source:

- https://doi.org/10.1007/s10543-020-00802-7

## 7. Integer normal forms

### Kannan–Bachem

R. Kannan and A. Bachem, *Polynomial algorithms for computing the Smith and Hermite normal forms of an integer matrix*, SIAM Journal on Computing 8 (1979), 499–507.

This source supports attribution and complexity for the integer normal-form stage after exact rational coordinates have been recovered. It does not imply that A2's analytic-body fitting, record acquisition, shape congruence, or conditional continuation is polynomial-time.

Primary source:

- https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040

The genuinely problem-specific arithmetic step in v22 is the proof of a finite denominator bound from physical cycle-vector and covolume bounds. Once the exact integer column group is available, standard Hermite/Smith machinery applies.

## 8. Novelty assessment used in the report

The focused comparison supports the following balanced conclusion.

1. I found no inspected source that obviously contains the exact v22 combination of:
   - a two-density intrinsic billiard inverse;
   - complete-image reference-free incidence recovery;
   - the missing-area/period-index defect;
   - bounded-denominator cycle subgroup recovery before calibration;
   - an omission-safe noisy completion decision.
2. The local principle—endpoint travel-time/action data and their derivatives determine nearby geometry—has close precedents in boundary, lens, and obstacle-scattering rigidity.
3. The exact defect identity is an elegant new consistency/completeness mechanism, but it is elementary once absolute free area, complete visible body images, and the physical cycle subgroup are known.
4. The theorem uses a much richer observation than ordinary marked lengths or collision counts. Its novelty should be stated within that information category.
5. The v22 hidden-area lower bound is a direct two-point statistical result for one subexperiment, not a whole-table minimax theorem.
6. No exhaustive priority search was performed. The report's rejection at the requested benchmark is not based on a claim that the complete theorem is already published elsewhere; it is based on the theorem's naturality, acquisition gap, strong priors, and conceptual reach relative to its rich data.

## 9. Limits

The primary sources listed above were used for theorem/input comparison only. This audit does not certify historical priority, survey all lens/scattering literature, re-referee the cited papers, or convert bibliography accuracy into a proof of A2. A formal submission should be checked by a human expert in dispersing billiards and inverse scattering before publication claims are made.
