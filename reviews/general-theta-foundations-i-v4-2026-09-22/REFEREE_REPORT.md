## 2. Executive assessment

Version 4 is not a cosmetic revision. It answers a remarkable fraction of the previous report with real mathematics.

The strongest improvements are:

1. the former one-sided posterior-orbit converse is now paired with an explicit finite-state realization theorem, with every age state charged;
2. the doubling-map benchmark has been replaced by a higher-dimensional integer expanding-torus theorem with noisy acquisitions, a nongeometric renewal class, an explicit suffix machine, and a uniform critical window;
3. the paper now derives a singular inverse modulus in an actual moment experiment rather than assuming a global contact equivalence;
4. the literature comparison now explicitly distinguishes persistent-state cardinality from communication rate and dynamical rate distortion;
5. the history audit is admirably disciplined about not importing unproved historical spectral, LDP, kinetic, operator, or phase claims.

I therefore withdraw several objections in their v3 form. It is no longer fair to say that the paper has only a general converse, only perfect acquisition, only a binary map, no near-critical analysis, or only an assumed singular contact law.

Nevertheless, the fourth revision still falls short of the requested four-journal level.

The decisive issue has shifted. The paper now contains several good theorems, but the common “General Theta” claim is still not established by a theorem of comparable breadth. The general posterior-orbit theorem transfers a pre-existing Hilbert quantization invariant into a causal-memory statement; it does not identify that invariant from intrinsic dynamical data. The sharp expanding theorem computes the invariant in a class whose geometry is still generated by exact symbolic expansion or by conjugacy to it. The collision theorem is a clean finite-dimensional algebraic-statistical model, but it does not close the singular geometry of a difficult downstream physical model. Finally, none of the historical load-bearing gates in the eleven-paper pipeline is closed by the new foundation.

At a strong specialist journal I would regard parts of this revision as potentially publishable after substantial refocusing. At the requested four-journal level, the manuscript still needs one theorem that changes the status of the subject rather than one more layer of carefully controlled examples.

## 3. What v4 genuinely closes from the v3 report

### 3.1 The causal-realizability gap is materially addressed

Theorem thm:v4-orbit is the most important advance in the revision.

For arbitrary standard Borel dynamics inside independent renewal cycles, arbitrary noisy reset observations, and bounded vector readouts, it defines the posterior future orbit

\[
Z(y)=\left(\sqrt{w_k}\,m_k(y)\right)_{k\ge0}
\]

and proves both

\[
e_M^2(Z)\le E_M^{\rm av}
\]

and the constructive bound

\[
E_{Nn+1}^{\rm av}\le e_N^2(Z^{<n})+F^2W(n).
\]

The age register is not hidden. The upper machine really has states indexed by codeword and age, plus an exhausted state. The converse allows randomized, time-dependent machines. The proof freezes the independent randomization, conditions at a renewal, obtains at most M deterministic output strings over a no-renewal continuation, and then uses renewal-reward counting. I do not see a fatal flaw in this argument.

This is a substantial improvement over v3.

The exact shift-closed ray characterization for stationary deterministic autonomous machines is also conceptually useful. The two-state counterexample correctly demonstrates that an orbit codebook can have cardinality one while causal execution needs an additional state to remember whether the current renewal event occurred.

### 3.2 The phase-transition theorem is no longer a one-dimensional noiseless curiosity

Theorem thm:v4-noisy treats

\[
T_bx=bx \pmod 1
\]

in every dimension, every integer base \(b\ge2\), wrapped Gaussian acquisition noise, and renewal laws satisfying the displayed bounded-mean-residual-lifetime condition. The posterior attenuation is explicit, and the excess-risk profile

\[
\Psi_v(n)
=
\sum_{k<n}v_k b^{-2(n-k)}
+
\sum_{k\ge n}v_k
\]

is bounded above and below by constants after converting the state budget into the exactly counted suffix-state depth.

The lower bound is not restricted to interval quantizers or to on-image codebook centres. The upper construction pays for all suffix lengths. The critical window

\[
q_n=b^{-2}e^{x/n}
\]

and its limiting crossover function are also genuinely present. The manuscript carefully avoids converting its constant-factor optimal bounds into an unsupported exact optimal prefactor.

Again, I do not find a fatal error in the proof as written.

### 3.3 Noisy acquisition and post-acquisition transport are combined in one experiment

The v3 report asked for a model in which filtering uncertainty and expanding information transport interact rather than being presented as separate examples. Version 4 does this.

The decomposition of the raw risk into the full-history Bayes floor plus finite-state excess risk is natural, and the posterior attenuation

\[
a_k=\exp(-2\pi^2\sigma^2b^{2k})
\]

correctly enters the same weighted orbit used for the memory lower bound. The order comparison between the Bayes noise floor and the memory profile is a useful result.

### 3.4 The singular geometry is now derived in an actual model

Theorem thm:v4-roots studies noisy observations of the first \(d\) power sums of \(d\) ordered roots, with an unrestricted additive calibration nuisance measured in a correlated Gaussian pair.

The proof chain is coherent:

- Newton identities turn moment discrepancies into coefficient discrepancies;
- a Rouché argument gives global and separated-cluster inverse root moduli;
- perturbing the constant coefficient creates real colliding configurations whose first \(r-1\) moments match;
- a Gaussian two-point argument supplies the statistical lower bound;
- a positive-volume covering argument supplies the hard-label lower bound.

This directly answers the earlier complaint that the contact exponent had merely been assumed.

### 3.5 The theorem-level literature positioning is much better

The revision now explicitly compares its resource model with Tatikonda–Mitter and with dynamical rate distortion/metric mean dimension, and it correctly positions the Gaussian nuisance argument within invariant minimax theory. This is a meaningful improvement over v3.

## 4. Decisive objection E1: the “general” orbit theorem still transfers an unknown invariant rather than deriving one

The central general theorem now has a lower and an upper side. That is important. But its strongest universal consequence is still conditional:

> if the posterior-orbit quantization error has a finite positive exponent, and the renewal tail is stretched exponential, then the causal risk has the same exponent.

Thus the theorem does not answer the hardest question for a general dynamical system:

\[
\text{What is } e_M^2(Z) \text{ in terms of intrinsic dynamics?}
\]

The paper leaves that quantity as an input.

For a four-journal theorem, I would expect some structural bridge from the dynamics and observation law to the orbit quantization complexity—something involving entropy, Lyapunov data, pressure, metric mean dimension, a Markov partition, a hyperbolic product structure, or another recognizable invariant. Without such a bridge, the “general” theorem is a powerful transfer principle but not yet a general dynamical complexity theory.

This distinction is not semantic. If an application must first solve a new infinite-dimensional functional-quantization problem for \(Z\), then the abstract theorem has relocated the difficulty rather than classified it.

The current exact autonomous theorem does not close this gap. It characterizes stationary deterministic realizability by left-shift closure of a finite ray codebook. That is elegant, but it does not characterize the asymptotic overhead of approximately shift-closed near-optimal codebooks, nor does it cover general randomized/nonstationary optimality.

A stronger foundation would prove a theorem of the form

\[
E_M^{\rm av}
\asymp
\mathfrak C_M(T,K,f,\mu,L)
\]

for a broad dynamical class, with \(\mathfrak C_M\) expressed in intrinsic terms that can actually be estimated without separately solving the full orbit quantization problem.

## 5. Decisive objection E2: “arbitrary Borel dynamics” is bought by complete regeneration

Theorem thm:v4-orbit allows arbitrary Borel \(T\), but only inside independent renewal cycles. At each renewal the hidden state is replaced by a fresh independent pair \((U,Y)\). Between renewals there are no new data.

That structure is exactly why the proof can separate cycles and sum disjoint last-renewal events.

This is a legitimate and interesting model. It is not a general theorem for partially observed dynamics with persistent hidden memory.

The following important settings remain outside the theorem:

- partial resets rather than complete regeneration;
- Markov latent state carried across acquisition times;
- continuing noisy observations between renewals;
- action-dependent acquisition;
- endogenous or state-dependent renewal times;
- controlled dynamics;
- hidden regimes whose posterior, rather than physical state, carries memory between cycles.

These are precisely the settings in which the causal state itself evolves and where “future-orbit quantization” becomes genuinely difficult.

The paper should therefore not let the phrase “arbitrary Borel dynamics” obscure the strength of the regenerative independence assumption. The dynamics within a cycle are arbitrary; the stochastic dependence across cycles is not.

At the four-journal level, the next structural theorem should survive at least one nontrivial form of dependence across refresh events.

## 6. Decisive objection E3: the sharp expanding theorem still has exact symbolic geometry at its core

Version 4 greatly broadens the doubling example: base \(b\), dimension \(d\), noise, nongeometric renewal laws, and a uniform critical window are real advances.

But the sharp proof still depends on a special exact structure:

- multiplication by an integer on the torus;
- Haar resets;
- a Fourier readout with explicitly computable Gaussian attenuation;
- exact base-\(b\) symbolic suffixes;
- exact cube conditional means;
- a before-wrap separation estimate.

The advertised nonconstant-slope extension is by conjugacy. That is a valid robustness statement, but it preserves the same underlying symbolic model and the same Lyapunov exponent. It is not a pressure theorem for a genuinely nonconjugate expanding map.

The manuscript itself correctly says this. That honesty should be credited. But the consequence is that the universality question raised in v3 remains only partly answered.

A four-journal-level result would ideally show that the critical balance

\[
\text{renewal survival} \quad \text{versus} \quad \text{dynamical expansion}
\]

persists for a broad class in which the controlling quantity is not inserted by exact base-\(b\) arithmetic. For example: a Markov expanding map, a nonconstant Jacobian cocycle, a Gibbs measure, a subshift with nonuniform branch weights, or a genuinely pressure-controlled distortion law.

The current theorem is a sharp solved model. It is not yet the structural classification theorem that the programme suggests.

## 7. Decisive objection E4: the collision theorem is a clean algebraic-statistical benchmark, not yet the hard singular theory of the pipeline

The root-moment theorem is mathematically respectable. It should remain in the record.

But its significance must be calibrated correctly.

The observation map is finite dimensional and explicitly algebraic:

\[
\theta
\longmapsto
(P_1(\theta),\ldots,P_d(\theta)).
\]

The singularity is the classical collision of polynomial roots. Newton identities and root perturbation are exactly the right tools. The testing alternatives are constructed by a constant-term perturbation of a real-rooted polynomial.

This proves that the General Theta singular-modulus language can be realized in one nonregular model. It does not prove that the difficult singular geometries in the intended downstream physical models admit the same reduction.

In particular, it does not establish:

- the historical Sinai raw local-limit theorem;
- the hard-particle collision LDP;
- the modern A2 determinantal/contact geometry in its full model-specific form;
- a local asymptotic experiment at a changing physical stratum;
- a uniform theorem through collapsing gaps or growing dimension.

There is also a conceptual limitation in the displayed minimax law. The two lower bounds are obtained separately:

1. a two-point statistical singularity lower bound of order \(\sigma^{2/r_*}\);
2. a generic \(d\)-dimensional hard-label covering lower bound of order \(M^{-2/d}\).

They are combined using \(\max(u,v)\ge(u+v)/2\).

That is valid. But it means the theorem does not yet uncover a genuinely coupled compression-statistics phenomenon in the collision model. The label exponent comes from the positive-volume ambient parameter box, not from the collision singularity. The singularity changes the noise exponent while the representation exponent remains the ordinary ambient \(d\)-dimensional quantization rate.

For a foundational singular theorem, I would look for a regime in which the stratification itself changes the optimal representation geometry or where \(M\) and \(\sigma\) interact nonadditively.

## 8. Decisive objection E5: the historical pipeline still supplies no theorem-level validation of the new foundation

This remains the most important programme-level weakness.

The history audit correctly preserves the two fixed dependency chains

\[
A2\to A3\to A4\to C2\to D1
\]

and

\[
B2\text{-GC}\to B1\to B2\text{-MC}\to B3\to B4\to C1/C2\to D1,
\]

with A1 on a separate line.

It also correctly records that the historical hard gates remain model-specific obligations: spectral/Fourier control, path LDPs, renewal-memory estimates, canonical coefficient asymptotics, interacting collision laws, process CLTs, Mosco limits, nonlinear semigroup generation, filter charts, LAN/BvM, common operator domains, and physical phase/posterior constructions.

Version 4 does not silently assume any of these. That is excellent mathematical discipline.

But from the point of view of a paper titled *General Theta Foundations I*, the negative consequence is unavoidable:

**the new foundation has not yet caused one difficult downstream theorem to become proved.**

The paper advances T07/T08 and gives examples for G1/G2/G3. It does not close a G4 model realization. Therefore the entire historical pipeline remains, theorem by theorem, where it was before this foundation was introduced.

A foundational paper at the highest level should normally demonstrate leverage. One hard downstream theorem should become shorter, clearer, or newly provable because the foundation exists. Here the interfaces are cleaner, but the load-bearing model mathematics remains unresolved.

I do not regard “we do not use the old failed theorems” as evidence that the foundation supports the old programme. It only shows that the new results are logically independent.

## 9. Decisive objection E6: the integrated 64-page architecture is now in tension with the project’s own master plan

The master General Theta document explicitly proposes three volumes and says that T01–T12 are reliable foundations, many of them classical or direct, while the genuinely new theory should come from G1–G4.

The fourth revision nevertheless compiles the new orbit, torus, and root-moment results together with the preceding quantitative development and the full foundational appendices.

Preservation in the repository is desirable. Preservation inside the principal journal article is not automatically desirable.

The current architecture creates three problems.

First, the manuscript has multiple mathematical centres with different audiences:

- causal finite-state prediction and functional orbit quantization;
- expanding dynamics and symbolic memory;
- invariant Gaussian nuisance reduction;
- polynomial root singularities;
- filter/control foundations and programme interfaces.

Second, the genuinely new v4 chain is front-loaded into a small part of a 64-page object, while much of the remaining material is retained because of project history rather than because it is necessary for the new theorems.

Third, the integrated format makes the “foundations” label carry more rhetorical weight than the proven dependencies justify.

I would strongly prefer a focused paper around the posterior-orbit theorem plus the noisy expanding theorem, with the calibration/root-moment theorem developed separately unless a deeper theorem genuinely couples the two sides. The general axiomatic material can remain a companion or programme paper.

Splitting alone would not make either paper an Annals/Acta/Inventiones/JAMS paper. But without splitting, it is harder to see the true depth of each contribution.

## 10. Decisive objection E7: the literature audit is improved but still not complete for the exact mathematical resource being studied

The new comparison with data-rate control and metric mean dimension is welcome.

However, the manuscript’s core resource is neither merely channel rate nor ordinary block rate distortion. It is a zero-delay/causal finite-state representation problem with hard persistent cardinality. There is a directly adjacent literature on zero-delay coding of Markov sources and on finite-memory/stationary coding policies that deserves theorem-level comparison.

In particular, the revision should discuss the relation to work such as:

- T. Linder and S. Yüksel, *On Optimal Zero-Delay Coding of Vector Markov Sources*, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 60 (2014);
- A. Wood, T. Linder, and S. Yüksel, *Optimal Zero Delay Coding of Markov Sources: Stationary and Finite Memory Codes*, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 63 (2017).

I am not claiming that these papers prove the present theorem. The point is that they address exactly the structural questions of causal coding policy classes, stationarity, and finite memory that the present manuscript treats as a central conceptual distinction.

Likewise, because the general invariant \(e_M^2(Z)\) is a Hilbert-space quantization error of a stochastic process/vector sequence and the expanding lower bound is a small-ball argument, the relation to functional quantization and small-ball/metric-entropy methods should be discussed directly. The manuscript should make clear which part is classical Hilbert-space quantization and which part is specifically new because the code must be realized causally by a finite persistent state.

For a four-journal claim, novelty must be isolated against these closest mathematical neighbours, not only against the project’s own A1/A2 papers.

## 11. The resource model is coherent, but it is narrower than ordinary “memory complexity”

The manuscript is much more careful about this than earlier revisions. Still, the distinction is important enough to repeat in a referee report.

The constrained object is the cardinality of the persistent state \(I_t\). The model permits:

- a free absolute clock;
- arbitrary fixed program descriptions;
- arbitrary real constants in the program;
- unbounded transient computation;
- transient workspace not charged to \(M\);
- time-dependent stochastic kernels.

This is a valid state-cardinality model. It is not a bound on total storage, communication rate, bit complexity, description length, or computational complexity.

The two-state boundary example also depends on a specific interface convention: the renewal indicator is observed by the update kernel but is not separately available to the decoder after the update. Therefore the update must store the current renewal information in the persistent state if the output needs it.

That convention is coherent and explicitly stated. But it materially affects exact zero-error cardinalities. I would ask the final paper to elevate this point from a technical definition to a prominently stated modelling choice.

## 12. Proof audit of the new principal theorems

### 12.1 Posterior-orbit theorem

I did not find a fatal defect.

The orthogonal decomposition against the complete-history conditional mean is standard and correctly used. Because a fresh acquired pair is independent of previous cycles and survival is independent of the fresh pair, the posterior mean at age \(k\) is indeed \(m_k(Y)\).

The lower-bound argument correctly conditions at a renewal, freezes all independent randomization, and observes that there are at most \(M\) no-renewal output strings determined by the \(M\) possible post-renewal states. Summing over disjoint last-renewal events and invoking the Cesàro renewal count gives the desired average lower bound.

The upper construction genuinely realizes a truncated orbit code with \(Nn+1\) states.

My remaining objection is structural, not local: the theorem preserves exponents only after the functional-quantization exponent is supplied and after imposing a sufficiently strong tail assumption.

### 12.2 Expanding small-ball lower bound and suffix upper bound

I did not find a fatal defect.

The before-wrap separation estimate, profile monotonicity, and volume estimate combine in a coherent way. The argument explicitly accommodates Hilbert-ball centres not lying on the orbit image.

The suffix construction charges all word lengths and uses the conditional mean over a \(b\)-adic cube, yielding the displayed sinc factor. The critical-window Riemann-sum limit is consistent with the exact profile.

The word “sharp” should, however, be read as “matching profile up to constants,” not “exact optimal leading constant.” The text mostly respects this distinction; the terminology should remain disciplined throughout.

### 12.3 Root-moment inverse modulus and minimax theorem

I did not find a fatal defect.

The Newton/Rouché argument is a plausible and standard way to obtain the global \(1/d\) Hölder inverse and the cluster \(1/r_j\) inverse. The real-root testing construction via \(Q+c\) correctly preserves the coefficients above the constant term, hence the power sums below order \(r\).

The minimum-distance upper bound and two-point lower bound are consistent with those moduli. The label lower bound is independent of the observation model, as it should be.

Again, my objection is not that this theorem is wrong. It is that, at the requested venue level, it remains a tractable finite-dimensional algebraic model rather than the broad singular-observation theorem suggested by the programme.

### 12.4 Exact calibration quotient

The retained theorem used by the new collision result also appears coherent within its stated scope.

The Gaussian transformation separates \(D=Y-W\) from a nuisance-carrying residual. Diffusing the nuisance prior makes that residual asymptotically parameter independent, and a \(D\)-encoder can privately simulate the replacement residual without increasing its label count. The conclusion is equality of the minimax decision problems for \(\theta\), not Blackwell equivalence for the full \((\theta,\eta)\) experiment.

That limitation is correctly stated and should remain prominent.

## 13. Disposition of the v3 decisive objections

For clarity, I record how the new revision changes the previous report.

### v3 E1: general converse without realization

**Substantially closed, but replaced by a deeper structural objection.**

There is now a general \(Nn+1\)-state realization theorem and an exponent-preservation corollary. What remains missing is an intrinsic theorem that computes the orbit quantization invariant for a broad dynamical class.

### v3 E2: one engineered example and no critical scaling

**Substantially closed.**

Noise, higher dimension, every integer expansion base, nongeometric renewal laws under a residual-life condition, and a uniform critical window are now present.

What remains is universality beyond exact symbolic/conjugate expansion.

### v3 E3: finite-rate control and dynamical rate-distortion comparisons

**Largely closed as originally stated.**

The paper now makes the resource distinctions directly. Additional comparison with zero-delay finite-memory coding and functional quantization is still needed.

### v3 E4: classical invariant minimax theory

**Improved, not a source of four-journal novelty by itself.**

The paper now locates the quotient honestly in invariant minimax theory. This makes the theorem better positioned but also confirms that it should not carry the main novelty burden.

### v3 E5: assumed singular contact geometry

**Closed for the new root-moment model, not for the intended difficult physical models.**

The new theorem derives the modulus and matching testing alternatives. This is real progress.

### v3 E6: relation to the historical pipeline

**Not closed.**

The audit is better, but no historical hard gate is proved.

### v3 E7: over-integrated architecture

**Not closed.**

The article has grown from roughly 52 to 64 pages while retaining the full preceding development.

## 14. What would change my recommendation

I would not recommend another revision whose main additions are more diagnostics, more examples of conjugate torus maps, more retained appendices, or another finite-dimensional algebraic benchmark.

At least one of the following would materially change the four-journal assessment.

### Route A: an intrinsic orbit-complexity theorem

For a broad class of partially observed regenerative or nonregenerative systems, derive the posterior-orbit quantization exponent—or a matching causal risk law—from recognizable dynamical invariants.

A serious theorem should make the quantity \(e_M(Z)\) an output rather than an assumption.

### Route B: a genuinely nonconjugate expansion theorem

Replace exact base-\(b\) symbolic arithmetic by a class in which the threshold is governed by pressure, Lyapunov data, entropy, or another invariant, and prove the critical crossover uniformly.

### Route C: causal realization with persistent hidden memory

Extend the lower/upper theory beyond complete regeneration, for example to partial refreshment or a hidden Markov state carried across acquisitions. This would connect the new orbit theorem to the paper’s retained filtering foundations in a nontrivial way.

### Route D: a singular model in which compression and statistical degeneracy genuinely interact

Prove a theorem where the optimal \(M\)-state representation itself changes across singular strata or where \(M\) and the physical noise scale interact in the minimax exponent, rather than entering through two separately forced additive floors.

### Route E: close one historical hard gate

Use the GTF machinery to prove one theorem that remains unresolved in the historical pipeline: a genuine spectral/local-limit result, a path LDP, a kinetic/particle theorem, a nonlinear semigroup result, an unbounded-operator closure theorem, or a model-specific phase/posterior theorem.

This would be the clearest evidence that the word “Foundations” denotes mathematical leverage rather than only a clean interface language.

## 15. Editorial recommendation

**REJECT at the Annals/Acta/Inventiones/JAMS standard.**

This is a much stronger paper than the version I previously reviewed. I would no longer describe its main mathematical content as one-sided, noiseless, or dependent on an assumed contact geometry. The v4 author has answered serious criticism with serious theorems.

The remaining objection is therefore more demanding, not less: the manuscript now needs a theorem that explains why these results belong to one new general theory and why that theory changes what can be proved in difficult models.

At present:

- the general causal theorem transfers an orbit quantization invariant but does not derive it;
- the sharp dynamical theorem remains rooted in exact symbolic/conjugate expansion;
- the singular theorem is a clean finite-dimensional algebraic model;
- the historical pipeline remains mathematically unresolved;
- the 64-page integrated architecture preserves far more than the principal argument requires.

I do not see a demonstrated fatal counterexample to the new principal theorems in the scope of this review. My recommendation is a **four-journal significance, generality, and architecture rejection**, not a claim that the paper is mathematically empty or that its new central proofs obviously fail.

A focused causal-memory paper containing Theorems thm:v4-orbit and thm:v4-noisy, strengthened by one nonconjugate structural theorem, could become a substantially more compelling submission. The calibrated singular-statistics work should either be connected by a genuinely common theorem or developed as a separate paper.
