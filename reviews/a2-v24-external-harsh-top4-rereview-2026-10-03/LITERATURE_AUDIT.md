# Focused literature audit for the external A2 v24 rereview

**Date checked:** 3 October 2026  
**Scope:** theorem/input comparison using primary papers and the manuscript's cited sources. This is not an exhaustive priority search.

## 1. Closest observation categories

### Noakes–Stoyanov: planar obstacle lens/travel-time rigidity

L. Noakes and L. Stoyanov, *Lens Rigidity in Scattering by Unions of Strictly Convex Bodies in \(\mathbb R^2\)*, arXiv:1803.02542.

Their Theorem 1.1 states uniqueness of a finite union of strictly convex planar obstacles from almost-everywhere travelling times or scattering length spectra. The data are global exterior-scattering data on a surrounding observation surface, not finitely many selected periodic return branches, local probes, or absolute periodic-cell normalization.

This source confirms that travel-time differentiation and obstacle recovery belong to an established inverse-scattering framework. It does not obviously supply the v24 finite local fingerprint, periodic network completion defect, or reference-free period-lattice recovery.

### Gurfinkel–Noakes–Stoyanov: travelling times in curved-space obstacle scattering

T. Gurfinkel, L. Noakes and L. Stoyanov, *Travelling Times in Scattering by Obstacles in Curved Space*, J. Differential Equations 269 (2020), 9508–9530; arXiv:2003.12261.

The paper studies exterior travelling-time data, flow comparison and obstacle recovery under its geometric hypotheses. Its observation set differs from the selected, locally marked three-impact laws used in A2 v24.

### Noakes–Stoyanov: travelling times in scattering by obstacles

L. Noakes and L. Stoyanov, *Travelling times in scattering by obstacles*, arXiv:1404.4147.

This work is relevant to endpoint-direction identities and constructive obstacle recovery from exterior travelling-time information. A2 v24 correctly avoids claiming that action differentiation is a new abstract lens principle.

## 2. Boundary and lens rigidity

### Stefanov–Uhlmann–Vasy

P. Stefanov, G. Uhlmann and A. Vasy, *Local and global boundary rigidity and the geodesic X-ray transform in the normal gauge*, Ann. of Math. 194 (2021), 1–95; arXiv:1702.03638.

The unknown is a Riemannian metric and the data are local boundary distances or lens data under convexity/foliation hypotheses. The gauge and geometric setting differ from periodic Euclidean obstacles, but the paper is a close conceptual comparator for reconstruction from endpoint action information.

### Cekić–Guillarmou–Lefeuvre

M. Cekić, C. Guillarmou and T. Lefeuvre, *Local lens rigidity for manifolds of Anosov type*, arXiv:2204.02476.

This work proves local lens rigidity for negatively curved/Anosov-type manifolds with strictly convex boundary under its stated conditions. It reinforces that local lens information is a mature inverse-geometric data category. It does not address A2 v24's local branch recognition or periodic completion problem.

## 3. Dispersing-billiard marked-length rigidity

### Osterman

O. V. Osterman, *On Length Spectrum Rigidity of Dispersing Billiard Systems*, arXiv:2208.12244.

The paper studies marked length spectrum rigidity for three analytic scatterers under a non-eclipse hypothesis and relates equality of marked spectra to analytic conjugacy near a homoclinic orbit. Its data are dynamical marked lengths rather than local endpoint-density functions and certified geometric probes.

The broader marked-length papers retained in the A2 source likewise concern a more conventional dynamical observation category. A2 v24 does not prove equivalence with that category.

## 4. Analytic continuation

L. N. Trefethen, *Quantifying the ill-conditioning of analytic continuation*, BIT Numer. Math. 60 (2020), 901–915; arXiv:1908.11097.

A2 v24 uses a two-constants/harmonic-measure continuation estimate. This is a classical conditional mechanism whose exponent and constants depend strongly on the analytic prior. The manuscript correctly does not claim a universal or optimal continuation rate.

## 5. Assessment of the new finite fingerprint

I found no primary source in this focused search that states the exact v24 lemma: one finite contact order uniformly separates all bounded oriented bridge translation classes coexisting in every table of a compact analytic, asymmetric, shape-separated periodic class.

The proof mechanism is nevertheless elementary at a conceptual level:

1. equality of all contact coefficients gives equality of analytic arcs;
2. analytic continuation identifies complete pair images;
3. shape separation and asymmetry reduce pair congruence to translation;
4. compactness upgrades pointwise distinction to a uniform finite order and positive gap.

This is a useful lemma tailored to the acquisition problem. The focused search does not establish priority, and the absence of an obvious duplicate is not evidence by itself of top-four significance.

## 6. What appears genuinely new in the package

Subject to full proof verification, the distinctive combination is:

- finite local recognition of translated periodic bridge classes without a complete-body catalogue;
- robust coherent-sign registration;
- derivation of a discovery hazard from protected Liouville return volume;
- propagation of contact-coordinate error through endpoint-density differentiation;
- coupling to the missing-area/period-index completion defect and reference-free rational lattice recovery.

The closest inverse-geometry sources do not obviously combine these ingredients.

## 7. Editorial relevance

The same comparison limits the novelty claim. The local action inverse, endpoint-direction/curvature extraction, analytic continuation, coupon bound, error spending, histogram interpolation and integer normal forms are established mechanisms. The new result is their careful integration under a strong local-probe and compact-prior contract.

I therefore found no literature reason to call the v24 theorem false or clearly subsumed. I also found no basis in this focused search for treating it as a transformation of the standard marked-length, travelling-time, or passive billiard inverse problems. This supports the referee report's specialist-journal rather than top-four assessment.

## 8. Search limitation

This audit is deliberately non-exhaustive. It does not certify priority, inspect every paper in the retained bibliography, or search all active 2026 preprints. Any submission should undergo an independent human literature review.
