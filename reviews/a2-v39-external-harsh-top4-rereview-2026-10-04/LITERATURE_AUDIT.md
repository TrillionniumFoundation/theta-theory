# Focused literature audit for the external A2 v39 rereview

Checked on 4 October 2026. This is a focused comparison of mechanisms and information categories, not an exhaustive priority search.

## 1. Planar angular inversion

A. K. Louis, M. Riplinger, M. Spiess, and E. Spodarev, *Inversion algorithms for the spherical Radon and cosine transform*, **Inverse Problems** 27 (2011), 035015.

The planar sine/cosine transform admits a local second-order differential inverse in the even angular setting. The identity

`(partial_theta^2 + 1)(-cos theta)_+ = delta_{pi/2} + delta_{-pi/2}`

is therefore a classical transform mechanism, not itself the novelty. It leaves an antipodal sum. The v39 collision-specific step is to retain the nominal-position variable, separate the resulting spatial density copies, normalize their masses, and use their centers to recover the contacts and unknown launch density.

## 2. Directional variation and covariograms

B. Galerne, *Computation of the perimeter of measurable sets via their covariogram. Applications to random sets*, **Image Analysis and Stereology** 30 (2011), 39--51.

Directional derivatives of covariograms and directional variation are established antecedents for extracting boundary information and perimeter from infinitesimal translations, including random-set formulations. The present sensor differs because it records first-collision bits at controlled nominal centers under one hidden stationary launch displacement. The manuscript proves the entering-strip disintegration and retains spatial location rather than reducing to a single covariogram scalar.

## 3. First variations of dilations

M. Kiderlen and J. Rataj, *Dilation volumes of sets of finite perimeter*, **Advances in Applied Probability** 50 (2018), 1095--1118.

One-sided first variations of dilation volume by finite structuring sets, including two-point directional cases, are established geometric-measure inputs. They support interpretation of the short-flight limit as boundary variation. They do not alone establish joint recovery of an unknown probability density and a separated obstacle configuration from the full nominal-position-dependent collision germ.

## 4. Unknown probes and mathematical morphology

J. S. Villarrubia, *Algorithms for scanned probe microscope image simulation, surface reconstruction, and tip estimation*, **Journal of Research of the National Institute of Standards and Technology** 102 (1997), 425--454.

Blind probe reconstruction in scanned-probe microscopy is a close information-category comparison. Morphological dilation, erosion, consistent-probe envelopes, and ambiguity under an unknown structuring body are well established. The v39 exact theorem is stronger in one direction: after angular differentiation it claims to recover not only the convex support of the unknown probe but its complete probability density up to translation. That conclusion comes from positive translated density copies and their masses, not from support morphology alone.

## 5. Convex support and normal coordinates

R. Schneider, *Convex Bodies: The Brunn--Minkowski Theory*, second expanded edition, Cambridge University Press, 2014.

Support addition, Steiner centering, Gauss parametrization, curvature radius, parallel bodies, mixed area, and Cauchy's perimeter formula are classical infrastructure. The main exact novelty should be framed as collision-specific joint factorization of the directional spatial response, not novelty of support cancellation or the angular differential operator.

## 6. Finite statistical context

The retained finite programme has established antecedents in active boundary estimation, informative rare events, finite query precision, convex-support estimation, stopped Poisson inversion, and information-theoretic packing. The v39 finite theorem concerns a different nuisance problem from the retained known-disk minimax theorem: it jointly estimates geometry under one unknown launch law and gives a sufficient, explicitly nonsharp exponent.

The new exponent is

`Q_gamma = ((3 gamma + 27/2) s)/(s-2)`.

No matching lower bound is supplied. At `gamma=0` and `s=7`, the exponent is 18.9. It reflects finite angular differentiation, spatial quadrature, boundary mass, and very short commands; it is not a universal complexity law for the exact germ inverse.

## 7. Editorial comparison

The single-law theorem is cleaner than the earlier multiple-setting calibration results. It removes homothety apparatus from the principal inverse and gives a complete common-translation fiber. Its strongest datum, however, remains a full active continuum response: all nominal centers, all direction labels, and a short-length germ.

The focused search did not identify a cited predecessor containing the complete chain of one-sided collision germ, spatial density-copy separation, joint density/table reconstruction, and full response completion. This observation is not a priority determination. At the requested top-four benchmark, the relevant question is whether the active-field rigidity changes the broader understanding of billiard rigidity or inverse dynamics. The accompanying report answers negatively while crediting the theorem as serious specialist-journal mathematics.
