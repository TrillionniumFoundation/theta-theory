## 1. Executive assessment

V29 is not merely v26 with another theorem number. The occupation-flow relaxation, its exactness for the specified local path data, the seedwise pre-acquisition refinement, and the corresponding dual are genuine mathematical additions. The intervening v27 and v28 developments also address actual weaknesses in the earlier significance case: there is now a complete same-model numerical evaluation, a comparison for resolution-blind acquisition laws rather than deterministic orders alone, and a nonvacuous graph-growth theorem for a precisely enlarged prediction task.

I do not find a demonstrated fatal counterexample to the eight new v29 statements under their printed hypotheses. Their essential arguments survive the examination below. The same is true of the new v28 localization and growing-graph arguments and the examined principal scalar acquisition/confluence/covering/causal route. This is a positive correctness assessment within the scope of this review, not an assertion that every assertion in every companion has been certified.

There is one concrete inherited mathematical misstatement requiring correction: the optimized finite-menu excess cannot be infinite merely because the accuracy set approaches zero when both the graph and calibration are fixed. Section 4 gives a proof and an exact repair. This error is in explanatory text, not a counterexample to the principal menu comparison or its collision-path corollaries.

My negative recommendation rests principally on significance and architecture of the contribution, not on manufacturing a false theorem. The new exactness is an exactness result for a deliberately relaxed class of path laws and losses, not an exact characterization of general finite-state statistical prediction. The strongest fully evaluated statistical application of the new flow machinery is still a one-checkpoint, one-dimensional experiment, where the improvement can be obtained directly by fixing the initial choice and applying a scalar small-ball bound. The growing-graph theorem has meaningful content, but for an augmented local-and-tensor loss under strong balanced-cut and independence hypotheses. These are valuable distinctions and legitimate results. The submission has not yet made a convincing case that their conjunction is an advance of the exceptional reach sought at the requested venues.

A correction of the concrete error, another large diagnostic count, or another improvement relative to the author's own earlier certificate would not by itself reverse that recommendation.

## 3. The inherited scalar and fixed-graph foundation

### 3.1 Acquisition, not merely dimension of a test space

The principal route deserves more credit than the phrase “finite-dimensional quantization” would suggest. `core/02_experiments.tex` identifies the exact future test space from executable report factors, retaining the requirement that the future query is selected only after compression. `core/03_transversality.tex` constructs an attainable patch with a section to histories and establishes the relevant rank through positive mixed exponential pairings. This is not an inference that every direction of an ambient moment space is automatically acquired.

In `text/collision_flags.tex`, the complete confluent flag is important: one cannot replace it by arbitrary isolated derivative coordinates. The normalized Leja construction separates scales from bounded coordinate changes, keeps formal labels through collisions, and omits unavailable positive-volume indices instead of dividing by zero. The determinant comparison between the prefix product and the maximal Vandermonde product is used with its finite factorial cost. The acquired lower measure comes from a positive-volume command region after integrating kernel coordinates, not from freezing those coordinates on a null slice.

The positivity argument permits a fixed full-support prior without assuming that the prior itself has a density. What subsequently has the needed local density is an acquired statistic under continuously distributed commands. Those are different measures. I found no step in the examined route that illicitly substitutes the latent prior for that acquired law.

### 3.2 Global covering and a common causal filter

`text/analytic_inputs.tex` supplies a bounded-format global covering input; `text/collision_direct.tex` applies it to the whole attainable image, not only the lower-bound chart. The semialgebraic complexity is in the command variables at fixed calibration, with real moment coefficients allowed. It does not require the prior moment functions themselves to be algebraic in the calibration.

The causal construction uses reachable representatives and raw moment updates whose denominators are positive evidence probabilities. The examined update formulas do not introduce reciprocal collision gaps. The finite horizon permits a bounded accumulation of approximation error. The lower bounds then use the same common exploration law and the infimum over one filter outside the checkpoint maximum. Thus a family of unrelated checkpoint encoders has not been silently substituted for a common filter.

These statements are uniform over the specified compact calibration chamber for a fixed experiment, horizon, and prior. They are not uniform over all full-support priors, all horizons, or unknown calibrations. The operational definition counts persistent labels and permits a fixed real-valued program and temporary arithmetic; it is not a total workspace or computational complexity theorem.

### 3.3 Adaptive graph acquisition

The v25 fixed-graph lower bound retains unconditional product-tape measures before restricting to a selected order. Its recovery constants may depend on the fixed graph. The v26 separator reformulation counts visited sets rather than unnecessarily distinguishing entire traces when the decoder only sees the visited set. The proof explicitly pays the conversion from an expected maximum to a maximum of expectations. The max-flow interpretation is classical and is not misrepresented as a new network-flow theorem.

The fixed-graph bit law and analytic order phases follow from the independently capped edge indices and finite cut/order optimization. The actual three-edge, 24-trial star is consistent with the displayed four phases and with the distinction between the full interval and its two endpoints. None of these observations establishes a graph-size-uniform bound for the original tensor task; the manuscript now appropriately keeps that issue separate.

## 4. E29.1: a false fixed-calibration infinity statement

**Location:** `v27/policy_menus.tex`, immediately after `eq:v27-menu-functional`, around source lines 50–55. The passage is still present in the v29 copy, blob `05c5f77e24b284d969f5f0d16181517a852e2d28`:

> “The expression is nonnegative, and may be infinite when the accuracy set approaches zero.”

For the printed optimized functional, this is false. At each fixed finite graph and fixed calibration, for every integer K at least one and every nonempty accuracy set contained in (0,1], one has

$$
0\le r_K(\mathcal E,a)\le r_1((0,1],a)<\infty.
$$

Here is a proof, which also specifies the precise repair. Put x = log₂(1/ε). After omitting zero determinant volumes, each edge cost is a maximum of finitely many affine functions,

$$
b_e(x,a)=\max_{\ell}\{(\ell/2)x+c_{e,\ell}\},
\qquad c_{e,\ell}=\log_2 V_{e,\ell}(a),
$$

including the zero branch. At fixed calibration all retained intercepts are finite. If d_e is the largest retained index, then for all sufficiently large x the edge cost is exactly (d_e/2)x + c_{e,d_e}. One can choose the threshold by taking the maximum of zero and the finitely many pairwise crossing values with lower-slope branches.

Every order cost is a maximum of finitely many cut sums. It is therefore eventually affine. There are only finitely many vertex orders, so one order π* realizes the minimum of their eventual affine functions for every x beyond a common finite threshold X. In particular,

$$
W_{\pi_*}(x,a)=W_G(x,a)\qquad(x\ge X).
$$

On the compact interval [0,X], the difference is continuous and bounded. The singleton menu consisting of π* consequently has finite excess on the entire half-line. Allowing up to K orders cannot increase the optimized excess. The empty graph is trivial, and exact collisions cause no difficulty because their zero-volume branches have already been omitted.

A *prescribed suboptimal menu* can have infinite excess. A bound *uniform over calibrations approaching a collision* can also diverge. Neither is the same assertion as infinity of the optimized functional at one fixed calibration. The contact-order examples vary the calibration with θ, and their divergence remains compatible with the finite result just proved: X and the bounded excess can deteriorate as θ tends to zero.

**Required repair:** replace the quoted sentence by the fixed-calibration finiteness statement and distinguish it explicitly from calibration-uniform divergence. The infinity contingency in `cor:v27-menu-regret` is then vacuous for the specified functional. It should be removed or expressly attached to a different, enlarged parameter domain. The introductory sentence in `v25/graph_consequences.tex` about absence of a single bounded-excess order should likewise say “uniformly over the stated collision calibrations”; its formal star corollary already has the correct quantifiers.

**Severity:** a genuine, nonfatal mathematical exposition error. It does not refute the menu comparison, the heavy-trace argument, or the analytic collision-path law. I would not characterize it as a collapse of the central theorem.

The resolution-blind policy theorem itself survives the audit: its acquisition law and seed law are fixed across resolutions, so the positive-mass trace can be selected independently of the budget. Predictors may be reoptimized. A scheduler that receives budget-dependent encoder feedback would be a different class; the definition excludes that enlargement.

## 5. V28 localization and growing graphs

### 5.1 Selected-edge measures and a compulsory level

In `v28/localization.tex`, the robust event requires a usable local witness for every candidate visited set at one compulsory level. It does not require every edge to be regular. For each fixed witness, unselected edges and completion reports are integrated out before the adaptive visited-set restriction is imposed. The product density bound is consequently an unconditional subprobability estimate. Conditioning on the selected witness and then asserting independence would be incorrect, but that is not the printed argument.

The decoder has at most M times the number of admitted finite descriptor values prediction centres at a given visited set. The recovery map is applied to arbitrary centres, not only to points in the attainable image. The union bound therefore legitimately gives the displayed sum of density, determinant, recovery-norm, and centre-count factors. Layer-cake integration yields the power-tail expression with exponent 1 + 2/k on the retained mass. No extra factor for the number of checkpoints is needed when this converse is proved at one compulsory checkpoint.

### 5.2 The dimension-dependent constant really matters

For r selected edges and an l-coordinate allocation on each, k = rl. In the mixed query norm, the recovery operator costs at most the square root of 2P times the scalar norm. The volume of a k-dimensional Euclidean ball supplies a compensating factor at most (2πe/k)^(k/2).

At a target risk ε, Markov's inequality is applied at 4ε on an event of mass at least 3/4. Combining these ingredients gives the factor

$$
\left(16\pi e L_\ell^2 P/k\right)^{k/2},
$$

not the superficially similar factor with 8. The factor 16 is the product of the norm-square factor 2, the Markov factor 4, and the Gaussian-volume factor 2. The dimension normalization is precisely what prevents an uncontrolled P log P loss when r is proportional to the number of vertices. The submitted expression has the correct factor.

The witness count is at most 2^(v+P), and the determinant-prefix comparison costs at most a fixed factorial per selected edge. Under P at most dv and r bounded below by a fixed multiple of v, these losses are O(v). Maximization over positive l then gives the lower bit law. Zero determinant terms remain omitted, including at exact collisions.

### 5.3 Upper construction and nonempty graph families

In `v28/readout.tex`, an edge is quantized when first acquired and its index is retained unchanged until the second endpoint. It is not repeatedly re-quantized as unrelated vertices arrive. This removes the otherwise serious accumulation issue. The tensor part is not discarded: its product-error bound includes the damping factor q*^(2(f−1)), so the supremum of f²q*^(2(f−1)) is finite. The local half and tensor half can therefore both be controlled by one scalar resolution choice.

The balanced-cut event estimate uses independent edge indicators before selection and a union bound over candidate cuts. The strong inequality β₀h/8 > log 2 is substantive. The bipartite random multigraph construction establishes nonemptiness for sufficiently large fixed degree parameters. It proves linear edge count and bounded average degree; it should not be advertised as a bounded-maximum-degree construction without a separate argument. Parallel edges are labelled independent experiments, not silently collapsed.

These arguments support the stated size-uniform comparison and the joint graph-growth/collision consequence. They do not provide an exact limiting coefficient, a result for every sparse graph, or a bound for memory after each individual raw trial within an atomic vertex batch.

### 5.4 Retaining a task is not preserving its metric uniformly

The augmented menu retains the original tensor queries, but this does not make the two prediction losses uniformly equivalent as graph size increases. Here is an explicit diagnostic calculation. At a cut with f active edges, change only one reachable scalar posterior and hold the others fixed. Under the product query distribution,

$$
\|\Delta q_{\rm ten}\|^2
=\|\Delta q_e\|^2\prod_{h\ne e}\|q_h\|^2
\le q_*^{2(f-1)}\|\Delta q_e\|^2.
$$

The local half of the augmented loss contributes at least the same scalar squared difference divided by 2P. Thus the mixed-to-tensor squared-distance ratio is at least q*^(−2(f−1))/(2P), which can grow exponentially along linear-size active cuts. Equality of exact test spaces is not a uniform equivalence of their weighted norms.

The manuscript's current scope statements are correct on this point. It would be wrong to transfer the mixed-task lower bound to the original tensor-only task. V29's original-task flow certificate does not effect that transfer either; its graph dependence remains in the input capacities.

## 6. The new occupation-measure theorem and exactness

**Sources:** `v29/occupation.tex`, `thm:v29-occupation`, `prop:v29-separator-dominance`, and `thm:v29-relaxation-exact`.

Write x_w = Pr(w is visited | A), with Pr(A) = β, and assume 0 ≤ L_j ≤ 1. The local information is

$$
\Pr(A,w\in P,L_{j(w)}\le t)\le\beta c_w(t).
$$

The terminal condition x_w ≤ c_w(1) is necessary. It is not decorative: without it, an alleged unit-flow model can violate the bounded range of the losses. The actual conditional arc incidences give one feasible flow satisfying these caps.

For G_w(t) equal to the local subdistribution divided by β, both G_w(t) ≤ x_w and G_w(t) ≤ c_w(t) hold. Hence layer cake gives

$$
\mathbb E[1_A1_{\{w\in P\}}L_{j(w)}]
\ge\beta\int_0^1[x_w-c_w(t)]_+\,dt.
$$

Exactly one vertex is visited at each level. Summing at a fixed level, then taking the maximum, and finally minimizing over feasible occupations proves the claimed bound βΓ(c). The same flow is retained through every threshold and level. This is stronger information than choosing unrelated local minimizers, and no maximum is moved through an expectation.

The comparison with the separator integral also has the correct direction. A unit flow meets every separator with total occupation at least one, even when a path can meet that separator more than once. Summing the positive deficits over all vertices, integrating, and dividing by the level count gives the old comparator. Feasibility at t = 1 implies that the separator capacity is at least one there, justifying extension of the integral to infinity for monotone extensions of the capacities.

For c(t) = min(1,at^(k/2)), the power cost is k/(k+2) times a^(−2/k)x^(1+2/k) on the feasible interval. For an envelope of several capacities, the manuscript correctly retains the integral of the envelope instead of exchanging an integral and a maximum.

The exactness theorem has a valid construction. Decompose a minimizing flow into a path distribution. At each positively occupied vertex sample a uniform variable on [0,x_w] and apply the generalized inverse of c_w. Monotonicity and right continuity give the required clipped distribution function, including jumps and mass at zero. The terminal caps ensure that the inverse exists almost surely. Set losses to zero outside A. The resulting abstract model attains βΓ(c) simultaneously at every level.

The last qualifier is essential: this constructs losses and a path law, not a common finite-label prediction decoder with prescribed posterior geometry. The manuscript explicitly makes that distinction. I accept the abstract sharpness claim on that reading; I do not read it as a solution of the general statistical minimax problem.

## 7. Pre-acquisition information and the dual

**Source:** `v29/initial_information.tex`, `thm:v29-pre`, `thm:v29-dual`, and `cor:v29-graph`.

The first visited vertex is fixed after conditioning on a tape-independent seed, because it is chosen before any command or report is observed. The required local capacity inequalities hold seed by seed with common constants. Applying the occupation bound separately at each fixed checkpoint, averaging seeds within their first-vertex classes, and using conditional Jensen yields the anchored-flow mixture objective Γ_pre. Only after this averaging is the checkpoint maximum taken. This is the correct order of operations.

The perspective formulation is well defined on its feasible domain. At zero anchor mass, the corresponding flow is zero and the perspective tends continuously to zero because the unscaled costs are bounded. Its constraints are linear and its costs convex. Compactness gives an attained minimum. Jensen on the mixture of anchored flows proves Γ_pre ≥ Γ. An unanchored feasible flow need not admit a feasible initial anchor; the printed theorem avoids that problem by assuming an actual seedwise pre-acquisition model and discarding empty anchored polytopes.

The supporting representation uses slopes between zero and one, consistent with the monotonicity and Lipschitz bound of the integrated deficits. For fixed checkpoint weights, compact convex minimax gives the anchored dual. The outer maximization over checkpoint weights follows from separation of the upward-closed convex set of attainable upper risk vectors. The final exchange involving the minimum over anchors is legitimate here because the dual variables for the finitely many anchors can be chosen independently and simultaneously; it would not be a general min-max identity for coupled variables.

The zero-weight convention is continuous. Node terminal capacities must remain in the inner minimum-cost flow; only when they are all one is an ordinary shortest-path computation sufficient. The stated dual correctly preserves this distinction.

Most importantly, a feasible dual objective has the lower-bound direction needed for a risk certificate. A feasible but nonoptimal primal flow has the opposite direction and cannot be inserted as a certified risk lower bound. The source explicitly warns against this error and supplies matching dual values in its evaluated application.

The graph specialization respects the information model. Additional finite history descriptors multiply the number of centres by their cardinality. A full order prefix can cost up to |S|! possibilities at a fixed visited set, or be represented on a lifted prefix graph. No discarded continuous observation is supplied for free. The constants remain those of the fixed graph experiment; this is not a graph-uniform duality theorem for arbitrary correlated latent variables.

## 8. Evaluated refinement: what the numerical claims do and do not show

**Source:** `v29/evaluated_refinement.tex`, with the complete acquired-law derivation in `v27/evaluated_model.tex`.

The query vector is q(z) = (3/4, 1/2 + z/8, 1/2 − z/8, 1/4). Its query-averaged squared distance is (z−z′)²/128. The affine recovery norm therefore has square 128. The first-failure submeasure has mass 1/2 and density bounded by 26. These are evidence-weighted acquired-law quantities; the two accepted-report atoms remain part of the full law.

With a_M = 2LMH/β, the two-choice, one-level unanchored relaxation is minimized at occupations (1/2,1/2) and equals 1/(12a_M²). Fixing the initial choice before observing the tape forces occupations (1,0) or (0,1). The anchored mixture value is 1/(3a_M²), exactly four times larger. The supplied support points give matching dual values, so this is not a numerical primal guess.

Consequently the scalar density bound is β³/(12L²H²) times M^(−2). Substituting β = 1/2, H = 26, and L² = 128 gives exactly

$$
\mathcal R^{\rm av}_{G,M}\ge\frac{M^{-2}}{8306688}
\qquad(M\ge1).
$$

Requiring a completion failure multiplies the entire first-block submeasure, including its density, by one half because its command-averaged likelihood is one half at every latent value. The coefficient becomes 1/16613376. This comparison retains all density and recovery costs and is not a comparison of event masses alone.

Keeping the older restricted command event gives coefficient 1071875/3113159280132096, exactly four times its separator predecessor. Passing additionally to the entire failure law gives the total new-to-old ratio 1499109768/1071875, which exceeds 1398. Both improvements are real. The large ratio is not a general adaptivity gap, not an improvement factor for an algorithm, and not evidence of a factor-1398 change in the underlying minimax law.

The retained exact average-risk reduction is D_M(ν)/128. The self-contained one-dimensional high-resolution proof handles the continuous density and finitely many atoms separately and yields

$$
\kappa_{\rm ex}=\frac1{1536}\left(\int_{10/21}^{14/27}f(z)^{1/3}\,dz\right)^3.
$$

Independent rational endpoint sums give an enclosure lying between 4.8846179695 × 10^(−7) and 4.8870869287 × 10^(−7), with those displayed endpoints rounded outward. Multiplication by 8306688 puts the asymptotic sharp-to-new-certificate ratio between 4.05749 and 4.05956. Thus the manuscript's broader interval (4.05,4.06) is supported. This is an asymptotic coefficient comparison, not a bound on the finite-M ratio for all budgets and not an equality with maximum-tape risk.

## 9. An independent multilevel diagnostic of the new mechanism

The accompanying implementation includes a two-level abstract example not used as a substitute for a statistical realization. There are two disjoint paths, (u,a) and (v,b). Assign square-root capacities with squared scale parameters (1,4,4,1) to (u,v,a,b). All terminal capacities are one, and the integrated costs are x³ divided by three times the respective squared scale.

A common flow has occupations (x,1−x,x,1−x). The maximum of its two level costs is minimized at x = 1/2 and equals 5/96. Optimizing the levels separately instead gives 1/27, strictly smaller. Thus retaining a common occupation law makes a concrete difference, beyond the elementary chain example.

A dual with checkpoint weights (1/2,1/2) and support point 1/2 at all four nodes gives minimum path cost 5/32 and conjugate sum 5/48. Their difference is 5/96, matching the primal exactly. With the pre-acquisition anchor constraint, the optimal mixture gives 5/24; support one on the forced path and zero elsewhere gives path cost 5/8 and conjugate sum 5/12, again matching exactly.

These equalities verify the common-flow and perspective mechanisms on a genuinely multilevel finite example. They also expose why independently optimizing each checkpoint, or treating a feasible primal objective as a lower certificate, is invalid. The example remains an abstract local-capacity model. It does not close the missing statistical-realization question discussed next.

## 10. Why the requested venue recommendation is still negative

### 10.1 The advance since v26 must be acknowledged

The old objection that there is no evaluated same-model certificate is no longer applicable. The fixed-order-only restriction has been genuinely strengthened by the v27 resolution-blind policy and menu results. The v28 graph-growth theorem is not vacuous, and its dependence on the augmented task is stated. V29 goes beyond a fresh separator union bound: one occupation law is kept across all thresholds, and its strongest abstract consequence is characterized.

I therefore do not simply carry forward the v26 conclusion without reassessing its premises. Nor do I allege that a cited classical source already proves the entire submitted conjunction. Establishing that priority claim would require evidence not provided by a generic reference to quantization, convex duality, or graph width.

### 10.2 Exactness of a relaxation is not the sought statistical structural theory

Once the local distribution bounds are specified, the exactness construction can assign independent generalized-inverse losses at the visited vertices. It need not satisfy the compatibility constraints imposed by one decoder, a posterior map, or a causal finite-state transition mechanism. The pre-acquisition refinement records the first nonanticipativity constraint, but it is not an exact encoding of all later information constraints.

This is not a flaw in the theorem as stated. It is the boundary of its significance. A broad structural classification of statistical memory would need either a realizability result, a controlled relaxation gap on a substantial class, or consequences for actual multilevel experiments that cannot be recovered by the already available scalar and finite-selection estimates. The present submission does not establish such a result merely by giving an exact abstract dual.

The value of elementary arguments should not be dismissed. Here, however, layer cake, finite flow decomposition, generalized inverses, perspectives, and compact convex minimax account for the new proof once the capacities are available. The question for these venues is the reach of the consequence. That reach remains insufficiently demonstrated.

### 10.3 The strongest statistical showcase does not need a network

In the actual two-vertex application there is only one checkpoint. The initial vertex is selected before data, so fixing an independent seed removes the spurious two-way choice. The resulting bound follows directly from the density of one scalar acquired mean and M reconstruction centres. The flow dual organizes this calculation correctly, but the network coupling is not doing substantive multilevel statistical work in that example.

The factor exceeding 1398 is also measured against a particularly wasteful earlier restricted-event certificate, not against an external optimum or a previously sharp general theorem. The manuscript responsibly compares against the actual sharp scalar coefficient as well, which is more informative. Even so, improving a weak certificate for the same one-dimensional example does not by itself establish the exceptional importance of the general framework.

A carefully evaluated multilevel statistical family in which occupation consistency or the dual produces a qualitatively new memory conclusion would be materially stronger evidence. This is an explanation of the current significance deficit, not a declaration that such a theorem is necessary for the correctness of the present statements, nor a mechanical promise of acceptance after one additional result.

### 10.4 The graph-growth result is meaningful but narrower than the headline ambition

The graph-uniform result uses independent identical edge experiments, strong retained balanced cuts, a selected compulsory boundary, atomic vertex batches, and a loss giving constant total weight to local edge readouts. These hypotheses are neither concealed nor inconsistent. They explain why a positive density of independently acquired scalar directions forces a number of bits proportional to graph size times the scalar bit profile.

This is a useful extensive-memory law for that model. It is not yet a graph-uniform theory of the original tensor task, of general adaptive observation systems, or of arbitrary correlated experiments. The report should not demand those results as repairs of a false theorem; equally, the manuscript should not treat honest delimitation of its hypotheses as evidence that it already has those broader consequences.

### 10.5 The principal article still reads as an accumulation of revisions

The active entry point inputs an original introduction followed by introductions for v25, v26, v27, v28, and v29. The abstract moves from collision geometry to graph width, policy menus, local readouts, occupation duality, and a large numerical certificate ratio. The source organization preserves the research history, but preservation and a persuasive journal article are different objectives.

The article needs one hierarchy of mathematical questions and one explanation of which result changes the subject. In particular, a large improvement relative to an older bound should not function as a proxy for the importance of the main theorem. The existing separation between principal material and companions is worth retaining; the repair is not arbitrary deletion of valid mathematics. It is a coherent presentation of the contribution and its logical dependencies, with historical response material remaining available outside the principal narrative.

My editorial judgment is that the current combination is substantial specialist work but does not yet justify publication at the four-journal level requested. Another referee may assign more weight to the conjunction of collision-uniform acquisition, all-budget causality, and the new graph refinements. That evaluative uncertainty does not turn this negative recommendation into an acceptance prediction.
