# Focused literature and novelty audit for A2 v33

## Scope

This is a focused theorem-level comparison for the v33 additions. It is not an exhaustive priority search. The main report does not infer novelty from the absence of an immediately matching title or keyword.

## Active smooth-boundary learning

The retained comparison with R. M. Castro and R. D. Nowak, *Minimax Bounds for Active Learning* (IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 54 (2008), 2339–2353), remains relevant. Their model queries feature locations and receives binary class labels, with loss expressed through classification risk under boundary-fragment and noise assumptions.

A. Locatelli, A. Carpentier, and S. Kpotufe, *An Adaptive Strategy for Active Learning with Smooth Decision Boundary* (PMLR 83 (2018), 547–571), is the closest algorithmic comparison for adaptive line search and smooth interpolation. Their procedure operates in a membership-query classification model and adapts to unknown smoothness/noise parameters. A2 v33 assumes known smoothness, targets laboratory `C^2` geometry, and must first synthesize an approximate membership response from reciprocal pooled collision bits.

The adaptive boundary search and polynomial interpolation should therefore not be presented as new abstract learning mechanisms. The manuscript correctly locates its contribution in the physical reduction and resource coupling.

## Query learning and stopped binary transcripts

General membership-query learning studies adaptive binary transcripts and query complexity. For example, N. H. Bshouty and C. A. Haddad-Zaknoon, *Adaptive Exact Learning of Decision Trees from Membership Queries* (PMLR 98 (2019), 207–234), treats adaptive exact learning in a discrete concept class. Region-based active-learning work similarly studies adaptively chosen binary labels.

These papers do not contain the v33 physical collision observation, centered convex-body packing, or calibration coupling. They do reinforce the point that adaptive binary-tree counting is an established information mechanism rather than a new general principle.

## Prefix coding and expected stopping

C. E. Shannon, *A Mathematical Theory of Communication* (Bell System Technical Journal 27 (1948), 379–423 and 623–656), is an appropriate source for prefix coding and entropy. The v33 argument is essentially self-contained: conditional on the controller seed, terminal words are prefix-free; Kraft/log-sum bounds entropy by expected length; a decoding entropy inequality supplies the lower bound.

The novelty is not the abstract entropy inequality. It is its careful application to a physical smooth-body packing under an admissible stopped collision protocol.

## Approximate membership and boundary layers

Computational-geometry work on approximate polytope membership permits an indeterminate region near a boundary and studies data-structure size/query time after the body is given for preprocessing. This is structurally different from reconstructing an unknown periodic body through physical collision commands. It is useful only as a conceptual comparison for boundary-layer labels, not as a competing inverse theorem.

## Calibration uncertainty

The v33 calibration converse is highly model-specific. It permits the actual-start perturbation law to depend on the unknown table, nominal command, and nominal draw, subject only to a deterministic displacement bound; the actual start is not observed. Under those quantifiers, the two-implementation common-response coupling is a valid minimax indistinguishability construction.

The theorem should not be conflated with lower bounds for:

- one common fixed offset;
- table-independent jitter;
- a known mean-zero or parametric noise law;
- observed actual starts;
- repeated metrological calibration;
- arbitrarily long collision commands.

A focused primary-source search did not locate a theorem with the exact combination of convex swept-set collision bits, two table-dependent bounded-error implementations, complete adaptive transcript equality, and the exponent `s/(s-2)` obtained from a physical support bump. This is not an exhaustive priority claim.

## Novelty boundary

The credible v33 contribution is the integrated resource theorem inside the declared active sensor:

1. collision bits are reduced to local occupation queries by reciprocal cancellation and a killed Bellman inverse;
2. adaptive smooth-boundary recovery gives the upper exponent;
3. a physical centered packing gives deterministic and expected-bit lower exponents;
4. a physical common-response construction gives the matching worst-case position-resolution exponent;
5. finite period relations are recovered under a bounded periodic prior and known patch margin.

The work does not establish a new universal source-coding theorem, active-learning principle, convex-geometry approximation theorem, or passive billiard rigidity theorem. Its significance depends on whether the particular collision sensor and worst-case calibration class are judged mathematically natural enough for the intended venue.
