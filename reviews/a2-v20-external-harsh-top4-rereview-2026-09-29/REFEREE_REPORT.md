## 1. Recommendation

**Recommendation at the requested four-journal benchmark: reject.**

Version 20 is a genuine and mathematically substantive revision. It closes the most concrete global defect left in v19: uniqueness no longer depends on a hand-designed covering network containing a primitive pair of cycle displacements. The new obstruction-descent lemma shows that every sufficiently short obstructed closest-pair bridge can be replaced by a chain of strictly shorter clear bridges. A covering-radius argument then proves that the complete bounded-range clear-bridge graph is connected. Path lifting in the periodic graph proves that all of its cycles generate the entire period lattice. The new determinantal-gcd theorem recovers the index of the group generated by all observed cycles and gives uniqueness at index one even when no observed pair is a lattice basis. The physical `2/3/5` example demonstrates that this strictly improves the earlier primitive-pair criterion.

On the new v20 core audited in detail, I found no fatal counterexample. The obstruction descent, covering argument, deck-group generation, all-cycle index formula, nonprimitive generating example, and stable integer-locking extension are internally coherent under their stated hypotheses. Independent exact diagnostics accompanying this report support the finite lattice algebra and several periodic visibility-graph models. The exact-source GitHub Actions workflow for the reviewed commit also completed successfully and validated the primary article and the retained submission volumes. I therefore do **not** base the negative recommendation on an identified false headline theorem or on an unclosed build pipeline.

The remaining objection is the level and naturality of the result. The local datum remains two full endpoint-arclength density functions for a selected three-impact branch, with a marked local origin, paired absolute times, and a coherent reversal. The global datum is a **complete catalogue** of every clear shortest normal bridge below a range known to exceed twice a covering-radius bound. Completeness is supplied by the acquisition protocol rather than inferred from a partial observation. Analyticity, asymmetry, and pairwise noncongruence still perform the whole-image identification and incidence synchronization. The new global geometry is elegant, but its proof combines a proximity/visibility connectivity principle, elementary periodic graph lifting, and standard finite-index lattice algebra. In my judgment this does not yet produce the exceptional conceptual advance, natural invariant, or breadth expected at the four journals named above.

I would regard the focused local inverse and the complete-catalogue theorem as potentially serious material for a strong specialist journal, after a fresh human proof review and a sharper comparison with the closest computational-geometry literature. I would not recommend another open-ended revision cycle at the requested top-four benchmark.

## 3. What v20 substantively fixes

### 3.1 Coverage is now obtained from a uniform geometric protocol

The v19 theorem assumed a connected covering selected collection. Version 20 defines the lifted graph whose vertices are all obstacle copies and whose edges are clear shortest normal bridges of gap less than `R`. The new theorem proves that this graph is connected whenever

\[
R>2\rho_*,\qquad
\rho_* = \sup_{x\in\mathbb R^2}\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal O),
\]

where `\mathcal O` is the union of all obstacle copies.

This is a real improvement. Coverage is no longer a bespoke graph-theoretic property of a chosen finite list. It follows from one geometric range condition for the complete catalogue. The theorem correctly distinguishes this from a method for discovering or certifying an unknown list: completeness and a sufficient range bound remain acquisition inputs.

### 3.2 The primitive-pair assumption is removed

The v19 uniqueness theorem used one recovered cycle pair with determinant equal to the covolume. Version 20 instead uses every non-tree cycle displacement. If `D=[d_1\ d_2]` is any independent pair and

\[
n=\frac{|\det D|}{V},\qquad b_j=nD^{-1}d_j\in\mathbb Z^2,
\]

then the integer subgroup generated by the `b_j` has determinantal divisor

\[
g=\gcd_{i<j}|\det(b_i,b_j)|.
\]

The cycle subgroup has calibrated index

\[
q=[\Lambda:\Gamma]
 =\frac gn
 =\gcd_{i<j}\frac{|\det(d_i,d_j)|}{V}.
\]

Thus all observed cycles generate the full lattice exactly when `q=1`, irrespective of whether any pair is a basis. The candidate bound sharpens from `\sigma_1(n)` for an arbitrary pair to `\sigma_1(q)` for the complete cycle group.

The displayed `2/3/5` example is useful and nontrivial: the three cycle vectors have pair determinants `2,3,5`, so no pair is primitive, while their gcd is one and the full group is generated.

### 3.3 The period-generation theorem is exact, not statistical rhetoric

For the complete catalogue, connectedness of the lifted graph gives a path from a root copy to every translate of that copy. Projecting the path to the finite quotient produces a closed walk whose signed edge translations sum to the prescribed period. Hence the cycle translations generate every element of the period lattice. This is the right path-lifting argument and removes the need to guess a distinguished pair.

The theorem does not declare arbitrary noisy real vectors to be an integer group. The finite-data extension compares admissible physical candidates, first locks their bounded integer coordinates, and only then applies a common integer-basis algorithm. This logical order is important and is handled correctly.

## 4. Correctness audit of the geometric completeness theorem

### 4.1 Uniqueness and obstruction descent

For distinct strictly convex copies `C,D`, the shortest connecting segment is unique and normal to both boundaries. Suppose its open segment meets a third body `F` at a point `z`. Then

\[
\ell(C,F)\le |p_{CD}-z|<\ell(C,D),\qquad
\ell(F,D)\le |z-q_{CD}|<\ell(C,D).
\]

The inequalities remain strict when the intersection is a tangency in the open segment. Periodicity implies that below a fixed range there are only finitely many relative pair types and hence finitely many gap lengths. Induction over those lengths replaces every obstructed pair by a clear-edge path with the same endpoints.

This proves that the clear shortest-bridge graph and the unrestricted short-gap graph have the same connected components. I found no infinite-descent gap: periodicity supplies exactly the finiteness needed by the induction.

### 4.2 Covering-radius connectivity

Choose `a` with `\rho_*<a<R/2`. The open `a`-neighborhoods of all obstacle copies cover the plane. Their intersection graph is connected; otherwise the unions associated with two graph components would be disjoint nonempty open sets partitioning the connected plane. Intersecting neighborhoods imply gap less than `2a<R`, so the unrestricted short-gap graph is connected. Obstruction descent then gives connectedness of the clear graph.

This proof is correct and does not use finite horizon. The covering radius controls distance to some obstacle, not every free-flight length.

### 4.3 Cycle translations generate the deck lattice

After choosing quotient representatives and gauging a spanning tree to zero translations, every non-tree edge has a period translation. A lifted path from the root to its translate by `\lambda\in\Lambda` projects to a closed quotient walk. Its signed translation sum is `\lambda`; closed walks are integral combinations of the fundamental cycles. Conversely every edge translation is a period. Hence the cycle group equals `\Lambda`.

The conclusion is independent of the spanning tree and representative-copy gauge. This is standard periodic graph lifting, and the manuscript now attributes that viewpoint while retaining a complete direct proof.

### 4.4 Scope of the geometric theorem

The connectivity theorem itself does not need analyticity, asymmetry, or distinct obstacle shapes. Those assumptions enter only when local records must be grouped into obstacle classes and synchronized. This separation is mathematically clean and should remain prominent.

## 5. Correctness audit of the all-cycle index

Let `\Delta=D\mathbb Z^2\subset\Lambda` have index `n`. The order of the finite quotient `\Lambda/\Delta` annihilates every element, so `n\Lambda\subset\Delta`; therefore all `b_j=nD^{-1}d_j` are integral. If `M` is their column lattice and `H` an integer column basis, then

\[
\Gamma=\sum_j\mathbb Z d_j=\frac1n DH\mathbb Z^2.
\]

Writing the coordinate matrix as `B_0=HE`, its columns generate `M`, so the columns of `E` generate `\mathbb Z^2`. The gcd of the two-column minors of `E` is one. Cauchy--Binet then gives

\[
\gcd_{i<j}|\det(b_i,b_j)|=|\det H|=[\mathbb Z^2:M].
\]

Taking covolumes yields `q=g/n`. The alternative expression as the gcd of normalized physical determinants follows from `d_j=Db_j/n`. Since each pair consists of actual periods, those normalized determinants are integers.

The row/column and direct/dual lattice conventions are now stated explicitly, and the standard HNF attribution has been added. I found the formula and candidate-count reduction correct.

## 6. Correctness audit of stable saturation

The stable theorem works on compact classes with:

- bounded catalogue size and a fixed local indexing chart;
- positive gap-to-cutoff and channel-clearance margins;
- uniform analytic strips and physical bounds;
- quantitative shape separation and asymmetry;
- onset brackets and active-density margins;
- bounded lattice presentations and positive covolume.

Under these hypotheses, the retained histogram argument gives complete-image and cycle-vector errors. Any independent physical cycle pair has determinant at least the covolume lower bound, so its inverse is uniformly controlled. The calibrated integer `n` and every bounded integer coordinate `b_j` lock once the physical errors are small. A deterministic basis procedure then chooses the same `H` for two compatible candidates. If the true all-cycle index is one, corresponding period bases are close.

This is a coherent conditional stability result. It is not a statistical test of catalogue completeness, a minimax theorem, or a computationally efficient reconstruction. The manuscript states all three limitations.

## 7. Required corrections and qualifications for any resubmission

### 7.1 Add the closest proximity- and visibility-graph comparison

The new geometric mechanism belongs not only to voltage graphs and integer lattices, but also to the classical computational-geometry neighborhood of proximity graphs and visibility among disjoint convex obstacles. In particular, relative-neighborhood-type graphs, their relation to Euclidean minimum spanning trees, and visibility/free-bitangent structures are much closer to the obstruction-descent idea than the current bibliography indicates.

A submitted version should compare the exact theorem with the work of Toussaint on the relative neighborhood graph, the Jaromczyk--Toussaint survey of proximity graphs, and Pocchiola--Vegter on visibility complexes/free bitangents of disjoint convex obstacles. I am not asserting that one of those papers already proves the periodic covering-radius theorem in the manuscript. The issue is that the nearest conceptual literature has not yet been examined at theorem level, so the novelty and significance of “obstruction descent” cannot be assessed from the current Cohen/Gross attribution alone.

### 7.2 “Canonical catalogue” remains an acquisition oracle

The catalogue contains one record for every translation orbit of every clear shortest bridge below `R`. The acquisition device is therefore assumed to know that no orbit has been omitted and to quotient an infinite lifted collection into a finite multiset, even though the period lattice is an unknown output of the inverse problem.

This is a mathematically legitimate datum, but it is much stronger than merely fixing a time range. The paper should define equality of complete catalogues independently of a chosen lattice presentation and explain operationally what it means to acquire one representative of every unknown translation orbit. The current text correctly says completeness is not statistically certified; that qualification should appear in every headline use of “canonical.”

### 7.3 The covering-radius bound is substantial prior information

A known `\rho_0\ge\rho_*` and a choice `R>2\rho_0` are not numerical decorations. They ensure both coverage and period generation. Without them, the v19 finite-index ambiguities remain. The abstract and theorem statements already mention the range, but the editorial narrative should emphasize that the uniqueness theorem is conditional on this global geometric bound and exhaustive bounded-range acquisition.

### 7.4 The stable theorem freezes catalogue topology

To obtain continuity, the statistical theorem imposes a positive margin from the cutoff, positive clearance, a bounded number of records, and charts in which the catalogue is indexed before quotienting by permutations and reversals. These priors exclude births, deaths, and tangencies of catalogue edges. Thus the stable theorem does not robustly discover the complete graph near a catalogue bifurcation. This should be stated as a topology-freezing prior, not only as “regular catalogue classes.”

### 7.5 Preserve the global genericity qualifications

The local theorem is smooth and does not require asymmetry. The whole-table theorem is analytic, asymmetric, and pairwise shape-separated. Those assumptions identify full images, obstacle multiplicities, and unique placements. They are not incidental technical hypotheses. Symmetric or repeated-shape tables retain the earlier registered or finite-candidate results rather than the new unlabelled uniqueness theorem.

### 7.6 Do not convert exact mathematical inversion into an algorithmic claim

The exact theorem groups recovered analytic functions by congruence and assumes an exhaustive catalogue. The stable theorem minimizes over a compact physical prior. Neither supplies an efficient channel-discovery, analytic-function comparison, or global optimization algorithm. The manuscript currently disclaims this; the disclaimer should remain adjacent to any claim of “reconstruction.”

## 8. Top-four significance assessment

The positive case is substantial:

- a smooth function-level inverse from two intrinsic endpoint laws is retained;
- complete analytic images recover incidence without supplied obstacle labels;
- volume normalization determines the period covolume;
- a finite-range geometric theorem forces lifted connectivity;
- all cycles, not one selected pair, recover the full period group;
- the all-cycle gcd gives an explicit finite-index certificate;
- exact partial-catalogue ambiguity and stable complete-catalogue recovery are both retained;
- exact-source full-package qualification has now passed.

The negative case remains decisive at the requested benchmark:

1. **The local observation is still extremely rich.** It is function-valued local scattering/lens data for a selected branch, not a standard count, marked-length, or unmarked dynamical spectrum.
2. **The global uniqueness datum is exhaustive by definition.** The observer supplies a complete catalogue of all short clear normal channels below a range controlled by a global covering-radius bound.
3. **The new geometric proof is elegant but elementary in ingredients.** It combines connected covers, shorter-obstruction replacement, periodic graph lifting, and standard determinantal lattice theory.
4. **The whole-table inverse is strongly generic.** Analyticity, asymmetry, and pairwise noncongruence do much of the incidence and synchronization work.
5. **The stable result is conditional on a topology-frozen compact prior.** It is neither minimax nor an algorithm for discovering catalogue completeness.
6. **The closest proximity/visibility literature remains unreviewed.** This makes a four-journal novelty judgment premature and weakens the current significance claim.
7. **Natural lower-information problems remain unresolved.** The theorem does not give count-only analytic rigidity or derive the same conclusion from a conventional global billiard invariant.

The new completeness theorem is a meaningful improvement over v19, but it replaces a designed primitive network by a designed exhaustive bounded-range catalogue. In my judgment this does not cross the conceptual threshold from a strong specialized inverse theorem to an exceptional result for *Annals*, *Acta*, *Inventiones*, or *JAMS*.
