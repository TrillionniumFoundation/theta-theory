https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/rational-interpolation-and-state-variable-realizations/

The revision therefore treats realization and linear interpolation as direct algebraic predecessors, not merely as neighbouring applications. No unverified numbered theorem or full-text priority conclusion is attributed to this source.

## Comparison made in the manuscript

Theorem 5.1 states the exact interpolation module with an arbitrary rectangular numerator K and scalar denominator q. Its gcd is `gcd(q, entries K)`. The equation follows from the finite-node polynomial identity and divisibility, with no stochastic or diagonalization assumption. This is explicitly presented as a classical specialization.

The observation-specific distinctions are then separated: the original-degree numerator spectrum can change along a cancelled factor; Lemma 2.2 realizes finite-clock ambiguities inside positive fixed-channel parameters; Proposition 2.6 supplies a second sharp clock count for full polynomial span; and Theorem 3.1 transports scalar normalization error through the joint coefficient algebra with an additive, observable conditioning scale. These are statements proved in the revision, not negative claims about Loewner algorithms.

The classical spectral exclusion and multiplicity-counting mechanism is attributed to Bauer–Fike and the existing pencil, polynomial and structured-pseudospectrum literature. Theorem 4.1 specifies the observation metric for its exact local condition. Theorems 9.2 and 9.3 specify the positive statistical experiment and the signal-class or neighbourhood quantifiers for their lower bounds. This division is intended to make the originality comparison testable at the level of individual assertions.

The full historical bibliography is retained. The revision does not infer exhaustive novelty from searching three added references, nor present publisher abstracts as independent validation of its new proofs.
