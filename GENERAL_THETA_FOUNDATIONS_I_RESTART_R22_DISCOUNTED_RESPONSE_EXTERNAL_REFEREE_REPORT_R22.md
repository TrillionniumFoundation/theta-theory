## Recommendation

**Reject at the level of the Annals of Mathematics, Inventiones Mathematicae, the Journal of the American Mathematical Society, or Acta Mathematica in the manuscript's present form.**

This is **not a technical-correctness rejection**. Revision R22 is a genuine mathematical advance over R20 and, in my assessment, the most coherent native article in the restart sequence. The central continuity argument is real and necessary. A controller row is reused at many times, so weak-star convergence of rows does not by itself justify multiplication on one report coordinate. The manuscript instead assumes domination of every finite open-loop report word by a product reference measure and tests repeated copies of the changing row on distinct coordinates. The rectangle-density argument in Proposition `prop:tensor` then proves continuity of every finite-prefix response. A uniform discounted tail converts those finite-prefix facts into infinite-horizon compactness.

The negative top-four judgment concerns the **concentration, intrinsic reach, and external mathematical impact of the advance**, rather than an identified fatal proof defect. Once the finite-product tensor continuity has been established, robust attainment and the finite-model identity follow from standard compactness, lower semicontinuity, and the finite-intersection principle. The effective theorem is a careful and useful same-budget comparison, but it assumes the decisive response-approximation errors, controller-uniform model modulus, computable model nets, readout nets, and certified integrations, and then uses exhaustive enumeration. For a strong specialist journal in stochastic control, probability, information theory, robust POMDPs, controller synthesis, quantization, or mathematical quantum information, my assessment is **favorable after a focused major revision**.

## 2. Executive assessment of Revision R22

R22 changes the mathematical interface of the paper. R20 gave an exact common-programme theorem on a finite horizon, with exact stopping and a compact predictive allocation invariant. R22 instead fixes a stationary finite-state controller and a strictly discounted continuing experiment. The new primitive object is the collection of finite-intervention report and payoff responses.

For every open-loop action word, model, horizon, and terminal readout, the manuscript records:

```text
report law P_{theta,t}^{a_1:t},
payoff submeasure Lambda_{theta,t}^{a_1:t,d}.
```

Every finite report word is assumed dominated by the product of action-indexed reference measures. A stationary controller is then represented by finitely many action simplexes, finitely many simplex-valued `L^infty` report rows, and finitely many compact readouts.

The mathematical progression is:

```text
finite-history product domination
      + raw interventional expansion
      + tensor continuity for one reused row
      + compact controller space
      + uniform discounted tail
      => attained robust discounted optimum
      + finite-model value identity.
```

Under additional effective response data, the progression continues:

```text
common report partitions
      + one cell average of every reused row
      + dyadic row/readout rounding
      + finite model net and all-controller modulus
      + complete finite-table enumeration
      => same-M implementing upper
      + same-M lower against every Borel controller.
```

This is a cleaner theorem architecture than the long combined R20 article. The exact, effective, geometric, and transfer layers are visibly separate. The new 16-page article is substantially easier to referee as one paper.

The advance over R20 is also mathematically substantive:

1. the exact discounted theorem no longer needs a finite-dimensional physical or predictive carrier;
2. the controller is genuinely reused forever rather than assigned a finite sequence of phase rows;
3. report supports may change, because the continuing interface makes all actions perpetually legal and imposes no exact internal deadline;
4. the robust family may be arbitrary at the exact level, with the parameter fixed throughout each path;
5. the effective theorem retains the same persistent alphabet on both sides; and
6. the nonlinear and projective quantum verifications operate directly on raw responses rather than on stable normalized filters.

These are meaningful improvements. They narrow, but do not remove, the remaining general-journal objection.

## 4. Audit of the discounted raw-response theorem

### 4.1 Raw responses and the model quantifiers

The parameter set `Theta` may be arbitrary at the exact level. A realized parameter is fixed for the whole execution. The controller is chosen before the model supremum and never receives the parameter. This is maintained in the definition of the controller, the interventional expansion, the compactness proof, the effective model-net comparison, the examples, and the simulator theorem.

The scored outcome at time `t` is deliberately not feedback. Its role is to define a positive payoff submeasure under one executable audit at that scoring call. This avoids postulating a joint classical path of incompatible quantum audits. The distinction is operationally coherent.

The exact hypotheses are stated on finite open-loop intervention words. This is the correct level for the proof: an adaptive stationary controller is reconstructed by summing over its finite action and label words. The proof does not run the controller on an unrelated open-loop law.

### 4.2 Product-history domination

The temporal domination assumption is stronger than one-time marginal domination and the manuscript correctly says so. The diagonal counterexample in Remark `rem:diagonal` is important: marginal densities do not justify weak-star continuity of repeated multiplication on one coordinate.

Under the displayed assumption, each finite payoff response has an `L^1` density on a product reference space. That is exactly what permits the tensor argument.

This hypothesis also marks a major boundary of the theorem. “Changing support” here means changing or vanishing densities inside a common finite-product reference structure. It does not cover arbitrary path-singular report laws. Deterministic repeated observations, noiseless constraints producing diagonal path laws, and many nondominated sensor families can fail the hypothesis even though every one-time marginal is simple.

The introduction and scope audit state this boundary honestly. For publication, it should be emphasized even more prominently in the theorem's informal summary.

### 4.3 Compactness of the controller space

For each controller state and action, the report row lies in a simplex-valued weak-star compact subset of `L^infty(sigma_a)^M`. Positivity and the row-sum identity are weak-star closed. There are finitely many such rows, finitely many Euclidean action simplexes, and `D^M` is compact. The product is compact.

The manuscript works with equivalence classes modulo the reference measures and then chooses Borel versions. On standard Borel probability spaces, this is legitimate. Completing all finitely many rows on one union of reference-null exceptional sets makes the representative common to all models and finite histories because of product domination.

I found this part coherent. A final version should cite a measurable Radon--Nikodym/version theorem rather than leaving all version facts implicit.

### 4.4 Tensor continuity for a reused row

Proposition `prop:tensor` is the load-bearing step. If bounded functions converge weak-star in each one-coordinate `L^infty` space, their tensor product converges against any `L^1` density on the finite product. The proof first treats rectangles, then finite sums of rectangle indicators, and finally uses `L^1` density.

The same changing row may appear several times, but each occurrence is evaluated at a different report coordinate. The proof therefore does not claim continuity of the map `u -> u^2` on one `L^infty` space. This is precisely the distinction exposed by the diagonal counterexample.

Expanding the actual adaptive controller over finite action and label words then gives a finite sum of continuous tensor integrals. Variation of the terminal readout is handled by the assumed `L^1` continuity of payoff densities. I found no missing factor or unjustified independence step in this expansion.

### 4.5 Infinite discounted risk

The stage losses lie in `[0,1]`. The omitted normalized discounted tail after `N` calls is at most `beta^N`, uniformly in the controller and model. Consequently each fixed-model infinite discounted risk is a uniform limit of continuous finite-prefix risks and is continuous on the compact controller space.

The supremum over models is lower semicontinuous and attains its minimum. No compactness or topology on `Theta` is needed for this exact existence statement.

The prefix inequality

```text
V_{beta,N}^Theta(M) <= V_beta^Theta(M)
                     <= V_{beta,N}^Theta(M)+beta^N
```

uses the same continuing stationary controller class in both values. It does not give the deployed controller an external clock or permit phase-dependent rows. This is an important and correctly stated distinction.

### 4.6 Finite-model value identity

For each model, a risk sublevel set is closed in the one compact common-controller space. If every finite subfamily admits a common controller below a level `r`, the family of closed sets has the finite-intersection property and hence a point in the full intersection. Taking levels just above the supremum of finite-subfamily optima proves

```text
V_beta^Theta(M)
 = sup_{finite nonempty F subset Theta} V_beta^F(M).
```

This is correct. It is not a minimax interchange. It is also not, by itself, a new compactness principle. The new work lies in showing that the stationary reused-row controller class has the required compact topology and continuous finite-history responses.

The theorem gives no bound on the size of a witnessing finite family and no procedure to find it. The paper correctly reserves computability for the stronger effective layer.

## 5. Audit of the same-budget effective theorem

### 5.1 Supplied versus derived information

The effective theorem requires substantially more than the exact theorem:

- computably compact model family and finite model nets;
- finite report partitions with executable membership;
- uniform `L^1` conditional-expectation errors for every finite payoff response;
- a controller-uniform finite-prefix model modulus;
- finite readout nets and certified risk integrations.

These assumptions are legitimate, but they contain much of the difficult quantitative information. A reader should not infer that product domination alone yields an algorithm or a rate.

### 5.2 One common cell average

The averaging lemma is correctly formulated for a reused row. Each report row is averaged once on every one-coordinate cell. On a product cell, the integral of the product of the original rows factors into the product of their cell integrals. This gives the identity between the conditional expectation of the payoff density and the averaged controller.

The proof then groups label paths by final label. For each action word and report word, the nonnegative controller weights over all label paths sum to at most one. The resulting error bound has an explicit factor `M` for the final readout and an explicit sum over all action words. It does not hide an uncounted `M^t`, but the action-word explosion can still be enormous.

No equal-support condition is required because every action remains legal forever and there is no exact internal stopping constraint. New low-probability paths may affect risk but cannot make the continuing controller illegal.

### 5.3 Probability-table rounding

The dyadic approximation changes each action or transition row by an explicit total-variation allowance and couples the samples until their first discrepancy. The probability of a discrepancy by time `t` is bounded by `t(|A|+M)/Q`. The readout net contributes through the loss modulus.

This comparison preserves the alphabet `M`. It need not preserve zero supports, and in the continuing interface it does not need to. The paper correctly does not transplant this argument into the exact-deadline setting.

### 5.4 Model-net comparison and interval direction

Let the finite list contain every denominator-`Q` table and every readout-net choice. Its optimum over the full model family lies between the Borel prefix optimum and that optimum plus the report and rounding errors.

The model net is a subset of the family, so it supports the lower endpoint without a modulus. The controller-uniform modulus is needed to carry an upper candidate from the model net to the full family. Hence the interval

```text
L-E_N-B_N <= V_beta^Theta(M)
            <= U+Omega_N(s)+beta^N
```

has the error terms on the correct sides. The selected table is a genuine stationary table on the original experiment; `N` is only an analysis truncation.

I found no minimax exchange or local-optimizer substitution in this proof.

### 5.5 Computational status

The list bound is finite and explicit, but exhaustive. The theorem is an effectivity result, not a practical complexity result. The error data, table count, model-net size, and certified integration cost can all be prohibitive.

The continuous certificate is valuable because it instantiates every component and distinguishes the generic rounding estimate from an instance-specific value-preserving reduction. It should remain, but its purpose-built scalar structure should not be used as evidence of broad computational tractability.

## 6. Audit of the raw realizations

### 6.1 Arbitrary hidden dynamics with dominated observations

The hidden-state theorem permits standard-Borel and even infinite-dimensional hidden dynamics. At each step, conditional on the visible past, the report law is a mixture of kernels dominated by the same action reference. Iterated disintegration gives a finite-word density.

This is the right mechanism, and it allows report-dependent hidden transitions. The proof does not condition on a hidden path as though it were independent of the reports.

For full publication, the jointly measurable Radon--Nikodym construction should be stated or cited explicitly. The present short proof is plausible and standard, but this is one place where a reader should not have to reconstruct a kernel-version theorem.

### 6.2 Discount--expansion modulus

The theorem assumes one consistent causal coupling valid under every common adaptively selected action. Until the first report mismatch, hidden-state discrepancies grow at most like `L^t s`; one-call report mismatch and matched-report score differences are controlled by the hidden discrepancy and parameter distance.

Using the same controller uniforms keeps the labels, actions, and readouts identical until report disagreement, even for discontinuous Borel rows. The stage-risk difference is therefore at most `min(1,C L^t s)`. Splitting the discounted series where `L^t s` reaches order one gives the Lipschitz, critical logarithmic, and Hölder regimes.

The exponents and the sharp expanding witness are correct. The theorem is a useful controller-uniform robustness statement. Its coupling assumptions are strong and should be presented as such: they already supply a pathwise common-action comparison of the two raw models and a score bound uniform over every decision.

The logistic-map calculation is a nontrivial moving-support verification, but it remains primarily a sensitivity example rather than a solution of a difficult finite-memory-control problem.

### 6.3 Quantum instruments

The quantum theorem uses unnormalized finite-dimensional completely positive instrument densities. Finite products give positive trace-class report densities; applying one bounded terminal scoring effect gives the payoff density. No normalization by a possibly zero report probability occurs.

This permits projective zeros, pure states, unitary dynamics, and changing ranks. The controller is classical and finite-state; the evolving quantum system is part of the experiment, not a free observer register.

The mathematical scope is accurately limited to finite dimension. The effective clause would benefit from a more explicit theorem-level map from operator approximation bounds to the `eta`, `Omega`, and certified integration data required in Section 3.

### 6.4 Singular references

The Cantor, countable, and tagged-stratum corollary correctly observes that the tensor and averaging proofs use finite product probability spaces and `L^1` approximation, not Lebesgue density. Actual tail and stratum masses remain visible.

This is not a general nondominated theorem. The common singular reference or observed tag still supplies domination. The separate blind theorem is the only general nondominated preparation statement in the native article.

## 7. Acquired geometry, resource transfer, and exact examples

### 7.1 Blind acquired geometry

The blind experiment is exactly solvable. Every controller has at most `M` fixed readouts, so its conditional loss at any time is bounded below pointwise by distance to the nearest of those readouts. A nearest-center encoder at the first tagged report and stationary self-loops attain the robust quantization value at every call.

The proof works for an arbitrary family of preparation laws, including the uncountable Dirac family. It is a clean nondominated theorem, but it succeeds because all information arrives in the first report and the subsequent dynamics are completely blind.

The small-ball and covering corollary correctly retains the actual submeasure mass and allows noninteger singular exponents.

### 7.2 Discounted simulation and erasure

The simulator theorem compares complete scored prefixes, including action, report, readout, task outcome, state count, and acquisition clock. The source stores the product of target and simulator states. Prefix defects are discounted with the target-call score index; source physical-time discount is not silently identified with target-call discount.

The irreversible-erasure example attains the exact amplification

```text
e / [4(1-beta+beta e)].
```

The lower holds even for unrestricted history because every post-failure fresh bit is independent and fair. Three readouts attain it. This is a useful sharp witness showing that an actual per-call defect can accumulate at the expected discounted horizon scale.

### 7.3 Continuous acquired-memory certificate

The continuous likelihood family has uniform report marginals and a posterior mean affine in the current report. The least favorable endpoint is `p_0`. Scalar uniform quantization yields the exact robust value

```text
1/4 - p_0^2(1-M^{-2})/12.
```

The equal-cell stationary controller is common to the full parameter interval and uses no phase counter. The inaccessible offset adds exactly `delta^2` by a two-point averaging lower and zero-mean-error upper.

For the declared `M=2`, `beta=1/2` finite decision set, the 729-table enumeration and rational resolvent calculation are internally consistent. The generic finite certificate and the independent analytic all-Borel lower are correctly distinguished.

This section is exemplary as a reproducible certificate. It is, however, an intentionally soluble iid scalar experiment and should be described as validation of the theorem rather than a source of broad external significance.

## 9. Novelty and literature positioning

The manuscript now correctly acknowledges that the following ingredients are established:

- Witsenhausen-style static reduction to an independent reference measure;
- compactness and existence methods for strategic measures;
- robust finite-state-controller synthesis for uncertain finite POMDPs;
- finite-controller parameter and inductive synthesis;
- finite-window and finite-memory approximation in partially observed control;
- compactness, discount tails, finite-intersection arguments, simplex rounding, and scalar quantization.

The candidate contribution is the precise combination of:

1. one fixed finite report row reused at all times and models;
2. finite-product raw-response domination rather than a finite-dimensional belief state;
3. an arbitrary fixed-model robust family;
4. attained strictly discounted value and finite-model equality;
5. a global same-`M` finite-table comparison against all Borel controllers; and
6. direct nonlinear, moving-support, and projective quantum verifications.

I found no evidence in the supplied material that this precise package is merely a restatement of one cited theorem. However, the nine-item native bibliography is too narrow for the breadth and level claimed.

A revised literature section should compare the exact hypotheses and conclusions with at least the following nearby routes:

- S. Yüksel, *Another Look at Partially Observed Optimal Stochastic Control: Existence, Ergodicity, and Approximations without Belief-Reduction*, arXiv:2301.11244 and subsequent publication status. This work studies discounted and average infinite-horizon control directly on history spaces without belief reduction and proves finite-window approximation results.
- H. Yu, *On Strategic Measures and Optimality Properties in Discrete-Time Stochastic Control with Universally Measurable Policies*, Mathematics of Operations Research 49 (2024), 1734--1760, DOI `10.1287/moor.2022.0188`. Its strategic-measure and minimax results are close to the compactness/existence layer.
- M. Kim, *Finite Memory Belief Approximation for Optimal Control in Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes*, arXiv:2601.03132 (2026). Its policy-conditional metric comparison is different from fixed-cardinality optimization, but close enough to require explicit differentiation.
- D. Hudák et al., *Finite-State Controllers for (Hidden-Model) POMDPs using Deep Reinforcement Learning*, arXiv:2602.08734 (2026). Its robust hidden-model controller extraction is computational rather than an all-Borel same-budget theorem, but directly adjacent in information pattern.
- Classical small-policy complexity results, including M. Mundhenk's *The Complexity of Optimal Small Policies*, Mathematics of Operations Research 25 (2000), 118--129, should be mentioned when discussing exhaustive finite-controller synthesis.

These works do not obviously imply the manuscript's exact tensor-continuity result or its same-`M` all-Borel lower. That is precisely why a theorem-by-theorem comparison would sharpen rather than weaken the paper's novelty claim.

The authors should also say explicitly whether Theorem `thm:main` can be recovered as a corollary of an existing strategic-measure existence theorem after restricting the policy variable to a stationary finite-state controller with repeated rows. If not, the obstruction and the additional argument should be isolated in a proposition comparing the two frameworks.

## 10. Major issues before specialist-journal publication

### 10.1 Isolate the exact new theorem relative to static reduction

The article says the right things informally, but the novelty still rests on a comparison that the reader must reconstruct. State a precise theorem-level nonimplication or missing hypothesis:

- what existing static-reduction/strategic-measure existence theorem gives;
- why it does not immediately preserve one reused finite row;
- what Proposition `prop:tensor` adds; and
- which part of Theorem `thm:main` is then standard compact analysis.

This is the most important revision for priority assessment.

### 10.2 Foreground the strength of finite-product domination

The theorem allows moving and vanishing supports, but only inside a common product-dominated finite-history family. That distinction should appear in the theorem's first informal sentence, not only in the diagonal counterexample and scope section.

A compact list of included and excluded observation mechanisms would help. In particular, noiseless deterministic repeated observations can produce path laws singular to every product of one-time references.

### 10.3 Separate exact response data from effective response data more aggressively

The effective theorem assumes the response approximation errors and a controller-uniform model modulus. These are powerful supplied certificates. Explain which natural model classes provide them algorithmically, which merely provide qualitative convergence, and which do not.

The hidden-state and quantum sections should explicitly derive all effective inputs required by Theorem `thm:compile`, not only indicate that suitable approximations imply them.

### 10.4 Expand the modern literature map

The current bibliography is too compressed for a top-level submission. The direct history-space, universally measurable strategic-measure/minimax, current finite-memory approximation, hidden-model FSC, and small-policy-complexity literatures should be treated at theorem level.

### 10.5 Strengthen the natural application

The logistic calculation verifies a useful modulus, but it does not determine an optimal finite-memory controller curve or settle a recognized open problem. A materially stronger application would connect the general theorem to a natural robust filtering or coding model whose fixed-memory optimum, existence, or approximation law was previously unresolved.

### 10.6 Clarify the status of the discount--expansion assumptions

The coupling is required to work under all commonly and adaptively selected actions, and the matched-report score estimate is uniform over decisions. These are substantial raw assumptions. State examples where they fail and whether a weaker response-density condition can replace them.

### 10.7 Make the hidden-kernel density construction fully measurable

Theorem `thm:hidden` should cite or prove the jointly measurable Radon--Nikodym kernel used in the sequential disintegration. The current paragraph is credible but too compressed for a theorem whose scope includes arbitrary standard-Borel hidden histories.

### 10.8 Complete the quantum effective interface

Give a displayed implication from operator approximation data to the finite-prefix response errors, model modulus, and certified integrals of Section 3. This would make the quantum result a complete effective verification rather than an exact verification followed by an informal computational clause.

### 10.9 Keep the 16-page article genuinely standalone

The decision to move R20 and earlier papers to companions is editorially correct. The native article should nevertheless contain every definition and comparison needed to understand its own contribution. References to companion A should be limited to inherited theorems not required by R22.

### 10.10 Reconsider title versus proved scope

The current title is historically understandable, but “Foundations” suggests an intrinsic classification of broad causal experiments. The native theorem is a strong discounted-controller theorem under product domination. Either narrow the title/subtitle or make the abstract's scope qualification still more conspicuous.

## 12. What would materially change the top-four assessment

Further source checks, more archived companions, or another soluble finite example would not by themselves change the recommendation. A top-four case would require an advance of a different order, for example one of the following.

### 12.1 An intrinsic response criterion beyond full finite-history tensors

A simpler invariant, duality theorem, or near-necessary numerical criterion determining fixed-memory predictability across a broad raw class would move the work beyond compact optimization over all finite responses.

### 12.2 A vanishing-discount or average-cost theorem

A theorem controlling optimal values and controllers uniformly as `beta` tends to one, or an average-cost common-controller result under natural recurrence conditions, would supply a genuinely stronger long-run theory.

### 12.3 A general nondominated common-controller theorem

The blind quantization theorem is exact but structurally special. A compactness or duality theory for broad nondominated adaptive report families would materially enlarge the scope.

### 12.4 A recognized hard natural application

A sharp fixed-memory law or new existence/approximation theorem for a recognized nonlinear filtering, robust control, causal coding, or quantum-control problem would demonstrate external mathematical impact beyond verification examples.

### 12.5 Non-exhaustive synthesis complexity

A polynomial, fixed-parameter, approximation-scheme, or lower-complexity theorem for a meaningful class would transform the effective result from finite certifiability into algorithmic mathematics.

### 12.6 A matched multi-resource region

A theorem with matching upper and lower bounds for persistent labels together with program description, workspace, precision, planning time, physical time, or simulator state would complete more of the advertised resource programme.

## 13. Final evaluation

Revision R22 is a serious and successful response to the R20 report. It is mathematically distinct from the finite-horizon allocation paper and should be assessed as a concise discounted-control article rather than as another layer appended to a 234-page manuscript.

The proof architecture is sound in the principal arguments checked. Finite-product domination supports a correct tensor-continuity argument for one reused weak-star row; compactness and a uniform discount tail give robust attainment; the finite-intersection property yields a finite-model value identity; and the effective theorem compares a complete finite table class with every same-budget Borel controller. The nonlinear, projective quantum, blind, erasure, and continuous-report examples illuminate genuinely different parts of the theorem.

I found no elementary fatal error in those arguments. The repository's provenance and build discipline are careful and accurately bounded.

The paper nevertheless remains below the exceptional threshold of the four leading general mathematics journals. Its main novelty is a precise compactness-and-approximation package within a product-dominated discounted controller class. The decisive structural tools are close to established static reduction, strategic-measure compactness, robust finite-state control, and finite-memory approximation. The manuscript has not yet demonstrated that its new combination resolves a major natural problem, yields an intrinsic general classification, establishes an average-cost or nondominated long-run theory, or produces nontrivial synthesis complexity.

Accordingly:

**Top-four general-journal recommendation:** reject in the present form.  
**Reason:** insufficient concentration of novelty, intrinsic reach, and demonstrated external impact for that exceptional level; not an identified fatal correctness defect.  
**Specialist-journal outlook:** favorable after a focused major revision addressing theorem-level priority, domination scope, effective-data verification, modern literature positioning, and a stronger natural application.
