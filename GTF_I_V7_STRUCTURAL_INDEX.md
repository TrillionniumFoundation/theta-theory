Every retained vertex is charged. The exact count is `1+sum_internal actual_outdegree`, at most `(U+1)(2L-1)` for L leaves under the stated unary-chain bound. The same theorem has two genuinely different uses: deleting a known future suffix between independent acquisitions, and inserting each new report into a continually observed past. The second application retains an infinite hidden tail and uses a direct posterior-geometry converse, not a renewal argument. For a fair binary symmetric observation channel its sharp average risk is `epsilon(1-epsilon)+Theta((1-2epsilon)^2 M^(-alpha))`, with `alpha=-2 log(r)/log(2)` for the target weight r. Complete erasure has zero excess. Initialization is explicitly paid.

The measure-valued theorem separately implements a true W1-contracting posterior using exactly `binomial(2n,n)` empirical grid laws, covering radius `3/(2n)`, and recursive error `rho^t+[3/(2n)+eta]/(1-rho)`. The verified nonlinear instrument has `rho=6/25`, rational transient implementation, no fixed-step common minorizer and no finite polynomial-moment recursion on all priors. Its logarithmic finite-register upper bound is not represented as a universal optimal rate.

The Gaussian theorem proves `inf_{|F|<=M} Delta(F,G) ~ M^(-2/r)` up to constants for a fixed compact mean set with nonempty interior and fixed positive-definite covariance. The lower bound covers arbitrary finite-output experiments, not merely grid encoders or Gaussian garblings. The upper construction compactifies the Gaussian observation and uses positive tensor hats to achieve quadratic reconstruction without overflow logarithms.

The A2 theorem retains the actual deterministic unjittered physical statistic and proves `Delta <= C[N^(-1/2)+J/sqrt(N)+J^(-2)]` with at most J^r designated labels. All comparison randomization is parameter independent and external to that physical statistic. When the local mean set contains an r-dimensional ball, the universal finite-experiment lower bound matches the construction for `2^r<=M<=K N^(r/6)`. Achieving the earlier target `N^(-1/5)` requires and is achieved with order `N^(r/10)` labels. This is a fixed known-mark, fixed-rank theorem, not a claim uniform through rank degeneration or an unrestricted optimal sample-size rate.
