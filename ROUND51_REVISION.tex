\title[Single-atom boundary identification]{Single-Atom Boundary Identification with Retained State and Logarithmic Physical Depth}
\author{Qian Qi}
\date{September 5, 2026}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
One sufficiently short positive-mass atom in a bounded-duration probe law
suffices to recover logarithmically many labeled coefficients of an infinite
damped Jacobi lattice. We choose a fast observation clock, retain the state,
and permit bounded feedback between precommitted diagnostic blocks. A
channel-corrected sampled logarithm has a finite rational transform and a
$16^{-N}$ remainder. Averaging every block sign gives an exact weighted
response-energy identity; every stage using the short duration contributes,
whether or not it was labeled exploratory. A variance-sensitive argument
then controls the working posterior of all completed readouts, uniformly over
bounded initial states and arbitrary working initial-state laws on that ball.
Explicit constants yield depth $\lfloor r\log n\rfloor$ and coefficient error
$n^{-s}$ when $C_b r+p_b s<1$, with $p_b=3+\log(1/p_*)/\log16$ and
$p_*=1-\rho+\rho w$. Elapsed time is deterministically linear in the readout
count. We also prove a finite-response-to-physical-coefficient reduction,
including dimension-uniform finite-section bias and a nonreset response
estimator. An all-policy propagation bound matches logarithmic depth order.
Exact-rational outer certificates retain distinct statistical, representation,
spatial, mesh and Taylor error budgets.
\end{abstract}
