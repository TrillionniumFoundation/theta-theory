\section{Round-sixteen positive closure: connected platform response and well-typed memory forms}
\label{sec:r16-c2}

There is no single untyped Hilbert space shared by the Sinai and hard-sphere
platforms.  Each platform is treated on its own ambient transfer or kinetic
bundle.  Parameter-dependent Hilbert metrics are transported to a fixed
reference fiber; their connection derivative is part of every response
formula.  Resolved memory is compressed either on a common operator core or
entirely at the closed-form level.

\subsection{Strict and strong cotangent topologies}

For a platform path space \(\Omega\), let \(C_W(\Omega)\) carry the weighted
strict topology and let \(\mathscr A\subset C_W\) be the model-specific strong
H\"older/graph source space.  The invariant-integral nullspace is
\[
 \mathscr N_{\rm inv}=
 \overline{\operatorname{span}}^{C_W}
 \{U-U\circ\Theta_t:U\in C_W,t\in\mathbb Q\};
\]
constants are not included.  The pressure nullspace is
\(\mathbb R1+\mathscr N_{\rm inv}\).

\begin{theorem}[Signed-Radon invariant dual]
\label{thm:r16-c2-dual}
The continuous dual of the weighted strict space is the vector space
\(\mathcal M_W(\Omega)\) of countably additive signed Radon measures with
finite \(W\)-variation.  The annihilator of
\(\mathscr N_{\rm inv}\) is exactly the invariant signed Radon measures.
The quotient is Hausdorff.  Pullback along
\(\mathscr A\hookrightarrow C_W\) embeds this Radon dual into the strong dual,
but does not exhaust the latter.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On compact weight sublevels, strict continuity is sup-norm continuity and is
represented by compatible signed Radon measures.  Uniform bounded-set control
makes their weighted variations summable, so they paste to one member of
\(\mathcal M_W\).  Conversely weighted dominated convergence proves strict
continuity of integration.  Every invariant signed measure annihilates time
coboundaries.  If a class is outside the closed coboundary span, Hahn--Banach
separates it by a strict continuous functional; Ces\`aro averaging its Radon
representer, using the platform Lyapunov tightness, gives an invariant signed
Radon separator.  The final assertion follows because a strong distributional
dual can contain singular currents not continuous for the strict topology.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fixed reference bundles and connection derivatives}

On the Sinai platform, the A2 parent space \(\mathbb B\) is fixed and the
physical Riesz range is \(P_\eta\mathbb B\).  Kato parallel transport
\(U_\eta\) solves
\[
 \partial_\eta U_\eta=[\partial_\eta P_\eta,P_\eta]U_\eta,
 \qquad U_0=I,
\]
on the finite Riesz range and extends by identity on a fixed complement.  On
the hard-sphere platform, choose a fixed nuclear test space and transport the
finite resolved covariance ranges by their positive Gram matrices.  In either
case the transported Hilbert inner product on the fixed reference fiber is
\[
 (x,y)_\eta=(G_\eta x,y)_0,
 \qquad G_\eta>0.
\]
Define the metric connection
\[
 \nabla_\eta x=\partial_\eta x
 +{1\over2}G_\eta^{-1}(\partial_\eta G_\eta)x.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Covariant resolvent response]
\label{thm:r16-c2-connection}
Let \(L_\eta\) be a type-(A) family on a common transported operator core, or
a type-(B) family of closed real coercive forms on a common form domain.  On a
fixed reference fiber,
\[
 \nabla_\eta(z-L_\eta)^{-1}
 =(z-L_\eta)^{-1}
  (\nabla_\eta L_\eta)(z-L_\eta)^{-1}.
\]
In an untransported moving fiber, \(\nabla_\eta L_\eta\) includes the metric
term \(\tfrac12[G_\eta^{-1}G_\eta',L_\eta]\).  The same connection
terms occur in derivatives of eigenvectors, pairings, and resolved
projections.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conjugate the operator or form resolvent by the transport \(U_\eta\) to the
fixed reference space.  Differentiate
\((z-\widetilde L_\eta)\widetilde R_\eta=I\) there.  Undoing the transport
replaces ordinary derivatives by \(\nabla_\eta\).  Differentiating the
transported inner product produces the displayed metric connection and its
commutator contribution.  Kato's form theorem gives the same identity in
\(V^*\)-to-\(V\) form duality for a type-(B) family.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Well-typed compressed memory}

There are two permitted cases.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Operator compression:} a fixed finite-dimensional resolved space
\(\mathcal R\) is contained in a common operator core \(D\subset D(L_\eta^2)\).
\item \emph{Form compression:} resolved vectors lie only in the common form
domain \(V\); then every occurrence of \(PLP\) is replaced by the finite
matrix \(a_\eta(r_i,r_j)\), and the Schur complement is taken in
\(V\subset H\subset V^*\).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[Connected memory response]
\label{thm:r16-c2-memory}
In either permitted case the compressed resolvent
\[
 C_\eta(z)=P_\eta(z-L_\eta)^{-1}P_\eta
\]
and memory transform are well defined on the transported finite fiber.  In
the operator case,
\[
 \widehat M_\eta(z)=zP_\eta-P_\eta L_\eta P_\eta-C_\eta(z)^{-1};
\]
in the form case \(P_\eta L_\eta P_\eta\) is replaced by the
resolved form matrix.  Their covariant derivative contains
\[
 -\nabla_\eta(P_\eta L_\eta P_\eta)
 +C_\eta^{-1}(\nabla_\eta C_\eta)C_\eta^{-1}
\]
together with the connection derivatives of both projections.  These formulas
agree with the derivative obtained by first linearizing the A4/B4 nonlinear
pressure and then forming the compressed resolvent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The domain hypotheses make every term legal.  Differentiate the inverse matrix
by the covariant product rule and use
Theorem~\ref{thm:r16-c2-connection}.  In the form case, the Schur complement is
an operator from the finite resolved coefficient space to itself built from
the form resolvent, so no vector is incorrectly placed in \(D(L)\).  The A4
Feynman--Kac derivative and B4 ensemble derivative are Duhamel insertions of
the same transported generator/form perturbation.  Laplace transformation and
finite-dimensional compression commute with that insertion, proving the last
statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Platform-specific rigidity}

Let \(Q(F)\) denote the complete perturbed pressure functional on a
model-specific open source set, not one scalar pressure value.

\begin{theorem}[Sinai and hard-sphere pressure separation]
\label{thm:r16-c2-rigidity}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item On the Sinai strong source algebra, if
\(Q(F+H)-Q(F)=Q(G+H)-Q(G)\) for every sufficiently small strong perturbation
\(H\), then \(F-G\) is a constant plus a closed time coboundary.
\item On the hard-sphere density/contact source algebra, equality of the full
local pressure perturbation functional implies that the two source pairs
differ by the closed balance gauge \((r,-\Delta r)\) and collision invariants.
\end{enumerate}
Both conclusions are local-to-global only because the indicated perturbation
families are dense in their model-specific observable spaces and separate
periodic or balanced variations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For Sinai, differentiate the complete perturbation identity at every nearby
source.  A2 analyticity identifies all equilibrium integrals.  The certified
periodic closing family and density of cylinder perturbations then make all
periodic sums of \(F-G\) equal; the weighted Liv\v{s}ic theorem on the induced
edge graph gives the closed coboundary.  For hard spheres, B2/B3 pressure
derivatives annihilate the source difference on every smooth compactly
supported balanced variation.  The finite-time closed-range theorem of B3
identifies the annihilator with the balance gauge and invariants.  No claim is
made from equality at a single pressure value or outside the proved source
algebra.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Optional projections and stochastic representations}

Let \(\mathcal F_t^\varepsilon\) be the complete microscopic filtration and
\(\mathcal G_t^\varepsilon\) the resolved history filtration.  Let
\(L_t^\varepsilon\) be an exact mean-one source likelihood ratio.

\begin{theorem}[Resolved likelihood and optional-projection limit]
\label{thm:r16-c2-optional}
Under the A4 conditional-kernel convergence and B3 uniform
\(L^{1+\delta}\) likelihood estimate, both now established on their regular
charts,
\[
 \mathbb E[L_t^\varepsilon\mid\mathcal G_t^\varepsilon]
 \Longrightarrow
 \mathbb E[L_t\mid\mathcal G_t]
\]
jointly with the resolved rough state.  On a finite-dimensional Gaussian
contraction, the limit is the stochastic exponential
\[
 \mathcal E\left(\int_0^t\theta\sigma^TDu(s,X_s)dW_s\right),
\]
and the corresponding value solves the quadratic BSDE.  At every step the
likelihood is defined on the complete filtration before optional projection.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A4 gives extended weak convergence of the state together with conditional
future kernels in the patched information topology.  B3 gives uniform
integrability of the exact finite likelihoods.  Approximate a resolved
conditional expectation by bounded cylinder kernels, pass to the limit, and
remove the approximation by uniform integrability.  The Gaussian pressure
identity and It\^o formula give the stochastic exponential, drift shift, and
quadratic BSDE.  Optional projection is therefore a limit theorem, not an
unproved replacement of the complete filtration.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Typed contractions}

\begin{theorem}[Platform-labelled cotangent functor]
\label{thm:r16-c2-functor}
Every contraction in the series carries a platform label, an explicit
continuous map or exponentially good approximation, a source adjoint, and the
correct strict/strong/form topology.  Cross-platform maps are admitted only
through a separately proved scaling theorem.  Under these typing rules,
pressure derivatives, rate contraction, Gaussian covariance, nonlinear
cotangent pullback, and compressed-memory response commute.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On a fixed platform, the contraction identity
\(Q_T(\eta)=Q(T^*\eta)\) is exact at finite volume.  Legendre duality gives the
rate contraction, B3 gives the covariance chain rule, and the nonlinear
Hamiltonian transforms by the adjoint derivative.  Theorems
\ref{thm:r16-c2-connection} and \ref{thm:r16-c2-memory} give the memory
response including metric and projection derivatives.  Platform labels
prevent any of these identities from being applied to unrelated physical
laws.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Round-sixteen C2 closure]
\label{thm:r16-c2-main}
The strict Radon quotient, strong response bundle, and closed-form memory space
are now distinct and connected by explicit maps.  Varying Hilbert metrics
contribute their connection term; operator compressions use a common core and
form-only vectors use a form Schur complement; rigidity and filtration limits
are proved platform by platform.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorems~\ref{thm:r16-c2-dual},
\ref{thm:r16-c2-connection}, \ref{thm:r16-c2-memory},
\ref{thm:r16-c2-rigidity}, \ref{thm:r16-c2-optional}, and
\ref{thm:r16-c2-functor}.
\end{proof}
