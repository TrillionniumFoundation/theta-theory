\section{A1: strong finite-cylinder response on a common reporting space}
\label{sec:r33-a1}

The distributional loss direction from earlier revisions is retained. The missing ingredient is a norm-convergent differentiability argument, not weak boundedness of difference quotients. We formulate a sufficient, checkable cylinder construction and prove its projection and covariance estimates. Its application to an unweighted geometric seam is distinguished from an explicitly depth-weighted benchmark.

\subsection{A strongly differentiable shell construction}
Let $H$ be a real separable Hilbert space. In a loss scale it is chosen once as the common reporting level $H=\mathbb J^{m+2r}$, with all lower-level jets embedded in $H$. Let $\mathcal A$ be a finite alphabet and let $w_i(\theta)$, $\theta\in K\Subset\mathbb R$, be $C^r$ probabilities with $w_i\ge c>0$. The reference law is the product $\mu_\theta=w(\theta)^{\mathbb Z}$. No Radon--Nikodym derivative between different infinite product laws is used.

For $n\ge0$, let $f_n(\theta,\omega)$ depend only on $\omega_{-n},\ldots,\omega_n$, be $C^r$ as an $H$-valued function for each word, and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r33-a1-shell}
 \max_{0\le j\le r}\sup_{\theta,\omega}
 \|\partial_\theta^j f_n(\theta,\omega)\|_H
 \le C(1+n)^b e^{-\lambda n}.
\end{equation}
Set $F_\theta=\sum_{n\ge0}f_n(\theta,\cdot)$. Boundary contributions, when present, must be included in these actual shell functions. The jet relation is defined only for $j<r$; the top-order jet is an endpoint of the scale, not an equation involving an undeclared $(r+1)$st derivative.

\begin{theorem}[Strong response without an infinite score density]
\label{thm:r33-a1-response}
The sums defining $F_\theta$ and its derivatives through order $r$ converge uniformly in $H$. The mean $m(\theta)=E_\theta F_\theta$ is $C^r$. Its derivatives are obtained by differentiating the finite sums
\[
 E_\theta f_n=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A^{2n+1}}
      f_n(\theta,a)\prod_{k=-n}^n w_{a_k}(\theta)
\]
and summing over $n$. These derivative sums converge uniformly in $H$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each order $j$, derivatives of a finite product probability divided by that product are Bell polynomials in the first $j$ log derivatives of the weights. Since the weights are bounded below, their absolute values are bounded by $C_j(1+n)^j$. The product probabilities sum to one. Leibniz' rule and \eqref{eq:r33-a1-shell} therefore bound the $j$th derivative of $E_\theta f_n$ by $C_j(1+n)^{b+j}e^{-\lambda n}$. The Weierstrass derivative theorem follows directly from the Banach-valued fundamental theorem of calculus: pass to the uniform limit in
$g_N(\theta+h)-g_N(\theta)=\int_0^h g_N'(\theta+s)ds$ and repeat for higher orders. This proves strong differentiability rather than inferring it from weak bounds. The same argument without expectation proves the uniform $H$-valued statement for $F$.
\end{proof}

If shells are denoted $b_{n,j}$ at derivative level $j$, their compatibility is $b_{n,j}=\partial_\theta^j b_{n,0}$ for $j\le r$, with \eqref{eq:r33-a1-shell} for these derivatives. Thus cumulative boundaries $B_j=\sum_n b_{n,j}$ have derivatives $B_j'=B_{j+1}$ for $j<r$. Independently specifying unrelated boundary arrays is not a substitute for this relation.

\subsection{Exact ancestry of the one-sided projections}
Write $\mathcal F_i^- =\sigma(\omega_k:k\le i)$ and $P_i^\theta Z=E_\theta[Z\mid\mathcal F_i^-]-E_\theta[Z\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}^-]$. Let the shift convention be $(\sigma^q\omega)_k=\omega_{k+q}$. For a depth-$n$ shell, $f_n\circ\sigma^q$ depends on the interval $[q-n,q+n]$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-cylinder projection support]
\label{lem:r33-a1-ancestry}
If $|q|>n$, then $P_0^\theta(f_n\circ\sigma^q)=0$. Hence, with $Y_q=F_\theta\circ\sigma^q-m(\theta)$,
\[
 \|P_0^\theta Y_q\|_{L^2(H)}
 \le 2C\sum_{n\ge|q|}(1+n)^b e^{-\lambda n}.
\]
In particular $\sum_q(1+|q|)^{1/2}\|P_0^\theta Y_q\|_2<\infty$ uniformly in $\theta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
When $q>n$, every coordinate is strictly positive. Product independence makes both conditional expectations equal to the same unconditional expectation. When $q<-n$, every coordinate is at most $-1$, so the shell is already $\mathcal F_{-1}^-$ measurable and both conditional expectations equal the shell. In the remaining cases conditional expectation is a contraction and its difference has norm at most twice the shell bound. Summation proves the estimate. This is the measurability calculation required by the ancestry claim.
\end{proof}

Set $D_0=\sum_qP_0Y_q$ and $D_i=D_0\circ\sigma^i$. The sum is uniformly convergent in $H$ and $D_i$ is a bounded stationary martingale-difference sequence.

\begin{lemma}[A uniformly bounded partial-sum remainder]
\label{lem:r33-a1-remainder}
There is a deterministic $C_*$ such that
\[
 \sup_{m\ge1}\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}Y_k-
                         \sum_{k=0}^{m-1}D_k\right\|_H\le C_*.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Prove the assertion first for one centered depth-$n$ shell $X_k$. The exact finite martingale decomposition is $X_k=\sum_{i=k-n}^{k+n}P_iX_k$. The corresponding martingale increment is $D_i^{(n)}=\sum_{k=i-n}^{i+n}P_iX_k$. Subtract the double sums over $0\le k<m$ and $0\le i<m$. All interior pairs cancel. Only pairs within distance $n$ of the two endpoints remain, at most $4(2n+1)^2$ pairs, each bounded by a constant times $\|f_n\|_\infty$. The resulting bound is independent of $m$. Summing over $n$ uses $\sum_n(1+n)^{b+2}e^{-\lambda n}<\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Continuous Hilbert limit and trace-norm response]
\label{thm:r33-a1-fclt}
The polygonal interpolation of $N^{-1/2}\sum_{k< Nt}Y_k$ converges in $C([0,1];H)$ to Brownian motion with covariance
\[
 Q_\theta=E_\theta[D_0\otimes D_0],\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}Q_\theta=E_\theta\|D_0\|_H^2.
\]
Moreover $\theta\mapsto Q_\theta$ is $C^r$ in the trace norm on this fixed reporting space.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The scalar martingale functional central limit theorem applied to finite-dimensional projections gives the finite-dimensional path limits: conditional brackets converge by ergodicity of the Bernoulli shift and bounded increments give Lindeberg. If $\Pi_d$ is an increasing sequence of finite-rank orthogonal projections, Doob's inequality bounds the maximal martingale tail by $4E\|(I-\Pi_d)D_0\|^2$, which tends to zero. This supplies Hilbert tightness. The bounded remainder lemma and bounded increments transfer the limit to the genuine polygonal path. The trace identity is Parseval and Tonelli. Hilbert martingale approximation in a more general setting is treated by Cuny--Merlevede, arXiv:1202.2964; only the elementary bounded-cylinder case is used here.

For covariance response, write $D_0=\sum_{n\ge0}d_n$, where $d_n=\sum_{|q|\le n}P_0(f_n\circ\sigma^q)$. Each conditional expectation is a finite sum over future coordinates, so its derivatives through order $r$ are bounded by $C_r(1+n)^{b+r}e^{-\lambda n}$. Thus $d_n$ depends only on $[-2n,0]$ and has a polynomial times exponential derivative bound. Expand $E_\theta(D_0\otimes D_0)=\sum_{n,l}E_\theta(d_n\otimes d_l)$. Leibniz' rule, the finite-product weight estimate and $\|x\otimes y\|_1=\|x\|\|y\|$ give a summable majorant $C(1+n+l)^B e^{-\lambda(n+l)}$ for every derivative. Uniform differentiation in the Banach space of trace-class operators proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A nonempty current-valued example with declared weights}
On the circle choose $s>r+1/2$ and $H=H^{-s}(\mathbb T)$. The Fourier coefficients of $\delta_x$ are $e^{-2\pi ikx}$, so
\[
 \sup_x\|\partial_x^j\delta_x\|_{H^{-s}}^2
 =\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}(1+4\pi^2k^2)^{-s}(2\pi k)^{2j}<\infty
 \quad (j\le r).
\]
For any finite-word locations $x_n(\omega_{-n:n})$ and $0<\gamma<1$, the explicitly defined observable
$F_\theta=\sum_n\gamma^n\delta_{\theta+x_n(\omega)}$ satisfies the theorem. The coefficients $\gamma^n$ are part of the observable; no probability factor is silently assigned to a geometric sheet.

\paragraph{Application boundary.}
To apply the result to the original, unweighted affine seam current one must construct its shell representation and prove \eqref{eq:r33-a1-shell} from the actual push-forward and incidence norms. The weighted example proves that the analytic theorem is nonempty, but does not establish that geometric identification. That identification remains recorded as a separate proof obligation.
