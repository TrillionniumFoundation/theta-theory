\section{A4: actual generation hypotheses and a fourth-order memory remainder}
\label{sec:r33-a4}

There are three different statements: contraction of measures in a cost, generation of a compressed semigroup, and a high-frequency expansion of its observed resolvent. This chapter proves the implications with their actual domains. It does not infer an $L^2$ gap from weak-Harris convergence or a global spectral bridge from boundedness of a potential.

\subsection{A norm transfer that uses the same distance}
Let $d$ be a measurable cost on a state space, let $V\ge1$, and suppose $\pi$ is invariant. Assume
\[
 W_d(P^n(x,\cdot),P^n(y,\cdot))\le C\rho^n d(x,y),\qquad
 \int d(x,y)\pi(dy)\le C_0V(x),\qquad \rho<1.
\]
Then for the space with norm $\|f\|=\sup|f|/V+\operatorname{Lip}_d(f)$,
\[
 \|P^nf-\pi f\|\le C_1\rho^n\operatorname{Lip}_d(f),
\]
whenever the integrals are defined. Indeed the coupled difference proves the Lipschitz part; integration of that difference against $\pi$ proves the weighted supremum part. Using a capped $d$ in the first estimate and an unrelated unbounded Holder seminorm in the conclusion is not this argument.

For iid append--shift histories over a finite alphabet, synchronous appending gives $d_\sigma(X_n,Y_n)=\sigma^nd_\sigma(x,y)$, with $d_\sigma=\sum_{j\ge0}\sigma^j1_{x_{-j}\ne y_{-j}}$. This is a nonempty example. For a potential depending on finitely many recent symbols, the transfer operator reduces on that finite-memory state to a primitive nonnegative matrix; a sufficiently high power has positive entries. Perron--Frobenius gives an isolated simple positive eigenvalue for every fixed finite potential. This finite-dimensional construction is not a proof of the required bridge on a singular countable billiard extension.

\subsection{A valid relative perturbation theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Dissipativity plus a range condition]
\label{thm:r33-a4-generation}
Let $L_0$ be a generator on a Hilbert space $\mathcal H$. Let $A:D(L_0)\to\mathcal H$ be $L_0$-bounded with relative bound strictly less than one. Assume $G=L_0+A$ satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle Gx,x\rangle\le-\omega'\|x\|^2
 \quad(x\in D(L_0)),
\]
and there exists a real $\lambda_*>-\omega'$ in $\rho(L_0)$ with
$\|A(\lambda_*-L_0)^{-1}\|<1$.
Then $G$ generates a semigroup with $\|e^{tG}\|\le e^{-\omega't}$. For every $z$ with $\|A(z-L_0)^{-1}\|<1$,
\[
 (z-G)^{-1}=(z-L_0)^{-1}[I-A(z-L_0)^{-1}]^{-1}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The relative bound less than one makes the graph norms of $L_0$ and $G$ equivalent and makes $G$ closed on the dense domain $D(L_0)$. Factor
$\lambda_*-G=[I-A(\lambda_*-L_0)^{-1}](\lambda_*-L_0)$. The Neumann series proves surjectivity. The shifted operator $G+\omega'I$ is dissipative and has full range at the positive real number $\lambda_*+\omega'$. The Lumer--Phillips theorem therefore makes it a contraction generator. The same factorization gives the stated resolvent. No differentiation of an arbitrary bounded resolvent is used to assert Hille--Yosida power estimates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A concrete domain-safe compression construction}
Let $L$ generate a strongly continuous semigroup on $\mathcal H$ and let $P$ be the orthogonal projection onto an orthonormal finite family $e_1,\ldots,e_d\in D(L)\cap D(L^*)$. Put $Q=I-P$. Then $P$ preserves $D(L)$,
\[
 B=QLP:P\mathcal H\to Q\mathcal H
\]
is bounded, and $C=PLQ$ extends boundedly to $Q\mathcal H$ by the coordinates $\langle Qx,L^*e_j\rangle$.

\begin{proposition}[Compression generation without a graph-angle assertion]
\label{prop:r33-a4-compression}
The operator $L_Q=QLQ$ on $QD(L)$ generates a strongly continuous semigroup on $Q\mathcal H$. If additionally
$\operatorname{Re}\langle Lq,q\rangle\le-\omega\|q\|^2$ for all $q\in QD(L)$, then $\|e^{tL_Q}\|\le e^{-\omega t}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The off-diagonal operator $B+C$ is bounded on $\mathcal H$, so the genuine bounded perturbation theorem applies to $L-(B+C)$. Its domain splits as $PD(L)\oplus QD(L)$ and it is block diagonal there, with blocks $PLP$ and $L_Q$. Both blocks therefore generate semigroups. The dissipativity inequality for $L_Q+\omega I$, together with the range condition at a sufficiently large real number supplied by generation, gives maximal dissipativity by Lumer--Phillips. This proves the decay estimate. Decay on $D(L_Q^k)$ with its graph norm follows from $L_Q^je^{tL_Q}x=e^{tL_Q}L_Q^jx$.
\end{proof}

This proposition constructs the complementary domain, but does not assert that every physical billiard projection satisfies the coercivity inequality. In particular, weak mixing of an invertible measure-preserving flow does not supply dissipativity on its $L^2$ orthogonal complement.

\subsection{Observed memory and the exact remainder}
Assume the stable compressed generator above, $B\in\mathcal L(P\mathcal H,D(L_Q^4))$ in the graph norm, and $C\in\mathcal L(Q\mathcal H,P\mathcal H)$. For $x\in D(L)$ the block equations and variation of constants give
\[
 u'(t)=PLPu(t)+\int_0^tCe^{(t-s)L_Q}Bu(s)ds+Ce^{tL_Q}Qx,
 \qquad u(t)=Pe^{tL}x.
\]
Thus the memory kernel is $K(t)=Ce^{tL_Q}B$, including its generic nonzero leading coefficient.

\begin{theorem}[Vertical half-plane expansion with the required input order]
\label{thm:r33-a4-memory}
For $\operatorname{Re}z\ge\sigma> -\omega$,
\[
 \widehat K(z)=C(z-L_Q)^{-1}B
 =z^{-1}CB+z^{-2}CL_QB+z^{-3}CL_Q^2B+O(|z|^{-4}).
\]
The remainder is uniform as $|z|\to\infty$ in this half-plane. On the common resolvent domain the Schur complement is
\[
 P(z-L)^{-1}P=[z-PLP-C(z-L_Q)^{-1}B]^{-1}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The semigroup Laplace transform gives $\|(z-L_Q)^{-1}\|\le(\sigma+\omega)^{-1}$. For $y\in D(L_Q^4)$, four applications of the resolvent identity give
\[
 (z-L_Q)^{-1}y=\sum_{j=0}^3z^{-j-1}L_Q^jy
                  +z^{-4}(z-L_Q)^{-1}L_Q^4y.
\]
After applying $C$ and $B$, the $j=3$ term and the final term are both $O(|z|^{-4})$, since $C$ is Hilbert-bounded and the fourth graph norm of $B$ is bounded. This is the advertised order. With only $D(L_Q^3)$ the final bounded-resolvent estimate would have order $|z|^{-3}$. Block Gaussian elimination proves the Schur identity.
\end{proof}

For the concrete two-dimensional generator
$L=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&c\\-c&-2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $P$ the first coordinate, $L_Q=-2$, $B=-c$, $C=c$, so $K(t)=-c^2e^{-2t}$ and $\widehat K(z)=-c^2/(z+2)$. The expansion is $-c^2z^{-1}+2c^2z^{-2}-4c^2z^{-3}+O(z^{-4})$, an exact nonempty test of the signs and powers.

\paragraph{Mechanical application still to be established.}
The original history-space weighted coupling, the actual physical projection domains, strip bounds for the suspension entrance/exit maps, and exposing-potential bridges must be verified on the billiard model. They are not consequences of the abstract lemmas proved here. Hairer--Mattingly--Scheutzow, PTRF 149 (2011), 223--259, supplies the appropriate weak-Harris framework, not an automatic gap in every chosen function norm.
