\section{B2: an integrable analytic-scale evolution and honest geometric dependencies}
\label{sec:r33-b2}

\subsection{What is proved and what it does not presuppose}
The Round-32 report identifies two different failures: a divergent radius-allocation integral, and an unproved all-genealogy collision-rank statement. They must not be repaired by the same assertion. This chapter proves the analytic evolution theorem, including a quantitative tail, endpoint control, uniqueness and stability. A separate finite-dimensional tangent calculation identifies the additional rank that a collision application must establish. Neither calculation assumes the hard-sphere LDP it is intended to support.

The method is the linear scale-of-spaces construction associated with Ovsyannikov; see Finkelshtein, \emph{Around Ovsyannikov's method}, arXiv:1412.8628, for related abstract evolution theory. We give the proof used here rather than invoking the name as a missing step.

\subsection{Compatible Banach scales}
Let $(E_a)_{a\in I}$ be Banach spaces embedded in a common Hausdorff vector space, with compatible contractive injections $E_a\hookrightarrow E_b$ when $a>b$. All operator identities below are identities after these injections. Suppose $C(t):E_a\to E_b$ is strongly measurable and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r33-b2-cauchy}
 \|C(t)\|_{E_a\to E_b}\le \frac{M}{a-b},\qquad a>b,
\end{equation}
with the same $M$ on a fixed finite radius interval. Products on intermediate spaces are assumed compatible. For clarity the initial proof has identity free evolution. Contractive free propagators can be inserted between the factors if they preserve every $E_a$, satisfy the evolution law and are compatible with the injections.

Fix $a_0>a_*>0$, $T>0$, and write
\[
 \Delta=a_0-a_*,\qquad q=\frac{eMT}{\Delta}<1.
\]
For $u_0\in E_{a_0}$ define the ordered terms
\[
 U_0(t)u_0=u_0,\qquad
 U_n(t)u_0=\int_{0<t_1<\cdots<t_n<t}
 C(t_n)\cdots C(t_1)u_0\,dt_1\cdots dt_n.
\]
The integral is a Bochner integral in $E_{a_*}$, defined using the compatible intermediate maps.

\begin{theorem}[Equal-radius allocation before time integration]
\label{thm:r33-b2-scale}
For $0\le t\le T$ and $n\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r33-b2-term}
 \|U_n(t)\|_{E_{a_0}\to E_{a_*}}
 \le \frac{n^n}{n!}\left(\frac{Mt}{\Delta}\right)^n
 \le \left(\frac{eMt}{\Delta}\right)^n.
\end{equation}
Consequently $U(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}U_n(t)$ converges uniformly on $[0,T]$ in the operator norm $E_{a_0}\to E_{a_*}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r33-b2-tail}
 \sup_{t\le T}\|U(t)\|\le\frac1{1-q},\qquad
 \sup_{t\le T}\Big\|\sum_{n>K}U_n(t)\Big\|
 \le\frac{q^{K+1}}{1-q}.
\end{equation}
Choose any $b$ with $a_*<b<a_0-eMT$. Then $u(t)=U(t)u_0$ is bounded in $E_b$ and satisfies
\[
 u(t)=u_0+\int_0^t C(s)u(s)\,ds
\]
as an identity in $E_{a_*}$. In particular there is nonzero endpoint control at $t=T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the term with exactly $n$ operators choose, before integrating any times,
\[
 a_j=a_0-j\Delta/n,\qquad j=0,\ldots,n.
\]
Every factor costs at most $Mn/\Delta$. This bound is independent of the time coordinates. The ordered simplex has volume $t^n/n!$, proving the first inequality in \eqref{eq:r33-b2-term}. The elementary estimate $n!\ge(n/e)^n$ gives the second. The geometric sum proves \eqref{eq:r33-b2-tail}.

Repeating the argument with target radius $b$ proves uniform convergence in $E_b$, because $eMT<a_0-b$. The map $C(s):E_b\to E_{a_*}$ is bounded by the fixed constant $M/(b-a_*)$. Therefore the normally convergent series may be integrated term by term in $E_{a_*}$. Fubini on the finite ordered simplices yields $\int_0^t C(s)U_n(s)ds=U_{n+1}(t)$. Summing gives the equation. None of these integrals contains $(t-s)^{-1}$. The final norm is the fixed norm of $E_{a_*}$, not a norm multiplied by a factor vanishing at $T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniqueness in the compatible bounded-solution class]
\label{prop:r33-b2-unique}
Suppose two compatible scale-valued solutions of the Volterra equation have the same initial value and are uniformly bounded in $E_b$ on $[0,T]$, for some $b>a_*$. Suppose also that their equations hold at every lower radius. They agree on $[0,T]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $w$ be their difference and choose $c<b$. On an interval of length $h<(b-c)/(eM)$ starting at a time where $w=0$, substitute the homogeneous integral equation into itself $n$ times. Equal allocation of the radius gap $b-c$ gives
\[
 \sup_{t\le h}\|w(t)\|_{E_c}
 \le B\left(\frac{eMh}{b-c}\right)^n,
\]
where $B=\sup\|w\|_{E_b}$. Let $n\to\infty$. Injectivity into the common ambient space implies equality also as $E_b$ elements. Thus the argument restarts at the right endpoint. Finitely many such intervals cover $[0,T]$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform limits and continuation with an actual reserve}
\begin{theorem}[Stability under a uniform scale bound]
\label{thm:r33-b2-stability}
Let $C_\varepsilon$ satisfy \eqref{eq:r33-b2-cauchy} with the same $M$. Suppose $u_{0,\varepsilon}\to u_0$ in $E_{a_0}$ and that, for every fixed $n$, $U_{n,\varepsilon}(t)u_0\to U_{n,0}(t)u_0$ uniformly on $[0,T]$ in $E_{a_*}$. Then
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}\|U_\varepsilon(t)u_{0,\varepsilon}-U_0(t)u_0\|_{E_{a_*}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
In particular finite-order convergence plus this verified geometric tail is sufficient; finite-order convergence alone is not.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The initial-value error is bounded by $(1-q)^{-1}\|u_{0,\varepsilon}-u_0\|$. For the common initial value split the series at $K$. The finite sum converges by hypothesis, whereas the sum of the two tails is at most $2q^{K+1}\|u_0\|/(1-q)$. First let $\varepsilon\to0$, then $K\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

For a time-dependent display radius one may take $a(t)=a_0-\Lambda t$, with $\Lambda>eM$, and choose a \emph{separate} target radius $0<a_*<a_0-\Lambda T$. These are independent choices; $a_*$ is not defined to equal $a_0-\Lambda T$. The total reserve must be tracked across all slices. The estimate does not imply arbitrary-time evolution at a fixed reserve, and it does not imply that the actual hard-sphere creation constant remains uniform as $a_0\to\infty$.

\subsection{A computable benchmark and the sharp logical limitation}
On analytic functions in disks, Cauchy's estimate gives \eqref{eq:r33-b2-cauchy} for $C=\partial_z$. The solution is $u(t,z)=u_0(z+t)$. For $u_0(z)=(R-z)^{-1}$ with $R>a_0$, the ordered terms are $t^n(R-z)^{-n-1}$ and the series fails when a translated pole enters the target disk. The theorem gives a sufficient interval $eMT<\Delta$; it does not incorrectly promise the entire exponential $\exp(MT/\Delta)$ for every time.

\subsection{Tangent projection is not a proof of surplus rank}
Let $F:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^r$ be the tree constraints, of rank $r$ at $z$, and let $G:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^s$ be the additional closures. Given a right inverse $R$ of $DF(z)$, define
\[
 \Pi_T=I-RDF(z),\qquad DF(z)\Pi_T=0.
\]
This constructs tangent directions but gives no lower bound for $DG(z)\Pi_T$.

\begin{proposition}[The exact missing rank condition]
\label{prop:r33-b2-rank}
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $DG(z)|_{\ker DF(z)}$ is onto $\mathbb R^s$;
\item the stacked derivative $D(F,G)(z)$ has rank $r+s$;
\item there are $s$ tangent vectors $X_1,\ldots,X_s$ with $\det(DG(z)X_j)\ne0$.
\end{enumerate}
These statements imply $s\le d-r$. On a compact family, a uniform lower bound follows only if the smallest singular value of the restricted derivative has a positive infimum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Split $\mathbb R^d=\ker DF\oplus V$, where $DF|_V$ is invertible onto its range. Elementary row elimination in the block derivative subtracts $DG|_V(DF|_V)^{-1}$ times the tree rows. The remaining lower block is exactly $DG|_{\ker DF}$. This proves the rank equivalence. Surjectivity supplies preimages of a basis and hence the determinant criterion. Conversely such vectors prove surjectivity. The compact statement is continuity of singular values, provided positivity holds at every point, not just at selected examples.
\end{proof}

Even $F(x,y)=x$, $G(x,y)=x$ has a perfect tree right inverse and zero surplus derivative on every tangent. Thus no bound for the tree inverse, and no naming of grazing strata, can replace the missing stacked-rank argument. For a collision application one must enumerate repeated labels and all dependencies, prove the restricted rank for the relevant graphs, and track its $\varepsilon$-dependence. Counting vertices does not by itself bound the number of repeated contacts.

\subsection{Cut kernels, connectedness and iterated cutoffs}
For standard Borel cut spaces, transition \emph{kernels} compose by
\[
 (K_1K_2)(x,A)=\int K_2(y,A)K_1(x,dy).
\]
They may be singular; in particular the deterministic kernel $\delta_{\Phi(x)}$ is allowed. Tonelli proves associativity for positive kernels. A signed-kernel version requires a finite variation bound ensuring absolute integrability. This is preferable to assuming every deterministic cut kernel has a Lebesgue density.

Composition in time is not disjoint union of labelled graphs. A logarithm for the composition product is not automatically the cumulant logarithm. For example the degree-two term of $\log(I+A)$ is $-A^2/2$, even when $A^2$ is just two successive transitions rather than a connected correlation. Connected cluster identification requires the separate set-partition/cumulant combinatorics of the generating functional.

When a singular estimate has the form
\[
 E_{K,L,\delta}(\varepsilon)\le C_{K,L,\delta}\varepsilon^\alpha
       +b_K(\delta)+t_K(L),
\]
with $b_K(\delta)\to0$ and $t_K(L)\to0$, valid qualitative convergence follows by holding $K,L,\delta$ fixed while $\varepsilon\to0$, then sending $\delta\downarrow0$, $L\uparrow\infty$ and finally the summable graph cutoff $K\uparrow\infty$. Substituting $L=\sqrt{|\log\varepsilon|}$ needs a quantitative growth bound for $C_{K,L,\delta}$ and is not implicit in this argument.

\paragraph{Mechanical application still to be established.}
The original all-genealogy hard-sphere pressure and joint contact/history LDP remain research targets. This chapter repairs the evolution and cutoff logic but does not claim that the required stacked rank, signed cluster estimates, all-source exponential tightness or positive recovery have thereby been proved. The primary hard-sphere results of Bodineau--Gallagher--Saint-Raymond--Simonella (Annals of Mathematics 198 (2023), 1047--1201) and their real-trajectory cluster construction (arXiv:2205.04110) are relevant imports; neither is cited here as automatically proving this dossier's stronger joint history theorem.
