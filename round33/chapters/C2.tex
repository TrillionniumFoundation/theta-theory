\section{C2. Policy-aware likelihoods and evidence-weighted prediction stability}
\label{sec:r33-c2}

Likelihood identities are proved for chronological joint laws, not for an
incorrect conditioning on all future adaptive actions.  Finite-horizon
prediction stability is obtained by an explicit coupling calculation.  Its
hypotheses concern observation likelihoods in $L^1$, which cannot be replaced
by weak convergence alone.  Statements on varying stochastic filtrations are
not inferred from stability on one fixed stochastic basis.

\subsection{The weighted strict-duality interface}

For a Polish space $E$, let $\beta_0$ denote the mixed strict topology on
$C_b(E)$, generated by
$p_{K,a}(g)=\sup_n a_n\sup_{x\in K_n}|g(x)|$ for arbitrary compact sets
$K_n$ and $a_n\downarrow0$.  There is no compact exhaustion requirement.
The following proof uses Hahn--Banach and the compact-space Riesz theorem,
not a compact exhaustion of $E$.  This is the classical strict-duality
result; it is not a new Banach--Dieudonne assertion.

\begin{theorem}[Strict dual and weighted pullback]
\label{prop:r33-c2-strict}
The continuous dual of $(C_b(E),\beta_0)$ is the finite signed Radon
measures.  For continuous $W\ge1$, define $C_W=\{f:f/W\in C_b(E)\}$ with
the pullback topology under $Tf=f/W$.  Its dual is
$\{\mu:\int W\,d|\mu|<\infty\}$, acting by integration.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A continuous linear functional $\ell$ is bounded by a constant times a finite
maximum of generating seminorms.  Interleave their compact sequences and
weights to obtain one seminorm
$p(g)=\sup_n a_n\|g|_{K_n}\|_\infty$, with $a_n>0$ and $a_n\to0$,
that dominates this maximum.  The map
$Jg=(a_ng|_{K_n})_n$ takes $C_b(E)$ into the Banach space
$c_0(C(K_n))$ and has norm $p(g)$.  Thus $\ell$ factors through the range
of $J$ as a bounded functional.  Hahn--Banach extends it to the full
$c_0$ sum.  The dual of that sum is the $\ell^1$ sum of the coordinate
duals: restriction to finitely many coordinates proves the sum-of-norms
bound, and approximation by finite-coordinate vectors proves the
representation.  Compact-space Riesz consequently supplies measures
$\lambda_n$ on $K_n$ with $\sum_n\|\lambda_n\|<\infty$ and
\[
 \ell(g)=\sum_n a_n\int_{K_n}g\,d\lambda_n.
\]
Their push-forwards give a finite Radon measure
$\mu=\sum_n a_n\lambda_n$ on $E$.  Radon tightness follows by first
truncating this absolutely convergent measure sum and then using compact
regularity of its finitely many terms.

Conversely, given a finite Radon $\mu$, choose increasing compact sets $K_n$
with $|\mu|(E\setminus K_n)\le2^{-3n}$ and let $K_0=\varnothing$.
Put $a_n=2^{-n}$.  The union of these compact sets has full $|\mu|$ measure,
although it need not be all of $E$.  Then
\[
 \left|\int g\,d\mu\right|
 \le p_{K,a}(g)\sum_n 2^n|\mu|(K_n\setminus K_{n-1}) = C_\mu p_{K,a}(g),\quad C_\mu<\infty.
\]
This proves continuity.  Finally $T$ is a linear homeomorphism.  Transporting
the represented functional back gives $d\mu=W^{-1}d\nu$ and
$\int Wd|\mu|=|\nu|(E)$; the reverse direction uses $d\nu=Wd\mu$.
No step assumes a proper weight or local compactness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Action factors and parameter-specific filters}

Let $\pi_k(da\mid y_{1:k-1},a_{1:k-1})$ be the same parameter-independent
policy kernel under both hypotheses.  Let $\Pi_{k-1}^\theta$ be the filter
computed recursively with parameter $\theta$, and let
$r_\theta(\Pi_{k-1}^\theta,a,y)$ be its predictive observation density.
Assume the relevant predictive laws are mutually absolutely continuous.

\begin{proposition}[Chronological likelihood identity]
\label{prop:r33-c2-likelihood}
On the sigma-field generated by the first $n$ actions and observations,
\[
 L_n(\theta,\theta_0)=
 \prod_{k=1}^n
 \frac{r_\theta(\Pi_{k-1}^\theta,A_k,Y_k)}
      {r_{\theta_0}(\Pi_{k-1}^{\theta_0},A_k,Y_k)}
\]
is the Radon--Nikodym likelihood martingale.  If the two policies themselves
differ with the parameter, their conditional action Radon--Nikodym factors
must multiply this expression.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Disintegrate the joint law in the order $A_1,Y_1,A_2,Y_2,\ldots$.
At step $k$ the common policy factor is identical at the observed past, and
cancels; the predictive observation factors give the quotient displayed.
Conditional integration of each likelihood increment under $\theta_0$ gives
one, proving the martingale identity.  If the action kernels differ, that
conditional quotient cannot be cancelled.  Also the two filters generally
already differ after $Y_1$, so one common filter cannot replace them.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Prediction of full paths without a cylinder-density shortcut}

Let $\mathbb D=D([0,T];E_X)$ have a specified Polish Skorokhod topology.
Consider a coupling of signal paths $(X_n,X)$ and nonnegative conditional
densities $g_{n,t}(X_n,y),g_t(X,y)$ for the observed past up to time $t$
relative to a common measure $\nu_t$.  Each density integrates to one for
almost every signal path.  An adaptive experiment can be treated this way
only after its joint conditional observation law, including its policy, has
actually been constructed.
Set
\[
 \epsilon_{n,t}=E\int|g_{n,t}(X_n,y)-g_t(X,y)|\,\nu_t(dy),
 \quad b_n=E|F(X_n)-F(X)|
\]
for bounded measurable $F:\mathbb D\to\mathbb R$.

\begin{theorem}[Evidence-weighted conditional prediction bound]
\label{thm:r33-c2-evidence}
Write $r_n(y)=E g_{n,t}(X_n,y)$, $r(y)=E g_t(X,y)$,
$N_n(y)=E[F(X_n)g_{n,t}(X_n,y)]$, and
$N(y)=E[F(X)g_t(X,y)]$.  With arbitrary versions on zero-evidence sets,
\[
 \int r_n(y)\left|\frac{N_n(y)}{r_n(y)}-
                         \frac{N(y)}{r(y)}\right|\nu_t(dy)
 \le b_n+2\|F\|_\infty\epsilon_{n,t}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Where $r>0$,
$r_n|N_n/r_n-N/r|\le|N_n-N|+\|F\|_\infty|r_n-r|$.
Where $r=0$, choose a version bounded by $\|F\|_\infty$ and the same bound
holds.  The evidence difference integrates to at most $\epsilon_{n,t}$.
Decompose the numerator difference as
$E[(F(X_n)-F(X))g_{n,t}]+E[F(X)(g_{n,t}-g_t)]$.
Normalization of $g_{n,t}$ gives the bound
$b_n+\|F\|_\infty\epsilon_{n,t}$ for its integral.
\end{proof}

If $X_n\to X$ almost surely in the coupling and $F$ is bounded continuous,
then $b_n\to0$ by dominated convergence.  No approximation of arbitrary
Skorokhod-continuous functions by discontinuous evaluation cylinders is
needed.  If $\sup_t\epsilon_{n,t}\to0$, the displayed estimate is uniform
in the expectations at each time; it is \emph{not} by itself an estimate of
the expected supremum over time.  Process convergence of optional projections
still requires tightness of those processes and identification of their
finite-dimensional distributions.

\subsection{What is needed for second characteristics}

\begin{lemma}[Weak kernels plus genuine uniform integrability]
\label{lem:r33-c2-moments}
Let probability kernels $K_n(x,dz)$ on $\mathbb R^d$ converge weakly to
$K(x,dz)$ uniformly for $x$ in a compact set, and suppose
$\sup_{n,x}\int|z|^{2+\delta}K_n(x,dz)<\infty$ for some $\delta>0$.
Then their first and second moment matrices converge uniformly on that set,
provided the limiting truncated moment maps are continuous in $x$ in the
stated uniform-weak topology.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Replace $z_iz_j$ by a continuous compactly supported truncation.  Uniform
weak convergence passes the truncated integral to the limit.  The discarded
part is at most $CR^{-\delta}$ uniformly.  The first moment is easier.
First let $n\to\infty$ and then $R\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

A bounded second moment alone does not suffice: the laws
$(1-n^{-2})\delta_0+n^{-2}\delta_n$ converge weakly to $\delta_0$ but keep
second moment one.  Even with the lemma, predictable characteristics require
convergence after integration against the actual predictable state process;
weak convergence of a different cylinder kernel is not that assertion.

\subsection{A fully specified stochastic stability theorem}

Fix one complete right-continuous filtration generated by a Brownian motion
$B$ and an independent Poisson random measure $N$ of deterministic intensity
$dt\,\lambda(de)$, with its usual augmentation.  Use this same basis for all
indices.  Suppose it has the martingale representation property.  Define
$\mathcal S^2$, $\mathcal H_B^2$, and $\mathcal H_N^2$ by
\[
 E\sup_{t\le T}|Y_t|^2,
 \qquad E\int_0^T|Z_t|^2dt,
 \qquad E\int_0^T\!\int|U_t(e)|^2\lambda(de)dt.
\]
Let the BSDEs have terminal data $\xi_n\to\xi$ in $L^2$ and drivers
$f_n(t,y,z,u)$ uniformly Lipschitz in $(y,z,u)$ with respect to
$|y|+|z|+\|u\|_{L^2(\lambda)}$.  Assume square integrability at zero and
\[
 E\int_0^T|f_n(t,Y_t,Z_t,U_t)-f(t,Y_t,Z_t,U_t)|^2dt\to0
\]
at the solution of the limiting equation.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-basis Brownian--jump BSDE stability]
\label{thm:r33-c2-bsde}
The solution differences tend to zero in
$\mathcal S^2\times\mathcal H_B^2\times\mathcal H_N^2$.
The purely Brownian assertion is obtained by omitting $N$; the purely
Poisson assertion is obtained by omitting $B$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Subtract the two equations and apply the jump It\^o formula to
$e^{ct}|Y_t^n-Y_t|^2$.  The continuous and jump quadratic variations give
positive $\int|Z^n-Z|^2$ and $\int\|U^n-U\|^2$ terms.  Split the driver
error into its Lipschitz part and the displayed error evaluated at the limit.
Young's inequality absorbs half the two integrand norms; choose $c$ above
the remaining Lipschitz constant.  The resulting estimate bounds these
integrals and $\sup_t E|Y_t^n-Y_t|^2$ by
$C(E|\xi_n-\xi|^2+E\int|\delta f_n(t)|^2dt)$.
The Burkholder--Davis--Gundy inequality in the difference equation, followed
by Young's inequality once more, gives the same bound for
$E\sup_t|Y_t^n-Y_t|^2$.  This tends to zero.
\end{proof}

For a fixed finite discrete filtration, the analogous backward recursion is
proved by backward induction and contraction of conditional expectation in
$L^2$; a general discrete martingale difference must not be forced into a
Brownian integrand.  Across changing filtrations one instead needs extended
convergence, compatible representation spaces, and uniform integrability of
likelihoods.  Those are additional application hypotheses, not conclusions
of the theorem above.

\subsection{A finite-graph rigidity check and the unresolved extension}

On a finite strongly connected directed graph, an edge function $\phi$ is of
the form $\phi(i,j)=u(j)-u(i)$ if and only if its sum around every directed
cycle vanishes.  To prove sufficiency, fix a root, define $u(i)$ by the sum
along any path from the root to $i$, and append a common return path to compare
two such paths.  Their closed-walk sums vanish, so $u$ is well defined.
This gives a complete finite-state coboundary test.  It does not construct the
separated Riesz contours or the singular billiard Livsic transfer required
by the original pressure-rigidity programme.  Those extensions, as well as
full mechanical path-prediction tightness, remain explicitly identified
application obligations.
