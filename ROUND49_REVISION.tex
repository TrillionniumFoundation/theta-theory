\title[Bounded probes and logarithmic depth]{Logarithmic-Depth Boundary Identification with Bounded Probes and Retained State}
\author{Qian Qi}
\date{September 5, 2026. Round 49 revision responding to Round 48.}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We recover an infinite damped Jacobi lattice from one noisy boundary coordinate.
The fixed bounded-duration sampling law already permits logarithmic recovery
depth without resetting the state or using long individual probes. The new
construction commits the bounded baseline within a diagnostic block, while
allowing arbitrary feedback between blocks. A convergent sampled-generator
logarithm transfers delayed short-pulse responses to physical coefficient
jets with exponential depth cost. A block-conditional information inequality
then applies to the exact likelihood of every readout, not a likelihood
conditioned on successful blocks. For explicit constants $C_b,p_b>0$, depths
$J_n=\lfloor r\log n\rfloor$ and coefficient errors $n^{-s}$ are attainable
when $C_b r+p_b s<1$, in deterministically linear elapsed time. An all-policy
two-way propagation bound supplies the matching logarithmic depth order.
A variance-sensitive finite-sample posterior inequality also enlarges the
previous unrestricted-feedback sufficient regions from exponent $1/2$ to $1$.
We distinguish sampling probabilities from confidence allocations, account for
input-enclosure error in certified outer sets, and give executable exact-rational
certificates. Optimal leading constants and a matching joint exponent curve
are not asserted.
\end{abstract}
