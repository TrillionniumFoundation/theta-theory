## Mathematical change

One conditional endpoint law per oblique marked phase now determines both the complete smooth reflecting contact germ and its fixed, previously unreported positive offset. No second offset, acceptance probability, flux amplitude or curvature is supplied. The two endpoint actions need not be equal. The anchored single-law quotient cancels the normalization and both endpoint amplitudes; its mixed derivative at the origin is the marked squared momentum divided by the squared offset. The known signed slopes then fix the remaining rank-one ambiguity. Fixed-slice reconstruction preserves the finite differentiated topology required by the actual smooth envelope inverse.

The new finite theorem estimates profiles and offsets jointly on the realizable image, with one group per phase and every failed preparation charged. A first-collision argument proves that every clear periodic polygon with at least three distinct physical contacts is oblique. The original general two-known-offset theorem, the older symmetric one-known-offset inverse, and the complete analytic/multichannel catalogue are retained.

The offsets are fixed nuisance constants in a supplied positive interval, not random clock drift. Polygon/frame marks, exact event tags and gates, independent resets, finite smoothness bounds and uniform physical margins remain hypotheses. This is actual smooth local contact determination, including flat differences, not an unmarked whole-table theorem. The unchanged corrected planar lens comparison is not presented as same-data superiority.
