\title[Effective adaptive Jacobi identification]{Effective Adaptive Boundary Identification of Infinite Damped Jacobi Lattices}
\author{Qian Qi}
\date{September 5, 2026}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We study identification of a damped half-line lattice from a single noisy
boundary coordinate under chronological feedback. For a homogeneous bath,
six short response values identify five physical coefficients. Finite-prefix
calibration and fresh signs give uniform contrast without resetting the
state, leading to a random-information quasi-Bernstein--von Mises theorem,
strong filter jets, and a functional posterior for the memory kernel.
For a fully unknown Jacobi stiffness sequence, we derive a constructive
response-jet inverse with condition bound \(Q^{64(J+1)^2}\) through depth
\(J\), using a finite moment recursion and explicitly bounded Gram solves.
A multiscale sampling law then yields response separation at least
\(\exp\{-A_0(J+1)^3\}\delta^{20(J+1)}\).
Policy-uniform finite-sample likelihood bounds give shrinking coefficient
recovery for \(J_n=o((\log n)^{1/3})\) and contraction in an exponentially
weighted operator norm. The washed experiment has a full posterior LDP;
a no-washout experiment supports arbitrary bounded adaptive baselines in
linear physical time. In the latter, a posterior Laplace-tracking theorem
identifies full LDPs along convergent conditional-information subsequences,
while preserving an unconditional information-floor upper bound.
Simultaneous frequentist confidence cylinders are valid at all observation
times by a stopped exponential-supermartingale argument.
\end{abstract}
