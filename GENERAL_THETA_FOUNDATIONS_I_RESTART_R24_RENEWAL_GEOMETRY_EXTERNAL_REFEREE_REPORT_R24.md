## Recommendation

**Reject at the level of the Annals of Mathematics, Inventiones Mathematicae, the Journal of the American Mathematical Society, or Acta Mathematica in the manuscript's present form.**

This is **not a technical-correctness rejection**. Revision R24 is a genuine and substantial advance over R22. The renewal passage is mathematically coherent. Finite-history product domination supplies continuity of repeated Borel report rows on distinct report coordinates. A controller-uniform return-tail envelope gives continuity of cycle reward and length, robust average-risk attainment, finite-model determination, and explicit overshoot and Abel bounds. A uniform `1+p` return moment yields the stated `N^{-p}` and `(1-beta)^p` orders, and the rare-long-cycle examples establish their sharpness over the declared moment class. The cycle-defect theorem correctly compares both reward and length and produces the sharp exponent `p/(1+p)`, rather than asserting a generally false linear average-risk perturbation bound.

The occupation-geometric layer is also meaningful. The checkpoint lower uses the actual renewal-occupation submeasure, not the preparation law or an ambient reachable set. The online upper is implemented by one finite recursive cover under an all-candidate stability condition. The hidden-state and quantum examples derive their predictive coordinates and acquired mass from raw likelihoods and positive instruments, including noncontractive propagation and genuine rank-changing mass. For a strong specialist journal in stochastic control, probability, information theory, robust POMDPs, quantization, controller synthesis, or mathematical quantum information, my assessment is **favorable after a focused major revision**.

## 2. Executive assessment of Revision R24

R24 changes the long-run interface in a mathematically significant way. R22 considered one stationary finite-state controller under a strictly discounted continuing objective. R24 introduces a cycle structure with all of the following explicitly included:

1. a charged physical preparation;
2. a visible cycle boundary;
3. compulsory overwrite of the observer state to a distinguished label;
4. no intercycle seed, counter, or retained record;
5. independent physical innovations and controller randomization across cycles conditional on one fixed inaccessible model parameter; and
6. actual cycle length, preparation calls, audit marks, and reset time in the resource ledger.

For a fixed program and model, the cycle pairs `(tau_j,R_j)` are therefore iid. The average risk is the renewal-reward ratio

```text
g_theta(gamma) = E_theta^gamma R / E_theta^gamma tau.
```

The principal theorem then establishes four logically distinct conclusions:

- compact attainment of the robust average value at fixed `M`;
- equality with the supremum of finite-model robust values;
- a uniform comparison with the expected `N`-call average; and
- a uniform comparison with normalized discounted risk.

The proof does not infer these statements from a pointwise discounted theorem. It first proves continuity of finite responses under reuse of the same report row, then uses a uniform tail certificate to pass from truncated cycle functionals to the full ratio. The finite-acquisition comparison is proved by a stopped centered-cycle sum and an overshoot estimate. The discount comparison is then an Abel mixture of the finite-acquisition averages.

This is a real theorem. Its precise scope is equally important: it is an average theorem for an experiment that itself resets the observer. It is not an average theorem for an arbitrary nonreset finite-state controller, nor a proof that physical mixing erases controller memory.

The other principal contribution is the coupling of renewal time with actual acquired geometry. The relevant measure is

```text
nu = (1 / E tau) E sum_{data calls t <= tau} delta_{p_t},
```

not the preparation law. Checkpoint prediction is exactly quantization of this submeasure. Every online `M`-state predictor has the same quantization lower, while a global all-candidate cover and an occupation-weighted stability estimate give an implementable recursive upper. This identifies a genuine length-bias phenomenon and supports singular and heavy-tailed examples.

## 4. Audit of the exact renewal theorem

### 4.1 The reset protocol is mathematically load-bearing

The paper does not merely assume that the physical system regenerates. It also overwrites the observer state and forbids retained random seeds or counters. This makes the complete cycle pairs iid conditional on the fixed model and fixed program.

This condition is essential. With physical regeneration but no observer overwrite, a finite controller can retain information about the fixed parameter across cycles. The cycle pairs then need not be iid under the augmented controller state, and the displayed ratio proof does not apply without enlarging the regenerative state or using a different argument.

The manuscript states this boundary accurately. It should remain in the theorem title or first sentence, not only in surrounding prose.

### 4.2 Reused-row tensor continuity

For each action, all finite report words are dominated by a product reference measure. The controller has finitely many simplex-valued `L^infty` rows. Positivity and row sums are weak-star closed, so the controller space is compact.

The central continuity point is correct. If bounded one-coordinate functions converge weak-star, products evaluated on distinct coordinates converge against every `L^1` density on the finite product. The proof first treats rectangles and then uses their `L^1` density. Repeated appearances of the same controller row cause no problem because they are copies on distinct report coordinates.

The diagonal counterexample correctly shows why one-time marginal domination is insufficient. If two reports are the same continuous random variable, weak-star convergence of an oscillating row does not imply convergence of its square.

This tensor step is the main nonstandard topological point in the exact theorem. The paper appropriately does not claim that subsequent compact minimization or the finite-intersection principle is new.

### 4.3 Continuity of cycle reward and length

The all-program return envelope

```text
sup_{theta,gamma} E (tau - T)_+ <= a(T),   a(T) -> 0
```

controls the tails of both reward and length because `0 <= R <= tau`. Truncated reward and length are continuous finite-response functionals. Uniform convergence therefore makes their full expectations continuous. Since mean cycle length is at least one, the ratio is continuous.

This argument is sound. The envelope is substantially stronger than a bounded mean and is imposed before optimization. The rare-long-cycle counterexample correctly shows that a bounded mean alone does not make average reward continuous under finite-response convergence.

### 4.4 Robust attainment and finite-model determination

For each fixed model, the cycle ratio is continuous on one compact controller space. The worst-model risk is lower semicontinuous and attains its minimum.

The finite-model identity follows from the finite-intersection property for the closed risk sublevel sets. This is not a minimax interchange: the same controller variable is shared across the full family and every finite subfamily.

The result is exact but topological. It supplies no cardinality bound or algorithm for the witnessing model family. The manuscript states this qualification correctly.

### 4.5 Finite-acquisition comparison

Let `K_N` be the first cycle index whose cumulative length exceeds `N`. Because cycle lengths are positive integers, `K_N <= N+1`. The centered cycle increments have mean zero, and the event that the `j`th cycle is reached depends only on preceding cycles. Thus the stopped centered sum has expectation zero without requiring a general optional-stopping theorem.

Removing the residual reward and residual time after call `N` costs at most the overshoot. Summing over possible renewal epochs gives

```text
|E C_N - N g| <= sum_{s=0}^N E (tau-s)_+.
```

This proves the displayed uniform bound after division by `N`. The argument does not assume stationary initialization or a second moment.

### 4.6 Discount comparison and moment rates

The Abel identity

```text
J_beta = (1-beta)^2 sum_{N>=1} N beta^(N-1) J_N
```

is correct, and the weights form a probability distribution. It converts the common finite-acquisition bound into a normalized-discount bound without changing the program class.

Under a uniform `1+p` moment, the manuscript uses

```text
sum_{s=0}^N (tau-s)_+ <= tau min(tau,N+1)
```

and obtains the stated `N^{-p}` order. Jensen's inequality for the geometric mixing index gives the `(1-beta)^p` order. The constants are conservative but valid.

The rare-long-cycle construction establishes sharpness of both orders uniformly over the moment class. It also correctly distinguishes a uniform-class lower example from a claim about every fixed experiment.

### 4.7 Cluster points of finite and discounted optimizers

The common uniform comparison implies that asymptotically optimal finite-acquisition or discounted controllers are asymptotically average-optimal. Compactness supplies convergent subnets, and lower semicontinuity makes every cluster point average-optimal.

This conclusion is correct under the full all-program envelope. It would generally fail without such uniformity.

## 5. Audit of recurrence defects and effective certification

### 5.1 Completed-cycle defect

The defect theorem compares complete scored-cycle laws, including preparation, actions, reports, visible flags, audit marks, readouts, and cycle length. This is the correct object for average reward.

Truncating reward and length at `T` changes each endpoint by at most `a(T)`. A bounded functional of range `[0,T]` changes in expectation by at most `T epsilon` under the paper's total-variation convention. The ratio comparison then yields

```text
2 T epsilon + 4 a(T).
```

The alternative Holder calculation under a `1+p` moment produces the stated order `epsilon^{p/(1+p)}`. The factor four accounts for both reward and length. I found this calculation correct.

The joint rare-long-cycle example genuinely attains that exponent. It is therefore not merely an artifact of the proof.

### 5.2 Primitive classical and quantum comparisons

For classical marked kernels, coupling until the first complete marked-transition mismatch gives a cycle defect bounded by the per-call error times expected cycle length. Including next hidden state, report, flag, and performed audit is essential; report marginals alone would be insufficient.

For finite-dimensional quantum instruments, the hybrid telescoping argument on unnormalized states is the correct substitute for a classical hidden-state coupling. The alive trace mass controls each replacement, and the remainder of the instrument is trace-norm contractive. No positive lower report probability or normalized posterior is used.

The theorem correctly warns that deterministic parameter-dependent transitions may be singular in operational norm despite continuous trajectories.

### 5.3 One common report-cell average

The reconstructed experiment preserves the exact joint cell/physical/audit/flag output and redraws only the within-cell report from the reference conditional law. Averaging a Borel controller row once on each cell therefore gives exactly the same marked law as a stored cell table on the original experiment.

This identity is stronger and cleaner than separately approximating a row at every phase. It preserves the same `M` states and inherits the original tail certificate through equality with an original legal cell program.

### 5.4 Same-memory finite certificate

Every Borel program is first covered by a cell program and then by dyadic action and transition rows plus a decision net. Because all ordinary actions remain legal in the continuing protocol and there is no exact internal deadline, support changes caused by rounding do not create an interface violation.

The lower and upper directions are correct:

- finite-net inclusion supports the lower endpoint;
- the all-Borel coverage error is subtracted from that lower endpoint;
- the controller-uniform model modulus belongs on the full-family upper endpoint; and
- the selected upper table runs on the original experiment with the same `M` states.

The theorem is genuinely global relative to all Borel programs of the declared size, not merely a certificate for a local optimizer.

Its limitation is equally substantial. Computable partitions, operational reconstruction errors, model nets, decision nets, return tails, and rigorous finite integrations are supplied. The exhaustive table count proves effectivity, not practical synthesis complexity.

## 6. Audit of renewal-occupation geometry

### 6.1 Checkpoint identity and online lower

For squared prediction, conditional projection gives the decomposition into irreducible full-history risk and squared error in the predictive mean. With one fixed set of at most `M` readouts, pointwise nearest-center distance lower-bounds every randomized encoder. A full-history checkpoint encoder attains this bound.

Integrating over the actual renewal-occupation submeasure proves

```text
R_cp,av(M) - B_infty = q_M(nu),
R_on,av(M) - B_infty >= q_M(nu).
```

The measure `nu` is allowed to have mass below one because preparation calls or other unscored calls remain in the physical-time denominator. This is the correct accounting.

### 6.2 Recursive online upper

The proposed predictor stores one cover-center label. After a report it applies the predictive update to the stored center and projects back to the global cover. Under the all-pair candidate-valid Lipschitz bound,

```text
e_t <= L_t e_(t-1) + r_M.
```

The resulting accumulated gain `G_t` is then squared and integrated against the actual occupation law. The comparison state is used only in the proof; the running observer stores only the cover label.

This is a valid implementation provided that the declared predictive state includes every phase/history variable needed to make the update stationary and that the fixed exploration is external to, or implementable by, the declared observer interface. The revision should state this point in the theorem rather than leave it implicit.

### 6.3 Actual small-ball lower

The union-of-balls argument is correctly applied to a positive submeasure of the actual occupation law. The mass `w` remains in the constant. No rare stratum is normalized to probability one.

Together with a global cover of order `M^{-1/d}` and a uniform occupation-stability bound, this gives matching `M^{-2/d}` orders.

### 6.4 Noncontractive cycles

After an exact first prior and first informative update, nonexpansive propagation makes the approximation error grow at most linearly with data age. Summing squared ages over a cycle gives the third-moment condition. The finite-acquisition and normalized-discount bounds follow from renewal-epoch counting and the positive Abel mixture.

This is a useful demonstration that strict filter contraction is not necessary in the regenerative class. It does not, however, remove the global-cover or fixed-exploration assumptions.

### 6.5 Renewal holding geometry

The holding theorem is exact. The relevant measure is length-biased:

```text
nu(dv) = E[L | V=v] pi(dv) / E(L+1).
```

A nearest-center label chosen at the visible load report and retained through blank holding reports attains the pointwise lower. The theorem includes arbitrary preparation families, including nondominated ones, by a direct proof.

The example `L = ceil(V^{-alpha})` correctly shows that preparation mass and occupation mass have different local exponents. It is one of the clearest demonstrations in the manuscript of why actual acquired geometry matters.

### 6.6 Scope of the geometry theorem

The general renewal theorem optimizes over the full declared stationary program class. The geometric theorem does not: it fixes preparation and exploration and studies prediction compression under that execution.

This distinction is clearly acknowledged but should be made visually unavoidable in the abstract and theorem summary. A fixed-policy matching memory law is not an unrestricted adaptive-control memory law.

## 7. Audit of the raw realizations

### 7.1 Measurable density lemma

The finite generating-partition construction gives jointly measurable conditional-expectation approximants. For each fixed parameter, martingale convergence yields a Radon--Nikodym density, and the joint liminf supplies a measurable version. The exceptional null set may depend on the parameter; the theorem correctly does not claim a common exceptional set over an uncountable family.

Sequential standard-Borel disintegration then produces finite-word densities without conditioning on a hidden path as if it were independent of the reports.

The argument is credible. For publication, it would be preferable to cite a precise measurable Radon--Nikodym kernel theorem and state the generating-partition assumptions explicitly rather than rely only on the compressed construction.

### 7.2 Hidden-state realization

Conditional domination of each one-step report kernel implies product domination for every finite intervention word. The prepared reset and assumed return envelope supply iid cycles and tail control. No finite-dimensional belief or filter contraction is required.

The effective subclass derives cell errors and model errors from complete marked density entries, not merely report densities. Visible flags and clock outcomes remain separate cells. The Gaussian tail is retained as an actual report cell.

### 7.3 Noncontractive hidden geometry

The first likelihood family is positive and normalized. The displayed inverse from posterior coordinates to the report is correct. On the compact cube, the posterior map is a smooth full-rank chart with bounded Jacobian and inverse Jacobian, so the actual first-posterior law has a bounded full-dimensional density.

Subsequent stochastic matrices are nonexpansive in `l1`, including permutations. The first posterior submeasure appears with positive occupation weight at average, finite-acquisition, and normalized-discount interfaces. The small-ball lower and recursive cover therefore give the stated order.

The finite-`N` and discounted occupation-weight calculations should be expanded by two lines in the final text. They are correct in substance, but the renewal-counting and first-data timing are currently terse.

### 7.4 Quantum realization

The exact quantum theorem composes unnormalized completely positive instrument densities, takes traces for report laws, and weights actual audit outcomes for payoff laws. Projective zeros and rank changes therefore create no posterior denominator problem.

The Kraus perturbation estimate correctly implies an operational one-call bound. The effective statement should continue to distinguish an approximation of the complete instrument from an approximation of one chosen Kraus representation; cell-averaging the full instrument, rather than averaging Kraus matrices and resquaring, is the right construction.

### 7.5 Noncommuting acquired geometry

The continuous branch maps a uniform report cube affinely into the trace-one density-matrix space. The rank-one branch remains an actual atomic stratum with its true weight. The informationally complete audit supplies separating affine prediction coordinates, and its average back-action enters the subsequent physical state.

Positive trace-preserving maps are trace-norm nonexpansive on Hermitian differences, so noncommuting unitary/audit propagation fits the noncontractive theorem. The lower constant contains the actual continuous mixture mass and disappears at weight one, as it should.

This is a legitimate finite-dimensional quantum realization. It is not a theorem about quantum observer memory or unbounded operators.

## 8. Audit of the sharp joint resource example

The joint example combines three actual coordinates:

- quantization of an acquired vector `V`;
- a fixed hidden calibration sign `u`; and
- an attained source-to-target cycle deficiency caused by erasing a fixed hidden bit on rare long cycles.

The lower bound averages fixed parameter alternatives only to establish a minimax lower; the parameter is not redrawn during execution. On a long source cycle, no report available to the controller contains the bit, so the squared-loss lower `1/4` applies at every data call. The hidden calibration sign contributes `delta^2` per scored data call. The vector coordinate gives the nearest-center lower.

The upper uses `J=floor(M/3)` vector centers crossed with readouts `0,1,1/2`. The reset label is one of the existing labels because the preparation has zero score and the visible load/hold tags distinguish the subsequent transitions. The count `3J <= M` is therefore valid.

The exact cycle deficiency is `e/2`: on a long source cycle the simulator must issue the target bit before any later acquisition and can do no better than a fair guess uniformly over the two fixed bit models. The later true audit cannot be used as earlier feedback.

Choosing `L` at scale `e^{-1/(1+p)}` keeps the `1+p` moment bounded and produces average loss of order `epsilon^{p/(1+p)}`. Combined with a regular quantization law, this gives the advertised matched slice.

This is a sharp and useful construction. It remains a selected slice, not a full lower region for program description, workspace, planning time, physical time, or simulator state.

R24's distinction is fixed `M`, robust common-model optimization, compulsory reset, and same-`M` certification. That distinction must be made explicitly.

2. **X. Venel and B. Ziliotto**, *Strong Uniform Value in Gambling Houses and Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes*, SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization 54 (2016), 1983--2008, DOI `10.1137/15M1043340`. ## 11. Major issues before specialist-journal publication

### 11.1 Put the compulsory joint reset in the main theorem's headline

The strongest assumption is not merely regeneration of the physical process. The observer itself is overwritten, and no seed, parameter estimate, or counter survives.

This should appear in the theorem title or its first displayed hypothesis. Calling the result simply an average-risk theorem for a finite-state observer risks suggesting a nonreset average-cost statement.

### 11.2 Compare directly with finite-memory long-run-average POMDP theory

The Chatterjee--Saona--Ziliotto theorem and the Venel--Ziliotto strong-uniform-value theorem are too close to omit. The introduction should contain a compact comparison table covering:

- strategy class;
- fixed versus growing memory;
- robust model family;
- reset versus nonreset information pattern;
- finite-`N`, discounted, and long-run objectives;
- type of approximation or attainment;
- computability/complexity conclusion; and
- whether the comparison is at fixed `M`.

### 11.3 Compare with restart and intermittently observed models

The new 2025--2026 restart/intermittent-observation papers should be discussed. R24's physical and observer reset is neither ordinary intermittent observation nor a controller-chosen restart. This difference should be formalized rather than left to terminology.

### 11.4 Separate the general average theorem from fixed-exploration geometry more sharply

The current abstract places average-risk optimization and matching memory geometry in one narrative. The first optimizes over the declared program class; the second fixes exploration.

A reader should not have to inspect Section 5 to discover this distinction. State it in the abstract and in a boxed theorem hierarchy.

### 11.5 Clarify the stationary implementation of the geometric upper

The predictive state must contain enough phase/history information that one Borel update `U_y` is well typed across the whole cycle. The fixed exploration must either be part of the experiment or be implementable within the counted interface.

Add an explicit interface sentence to Theorem 5.1. Otherwise the phrase “one `M`-state recursive observer” can be read as inheriting an uncounted external exploration state.

### 11.6 Make the strength of the return envelope operational

The envelope is uniform over every legal Borel program and every model. In applications this may be more difficult to verify than the response domination itself.

The manuscript should provide at least one general primitive sufficient condition for an all-controller envelope, beyond assuming an independent clock. A drift/minorization or hazard lower-bound proposition would materially improve applicability.

### 11.7 Distinguish common-reference changing support from genuine nondomination

The exact theorem allows densities to vanish and move inside one product reference. This is stronger than equivalent active supports but weaker than arbitrary mutually singular translated path laws.

State this distinction in the abstract, not only in the scope audit. The holding theorem is a direct special nondominated result and must not be used rhetorically to blur that boundary.

### 11.8 Tighten the effective theorem's contribution statement

The same-`M` lower is valuable, but the theorem assumes precisely the response approximation, model modulus, tail, and integration data needed to make the exhaustive search converge.

The paper should identify which part is theorem and which part is input specification. A worked nontrivial hidden or quantum certificate with explicit numerical constants would be more persuasive than general computability language alone.

### 11.9 Add one natural application where reset is not purpose-built

The current examples are mathematically clean but tailored to the certificate. A specialist publication would be strengthened by a theorem for a recognized regenerative filtering/control model in which:

- the physical regeneration is natural;
- observer reset is operationally required or economically optimal rather than imposed for proof convenience;
- the acquired occupation geometry is not selected merely to expose a chosen power law; and
- the result improves a known bound or settles a previously unresolved finite-memory question.

### 11.10 State the exact novelty of the finite-model identity

After compactness of the shared program space, finite-model determination is a finite-intersection consequence. The manuscript already acknowledges this, but the abstract still gives the identity prominent novelty weight.

The novel work is establishing the correct compact topology and uniform renewal passage for one reused finite program, not the abstract finite-intersection principle itself.

### 11.11 Expand the complexity discussion

Recent long-run-average POMDP results show strong complexity and undecidability phenomena in nearby classes. R24's exhaustive finite table search should be positioned against those results.

The manuscript should ask whether the regenerative fixed-`M` class has a meaningful upper or lower complexity classification, even if this is left open.

### 11.12 Calibrate the “Foundations” scope

The title may be retained as part of the long-term program, but the abstract and introduction should say immediately that the principal new theorem is a regenerative fixed-memory theorem with compulsory observer overwrite.

Without that framing, the title promises an intrinsic long-run classification that the present manuscript does not yet provide.

## 13. What would materially change the top-four assessment

Further polishing, another purpose-built regenerative example, or more build checks would not by itself change the recommendation. A top-four case would require an advance of a different order, for example:

1. **A nonreset theorem.** Extend the average-risk attainment and fixed-`M` transfer to controllers that may retain information across physical regenerations, with the retained observer state included in the regenerative analysis rather than forcibly erased.

2. **A near-necessary intrinsic criterion.** Characterize when actual acquired occupation geometry is recursively realizable at the checkpoint order, rather than assuming a global cover and sufficient all-candidate stability.

3. **A broad nondominated adaptive theorem.** Replace common finite-product domination by a genuinely intrinsic condition covering important mutually singular path families.

4. **A recognized hard natural application.** Resolve a finite-memory or robust average-risk problem already central in filtering, control, causal coding, intermittent observation, or quantum information.

5. **A substantive complexity theorem.** Give nontrivial upper and lower complexity bounds for fixed-`M` synthesis or certification, rather than only exhaustive effectivity.

6. **A full multi-resource region.** Prove matching upper and lower bounds for several intrinsic coordinates beyond labels, calibration, and one cycle-defect parameter.

7. **A general vanishing-discount/average bridge without imposed reset.** Establish uniform optimality for a natural nonregenerative partially observed class at fixed memory.

Any one of these could concentrate the architecture into a theorem of broader general-mathematics significance.

## 14. Final evaluation

Revision R24 is a serious and successful response to the R22 report. It does not merely relabel the discounted result. It introduces a fully specified regenerative information pattern, proves an average-risk theorem at unchanged persistent memory, derives sharp finite-acquisition and vanishing-discount rates, establishes a tail-dependent cycle-defect modulus, and connects the long-run value to actual renewal-occupation geometry. The hidden and quantum realizations are mathematically distinct and verify the general hypotheses without normalized-filter contraction. The holding theorem and the joint sharpness example are particularly clear demonstrations of length bias and recurrence-sensitive resource transfer.

I found no elementary fatal error in the principal arguments checked. The source/provenance pipeline is unusually disciplined and accurately limits what its finite checks and rebuilds certify.

The manuscript nevertheless remains below the exceptional threshold of the four leading general mathematics journals. Its central average theorem is tied to a compulsory physical-plus-observer reset and a strong uniform return envelope. Its geometric matching theorem fixes exploration and uses sufficient global cover/stability hypotheses. Its effective theorem is exhaustive and data-intensive. The examples verify the theory but do not yet resolve a broadly recognized hard natural problem. The closest long-run finite-memory, uniform-value, restart, intermittent-observation, and complexity literature also requires a broader theorem-level comparison.

Accordingly:

**Top-four general-journal recommendation:** reject in the present form.  
**Reason:** insufficient intrinsic breadth, natural-problem impact, and concentration of novelty for that exceptional level; not an identified fatal correctness defect.  
**Specialist-journal outlook:** favorable after a focused major revision addressing the reset scope, direct long-run POMDP literature, theorem hierarchy, stationary implementation of the geometric layer, primitive verification of the return envelope, and complexity positioning.
