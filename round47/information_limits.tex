\subsection{Actual adaptive information, LDPs, and retained washout conclusions}
Define the realized predictable information function
\[
 Q_n(\beta)=\frac1{2n\sigma^2}\sum_{i\le n}
       \E[f_i(\beta)^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}].
\]
It includes the baseline contribution, not just the exploration floor.
For a fixed infinite policy, or any chosen coupling of horizon-dependent
policy rows, summability of \eqref{eq:field-tail} at each fixed positive
threshold and of the transient tails implies
\begin{equation}
 \|\ell_n+Q_n\|_\infty\longrightarrow0\quad\text{almost surely}.
 \label{eq:tracking}
\end{equation}
This assertion is for each chosen policy sequence and coupling; it is not an
intersection of probability-one events over an uncountable class of policies.
The finite-$n$ bounds, in contrast, are common to the full policy class.

\begin{proposition}[Laplace tracking and cluster LDPs]
\label{prop:ldp}
For a fixed full-support prior on $\Bcal$, on the event \eqref{eq:tracking},
for every continuous $\varphi:\Bcal\to\R$,
\[
 -n^{-1}\log\int e^{-n\varphi(\beta)}\Pi_n^{\zeta_n}(d\beta)
 -\inf_{\beta\in\Bcal}\{\varphi(\beta)+Q_n(\beta)\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The $Q_n$ are precompact in the uniform norm. Every uniformly convergent
subsequence $Q_{n_k}\to I$ gives a full good posterior LDP with rate $I$ and
speed $n_k$. Without an information limit one still has, for every closed $F$,
\[
 \limsup_n n^{-1}\log\Pi_n^{\zeta_n}(F)
 \le-\frac{\rho a^2}{2\sigma^2}\inf_{\beta\in F}D_\chi(\beta,\beta_0).
\]
All continuous functions and Borel-set LDP inequalities are covered on that
same event.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The bounds $0\le Q_n\le M^2/(2\sigma^2)$,
$Q_n(\beta_0)=0$, and
$|Q_n(\beta)-Q_n(\gamma)|\le M d_\infty(\beta,\gamma)/\sigma^2$
give precompactness by Arzela--Ascoli. Full support and a finite cover imply
$m(s)=\inf_\beta\Pi(B_d(\beta,s))>0$ for every $s>0$. Integrating over an
$s$-ball around a minimizer gives
\[
 \inf(\varphi+Q_n)\le-n^{-1}\log\int e^{-n(\varphi+Q_n)}d\Pi
 \le\inf(\varphi+Q_n)+\omega_\varphi(s)+Ms/\sigma^2-n^{-1}\log m(s).
\]
Use \eqref{eq:tracking}, then $n\to\infty$ and $s\downarrow0$, also for
$\varphi=0$ to normalize. This deterministic argument applies simultaneously
to all continuous $\varphi$. On a convergent subsequence the likelihood tracks
$-I$ uniformly. Integration over a small neighborhood gives the open lower
bound; the supremum over a closed compact set gives the closed upper bound.
The normalizer has exponent zero. For the unconditional upper bound use
$Q_n\ge\rho a^2D_\chi/(2\sigma^2)$ instead of an equality.
\end{proof}

For completeness, the earlier washed experiment is preserved. Under bounded
pre-probe forcing, insert a zero-force interval
$w_i=(1+\epsilon_w)\lambda_*^{-1}\log(i+1)$ before a fresh signed probe.
The entire prior forcing contributes a deterministic residual bounded by
$C_*^2(R_0+U_*/\lambda_*)(i+1)^{-1-\epsilon_w}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:remainder}
and Proposition~\ref{prop:concentration} apply to
$f_i=aS_i(h_\beta(t_i)-h_{\beta_0}(t_i))$; the common metric
\eqref{eq:common-metric} still suffices. With independent duration law $\chi$
on every probe the actual information is
$I(\beta)=a^2D_\chi(\beta,\beta_0)/(2\sigma^2)$, so a full LDP holds without
subsequence extraction. For the original bounded-duration law its clock is
$(1+\epsilon_w)\lambda_*^{-1}n\log n+O(n)$. These washed statements do not
supply the linear clock of the no-washout construction.

For a fixed finite-cylinder derivative of order $r$, the ordered Duhamel
formula gives $C_r(1+w)^re^{-\lambda_*w}$ at washout duration $w$: the
semigroup durations in each integrand sum to $w$ and the simplex volume is
$w^r/r!$. Bounded full-input derivatives before washout follow by integrating
polynomial times exponential stability. Thus the differentiated residual is
bounded by $C_r(1+\log(i+1))^r(i+1)^{-1-\epsilon_w}$, summable after using any
smaller positive margin. The logarithmic factors are not omitted.

The weighted operator conclusion also persists. If $W_\tau e_k=e^{-\tau k}e_k$,
then a prefix/tail split of the tridiagonal entries gives
\[
 \|W_\tau(\Jop_\beta-\Jop_{\beta_0})W_\tau\|_\op
 \le3d_J(\beta,\beta_0)+(2b_++4a_+)e^{-2\tau(J+1)}.
\]
This is a quantitative product-topology consequence, not ordinary operator-norm
identification; damping is included separately in $d_J$.
