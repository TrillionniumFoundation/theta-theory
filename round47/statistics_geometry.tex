\section{Chronological likelihood bounds with a common infinite-time metric}
\label{sec:statistics}
The statistical argument works for both duration laws and is unchanged by a
large selected duration. Define
\begin{equation}
 d_\infty(\beta,\gamma)=(U_*+a)\|g_\beta-g_\gamma\|_{L^1(0,\infty)}.
 \label{eq:common-metric}
\end{equation}
It is a compact metric inducing the product topology: continuity follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:stability}, and injectivity follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:jet}. For every bounded realized input,
$\|(g_\beta-g_\gamma)*u\|_\infty\le U_*\|g_\beta-g_\gamma\|_1$.
Write $f_i(\beta)=m_i^\beta-m_i^{\beta_0}$. Removing only the current signed
pulse gives the exact decomposition
\begin{equation}
 f_i(\beta)=P_i(\beta)+aS_i\{h_\beta(t_i)-h_{\beta_0}(t_i)\},\qquad
 \E[f_i(\beta)^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\ge\rho a^2D_\chi(\beta,\beta_0),
 \label{eq:sign-floor}
\end{equation}
where $\chi=\omega$ or $\nu$ and $P_i$ is measurable before $S_i$.
The cross term vanishes conditional on those pre-sign actions. All remaining
conditional-square terms are nonnegative. This proves the inequality without
removing past forcing from candidate likelihoods.

The common field bounds are
\[
 |f_i(\beta)|\le M:=\max(1,2U_*C_*/\lambda_*),\qquad
 |f_i(\beta)-f_i(\gamma)|\le d_\infty(\beta,\gamma).
\]
Moreover $|b_i^\beta(z)|\le r_i=C_*R_0e^{-\lambda_*\tau_gi}$, so
\begin{equation}
 R_1=\sum r_i=\frac{C_*R_0}{e^{\lambda_*\tau_g}-1},\qquad
 R_2=\sum r_i^2=\frac{C_*^2R_0^2}{e^{2\lambda_*\tau_g}-1}.
 \label{eq:transient-sums}
\end{equation}
Neither these constants nor the metric depends on a maximum probe duration.

\subsection{Explicit entropy and prior thickness}
Suppose all coordinate prior densities are at least $p_->0$. Let $w_->0$ be
the minimum box width and $W\ge1$ its maximum enlarged to one. Put
$C_d=\max(1,4(U_*+a)C_*^2/\lambda_*^2)$. For $0<\epsilon<1$, choose
\begin{align}
 S(\epsilon)&=\max\left\{1,
 \lambda_*^{-1}\log\frac{16(U_*+a)C_*}{\lambda_*\epsilon}\right\},\notag\\
 K(\epsilon)&=\min\left\{K\ge0:
 2(U_*+a)S e^{\Lambda S}\frac{(\Lambda S)^{K+1}}{(K+1)!}
                  \le\epsilon/8\right\},\notag\\
 r(\epsilon)&=\min\{w_-,1,\epsilon/(4C_d)\},\notag\\
 \overline{\mathcal H}(\epsilon)&=(2K(\epsilon)+3)
                       \log(2+W/r(\epsilon)),\notag\\
 \mathcal P(\epsilon)&=(2K(\epsilon)+3)\log(1/(p_-r(\epsilon))).
 \label{eq:entropy}
\end{align}
For larger radii use the values at $\min(\epsilon,1/2)$, which remain valid
upper bounds. These quantities have the following deterministic meaning.

\begin{lemma}[Covers and mass without an inverse-map assumption]
\label{lem:entropy}
The log covering number $\mathcal H(\epsilon)$ for $(\Bcal,d_\infty)$ is at
most $\overline{\mathcal H}(\epsilon)$. Every open metric ball of radius
$\epsilon$ has prior mass at least $e^{-\mathcal P(\epsilon)}$.
Both upper bounds are $O((1+|\log\epsilon|)^2)$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If two parameters agree through $c,a_0,b_0,\ldots,a_K,b_K$, the boundary
entries of generator powers through degree $K$ agree by finite propagation of
a matrix word. Exponential series and the stability tail give
\[
 \|g_\beta-g_\gamma\|_1\le
 2S e^{\Lambda S}\frac{(\Lambda S)^{K+1}}{(K+1)!}
            +\frac{2C_*}{\lambda_*}e^{-\lambda_*S}.
\]
The choices in \eqref{eq:entropy} make the metric tail at most $\epsilon/4$.
Changing these finitely many coordinates by at most $r$, with a common tail,
changes the generator in operator norm by at most $4r$. Duhamel's formula
with stable factors gives the additional $L^1$ error at most
$4rC_*^2/\lambda_*^2$. Insert an admissible hybrid to combine these estimates.
A coordinate mesh of radius $r(\epsilon)$ with one fixed tail is therefore an
$\epsilon/2$ cover, of size at most
$(2+W/r(\epsilon))^{2K+3}$. At any center, including boundary truths,
coordinate intervals of length at least $r(\epsilon)$ lie inside its open
$\epsilon$ ball; their product mass is at least $(p_-r(\epsilon))^{2K+3}$.
Finally $S=O(1+|\log\epsilon|)$. Taking $K+1$ at least
\[
 \max\left\{2e\Lambda S,
 \frac{\log(16(U_*+a)S/\epsilon)+\Lambda S}{\log2},1\right\}
\]
makes the factorial expression small enough. Thus $K=O(1+|\log\epsilon|)$
and $\log(1/r)=O(1+|\log\epsilon|)$, proving the stated bounds.
\end{proof}
