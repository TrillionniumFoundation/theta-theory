\section{Model, stability, and chronological observations}
\label{sec:model}
Fix
\[
0<c_-<c_+<\infty,\quad 0<a_-<a_+<\infty,\quad
2a_+<b_-<b_+<\infty.
\]
Let $\Bcal=[c_-,c_+]\times\prod_{k\ge0}[a_-,a_+]
\times\prod_{k\ge0}[b_-,b_+]$ with its product topology and write
$\beta=(c,(a_k),(b_k))$. On $\ell^2(\N_0)$ put
\[
(\Jop_\beta q)_0=b_0q_0-a_0q_1,\qquad
(\Jop_\beta q)_k=b_kq_k-a_{k-1}q_{k-1}-a_kq_{k+1}.
\]
Thus $k_-I\le\Jop_\beta\le k_+I$, where
$k_-=b_--2a_+>0$ and $k_+=b_++2a_+$. On the position--velocity Hilbert space,
\begin{equation}
 A_\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-\Jop_\beta&-cI\end{pmatrix},\quad
 B=\binom0{e_0},\quad \ell(q,v)=q_0,\quad
 g_\beta(t)=\ell e^{tA_\beta}B,\quad h_\beta(t)=\int_0^t g_\beta(s)\,ds.
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
Here $g$ is the impulse response and $h$ the step response. The input force
$u(t)$ acts through $B$, and the measured coordinate from initial state $z$ is
$\ell e^{tA_\beta}z+(g_\beta*u)(t)$. Define
\[
 d_J(\beta,\gamma)=\max\{|c-\widetilde c|,
 \max_{0\le k\le J}|a_k-\widetilde a_k|,
 \max_{0\le k\le J}|b_k-\widetilde b_k|\}.
\]
All operator norms use the Hilbert norm; finite coefficient vectors below use
the maximum norm or the explicitly indicated one-norm. Set
\begin{equation}
 B_0=\max(1,k_+),\quad \Lambda=1+c_++B_0,\quad
 Q=8\max(2,\Lambda,T,T^{-1},a_-^{-1}),\quad T>0.
 \label{eq:box-constants}
\end{equation}
In particular $\|A_\beta\|\le\Lambda$ and $Q\ge16$.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit common stability and response compactness]
\label{lem:stability}
There are computable constants $C_*\ge1$, $\lambda_*>0$, common to the box,
with $\|e^{tA_\beta}\|\le C_*e^{-\lambda_*t}$. The maps
$\beta\mapsto g_\beta$ into $L^1(0,\infty)$ and
$\beta\mapsto h_\beta$ into $C([0,T])$ are continuous with compact images.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take $\epsilon=\min(1,c_-/2,\sqrt{k_-}/2)$ and the quadratic energy
\[
 E_\beta(q,v)=\tfrac12(\|v\|^2+\langle\Jop_\beta q,q\rangle)
             +\epsilon\langle q,v\rangle+\tfrac12\epsilon c\|q\|^2.
\]
With $m_E=\min(1,k_-)/4$ and
$M_E=\max(3/4,k_+/2+\epsilon^2+\epsilon c_+/2)$, the inequality
$\epsilon|\langle q,v\rangle|\le\|v\|^2/4+\epsilon^2\|q\|^2$
gives $m_E\|(q,v)\|^2\le E_\beta\le M_E\|(q,v)\|^2$.
Direct differentiation along the unforced equation gives
\[
 \dot E_\beta=-(c-\epsilon)\|v\|^2-\epsilon\langle\Jop_\beta q,q\rangle
 \le-d_E\|(q,v)\|^2,\qquad d_E=\min(c_-/2,\epsilon k_-).
\]
One may therefore take $C_*=\sqrt{M_E/m_E}$ and
$\lambda_*=d_E/(2M_E)$.
Coordinate convergence implies strong convergence of the uniformly bounded
Jacobi operators by first testing finite support. Exponential series then give
strong convergence of semigroups uniformly on compact time intervals. Applying
$B$ and $\ell$, and using the common integrable tail $C_*e^{-\lambda_*t}$,
proves the asserted continuity. Compactness follows from compactness of
$\Bcal$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Policies, nuisance priors, and two probe laws}
The initial state has norm at most a fixed $R_0$. Fix $a>0$, $U_0\ge0$,
$\tau_g>0$, $T_0\ge0$, and $\rho\in(0,1]$. At stage $i$, a
parameter-independent policy chooses a baseline segment of length in
$[\tau_g,\tau_g+T_0]$. A fresh exploration flag has probability $\rho$;
on exploration a diagnostic cell is drawn independently from the declared law,
and otherwise the policy chooses a probe of duration at most $T$. The baseline
force throughout the baseline and probe is bounded by $U_0$ and is fixed before
a fresh independent Rademacher sign $S_i$. The force $aS_i$ is added during the
probe. The readout at its end is corrupted by a fresh $N(0,\sigma^2)$ noise
$\xi_i$. In particular, $U_*=U_0+a$ bounds the complete realized force. Baseline
randomizers and the chosen duration are included in the information before the
sign. The policy may depend on the terminal horizon, but not on the unknown
parameter as an oracle. It receives no unrecorded intermediate observations.

The order is past, baseline and cell, sign, noise, readout. This order also
specifies the likelihood: every candidate is evaluated at the \emph{entire}
realized action record. Policy factors cancel in likelihood ratios because the
same kernel is evaluated on the same history. Let $m_i^\beta$ be the zero-state
mean under that record and $b_i^\beta(z)=\ell e^{\mathsf T_i A_\beta}z$.
For any probability $\zeta_n$ on the radius-$R_0$ Hilbert ball define
\begin{equation}
 L_n^{\zeta_n}(\beta)=\int\prod_{i=1}^n
 \varphi_\sigma(Y_i-m_i^\beta-b_i^\beta(z))\,\zeta_n(dz),\qquad
 \Pi_n^{\zeta_n}(d\beta)\propto L_n^{\zeta_n}(\beta)\Pi(d\beta).
 \label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
The true initial state need not be in the support of $\zeta_n$. This is then
an exact working likelihood and a quasi-posterior under the external truth;
no density on the initial-state space is imposed.

The compact-duration law keeps a half-mass dense enumeration of rational times
in $(0,T]$, with weights $2^{-m-1}$, $m\ge1$. Its other labeled cells are
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 R_K&=4K+8,\quad q=(K,s,k),\quad K\ge0,
 \ s\ge1,\ 1\le k\le R_K,\\
 \zeta_R&=\min(1,T/(2R)),\qquad
 t_q=k\zeta_{R_K}2^{-s},\qquad
 \omega_q=2^{-K-s-2}/R_K.
\end{split}\label{eq:compact-law}
\end{equation}
Duplicate times carry distinct labels, and there is no measured zero-time cell.
For a law $\chi$ on labels define
$D_\chi(\beta,\gamma)=\sum_q\chi_q[h_\beta(t_q)-h_\gamma(t_q)]^2$.
The second, finite-mean law is specified in Section~\ref{sec:long}; only that
extension allows unbounded single-probe durations.
