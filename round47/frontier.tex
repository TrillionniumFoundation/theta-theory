\section{Propagation, distinguishability, and matching depth orders}
\label{sec:frontier}
We now compare the attainable rates with information available in the physical
experiment. Choose two admissible parameters with the same damping and all
coefficients equal except for $b_J$, whose difference is $\delta>0$. Such a
pair exists for each $0<\delta<b_+-b_-$ by using interior values. For this
section the true initial state is zero under both parameters, which is an
allowed subexperiment of every initial-state class considered above.

\begin{lemma}[Two-way boundary propagation]
\label{lem:two-way}
Write $q_k=(e_k,0)$, $v_k=(0,e_k)$ and $m=2J+1$. Then
$\ell A^r v_J=0$ and $q_J^*A^rB=0$ for $r<m$. Consequently, for the pair
just specified and $t\ge0$,
\begin{align}
 |g_\beta(t)-g_\gamma(t)|
 &\le\delta e^{\Lambda t}\Lambda^{4J+2}
                         \frac{t^{4J+3}}{(4J+3)!},\notag\\
 |h_\beta(t)-h_\gamma(t)|
 &\le\delta e^{\Lambda t}\Lambda^{4J+2}
                         \frac{t^{4J+4}}{(4J+4)!}.
 \label{eq:two-way}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A position--velocity matrix word from $v_0$ to $q_J$ requires one initial
velocity-to-position step and two steps for each displacement along an edge.
The shortest length is $2J+1$; damping loops cannot shorten it. The same count
holds from $v_J$ to $q_0$. This proves the vanishing statements, also for the
infinite operator because any fixed word has finite support. Each of the two
propagators is thus bounded by $e^{\Lambda t}(\Lambda t)^m/m!$.
Since $A_\beta-A_\gamma=-\delta v_Jq_J^*$ up to the sign of the difference,
Duhamel's formula factors the response difference into the convolution of
these propagators. The beta integral
$\int_0^t(t-s)^ms^m\,ds=(m!)^2t^{2m+1}/(2m+1)!$
gives the first bound. Integrate once, bounding $e^{\Lambda s}$ by
$e^{\Lambda t}$, to obtain the second.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-window information order]
\label{thm:fixed-frontier}
For every probability law $\chi$ supported in $[0,T]$ the above pair satisfies
\[
 D_\chi(\beta,\gamma)\le\delta^2 B_J(T)^2,\qquad
 B_J(T)=e^{\Lambda T}\Lambda^{4J+2}T^{4J+4}/(4J+4)!.
\]
For a fixed $0<\delta_0<\min(1,b_+-b_-)$, the original law $\omega$ therefore
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 -\log\inf_{d_J(\beta,\gamma)\ge\delta_0}D_\omega(\beta,\gamma)
                =\Theta(J\log J)\quad(J\to\infty).
 \label{eq:fixed-order}
\end{equation}
In a reset, zero-state experiment with $n$ windows of duration at most $T$,
arbitrary forces bounded by $U$ and adaptively selected between windows,
$\KL(P_\beta^{\pi,n},P_\gamma^{\pi,n})\le
nU^2\delta^2B_J(T)^2/(2\sigma^2)$. Thus fixed-accuracy uniform recovery in
that experiment cannot hold when $J_n\log J_n\gg\log n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first bound is immediate from \eqref{eq:two-way}. The lower bound on the
infimum is \eqref{eq:compact-separation}; the upper bound comes from the
chosen pair. Stirling's estimate, or elementary factorial upper and lower
bounds, gives $-2\log B_J(T)=8J\log J+O(J)$ for fixed box and $T$,
which proves \eqref{eq:fixed-order}. For a bounded force on a reset window,
$|(g_\beta-g_\gamma)*u|\le U\int_0^T|g_\beta-g_\gamma|\le U\delta B_J(T)$.
In the chronological chain rule, the parameter-independent action kernels
cancel and the conditional Gaussian relative entropy is the squared mean
difference divided by $2\sigma^2$. Summing proves the stated entropy bound.
The testing inequality used in the next theorem gives the recovery conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Equation~\eqref{eq:fixed-order} describes the fixed-window response metric.
The reset-window testing statement is not asserted for an arbitrary
no-washout history: old forcing may carry information over a longer interval.
This distinction is precisely why a new duration law, rather than just new
constants in the original floor, is used for the logarithmic construction.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[All-time information bound for every bounded feedback policy]
\label{thm:global-frontier}
Set
\[
 \vartheta=\min\{\log2,\lambda_* /(8e\Lambda)\},\qquad
 D_0=(8e\Lambda)^{-1}+C_*/\lambda_*.
\]
For the two-point pair and any parameter-independent chronological policy
with complete force bounded by $U$, arbitrary finite selected observation
times, and $n$ Gaussian readouts,
\begin{equation}
 \KL(P_\beta^{\pi,n},P_\gamma^{\pi,n})
 \le \frac{nU^2\delta^2D_0^4}{2\sigma^2}
                       e^{-4\vartheta(2J+1)}.
 \label{eq:global-kl}
\end{equation}
If the right side is $K_n$, every coefficient estimator obeys
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\alpha\in\{\beta,\gamma\}}
 P_\alpha^{\pi,n}\{d_J(\widehat\beta,\alpha)\ge\delta/2\}
 \ge\tfrac12\{1-\sqrt{K_n/2}\}.
 \label{eq:testing}
\end{equation}
A negative right side can be replaced by zero. This is uniform over feedback
policies, regardless of how long they wait between readouts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For either off-boundary propagator in Lemma~\ref{lem:two-way}, split its
$L^1$ integral at $S=m/(8e\Lambda)$, $m=2J+1$. On $[0,S]$, Taylor vanishing
and $m!\ge(m/e)^m$ give
\[
 \int_0^S|\ell e^{tA}v_J|\,dt
 \le S e^{\Lambda S}(\Lambda S)^m/m!
 \le \frac{m}{8e\Lambda}4^{-m}
 \le(8e\Lambda)^{-1}2^{-m}.
\]
The bound $e^{1/(8e)}<2$ justifies the middle inequality. The stability tail
is at most $(C_*/\lambda_*)e^{-\lambda_*S}$. Thus each propagator has
$L^1$ norm at most $D_0e^{-\vartheta m}$.
Duhamel's convolution identity now yields
\[
 \|g_\beta-g_\gamma\|_1\le\delta D_0^2e^{-2\vartheta(2J+1)}.
\]
At any common realized history, the entire selected input is the same bounded
function for the two candidate laws. Their conditional means, from the common
zero initial state, differ by at most $U$ times this $L^1$ norm, at \emph{every}
selected time. Applying the chronological Gaussian relative-entropy chain rule
proves \eqref{eq:global-kl}. It does not freeze an adaptive design before
taking its true expectation. The two disjoint strict balls of radius
$\delta/2$ define a test by choosing the closer parameter. Its sum of errors
is at least $1-\TV(P_\beta,P_\gamma)$, and Pinsker's inequality bounds total
variation by $\sqrt{\KL/2}$, proving \eqref{eq:testing}.
\end{proof}

If $J_n/\log n\to\infty$ the right side of \eqref{eq:global-kl} tends to zero
for fixed $\delta>0$, and the error lower bound tends to $1/2$. Together with
Corollary~\ref{cor:rates}, this proves the depth-order part of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. With inter-readout time at least $\tau_g$, a deadline
$t$ allows at most $\lfloor t/\tau_g\rfloor$ readouts, so the same lower bound
holds with this maximum count (by the chain rule for a stopped record).
Proposition~\ref{prop:clock} gives the attainable matching order in physical
time. Feedback may improve constants or the actual information $Q_n$; it
cannot change this order under the fixed force bound and observation model.
No claim that every feedback policy attains the best constant is made.
