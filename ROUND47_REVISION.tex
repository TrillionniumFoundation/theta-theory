\title[Sharp depth scales for boundary identification]{Sharp Depth Scales for Adaptive Boundary Identification of Infinite Damped Jacobi Lattices}
\author{Qian Qi}
\date{September 5, 2026. Round 47 revision responding to Round 46.}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We identify an infinite damped Jacobi lattice from noisy observations of one
boundary coordinate, with bounded chronological feedback and an unknown initial
state. A closed response--moment formula and orthogonal Gram factorization give
a response-jet inverse with condition number at most $Q^{25(J+1)}$ through depth
$J$. Oversampling on a fixed time window improves the previously used multiscale
experiment to separation $\exp\{-C(J+1)\log(e(J+1))\}\delta^{12}$, without
changing its sampling law. A different, explicitly declared probe law has
unbounded support but finite mean duration and gives separation
$\exp\{-C_\infty(J+1)\}\delta^8$. The exact posterior then recovers a positive
multiple of $\log n$ coefficients with polynomially shrinking error, uniformly
over bounded predictable baselines, in linear elapsed time with exponential
clock tails. A two-way propagation estimate gives a matching logarithmic
\emph{order} upper bound on recoverable depth for every bounded-input adaptive
policy. On fixed response windows the corresponding distinguishability order
is $J\log J$. We give finite-sample posterior budgets, simultaneous confidence
cylinders, and a terminating rational outer-enclosure algorithm. The information
floor, the actual adaptive information, and frequentist coverage are kept
separate. No optimal depth constant or nonparametric Gaussian limit is asserted.
\end{abstract}
