\section{Research object and precise relationship to the previous revision}
\label{sec:r35-scope}
Round 33 supplied a valid adaptive calibration example whose sensor offset did not enter the hidden mechanics. Here both unknown coordinates enter an infinite system of mechanical equations. The observation is a physical displacement. The same unknown initial state persists throughout the experiment; no diagnostic word prepares a new state. The central result is the construction of a finite diagnostic library which verifies, on this model, the statistical assumptions previously left at an interface.

The argument does not assert that information coercivity implies filter contraction. Dissipation proves state forgetting; a two-duration transfer calculation proves identification; randomized signs remove cancellation by the existing state; and a global likelihood bound proves posterior concentration. These are separate steps.

\subsection{Literature comparison and the proposed contribution}
Adaptive Gaussian posterior approximations without convergence of normalized information already occur in Du--Nair--Janson \cite{DNJ}, Theorem 1. Their displayed experiment has a mean linear in an unknown finite coefficient and a parameter-free observed covariate. Our mean is the nonlinear parameter-dependent response of a hidden infinite lattice; a bounded initial-state nuisance is integrated out. We prove the required global contrast and likelihood localization directly. This is a model-specific extension and verification, not a claim that adaptive Bernstein--von Mises theory begins here. Their distinction between a Gaussian posterior approximation and frequentist coverage is retained.

The $n^n/n!$ analytic-scale majorant in Round 33 B2 is classical Ovsyannikov methodology. Finkelshtein \cite{Finkelshtein}, equation (2.12), already uses this structure. We do not claim it as the new theorem here, nor use it to replace missing hard-sphere geometry. The present bounded Hilbert generator needs no loss-of-radius theorem.

Bayesian identification of finite-dimensional linear systems in canonical coordinates is already treated by Bryutkin et al.~\cite{Canonical}. Infinite-dimensional evolution posterior approximations also exist, for example Nickl's initial-condition inference for parabolic evolution~\cite{Nickl}. Those comparisons rule out a claim that either Bayesian mechanical identification or infinite-dimensional posterior approximation is new by itself. Our unknown is a local coefficient pair under adaptive forcing, rather than an unknown initial function; the latter is a decaying nuisance here. For these two additional works we use their stated research scope, not an unverified theorem-level import.

The contribution proposed for review is the explicit four-word, infinite-mechanical-state construction, including uniform control over feedback policies and initial preparations, together with its derived filter, evidence, and posterior consequences. Priority for this particular package is not asserted on the basis of an exhaustive literature search. The rigorous distinction from the cited adaptive linear experiment is its verified nonlinear mechanical mean and transient infinite-dimensional nuisance, not a stronger claim about arbitrary adaptive systems.

\section{A coupled infinite mechanical experiment}
\label{sec:r35-model}
Let $\mathcal H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_0;\R)$ and $\Hh=\mathcal H\oplus\mathcal H$, with state $z=(q,v)$. Put $Pq=\ip{q}{e_0}e_0$ and $Q=I-P$. The half-line spring operator is
\[
 (Lq)_0=q_0-q_1,\qquad (Lq)_j=2q_j-q_{j-1}-q_{j+1}\quad(j\ge1).
\]
Initially define it on finitely supported sequences. Since
$\ip{Lq}{q}=\sum_{j\ge0}(q_{j+1}-q_j)^2$ and $\norm L\le4$, it extends to a bounded nonnegative self-adjoint operator. In particular $\ip{Le_0}{e_0}=1$.

The unknown parameter is $\theta=(c,k)$ in the positive rectangle
$\Th=[c_-,c_+]\times[k_-,k_+]$, with $c_-,k_->0$. Known constants $c_b,k_b>0$ and $\epsilon\ge0$ define
\[
 C_\theta=cP+c_bQ,\quad K_\theta=kP+k_bQ+\epsilon L,\quad
 A_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-K_\theta&-C_\theta\end{pmatrix},\quad
 B=\binom0{e_0},\quad \ell(q,v)=q_0.
\]
The actual equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r35-mechanics}
 \dot q=v,\qquad \dot v=-C_\theta v-K_\theta q+e_0u(t).
\end{equation}
Thus the unobserved coordinates interact with the observed coordinate whenever $\epsilon>0$. Damping is an explicit physical assumption. No dissipative conclusion is transferred to an invertible measure-preserving billiard.

\subsection{Chronological actions and observations}
Fix force bound $a>0$, diagnostic fraction $\rho\in(0,1]$, noise variance $\sigma^2>0$, and a duration $\tau>0$ chosen in Section~\ref{sec:r35-words}. At block $i$, a policy chooses $(d_i,u_i)\in[\tau,2\tau]\times[-a,a]$ from the observed past and a fresh independent randomizer. It holds the force $u_i$ for duration $d_i$, then draws a new independent $N(0,\sigma^2)$ readout error. Set $t_i=\sum_{j\le i}d_j$, $t_0=0$, and observe
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r35-observations}
 Y_i=\ell z_\theta(t_i)+\xi_i.
\end{equation}
Each of the four atoms $(\tau,a),(\tau,-a),(2\tau,a),(2\tau,-a)$ has conditional probability at least $\rho/4$. The remaining probability is unrestricted within the bounded action space. Blocks do not overlap. The policy kernel is the same measurable function of the observed record under all parameters; it may compute a posterior internally, but may not use the unknown true parameter as an oracle.

Write $T_\theta(t)=e^{tA_\theta}$, and for the realized, held-fixed action record put
\[
 x_i^\theta=\int_0^{t_i}T_\theta(t_i-s)Bu(s)\,ds,\quad
 m_i^\theta=\ell x_i^\theta,\quad b_i^\theta(z)=\ell T_\theta(t_i)z.
\]
The readout mean for initial state $z$ is $m_i^\theta+b_i^\theta(z)$. Differentiation always holds the realized actions and durations fixed, as required by the chronological likelihood.

The parameter prior has a continuous density $\pi$ on $\Th$, positive near the true parameter $\theta_0\in\operatorname{int}\Th$. Independently it assigns any Borel probability $\nu$ to the ball $\{\norm z\le R\}$ of $\Hh$. The ball need not be compact. The true initial state $z_0$ is any point of the same ball; membership in the topological support of $\nu$ is not required for the parameter conclusion, because the possible initial-state misspecification is transient. This does not assert posterior consistency for the initial state itself.

All uniform probability assertions below take a supremum over admissible policies, $\norm{z_0}\le R$, and $\theta_0$ in a fixed compact subset of the interior where the parameter prior is bounded below. They also hold uniformly over the initial priors just specified. Fixed model constants, including $\tau,\rho,a,\sigma,R$, are not allowed to deteriorate with the sample size. Policies may depend on that size.

\section{Energy, differentiability, and the bounded initial-state correction}
\label{sec:r35-energy}
Put
\[
 c_* =\min(c_-,c_b),\quad c^*=\max(c_+,c_b),\quad
 k_* =\min(k_-,k_b),\quad k^*=\max(k_+,k_b)+4\epsilon.
\]
\begin{lemma}[Uniform physical energy estimate]\label{lem:r35-energy}
There are explicit $M\ge1$ and $\lambda>0$ such that
$\norm{T_\theta(t)}\le Me^{-\lambda t}$ for all $t\ge0$ and $\theta\in\Th$.
For every fixed derivative order $r$ there are $M_r,\lambda_r>0$ such that
\[
 \sup_\theta\norm{\partial_\theta^\alpha T_\theta(t)}
 \le M_r e^{-\lambda_r t}\quad(|\alpha|\le r).
\]
Consequently all fixed-order parameter derivatives of $x_i^\theta$ are uniformly bounded, and those of $b_i^\theta(z)$ are bounded by $C_rR e^{-\lambda_r i\tau}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\delta=\min(c_*/2,\sqrt{k_*}/2)$ and define
\[
 \mathcal E_\theta(q,v)=\tfrac12\norm v^2+\tfrac12\ip{K_\theta q}{q}
       +\delta\ip qv+\tfrac\delta2\ip{C_\theta q}{q}.
\]
The inequality $\delta|\ip qv|\le\norm v^2/4+\delta^2\norm q^2$ gives
\[
 e_*\norm{(q,v)}^2\le\mathcal E_\theta(q,v)\le e^*\norm{(q,v)}^2,
\]
where $e_* =\min(1,k_*)/4$ and
$e^*=\max(3/4,k^*/2+\delta^2+\delta c^*/2)$.
For zero force the cross terms cancel exactly:
\[
 \mathcal E_\theta'=-\ip{(C_\theta-\delta I)v}{v}
                    -\delta\ip{K_\theta q}{q}
 \le-\eta\norm{(q,v)}^2,
 \quad \eta=\min(c_*-\delta,\delta k_*)>0.
\]
Take $M=(e^*/e_*)^{1/2}$ and $\lambda=\eta/(2e^*)$.
The bounded operator $A_\theta$ generates its exponential on all of $\Hh$, so there is no domain or range condition omitted in this calculation.

The two first derivatives of $A_\theta$ have norm one and all higher derivatives vanish. Repeated differentiation of the exponential gives finitely many ordered integrals of $r+1$ semigroups and $r$ such derivatives. Their norm is at most $C_r t^r e^{-\lambda t}$; reducing the decay exponent absorbs the polynomial. Integrating these bounds against the bounded force proves the last assertions, also on a small positive parameter neighbourhood of $\Th$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact nuisance likelihood and its derivative budget]\label{prop:r35-nuisance}
After cancellation of the common chronological policy factors, the marginal likelihood is
\begin{align}
 L_n^\nu(\theta)&=L_n^0(\theta)e^{R_n^\nu(\theta)},&
 L_n^0(\theta)&=\prod_{i\le n}\varphi_\sigma(Y_i-m_i^\theta),\label{eq:r35-likelihood}\\
 R_n^\nu(\theta)&=\log\int e^{V_n^\theta(z)}\nu(dz),&
 V_n^\theta(z)&=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{i\le n}
 \left[(Y_i-m_i^\theta)b_i^\theta(z)-\tfrac12 b_i^\theta(z)^2\right].\nonumber
\end{align}
For every fixed $r$ there is a random variable $W_r$ with moments of all finite orders, bounded uniformly in the preceding experiment class, such that
\[
 \sup_{n,\theta,\norm z\le R}\sum_{|\alpha|\le r}
 |\partial_\theta^\alpha V_n^\theta(z)|\le W_r,
 \qquad \sup_n\norm{R_n^\nu}_{C^r(\Th)}\le C_r(1+W_r^r+W_r).
\]
For any one fixed infinite policy, $R_n^\nu$ converges almost surely in $C^r(\Th)$ to a finite $R_\infty^\nu$. This last pathwise assertion is not a coupling of different horizon-dependent policies.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Disintegrate in the order $(d_1,u_1),Y_1,(d_2,u_2),Y_2,\ldots$ for a fixed initial state. Its Gaussian readout factors yield the product in \eqref{eq:r35-likelihood}; integration over the initial state gives the formula. Although the force record is data dependent, its policy factors are constant in $\theta$ at that record and can be removed before this integration. Conditioning on all future actions is not used.

Under the true experiment, $|Y_i|\le C+|\xi_i|$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:r35-energy}. Every differentiated summand of $V_n$ is bounded by
$C_r e^{-\lambda_r i\tau}(1+|\xi_i|)$. The infinite sum of these bounds is almost surely finite and has all finite moments by Minkowski's inequality and the Gaussian moments. Differentiating a log integral expresses its derivatives as averages and cumulants of the bounded derivatives of $V_n$ under its positive normalized tilt. This gives the polynomial bounds displayed, independently of $\nu$. Uniform summability of the differentiated series gives the asserted $C^r$ limit, by dominated differentiation and positivity of the integral. No density on the infinite Hilbert space is assumed.
\end{proof}

\section{Two durations and four signs verify mechanical identifiability}
\label{sec:r35-words}
Define the scalar step response
\[
 h_\theta(t)=\ell\int_0^tT_\theta(s)B\,ds.
\]
The first four Taylor coefficients follow from the physical operator:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r35-step}
 h_\theta(t)=\frac{t^2}{2}-\frac{ct^3}{6}
             +\frac{(c^2-k-\epsilon)t^4}{24}+r_\theta(t).
\end{equation}
In particular the unknown $k$ is not an additive sensor offset. Its first contribution is of order $t^4$.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit global two-duration embedding]\label{lem:r35-embedding}
Set $D=1+c^*+k^*\ge1$, $K=D^4e^{2D}$, and
\[
 \tau_0=\min\left(1,\frac1{(600+144c_+)K}\right).
\]
For $0<\tau\le\tau_0$, the map
$H_\tau(\theta)=(h_\theta(\tau),h_\theta(2\tau))$ satisfies
\[
 |H_\tau(\theta)-H_\tau(\theta')|\ge b_\tau|\theta-\theta'|,
 \qquad |DH_\tau(\theta)v|\ge b_\tau|v|,
 \quad b_\tau=\frac{\tau^4}{52c_++128}>0.
\]
A less conservative duration $0<\tau\le1$ is also valid if one verifies $\sup_\Th\norm{DF_\tau-I}\le1/2$ and $\sup_\Th|f_1|\le c_++1$ for the map below. This is a finite-time mechanical calculation, not an information assumption.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We have $\ell B=0$, $\ell A_\theta B=1$, $\ell A_\theta^2B=-c$ and $\ell A_\theta^3B=c^2-k-\epsilon$, proving \eqref{eq:r35-step}. For $0\le t\le2$, expansion of the remaining operator powers gives
\[
 \max\bigl(|r_\theta(t)|,\norm{D_\theta r_\theta(t)}\bigr)\le Kt^5.
\]
Indeed $\norm{A_\theta}\le D$ and
$\norm{\partial_j A_\theta^l}\le lD^{l-1}$; summing the exponential tails bounds the two remainders respectively by $D^4t^5e^{Dt}/5!$ and $\sqrt2D^3t^5e^{Dt}/4!$.

Put
\[
 f_1=\frac3{4\tau^3}[h(2\tau)-16h(\tau)+6\tau^2],\quad
 f_2=\frac3{\tau^4}[h(2\tau)-8h(\tau)+2\tau^2],\quad
 F_\tau=(f_1,f_1^2-f_2-\epsilon).
\]
Then $f_1=c+e_1$, $f_2=c^2-k-\epsilon+e_2$ with
$\norm{e_1}_{C^1}\le36K\tau^2$ and $\norm{e_2}_{C^1}\le120K\tau$,
where the $C^1$ norm here is the maximum of the value and Euclidean-gradient bounds. Our choice of $\tau$ makes $|e_1|\le1$, and hence
\[
 \norm{DF_\tau-I}
 \le(180+72c_+)K\tau^2+120K\tau\le\tfrac12.
\]
Integrating $D(F_\tau-I)$ along a straight segment in the convex rectangle gives
$|F_\tau(\theta)-F_\tau(\theta')|\ge|\theta-\theta'|/2$.
As a function of $H_\tau$, the derivative of $F_\tau$ has norm at most
$(26c_++64)\tau^{-4}$ on the joining chord: the first linear row has norm at most $13\tau^{-3}$, the second linear row for $f_2$ at most $25\tau^{-4}$, and $|f_1|\le c_++1$. This proves both inequalities, the differential one also following by differentiation. The argument is entirely on $\Hh$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A nonzero-coupling duration certified over a whole rectangle]
\label{prop:r35-certificate}
Take $c,k\in[1,2]$, $c_b=3/2$, $k_b=1$, $\epsilon=1/10$ and $\tau=1/32$.
The two explicit checks in Lemma~\ref{lem:r35-embedding} hold. The certificate in
\texttt{tools/certify\_round35.py} uses rational polynomial arithmetic, not a grid of parameter points. It yields
\[
 \sup_\Th\norm{DF_\tau-I}^2\le 0.036375<1/4,
 \qquad \sup_\Th|f_1|<3.
\]
The displayed decimal is rounded upward from the exact rational bound stored with the certificate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Compute $\ell A_\theta^jB$ for $0\le j\le9$ as polynomials in $(c,k)$ on eleven sites. Bandedness implies that nine applications cannot reach the artificial far boundary; these coefficients therefore equal those of the infinite operator. On the rectangle, $\norm{A_\theta}\le D=27/5$ and each coefficient derivative obeys $\norm{\partial A_\theta^j}\le jD^{j-1}$. For $Dt<1$ the value and each first partial derivative of the omitted response tail are bounded by the rational quantity
\[
 T_9(t)=\frac{2D^{10}t^{11}}{10!(1-Dt)}.
\]
This follows by bounding the exponential series from index ten and summing a geometric majorant. Propagate $T_9(\tau),T_9(2\tau)$ through the two linear combinations and through the square $f_1^2$, including the product of both errors. Center every resulting polynomial at $c=3/2+x/2$, $k=3/2+y/2$; the sum of the absolute coefficients bounds it on $|x|,|y|\le1$. The four derivative-error bounds obtained this way are, rounded upward,
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0.000934563&0.000381052\\0.176511060&0.072232476\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their underlying exact rational squares have sum less than $0.036375$. The same value calculation gives $|f_1|<3$. Every arithmetic comparison is rational; the geometric tail covers all uncomputed powers and hence the entire infinite lattice. The executable proof certificate and independent locality checks accompany the source.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Random signs remove the pre-existing state]\label{lem:r35-excitation}
Let $\mathcal F_{i-1}$ be the observed history before block $i$, enlarged by the true initial state when necessary. For any candidate $\theta$, under the true experiment,
\begin{align}
 \E[(m_i^\theta-m_i^{\theta_0})^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]
   &\ge \alpha|\theta-\theta_0|^2,\label{eq:r35-contrast}\\
 \E[(v^TD_\theta m_i^{\theta_0})^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]
   &\ge\alpha|v|^2,\qquad \alpha=\rho a^2b_\tau^2/2.\nonumber
\end{align}
There are $c_1,c_2>0$ such that, outside an event of probability at most $c_1e^{-c_2n}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r35-empirical}
 \sum_{i\le n}(m_i^\theta-m_i^{\theta_0})^2
 \ge\frac\alpha2 n|\theta-\theta_0|^2\quad\hbox{for every }\theta\in\Th.
\end{equation}
The constants are uniform in policies and true initial states.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Condition on the past. For duration $s\in\{\tau,2\tau\}$ the mean difference has the form $d_s+u[h_\theta(s)-h_{\theta_0}(s)]$, where $d_s$ contains the whole preceding, possibly infinite, state response. The two sign atoms contribute at least
\[
 \frac\rho4\{(d_s+ab_s)^2+(d_s-ab_s)^2\}
 =\frac\rho2(d_s^2+a^2b_s^2).
\]
Sum over the two durations and use Lemma~\ref{lem:r35-embedding}. The same calculation with a parameter tangent proves the information inequality. No independent block states are asserted.

Here are details needed to make the empirical bound uniform near $\theta_0$. Parameterize a normalized difference as
\[
 q_i(\theta,v)=\int_0^1v^TDm_i^{\theta_0+s(\theta-\theta_0)}\,ds,
 \quad\theta\in\Th,\quad v\in S^1.
\]
These functions and their parameter-index Lipschitz constants are bounded deterministically by Lemma~\ref{lem:r35-energy}. The differences
$q_i^2-\E(q_i^2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})$ are bounded martingale differences. A fixed sufficiently fine net of the compact index set and Azuma's inequality show that their empirical averages have absolute value at most $\alpha/2$, simultaneously in the index, except with probability $c_1e^{-c_2n}$. The net size is independent of $n$; interpolation costs at most twice the deterministic Lipschitz constant times its mesh. At $v=(\theta-\theta_0)/|\theta-\theta_0|$, $q_i$ is precisely the normalized difference in \eqref{eq:r35-contrast}, which proves \eqref{eq:r35-empirical}. The argument does not require the contrast lower bound for unrelated pairs $(\theta,v)$ in the net.
\end{proof}

\section{Likelihood localization and the mechanical posterior theorem}
\label{sec:r35-posterior}
Write $g_i=Dm_i^{\theta_0}\in\R^2$ as a column and define
\[
 I_n=\frac1{n\sigma^2}\sum_{i\le n}g_ig_i^T,\qquad
 \Delta_n=\frac1{\sigma^2\sqrt n}\sum_{i\le n}g_i\xi_i.
\]
These are realized sensitivity information and an innovation score. The predictable bracket averages over the fresh action as well; its difference from $I_n$ tends to zero in probability by a bounded martingale estimate. No claim that a future-dependent adaptive design makes the score conditionally Gaussian is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform martingale smooth-field estimate]\label{lem:r35-score}
For any fixed derivative order $j$,
\[
 \E\sup_{\theta\in\Th}\left|\sum_{i\le n}\xi_i\partial_\theta^\beta m_i^\theta\right|
 \le C_j\sqrt n\quad(|\beta|\le j).
\]
In particular $\Delta_n=O_P(1)$ uniformly. There is a deterministic upper bound for $I_n$; its smallest eigenvalue is at least $\alpha/(2\sigma^2)$ except with exponentially small probability.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At any fixed parameter each summand is an innovation martingale difference: the derivative depends only on past actions and the action chosen before $\xi_i$. Its second moment is bounded by $C_j$, so its sum has $L^2$ norm at most $C_j\sqrt n$.
For a smooth scalar field $S$ on a rectangle with corner $(c_-,k_-)$,
\begin{align*}
 S(c,k)={}&S(c_-,k_-)+\int_{c_-}^c S_c(s,k_-)ds
 +\int_{k_-}^k S_k(c_-,r)dr\\
 &+\int_{c_-}^c\int_{k_-}^k S_{ck}(s,r)drds.
\end{align*}
Take absolute values, suprema and expectations, and use the fixed-parameter bound on these four terms. All additional derivatives are bounded by Lemma~\ref{lem:r35-energy}. This proves the displayed estimate without an unproved stochastic equicontinuity step. The upper information bound is immediate. For the lower bound differentiate \eqref{eq:r35-empirical} at the interior point $\theta_0$ along any unit vector. It holds for every candidate on one event, hence gives the matrix inequality simultaneously in directions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Verified uniform quadratic likelihood and posterior concentration]\label{thm:r35-lan}
For each fixed $M<\infty$, uniformly on $|h|\le M$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r35-lan}
 \log\frac{L_n^\nu(\theta_0+h/\sqrt n)}{L_n^\nu(\theta_0)}
 =h^T\Delta_n-\tfrac12h^TI_nh+O_P(n^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
On an event with exponentially small complement there is a uniformly tight random $Z_n$ such that, simultaneously for every $\theta\in\Th$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r35-global}
 \log\frac{L_n^\nu(\theta)}{L_n^\nu(\theta_0)}
 \le -\frac\alpha{4\sigma^2}n|\theta-\theta_0|^2
                   +Z_n\sqrt n|\theta-\theta_0|.
\end{equation}
Consequently the posterior is uniformly tight at scale $n^{-1/2}$: for each $\eta>0$,
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\mathrm{experiments}}
 \Prob\{\Pi_n(\sqrt n|\theta-\theta_0|>M)>\eta\}=0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The zero-initial-state log likelihood ratio is exactly
\[
 \frac1{\sigma^2}\sum_{i\le n}\xi_i d_i
 +\frac1{\sigma^2}\sum_{i\le n}b_i^{\theta_0}(z_0)d_i
 -\frac1{2\sigma^2}\sum_{i\le n}d_i^2,
 \quad d_i=m_i^\theta-m_i^{\theta_0}.
\]
The initial-state cross term is bounded by $C|\theta-\theta_0|$. By the fundamental theorem of calculus and Lemma~\ref{lem:r35-score}, the innovation sum is bounded by $O_P(\sqrt n)|\theta-\theta_0|$ uniformly. Proposition~\ref{prop:r35-nuisance} bounds $|R_n^\nu(\theta)-R_n^\nu(\theta_0)|$ by $O_P(1)|\theta-\theta_0|$. Combining these with \eqref{eq:r35-empirical} proves \eqref{eq:r35-global}.

For $\theta-\theta_0=h/\sqrt n$, expand $d_i$ to second order. The innovation times the second-order term is $O_P(n^{-1/2})$ by the fixed-parameter martingale estimate for the Hessian. Its third-order remainder is bounded by $Cn^{-3/2}\sum|\xi_i|=O_P(n^{-1/2})$. The remainder in the quadratic sum is at most $Cn|h/\sqrt n|^3$, and the initial-state and nuisance corrections are $O_P(n^{-1/2})$. These estimates are uniform on the fixed ball and prove \eqref{eq:r35-lan}.

The prior is bounded above on $\Th$ and below on a fixed neighbourhood of $\theta_0$. Integrating \eqref{eq:r35-lan} over $|h|\le1$ bounds the evidence ratio below by $n^{-1}e^{-O_P(1)}$. On the event where $Z_n$ and this $O_P(1)$ term are bounded, \eqref{eq:r35-global}, with $h=\sqrt n(\theta-\theta_0)$, bounds the posterior tail by a constant times
$\int_{|h|>M}\exp[-\alpha|h|^2/(4\sigma^2)+Z_n|h|]dh$.
It tends to zero as $M\to\infty$ uniformly on that event. Uniform tightness of its constants proves the assertion. This is the annular/localization argument, rather than an assumed testing conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Mechanical Bernstein--von Mises and normalized evidence]\label{thm:r35-bvm}
The actual marginal parameter posterior satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r35-bvm}
 \TV\left(\mathcal L(\sqrt n(\theta-\theta_0)\mid\mathcal F_n),
            N(I_n^{-1}\Delta_n,I_n^{-1})\right)\longrightarrow0
\end{equation}
in probability, uniformly in the stated class. Define the Gaussian arbitrarily on the exponentially unlikely event of singular $I_n$.
In addition, for $\mathcal Z_n^\nu=\int_\Th\pi(\theta)L_n^\nu(\theta)d\theta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r35-evidence}
 \frac{\mathcal Z_n^\nu}{L_n^\nu(\theta_0)}
 =\frac{2\pi\,\pi(\theta_0)}{n\sqrt{\det I_n}}
       \exp\left(\tfrac12\Delta_n^TI_n^{-1}\Delta_n\right)[1+o_P(1)].
\end{equation}
Neither result assumes convergence of $I_n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On a fixed $h$ ball use \eqref{eq:r35-lan} and continuity of the prior to compare the unnormalized posterior to
$\pi(\theta_0)\exp(h^T\Delta_n-h^TI_nh/2)$ in $L^1(dh)$.
For completeness the comparison is uniform after restricting to events where $|\Delta_n|$ is bounded and $I_n$ lies between its positive deterministic bounds. The complement has arbitrarily small probability by Lemma~\ref{lem:r35-score}. The global integrable bound in \eqref{eq:r35-global} makes the unnormalized tails arbitrarily small on the same type of event. The Gaussian tails are uniform there as well. Thus the full unnormalized integrals and densities converge relatively in $L^1$; their normalizing constants are bounded away from zero on the event by integration over the unit ball. Normalization yields \eqref{eq:r35-bvm}.
Completing the two-dimensional Gaussian square gives
$\int\exp(h^T\Delta_n-h^TI_nh/2)dh=2\pi(\det I_n)^{-1/2}\exp(\Delta_n^TI_n^{-1}\Delta_n/2)$.
The Jacobian is $d\theta=n^{-1}dh$. This proves \eqref{eq:r35-evidence}, including its relative error and continuous-density normalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is and is not LAN or a confidence theorem]
Without an information limit, \eqref{eq:r35-lan} is a uniform local quadratic approximation with random information; it is not called classical LAN with one deterministic limiting matrix. If $I_n\to I>0$ deterministically, bounded predictable sensitivity coefficients, Gaussian innovation moments, and convergence of the predictable bracket give $\Delta_n\Rightarrow N(0,I)$ by the martingale central limit theorem, and the usual LAN conclusion follows. Gaussian posterior approximation alone does not establish Wald coverage under every adaptive policy; this distinction agrees with \cite{DNJ}.
\end{remark}

\section{Actual infinite-state filtering and its derivative jets}
\label{sec:r35-filter}
For a fixed parameter let
\[
 w_{n,\theta}^\nu(dz)=e^{V_n^\theta(z)-R_n^\nu(\theta)}\nu(dz),\qquad
 \Pi_{n,\theta}^\nu=(z\mapsto x_n^\theta+T_\theta(t_n)z)_\#w_{n,\theta}^\nu.
\]
These are the initial-state posterior and the physical current-state filter, respectively. Their likelihoods are everywhere positive for a finite record. For $r\ge0$, $C_b^r(\Hh)$ denotes bounded, continuously Frechet differentiable tests with all derivatives through order $r$ bounded, with the maximum derivative norm. Signed derivative functionals are measured in its norm dual. No finite-dimensional Lebesgue density for a belief is used.

\begin{theorem}[Time-uniform filter-jet stability from mechanics]\label{thm:r35-filter}
For $j=0,1,2$ and every finite $p\ge1$, there are $C_p,\gamma_p>0$ such that
\[
 \E\sup_{\theta\in\Th}
 \norm{\partial_\theta^j\Pi_{n,\theta}^\nu
       -\partial_\theta^j\delta_{x_n^\theta}}_{(C_b^{j+1}(\Hh))'}^p
 \le C_p e^{-\gamma_p n}.
\]
The parameter derivatives are strong derivatives in the displayed dual after using one further bounded test derivative for the Taylor remainder. In particular the first two filter jets have uniformly bounded moments. For any two initial priors supported in the ball, their filter jets differ by the same type of exponentially decaying bound. For the undifferentiated filters,
\[
 W_1(\Pi_{n,\theta}^{\nu_1},\Pi_{n,\theta}^{\nu_2})
 \le 2MR e^{-\lambda n\tau}
\]
holds deterministically for every observation record.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All posterior initial states remain in the fixed ball. Coupling any two such states gives the last bound by Lemma~\ref{lem:r35-energy}, irrespective of the posterior reweighting.
For a test $\phi$, subtract $\phi(x_n^\theta)$ before differentiating:
\[
 \Pi_{n,\theta}^\nu(\phi)-\phi(x_n^\theta)
 =\int[\phi(x_n^\theta+T_\theta(t_n)z)-\phi(x_n^\theta)]w_{n,\theta}^\nu(dz).
\]
The bracket and its first $j$ parameter derivatives are bounded by
$C_j e^{-\lambda_j n\tau}\norm\phi_{C_b^{j+1}}$; the extra test derivative controls differences of derivatives at the two nearby points. Derivatives of the normalized weight through order $j$ have total variation bounded by a fixed polynomial of $1+W_{j+1}$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:r35-nuisance}. For example the first density derivative is $\partial V-\int\partial V\,dw$, and the second is the corresponding squared centered score plus its derivative, all integrable uniformly.
Leibniz's rule gives an exponential bound times that polynomial. Its $p$th moment is finite uniformly, proving the result. Uniform Taylor bounds on the test unit ball, bounded parameter derivatives of the trajectories, and the same likelihood majorants justify the strong derivatives rather than only pointwise test differentiation. Applying the estimate to both priors proves their comparison. The information inequality is not used anywhere in this filter proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A physical-state posterior approximation]\label{cor:r35-state}
Let $G_n=D_\theta x_n^{\theta_0}:\R^2\to\Hh$. For the joint parameter/initial-state posterior, the conditional law of
$\sqrt n(z(t_n)-x_n^{\theta_0})$ is at bounded-Lipschitz distance $o_P(1)$ from the image of $N(I_n^{-1}\Delta_n,I_n^{-1})$ under $G_n$. Its covariance is the trace-class operator $G_nI_n^{-1}G_n^*$, of rank at most two. This is an approximation by a sequence of random Gaussian measures; convergence of $G_n$ is not assumed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On $|h|\le M$, the bounded second parameter derivative gives
$\sqrt n(x_n^{\theta_0+h/\sqrt n}-x_n^{\theta_0})=G_nh+O(M^2/\sqrt n)$ in $\Hh$ uniformly. The initial-state displacement is at most $\sqrt nMR e^{-\lambda n\tau}$ for every posterior initial state. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:r35-bvm} to the parameter and Theorem~\ref{thm:r35-lan} to discard its tails, then let $M\to\infty$. Finite rank gives trace class directly. No total-variation approximation on the full infinite-dimensional state is claimed.
\end{proof}

\section{The same infinite mechanical model has an exact nonzero memory kernel}
\label{sec:r35-memory}
Resolve the coordinate $(q_0,v_0)\in\R^2$ and let the unresolved state contain every site $j\ge1$. The unresolved generator $A_Q$ has damping $c_bI$ and stiffness $k_bI+\epsilon L_D$, where $L_D$ is the half-line Jacobi matrix with diagonal two and off-diagonal minus one. It is bounded and exponentially stable by the same energy proof. The resolved block is
\[
 A_P=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-(k+\epsilon)&-c\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The coupling $B_Q:\R^2\to\Hh_Q$ inserts $\epsilon q_0$ into the acceleration at site one, while $C_Q:\Hh_Q\to\R^2$ sends $q_1$ to $(0,\epsilon q_1)$. Both are bounded finite-rank maps on the underlying Hilbert spaces.

\begin{theorem}[Verified mechanical memory and its resolvent]\label{thm:r35-memory}
For $r(t)=(q_0(t),v_0(t))$ the exact resolved equation is
\[
 \dot r=A_Pr+\binom0{u(t)}+
       \int_0^t\mathcal K(t-s)r(s)ds+C_Qe^{tA_Q}z_{Q,0},
 \quad \mathcal K(t)=C_Qe^{tA_Q}B_Q.
\]
The kernel and initial-state forcing decay exponentially. For $\epsilon>0$ and $\operatorname{Re}z$ sufficiently large, put $p(z)=z^2+c_bz+k_b+2\epsilon$. Then
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal K}(z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\s(z)&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 s(z)=\frac{p(z)-\sqrt{p(z)^2-4\epsilon^2}}2,
\]
where the branch is fixed by $s(z)\sim\epsilon^2/z^2$ on the positive real axis. It is the analytic branch provided by the stable resolvent, not an independently chosen sign at each frequency. In particular
\[
 s(z)=\epsilon^2z^{-2}-c_b\epsilon^2z^{-3}+O(|z|^{-4}).
\]
The zero-coupling kernel is identically zero. Every finite-order input-domain condition is satisfied because $A_Q$ is bounded.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Variation of constants in the unresolved block, followed by substitution into the resolved equation, proves the time identity. Exponential stability and bounded couplings give the decay. Eliminating the unresolved velocity from $(z-A_Q)^{-1}B_Q$ reduces its first-position response to the $(1,1)$ entry $g(z)$ of
$[p(z)I-\epsilon(S+S^*)]^{-1}$, where $S$ is the unilateral shift on the bath sites. For sufficiently large positive real $z$, the operator is positive and invertible. Deleting its first row and column leaves an identical Jacobi tail, so a scalar Schur complement gives $g=1/(p-\epsilon^2g)$. Its solution compatible with $g\sim p^{-1}$ is
$g=(p-\sqrt{p^2-4\epsilon^2})/(2\epsilon^2)$. Analytic continuation through the actual resolvent defines the branch wherever that continuation is used, and $s=\epsilon^2g$. Alternatively its Neumann series converges directly when $|p|>2\epsilon$.
Since $A_Q$ is bounded, the resolvent Neumann series for $|z|>2\norm{A_Q}$ has a norm-controlled remainder. The first coupling product is $C_QB_Q=0$, the next is the matrix with lower-left entry $\epsilon^2$, and the following one has lower-left entry $-c_b\epsilon^2$. This proves the displayed expansion on every fixed vertical line at large modulus, as well as in the exterior region. Nonzero coupling therefore gives a genuine, computed mechanical memory, not a spectral-gap assumption attached to a toy matrix.
\end{proof}

\section{Preparation labels have derived, generally random amplitudes}
\label{sec:r35-preparation}
Take finitely many initial priors $\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_J$ in the same ball with positive label probabilities $w_1,\ldots,w_J$. The common parameter prior is $\pi$, and one common adaptive policy is used for all labels. A label changes the initial preparation, not the force selected after knowing that label.

\begin{theorem}[Exact preparation-memory amplitudes]\label{thm:r35-labels}
Under any fixed infinite admissible policy, define
\[
 \rho_{j,\infty}=
 \frac{w_j\exp R_\infty^{\nu_j}(\theta_0)}
      {\sum_lw_l\exp R_\infty^{\nu_l}(\theta_0)}.
\]
The label posterior converges in probability to this vector. Jointly with the scaled parameter, its posterior is at total-variation distance $o_P(1)$ from
\[
 \left(\sum_j\rho_{j,\infty}\delta_j\right)
          \otimes N(I_n^{-1}\Delta_n,I_n^{-1}).
\]
All amplitudes above are obtained from the convergent initial-state likelihood series of Proposition~\ref{prop:r35-nuisance}; they are not inserted as an assumed phasewise local density. They are strictly positive but generally random. For horizon-dependent policies the same conclusion holds with the finite-$n$ weights proportional to $w_j e^{R_n^{\nu_j}(\theta_0)}$, without asserting a common almost sure limit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:r35-evidence} for each of the finitely many priors. Since $L_n^{\nu_j}(\theta_0)=L_n^0(\theta_0)e^{R_n^{\nu_j}(\theta_0)}$, every factor except the latter exponential is common. Division gives the finite-$n$ weights with an $o_P(1)$ error. Proposition~\ref{prop:r35-nuisance} gives their almost sure limiting values for one fixed infinite policy. Each conditional parameter posterior has the same Gaussian approximation by Theorem~\ref{thm:r35-bvm}; the total variation distance of the finite joint mixture is bounded by the sum of its weight error and weighted component distances. This proves the claim. It is compatible with forgetting of the current state: bounded early evidence can distinguish initial preparations without increasing indefinitely.
\end{proof}
