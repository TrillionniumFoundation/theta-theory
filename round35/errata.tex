\section{Authoritative corrections to the Round 33 supporting statements}
\label{sec:r35-errata}
These corrections answer R34-C2-01, R34-B1-01 and the two specification remarks in Section 5 of the controlling report. The original Round 33 files remain identifiable at their reviewed commit. The replacement statements below, not the unqualified old sentences, govern the Round 35 submission.

\subsection{Zero-evidence versions are bounded}
\begin{theorem}[Corrected evidence-weighted comparison]\label{thm:r35-bounded-evidence}
Let $(X_n,X)$ be a coupling and $g_n(X_n,y),g(X,y)\ge0$ be normalized conditional observation densities relative to $\zeta(dy)$. Let $F$ be bounded measurable and put $M=\norm F_\infty$,
\[
 r_n=\E g_n,\quad r=\E g,\quad N_n=\E[F(X_n)g_n],\quad N=\E[F(X)g],
\]
$e_n=\E\int|g_n-g|d\zeta$, and $b_n=\E|F(X_n)-F(X)|$.
Use the quotients $u_n=N_n/r_n$, $u=N/r$ where their denominators are positive and choose versions satisfying $|u_n|,|u|\le M$ everywhere, for example zero on zero-evidence sets. Then
\[
 \int r_n|u_n-u|d\zeta\le b_n+2Me_n.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Everywhere $N=ru$ and $N_n=r_nu_n$, including the zero-evidence sets. Hence
$r_n|u_n-u|\le|N_n-N|+M|r_n-r|$.
The second term integrates to at most $Me_n$. Decomposing $N_n-N$ as
$\E[(F(X_n)-F(X))g_n]+\E[F(X)(g_n-g)]$ and integrating gives $b_n+Me_n$, using normalization of $g_n$. This proves the bound.
\end{proof}
For $F=0$ the only permitted bounded version is zero. Choosing value one at a limiting null observation of positive approximating probability is precisely the counterexample excluded by the corrected quantifier. This restriction is not omitted when comparing different observation laws.

\subsection{The one-particle momentum--energy endpoint}
\begin{proposition}[Corrected anchor law including its atom]\label{prop:r35-anchor-endpoint}
For independent $V_1,\ldots,V_m\sim N(0,I_d)$ and integer $m\ge1$, set
$P_m=\sum V_i$, $E_m=\sum|V_i|^2/2$ and $W_m=E_m-|P_m|^2/(2m)$.
If $m=1$, $W_1=0$ almost surely, and the joint law of $(P_1,E_1)$ is the Gaussian measure on the graph $e=|p|^2/2$, not a density on $\R^{d+1}$.
If $m\ge2$, $P_m\sim N(0,mI_d)$ is independent of $W_m\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(d(m-1)/2,1)$. The joint density is the product of this Gaussian density and the gamma density at $w=e-|p|^2/(2m)>0$; it is zero otherwise. No gamma probability law with zero shape is invoked.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the identity defining $W_1$. For $m\ge2$, an orthogonal transformation in the label coordinate separates $P_m/\sqrt m$ from $d(m-1)$ independent standard normal coordinates. Half of their squared norm has the stated positive-shape gamma law. The transformation $(p,w)\mapsto(p,w+|p|^2/(2m))$ has Jacobian one.
\end{proof}
The Fourier smoothing budget in the original supporting B1 theorem already takes $m$ strictly above its stated lower bound. Its smoothing conclusion is unchanged.

\subsection{Hilbert summation retains the range of Minkowski's inequality}
The scalar-to-Hilbert moment implication in B3 is stated for $p\ge2$. If $(e_i)$ is an orthonormal basis at the scalar test level and the weaker-level embedding has singular values $s_i$ with $\sum_i s_i^2<\infty$, then
\[
 \left(\E\left[\sum_i s_i^2|Z(e_i)|^2\right]^{p/2}\right)^{2/p}
 \le\sum_i s_i^2(\E|Z(e_i)|^p)^{2/p}.
\]
Prove it for finite sums by Minkowski in $L^{p/2}$ and then use monotone convergence. The subsequent finite-grid theorem already assumes $p>2$. No claim with an $L^r$ triangle inequality for $r<1$ is made.

\subsection{Predictability and the jump intensity in the fixed-basis BSDE}
In the supporting C2 BSDE theorem, the mark space is standard Borel, $\lambda$ is a deterministic sigma-finite measure, and the Poisson random measure has compensator $dt\lambda(de)$. Jump integrands are predictable and belong to $L^2(\Prob\otimes dt\otimes\lambda)$; Brownian integrands are predictable and square integrable. Drivers are jointly measurable, progressively measurable in $(\omega,t)$ for fixed $(y,z,u)$, uniformly Lipschitz in the stated Hilbert norms, and square integrable at zero. The common usual filtration has the specified Brownian--Poisson martingale representation property. These specifications make the stochastic integrals and the fixed-basis stability estimate well typed; they do not assert convergence across changing filtrations.
