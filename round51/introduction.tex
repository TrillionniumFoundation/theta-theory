\section{The theorem and its statistical interface}
A short forcing interval and the age of its observed response are different
resources. An unreset system retains the effect of a short pulse and exposes
it at increasing lags. The result below quantifies this distinction for labeled
physical coefficients, while leaving the latent state infinite-dimensional.

\begin{theorem}[Single-atom logarithmic-depth identification]
\label{thm:main}
Fix the pinned, damped coefficient box of Section~\ref{sec:model}, a duration
cap $T>0$, noise variance $\sigma^2>0$, force amplitude $a>0$, baseline bound
$U_0$, and an initial-state radius $R_0<\infty$. Let $\chi$ be any probability
law on $(0,T]$ with an atom of mass $w>0$ at $t_0$, where
$2t_0\Lambda\le1/64$. Fix $\rho\in(0,1]$. Each stage has a gap of length
$t_0$ and a probe: independently use $\chi$ with probability $\rho$, and
otherwise use duration $t_0$. Add a fresh independent Rademacher force
$aS_i$ on every probe. Before each block, commit all bounded baseline profiles,
including their dependence on duration choices, without using signs or readouts
from that block. Arbitrary feedback from earlier blocks is allowed.

For the explicit constants in \eqref{eq:constants}, every $r,s>0$ with
$C_b r+p_b s<1$ is attainable at
\[
 J_n=\lfloor r\log n\rfloor,\qquad \delta_n=n^{-s},\qquad m_n=O(\log n).
\]
Under a product coefficient prior whose densities have a common positive lower
bound, the complete working posterior for $n'=m_n\lfloor n/m_n\rfloor$
readouts has $\Pi_{n'}\{d_{J_n}\ge\delta_n\}\to0$ in probability, uniformly
over admissible truths, bounded external initial states, working initial-state
laws on the radius-$R_0$ ball, and these block-committed profiles. Coordinatewise
posterior medians recover the prefix to error $\delta_n$ with probability tending
to one. Deterministically, $\mathsf T_{n'}\le(T+t_0)n'$.

A separate finite-response estimator has the same sufficient exponent region.
Over all bounded-force chronological policies, uniform fixed-accuracy recovery
at $J_n/\log n\to\infty$ is impossible by the two-point bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:global}. The best attainable depth order is therefore logarithmic.
\end{theorem}

The clock is chosen in this theorem, not externally imposed. Commitment is
within blocks only, and there is neither a state reset nor an unrecorded
measurement. The full posterior uses unsuccessful as well as successful blocks.
The single-atom hypothesis does not require a dense support or multiscale family;
the remainder of $\chi$ can even have a continuous component. A deterministic
short-probe design is included by $w=1$. The sufficient and necessary joint
exponent regions need not have identical leading constants.

\subsection{Comparison by mathematical components}
The deterministic response/moment reconstruction for Jacobi systems belongs
to inverse spectral and boundary-control theory \cite{Teschl,MM2019,MM2020}.
Here the finite-jet conditioning is explicit and uniform in the unobserved
tail. Logarithmic rates for exponentially ill-posed Bayesian problems likewise
have a substantial history \cite{ASZ2014}; a logarithm alone is not the contribution.

Nonparametric impulse-response estimation is a genuine comparator, not a
finite-dimensional straw target. Goldenshluger \cite{Gold1998} studies stable
transfer functions with polynomially or exponentially decaying impulse
responses, including FIR least-squares bounds and adaptation. We use this
verified scope without importing an unverified theorem-level statement from
that article. In the version of Sarkar--Rakhlin--Dahleh specified in
\cite{SRD2020}, Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.1 estimate Hankel blocks and FIR
vectors; Proposition 5.1 exposes both a dimension-dependent bound and a bound
through system norms. Their Assumption 1 and Algorithm 1 specify the stochastic
input/noise experiment. Neither a growing Hankel block nor a dimension-dependent
intermediate estimate proves that finite-response methods cannot apply here.

Section~\ref{sec:transfer} makes the reduction precise. A compatible FIR error
bound is a valid substitute for our response estimator. Its error is multiplied
by the sampled-channel and physical-inverse constants, and finite-section bias
must be smaller than that amplified error budget. We prove those constants
and a dimension-uniform truncation bound, rather than assert an obstruction
based only on the word ``infinite''. The additional statistical statement is
contraction of the \emph{complete} nonlinear working posterior with an
initial-state law that may miss the truth; it does not follow from a frequentist
FIR error bound without a likelihood argument. Sampled matrix logarithms and
aliasing are classical \cite{YTG2016}; our local norm condition and pulse-channel
correction are explicit. These componentwise statements specify the contribution
without claiming an exhaustive priority determination.
